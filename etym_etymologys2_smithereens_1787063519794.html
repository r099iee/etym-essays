Irish (Gaelic) (traditional)
smiodar
"a fragment,
a piece"
↓
Irish (diminutive)
smidirín
"a small fragment,
a little piece"
↓
English (1829)
smithereens
"shivering fragments,
tiny broken bits"
↓
English (present)
SMITHEREENS
"small shattered pieces,
esp. 'into smithereens'"

Note: The English spelling "smither-" is a folk-etymological reshaping under the influence of smith; the word has no connection to smiths. The Irish diminutive suffix -ín appears in English as "-een," the same little-suffix heard in "colleen." English speakers re-analysed the Irish singular smidirín as an English plural (smither + eens), producing the plural-only noun.
