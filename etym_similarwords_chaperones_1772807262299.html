• Guardian: Similar because both assume responsibility for a young person's welfare and safety; differs because a guardian holds legal or long-term responsibility for a minor's care and property, whereas a chaperone's role is situational supervision of conduct and reputation.
  • Escort: Similar because both accompany someone in public; differs because an escort may be purely temporary and non-moral (security, ceremonial), lacking the propriety-policing function that defines a chaperone.
  • Duenna: Similar because both are older women who supervise a young lady's conduct in public; differs because duenna is culturally specific to Spanish/Portuguese high society and strictly feminine, while chaperone is broader and today gender-neutral.
  • Governess: Similar because both are women in a supervisory household role over children; differs because a governess educates (academic instruction), whereas a chaperone protects conduct and reputation rather than teaching.
  • Nursemaid: Similar because both are household women entrusted with a child's daily care; differs because a nursemaid provides physical childcare of small children, while a chaperone escorts and polices propriety and may supervise older charges.
  • Bodyguard: Similar because both provide protective accompaniment; differs because a bodyguard guards against physical violence and attack, whereas a chaperone guards against social impropriety and scandal.
  • Attendant: Similar because both accompany and wait upon another; differs because an attendant implies service or ceremonial function, not the moral supervision a chaperone exercises.
  • Monitor: Similar because both oversee conduct; differs because a monitor is institutional (hall monitor, exam invigilator) rather than a social escort protecting reputation.
