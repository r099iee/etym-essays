• Sliver: Similar because it is a small thin piece broken off a larger mass; differs because a sliver is long and thin with sharp edges, whereas a flake is flat, broad, and fragile rather than sharp.
  • Chip: Similar because it is a small fragment broken off something; differs because a chip is chunkier and produced by impact or cutting, while a flake splits off along a flat plane or surface layer.
  • Shard: Similar because it is a broken piece of material; differs because a shard is typically sharper and larger, usually of glass, pottery, or metal broken in violence, not a thin layer peeled away.
  • Splinter: Similar because it is a small piece split off, usually of wood; differs because a splinter is narrow and pointed, whereas a flake is broad and flat.
  • Scale: Similar because it is a thin flat plate of skin, rust, or bark; differs because scales are regular, overlapping plates that form a surface, while flakes are irregular fragments separating from it.
  • Fleck: Similar because it is a tiny particle; differs because a fleck is a small spot or speck of colour or matter, not necessarily a flat, detached three-dimensional piece.
  • Speck: Similar because it is a very small particle; differs because a speck is smaller still and suggests a stain or dot rather than a flat fragment with real thickness.
  • Shaving: Similar because it is a thin piece removed from a surface; differs because a shaving is cut or planed off (wood, soap) and is often curled, not split off naturally.
  • Lamina: Similar because it is a thin flat layer; differs because "lamina" is a technical scientific term for a plate or layer, without the idea of breaking off.
  • Sheet: Similar because it is a broad flat piece; differs because a sheet is large, deliberate, and intact, not a fragment.
  • Unreliable person (figurative): Similar because a "flake" (slang) names someone who cannot be depended on; differs completely from the physical sense — here the word is a metaphor for a person who falls apart or fails to follow through, as in "flaky behaviour."
