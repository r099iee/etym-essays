- Antiquity to Middle Ages (Astronomical & Mythological Era) - Originating in ancient Greek myth as the seven daughters of Atlas (Pleïádes) and designated as the open star cluster in Taurus; borrowed into Classical Latin (Pleiades) and Old French as a celestial calendar marker signaling the opening of the Mediterranean sailing season.
- 16th Century (The French Renaissance Poetic Revolution) - Reappropriated in the 1550s by Pierre de Ronsard, Joachim du Bellay, and their five companions (initially calling themselves la Brigade) to christen their group of seven poets dedicated to elevating French vernacular verse to the heights of classical antiquity.
- 17th–18th Century (Classical and Enlightenment Metaphor) - Generalizes across French literature and Enlightenment rhetoric into an elevated metaphor for any brilliant constellation, assembly, or academy of eminent thinkers, scientists, or statesmen.
- 1931–Mid-20th Century (The Schiffrin & Gallimard Publishing Era) - Founded as a publishing collection in 1931 by Jacques Schiffrin and incorporated into Gallimard in 1936, the Bibliothèque de la Pléiade becomes the definitive, gold-embossed temple of global literary canonization, immortalizing Proust in 1954.
- Late 20th–21st Century (The Apex of Cultural Consecration) - Solidifies in international cultural discourse as the ultimate secular canonization; the phrase "entrer dans la Pléiade" is recognized worldwide as the supreme lifetime or posthumous honor in letters.
