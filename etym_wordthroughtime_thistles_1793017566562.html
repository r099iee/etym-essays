- Old English — the curse-weed: þistel names the spiny field weed, already the type of land gone wrong and labour made painful.
- Medieval — thorns of the fall: preachers rank thistles with thorns as the earth's punishment weed, springing where Adam's sweat falls.
- 13th–15th c. — Scotland adopts it: legend and badge turn the weed into a nation, crowned on coins and carved on abbeys.
- 1687 — the Order: the Most Ancient and Most Noble Order of the Thistle formalises the weed as chivalry, with its defiant motto.
- Burns and after — the poet's weed: Burns addresses the thistle with rough tenderness, and Victorians press it into jewellery, china, and railway posters.
- Today — weed and emblem at once: farmers still fight creeping thistle with law and spray, while athletes wear it with pride, one plant serving scorn and honour together.
