• Pillaging: Similar because both mean stripping a place of goods by force, especially in war; differs because pillage is the close military term for sack and plunder (from Latin pilare "to strip of hair"), while plundering centres on carrying off the household goods and keeps its German origin.
  • Looting: Similar because both mean seizing goods illegally during disorder; differs because looting (from Hindi lūṭ) is the modern word for crowd-and-riot theft and civil disturbance, less formal and less military than plundering.
  • Sacking: Similar because both mean devastating a captured place; differs because sacking implies the full sack and ruin of a captured city (the "bag" carried off, from Latin saccus), while plundering emphasizes the taking of goods rather than destruction alone.
  • Ransacking: Similar because both mean searching and taking by force; differs because ransacking stresses the thorough, violent search of a place, while plundering stresses the carrying off of valuables.
  • Ravaging: Similar because both describe destructive raids; differs because ravaging emphasizes devastation and ruin (from Old French ravager), while plundering emphasizes the theft of movables.
  • Despoiling: Similar because both mean stripping of possessions; differs because despoiling (from Latin spoliare) is more formal and can extend to honour or beauty, while plundering is blunter and goods-focused.
  • Marauding: Similar because both name raiding and roving violence; differs because marauding stresses the roaming, predatory search of a band (from French maraud "rascal"), while plundering stresses the seizure itself.
  • Robbing: Similar because both mean taking by force; differs because robbing is the broad, everyday word for any theft or violent taking, without the military or large-scale sense of plundering.
  • Stealing: Similar because both mean taking what is not one's own; differs because stealing is the neutral, general verb that need not involve force or war, while plundering implies force and scale.
  • Stripping: Similar because both mean emptying a place of its contents; differs because stripping is the general word for removing layers or contents and lacks the armed, predatory colour of plundering.
  • Depredation (noun): Similar because both name destructive plundering; differs because depredation (from Latin praedari "to prey upon") is formal, usually plural, and noun-only, while plundering works as verb, participle, and noun.
