• Matchless: Similar in having no rival; differs because matchless keeps the image of a contest in which no opponent even appears, while peerless speaks the language of rank and standing.
  • Unmatched: Similar in having no equal; differs because unmatched is the plain, factual word of a contest left unwon, whereas peerless carries the aristocratic, rank-conscious weight of the peerage.
  • Unrivalled: Similar in being above any competitor; differs because unrivalled imagines a rival who never draws level, while peerless denies the existence of an equal altogether.
  • Unparalleled: Similar in having nothing to be compared with; differs because unparalleled is the rhetorical superlative of the extraordinary, while peerless grounds its claim in the social ladder of equals and nobles.
  • Incomparable: Similar in being beyond comparison; differs because incomparable is the logical extreme — too different in kind to be compared at all — and is easily weakened into polite hyperbole, whereas peerless stays factual about rank.
  • Unequalled: Similar in measuring the absence of a match; differs because unequalled is dry and factual, recording what has not been achieved, while peerless is warmer, loftier, and rooted in the world of peers.
  • Unsurpassed: Similar in being the greatest of its kind; differs because unsurpassed concedes that others have come close, while peerless admits no nearness at all — it stands outside the scale.
  • Unique: Similar in being without a second of its kind; differs because unique means one of a kind, not necessarily superior, while peerless is explicitly a superlative of excellence, not merely of singularity.
