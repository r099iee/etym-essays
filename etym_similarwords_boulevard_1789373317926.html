• Avenue: Similar because both refer to wide, grand, tree-lined urban thoroughfares of significant civic importance; differs because avenue historically originated as an approach road leading up to a palace, country estate, or grand public monument (from French avenir "to arrive"), whereas boulevard historically originated on the circular perimeter of demolished fortifications, often retaining a wider multi-lane layout with specialized lateral service roads and landscaped medians.
  • Thoroughfare: Similar because both describe major roads open at both ends for public passage and vehicular traffic; differs because thoroughfare is a functional, generic legal and navigational term emphasizing unobstructed transit through an area without any implication of beauty, width, or trees, whereas boulevard specifically denotes a prestigious, landscaped street designed for aesthetic grandeur and pedestrian leisure.
  • Promenade: Similar because both serve as public spaces dedicated to leisurely walking, social strolling, and observing the urban spectacle; differs because promenade denotes the act or pedestrian pathway of walking—often located along a waterfront, beach, or park, and frequently pedestrianized without heavy vehicular traffic—whereas a boulevard is fundamentally a major, multi-lane vehicular street that integrates pedestrian paths alongside traffic.
  • Parkway: Similar because both are landscaped vehicular roadways bordered by trees and greenery; differs because a parkway is typically a modern, high-speed arterial roadway or controlled-access highway designed to pass through a scenic greenbelt or park setting (often with heavy commercial trucks prohibited), whereas a boulevard is an integrated urban street framed by commercial buildings, shops, and apartments.
  • Esplanade: Similar because both trace their etymological roots to flattened military fortification grounds; differs because an esplanade originally meant the flat, open glacis outside a fortress that provided a clear field of fire and has today become almost exclusively a wide, open walkway along a waterfront or seashore, whereas a boulevard is an inland urban avenue cutting through the interior of a city.
  • Concourse: Similar because both accommodate massive volumes of urban transit and pedestrian movement; differs because concourse refers to a large, open central gathering hall or indoor pedestrian corridor (such as in an airport, railway station, or shopping mall) where multiple paths converge, whereas a boulevard is an outdoor, linear, tree-canopied city avenue.
