Note on phonological adaptation and orthography: The Sanskrit forms Bāhlīka, Bālhika, Vāhlīka, and Vālhika represent direct phonological adaptations of the ancient Iranian toponym attested in Old Persian as Bāxtriš and in Avestan as Bāxδi- (ultimately from Proto-Indo-Iranian *Bāxδrī-). Because Indo-Aryan lacked the Iranian voiceless velar fricative /x/ and voiced dental fricative /δ/, Sanskrit scribes and speakers rendered the foreign cluster through metathesis and aspiration as -hl- or -lh-, while the initial voiced bilabial stop /b/ alternated freely with the labial-velar glide /v/ (Bāhlīka ~ Vāhlīka). Geographically and culturally, the name anchored the northern terminus of the Uttarāpatha (Great Northern Road), connecting the Indian subcontinent with the Silk Road and the Eurasian steppes.
