• Referee: Similar because both are the officials who enforce rules and decide play in games; differs because referee has narrowed to sport and to academic refereeing, while umpire retains older judicial and diplomatic senses.
  • Arbitrator: Similar because both are chosen neutral parties who settle disputes; differs because an arbitrator acts under a formal submission to decide a concrete legal case, not primarily in sport.
  • Arbiter: Similar because both denote a person whose decision is accepted as final between contending parties; differs because arbiter is the more august word for authority over taste, conduct, or policy, while umpire carries the concrete image of a third man deciding a game or quarrel.
  • Mediator: Similar because both are neutral third parties in a conflict; differs because a mediator tries to reconcile the parties without imposing a decision, while an umpire actually decides.
  • Judge: Similar because both decide between parties in a contest; differs because a judge acts by law with coercive state power, while an umpire's authority rests on agreed rules and consent.
  • Adjudicator: Similar because both are appointed to give a binding decision in a dispute; differs because adjudicator is the formal, administrative term for one who settles claims or competitions, while umpire belongs to games and the older legal "odd third."
  • Moderator: Similar because both preside over proceedings involving conflicting parties; differs because a moderator guides discussion and keeps order without necessarily deciding, while an umpire's function is to give a ruling.
  • Conciliator: Similar because both intervene in disputes between parties; differs because a conciliator works to restore goodwill and reach compromise, while an umpire pronounces a decision.
  • Negotiator: Similar because both deal with a contested matter between two sides; differs because a negotiator represents one party's interests in bargaining, while an umpire is impartial and decides for all.
  • Go-between: Similar because both stand between two parties in communication; differs because a go-between merely conveys messages, while an umpire's role is to judge and enforce.
