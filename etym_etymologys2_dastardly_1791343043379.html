PIE (reconstructed)
*dʰewh₂- / *dʰew-
"to die, become faint, lose consciousness"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*dōwijaną / *das-
"to be exhausted, dazed, or stuporous"
↓
Old Norse (c. 10th–12th c.)
dasa / dasaðr
"to become weary; dazed, exhausted"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
dasen
"to stupefy, stun, grow bewildered"
↓
Middle English (mid-15th c.)
dastard
"a dullard, a sluggish or cowardly person"
↓
Early Modern English (1560s)
dastardly
"contemptibly cowardly, sneakily base"
↓
Modern English (present)
dastardly
"cowardly, treacherous, maliciously underhanded"
Note: The base word dastard was formed in Middle English from the stem dast- (a variant of dased / dazed) combined with the French-origin pejorative suffix -ard (as seen in coward, drunkard, sluggard, and bastard). The original sense was "one who is stupified or sluggish," which chivalric culture equated with shameful desertion of duty.
