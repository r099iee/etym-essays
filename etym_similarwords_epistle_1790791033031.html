• Missive: Similar because both denote a written communication sent from one person to another; differs because a missive is typically an official, urgent, or weighty dispatch sent with command or purpose, whereas an epistle carries elevated literary, didactic, philosophical, or biblical prestige.
  • Letter: Similar because both refer to written messages sent to a recipient; differs because letter is the everyday, neutral, generic term for all personal, practical, or postal communications, while epistle denotes a stylized, formal, philosophical, poetic, or scriptural composition.
  • Dispatch: Similar because both are written messages sent across distance; differs because a dispatch is a prompt, official report on military, diplomatic, or journalistic business prioritizing speed and factual update over literary reflection.
  • Rescript: Similar because both are authoritative written communications; differs because a rescript is an official written decree or reply issued by a Roman emperor or the Pope resolving a legal or theological question.
  • Encyclical: Similar because both are authoritative doctrinal letters circulated to Christian congregations; differs because an encyclical is specifically a papal circular letter addressed to all bishops of the Catholic Church concerning matters of doctrine, morals, or social teaching.
  • Brief: Similar because both are written documents communicating instructions or news; differs because a brief is either a concise legal argument presented to a court or a formal papal document of lesser solemnity than a bull.
  • Memorandum: Similar because both convey written information between parties; differs because a memorandum is an informal, internal business note or diplomatic record intended for practical reference rather than public literary or spiritual instruction.
