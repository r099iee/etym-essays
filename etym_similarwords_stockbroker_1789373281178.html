• Broker: Similar because both function as licensed commercial agents facilitating transactions between buyers and sellers in exchange for a fee or commission; differs because broker is a broad generic umbrella covering real estate, maritime freight, insurance, customs, and commodities, whereas stockbroker specializes specifically in capital equities, corporate shares, and exchange-traded securities.
  • Investment Advisor: Similar because both assist individual and corporate clients in navigating financial markets and deploying investment capital; differs because an investment advisor provides holistic wealth management, tax planning, and strategic asset allocation bound by a strict fiduciary standard (putting the client's best interests first), whereas a traditional stockbroker operates primarily as a transactional executor bound by a suitability standard.
  • Trader: Similar because both engage directly in the active buying and selling of financial securities on market exchanges; differs because a trader often acts as a principal trading directly on behalf of a hedge fund, investment bank desk, or proprietary account to exploit short-term market inefficiencies, whereas a stockbroker acts as an agent executing orders for third-party clients.
  • Dealer: Similar because both execute transactions in debt and equity markets; differs because a dealer trades securities for their own account from their own inventory (acting as a market maker and principal taking inventory risk), whereas a broker acts purely as an intermediary matching outside buyers and sellers.
  • Asset Manager: Similar because both oversee client investments in financial markets; differs because an asset manager exercises discretionary fiduciary authority over pooled funds, institutional endowments, or managed portfolios to achieve quantitative performance benchmarks, whereas a stockbroker executes transactions directed by the individual investor.
  • Market Maker: Similar because both facilitate liquidity in securities markets; differs because a market maker is an exchange member firm obligated to continuously quote two-way buy and sell prices to provide liquidity to the market, whereas a stockbroker routes client orders to market makers or exchanges for execution.
