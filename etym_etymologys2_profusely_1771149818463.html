Root 1: fundere "to pour"
1. Proto-Indo-European *gheu- "to pour" (reconstructed root, extended as *ghu-n-d- / *gheu-d-; attested cognates: Greek kheo "I pour," Sanskrit juhoti "he pours an offering into the fire").
2. Latin fundo, fundere, fudi, fusus "to pour, pour out, shed" (attested; the nasal infix in fund- reflects the PIE nasal present *ghu-n-d-).
3. Latin profundere, profusi "to pour forth, pour out" — compound of pro- "forth, forward" + fundere (Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE).
4. Latin profusus "poured forth, lavish, extravagant" — past participle used as adjective (1st c. BCE; e.g., profusa largitas "lavish generosity").
5. English profuse "lavish, pouring forth freely" — borrowed from Latin profusus (mid-15th c.).
6. English profusely — adverb formed with the -ly suffix (16th c.).
7. PROFUSELY — present-day form.

Root 2: pro- "forth, forward"
1. Proto-Indo-European *pro "forward, forth, before" (reconstructed; attested cognates: Greek pro, Sanskrit pra, Gothic fra, English "from").
2. Latin pro "before, forward, forth" — preposition and prefix (attested).
3. pro- appears as the prefix in profundere "to pour forth" -> profusus -> English profuse -> PROFUSELY.

Debate note: the extension *-d- of the PIE "pour" root is standard but its exact morphological status is debated; the semantic connection of Latin fundere to the same family as English "fusion," "confuse," and "diffuse" is uncontroversial.
