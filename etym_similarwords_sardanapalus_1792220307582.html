• Sybarite: Similar because both denote an individual devoted to voluptuous luxury, sensory gratification, and effortless ease; differs because sybarite originates from the ancient Greek luxury colony of Sybaris and emphasizes delicate, effete pampering, whereas Sardanapalus represents supreme despotic power, imperial abdication, and apocalyptic self-destruction.
  • Hedonist: Similar because both prioritize sensory pleasure as the primary aim of life; differs because hedonist is a broad, neutral philosophical and psychological term, whereas Sardanapalus carries deep literary connotations of moral decay, courtly harems, and tragic ruin.
  • Voluptuary: Similar because both describe a person utterly surrendered to bodily appetites and luxury; differs because voluptuary focuses narrowly on physical, sensual pleasures (fine dining, eroticism), whereas Sardanapalus embodies the grand political and historical tragedy of a monarch destroying his realm through sloth.
  • Epicure: Similar because both pursue sensory delights and rich comforts; differs because an epicure (derived from Epicurus) cultivates refined, fastidious discrimination in food, wine, and aesthetics, whereas Sardanapalus represents vulgar, overwhelming, and unconstrained oriental indulgence.
  • Heliogabalus: Similar because both are classical sovereigns renowned in Western historiography for extreme luxury, cross-dressing, religious scandal, and effeminacy; differs because Heliogabalus was a historical teenage Roman emperor assassinated and dragged through the sewer by his Praetorians, whereas Sardanapalus is a semi-legendary Assyrian sovereign whose myth culminates in a sublime, voluntary funeral pyre.
  • Sensualist: Similar because both are driven by physical sensations rather than spiritual, intellectual, or moral pursuits; differs because sensualist is an abstract psychological descriptor, whereas Sardanapalus functions as a vivid historical and art-historical archetype of catastrophic luxury.
