• Scanty: Similar because both denote an insufficient amount; differs because scanty emphasizes smallness of physical extent, coverage, or area (e.g., scanty clothing, scanty records), whereas meager emphasizes a general lack of substance, fullness, or nourishment.
  • Sparse: Similar because both refer to thinness of quantity; differs because sparse refers specifically to low spatial density or items scattered thinly across a wide area (e.g., sparse vegetation, sparse crowd), while meager refers to absolute deficiency in volume or richness.
  • Paltry: Similar because both denote something small in amount; differs because paltry carries a strong emotional tone of contempt, insignificance, or worthlessness (e.g., a paltry sum, a paltry excuse), whereas meager describes the objective inadequacy or pitiful scarcity of the resource.
  • Exiguous: Similar because both describe extreme limitation in magnitude or quantity; differs because exiguous is a formal, highly literary Latinate term emphasizing precise dimensional or quantitative minuteness, whereas meager is more visceral and evocative of deprivation.
  • Inadequate: Similar because both describe supplies that fail to meet a required standard; differs because inadequate is a neutral, functional assessment of failure to satisfy a metric, lacking the vivid sensory connotation of thinness and starvation inherent in meager.
  • Lean: Similar because both trace back to physical absence of fat; differs because lean often carries positive connotations of athletic fitness, efficiency, or streamlined operations (e.g., a lean organization), whereas meager is almost invariably deficient and distressing.
  • Skimpy: Similar because both indicate undersized provisions; differs because skimpy is an informal register term emphasizing tightness, short measurement, or deliberate corner-cutting in fabric or serving sizes.
