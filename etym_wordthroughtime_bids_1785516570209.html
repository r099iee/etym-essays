- [Old English biddan] - 'to ask, entreat, pray, beseech' — the unmarked, humble word for asking; one bæd God for mercy and begged a favour of a friend.
- [Feudal upward drift (Middle Ages)] - when a lord 'bids', courtesy veils compulsion; the polite word for 'ask' hardens over centuries into the standard word for 'command'.
- [Auction & card-game senses] - by the modern era bid gains the commercial sense 'offer a price' (auction) and the card-game sense 'declare one's tricks'; these now dominate everyday use with the weak forms bid/bid/bid.
- [Archaic elevated register] - the command/invite sense survives as a refined, quasi-Biblical register — 'The Lord bids you' — rare, literary, dignified, and freighted with its ancient 'asking' courtesy.
