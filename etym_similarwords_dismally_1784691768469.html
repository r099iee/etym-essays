• Gloomily: Similar because both describe a depressing, sombre manner of acting or appearing; differs because gloomily suggests physical darkness and shadow, while dismally retains a flavour of ill omen and fated bad luck.
  • Drearily: Similar because both describe a cheerless manner; differs because drearily stresses monotony and boredom, while dismally colours the situation with doom and discouragement rather than tedium.
  • Bleakly: Similar because both describe a hopeless, unpromising outlook; differs because bleakly suggests a bare, exposed, windswept hopelessness, whereas dismally judges the thing as wretched and discouraging with an air of fate.
  • Miserably: Similar because both describe an unhappy, wretched manner; differs because miserably stresses the unhappiness or suffering of the person, while dismally describes the situation as disheartening and ill-omened.
  • Dejectedly: Similar because both describe low, discouraged spirits; differs because dejectedly names the psychological state of low spirits, while dismally is about the gloomy, discouraging quality of the thing described.
  • Sorrowfully: Similar because both describe a sad manner; differs because sorrowfully implies grief over a particular loss, while dismally is a general gloominess of scene or outcome without a named cause.
  • Morosely: Similar because both describe a gloomy, sullen manner; differs because morosely stresses a brooding, unsociable ill-humour of the person, whereas dismally describes the dispiriting quality of the situation.
  • Cheerlessly: Similar because both mean without cheer or comfort; differs because cheerlessly is a plain, neutral negation of cheer, while dismally adds the archaic undertone of calamity and ill omen.
  • Woefully: Similar because both intensify failure or misfortune ("woefully inadequate", "dismally poor"); differs because woefully stresses the sadness and insufficiency, while dismally stresses the gloom and the sense of fated doom.
  • Dreadfully / Awfully / Terribly: Similar because all three function as intensifiers of a bad or unfortunate condition; differs because these are broader, colloquial intensifiers (originally "inspiring dread / awe / terror") that have been bleached of literal meaning, while dismally keeps its specific colouring of gloom and ill omen.
