• omen: Similar because both words name a sign that foretells what will happen; differs because an omen is the bare sign itself, whether natural or accidental, while an augury is the formal, ritualistic interpretation of such a sign by a trained seer, and still carries the machinery of ancient Roman divination.
  • portent: Similar because both foretell the future; differs because a portent almost always carries heavy, threatening, catastrophic weight and signals momentous change, whereas an augury can be neutral, favourable, or unfavourable depending entirely on how the sign is read.
  • presage: Similar because both denote a presentiment or sign of things to come; differs because presage is a lighter, purely literary word for a hint or foreboding with no classical ritual machinery behind it, while augury presupposes an interpreter and a system of divination.
  • prophecy: Similar because both reveal the future; differs because a prophecy is a spoken or written declaration, often delivered directly by a deity's mouthpiece, whereas an augury is an interpreted sign read through ritual observation rather than a direct revelation.
  • prognostication: Similar because both name a forecast of what will happen; differs because prognostication is a clinical, technical term for a reasoned forecast (as in medicine or weather), stripped of all sacred or superstitious resonance, while augury keeps the archaic, numinous colour.
  • auspice: Similar because both come from Roman bird-divination and mean a favourable sign; differs because an auspice is specifically the observation of birds (avis "bird" + specere "to look") and survives mainly in the frozen phrase "under the auspices of," while augury is a fully general word for an interpreted omen.
  • foretoken: Similar because both denote a sign that points ahead to a future event; differs because foretoken is a plain, purely descriptive word for an advance indication with no divinatory or religious frame, unlike the ritually charged augury.
  • premonition: Similar because both involve an advance sense of what is coming; differs because a premonition is a private, psychological feeling of dread in the mind of one person, whereas an augury is an external, observable sign that is publicly interpreted.
  • presentiment: Similar because both indicate a feeling that something is about to happen; differs because a presentiment is an inner, intuitive intimation, while an augury is grounded in observed external phenomena and a tradition of reading them.
  • harbinger: Similar because both name something that announces what is to come; differs because a harbinger is a person or thing that literally precedes the event (originally an advance scout), while an augury is a sign that represents the future symbolically and can occur well before the event.
  • forecast: Similar because both predict the future; differs because a forecast is a rational, data-based projection (weather, economics) made by professionals, entirely without the supernatural or priestly frame that defines augury.
  • sign: Similar because both name something that conveys meaning about what is to come; differs because sign is the colourless generic term usable for anything from a road marker to a symptom, whereas augury is restricted to meaning-laden portents with a solemn, archaic register.
  • vaticination: Similar because both denote prophecy and prediction; differs because vaticination emphasizes the act of uttering inspired prophecy (from Latin vates "seer, poet") and is a rarer, more learned word, while augury centres on interpreting observed signs rather than speaking inspired truths.
