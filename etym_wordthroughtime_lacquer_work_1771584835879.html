- [Late 17th century: East Asian import] - 'Lacquer' enters English from the Portuguese trade in Indian and East Asian resin; 'lacquer-work' names objects coated in the glossy varnish, and connotation is exotic, precious, and connoisseurial - the luxury of Chinese, Japanese, and Indian cabinets.
- [18th century: European imitation] - European workshops imitate the craft as 'japanning'; 'lacquer-work' still connotes genuine Oriental provenance and slow, layered craftsmanship, prized in cabinets of curiosities.
- [Modern usage] - 'Lacquer-work' denotes objects finished in lacquer, decorative or artistic; connotation is traditional luxury and patient hand-craft, though the term now also reaches modern furniture and craft, losing some of its old exotic mystique.
