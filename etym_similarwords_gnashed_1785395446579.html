• Grind: Similar because both name the rubbing or crushing of one surface against another; differs because grind is the broad, mechanical term for crushing anything — grain is ground, blades are ground — and carries no emotion, while gnash is a convulsive, quasi-animal reflex of rage or anguish, always grinding the teeth and nothing else.
  • Grit (one's teeth): Similar because both bring the teeth together hard under stress; differs because to grit one's teeth is a deliberate, controlled clamping against pain — endurance rather than spasm, the body bracing — whereas gnashing is a violent, noisy, uncontrollable paroxysm of grinding.
  • Clench: Similar because both involve a tight jaw-locking; differs because clenching is silent and controlled — a held, quiet tension — while gnashing grinds the teeth edge to edge audibly, and sound is essential to it.
  • Snarl: Similar because both belong to the animal fury of the mouth; differs because a snarl is the curled lip and bared fang of an overt threat aimed outward, whereas gnashing grinds nothing but the teeth themselves — fury with nowhere to go, not a display to frighten anyone.
  • Chatter (of teeth): Similar because both are uncontrollable, reflexive tooth-sounds; differs because chattering is the rapid, light clacking of cold or fear, fast and shallow, while gnashing is a slow, violent, grinding crush of enamel on enamel.
  • Bite: Similar because both involve the teeth in aggression; differs because to bite always presumes something between the teeth — flesh, food, prey — while gnash operates on empty teeth, grinding nothing but itself, so that gnashing is aggression that cannot reach a target.
  • Chomp / crunch: Similar because both are audible teeth-actions; differs because chomping and crunching involve something being chewed or broken between the teeth, whereas gnashing is the self-contained grinding of empty jaws, and carries none of the satisfaction of eating.
  • Gnaw: Similar because both begin with the same guttural gn- sound of grinding; differs because gnawing works slowly on a surface, shredding it over time, while gnashing is a sudden, violent, convulsive grinding of the teeth together with no surface involved at all.
