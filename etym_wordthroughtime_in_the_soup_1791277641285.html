- 19th c. in the soup (American slang) - In trouble, vivid kitchen hyperbole, perceived as racy low slang.
- Early 1900s transatlantic spread - Adopted in British comic writing, lightened to mean social scrape rather than serious ruin.
- 1920s–1930s Wodehouse era in-the-soup friend - Hyphenated attributive use, affectionate comic marker of Bertie Wooster world, signals chummy willingness to scheme.
- Mid-20th c. idiom stock - Listed in slang dictionaries alongside in hot water, seen as slightly dated but still playful.
- Today - Nostalgic, self-consciously vintage way to say in trouble, evokes period comedy and avoids harsh judgment.
