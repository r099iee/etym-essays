• Outraged: Similar because both describe fierce emotional anger sparked by a violation of fairness, decency, or morality; differs because outraged conveys a more explosive, violent, and devastating shock at an egregious atrocity or scandal, whereas indignant implies an ethically structured, dignified, and articulate resentment.
  • Resentful: Similar because both involve lasting displeasure arising from mistreatment or unfairness; differs because resentful often carries a bitter, brooding, envious, or suppressed personal grievance over being overlooked, whereas indignant is open, proud, morally righteous, and immediately vocalized.
  • Irate: Similar because both denote a high degree of anger and displeasure; differs because irate focuses purely on the physiological intensity of raw, furious wrath regardless of whether the cause is morally just, whereas indignant is inherently anchored in a perceived breach of justice and dignity.
  • Offended: Similar because both stem from wounded feelings or perceived slights; differs because offended is a milder, broader, and more subjective state that can arise from mere bad manners, personal hypersensitivity, or social awkwardness, whereas indignant requires an objective sense of injustice or disgraceful behavior.
  • Incensed: Similar because both describe individuals heatedly provoked by unacceptable actions; differs because incensed (literally "set on fire") emphasizes the explosive, burning heat of sudden fury, whereas indignant preserves an intellectual, principled assessment of moral unworthiness.
  • Piqued: Similar because both involve displeasure triggered by a social friction; differs because piqued describes a minor, petty sting of injured pride, wounded vanity, or mild annoyance (often accompanied by sulking), whereas indignant represents a much deeper, principled moral rebellion.
  • Righteous: Similar because both claim moral justification for their emotional stance; differs because righteous is an overarching moral or spiritual attribute signifying purity, virtue, or compliance with divine law, whereas indignant specifically names the dynamic emotional anger aroused when that righteousness is violated.
