Linear derivation chain for GLINT: 1. Old Norse *glinta* "to gleam, to sparkle, to shine dimly" (9th–13th c. Scandinavia); related verb glentr in Old Icelandic usage, built on a North Germanic base *glent-/*glint- expressing a quick, glancing light. 2. Middle English glinten (variant glenten) "to flash, to gleam; to cast a quick sidelong glance" — Norse loanword recorded c. 1300 in the Cursor Mundi (as glentes/glenten). 3. Modern English GLINT — noun "a brief flash of reflected light" attested from the 16th c.; the modern verb "to glint" is a back-formation from the noun (or continuant of the ME verb) used from the 17th c. onward. Final node: GLINT. DEBATED / NUANCED STAGE: the ultimate Germanic ancestor is not securely reconstructed. The OED groups glint with a Scandinavian base of uncertain origin, possibly related to Middle High German glinsen "to gleam" and to Old Norse gljá "to glitter"; a PIE connection within the *ghel-/*ghol- "to shine" family (the stock of gold, glass, gleam, glow) has been proposed but cannot be demonstrated from the attested record, so the chain is deliberately left starting at Old Norse rather than at a reconstructed PIE etymon. The dialectal vowel alternation glinten/glenten points to a Germanic ablaut pair (*glint-/*glent-), a common pattern in West Germanic light-verbs, but the precise derivational morphology is unknown and no intermediate Germanic form is attested in writing.
