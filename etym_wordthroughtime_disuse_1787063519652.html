- 14th c. - verb disusen "to cease to use" (from Old French desuser); practical and active.
- 16th c. - noun disuse emerges as a neutral label for the state of no longer being used.
- 17th-18th c. - enters essayistic and legal prose; applied to customs, rights, and words that lapse; the formal tone grows.
- 19th c. - gains the fixed medical collocation "disuse atrophy"; develops scientific precision.
- 20th-21st c. - most alive in the idiom "fall into disuse", applied to skills, languages, roads, and infrastructure; faintly elegiac or cautionary.
