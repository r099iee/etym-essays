• kin: Similar in denoting family relations; differs because kin is the bare, terse noun for family members (as in "next of kin" and "kith and kin"), while kindred is the collective, warmer noun for the whole circle of relatives.
  • kinsfolk / kinfolk: Similar in referring to relatives; differs because kinfolk is more colloquial and regional, while kindred carries formal, literary weight.
  • kinsmen: Similar in naming relatives; differs because kinsmen is archaic and pointedly masculine, while kindred is gender-neutral and collective.
  • relatives: Similar in denoting family members; differs because relatives is the neutral, modern, legal-sounding word for kin by blood or marriage, without kindred's warmth or tribal gravity.
  • relations: Similar in denoting family connections; differs because relations is colder and more formal, the word of lawyers and etiquette, while kindred is evocative and organic.
  • family: Similar in denoting kin; differs because family in modern use means the domestic unit — parents and children — far narrower than kindred, though "family" can stretch to cover a clan.
  • folk: Similar in denoting a people bound together; differs because folk is broader and less specifically kin-based, meaning a people, community, or nation, while kindred is bound by blood and ancestry.
  • clan: Similar in denoting a group of kin; differs because a clan is a structured, named descent group with a common ancestor (from Gaelic clann "offspring"), while kindred is the looser, warmer circle of one's kin.
  • tribe: Similar in denoting a people of common descent; differs because a tribe is a political and social unit with its own leadership, while kindred is the personal network of relatives.
  • house: Similar in denoting a lineage or family line; differs because house implies a dynasty or noble line ("the house of Atreus"), while kindred is egalitarian and personal.
  • lineage: Similar in denoting descent; differs because lineage is the abstract line of descent itself, while kindred is the living body of people who share it.
  • cognates: Similar in sharing a common origin; differs because cognates is genealogical and linguistic jargon for things related by common descent, while kindred applies warmly to human relations.
  • consanguinity / consanguineous: Similar in denoting blood relationship; differs because these are formal legal or medical terms for shared blood, while kindred is the evocative, literary word for one's people.
  • affines: Similar in denoting relations; differs because affines names relatives by marriage (from Latin affinis "adjacent, related by marriage"), while kindred emphasises bloodline and shared ancestry.
  • in-laws: Similar in denoting relatives; differs because in-laws is the modern colloquial term for relatives by marriage, while kindred points to the ancestral bloodline.
  • kith: Similar in being paired with kin ("kith and kin"); differs because kith means known acquaintances and friends (from the PIE root for "to know"), while kin and kindred are bound by blood.
