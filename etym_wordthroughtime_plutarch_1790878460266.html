- Classical Antiquity (c. 1st–2nd c. CE) - Revered as Lucius Mestrius Plutarchus, leading Platonist philosopher and priest of Apollo at Delphi.
- Byzantine Era (9th–12th c.) - Canonized as an encyclopedic moralist, educator, and master of Atticizing Koine Greek prose.
- Renaissance (14th–16th c.) - Jacques Amyot and Thomas North translations establish Plutarch as the foundational wellspring for European drama, Shakespeare, and civic humanism.
- Enlightenment & Revolutionary Era (18th c.) - Celebrated by Rousseau, Hamilton, and the French Revolutionaries as the paramount teacher of republican virtue and civic courage.
- 19th–20th Century - Re-evaluated by modern academia as a primary source for ancient historical anecdotes and comparative ethical psychology.
- Modern / Contemporary - Regarded as the seminal architect of comparative biography and historical portraiture.
