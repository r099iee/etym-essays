- [16th century the scribe's farewell] - [Enters from Greek meaning a finishing stroke — the scribe's closing inscription at the end of a manuscript recording title, author, date, and sometimes a prayer of thanks; connotation of the crowning seal on finished work]
- [17th-18th century the printer's note] - [In early printed books the colophon is the printer's closing note, then shifts to the printer's device or emblem identifying the press; connotation of an identifying mark]
- [19th-20th century the publisher's badge] - [Modernized into the publisher's emblem on the title page or spine — now a registered trademark; connotation of brand identity protected by law]
- [20th-21st century bookish survival] - [A technical bibliographic term for the publisher's device; also borrowed into computing for the console boot message — but it keeps its ancient bookish flavour, the descendant of the scribe's signed farewell]
