• cords: similar because both are slender flexible ties used for binding; differs because a cord is any thick string with no specialized fastening hardware, while a lace is specifically drawn through eyelets or hooks to close an opening.
  • strings: similar because both are thin lengths of material used for tying; differs in register and function — strings are generic (apron strings, guitar strings), lacking the fashion and footwear associations of laces.
  • ties: similar because ties also fasten by knotting (necktie, cable tie); differs because a tie is usually a single flat band, whereas laces are narrow cords paired in eyelets, and ‘ties’ lacks the textile-ornament senses of lace.
  • ribbons: similar because both are narrow decorative strips used to tie or adorn; differs because a ribbon is a flat woven band that is tied in bows, while lace is a cord or an openwork web — ribbons are machine-made, lace is structurally an open lattice.
  • braid: similar because both are ornamental trimmings of interlaced strands; differs because braid is flat, dense, and uniform from plaiting, while lace (in the trim sense) is open or metal-threaded and in the fabric sense is a self-standing openwork textile.
  • trimmings: similar because both denote decorative additions sewn onto garments; differs because ‘trimmings’ is the generic umbrella term (fur, buttons, piping), whereas laces are one specific class of trim with its own craft and history.
  • embroidery: similar because both are esteemed needlework decorations on cloth; differs because embroidery is stitching thread onto a ground fabric to form pictures or patterns, while lace is created from threads alone and needs no ground cloth.
  • filigree: similar because both describe delicate, ornamental web-like work; differs because filigree is metalwork (gold or silver wire soldered into tracery), while laces are textile — the shared root-image is the intricate open pattern.
  • frills: similar because in the phrase ‘laces and frills’ both denote superfluous fabric ornament; differs because a frill is a gathered or pleated edging attached to a garment, while laces are cord-, braid-, or openwork-based; frills also carry the ‘needless extra’ idiom more strongly.
  • ruffles: similar because both are soft decorative edgings on clothing; differs because a ruffle is a gathered strip of the same fabric flaring out, while lace is usually a distinct material; ruffles suggest movement, lace suggests delicacy and expense.
  • furbelows: similar because the archaic pair ‘frills and furbelows’ describes ornate trimming as laces and frills do; differs in register — furbelow is now humorous or archaic and refers to flounces and pleated trims, not openwork.
  • finery: similar because both name showy, ornamental dress; differs because finery is the whole ensemble of elegant clothes and jewels, while laces are single ornaments within it.
  • embellishments / ornaments: similar because both cover any decorative addition in the figurative register; differs because these are abstract and medium-neutral, whereas laces always evokes the specific textile craft.
  • garnish: similar because both are trimmings added to make something more attractive; differs because garnish is applied to food and plates and is transient, while laces are durable ornamental textiles.
