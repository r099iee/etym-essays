Note on semantic transition: The historical journey of yield is a celebrated example of semantic divergence. Proto-Indo-European *gʰeldʰ- was purely financial and legal, denoting the compulsory payment of fines, compensation (such as Old English wergild), or agricultural tribute. In medieval Anglo-Norman warfare, "yielding" shifted from paying money to "surrendering one's person" to a captor upon defeat. By the late 16th century, the physical mechanical metaphor had developed: an object that yielded was one that bent, buckled, or collapsed under load. Prefixed with the ancient privative un-, unyielding came to denote both a physical substance that refuses to buckle or deform (like granite) and a moral will that refuses to surrender to coercion.
