• rippled: similar because both describe gentle water motion; differs because ripple is the surface pattern of small waves, while lap is the sound-and-motion of water meeting a solid object.
  • splashed: similar water striking something; differs because splash is louder, single, and careless (splash mud, splash in a pool), while lap is soft, repeated, and rhythmic.
  • washed: similar water against a shore; differs because wash is a broad covering motion, while lap is small repeated licks.
  • swashed: similar water moving with sound; differs because swash is broader and noisier (swashbuckling), while lap is gentle and quiet.
  • murmured: similar soft, soothing sound; differs because murmur is a voice-sound metaphor, while lap is the actual water sound.
  • gurgled: similar liquid sound; differs because gurgle is internal bubbling (a stream over stones), while lap is surface contact with a solid.
  • babbled: similar gentle water sound; differs because babble is chatty and broken (a babbling brook), while lap is a regular repeated lick.
  • slurped: similar the drinking sense; differs because slurp is deliberately noisy human drinking, while lap is animal tongue-scooping (or the water metaphor).
  • licked: similar the tongue motion; differs because lick is a single broad tongue-stroke, while lap is repeated scooping with a cupped tongue.
  • drank: similar consuming liquid; differs because drink is general human intake, while lap is animal-specific or greedy figurative intake.
  • sucked: similar the figurative "absorbed" sense (lapping up); differs because suck implies effortful pulling in, while lap up implies eager, greedy acceptance.
