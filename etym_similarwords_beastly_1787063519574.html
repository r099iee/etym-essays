• Brutal: Similar because both describe savage, uncivilized, or harsh qualities; differs because brutal emphasizes devastating physical force, cruelty, or harsh reality, whereas beastly is often milder, colloquial, or focused on unpleasantness and repulsive messiness.
  • Bestial: Similar because both derive from the same root meaning animal-like; differs because bestial is a formal, grave, often psychological or legal term reserved for depraved, savage, or subhuman sexual or violent acts, whereas beastly has softened into a British colloquial term for disagreeable things.
  • Abominable: Similar because both denote loathsome or highly unpleasant things; differs because abominable carries a much stronger sense of moral abhorrence, religious detestation, or extreme revulsion.
  • Nasty: Similar because both describe foul, unpleasant, or unkind things; differs because nasty is a common, universal term, whereas beastly has distinct British upper-class and literary overtones.
  • Loathsome: Similar because both cause intense disgust; differs because loathsome denotes deep visceral hatred or sickening repulsion, without the playful or exasperated tone that beastly can accommodate.
  • Foul: Similar because both describe dirty, offensive, or contaminated substances; differs because foul specifically emphasizes rotten odors, pollution, or sporting rule violations.
