Note: Latin interpolāre is formed from the prefix inter- (‘between, among’) and polīre (‘to polish, smooth, furbish’). The original craft metaphor was textile refurbishment: a fuller (*fullo*) would ‘interpolate’ old garments by cleaning them and inserting fresh pieces of wool to hide wear. Roman jurists seized upon this craft image to describe the criminal insertion of clauses into legal documents (*interpolāre testamentum*). The agent noun suffix -or designates the perpetrator.
