• Alexis de Tocqueville (in full): Similar because it is the same referent expanded; differs because full style is formal-bibliographic, bare Tocqueville is critical shorthand.
  • Montesquieu: Similar because he is the predecessor as French liberal theorist of regimes, mores and separation of powers; differs because he writes of monarchy/republic/despostism structurally, Tocqueville of democratic social condition existentially.
  • Edmund Burke: Similar because he is the fellow aristocratic anatomist of revolution and continuity; differs because he defends prescription and prejudice, Tocqueville analyses equality as fate.
  • John Stuart Mill: Similar because he is the contemporary liberal anguished by majority tyranny; differs because he is utilitarian-reformist and programmatic, Tocqueville diagnostic and mournful.
  • Max Weber: Similar because he is the successor as sociologist of rationalisation, bureaucracy and disenchantment; differs because he is systematic-German and value-neutral, Tocqueville essayistic and openly normative.
  • Gustave de Beaumont: Similar because he is the travelling companion and fellow observer of America and Ireland; differs because he remains minor, remembered through Tocqueville.
  • Democracy in America (the work): Similar because title and author are metonymic; differs because one is book, the other man — citing the book claims text, citing the man claims witness.
  • Jacksonian America (the milieu): Similar because it is what Tocqueville observed; differs because it is the object, not the observer.
