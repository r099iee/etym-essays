• Pizarro: Similar because both are Spanish conquistadors who destroyed great American empires and are paired in Macaulay's sentence; differs because Pizarro is the emblem of plain rapacity, while Cortes represents conquest with strategy, alliances, and self-justification.
  • Conquistador: Similar because Cortes is the archetypal conquistador and can stand for the whole class; differs because conquistador is the generic Spanish class noun for hundreds of soldiers, while Cortes names the specific conqueror of Mexico.
  • Conqueror: Similar in meaning one who subdues a people or realm by force; differs because conqueror is the broad, neutral English word for all times and places, whereas Cortes carries the specific historical and moral weight of the Aztec conquest.
  • Colonizer: Similar because Cortes extended European dominion over Mexico; differs because colonizer emphasizes settlement and lasting rule, while Cortes stands for seizure and overthrow of an existing empire.
  • Adventurer: Similar because 16th-century sources call Cortes an adventurer, a licensed captain under royal contract; differs because modern adventurer suggests romantic risk-taking without the predatory, imperial meaning.
  • Explorer: Similar as a figure of the age of discovery; differs because explorer implies the advance of knowledge and science, whereas Cortes's expedition was conquest for dominion and gold.
  • Caudillo: Similar in denoting a Spanish-speaking military strongman who seizes power by force; differs because caudillo is a modern Latin American political term, not a 16th-century royal captain.
  • Emperor: Similar in standing at the head of a vast dominion; differs because emperor names a ruler, while Cortes, though he became governor and Marqués del Valle, ruled as a subject of Charles V.
