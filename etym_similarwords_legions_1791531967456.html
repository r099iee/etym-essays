• Army: The whole land force of a state; similar as a large organised body, but it is the overarching formation, whereas a legion is one component of it.
  • Host: An archaic or literary word for a great army; it is grander and less technical, often implying a multitude rather than a structure.
  • Horde: A large, loosely organised crowd or force; it is pejorative and suggests disorder, the opposite of a legion’s discipline.
  • Multitude: A very large number of people or things; it is the neutral figurative equivalent, lacking the military image.
  • Throng: A dense crowd; it is physical and civilian, with no suggestion of command or formation.
  • Cohort: A subdivision of a Roman legion (about 480 men); it is smaller and technical, though it shares the Roman pedigree.
  • Century: A unit of about eighty to a hundred Roman soldiers; it is far smaller and is commanded by a centurion.
  • Maniple: The earlier Roman tactical unit of 120 men; it is smaller and historically prior to the cohort.
  • Phalanx: A dense Greek infantry formation; it is similar in mass and discipline but belongs to a different military tradition.
  • Brigade: A modern formation of several battalions; it is comparable in size but is defined by modern staff organisation, not by Roman precedent.
  • Regiment: A permanent infantry or cavalry unit; it is similar in identity and tradition, but smaller and nationally specific.
  • Battalion: A unit of several companies; it is smaller than a legion and lacks the word’s figurative reach.
  • Division: A large modern formation of several brigades; it is larger and strictly organisational.
  • Corps: A formation of two or more divisions; it is larger and more administrative.
  • Troop: A small unit of cavalry or soldiers; it is much smaller and less imposing.
  • Garrison: The troops stationed in a place; it is defined by location rather than by size or type.
  • Militia: Citizen soldiers called out in emergency; it is similar as a levy but connotes amateurism and locality.
  • Myriad: Ten thousand, or countless; it is purely numerical and has no military colour.
  • Swarm: A dense mass of insects or people; it is vivid and pejorative, implying chaotic movement.
  • Drove: A herd or crowd driven along; it is contemptuous when applied to people.
