- 1794 - Lettish is first recorded in English as a learned, German-influenced label for the Baltic language of the Letts; neutral and ethnographic.
- Nineteenth century - The word becomes standard in comparative philology and travel writing, paired with Lithuanian and Old Prussian; connotation scholarly, slightly exotic.
- 1918-1940 - The new Latvian state promotes Latvian and Latvia; Lettish begins to sound old-fashioned and Baltic German.
- Cold War - Emigre scholars and library catalogues keep Lettish alive; general usage moves decisively to Latvian.
- Twenty-first century - Rare and archaic; used mainly in historical quotations, bibliographies, and explanations of the older name, not as a neutral everyday term.
