The origin of the English word is uncertain: no securely reconstructed Proto-Indo-European (or even Proto-Germanic) etymon is attested for it, and the deeper continental stage below is stated as probable rather than certain. The chain therefore records the documented history, with its gaps named. Chain for the single root word SLOVENLY: - (Possible stage, uncertain) a West Germanic base *slōf- "careless, negligent, slack", and a speculative connection to the Germanic verb *slūpan- "to slip, glide" (whence Dutch sloffen "to shuffle, go slackly" and English slipper) — plausible but not securely established; no certain PIE root is reconstructible. State: the pre-Middle-English stage is a reconstruction, not an attestation. - Middle Low German slōf (adjective) "careless, negligent" (also noun "a sloven, an idle person") and Middle Dutch slof (adjective) "careless, negligent; slack, loose-fitting" (also "a loose slipper"), 14th–15th centuries — the probable continental source of the English noun. - Middle English sloven (noun) "a base, careless, filthy, or untidy person; a rascal", first recorded c. 1430–40; no Old English predecessor is attested, and the word's earliest recorded history is late and is preceded by a gap. - English slovenly (adjective, also adverb) "untidy, dirty, careless; slipshod", formed from sloven + the adjective suffix -ly, first recorded c. 1515 (16th century). - SLOVENLY Note on the debate: because sloven and its continental kin have no secure etymology beyond Middle Low German / Middle Dutch, older attempts to force the word onto a Proto-Indo-European root of "slipping" (e.g. *sleup-) are speculative. The card's earlier claim of a PIE *sleup- "to slide, slip" behind slovenly should be treated as unproven; the safest statement is that the word is of West Germanic origin with an uncertain deeper history.
