Stage 1 — PIE *op- "to work, be busy, produce in abundance; hence to have resources and power" (Pokorny *op-; Sanskrit ápas "sacred work", ápnas "property, possessions"). Stage 2 — PIE *dʰeh₁- "to put, place, make, do" — the second element of the compound, ancestor of Latin facere "to do, make". The Latin word is a compound of two PIE roots. Stage 3 — Proto-Italic compound *opi-faciom "the doing of work, service" (*opi- "work, help" + *fakiō "make, do"). Stage 4 — Latin officium "service, duty, kindness, ceremonial function, official post" — the assimilation *opi-f- > *ofif- > officium (attested from the 2nd century BC: Plautus; classical). Stage 5 — Old French ofice, office "duty, service; also the room where duties are done" (12th century). Stage 6 — Anglo-French → Middle English office "a service, a duty, a function" (13th century). Stage 7 — English office "a duty, service, ceremonial function; an official post; the room where business is done". FINAL NODE: OFFICE. NOTE: Church Latin uses officium for the liturgical services — the Divine Office, the Office for the Dead — the sense revived by this card's sentence. Latin compounds on -ficium form a family: beneficium → benefit, sacrificium → sacrifice, artificium → artifice, maleficium → malefice.
