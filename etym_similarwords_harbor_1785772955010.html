• Shelter: Similar because it is the closest physical synonym for giving protective cover; differs because shelter foregrounds defense against weather or danger, while harbor more readily includes secret lodging and the full psychological "hold inside" senses (grudges, doubts).
  • House: Similar because both mean to provide accommodation and keep something or someone; differs because house is purely residential or containment-oriented and lacks the refuge-from-danger and the mental-emotion senses of harbor.
  • Lodge: Similar because both involve temporary accommodation; differs because lodging is residential and temporary-stay in character, and one rarely "lodges a grudge" the way one harbors resentment.
  • Conceal: Similar because both overlap when harboring involves hiding someone or something; differs because conceal is only about keeping out of sight, whereas harbor adds lodging, maintenance, and sanctuary — keeping something alive or present, not merely unseen.
  • Hide: Similar because both can mean keeping someone or something from discovery; differs because hide is general and often physical or playful, while harbor implies sustained protective keeping within an interior and carries refuge or custodial weight.
  • Nurture: Similar because both can mean sustaining something within a protective relationship; differs because nurture implies fostering growth and is usually positive, whereas harbor is compatible with grudges, pathogens, and fugitives without implying wholesome care.
  • Foster: Similar because both can mean giving shelter or encouragement to something over time; differs because fostering is positive care aimed at development, while harboring is frequently neutral or suspicious in legal and emotional contexts.
  • Entertain (an idea): Similar because both can mean holding a thought in mind over time; differs because "entertain an idea" is lighter, more deliberative, and temporary, whereas harboring a suspicion or resentment implies deeper, longer retention.
  • Host: Similar because both mean receiving and keeping something (guests, organisms, pathogens); differs because hosting is usually public and welcoming, while harboring often implies secrecy, refuge, or culpability.
  • Accommodate: Similar because both can mean providing space for someone or something; differs because accommodate is neutral and practical, lacking the refuge, secrecy, and emotional-retention connotations of harbor.
  • Take in / Receive: Similar because both can mean accepting someone into one's care or space; differs because these are broad, neutral phrasal verbs without the sustained protective keeping or the accusatory legal coloring that harbor can carry.
  • Protect: Similar because both involve shielding something from harm; differs because protect is a general verb of defense that does not imply receiving the thing into an interior and keeping it there, as harbor does.
