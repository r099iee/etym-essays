• Beak: Similar because both refer to the projecting, horny jaws of a bird; differs because beak (from Celtic via Old French bec) typically connotes a hooked, sharp, predatory raptor’s anatomy, whereas bill is the broader native Germanic term often used for flat, slender, or granivorous birds (such as ducks, geese, and finches), as well as carrying vast polysemic ranges in commerce and law.
  • Rostrum: Similar because both denote an avian or biological beak-like snout; differs because rostrum is a formal anatomical and zoological term, also referring in architecture and rhetoric to a speaker's platform (derived from the bronze beaks of captured Roman warships).
  • Mandible: Similar because both refer to the biting jaw apparatus of an animal; differs because mandible is a precise osteological and entomological term designating the lower jawbone in vertebrates or the paired mouthparts of arthropods.
  • Invoice: Similar because both denote an itemized commercial statement of charges for goods or services rendered; differs because invoice belongs strictly to commercial accounting and shipping logistics, lacking the anatomical, legislative, and currency senses of bill.
  • Statute: Similar because both represent legislative enactments; differs because a bill is merely a draft or proposed law under consideration, whereas a statute is an officially enacted, binding law passed by the legislature and signed by the executive.
  • Banknote: Similar because both refer to paper currency; differs because banknote is the standard formal British term, whereas bill (e.g., a ten-dollar bill) is standard American and colloquial usage.
  • Placard: Similar because both denote a printed public notice or poster; differs because a placard is specifically carried or posted for demonstration or advertising, whereas bill in this sense (as in "playbill" or "billboard") relates to theatrical programs and public declarations.
