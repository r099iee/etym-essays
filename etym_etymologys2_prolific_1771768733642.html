PROLIFIC is a composite built from THREE PIE roots; each chain is given in isolation before the final joining.

CHAIN 1 — root *al- (*h₂el-) “to grow, to nourish”:
1. Proto-Indo-European *al- (reconstructed *h₂el-) “to grow, to nourish” (reconstructed).
2. → Latin alere “to nourish, feed, rear” (classical Latin).
3. → Latin olēscere “to grow” (inchoative in *-sk-).
4. → Latin prōlēs “offspring, progeny” (prō “forward” + olēs, “that which grows forth”).
5. → Medieval Latin prōlificus “fruitful, making offspring” (prōlēs + -ficus “making”; attested medieval Latin).
6. → English prolific, 17th c. — OUR CARD: PROLIFIC.

CHAIN 2 — root *per- “forward, before, through”:
1. Proto-Indo-European *per- “forward, before, through” (reconstructed).
2. → Latin prō “before, forward, on behalf of” — the prefix inside prōlēs (“that which grows forth”).

CHAIN 3 — root *dʰeh₁- “to put, place, set; to make, do”:
1. Proto-Indo-European *dʰeh₁- “to put, place, make” (reconstructed; the root of Greek títhēmi “I put,” Sanskrit dadhāti “he places”).
2. → Latin facere “to do, make” (from a velar-extended grade of *dʰeh₁-; the precise extension is debated but the connection is standard).
3. → Latin -ficus “making, producing” (suffixal form used in compounds such as prōlificus, beneficus).

JOINING: prōlēs (Chain 1, step 4, with the prefix from Chain 2) + -ficus (Chain 3, step 3) → Medieval Latin prōlificus → English prolific (17th c.).

Debate / nuance: the derivation of Latin facere from *dʰeh₁- involves a velar extension whose exact shape is debated in the handbooks (de Vaan, Beekes); the meaning connection “make, do” is secure. The compound prōlificus itself is a medieval formation, first recorded in ecclesiastical and legal Latin, and English borrowed it ready-made in the seventeenth century.
