Notes: (1) The move from "a ruler's court" to "romantic wooing" is the semantic bridge of the whole word: to court a sovereign was to seek favour; to court a beloved was to seek her favour in the same ritual language (cf. French faire la cour, "to pay court"). (2) "Cohort" in English (a military unit; a group) is a direct loan of Latin cohors, alongside "court", which arrived through French. (3) The PIE *skep- behind "-ship" is traditional, but its wider Indo-European relations are uncertain; the Germanic side of the suffix is fully attested.
