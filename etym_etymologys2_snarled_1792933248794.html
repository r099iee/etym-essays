Middle Dutch (14th c., cognate)
snarren
‘to growl, rattle, buzz’
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
snar
‘to snap, growl like a dog’
↓
Early Modern English (1590s)
snarl
‘to growl with bared teeth’
↓
Modern English (current, past tense)
snarled
‘growled viciously; tangled’

Note: the word is imitative in origin, so the chain traces first attestations rather than sound laws. A twin noun-verb snarl meaning ‘tangle, knot’ (from snar, ‘noose, snare’, akin to snare) merged in form with the growl-word by the 1600s; scholars dispute whether the two influenced each other or merely collided.
