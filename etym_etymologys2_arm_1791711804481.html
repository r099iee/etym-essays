Note: Modern English features two distinct etymological words merged into the single form 'arm'. The military verb ('to arm') and collective noun ('firearms') trace via Anglo-Norman from Latin 'arma' (implements of war). The anatomical noun ('arm', the upper limb) descends from Proto-Germanic *armaz via Old English 'earm'. Both independent branches ultimately stem from the identical Proto-Indo-European verbal root *h₂er- ('to fit together, join'), the anatomical branch reflecting the socket joint where the limb fits into the torso, and the martial branch reflecting joined tools and interlocking protective armor.
