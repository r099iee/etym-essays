Echoic / Onomatopoeic Base (c. 17th c. CE)
*pl- + -op
"imitative of soft flat impact in liquid"
↓
Early Modern English Dialect (c. 18th c. CE)
plop / ploppen
"to drop with a dull, flat sound"
↓
19th Century English (c. 1820s CE)
plop
"to fall suddenly with a muffled sound"
↓
Modern English (19th c. CE–present)
plop
"a dull dropping sound; to drop heavily"

Note: Plop is an echoic (onomatopoeic) formation mimicking the acoustic sound of an object entering water or landing on a soft surface, closely parallel to Dutch ploppen and German plumpsen.
