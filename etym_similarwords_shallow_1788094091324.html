• Superficial: Similar because both condemn a lack of depth in people and ideas. Differs because superficial is Latinate, more clinical and intellectual, describing what lies only at the surface; shallow keeps the physical water image and the moral note.
  • Skin-deep: Similar because both describe penetration no deeper than the surface (beauty, loyalty). Differs because skin-deep is an idiom confined to penetration metaphors; shallow also does full physical duty for water, dishes, and roots.
  • Surface-level: Similar because both describe analysis that never goes beneath appearances. Differs because surface-level is a modern analytical cliché; shallow is older, broader, and moral in tone.
  • Slight: Similar because both mean small in amount or degree. Differs because slight is about quantity and is not moral (a slight difference); shallow measures depth and judges substance.
  • Trivial: Similar because both dismiss things of little worth. Differs because trivial means unimportant, of trifles, whereas shallow things may look serious but lack depth.
  • Frivolous: Similar because both describe a lack of seriousness. Differs because frivolous attacks playful lightness; shallow attacks emptiness that pretends to be substance.
  • Flimsy: Similar because both describe what cannot bear weight (a flimsy argument). Differs because flimsy is about frailness of construction; shallow is about thinness of content.
  • Vacuous: Similar because both mean empty of thought. Differs because vacuous is extreme and clinical (a void), while shallow allows thin content and also works on water and soil.
  • Empty-headed: Similar because both mock mental emptiness. Differs because empty-headed is colloquial and total; shallow can be partial and is far wider in application.
  • Hollow: Similar because both suggest emptiness behind a surface. Differs because hollow implies an inner void (a hollow promise); shallow implies a thin layer with nothing beneath.
  • Shoal: Similar because shoal (adjective) literally means “shallow, of water,” and the noun shoal is a shallow place or sandbank. Differs because shoal is now chiefly technical or dialectal (shoal water) and has no figurative depth sense.
  • Low: Similar because low water is shallow water. Differs because low is relational (low tide) and never describes people or ideas in this way.
  • Light: Similar because light sleep and shallow sleep both mean little depth of rest. Differs because light is immensely broader (weight, colour, illumination) and lacks the moral sense.
  • Near the surface: Similar because both locate things close to the top. Differs because the phrase is purely descriptive and neutral, with no judgement attached.
