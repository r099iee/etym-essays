PIE (reconstructed)
*h₁wésu- / *wesu-
"good, excellent, noble, wealthy"
(source of Hittite assu-, Greek eüs/hēüs, Celtic Vesu-)
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (reconstructed)
*wásu- / *wás-išta-
"good, bright; best, most excellent"
(source of Avestan vohu- "good", vahishta- "best/paradise")
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1500–1200 BCE; Ṛgveda)
vasu (वसु)
"good, bright, excellent, beneficent, wealthy"
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1500–1200 BCE; Ṛgveda)
vasiṣṭha (वसिष्ठ)
"the most excellent, best, richest, most splendid"
(root vasu- + primary superlative suffix -iṣṭha)
↓
Classical Sanskrit / Modern English (via epic literature and Indology)
Vasiṣṭha
"the premier Vedic patriarch-sage; author of Mandala 7; preceptor of Rama"

Note on historical debate: Sanskrit grammarians and modern comparative philologists (such as Arthur Anthony Macdonell and Manfred Mayrhofer) note that Vasiṣṭha is the direct phonological cognate of Avestan vahišta- (the superlative form meaning "best, supreme", which became Middle Persian vahišt and modern Persian behešt, meaning "paradise"). A minor debate exists in early Sanskrit orthography between the spellings Vasiṣṭha (with single -s-, from vasu- "excellent") and the occasional variant Vasiṣṭha / Vaśiṣṭha (derived by folk etymology from vaś- "to control, will", interpreting the sage as "he who has mastered all senses"). Comparative Indo-Iranian linguistics definitively proves that the original Vedic and PIE derivation stems from *wésu- ("good, noble").
