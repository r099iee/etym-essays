• Thine: Similar because both are archaic second-person singular possessive forms of the same set; differs because thine is used before vowel-initial words ("thine eyes") and as a free-standing possessive pronoun ("it is thine"), while thy stands only before consonants ("thy hand").
  • Your: Similar because both are second-person possessive adjectives; differs because your is the modern, unmarked universal possessive for singular and plural, subject to no phonetic split and carrying none of the archaic intimacy or reverence of thy.
  • My / Mine: Similar because thy/thine followed the exact same pre-consonant vs pre-vowel split as my/mine; differs because my/mine are first person, while thy/thine are second person, and only my/mine remain standard today.
  • Thou: Similar because both belong to the same archaic second-person singular paradigm; differs because thou is the subject pronoun ("thou art"), whereas thy is the possessive adjective that modifies a noun ("thy book").
  • Thee: Similar because both descend from the same ancient pronoun and share the archaic register; differs because thee is the object pronoun ("I see thee"), while thy expresses possession ("thy wayes").
  • Thyself: Similar because it is built on the same archaic possessive stem and is likewise a reflex form; differs because it is the reflexive/emphatic second-person pronoun ("honour thyself"), a compound of thy plus self, not a plain possessive adjective.
