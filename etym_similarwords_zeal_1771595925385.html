• Enthusiasm: Similar because both mean lively, eager interest; differs because enthusiasm may be casual or passing, whereas zeal implies deeper, sustained commitment that drives action.
  • Ardor: Similar because both mean intense feeling; differs because ardor is the heat of passion, especially of love or desire, and need not be purposeful, whereas zeal is goal-directed, striding toward an objective.
  • Fervor: Similar because both mean burning intensity; differs because fervor, especially religious intensity, is nearly synonymous, but zeal adds the distinctive element of rivalry and striving — emulation, the urge to excel in a cause.
  • Devotion: Similar because both mean committed loyalty; differs because devotion is loyal attachment to a person or cause, while zeal adds the energetic, almost aggressive activity in pursuit.
  • Passion: Similar because both name strong emotion; differs because passion is any strong feeling, not necessarily productive, whereas zeal is energy channelled toward a cause.
  • Fanaticism: Similar because both name intense commitment to a cause; differs because fanaticism is zeal carried past reason — the excess of the zealot — whereas zeal itself is the praiseworthy or neutral form of the same fire.
  • Eagerness: Similar because both mean keen desire; differs because eagerness is lighter and more immediate, while zeal is weightier, more durable, more consuming.
  • Earnestness: Similar because both mean serious commitment; differs because earnestness is seriousness of purpose without heat, whereas zeal is suffused with passionate temperature.
  • Gusto: Similar because both mean enthusiastic enjoyment; differs because gusto is zestful relish in doing something, light and pleasurable, while zeal is earnest, sustained, and often self-denying effort.
  • Zealotry: Similar because both come from the same stem; differs because zealotry is the noun of excess — immoderate, uncompromising partisanship — while zeal may still be admired as rightly directed fervour.
