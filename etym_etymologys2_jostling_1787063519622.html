Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*yewg-
"to join, unite, yoke"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
iuxtā
"close to, near, next beside"
(Adverb/preposition from *iug-sto- "joined closely")
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th c. CE)
iuxtāre
"to approach, come close together, meet"
↓
Old French (c. 1150)
jouster / juster
"to tilt in a tournament, combat on horseback, meet in combat"
↓
Middle English (c. 1380)
jousten + -le (frequentative) → jostelen / justlen
"to knock against repeatedly, bump together, jostle"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1590)
jostle / justle
"to elbow, push roughly in a crowd, compete aggressively"
↓
Modern English (c. 1750)
jostling
"pushing or elbowing in a crowd; competing vigorously for position"
Note: Derives via Germanic frequentative suffix *-le* added to the Romance loanword *joust*.
