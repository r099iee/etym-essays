• Hydroplane: Similar because it means to skim on water, especially for vehicles; differs because hydroplane is the preferred American term for cars and can also mean a motorboat, while aquaplane retains older board-sport flavour.
  • Skim: Similar because it means to glide lightly over a surface; differs because skim is general and brief, aquaplane specifies water-lift with a plane.
  • Water-ski: Similar because it is a towed watersport on the surface; differs because water-ski uses narrow skis and is a distinct modern sport, aquaplane uses a single broad board.
  • Wakeboard: Similar because it is towed board-riding; differs because wakeboard is contemporary, trick-oriented, and shorter, aquaplane is historical and simpler.
  • Glide: Similar because it denotes smooth effortless motion; differs because glide is usually air or general, aquaplane is specifically water-supported planing.
  • Skid: Similar because it describes loss of traction on wet roads; differs because skid is general sliding, aquaplane specifies riding on a water film.
  • Plane (of a boat): Similar because it means to rise and ride on top of water at speed; differs because plane is nautical-technical for hulls, aquaplane adds explicit aqua and sport or car contexts.
  • Surf: Similar because it means to ride on water; differs because surf uses waves for propulsion, aquaplane uses motor tow or speed lift.
  • Float: Similar because it involves staying on water surface; differs because float is passive buoyancy, aquaplane is dynamic lift at speed.
