PIE *tragh- 'to draw, drag, pull' (reconstructed; the root's exact shape is debated — de Vaan writes *trağʰ-) —>
Latin trahō, trahere 'to pull, draw, drag' (attested from the 3rd century BCE) —>
Latin tragula 'dragnet; a thing for dragging' (formed on the root trag- of trahere with the instrument suffix -ula; attested in classical Latin) —>
Middle Dutch traghelen 'to drag (especially a fishing net)' (attested in the later medieval Low Countries, where the North Sea fisheries developed the dragnet) —>
English trawl (verb) 'to fish with a dragnet' and (noun) 'the dragnet' (1560s) —>
English TRAWLING 'the action of fishing with a trawl net; by extension, wide systematic searching through information or places' ◄ TARGET.

Debate note: the link Latin tragula from trahere is generally accepted, but the reconstruction of the Indo-European root is written variously (*tragh-, *trağʰ-, *tregh-), and the traditional comparison with Greek trekhō 'to run' is doubted; the route into English runs through Middle Dutch traghelen, not directly from Latin.
