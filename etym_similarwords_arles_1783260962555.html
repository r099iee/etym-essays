• Arelate: Similar because it is the same city under its Latin name, and Arles is its direct descendant; differs because Arelate is the ancient Roman form, while Arles is the modern French and English form produced by the loss of the final syllables.
  • Nîmes: Similar because it is another great Roman city of Provence (ancient Nemausus) with outstanding surviving monuments; differs because Nîmes was never an imperial residence or the seat of the Praetorian Prefecture of the Gauls as Arles was.
  • Marseille / Massalia: Similar because both are ancient Provençal cities on the same coast; differs because Marseille is a Greek colonial foundation (Massalia, c. 600 BC) and grew into France's chief Mediterranean port, while Arles is a Romanized Celtic settlement up the Rhône.
  • Avignon: Similar because it is a nearby city on the Rhône with a magnificent medieval and papal past; differs because Avignon was the 14th-century seat of the papacy (the "Babylonian captivity"), a role Arles never played.
  • Provence: Similar because Arles is a principal historical city of Provence and the two names are culturally inseparable; differs because Provence is the whole region, and its name derives from the Roman province (Provincia), not from the city.
  • Narbonne: Similar because it was the earlier chief city and capital of Gallia Narbonensis, Arles's great rival; differs because Arles eventually surpassed Narbonne as the seat of the prefecture and as Constantine's western residence.
  • Kingdom of Burgundy: Similar because the Kingdom of Arles was essentially the Kingdom of Burgundy, and the names are used interchangeably for the same medieval polity; differs because Burgundy names the kingdom and its dynasty, while Arles names the city that supplied the kingdom's alternative title.
  • Alyscamps: Similar because the Alyscamps is Arles's famous Roman and medieval necropolis, one of its most celebrated sights; differs because it is a specific monument within the city, not the city itself.
  • Van Gogh's Provence: Similar because Van Gogh painted both Arles and the wider Provençal landscape in the same period; differs because this is a modern artistic association layered onto the ancient toponym, which had carried meaning for two millennia before the painter arrived.
