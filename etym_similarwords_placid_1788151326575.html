• Calm: Similar because both describe absence of agitation; differs because "calm" is the broader, fully neutral term for any stillness or composure, whereas placid emphasizes a pleasing, undisturbed surface and often a hint of blandness.
  • Serene: Similar because both evoke deep peace; differs because "serene" carries an elevated, luminous, almost spiritual brightness ("a serene sky"), while placid is earthier and more concerned with stillness than radiance.
  • Tranquil: Similar because both indicate profound quiet; differs because "tranquil" suggests an established, healing calm in a place or mind, while placid foregrounds the absence of disturbance and can imply passivity.
  • Peaceful: Similar because both indicate freedom from conflict or noise; differs because "peaceful" is primarily about the absence of war, strife, or menace, while placid is about surface stillness and unruffled temper.
  • Still: Similar because both denote total motionlessness; differs because "still" is purely physical and momentary, whereas placid adds a temperamental or aesthetic quality of quiet agreeableness.
  • Quiet: Similar because both suggest low stimulation; differs because "quiet" refers to sound (or personal reserve) and is far broader, while placid describes a holistic calmness of scene or nature.
  • Composed: Similar because both describe a person self-possessed under pressure; differs because "composed" names a deliberate act of self-control, whereas placid is an inborn or effortless serenity, possibly even a lack of feeling.
  • Unruffled: Similar because both describe undisturbed surfaces and temperaments; differs because "unruffled" directly negates agitation and is used of someone facing a crisis, while placid is more about steady, everyday mildness.
  • Imperturbable: Similar because both describe emotional steadiness; differs because "imperturbable" emphasizes unshakeability under provocation, a stronger and more defensive quality, while placid is gentler and less heroic.
  • Unflappable: Similar because both denote resistance to stress; differs because "unflappable" is colloquial and about handling emergencies coolly, while placid is literary and about a general disposition of calm.
  • Equable: Similar because both describe even, moderate temperament; differs because "equable" stresses uniformity of mood across time and is used almost only of persons, whereas placid freely applies to scenes, water, and weather.
  • Sedate: Similar because both suggest quiet and unhurried composure; differs because "sedate" often implies deliberate decorum or regulation (a "sedate pace") and can suggest sobriety, while placid is more organic.
  • Gentle: Similar because both describe soft, non-aggressive character; differs because "gentle" is about kindness and tenderness of manner, while placid is about stillness and the absence of disturbance.
  • Mild: Similar because both imply moderation and low intensity; differs because "mild" is about degree (not strong, not harsh) across any quality, while placid is specifically about calmness.
  • Untroubled: Similar because both mean free of worry or agitation; differs because "untroubled" is a psychological state (carefree) and can seem oblivious, while placid may describe a scene with no mind in it at all.
  • Undisturbed: Similar because both mean left in stillness; differs because "undisturbed" is purely descriptive of a state produced by the absence of interference, while placid adds an aesthetic of quiet contentment.
