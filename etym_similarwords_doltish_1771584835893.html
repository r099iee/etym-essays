• Stupid: Similar in meaning low in intelligence; differs because stupid is the general, neutral word, too plain and global to capture the specific slow, stubborn, unpolished dullness that doltish names.
  • Foolish: Similar in meaning lacking good sense; differs because foolish points at bad judgment, which can coexist with quick wits, whereas doltish insists on sluggish, thick-headed intellect.
  • Obtuse: Similar in meaning slow to grasp a point; differs because obtuse is the intellectual's Latinate, polite word for failing to understand, while doltish is blunt, rural, and contemptuous.
  • Idiotic: Similar in meaning extremely stupid; differs because idiotic is far harsher and clinically dehumanising, suggesting profound mental deficiency, while doltish keeps a semi-humorous, country flavour.
  • Dull: Similar in sharing the same Germanic root family and the sense of slow wits; differs because dull is broader - blunt (of a knife), dim (of light), boring (of events) - while doltish is specifically and exclusively about persons and their stupidity.
  • Thickheaded: Similar in meaning dense, slow to learn; differs because thickheaded is informal and physical, imagining a thick skull, whereas doltish is literary and archaically insulting with a country flavour.
  • Blockheaded: Similar in meaning stupid, block-like; differs because blockheaded is a playful colloquialism comparing the head to a block of wood, while doltish carries the older, earthier charge of a rural clod.
  • Stolid: Similar in meaning slow and impassive; differs because stolid is neutral or even faintly respectful - dependable, unflappable - while doltish is actively contemptuous about dull intellect.
  • Cloddish: Similar in meaning boorish and dull, of the soil; differs because cloddish stresses boorishness and lack of polish more than raw stupidity, while doltish centres on thick, unteachable wits.
  • Simple: Similar in meaning of weak intellect; differs because simple is euphemistic and pitying, while doltish is mocking and contemptuous.
