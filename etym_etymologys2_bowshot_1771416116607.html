This is a compound noun of two root words, bow and shot; each root receives its own linear derivation chain. CHAIN 1 — the element "bow": PIE *bʰewgʰ- "to bend" (reconstructed; unattested) → Proto-Germanic *beuganą "to bend" (reconstructed), with the deverbal noun *bugō "a bend, an arch, a bow" → Old English boga "a bend, an arch, the arc-shaped weapon" (attested in Beowulf, c. 8th–11th century) → Middle English bōwe "the bow, a bend" (c. 13th century) → Modern English bow "the arc-shaped weapon" (first element of the compound) → Middle English boweshot "the distance a bow can shoot" (c. 1300) → Modern English bowshot. CHAIN 2 — the element "shot": PIE *skeud- "to shoot, hurl, throw, chase, drive" (reconstructed; unattested) → Proto-Germanic *skeutaną "to shoot, push" (reconstructed) → Old English scēotan "to shoot, dart, hurl" (attested c. 725) → deverbal noun Old English scot / gesceot "a shot, a missile, the flight of a missile" (c. 9th century) → Middle English shot / schot "a shot, a missile's flight" (c. 14th century) → Modern English shot "a single discharge; the flight of a missile" (second element of the compound) → the compound Middle English boweshot "the distance a bow can shoot" (c. 1300) → Modern English bowshot. DEBATED POINTS: - The PIE root behind the "bow" element is reconstructed as *bʰewgʰ- "to bend." A separate-looking root *bʰewg- "to flee" was traditionally equated with it (which is how Latin fugere "to flee" was once derived from the bending root); modern handbooks treat the two as distinct, so the Latin equation is now regarded as doubtful. Sanskrit bhuj- "to bend, curve" is a secure cognate of the bending root. - The PIE root behind "shoot" is conventionally reconstructed as *skeud-. A Vedic equation with Sanskrit chud- "to impel, urge" is proposed but debated in handbooks, which note the uncertain fit of the initial cluster. - The precise distance denoted by a bowshot was never legally standardized; attested figures range from about 100 yards to over 300 yards depending on the source.
