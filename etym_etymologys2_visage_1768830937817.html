Derivation chain for VISAGE (single root word): Stage 1 — PIE *weid- "to see, to know, to perceive" (reconstructed; one of the great roots of seeing and knowing, attested by reflexes across the family: Sanskrit veda "I know," Greek (w)eidénai "to know," Latin vidēre "to see"). Stage 2 — Latin vidēre "to see, perceive, observe" (attested from the 3rd century BCE onward; the perfect vīdī and the supine vīsum show the root clearly). Stage 3 — Latin vīsus "sight, a seeing, appearance, aspect, look" — the past participle of vidēre used as a noun (attested from the 1st century BCE, e.g. in Cicero and Lucretius). Stage 4 — Old French vis "face, countenance, appearance, sight" (from Latin vīsus), to which the noun suffix -age was added: Old French visage "face, countenance, look" (attested from the 10th–11th century). Stage 5 — Anglo-Norman / Middle English visage "the face, the countenance, the appearance" (attested from the 13th–14th century). Stage 6 — Modern English visage "a person's face or facial expression" — TARGET. Note: the chain is secure at every stage. Vision, visible, visual, revise, supervise, and the archaic "wit" (knowing) all descend from the same PIE root *weid- by different paths. There is no serious debate about the main line of descent.
