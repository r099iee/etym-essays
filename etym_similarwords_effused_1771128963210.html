• Emitted: Similar because both mean giving something off from a source; differs because emitted is clinical and mechanical, describing discrete, quantifiable releases (a beep, radiation), while effused suggests a fluid, generous, uncontainable pouring.
  • Radiated: Similar because both describe spreading outward from a centre; differs because radiated implies structured, linear dispersion of energy or light waves, whereas effused retains the liquid, enveloping quality of a pour.
  • Exuded: Similar because both mean releasing something from within; differs because exuded suggests a slow, seeping trickle (sweat, sap, charm), while effused commands a grand, atmospheric overflow.
  • Discharged: Similar because both mean releasing something outward; differs because discharged carries connotations of sudden, violent, or official release, without the natural benevolence or abundance of effused.
  • Issued: Similar because both mean sending something forth; differs because issued is bureaucratic and neutral (a statement, a passport), wholly lacking the liquid warmth and generosity of effused.
  • Shed: Similar because both describe letting something fall or flow out; differs because shed is broader and more neutral, used of light, tears, leaves, or skin, without the sustained, lavish imagery of a pour that effused carries.
  • Poured (out): Similar because both describe a liquid flowing freely from a container; differs because poured is the plain everyday verb and lacks the learned, Latinate and emotionally resonant register of effused.
  • Manifested: Similar because both can describe qualities becoming outwardly visible; differs because manifested stresses making something apparent or evident, while effused stresses a flow of substance, light, or feeling.
