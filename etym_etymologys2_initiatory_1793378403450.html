Note: Derived ultimately from PIE *h₁ey- ("to go"), which gave Latin īre ("to go"). Compounded with in- ("in, into"), inīre meant literally "to enter, go within". The noun initium ("beginning") took on a double life in Roman religion, where the plural initia designated esoteric mystery religions (such as the Eleusinian mysteries). Consequently, initiatory developed two twin strands in English: the secular and pedagogical ("introductory, rudimentary, elementary", as Ruskin uses: "the initiatory steps of art") and the ritual and anthropological ("pertaining to rites of passage, baptism, or entrance into a secret society").
