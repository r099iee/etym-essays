Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*dwis- + *ḱom- + *dʰeh₁-
"apart" + "together" + "to make/put"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
dis- + conficere
"to undo, take apart, destroy, ruin"
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 6th c. CE)
*disconfire / *disconfligere
"to overthrow, break up completely"
↓
Old French (c. 12th c.)
desconfire / desconfit
"to defeat utterly, rout in battle"
↓
Middle English (c. 13th c.)
discomfiten
"to vanquish, destroy an army"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th c.)
discomfit
"to thwart, confuse, embarrass"
↓
Modern English (present)
discomfiting
"causing awkwardness, unease, or embarrassment"
