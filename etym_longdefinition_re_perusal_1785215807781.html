RE-PERUSAL (noun) — at its plainest core Re-perusal is the act of reading, examining, or going through a text thoroughly a second time — and, by extension, a deliberate, repeated, point-by-point study of a written work. The hyphenated compound lays its weight on both halves: perusal is not casual reading but a complete, careful, attentive inspection from beginning to end, and the prefix re- insists that the inspection has been undertaken again. Re-perusal is thus the most emphatic member of the family of reading-words: it excludes skim, scan, browse, and dip, and demands that the reader go through the whole text once more, thoroughly, with the attention one would give a document for the first time. The sense in the Mill context In this sentence from the biography of John Stuart Mill, the word appears inside a parenthetical aside that is meant to humble its own subject. Writing a review of George Grote's History of Greece required Mill to re-read the Iliad and the Odyssey in the original Greek — and the biographer tells us that this enormous labour "were recreation to the student." The re-perusal of the two Homeric epics functions as the standard by which Mill's astonishing industry is measured: the scholarly task that would crush an ordinary classicist was, for him, leisure. The word's very formality — its learned, hyphenated, old-fashioned shape — reinforces the point, because it belongs to the world of serious scholarship in which reading Homer in the original, twice, is what a reviewer simply does. Primary senses
  • A second thorough reading: the deliberate act of going through a text completely again, item by item, from beginning to end.
  • Repeated study or examination: by extension, any careful, repeated inspection of a document, manuscript, or record — a re-perusal of the contract, of the minutes, of the evidence.
  • The passive sense (the reading received by a text): "the re-perusal of the classics" — the texts that are read again, as in the Mill sentence where the Iliad and Odyssey are the objects of re-perusal.
Polysemy: secondary, technical, and metaphoric senses
  • The weakening of peruse (a reverse drift): the verb peruse has drifted in modern colloquial use toward the weakened sense "to read casually, to browse or skim" (as in "please peruse the menu"), a meaning that careful writers reject. Re-perusal, because of its learned, hyphenated, scholarly shape, firmly resists this drift: no one re-peruses a menu. The compound therefore functions today as a guarantee of the word's older, stronger sense.
  • Textual criticism and scholarship: in academic usage a re-perusal is a collation or re-examination of a text for accuracy — checking an edition against the manuscript, verifying a quotation, re-reading a source in the original language, as Mill re-read Homer before reviewing Grote.
  • Legal and bureaucratic reading: a second careful reading of a contract, deed, statute, or minutes before signature or approval — where thoroughness is legally significant and skimming is dangerous.
  • Metaphoric extension: by analogy, a "re-perusal" of a life, a career, an argument, or one's own past — a deliberate, point-by-point re-examination of anything that repays being gone through again.
How to use the word precisely Rereading is the ordinary, unmarked term for reading something again and implies no special intensity or completeness; re-perusal insists on a thorough, complete second pass. Perusal alone stresses careful, attentive reading but not repetition — one can peruse a document once. Review, revision, and survey name a broad reconsideration or an official inspection rather than the repeated thorough reading of a text. Scrutiny suggests suspicious or investigative examination, often of conduct or accounts rather than of literature; scanning and skimming are its opposites, denoting hasty or partial reading. Re-perusal is thus the precise instrument a writer reaches for when the point is that a text has been read through carefully, and read through again — a word whose very formality declares the seriousness of the reader.
