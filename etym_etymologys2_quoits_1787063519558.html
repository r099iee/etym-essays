*culcita / *coita
Vulgar Latin / Gallo-Roman (c. 4th c.)
"flat pad, cushion; flat stone for throwing (debated)"
↓
coite / quoite
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 13th c.)
"flat throwing stone, disc, quoit"
↓
coyte / koyte
Middle English (c. 1380)
"a flat stone or iron ring thrown at a mark; game of quoits"
↓
quoit
Early Modern English (c. 16th c.)
"forged iron ring for pitching; verb: to pitch or hurl flatly"
↓
quoits
Modern English (present)
"traditional game of throwing iron, rope, or rubber rings at a peg"

Etymological Note: The ultimate origin is debated between Latin culcita ('flat cushion') and a native Celtic root for flat pitching stones.
