- To suffer or waste - From Old English pinian ('to torment, torture'), pine first meant to afflict and torment, the pain sense lying at the word's root.
- Wasting with longing - By the 14th century the verb shifts to 'to waste away with longing, grief, or confinement', fusing intense desire with physical decline.
- Modern longing - Today pine (and the collocation pine for) means to long intensely for an absent person, home, or thing, and the past form pined keeps an archaic, elegiac softness in literary use.
