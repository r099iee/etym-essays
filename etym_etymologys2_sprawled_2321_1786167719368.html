Linear derivation chain for SPRAWLED (single root word): R — PIE *sper- (to scatter, strew, spread; by extension to twitch or spasm) — the deepest plausible source connecting the Germanic family to the wider Indo-European scattering-roots; the link is probable but not universally proven. → E — Proto-Germanic *sprawlōn (to thrash, to move the limbs convulsively, to spread the limbs about). → D — Old English sprēawlian (to move convulsively, writhe, kick about; to sprawl the limbs). → C — Middle English spraulen / sprowlen (to sprawl, thrash, spread the limbs carelessly). → B — Modern English sprawl (verb: to lie or sit with the limbs spread out carelessly; noun: an unplanned outward spread, as of a city). → A — sprawled (past tense and past participle of sprawl) ◄ TARGET. Note on the debate: The connection between PIE *sper- and Proto-Germanic *sprawlōn rests on an irregular consonant frame and is disputed; some authorities treat the Germanic family as independent or of imitative/sound-symbolic origin. The Germanic-to-English chain (E → A) is itself fully secure and attested at every stage.
