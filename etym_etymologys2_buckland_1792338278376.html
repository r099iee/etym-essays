--- THE COMPOUND TOPONYM ---
Old English Compound (c. 8th–9th c.)
bōcland
"land granted by written charter or book"
↓
Anglo-Norman / Domesday Latin (1086 CE)
Bocheland
"manor held under chartered title"
↓
Middle English (c. 13th–14th c.)
Bokeland / Bukland
"village or estate formerly held by charter"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th–17th c.)
Buckland
"parish and district in Portsmouth, Hampshire"
↓
Modern English (present)
Buckland
"inner district of Portsmouth, Hampshire; birthplace of A. J. Arberry"

Note on compound phonology: The shift from Old English bōcland to Modern English Buckland represents a standard Germanic phonetic development known as pre-cluster vowel shortening. Before the heavy consonant cluster /-kl-/, the long back vowel /oː/ shortened in Early Middle English to /o/ and subsequently /ʊ/, which in southern Early Modern English underwent the seventeenth-century "foot-strut split", shifting the vowel to the short open-mid back unrounded vowel /ʌ/. The spelling with -ck- arose in Middle English orthography to indicate the shortened preceding vowel.
