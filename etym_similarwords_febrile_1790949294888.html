• Feverish: Similar because both mean marked by elevated temperature or frantic excitement; differs because feverish is the native, everyday Germanic-based English word suitable for all registers, whereas febrile is a formal, Latinate, and highly literary term.
  • Frantic: Similar because both denote agitated, disorderly excitement; differs because frantic emphasizes chaotic, desperate panic and wild physical movements rather than internal burning intensity or medical fever.
  • Frenetic: Similar because both describe fast-paced, high-energy agitation; differs because frenetic focuses on non-stop, hyperactive physical motion and frantic pace rather than feverish psychological tension.
  • Delirious: Similar because both can describe acute mental disturbance accompanying illness; differs because delirious specifically indicates disorientation, hallucinations, and incoherent speech resulting from fever or intoxication.
  • Agitated: Similar because both describe a state of nervous distress; differs because agitated is a broader emotional term denoting being perturbed or stirred up, lacking the specific connotations of burning fever and heightened volatility.
  • Hysterical: Similar because both convey uncontrollable emotional frenzy; differs because hysterical implies irrational, wild emotional outbursts and loss of self-control.
  • Restless: Similar because both describe inability to remain calm; differs because restless is mild and understated, whereas febrile conveys an acute, overheated, burning intensity.
