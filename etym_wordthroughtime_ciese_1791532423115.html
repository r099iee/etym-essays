- 5th–7th century: a Latin loanword in the West Germanic dialects - *kāsijaz names a preserved dairy food and travels with the settlers to Britain.
- 8th–11th century: Old English cēse / cīese - cheese is a staple of the monastic and peasant diet, and the word appears in glosses and food lists with no figurative colour.
- 12th–15th century: Middle English chese - the word becomes ordinary and regional, and cheese is central to manor, monastery and market.
- 16th–18th century: the age of regional cheeses - named varieties multiply, and cheese becomes a commercial and national product as well as a food.
- 19th–20th century: slang and industry - "cheese" generates "cheesy," "big cheese," "cheese it" and "say cheese," while industrial cheesemaking standardises the product.
- 21st century: craft revival and global trade - artisanal cheese, protected designations and food tourism give the old word a new prestige, while its slang senses remain firmly in use.
