- 16th century grammar and rhetoric - enters English from the Greek grammatical tradition as the descriptive word or phrase attached to a name, a stock device of epic (swift-footed Achilles); the register is classical and literary.
- 17th-18th century ornament and convention - epithets are the expected adjectives of poetry and panegyric, praised as dignity or mocked as padding in the age of Johnson.
- 19th century characterization - broadens from fixed poetic tags to any pointedly interpretive label fastened on a person, capable of carrying judgment and innuendo.
- Contemporary use - the neutral descriptive sense survives in Homeric and critical usage, but the plural has darkened: "epithets" now often means terms of abuse or slurs, as in "racial epithets."
