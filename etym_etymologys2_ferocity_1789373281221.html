PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*gʷʰer-
"wild beast, wild animal"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*feros
"wild, untamed, savage"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
ferus
"wild, untamed, uncivilized, beast"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
ferōx (stem ferōc-)
"fierce, wild-eyed, bold, warlike"
Note: Morphological compound of ferus ('wild') + *-ōkʷ- ('looking, aspect', from PIE *h₃okʷ- 'eye, see'); literally 'having the look of a wild beast'.
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
ferōcitās
"fierceness, wildness, haughty courage"
Note: Formed with abstract noun suffix -itās (PIE *-tāt-).
↓
Middle French (c. 16th c.)
férocité
"fierceness, savage cruelty"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1530s)
ferocitie / ferocity
"fierceness, wildness, savage nature"
↓
Modern English (present)
FEROCITY
