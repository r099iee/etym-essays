• Anthropologists: Similar because both study humankind and culture, often by fieldwork; differs because anthropologists is the wider profession — including biological and archaeological branches — while ethnographers are specifically the describing writers of living cultures.
  • Ethnologists: Similar because both take peoples as their subject and often share a department; differs because ethnologists compare and theorise across cultures, while ethnographers supply the first-hand descriptive monographs the comparison feeds on.
  • Fieldworkers: Similar because both gather knowledge on site rather than in the library; differs because fieldworkers is the plain generic term for anyone collecting outdoors — census takers, geologists — while ethnographers practise the immersive craft of participant observation.
  • Folklorists: Similar because in the source sentence both gather tales, songs, and rites from the people; differs because folklorists specialise in oral tradition and lore as such, while ethnographers describe whole ways of life of which lore is one part.
  • Sociologists: Similar because both observe how human groups organise, believe, and behave; differs because sociologists typically study modern complex societies with surveys and statistics, while ethnographers dwell inside communities with notebooks.
  • Participant observers: Similar because participant observation is the very method of ethnography; differs because participant observer names the role or technique in any setting, while ethnographer names the professional identity and its written product.
  • Chroniclers: Similar because both record the life of a community for posterity; differs because a chronicler compiles events reign by reign from testimony and documents, while an ethnographer lives the life described before writing it.
  • Cultural historians: Similar because both reconstruct how peoples lived, believed, and celebrated; differs because cultural historians work backward from archives and artefacts, while ethnographers work outward from living presence.
  • Travel writers: Similar because both journey among strangers and report home in vivid detail; differs because travel writers entertain passing readers with the picturesque, while ethnographers answer to a discipline — method, language, and return visits.
