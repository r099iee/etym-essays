• Horse: Similar because cavalry is literally soldiers on horseback; differs because horse in the military sense is the archaic collective noun (Belonging to the horse), while cavalry is the modern arm with its regiments and traditions.
  • Mounted troops: Similar because both describe soldiers who ride rather than march; differs because mounted troops is a loose descriptive phrase (including mounted police and infantry), while cavalry names the formal shock arm.
  • Dragoons: Similar because dragoons were classic cavalrymen, riding to battle and often fighting on foot; differs because dragoons name one historical type (mounted infantry turned horse), while cavalry is the whole arm.
  • Hussars: Similar because hussars are the archetypal light cavalry — pelisses, moustaches, daring raids; differs because hussars are the flamboyant light branch, while cavalry covers heavies, mediums, and lights together.
  • Lancers: Similar because lancers charged with the long lance in every European army; differs because lancers name the lance-armed regiments specifically, while cavalry is the umbrella for every mounted type.
  • Cuirassiers: Similar because cuirassiers were the ultimate heavy cavalry, breastplated shock troops; differs because cuirassiers are that one armoured elite, while cavalry includes the lightest scout alongside them.
  • Air cavalry: Similar because helicopter cavalry inherits the mission — speed, shock, reconnaissance; differs because air cavalry flies it in rotary-wing aircraft, while classic cavalry rode it on hooves.
  • Armoured corps: Similar because tanks inherited the cavalry’s shock role and often its titles; differs because the armoured corps is the modern mechanised branch, while cavalry is the historic horse-arm (or its titled descendant).
  • Yeomanry: Similar because Cobbett’s yeomanry cavalry were volunteer horsemen drilled like regulars; differs because the yeomanry were part-time county amateurs, while cavalry proper means full-time professional horse.
