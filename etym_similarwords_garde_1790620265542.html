• guard: Similar because it is the exact English doublet, a watching protective body; differs because guard is fully nativized and general, while garde is a marked French spelling tied to avant-garde.
  • vanguard / van / forefront: Similar because they denote the leading protective party; differs because they name position, while garde names the keeping function itself.
  • watch / lookout: Similar because they share the root sense of keeping watch; differs because they are actions or roles, not a corps with institutional identity.
  • warden / guardian / custodian / keeper: Similar because they denote one who keeps and protects; differs because they are individual offices, while garde is collective and military.
  • rearguard: Similar because it is the structural opposite within the same military frame; differs because it defends the rear and tradition, while garde in avant-garde advances ahead.
  • corps / detachment / brigade / troop: Similar because they denote organized bodies of personnel; differs because they are neutral military units, lacking garde’s watching and avant-garde’s artistic charge.
  • ward (archaic / legal): Similar because it shares the Germanic root and means keeping or protection; differs because it survives mainly in law and place-names, not in art criticism.
