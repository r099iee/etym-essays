“Shriek” has always named the high, piercing cry — sharper and more sudden than a shout — the sound forced out by terror, pain, or extreme alarm.
“Shriek” in older English was the word for genuine alarm calls — battle-shrieks, the cries of the wounded, the shrieking of birds at dusk — a sound of real danger.
“Shriek” by the 19th century gained a lighter, comic life: people “shriek with laughter,” and Victorian simile-writers, as Tolkien would later, could compare a shriek to a steam-whistle emerging from a tunnel.
“Shriek” today covers both extremes at once — the piercing cry of genuine panic and the high, excited squeal of delight — panic at the very top of the voice.
