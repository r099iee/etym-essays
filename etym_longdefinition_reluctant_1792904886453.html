reluctant means unwilling, holding back, consenting only under pressure or not at all. A reluctant volunteer steps forward slowly, a reluctant witness must be summoned, a reluctant hero would rather stay home, and reluctant bushes, in Tolkien’s playful personification, snag every pack and cloak rather than let walkers through.

In the source sentence the bushes and brambles are reluctant to let them through, as if the thicket itself had a will and refused passage. The party must push while the woodland pushes back, packs catching and thorns holding, so that a human mood of foot-dragging unwillingness is lent to vegetation. The comedy is gentle but the toil is real, and one adjective turns botany into character, the landscape becoming an unhelpful porter.

Core meaning in detail: To be reluctant is to struggle against in the mind what the body may yet do. The reluctant giver gives but grudges it, the reluctant speaker speaks but softens it, the reluctant traveller walks but looks back. Grammar marks the strain, reluctant to plus verb, reluctant before nouns, always facing the action it would avoid. It is weaker than refusal and stronger than hesitation, a wrestle held inside a single breast, and Tolkien extends that wrestle outward until whole bushes seem to wrestle with hobbits.

All senses, primary and secondary:

  • Unwilling, disinclined, holding back (primary modern sense): a reluctant apology, reluctant to admit fault. The bridge is direct from Latin reluctari, to struggle against, the mind wrestling with what duty demands.
  • Done grudgingly or under compulsion (transferred sense): reluctant consent, reluctant laughter. The bridge moves the mood from the person to the act, so the gift or grin itself seems to drag its feet.
  • Personified resistance of things (literary sense, Tolkien’s use): reluctant bushes, a reluctant door, a reluctant engine on a cold morning. The bridge lends human unwillingness to objects, making friction feel like temper.
  • The reluctant hero and reluctant witness (stock figures): the hero who refuses the call, the witness who must be compelled. The bridge is narrative, whole plots turning on a good person’s refusal to act until necessity overcomes reluctance.
  • Slow to change state, physics and engineering (technical metaphor): a reluctant magnetic circuit, reluctant markets. The bridge borrows the human wrestle for systems that resist being magnetised, moved, or persuaded, engineers hearing temperament in metal.

Register and connotation: reluctant is dignified and sympathetic, softer than defiant and kinder than unwilling. To call someone reluctant is usually to excuse them a little, they feel the pull of duty and the pull of fear at once. Even Pippin’s bushes earn a flicker of fellow feeling, fellow travellers who, like hobbits, would rather not be on this errand at all. Packs snag, walkers sigh, and the whole wood seems to share their mood, holding every bramble like a withheld hand.
