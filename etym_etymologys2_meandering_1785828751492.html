Greek
(1st millennium BCE)
Μαιάνδρος (Maíandros)
"the Maeander river in Caria,
renowned for its winding course"
↓
Latin
(1st c. BCE)
Maeander
"the Maeander river; hence
a winding course, a maze"
↓
English
(16th c.)
meander
"a winding, circuitous course"
↓
English
(c. 1600)
meander
"to flow or follow a winding,
circuitous course"
↓
Modern English
meandering
"winding, following a circuitous
or indirect course"
↓
Note: English meander comes from the Greek river
name (modern Büyük Menderes, Turkey); the river-name
itself is of pre-Greek (Anatolian) origin, not PIE.
