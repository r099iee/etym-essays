Unfeigned (/ʌnˈfeɪnd/) is an adjective describing feelings, actions, expressions, or states of mind that are genuine, sincere, heartfelt, and completely devoid of pretense, hypocrisy, dissimulation, or theatrical contrivance.

The word carries profound semantic resonance across emotional, ethical, religious, legal, and artistic domains, organized into distinct nuances and applications:

  • Authentic Emotional Expression (Primary Psychological Sense): Applied to emotional reactions—such as grief, delight, admiration, remorse, astonishment, or affection—unfeigned indicates that the outward affective display corresponds exactly to an inward, spontaneous psychological reality. Unlike polite, calculated, or simulated responses, unfeigned sorrow or unfeigned laughter erupts without conscious staging or social posturing, representing the purest transparency of the human heart in everyday human interaction.
  • Theological and Scriptural Sincerity (Biblical Register): In religious literature and moral theology, unfeigned holds a venerated place as the standard translation for sincere spiritual integrity without hypocrisy (Greek anypókritos, literally 'without playacting'). Familiarized through the King James Version of the Bible in phrases like 'love unfeigned' (2 Corinthians 6:6) and 'faith unfeigned' (1 Timothy 1:5, 2 Timothy 1:5), the term designates a purity of devotion and charity that is completely free from false piety, Pharisaic ostentation, or self-serving religious performance before God and humanity.
  • Ethical and Interpersonal Integrity (Moral Character Sense): In moral philosophy and character appraisal, an unfeigned commitment, unfeigned apology, or unfeigned friendship represents uncompromised loyalty and transparency. It implies an absolute absence of ulterior motives, strategic manipulation, deceitful flattery, or double-dealing in interpersonal relationships, establishing mutual trust and authentic human communion.
  • Artistic and Dramatic Authenticity (Aesthetic & Theatrical Sense): In theatre, acting theory, and literary criticism, unfeigned describes emotional delivery where the actor's portrayal transcends mere mimicry or mechanical technique, touching genuine, organic feeling (as in the historical anecdote of the ancient actor Polus holding the real ashes of his son to evoke true grief on stage, or the modern Stanislavskian concept of perezhivanie / 'experiencing'). In this context, it contrasts sharply with empty theatrical posturing and artificial stage clichés.
  • Legal, Testimonial, and Formal Assurances (Jurisprudential Sense): In formal oaths, affidavits, and historical covenants, unfeigned assent or unfeigned allegiance signifies a voluntary, uncoerced, and truthful pledge of fidelity, where the declarant formally asserts that no mental reservation, dissimulation, or deceitful intention exists in their solemn undertaking.

The semantic power of unfeigned stems from the negative prefix un- undoing the concept of 'feigning' (from Latin fingere, 'to fashion, invent, simulate'), affirming that the reality has not been artificially manufactured, molded, or fabricated by human artifice. It stands as a timeless beacon of unadorned, pure truthfulness across philosophical, theological, and literary traditions.
