• Loch Lomond: Similar because it is another famous Scottish loch with an outflowing Leven; differs because Lomond is large, deep, and Highland, while Leven (Kinross) is small, shallow, and Lowland.
  • Loch Katrine: Similar because Scott romanticised both as lady-haunted waters (Lady of the Lake); differs because Katrine belongs to Ellen and FitzJames, while Leven belongs to Mary and Roland Graeme.
  • Loch Tay / Loch Awe: Similar because they are central-Scottish freshwater lochs with castles and clans; differs because they are long glacial troughs, while Leven is a round lowland mere.
  • River Forth: Similar because the Leven (Fife) ultimately reaches the Forth; differs because Forth is the great estuary system, while Leven is its modest tributary.
  • River Leven (Dumbarton): Similar because it shares the identical Celtic name and links loch to firth; differs because it drains Loch Lomond to the Clyde, not Loch Leven to the Forth.
  • Loch Leven (Glencoe): Similar because it bears the same name in the Highlands; differs because it is a sea-loch off Loch Linnhe, not the freshwater Kinross loch.
  • Kinross: Similar because the town stands on Loch Leven and Scott pilgrims say Kinross for the loch; differs because Kinross is the burgh, while Leven is the water.
  • Lochleven Castle: Similar because visitors mean the castle when they say Loch Leven; differs because the castle is the island fortress, while Leven is the loch holding it.
  • St Serf's Island: Similar because it is the priory island in the same loch; differs because it is monastic ruins, while Leven-castle island is Marian prison history.
  • levin (lightning): Similar because it is spelled and sounded almost identically; differs because levin is an obsolete common noun for lightning of Germanic origin, while Leven is a Celtic proper-name water.
