PIE (Proto-Indo-European root)
*kap-
"to grasp, seize, hold, contain"
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*kap-ē- / *kapsā
"to hold, contain / box, receptacle"
↓
Latin (c. 3rd–2nd c. BCE)
capsa
"box, chest, case, receptacle (from capere 'to hold')"
↓
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 12th–13th c.)
chasse / casse / encassement
"frame, shrine, box, casing, enclosing border"
↓
Middle English (early 15th c.; c. 1440 Promptorium Parvulorum)
casement / casemente
"hollow molding in masonry; window frame, casing"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th c.; Shakespearean usage)
casement
"hinged window sash opening on side pivots"
↓
Modern English (present)
CASEMENT
"a window sash hinged on one side to open outwards or inwards"

Note: Formed in Anglo-Norman and Middle English by suffixing the noun case (from Old French casse/chasse < Latin capsa) with the nominal suffix -ment. Initially denoting the concave architectural frame or stone casing of an opening, the sense transferred via metonymy to the hinged window sash set within that casing.
