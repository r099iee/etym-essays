• Crevice: Similar because both mean a narrow crack in rock or stone; differs because a crevice is typically wider and deeper and is used almost exclusively of rock, while a cranny can be a small gap in any surface, including wood or masonry.
  • Crack: Similar because both denote a narrow split; differs because crack is a far more general word (it can be a fracture of any size, a loud sound, or a sharp blow), while cranny always implies a small, enclosed, concealable gap.
  • Chink: Similar because both describe a narrow opening through which light or air passes; differs because a chink is especially a vertical slit (as between boards) and often emphasises the light passing through, whereas a cranny emphasises the hiding or lodging of things.
  • Fissure: Similar because both mean an elongated crack in a surface; differs because fissure is more technical and usually much larger — a fissure can split a glacier or the earth — while a cranny stays small and domestic.
  • Cleft: Similar because both are narrow gaps formed by splitting; differs because a cleft tends to be a broader V-shaped split (often in rock, or anatomical, as in a cleft chin), while a cranny is thin and irregular.
  • Nook: Similar because nooks and crannies are idiomatically paired as small secluded places; differs because a nook is a corner or alcove open enough to sit or hide in, whereas a cranny is only a thin crack.
  • Recess: Similar because both denote an indented, tucked-away place; differs because a recess is an architectural or anatomical indentation of notable size, while a cranny is a mere slit.
  • Interstice: Similar because both mean a tiny gap between adjacent things; differs because interstice is a learned, technical word used of lattices, teeth, or spaces between cells, while cranny is ordinary, physical language.
  • Gap: Similar because both mean an opening in a surface; differs because a gap is any opening of any size and is completely neutral, whereas a cranny is specifically small, narrow, and evocative of concealment.
  • Crevasse: Similar because both are long narrow cracks; differs because a crevasse is a deep, wide, dangerous opening in a glacier or ice field — vastly larger and more dramatic than a cranny.
