• Rest: Similar because both denote the cessation of activity or movement; differs because "rest" is the plain everyday term with no emotional register, while "repose" implies a calm, deliberate, dignified stillness of body or object.
  • Relaxation: Similar because both involve rest from exertion; differs because "relaxation" is rest taken for refreshment and the easing of tension, while "repose" is undisturbed stillness without any suggestion of recreation.
  • Respite: Similar because both denote a pause from activity; differs because "respite" names an intermission or breathing-space that will soon end, while "repose" is a settled, continuing state of rest.
  • Recess: Similar because both denote a break from activity; differs because "recess" is a scheduled intermission (of a court, school, or assembly), whereas "repose" is an unmanaged state of stillness.
  • Quiescence: Similar because both name a state of stillness; differs because "quiescence" is technical and suggests a merely latent or suspended state (quiescence of a disease), while "repose" is the ordinary dignified rest of a person or object.
  • Slumber: Similar because both denote peaceful inactivity; differs because "slumber" is limited to sleep, whereas "repose" covers the waking dignity of a body or object lying perfectly at rest.
  • Calm: Similar because both denote freedom from agitation; differs because "calm" applies to weather, seas, and emotions alike and is neutral, while "repose" specifically pictures a body or thing at rest.
  • Tranquillity: Similar because both denote deep inner or outward stillness; differs because "tranquillity" stresses the absence of disturbance as a quality of a scene or mind, while "repose" stresses the act and state of resting.
  • Stillness: Similar because both denote absence of motion; differs because "stillness" is purely descriptive of motionlessness, while "repose" adds the connotation of ease, dignity, and freedom from burden.
  • Peace: Similar because both denote freedom from disturbance; differs because "peace" is the broad term for absence of conflict (personal or political), while "repose" is specifically the restful stillness of body, mind, or the dead.
  • Ease: Similar because both denote freedom from effort or discomfort; differs because "ease" is the general word for comfort and facility, while "repose" pictures actual resting and stillness.
  • Lull: Similar because both denote a temporary quiet; differs because "lull" is a brief, passing stillness (the lull before the storm), while "repose" is a settled and often prolonged state of rest.
