• Pornography: Similar because both involve explicit sexual depictions; differs because pornography is a descriptive term for material produced specifically to arouse erotic desire, whereas obscenity is a normative legal, moral, and aesthetic judgment denoting that the material is patently offensive, devoid of redeeming social value, and legally punishable.
  • Vulgarity: Similar because both describe coarse or offensive behavior and speech; differs because vulgarity implies a lack of refined taste, bad manners, or crude uncultured speech, whereas obscenity reaches a far higher threshold of moral revulsion, gross indecency, and societal taboo.
  • Profanity: Similar because profanities and obscenities are both spoken expletives; differs because profanity historically and strictly involves taking sacred or religious names and concepts in vain (blasphemy/sacrilege), whereas obscenity relates to sexual, excretory, and bodily taboos.
  • Indecency: Similar because both refer to violations of social decorum; differs because indecency is a milder legal and social category (often subject to broadcast time restrictions like safe-harbor hours), whereas obscenity is severe and completely stripped of constitutional protection.
  • Lewdness: Similar because both involve overt sexual offensiveness; differs because lewdness specifically emphasizes lustful, lascivious, or unchaste conduct in interpersonal interaction, whereas obscenity applies broadly to publications, art, language, and moral atrocities.
  • Abomination: Similar because both denote something morally repulsive; differs because abomination is a high theological or biblical register term signifying hatred, curse, and religious uncleanliness, whereas obscenity has a strong modern legal and social framework.
