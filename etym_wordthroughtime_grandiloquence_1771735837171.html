- [16th-17th century lofty speech] - [Enters from Latin meaning lofty, grand style of speaking; connotation of impressive, dignified oratory]
- [18th century the Augustan manner] - [Used of the stately, balanced, Latinate, periodic prose of the age — Gibbon's majestic symmetries and slow cadences; connotation appreciative but aware of the theatrical mask]
- [19th-20th century the risk of excess] - [Slips toward the territory of bombast — grand language whose matter may not match its manner; connotation increasingly ironic or critical in ordinary use]
- [20th-21st century rare and literary] - [Now uncommon and bookish; it names a grand oratorical manner with a built-in risk of pretension, judged by whether the substance equals the style]
