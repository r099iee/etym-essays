IRIDESCENT — derivation chain - Ancient Greek īris (ἶρις), genitive īridos (ἴριδος) "rainbow; also the goddess Iris" (attested in Greek literature from the 8th c. BCE, Homer onwards). Ultimate origin: uncertain. There is NO securely reconstructable PIE root; a proposed link to PIE *wei-/*wer- "to turn, bend" (the rainbow as the great bent arc) is a speculation without consensus, and the word is best treated as a native Greek noun of uncertain, possibly pre-Greek, provenance. The goddess Iris, daughter of Thaumas and Electra, was the swift messenger of the gods whose golden path across the sky was the rainbow. - Greek īris also named, from early scientific usage, the coloured ring of the eye (the anatomical iris) and the many-hued flower (the botanical iris), both by the same rainbow association. - Latin īris, genitive īridis "rainbow; iris" (loanword from Greek, attested in Latin from the 1st c. BCE–CE; Pliny uses it of the rainbow and of the iris flower). - Scientific / post-classical Latin īridēscēns, īridescent- "rainbow-like, gleaming like a rainbow" — a coinage built on the genitive stem īrid- "of the rainbow" plus the inchoative participial suffix -ēscent- ("becoming, tending to be," from -ēscere, as in effervescent, luminescent). The exact Latin formation is a Neo-Latin/scientific formation rather than a classical word. - Modern English iridescent "showing rainbow-like colours that shift with the angle of light" (coined in English scientific writing c. 1791; the OED records the adjective from the 1790s in the vocabulary of optics and mineralogy, describing mother-of-pearl, opals, and the play of colour on surfaces). → derivatives: iridescence (abstract noun), iridescently (adverb), iridesce (rare back-formation) → Modern English IRIDESCENT. Note on the elements: the word is a single loan-coined adjective, so there is only one linear chain; the two morphological parts (irid- and -escent) come together in the scientific formation of the late 18th century and are not independent root words. The Romance forms (French iridescent, Italian iridescente, Spanish iridiscente, attested 19th–20th c.) are reborrowings from English or parallel Neo-Latin formations, not stages in the English chain. Debated stages stated explicitly: - The origin of Greek īris itself is unknown; the *wei- "to bend" etymology is plausible but unproven, and the OED and standard handbooks (Beekes) treat the word as of uncertain or substrate origin. Everything from Greek īris onward is securely attested; only the deepest Greek stage is uncertain.
