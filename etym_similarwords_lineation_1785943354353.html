• Enjambment: Similar because both concern the poetic line; differs because enjambment is the specific device of running a sentence past the end of a line without pause, while lineation is the whole system of line-breaks of which enjambment is one result — enjambment only exists in relation to lineation.
  • Caesura: Similar because both shape rhythm within a poem; differs because a caesura is a pause inside a single line (a metrical rest within the verse), whereas lineation governs the breaks between lines.
  • Scansion: Similar because both analyse poetic form; differs because scansion is the metrical analysis of feet and stress within lines, while lineation concerns the lines themselves and their breaks.
  • Layout: Similar because both describe the arrangement of a page or text; differs because layout is general design (pictures, headings, margins, columns), while lineation concerns specifically the division into lines.
  • Arrangement: Similar in meaning "the way things are set in order"; differs because arrangement is a general word for any ordering, while lineation specifies ordering into lines.
  • Ruling: Similar because both mean the drawing of lines; differs because ruling is the practical act of ruling lines on paper (ruled paper), a craft term, while lineation has become a formal term for the resulting line-structure.
  • Outline: Similar because both concern lines on a surface; differs because an outline is the outer boundary of a shape, while lineation is the internal pattern or division into lines.
  • Alignment: Similar because both involve straight arrangement; differs because alignment means bringing things into a line or agreement (aligning objects, opinions, or molecules), while lineation is the presence and arrangement of the lines themselves.
  • Striation: Similar because both name parallel linear marks in nature; differs because striation denotes fine, scratched or grooved parallel lines (on rock, glass, or muscle), while lineation is the broader term for any linear arrangement, including the alignment of minerals.
  • Foliation: Similar because it, too, is a rock fabric studied by the same geologists; differs because foliation is a planar (layered) fabric in metamorphic rock, while lineation is a linear (one-directional) fabric — the one marks planes, the other marks lines.
  • Verse / verse division: Similar because both involve poetry laid out in lines; differs because "verse" can mean the line itself or poetry generally, while lineation names the system of dividing a text into lines, including prose arranged as verse.
  • Bedding: Similar because it is a geological term for layered structure; differs because bedding is the original sedimentary layering of strata, while lineation is a fabric imposed later by deformation, and they are distinct structures in the same rock.
  • Venation: Similar because it is the line-pattern of a leaf, close to biological lineation; differs because venation is specifically the network of veins in a leaf or wing, while lineation is the general word for any linear arrangement and is not confined to veins.
