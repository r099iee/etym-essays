Single root word; one linear chain: PIE *deu- "to do, perform, show favor, revere" (reconstructed; unattested; handbooks debate its conflation with the homophonous-looking sky-root *dyew- "sky, to shine") → Old Latin duenos "good" (attested in the Duenos inscription, c. 600–500 BCE), classicized as Latin bonus "good" (1st century BCE; diminutive *duenulus / benulus "the good little one" lies behind the next stage) → Latin bellus "pretty, charming, handsome, beautiful" (Cicero's letters, 1st century BCE; literally "the good little one") → Old French bel / bele "handsome, beautiful" (from Latin bellus, c. 9th–11th century) → Old French embellir "to make beautiful" (12th century; em- < Latin in- "into" + bel "beautiful") → Middle French embellir (same verb) → Middle English embellisen "to make beautiful" (late 14th century, borrowed from the French stem embelliss-) → Modern English embellish → past participle embellished. DEBATED POINTS: - The root is commonly written *deu- "to do, show favor," but handbooks note that several near-homophonous roots (*deu-, *dyew-, *dheu-) are easily confused; Sanskrit deva "god, the shining one" is often assigned to the separate sky-root *dyew- rather than to *deu-, and the debate testifies to how close the ideas of favor, goodness, and radiance stood in the ancient mind. - The intermediate diminutive is reconstructed as *duenulus (also written *benulus / *duenos-los); it is not directly attested but is required by the comparative grammar of Latin to bridge bonus and bellus.
