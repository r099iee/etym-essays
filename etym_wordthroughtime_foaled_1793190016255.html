- Old English — the young one: fola names the young horse or colt, a farmyard commonplace beside calf and lamb.
- Middle English — horseman's noun: fole serves knights, ploughmen, and poets, the mount's childhood in a syllable.
- c. 1600 — the verb arrives: mares begin to foal, converting the noun into the act of birth like lamb and calve before it.
- 1791 — the stud book era: the General Stud Book records every thoroughbred as foaled on such a date at such a place, making foaled a legal pedigree fact.
- 19th c. — racing's keyword: sale catalogues and racecards chant foaled, by, out of, turning birth into commerce and bloodline into poetry.
- Today — stable standard: foaled remains the worldwide breeding term, from Kentucky to Newmarket, while fairy tales borrow it to give magic horses believable pasts.
