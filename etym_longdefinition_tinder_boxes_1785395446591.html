TINDER-BOXES is the plural of tinder-box: small boxes or cases for carrying the materials of fire-making - tinder, flint, and a steel striker - the standard means of kindling fire before the friction match appeared in the early nineteenth century. The word is a transparent compound: 'tinder', the dry flammable substance (charred cloth, dried fungus, or shredded bark) that catches the spark struck from flint, and 'box', the container that holds the whole kit. In The Hobbit, Oin and Gloin 'had lost their tinder-boxes', and Gandalf has set the fire himself: the phrase does quiet period work, placing the party in a pre-modern world where losing one's tinder-box is a genuine small calamity, part of the everyday apparatus of wilderness survival. Its tone is homely and concrete, the vocabulary of hardware and household gear, and Tolkien uses it to ground high adventure in practical detail. In the nineteenth century the word also developed its metaphorical life: a 'tinder-box' (now usually spelled tinderbox, unhyphenated) is a situation or region ready to burst into flame - a powder-keg of a different substance - and it is this figurative sense that keeps the word alive in modern political and journalistic prose. CORE SENSES AND POLYSEMY
  • Primary sense (historical, literal): the small box holding tinder, flint, and steel for kindling a fire - the everyday fire-making kit of homes, travellers, and soldiers before the friction match.
  • Metaphorical / political sense: a situation, region, or issue ready to burst into conflict - the Balkans as the tinderbox of Europe; a volatile border region; a stock of grievances waiting for a spark. Recorded from the 19th century, the sense keeps the image of a place where the smallest spark sets off a blaze.
  • Collective / domain senses: in fire-history, the tinder-box names the whole kit in one word - fuel, striker, and container; the word is the standard term in museum and historical writing for the pre-match fire-making box.
  • Related implements: tinder alone names only the fuel; flint and steel names the implements rather than the box; a matchbox is the modern successor; a lighter is the twentieth-century device. Tinder-box comprehends all its parts in one compound.
  • Spelling: the literal sense is often hyphenated (tinder-box), while the metaphorical sense is usually written solid (tinderbox); both forms are current.
  • Register: the literal sense is historical, homely, and concrete; the metaphorical sense is journalistic and dramatic, a stock image of political volatility.
POLYSEMY - THE SEMANTIC BRIDGE FROM FIRE-KIT TO POWDER-KEG The two senses of the word are joined by the physics of fire. A tinder-box works because it contains a substance - the charred cloth or dried fungus called tinder - that will catch a spark at the slightest touch and hold it long enough to be blown into flame. The whole object is designed to convert a small spark into a fire, and its function is its meaning: it is the container in which readiness to burn is kept. In the nineteenth century this readiness became a metaphor. A political 'tinderbox' is a situation in which the smallest spark - a rumour, an insult, an accident - can set off a conflagration of conflict, war, or revolution. The metaphor is a perfect one: like the tinder in the box, the grievances, hatreds, and instabilities of the region are dry, volatile, and waiting; what is needed is only a spark. The modern journalistic word, almost always in the solid spelling tinderbox, has carried this image into reports of the Balkans, the Middle East, and every other region judged ready to burst into flame. DISTINCTIONS AND REGISTER Tinder is the fuel alone; flint and steel are the implements; matches are the modern successor, whose box (matchbox) is a lighter and more compact container; lighter names a twentieth-century device; and tinder-box names the whole kit in one word - fuel, striker, and container. In Tolkien's sentence the literal sense is fully alive: losing one's tinder-boxes on a mountain is a genuine small disaster, and the homely, hardware-word dignity of the compound grounds the epic scene in the practical detail of wilderness survival.
