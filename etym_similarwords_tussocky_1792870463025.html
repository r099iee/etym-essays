• tufty: Similar because both describe grass growing in separate clumps rather than smooth sward; differs because tufty is the smaller, garden-scale word for little tufts, while tussocky means big ankle-high hillocks of moor and meadow.
  • hummocky: Similar because both name lumpy uneven ground that trips walkers; differs because hummocky stresses the earth mounds themselves, whether grassy or bare, while tussocky stresses the grass clumps growing on them.
  • reedy: Similar because both describe coarse clump-growing waterside plants in wet ground; differs because reedy names tall hollow-stemmed reeds and rushes by water, while tussocky names grass domes of pasture, moor, and rough field.
  • rank: Similar because both describe coarse overgrown grass that hinders movement; differs because rank stresses tallness, lushness, and neglect, while tussocky stresses the clumped architecture and the lumps underfoot.
  • coarse: Similar because tussock grasses are the textbook coarse grasses, rough to touch; differs because coarse is general texture language for anything rough, while tussocky names the specific clump-growth habit.
  • boggy: Similar because tussocky ground is often also wet and springy between the clumps; differs because boggy names waterlogged softness that sucks at boots, while tussocky names the dry domes that trip them.
  • unkempt: Similar because both suggest shaggy uncut growth, whether of sward or hair; differs because unkempt is a judgement of neglect applied to lawns, verges, and people, while tussocky is a neutral botanical description of a natural growth form.
  • rugged: Similar because both warn that terrain will be hard going; differs because rugged usually means rocky, steep, and grand, while tussocky means low, grassy, and meanly tripping at ankle height.
