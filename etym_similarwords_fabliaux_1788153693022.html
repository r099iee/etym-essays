• Fables: Similar because both are short tales built on stock types and often on beasts. Differs because fables teach a moral with a stated lesson, while fabliaux are amoral comic realism pursuing laughter, not wisdom.
  • Exempla: Similar as short narratives doing one pointed job in a sermon-soaked culture. Differs because exempla serve the preacher’s moral and the conversion of sinners, whereas fabliaux often mock priests and celebrate the trickster.
  • Novelle: Similar as the Italian comic short-story tradition that absorbed the fabliau (Boccaccio’s Decameron). Differs because novelle are prose, later and broader in register — tragedy, romance and jest together — while fabliaux are verse and almost purely comic.
  • Lais: Similar as Old French verse narratives of the same era. Differs because the lai (Marie de France) is courtly, romantic and often supernatural or Breton, while the fabliau is urban, physical and cynical.
  • Farces: Similar as the comic French genre that succeeded the fabliau in the 15th century, sharing its stock characters. Differs because farce is dramatic and staged, while the fabliau is a recited narrative.
  • Tall tales: Similar in spirit — boasting, tricks and laughter at authority. Differs because the modern phrase evokes American oral folklore and vernacular exaggeration rather than medieval Picard verse.
