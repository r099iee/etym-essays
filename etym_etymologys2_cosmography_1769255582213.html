CHAIN 1 — the "world/order" element (kósmos): no secure PIE ancestor is agreed, so the chain begins at the attested Greek word:
Greek kósmos "order, arrangement, ornament; (by philosophical extension) the ordered universe, the world" — first applied to the universe by the Presocratic philosophers, above all the Pythagoreans (the "world" sense attested from the 5th c. BCE; the earlier senses "order, adornment" are attested from Homer, 8th c. BCE). No Indo-European etymology is generally agreed: standard handbooks (Beekes) class kósmos as of uncertain, possibly Pre-Greek origin. No earlier PIE stage is reconstructed here because none is securely established.
→ Greek kosmographía "description of the world" — kósmos + -graphía (attested in Strabo, Geographica, c. 1st c. BCE)

CHAIN 2 — the "write/describe" element (-graphy), traced in isolation:
PIE *gerbh- "to scratch, carve, cut" (reconstructed, c. 4500–3500 BCE; the same root gives English carve, German kerben "to notch")
→ Greek gráphō "to scratch, carve, draw, write" (the verb; attested from Homer)
→ Greek graphḗ "drawing, writing, description, record" (attested 5th c. BCE)
→ Greek -graphía "-writing, -description" (the suffix; as in geōgraphía, kosmographía, biographía)

COMPOUND AND FUSED CHAIN — the two elements join in one word, which then descends to the target:
Greek kosmographía "description of the world" (attested in Strabo, c. 1st c. BCE)
→ Latin cosmographia "description of the world" (attested in the 1st c. CE tradition of Pomponius Mela's De situ orbis, also titled De cosmographia)
→ Old French cosmographie / Middle English cosmographie "description of the world, esp. its heavens and its lands" (attested from the 14th c.)
→ English cosmography ◄ TARGET (present day)

DEBATE / NUANCE: The Greek element kósmos has no certain etymology. Older attempts to connect it to a PIE root of "order" (and to Vedic saṃ- "together" or Sanskrit śaṃs- "to praise, declare") are rejected or judged unproven in modern handbooks; Beekes classes kósmos as of uncertain, probably Pre-Greek origin. Everything from Greek kósmos onward is attested. The second element *gerbh- "to scratch" is standard and well attested across Indo-European (English carve, Old Norse karfa, German kerben, Greek gráphō). The two chains must be kept separate until the Greek compound — they are independent roots that only meet inside kosmographía itself.
