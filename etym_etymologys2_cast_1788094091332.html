Old Norse (9th–13th c.)
kasta
"to throw, cast, fling"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
casten
"to throw, hurl; reckon; allot"
↓
Modern English (14th c.–)
CAST
"to throw; to mould; to assign"
"(n.) a throw, mould, troupe"

NOTE: Old Norse kasta is of uncertain origin — no secure Proto-Indo-European etymon can be established, and the OED marks its derivation as obscure. Cognates in the other Scandinavian languages (Old Swedish kasta, Danish kaste, Norwegian kaste) and in Middle Low German kasten "to throw" point to a common North/West Germanic formation, but earlier ancestry is speculative: some scholars suggest a link to Old Norse kǫst- "heap, pile" (to throw into a heap) — an attractive but unproven hypothesis. The verb is one of the great Scandinavian loanwords of English: native Old English used weorpan "to throw" (cognate with German werfen), displaced by casten after the Viking settlements. The word is a single form serving as verb, past tense, past participle, and noun; in the source context it is the past tense "cast", unchanged in form.
