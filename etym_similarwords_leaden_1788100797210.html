• Heavy: Similar because both mean great weight; differs because heavy is the general word for any weight while leaden names the archetype (lead-weight) and adds dull greyness and inertness; a heavy box is merely heavy, a leaden one feels dead.
  • Weighty: Similar because both imply significant weight; differs because weighty is often figurative for importance or seriousness (a weighty matter), while leaden stays physical, colourless, and oppressive; leaden never means important.
  • Ponderous: Similar because both describe slow, heavy movement or speech; differs because ponderous stresses laborious slowness and clumsiness (and can mean dull and pretentious), while leaden stresses inert weight and the grey heaviness of mood.
  • Dull: Similar because both mean lacking brightness, sharpness, or life; differs because dull is far broader (a dull knife, a dull ear, a dull day) and neutral, while leaden always carries the metal: grey, weighted, oppressively still.
  • Grey / Gray: Similar because both name the colour of overcast things; differs because grey is a neutral colour word with countless shades, while leaden is the specific dull, dark, dead grey of tarnished lead, plus the heaviness the grey implies.
  • Gloomy: Similar because both describe darkness and sadness; differs because gloomy is about darkness and pessimism of mood (a gloomy person, a gloomy forecast), while leaden describes the heavy, still, weighed-down quality that often accompanies gloom.
  • Oppressive: Similar because both describe something pressing down on the spirit; differs because oppressive usually names the effect (oppressive heat, an oppressive regime) and is active in feeling, while leaden names the quality itself — inert, silent, heavy — without the moral overtones.
  • Sluggish: Similar because both mean slow and unresponsive; differs because sluggish is physiological or economic (a sluggish engine, a sluggish market) and tempo-focused, while leaden is heavier, greyer, and more emotional; one recovers from sluggishness, leadenness is a state of weight.
  • Torpid: Similar because both mean inert and benumbed; differs because torpid is near-scientific for dormancy (torpid bears) and implies suspended animation, while leaden implies active heaviness and dullness rather than hibernation.
  • Inert: Similar because both mean without movement or reaction; differs because inert is a neutral technical word (inert gas, inert matter), while leaden is value-laden and literary — inert matter is simply still; leaden stillness is a mood.
  • Sombre / Dreary: Similar because both describe dark, depressing atmospheres; differs because sombre and dreary belong to light, colour, and weather or mood broadly, while leaden anchors the same atmosphere in the concrete image of lead — grey, heavy, silent, unringing.
