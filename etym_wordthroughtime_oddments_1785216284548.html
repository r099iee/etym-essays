- [1780: the word is coined] - oddment is formed from odd ('surplus, extra, unmatched') plus -ment; it names a single odd article, a leftover, an extra - an accounting-book word for what remains after the main supply is gone.
- [late 18th-19th century: the plural wins] - oddments, recorded almost immediately, fixes the word as a synonym of 'odds and ends': miscellaneous remnants of little value; the plural insists on a scatter rather than one curiosity.
- [19th-20th century: a trade sense] - in printing and bookbinding oddments becomes the technical name for leftover sheets and end-matter of a book - a professional use that hardens the idea of surplus material remaining after the main work.
- [20th century-present: the bookish register] - daily conversation prefers 'odds and ends' or 'bits and pieces'; oddments survives in narrative and reflective prose with a faintly old-fashioned, whimsical flavour, still preserving the older sense of odd as unmatched and surplus - each item the third glove of a vanished pair.
