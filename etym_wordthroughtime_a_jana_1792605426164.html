- [Rigvedic & Late Vedic Era (c. 1500–800 BCE)] - root añj and noun añjana designated the sacred anointing of sacrificial posts (yūpa-añjana), ritual soma extraction, and the medicinal anointing of the eyes with sacred ghee and antimony - [perception: revered as a sacred, purificatory ritual act conveying divine luster, spiritual sight, and cosmic order]
- [Epic & Puranic Period (c. 500 BCE–500 CE)] - personified in the Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa, and Brahma Purāṇa as the cosmic guardian bull elephant Añjana, world-bearer of the western quarter, whose descendants form the elite war-elephants of Ayodhyā - [perception: venerated as a primordial, awe-inspiring symbol of cosmic stability, royal power, and immense physical strength]
- [Classical Sanskrit Kāvya Era (c. 4th–10th c. CE)] - celebrated by poets (Kālidāsa, Bhāravi, Māgha) as the quintessential aesthetic metaphor for rich, inky darkness—describing monsoon clouds, night skies, bees, and the kohl-lined eyes of noble women - [perception: celebrated as the supreme poetic standard for glossy darkness and sensual elegance]
- [Medieval Bhakti & Vedantic Philosophy (c. 10th–17th c. CE)] - consolidated into the celebrated philosophical metaphor of jñānāñjana-śalākā (the divine eye-salve of knowledge applied by the Guru), while the feminine Añjanā is venerated as the blessed mother of Hanumān (Āñjaneya) - [perception: venerated spiritually as the instrument of divine illumination and maternal sanctity]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era] - preserved in Ayurvedic pharmacopeia (ocular treatments), classical Indian dance (abhinaya of applying kajal), temple iconography, and Indological studies of Hindu cosmography - [perception: recognized as an enduring cultural emblem linking ancient veterinary myth, cosmetic traditions, and metaphysical allegory]
