• Miles: Similar because both are traditional road-units of roughly a kilometre-plus; differs because the mile is the native English measure (1.609 km), while the verst is its Russian counterpart (1.067 km) with exotic literary colour.
  • Kilometres: Similar because a verst is only slightly longer than a kilometre, inviting direct conversion; differs because kilometres are neutral metric modernity, while versts are imperial, historical, and romantic.
  • Leagues: Similar because both are old literary journey-measures of fairy-tale vastness; differs because a league is roughly three miles of seafaring romance, while a verst is two-thirds of a mile of Russian road.
  • Furlongs: Similar because both are obsolete traditional units kept alive in literature; differs because the furlong (220 yards) measures fields and horseraces, while the verst measures highways and exiles.
  • Sazhens: Similar because both belong to the old Russian measuring system and appear in the same novels; differs because the sazhen (2.13 m) is a short fathom-like unit of depth and building, while the verst (500 sazhens) is the long road unit.
  • Parasangs: Similar because both are exotic Eastern road-measures met in translated tales; differs because the parasang is Persian (about 5–6 km, from Xenophon), while the verst is Russian and far shorter.
  • Li: Similar because both are foreign itinerary units that colour translated literature; differs because the li is Chinese (about half a kilometre), while the verst paces the Russian steppe.
  • Stages: Similar because fairy-tale journeys are often counted in stages as well as versts; differs because a stage is a day's travel between rests, a unit of effort, while a verst is a fixed unit of ground.
  • Milestones: Similar because verst-posts were Russia's milestones, marking the same hope of progress; differs because milestones now mostly mean figurative achievements, while versts still mean literal snowy ground.
