Note on semantics and etymological psychology: The derivation of Latin suādēre ("to advise, recommend") from PIE *swād- ("sweet, pleasant") offers a profound insight into ancient psychology: to advise someone was literally "to make a course of action sweet or attractive to them". The addition of the privative prefix dis- ("away, against") reversed the polarity: dissuādēre was "to render an intended action distasteful, bitter, or unappealing through reasoned argument". This verbal root is direct kin to Latin suavis ("sweet, agreeable", yielding English suave), Greek hēdonē ("pleasure", yielding English hedonism), and Germanic *swōtuz (yielding Old English swēte and Modern English sweet).
