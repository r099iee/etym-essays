• Trellis: Similar because both denote an open framework of crossed wood or metal supporting climbing plants; differs because a trellis is specifically an outdoor garden structure or freestanding vertical screen built for horticultural training, whereas lattice is a much broader architectural, mathematical, and crystallographic term for any crossed regular grid.
  • Grid: Similar because both describe a network of intersecting horizontal and vertical lines; differs because a grid typically features straight perpendicular lines at 90-degree angles forming uniform squares or coordinates (e.g., street grid, electrical grid), while a lattice often features diagonal or diamond-shaped crosshatching and open diamond apertures.
  • Framework: Similar because both provide structural support or scaffolding; differs because framework is a general term for any supporting skeleton, conceptual schema, or structural chassis, without requiring regular crisscrossed strips or repeating points.
  • Mesh: Similar because both consist of intersecting strands forming openings; differs because mesh emphasizes flexible, fine, or pliable material (like wire screening, netting, or fabric), whereas lattice implies rigid, spaced slats, bars, or periodic mathematical arrays.
  • Network: Similar because both comprise interconnected nodes or lines; differs because a network emphasizes connectivity, communication pathways, or functional flow (computer network, vascular network), while lattice denotes geometric symmetry and regular periodic spacing.
  • Grille: Similar because both are perforated metal or wooden barriers; differs because a grille is primarily a protective grating or ornamental vent over a window, door, or vehicle radiator, often composed of parallel or decorative bars rather than diagonal crisscrossed slats.
  • Matrix: Similar because both involve organized arrays of elements; differs because matrix is a mathematical or biological term for an enclosing rectangular array or embedding substance (extracellular matrix), whereas lattice denotes structural crisscrossing or periodic geometric translation symmetry.
