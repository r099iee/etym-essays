• Sniff: Similar because both are sounds made through the nose; differs because a sniff is a quiet, often inquisitive or tearful nasal inhalation, while a snort is a loud, explosive blast of air.
  • Snore: Similar because both are nasal breathing sounds from the same expressive sn- family; differs because snoring is involuntary heavy breathing during sleep, while snorting is a sudden, deliberate or semi-deliberate blast.
  • Grunt: Similar because both are short, harsh, animal-like noises of reaction; differs because a grunt is produced in the throat or chest, while a snort is forced through the nose.
  • Huff: Similar because both express offended or indignant breath; differs because a huff is a sighing or blowing breath of annoyance without the sharp nasal crack of a snort.
  • Sneer: Similar because both express contempt and derision; differs because a sneer is a facial expression (and a verbal taunt), while a snort is the sound and act of nasal dismissal.
  • Harrumph: Similar because both are throat-and-nose sounds of skeptical disapproval; differs because a harrumph is a throaty throat-clearing interjection, while a snort is a sharper, purely nasal explosion.
  • Snicker: Similar because both can express suppressed amusement; differs because a snicker is a half-stifled laugh, while a snort is a breath-forced nasal blast (though one can also "snort with laughter").
  • Snuffle: Similar because both involve noisy nose-breathing; differs because snuffling is wet, wheezy, sniffling breathing, while snorting is a single sharp explosion.
