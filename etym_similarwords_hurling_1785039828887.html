• Flinging: Similar because both mean throwing with force; differs because flinging adds abandonment and a whipping, loose motion, while hurling stresses violent speed and a hard, aimed landing.
  • Throwing: Similar because both denote casting through the air; differs because throwing is the neutral, all-purpose verb, while hurling is its superlative of force and violence.
  • Casting: Similar because both mean projecting something; differs because casting is smooth, deliberate, and often aimed (a fishing line, a net), with no necessary violence, while hurling implies power.
  • Pitching: Similar because both mean throwing at a target; differs because pitching suggests an intended arc and a controlled throw, while hurling implies raw, forceful projection.
  • Tossing: Similar because both mean throwing; differs because tossing is light, careless, and short-range, the exact opposite of hurling's violence and distance.
  • Heaving: Similar because both involve powerful movement of a heavy object; differs because heaving stresses the effort of lifting and letting go, while hurling stresses the speed and violence of the throw.
  • Slinging: Similar because both mean throwing; differs because slinging suggests a loose, whirling motion from the arm, as of a stone from a sling, while hurling is a direct, violent cast.
  • Launching: Similar because both mean sending something through the air; differs because launching suggests a machine, engine, or deliberate push-off, while hurling is pure muscular violence.
  • Catapulting: Similar because both mean projecting with great force; differs because catapulting names mechanical or magical projection from a device, while hurling is a human or animal act.
  • Hurtling: Similar in sound and association; differs because hurtling is intransitive — it is the object that rushes violently through the air — while hurling is transitive, the act of throwing it.
