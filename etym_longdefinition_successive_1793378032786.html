Successive is an adjective describing events, objects, persons, intervals, or units that follow immediately one after another in an unbroken, regular, or systematically ordered chronological, spatial, or numerical sequence. It implies a continuous linear progression where each subsequent member enters the series in direct relation to its predecessor, forming a coherent chain without disruptive intervals.

Primary Modern Meanings & Tactical Dimensions:

  • Chronological Progression Across Time: Most frequently, successive denotes consecutive periods, days, years, or generations unfolding in direct temporal continuation. Phrases such as "for five successive years" or "over successive generations" emphasize both the persistent duration and the sequential continuity of an ongoing phenomenon.
  • Spatial and Tactical Echelonment: In military, naval, and operational tactics (as highlighted in the card's context: "the attack by successive divisions"), successive describes combat echelons, naval columns, or flight waves deployed to strike an adversary in sequential waves. Rather than committing all reserves simultaneously, attacks by successive divisions apply escalating, relentless pressure, exhausting the enemy's frontline while maintaining operational momentum.
  • Hereditary and Dynastic Succession: In political, constitutional, and feudal history, the term applies to the ordered, lawful transfer of an office, crown, title, or estate from one incumbent to the next recognized successor in line (e.g., "successive monarchs of the Tudor dynasty").

Polysemic Scope, Technical Jargon & Domain Applications:

  • Mathematics and Computational Algorithms: In number theory, analysis, and numerical computing, successive refers to consecutive integers ($n, n+1, n+2$) or iterative algorithmic approximations. In numerical analysis, the method of "successive approximations" involves refining a mathematical estimate step-by-step, where each calculation utilizes the output of the preceding step to converge toward an exact solution.
  • Ecological Science ("Successive Stages / Ecological Succession"): In environmental biology and ecosystem dynamics, successive designates the predictable, orderly stages through which an ecological community develops following an environmental disturbance. Pioneer lichens and mosses are replaced by successive communities of grasses, shrubs, and ultimately a mature climax forest.
  • Property Law and Jurisprudence: In trust law and estate planning, successive interests (or successive estates) denote legal rights that take effect sequentially upon the termination of a prior interest—most commonly exemplified by a life interest granted to a surviving spouse followed by a successive remainder interest passing to surviving children.
  • Epistemology and Psychology of Perception: In cognitive philosophy, famously articulated by John Locke and David Hume, human perception of time itself is analyzed as a "successive train of ideas" passing through the conscious mind, where the experience of temporal duration is generated by the sequential replacement of mental impressions.

Semantic Bridges & Historical Evolution:

Etymologically descending from Latin succēdere (compounded from sub-, beneath or from behind, and cēdere, to go or step), the primary physical image of the root was that of a soldier or traveler stepping up close behind another to take his place in line. Over centuries of legal, military, and philosophical adoption in medieval France and Renaissance England, this physical spatial image broadened into abstract temporal continuity. Consequently, successive retains this double power today: describing both the physical march of echelons and the seamless passage of time across history.
