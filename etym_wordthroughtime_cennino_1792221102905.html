- Late 14th / Early 15th c. — Cennino d'Andrea Cennini composes Il Libro dell'Arte in Padua, conceived as an apprentice's handbook recording the secret workshop methods handed down from Giotto through Taddeo and Agnolo Gaddi.
- 16th c. — Acknowledged by Giorgio Vasari in Le Vite de' più eccellenti pittori as an earnest practitioner of Giotto's style who generously documented workshop secrets, though viewed through the High Renaissance lens as technically primitive.
- 18th–Early 19th c. — Long regarded as a forgotten archival manuscript until rediscovered in the Vatican Library and published in Rome by Giuseppe Tambroni in 1821, sparking immediate international interest among antiquarians.
- Mid-to-Late 19th c. — Embraced fervently by the English Pre-Raphaelites, John Ruskin, and the Arts and Crafts movement as the ultimate bible of authentic, uncorrupted medieval craftsmanship and honest artisanal labor.
- 20th–21st c. — Established as an essential cornerstone of technical art history, heritage conservation, and pigment reconstruction, universally known through Daniel V. Thompson's English translation (*The Craftsman's Handbook*).
