- "a pulling, a drawing" - the concrete action sense, the deverbal noun of draw (Old English dræht)
- "a thing drawn: a haul of fish, a drawing of a sword, a breath" - the concrete objects of drawing (Middle English)
- "a current of air drawn through a space" - the chimney and hall draught (16th c.); the sense Tolkien uses
- "a quantity of liquid drawn and drunk" - a draught of ale; draught beer (16th c.)
- "the depth a ship draws; a drawing of water" - nautical sense (16th c.)
- "a preliminary sketch or plan" - the "drawn out" first version; the draft spelling begins to separate (17th c.)
- "the board game played by moving pieces" - draughts, the British name for checkers (16th-17th c.)
- 19th c. - American English collapses all senses into draft; British English keeps draught for air, liquid, depth, and the game; the two spellings diverge in prestige and territory
- modern - "draught" chiefly British and literary; "draft" (document, conscription, beer) dominant in America; Tolkien's draughts of the mountain air keeps the old spelling alive in fantasy prose
