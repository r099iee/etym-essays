• Delicious: Similar because both mean highly pleasing to the taste; differs because delicious is the modern all-purpose word for superb flavour, while toothsome is old-fashioned, literary, and specifically pictures the mouth and the bite.
  • Tasty: Similar because both describe food that tastes good; differs because tasty is plain, colloquial, and general, while toothsome is richer, warmer, and more sensual in register.
  • Appetizing: Similar because both mean pleasing to the appetite; differs because appetizing stresses the power to stimulate hunger in advance of eating, while toothsome stresses the satisfaction of the bite itself.
  • Palatable: Similar because both mean acceptable or agreeable to the taste; differs because palatable is more neutral and can mean merely "edible enough", while toothsome implies actively delightful flavour.
  • Savoury: Similar because both can describe rich, full-flavoured, mouth-watering food; differs because savoury specifically points to the salty, meaty, spicy side of flavour rather than sweetness, while toothsome covers both.
  • Luscious: Similar because both describe intensely pleasing, rich food; differs because luscious suggests soft, juicy, sweet, decadent richness, while toothsome suggests something that invites biting and chewing.
  • Scrumptious: Similar because both are informal words for delicious food; differs because scrumptious is a jocular, almost childish enthusiasm, while toothsome is a more literary, archaic warmth.
  • Yummy: Similar because both mean very pleasing to the taste; differs because yummy is childish and colloquial, while toothsome is adult, literary, and old-fashioned.
  • Mouth-watering: Similar because both describe food that stirs the appetite; differs because mouth-watering emphasises the physical response of salivation before eating, while toothsome emphasises the pleasure of the bite.
  • Moreish: Similar because both describe food so good one keeps eating; differs because moreish (British slang, 20th c.) stresses compulsive repetition, while toothsome stresses intrinsic tastiness.
  • Delectable: Similar because both mean highly pleasing, especially to the taste; differs because delectable is more elevated and refined, while toothsome is heartier and homelier.
  • Succulent: Similar because both describe juicy, satisfying food; differs because succulent stresses moistness and juice, while toothsome stresses the appeal to the tooth and palate generally.
  • Dainty: Similar because both can describe food chosen to please the taste; differs because dainty suggests something delicate, small, and refined, while toothsome suggests something substantial and hearty.
  • Tempting: Similar because both describe food one is drawn to eat; differs because tempting can apply to anything desirable, not only food, while toothsome is anchored in eating and tasting.
