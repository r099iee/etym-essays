• Dogmatic: Similar because both denote rigid adherence to doctrine; differs because a dogmatic person asserts truths without proof and refuses to question them, while a doctrinaire person imposes a doctrine on every case regardless of practical consequences.
  • Ideological: Similar because both are guided by a system of ideas; differs because ideological is the neutral descriptive adjective of an ideology and implies nothing about rigidity, whereas doctrinaire is always pejorative.
  • Inflexible: Similar because both refuse to bend; differs because inflexible describes temperament or method generally, while doctrinaire names the specifically theory-driven form of rigidity.
  • Fanatical: Similar because both show extreme commitment to a doctrine; differs because fanatical carries emotional heat and zealotry, which doctrinaire deliberately lacks — the doctrinaire is cool and systematic.
  • Rigid: Similar because both describe unyielding adherence; differs because rigid is general about stiffness of method or structure, while doctrinaire is specifically about the imposition of ideology.
  • Theoretical: Similar because both concern theory; differs because theoretical describes a concern with theory as such and does not imply imposing it on reality, whereas a doctrinaire forces theory onto every case.
  • Abstract: Similar because both detach from practicalities; differs because abstract is neutral — merely general and non-concrete — whereas doctrinaire condemns the rigid application of the abstraction.
  • Sectarian: Similar because both show loyalty to a party or school; differs because sectarian implies loyalty to a sect or faction, while doctrinaire implies devotion to a doctrine itself.
  • Pedantic: Similar because both are fault-findingly rule-bound; differs because pedantic concerns obsessive attention to details and rules of procedure, while doctrinaire concerns the imposition of an ideology.
  • Hidebound: Similar because both describe narrow, unyielding adherence; differs because hidebound stresses resistance to change from habit or tradition, whereas doctrinaire stresses the active application of a theory.
