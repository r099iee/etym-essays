• Impeded: Similar because both mean "slowed or obstructed"; differs because impede is more formal and technical, typically of processes and movement, while hinder is the everyday word for people and actions.
  • Obstructed: Similar because both mean "blocked"; differs because obstruct implies a physical or official barrier in the way (obstructed view, obstructing justice), while hinder implies making progress difficult rather than impossible.
  • Hampered: Similar because both mean "made difficult"; differs because hamper suggests tangling or encumbering (hampered by a heavy coat), a more physical image than hinder.
  • Blocked: Similar because both describe stopping progress; differs because blocked means completely sealed off, while hindered means slowed but not necessarily stopped.
  • Thwarted: Similar because both describe frustrating someone's efforts; differs because thwart implies deliberate opposition to a plan (thwarted by the enemy), while hinder may be entirely accidental.
  • Foiled: Similar because both mean "prevented from succeeding"; differs because foiled implies a plan defeated at the last moment — more dramatic and complete than hindered.
  • Frustrated: Similar because both mean "prevented from achieving"; differs because frustrated emphasises the resulting disappointment of the person, while hindered focuses on the obstruction itself.
  • Delayed: Similar because both mean "made late or slow"; differs because delayed is purely about time, while hindered is about difficulty and obstruction generally.
  • Encumbered: Similar because both mean "weighed down and slowed"; differs because encumber suggests a heavy burden carried (encumbered with baggage), while hinder is more general.
  • Inhibited: Similar because both mean "held back"; differs because inhibit is used of internal restraints (inhibited by fear) and of chemical or biological processes, while hinder covers external obstacles too.
  • Stymied: Similar because both mean "prevented from progressing"; differs because stymie is informal, American, and implies complete bafflement or deadlock — stronger than hindered.
  • Hobbled: Similar because both mean "made to move with difficulty"; differs because hobble is a vivid metaphor of a tied leg (hobbled by debt), more graphic than hinder.
  • Restrained: Similar because both mean "held back from action"; differs because restrain implies deliberate control or physical holding, while hinder implies an obstacle without intention.
  • Checked: Similar because both mean "stopped or slowed"; differs because check implies a sudden arrest of progress (checked in his career), more abrupt than hinder.
  • Prevented: Similar because both involve stopping action; differs because prevented means the action did not happen at all, while hindered means it happened only with difficulty.
