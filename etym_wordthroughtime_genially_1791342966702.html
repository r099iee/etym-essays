- Late 16th c. "nuptially; in relation to marriage or generation" - reflecting Latin genialis (belonging to birth, the marriage bed, or the tutelary genius); formal and ceremonial.
- Mid-to-late 17th c. "beneficently, invigoratingly; with mild life-giving warmth" - applied in natural philosophy to seasons, soils, and climates that foster vegetative growth.
- 18th c. "festively, jovially, merrily" - influenced by the classical idiom genio indulgere (to indulge one's genius, i.e., feast well and enjoy life); associated with table-cheer and hospitable entertainment.
- 19th c. "with pleasant warmth, affability, and sunny cordiality" - the modern social sense blooms in Victorian prose (Dickens, Thackeray), characterizing good-natured gentlemen and hospitable hosts.
- 20th–21st c. "in an easygoing, pleasantly cheerful manner" - standard literary and narrative adverb; often used with affectionate irony in comedic fiction to contrast carefree bonhomie with surrounding chaos.
