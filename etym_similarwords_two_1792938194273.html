• Pair: Similar because both denote exactly two things together; differs because pair stresses the matched set (a pair of shoes), while two merely counts, matched or not.
  • Couple: Similar because both name two of something, often people; differs because couple suggests union or romance (a married couple), while two is neutral arithmetic.
  • Both: Similar because both lamps and two lamps pick out the same brace; differs because both emphasises the two together as a known set, while two simply states the number.
  • Twain: Similar because it is the archaic poetic twin of two; differs because twain survives only in scripture and verse (cleft in twain), while two does everyday counting.
  • Double: Similar because doubling makes two of one; differs because double stresses multiplication or duplication (a double portion), while two states the resulting count.
  • Duplicate: Similar because a duplicate gives you two copies; differs because duplicate stresses exact likeness to an original, while two counts without judging likeness.
  • Binary: Similar because binary is the system built on twoness; differs because binary is technical (two states, on and off), while two is universal and pre-technical.
  • Duo: Similar because a duo is two performers; differs because duo is borrowed performance-talk (a musical duo), while two belongs to every register.
  • Brace: Similar because a brace is two of a kind, especially game birds; differs because brace is sporting and countrified (a brace of pheasants), while two counts anything.
