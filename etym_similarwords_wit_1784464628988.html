• Humor: Similar because both name the capacity to be amusing; differs because humour is broad, warm, and often kind comic effect, whereas wit is sharp, fast, intellectual, and verbal, founded on quick perception.
  • Intellect: Similar because both denote mental power; differs because intellect is the serious, systematic capacity for reasoning, while wit is rapid, inventive cleverness with a playful or social edge.
  • Intelligence: Similar because both denote cognitive ability; differs because intelligence is a general and neutral measure of mental ability, while wit emphasizes quickness, agility, and verbal flair.
  • Cleverness: Similar because both denote mental quickness; differs because cleverness applies to practical problem-solving and even cunning, whereas wit is specifically bright and usually amusing.
  • Wisdom: Similar because both are prized mental qualities; differs because wisdom is deep, slow, experiential sound judgment, while wit is instantaneous sparkle — the two are proverbially contrasted ("wit without wisdom").
  • Repartee: Similar because both involve clever verbal play; differs because repartee is specifically the rapid witty reply in dialogue, a performance of wit, while wit is the underlying faculty.
  • Banter: Similar because both are playful verbal exchange; differs because banter is light, friendly, teasing talk between equals, while wit is the intellectual sharpness that may or may not be playful.
  • Jest: Similar because both touch the comic; differs because a jest is a joke or quip — a concrete act — while wit is the mental capacity that produces the jest.
  • Sense: Similar because the plural "wits" approximates "senses"; differs because sense is everyday practical judgment, while wits retain the archaic flavour of the mental faculties and composure.
  • Ingenuity: Similar because both denote cleverness; differs because ingenuity is inventive, constructive cleverness applied to making or solving, while wit is verbal and social.
