Derivation chain for DOTED (Proto-Germanic expressive base; no securely established PIE etymon):
  • Expressive base *dut(t)- / *dott- "to nod, doze, be silly" (Proto-Germanic / Low German region) - an onomatopoeic or expressive base echoing the nodding of a drowsy or senile head; origin obscure, with no secure PIE ancestry.
  • Middle Low German doten "to be silly, be foolish" and Middle Dutch doten "to be silly, be foolish" (medieval North Sea dialects); modern Dutch dutten "to doze, snooze" and dut "a doze, a nap" continue the family, as does Icelandic dotta "to nod, doze, slumber".
  • Middle English doten "to behave foolishly, to act as one in one's second childhood" (13th century) - borrowed from the continental forms.
  • English dote (intransitive) "to be foolishly and excessively fond (on/upon)" (semantic shift, 14th century) - from "to be silly with age" to "to love without judgment".
  • English doted, the past tense and past participle (14th century; also the participial adjective doted/doting "foolishly fond; weak in mind with age").
  • DOTED (the form on this card) - the past tense of dote, used in the collocation "dote on".
Related family members: dotage (14th century) "senile feebleness; foolish fondness" and dotard (14th century) "a senile, foolish old person". Debated / nuanced points: no reliable link to PIE *dhewh2- "to breathe, smoke" or to *dheu- "to run, flow" has been demonstrated; the OED marks the etymology of dote as uncertain, so the chain begins at the Germanic / Low German expressive base and does not pretend to a PIE root core.
