• Extinguish: Similar because both mean "put out (fire or light)"; differs because extinguish is the formal, general term and cannot be used of thirst.
  • Douse: Similar because both mean "put out with water"; differs because douse specifies drenching and is more physical, usually of flames only.
  • Smother: Similar because both mean "put out"; differs because smother means suffocate by cutting off air (blanket, foam) and also means "to overwhelm".
  • Stifle: Similar because both mean "suppress"; differs because stifle implies suffocation of breath or sound and is internal, not used of fires or thirst.
  • Slake: Similar because both mean "satisfy (thirst)"; differs because slake is archaic-literary and applies only to thirst or lust, never to fire.
  • Satisfy: Similar because both mean "meet a need"; differs because satisfy is the broad, neutral word for any need, with no fire image.
  • Sate: Similar because both mean "fill up"; differs because sate implies overindulgence to excess, usually of appetite.
  • Assuage: Similar because both mean "relieve"; differs because assuage is formal and used of pain, fear, or grief, not fire or thirst.
  • Allay: Similar because both mean "calm, reduce"; differs because allay applies to fears, doubts, and pain, never to fire or thirst.
  • Appease: Similar because both mean "pacify"; differs because appease is used of people and anger, and has political connotations of concession.
  • Abate: Similar because both mean "reduce"; differs because abate describes gradual lessening (storms, noise), not sudden extinction.
  • Cool: Similar because both mean "lower temperature"; differs because cool is gradual and partial, while quench implies sudden, complete extinction.
  • Dampen: Similar because both mean "reduce intensity"; differs because dampen is partial ("dampen enthusiasm"), never final.
  • Put out: Similar because both mean "extinguish"; differs because put out is the plain phrasal verb, colloquial, and also means "to annoy".
  • Snuff out: Similar because both mean "extinguish abruptly"; differs because snuff out evokes pinching a candle flame and colloquially means "to kill".
  • Suppress: Similar because both mean "put down, prevent expression"; differs because suppress implies authority or force and is abstract.
  • Temper: Similar because both describe heat-treating metal; differs because tempering is a slower reheating-and-cooling process, while quenching is the sudden plunge.
  • Kill (colloquial): Similar because both can mean "destroy utterly"; differs because kill is broad, violent slang in this use, with none of the water-versus-fire image.
