• Absorbed: Closest synonym in the physical and figurative senses; it stresses the image of liquid drawn into a larger body. It differs in being more concrete and less sociological: a sponge absorbs water, a firm absorbs a rival, but a minority is assimilated only when it also changes to resemble the majority.
  • Integrated: Similar in describing the joining of parts into a whole; differs crucially in allowing the parts to remain distinct. Integration is the modern policy ideal, assimilation the older and more forceful one.
  • Incorporated: Similar as “taken into a body”; differs in being legal and corporate, and in implying no change of character — an incorporated subsidiary keeps its own name and culture.
  • Acculturated: Similar in the cultural sense; differs in denoting the adoption of traits without full absorption into the host group. One can be acculturated yet socially separate, whereas assimilation implies entrance into the majority’s primary groups.
  • Amalgamated: Similar as “combined into one”; differs in implying the merging of two organisations or groups into a new whole, rather than the one-sided absorption of a minority by a majority.
  • Digested: Similar through the food metaphor; differs in emphasising the breaking-down of what is taken in. Ideas are digested when they are thought through, not merely absorbed.
  • Internalised: Similar in the cognitive sense; differs in locating the process inside the mind, and in typically referring to norms and values rather than to persons.
  • Naturalised: Similar in the civic sense; differs in being a legal status (citizenship) that does not require cultural sameness.
  • Homogenised: Similar in implying growing uniformity; differs in describing the result for the whole mixture rather than the fate of one ingredient, and often carries a critical tone.
  • Blended: Similar as “mixed into one”; differs in suggesting a harmonious mixture that retains something of both sources, the opposite of the effacement implied by assimilation.
  • Subsumed: Similar in the logical sense of being taken under a larger category; differs in being abstract and argumentative rather than social or biological.
  • Co-opted: Similar in the political sense of being absorbed by a dominant system; differs in stressing that the absorbed party is recruited and neutralised, usually with a hint of betrayal.
  • Conformed: Similar in outward resemblance to the majority; differs in referring only to behaviour and rules, not to identity or belonging.
  • Reabsorbed: Similar as “taken back in”; differs in presupposing prior membership — a lapsed member is reabsorbed, whereas an outsider is assimilated.
