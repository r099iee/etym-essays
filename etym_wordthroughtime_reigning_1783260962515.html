- [Middle English royal sense] - In its current form since Middle English, 'reigning' has always meant currently holding royal office - the present occupant of the throne, as opposed to a future or former holder.
- [Distinct from ruling] - Through the centuries the word kept its precise constitutional nuance: a reigning monarch holds the title and office, while a ruling monarch actively exercises government - a distinction sharpened in legal and historical prose.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'reigning' is used both of actual monarchs (the reigning queen) and, by extension, of current champions (the reigning world champion), still carrying the sense of 'presently holding the title'.
