Origin of English "pad" (soft cushion) — origin debated; the "cushion" and "path" senses may belong to one word family or to two distinct words. Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *pent- "to tread, go, pass along" (reconstructed root; the proposed ancestor of the Germanic "path" family). NOTE: this link is uncertain; *paþaz has no sure cognates outside Germanic. Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *paþaz / *patha- "trodden track, path" (reconstructed, 1st millennium BCE). Stage 3 — Middle Dutch pad / Low German pad "path, way" (attested from the 13th century); in Low German the same word also develops the transferred sense "sole of the foot" — the soft instrument of treading. Stage 4 — English pad "soft cushion, wad, stuffed bag" (first recorded c. 1553), borrowed from Low German / Dutch in the "soft sole / cushion" sense; the OED marks the origin "obscure." Stage 5 — English pad extended to "the fleshy cushion under an animal's foot" (natural-history writing, 18th-19th centuries). Stage 6 — pads, plural noun, the standard modern form ("the spread pads of dogs"). Debate note: The connection between the "path" word and the "cushion" word is not fully secure; some lexicographers treat pad "cushion" as a word of unknown origin, and the PIE *pent- ancestor of *paþaz is itself disputed (no reliable non-Germanic cognates are known). These uncertainties are genuine and are stated here rather than glossed over.
