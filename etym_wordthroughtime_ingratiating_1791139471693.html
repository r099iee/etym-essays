- 1620s coinage - to ingratiate oneself with: courtly art of getting into favour; neutral-to-admired skill in patronage world
- Late 1600s participle - ingratiating smile, address: deliberately flattering; already tinged with servility
- 1700s moral condemnation - Chesterfield and Johnson treat ingratiating arts as fawning, unmanly; polite disapproval
- 1800s novelistic use - ingratiating servants, suitors, clerks; stock character trait signalling social climbing
- Contemporary dual sense - calculated sycophancy (dominant, negative) versus naturally pleasing quality (secondary, positive); social psychology adopts ingratiation as technical tactic
