babble denotes inarticulate, rapid, copious and often meaningless utterance, and by extension any confused murmur resembling such utterance. As a verb it means to talk incessantly and incoherently, to prattle, to reveal secrets by chattering, and of infants to produce repetitive consonant-vowel sequences like ba-ba-ba in rhythmic play. As a noun it means such talk, and also the gurgling of a brook.

The infantile sense is developmental and affectionate: canonical babbling at six to ten months, reduplicated then variegated, is practice for speech, enjoyed for rhythm and motor pleasure. Parents delight in babble as proto-language. The text captures this: the infant babbles in rhythm, the child repeats words as toys. Here babble is not failure but play, the raw joy of sound before sense.

The adult sense is usually pejorative: to babble is to talk foolishly, to blabber without content, to emit empty political or drunken chatter. To babble out or babble forth is to disclose secrets through loose talk. Rivers, fountains and crowds are said to babble by metaphor: continuous low murmuring without message. Computers babble when they emit logorrheic output; brooks babble in poetry.

  • Infant vocal play: to utter ba-ba, da-da, ma-ma repetitively, practicing articulation and intonation.
  • Incoherent adult talk: to speak rapidly and unintelligibly through excitement, fear, fever or infancy of thought.
  • Foolish empty chatter: to prattle on without sense or substance, to talk idly and at length.
  • Indiscreet revelation: to blurt secrets, to babble out plans, to betray by talkativeness.
  • Murmuring sound of water: of streams and fountains to make a continuous bubbling murmur; noun: the babble of the brook.
  • Confused mixture of voices: the babble of the market, the Tower of Babel echo, many tongues at once.

Bridges between senses rest on sound symbolism. Reduplicated ba-ba imitates both baby lips and bubbling water: lips parting and water gurgling make similar intermittent bursts. From baby sound to foolish adult talk is evaluative transfer: adult nonsense is like baby pre-sense. From human to water is acoustic metaphor ratified by poets. From mere noise to betrayal adds a social judgment: uncontrolled flow, whether of water or words, leaks what should be contained.

Register is flexible. Of babies babble is tender and scientific. Of brooks it is literary and soothing. Of adults it is dismissive: do not babble, stop babbling. Blab emphasizes betrayal, prattle emphasizes childishness, chatter emphasizes lightness, gabble emphasizes speed, gibber emphasizes madness, babble covers all with focus on unintelligibility and flow. The Lorenz-adjacent context redeems it: babbling in rhythm is musical, exploratory play, the vacuum behavior of language itself, happening for its own pleasure.

Further depth: developmentalists distinguish reflexive vegetative sounds, cooing, canonical reduplicated babbling and variegated jargon babble with sentence intonation but no words. Deaf infants babble vocally less and manually in sign, proving babble is modality-specific practice, not mere noise. Clinicians screen for absent babble by ten months as early risk for autism or hearing loss. Poets from Wordsworth to Hughes redeem babble as holy prattle, while information theory reframes adult media babble as high-entropy low-signal flow, the auditory equivalent of static that nevertheless comforts like a brook.
