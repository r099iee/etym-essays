• Waterfall: Similar because both name a river falling over a ledge or step; differs because "waterfall" is the neutral everyday generic, while "cataract" implies a huge volume, a violent plunge, and a literary or geographical register.
  • Falls: Similar because the everyday American term for a river's descent (Niagara Falls); differs because it usually names named sites in the plural and carries none of the medical sense.
  • Cascade: Similar because a descent of water in stages; differs because "cascade" is small, graceful and often artificial (fountains, garden features), whereas a cataract is large, violent and natural.
  • Rapids: Similar because fast, broken, turbulent river water over rock; differs because rapids need no vertical drop and may be navigable, while a cataract is an impassable plunge or barrier.
  • Torrent: Similar because a violent rushing flood of water; differs because a torrent need not fall over a precipice and is the word for rain-floods, used figuratively of emotions and speech.
  • Deluge: Similar because a great inundation of water; differs because it suggests catastrophic, widespread flooding (and the biblical Flood), not a defined waterfall.
  • Portcullis / floodgate: Similar because the archaic sense of a grating that drops to block a gate or water; differs because they are engineered mechanisms, now obsolete as synonyms.
  • Clouding / opacity: Similar because the underlying symptom of the eye disease; differs because they describe the condition, while "cataract" names the disease entity.
  • Glaucoma: Similar because another common blinding eye disease; differs because glaucoma is raised pressure damaging the optic nerve, not a lens opacity — and its name comes from Greek "grey", not from water.
