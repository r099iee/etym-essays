Composite word - two independent chains: CHAIN 1 (inter-): 1. PIE *h₁en "in" (preposition/adverb). 2. PIE *h₁enter "between, among" - *h₁en + the contrastive/comparative suffix *-ter. 3. Latin inter "between, among" (preposition, attested from early Latin) - source of the productive prefix inter-. 4. English inter- (prefix) "between, among, mutually" - borrowed wholesale as a Latin prefix (international, intervene, interlock). CHAIN 2 (twine): 1. PIE *dwóh₁ "two" (cardinal numeral) - the root of all "two" words across Indo-European (Greek duo, Latin duo, Sanskrit dva). 2. Proto-Germanic *twi- "two; double, twofold" (combining form). 3. Old English twin (noun) "twisted thread, a double thread" - literally "a two-fold strand", a thread made of threads; with the verb twinan "to twist, to twine". 4. Middle English twine (noun) "a cord or thread of twisted fibres" and twinen (verb) "to twist together". 5. English twine (noun and verb) "a strong thread or cord made by twisting strands together; to twist or wind about". 6. English intertwine (inter- + twine), first recorded 1640, "to twist together, to twine one with another". 7. English intertwined (past participle used as adjective) "twisted or woven together; closely connected while remaining partly distinct" - the composite form of the two chains.
