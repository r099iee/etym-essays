- Mycenaean & Archaic Greek (c. 8th c. BCE) - The wooden walking staff (*skeptron*) leaned upon by judges, heralds, and tribal elders as a token of sacred, inviolable speech.
- Classical & Hellenistic Greece (5th–2nd c. BCE) - Sacred badge of kings and Olympian deities representing divinely sanctioned judicial rule.
- Roman Republic & Empire (1st c. BCE – 4th c. CE) - Consular and imperial ivory wand (*sceptrum*), signifying military imperium and triumph.
- Medieval Christendom (5th–15th c.) - Integral coronation regalia crowned with a cross or dove, embodying temporal stewardship under God.
- Early Modern Era (16th–18th c.) - Solidified in political philosophy and literature as a universal metonym for monarchical rule and sovereign power.
- Modern / Contemporary - Chiefly ceremonial regalia, symbolic of hereditary monarchy, or used figuratively for dominant leadership and authority.
