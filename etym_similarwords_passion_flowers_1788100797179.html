• flowers: Similar because passion-flowers are flowers; differs because “flowers” is fully generic, while “passion-flowers” names a specific genus carrying a heavy symbolic and theological load.
  • blooms: Similar because both name blossoms; differs because “bloom” stresses the state of opening and freshness (and the verb “to bloom”), without species reference or symbolism; passion-flowers may be in bloom but a bloom is never symbolic in the same way.
  • blossoms: Similar because both mean flowers, often in clusters on shrubs and trees; differs because “blossom” is smaller-scale and orchard- or spring-associated, whereas passion-flowers are large, tropical and vine-borne.
  • Passiflora: Similar because it is the scientific name of the same plant; differs because it is the Latinate genus name (Linnaeus, 1753) used in botany and horticulture, free of the devotional associations the English name carries.
  • maypop: Similar because it is the folk name of the American species Passiflora incarnata; differs because “maypop” is a regional colloquialism (from the fruit’s popping when trodden) and names a species, not the genus and not the emblematic flower.
  • granadilla: Similar because it is the older Spanish name for the passion fruit and its flower-bearing vine; differs because it refers chiefly to the fruit (“little pomegranate”) and belongs to Spanish colonial usage, while “passion-flower” is the English devotional term for the blossom.
  • passion fruit: Similar because it is produced by the same vines and takes its name from the same symbolism; differs because it denotes the edible fruit, not the flower, and has its own colonial and culinary history.
  • clematis: Similar because it is a showy climbing vine with many-flowered displays, a garden twin; differs because clematis has no corona, no fruit of note, and carries no Passion symbolism — the resemblance is purely horticultural.
  • hibiscus: Similar because it is another large, trumpet-like, showy tropical flower with a central column of stamens; differs because hibiscus is a different family (Malvaceae), with five petals, no corona and no religious naming.
  • liana: Similar because passion-flowers grow as lianas (woody climbers); differs because “liana” names the growth habit, not the flower or the plant identity — a liana need not flower at all.
