• Equivocating: Similar because both denote using ambiguous, double-edged language to conceal the truth; differs because 'equivocating' (from Latin aequi- + vocare, 'equal voice') specifically focuses on using words with multiple logical interpretations to avoid commitment, whereas 'paltering' often implies a more contemptible, petty, or evasive dodging of moral reality ('paltering with the truth').
  • Prevaricating: Similar because both involve evading the straightforward truth without necessarily uttering a direct lie; differs because 'prevaricating' (literally 'walking crookedly' from Latin varicare) emphasizes straying from the line of honesty, often in formal or legal interrogations, whereas 'paltering' carries an older tone of trifling, haggling insincerity.
  • Quibbling: Similar because both describe raising petty, evasive objections; differs because 'quibbling' focuses on trivial linguistic distinctions, minor flaws, or pedantic hair-splitting, whereas 'paltering' involves fundamental insincerity regarding significant truths.
  • Haggling: Similar because in its historical sense, 'paltering' meant bargaining or dealing in petty merchandise; differs because 'haggling' is strictly focused on negotiating the price of goods or terms of a contract, whereas modern 'paltering' is predominantly moral and linguistic.
  • Hedging: Similar because both involve avoiding a direct commitment or statement; differs because 'hedging' is a defensive strategy to limit financial risk or protect one's reputation in case of error, whereas 'paltering' conveys moral evasiveness or deceit.
  • Dithering: Similar because both involve indecisiveness; differs because 'dithering' arises from nervous confusion, anxiety, or paralysis of will, whereas 'paltering' is calculated, evasive, or insincere.
  • Lying: Similar because both mislead the listener; differs because 'lying' involves stating an outright factual falsehood, whereas 'paltering' often uses technically true or half-true statements structured deceptively to create a false impression.
  • Terversating: Similar because both denote changing one's position or speaking evasively; differs because 'tergiversating' is an ultra-rare, high-academic term denoting apostasy or outright desertion of a cause or principle, whereas 'paltering' is standard literary English.
