- Early 17th Century (c. 1620s–1640s) - West Country English Dialect: Emerged in southwestern England (notably Devonshire) as an expressive colloquial noun for a violent tumult, noisy tavern quarrel, or tumultuous brawl; perceived as an earthy, rustic term for boisterous, headstrong public commotion.
- Mid-17th Century (c. 1650s) - Caribbean Colonial Plantation Emergence: Carried across the Atlantic by English settlers, indentured servants, and mariners to the island of Barbados, where it was adopted as the name for the rough, fiery new spirit distilled from sugarcane molasses ("rumbullion, alias kill-devil"); perceived as a scorching, intoxicating, and dangerous liquor of extreme proof that incited violent brawls.
- Late 17th to 18th Century - Lexical Clipping and Colonial Export: Quickly clipped by merchants and sailors to the punchy monosyllable *rum* (first recorded c. 1654), which became the official international naval and commercial standard; the parent word *rumbullion* faded from daily colloquial speech, retreating into archival legal records, regional dialect, and maritime histories.
- 19th Century - Antiquarian and Philological Rediscovery: Excavated by Victorian etymologists, naval historians, and dialectologists investigating the etymology of *rum* and oceanic folklore; viewed as an interesting linguistic fossil and picturesque specimen of early colonial buccaneer vocabulary.
- Early to Mid-20th Century - Lexicographical Curiosity: Maintained in comprehensive dictionaries and historical treatises as an obsolete, quaint archaism; occasionally cited in educational texts illustrating clipping and folk etymology alongside *chum* and *bus*.
- Contemporary Era (Late 20th to 21st Century) - Craft Distilling and Mixology Renaissance: Re-emerged in high-end spirits marketing, craft rum branding, and historic cocktail scholarship as a prestigious emblem of authentic, heavy-pot-still Caribbean distillation, while in historical fiction and literary prose it is occasionally summoned to depict rowdy, rollicking period brawls.
