• hate: Similar because both express strong aversion to someone or something; differs because hate is broader, hotter, and far more common in everyday speech, while detest implies a more reasoned, formal, and settled aversion.
  • loathe: Similar because both mean extreme dislike; differs because loathe carries visceral, physical revulsion or nausea, while detest is more intellectual and moral — you can detest an idea but loathe a smell.
  • abhor: Similar because both mean to regard with repugnance; differs because abhor suggests shrinking away in moral horror, while detest is a colder, more deliberate verdict.
  • despise: Similar because both are strong negative feelings; differs because despise adds contempt — looking down on something as inferior — whereas detest may involve no feeling of superiority, only aversion.
  • execrate: Similar because both can mean to denounce violently; differs because execrate retains the archaic sense of formal cursing and is rarer and harsher.
  • abominate: Similar because both express intense hatred; differs because abominate is very formal and suggests disgust of a quasi-religious kind, and is far less common than detest.
  • dislike: Similar because both are negative feelings toward someone or something; differs because dislike is mild, casual, and everyday, sitting at the opposite end of the intensity scale from detest.
  • scorn: Similar because both signal rejection of a person or thing; differs because scorn is open contempt and derision directed downward, while detest is a private inner feeling that need not be displayed.
  • repudiate: Similar because both involve rejecting something strongly; differs because repudiate is an action (to refuse, disown, or disavow) whereas detest is a state of feeling.
