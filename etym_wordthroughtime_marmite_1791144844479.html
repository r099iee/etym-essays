- 14th c. French “pot; also painted pot-face, hypocrite” - marmite as kitchen vessel, with a joking homonym for a sly face on a pot; humble, domestic.
- 17th–18th c. “the bourgeois simmerer” - petite marmite (pot and soup) enters classic cookery; respectable, nourishing, thumbnail of French table culture.
- 19th c. English literary borrowing - travellers and historians (the source passage’s world) use marmite for local colour; faintly comic-exotic in English ears.
- 1902 “the spread” - British Marmite yeast extract named for its pot-shaped jar; nursery, ration-pack, and toast-rack staple; affection and disgust from the start.
- 1990s–present “the metaphor” - “Love it or hate it” ads coin the Marmite effect; “Marmite” becomes shorthand for anything divisive — the pot’s name now seasons politics, pop, and people.
