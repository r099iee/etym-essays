PIE *slebh- "to be slack, hang loose, be limp" (reconstructed; IEW 656; with variants *lebh- / *lēbh-; the root shows an s-mobile) → Latin lābī "to glide, slip, fall" (deponent verb; initial *s- lost before *l-: *slabh- > *lāb-; attested in classical Latin) → Latin ēlābī "to slip out, glide away" (ex- "out of" + lābī; attested in classical Latin, e.g. of time and of life slipping away) → Latin ēlapsus "having slipped away" (past participle; the -laps- stem also underlies lapsus "a fall, slip") → English elapse "to pass away (of time)" (direct Latin borrowing, c. 1590) → English ELAPSE "to pass, go by (of time)" (modern; as on this card) DEBATED / NUANCED STAGES: (1) The connection of Latin lābī to PIE *slebh- is traditional and broadly accepted (de Vaan accepts the family lābī, lapsus, lābilis under *slebh-), but the PIE root is reconstructed with an s-mobile and its exact vocalic grade is debated; the Latin loss of initial *s- before *l- is standard historical phonology. (2) ANTI-MERGE GUARD: English slip is NOT from this root; slip descends from PIE *sleib- "slippery" (Old English slīpan), a separate root — the "slip" words and the "slack/sleep" family must not be merged. (3) The Germanic "sleep" words (Proto-Germanic *slēpaną, English sleep, German schlafen) are often grouped with *slebh- as "to fall slack, be limp", but this is debated; they are recorded here as uncertain collateral kin, not as ancestors of elapse.
