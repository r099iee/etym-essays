• Caledonians: Similar because they were the Roman-era northern British tribes; differs because Caledonians is the earlier (1st–2nd c.) Roman name, Picts the later (3rd c. onward) successor label.
  • Britons: Similar because they were Celtic inhabitants of Britain; differs because Britons usually means southern Roman-British, Picts the unconquered northerners.
  • Scots: Similar because they were Gaelic neighbours who merged with Picts; differs because Scots came from Ireland (Dál Riata), Picts were native northern Britons.
  • Gaels: Similar because they shared Gaelic Christianity and Alba; differs because Gaels spoke Goidelic, Picts originally spoke Brittonic.
  • Irish: Similar because they were Insular Celtic peoples cited by Durant; differs because Irish lived in Ireland, Picts in Scotland.
  • Iberians: Similar because they were ancient tribal peoples in Durant's cannibal list; differs because Iberians lived in Spain, Picts in Scotland.
  • Danes: Similar because they were northern raiders in Durant's list; differs because Danes were Viking-era Scandinavians, Picts Iron-Age Britons.
  • Vikings: Similar because they raided Britain by sea; differs because Vikings were 8th–11th c. Scandinavians, Picts earlier native defenders.
  • Saxons: Similar because they were early-medieval invaders of Britain; differs because Saxons were Germanic settlers of the south and east, Picts Celtic natives of the north.
  • Celts: Similar because Picts were Celtic-speaking; differs because Celts is the vast language-family umbrella, Picts one specific confederation.
  • Druids: Similar because they evoke Celtic mystery; differs because druids were priests, Picts a whole people.
  • Attacotti: Similar because they were allies of Picts accused of cannibalism by Jerome; differs because Attacotti were a specific war-band, Picts the wider nation.
