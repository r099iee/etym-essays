- Roman law — decidere means to cut off, physically and judicially: severing heads of argument in rhetoric and causes in court.
- Late 14th c. — English borrows deciden: to resolve, to determine, a learned word of clerks, courts, and chroniclers.
- 16th–17th c. — the general mind adopts it: Shakespeare's characters decide fates and quarrels, and divines decide cases of conscience.
- 18th–19th c. — institutions multiply it: courts decide suits, umpires decide matches, voters decide elections; the decider match is born.
- 20th c. — the pollster's noun: the undecided voter becomes a political species, and to decide is the hinge of every thriller and boardroom.
- Today — neutral and total: from algorithms that decide loan applications to friends deciding where to eat, the word covers every cut from the trivial to the fateful.
