Note on historical nuance: English birch and Sanskrit bhūrja (the Himalayan birch, Betula utilis, whose bark bhūrja-patra is the exact writing medium described in the context) are direct linguistic cognates descending from the same Proto-Indo-European root *bʰerHg̑- ("to shine, gleam, be white"). Both prehistoric Indo-European branches independently preserved this exact tree name because of the striking, radiant, chalk-white or silvery peeling bark that distinguished it from all other northern forest flora. The root also produced Russian berëza (берёза), Lithuanian béržas, Latvian bērzs, and Ossetian bærz. In Germanic, Proto-Germanic *berkōn- gave Old Norse bjǫrk, Old High German birka, and Dutch berk. In Old English, palatalization of the final velar consonant after a front vowel shifted *birkī to birce / bierce, producing the modern English soft affricate /tʃ/.
