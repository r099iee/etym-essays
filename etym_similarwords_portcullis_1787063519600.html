• Drawbridge: Similar because both are kinetic, mechanical fortifications controlling ingress at a gatehouse; differs because a drawbridge is a horizontal platform hinged to pivot upward across a moat, whereas a portcullis is a vertical lattice dropped downward along stone tracks.
  • Barbican: Similar because both defend the gateway of a castle; differs because a barbican is an entire defensive outer fortification or fortified gatehouse projection, whereas a portcullis is a specific sliding mechanical gate within that structure.
  • Yett: Similar because both are defensive grilles of interlocking wrought-iron bars used in medieval British castles (especially Scottish tower houses); differs because a yett is a hinged, swinging gate, whereas a portcullis operates exclusively on a vertical drop-and-lift axis.
  • Guillotine Gate: Similar because both utilize vertical sliding motion to shut an opening; differs because a guillotine gate is a modern industrial or hydraulic apparatus (or execution blade), whereas a portcullis denotes a historical, fortified architectural lattice.
  • Palisade: Similar because both present an impassable barrier of sharpened stakes; differs because a palisade is a static, continuous outdoor wooden fence driven into the earth, lacking the vertical mechanical mobility of a portcullis.
  • Sluice Gate: Similar because both slide vertically in grooves to block a conduit or stream; differs because a sluice gate is designed primarily for fluid management and hydrological control rather than resisting armed military siege.
  • Herse: Similar because it is the direct French architectural predecessor and heraldic equivalent of the portcullis; differs because in English "herse" became specialized into funeral hearses (from the spiked candelabra framework resembling a harrow) while "portcullis" remained the military and architectural standard.
  • Barricade: Similar because both obstruct passages against hostile forces; differs because a barricade is an improvised, stationary obstacle piled across a thoroughfare, whereas a portcullis is a permanently engineered, mechanized fortification.
