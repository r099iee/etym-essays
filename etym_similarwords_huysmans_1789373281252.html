• Wilde: Similar because Oscar Wilde and Joris-Karl Huysmans were the twin supreme figureheads of fin-de-siècle Decadence and aestheticism, celebrating the triumph of artificiality over nature; differs because Wilde expressed his aestheticism through sparkling paradoxical wit, social comedies, and aristocratic public flamboyance in London society, whereas Huysmans explored decadent neurosis through solitary, dense, sensory prose while laboring as a quiet, reclusive Paris bureaucrat.
  • Mallarmé: Similar because Stéphane Mallarmé and Huysmans were intimate friends and towering luminaries of the French Symbolist-Decadent circle who rejected bourgeois materialism in pursuit of ideal beauty; differs because Mallarmé sought absolute purity and metaphysical void through hermetic Alexandrine verse, whereas Huysmans wallowed in tactile, earthy, eccentric, and visceral prose descriptions of physical matter and bodily decay.
  • Zola: Similar because Émile Zola was Huysmans' original mentor, and both shared a commitment to unflinching documentary observation and the dissection of modern environments; differs because Zola remained a lifelong, dogmatic champion of scientific positivism, physiological heredity, and mass social movements, whereas Huysmans violently revolted against scientific materialism to embrace decadent individualism, the occult, and Catholic mysticism.
  • Pater: Similar because Walter Pater and Huysmans were foundational philosophers of modern aestheticism who urged their readers to burn with a "hard, gem-like flame" of exquisite sensory impressions; differs because Pater advocated an understated, serene, Oxford-educated Renaissance humanism, whereas Huysmans favored bizarre, grotesque, macabre, and pathological sensory extremes.
  • Baudelaire: Similar because Charles Baudelaire was Huysmans' supreme poetic idol, whose concepts of *spleen*, artificiality, and synesthetic correspondences directly inspired the creation of Des Esseintes in *À rebours*; differs because Baudelaire articulated his visions through classical, highly disciplined French verse and poetic prose essays, whereas Huysmans expanded these motifs into the full-length encyclopedic novel and applied them to modern Catholic mysticism.
  • Barrès: Similar because Maurice Barrès and Huysmans were prominent fin-de-siècle French authors who began in intense aesthetic decadence and dandyism before seeking spiritual salvation; differs because Barrès transitioned from individualist "cult of the ego" (*le culte du moi*) into militaristic, organic French nationalism, whereas Huysmans retreated into universal, medieval Catholic monasticism and the quietude of a Benedictine cloister.
