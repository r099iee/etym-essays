PIE *tre- "three" (reconstructed numeral, c. 4500-3500 BCE) → Latin testis "witness; etymologically 'the one who stands as third' (reconstructed compound *tri-st-i-, widely accepted but debated in detail)" (attested 3rd-2nd c. BCE) → Latin testari "to bear witness, to testify" (verb, attested 2nd-1st c. BCE) → Latin testamentum "a will, a witnessed declaration of final wishes" (attested 1st c. BCE) → Late Latin testamentarius "pertaining to a will" (imperial legal texts, 4th-6th c. CE) → Old French testamentaire (12th-14th c.) → Middle English testamentary / testamentarie (14th-15th c.) → testamentary (modern English, /ˌtɛstəˈmɛntəri/) Note on the debated stage: the derivation of Latin testis from *tre- "three" + *stā- "stand" (the "third one standing by") is the standard scholarly account but rests on reconstruction; some linguists treat the inner compound as unverifiable. The noun testament additionally acquired the biblical sense "covenant" (Old/New Testament) from Greek diatheke via Christian Latin, a semantic extension parallel to the legal sense.
