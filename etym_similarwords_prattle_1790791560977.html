• Babble: Similar because both denote continuous, rapid speech lacking adult logic or clarity; differs because babble emphasizes indistinct, unintelligible, or incoherent sounds (like a baby or a rushing brook), whereas prattle consists of recognizable words strung together inconsequentially.
  • Prate: Similar because both share the same Germanic root and denote idle, foolish talking; differs because prate carries a harsher, more pompous and tedious connotation of bragging or holding forth arrogantly on matters of little value.
  • Chatter: Similar because both describe rapid, informal conversation; differs because chatter can be lively, practical, or neutral social interaction, whereas prattle strongly implies vacuity, childishness, or lack of substance.
  • Jabber: Similar because both describe fast and trivial speech; differs because jabber emphasizes rapid, noisy, excited, and garbled vocalization that is difficult to understand.
  • Blather: Similar because both denote foolish, long-winded talk; differs because blather typically refers to voluble, nonsensical, or pompous nonsense uttered by adults, lacking the light, frequentative childish quality of prattle.
  • Drivel: Similar because both describe worthless talk; differs because drivel carries severe disgust and contempt, implying completely senseless, idiotic, or decaying speech, often compared to drool or slime.
