Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)

"to touch closely, border on"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. CE)
contiguus
"touching, adjacent, neighboring"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th c. CE)
contiguitās
"state of touching, proximity"
↓
Middle French (c. 15th c.)
contiguïté
"touching, continuous contact"
↓
Modern English (c. 1610)
contiguity
"state of touching, direct adjacency"

Note: Latin contiguus is formed from the assimilated prefix com- / con- ('together, with') and tingere / tangere ('to touch'), exhibiting regular Latin root-vowel weakening from short -a- to -i- in medial syllables of compound verbs. English directly adapted the Late Latin abstract noun contiguitās via Middle French contiguïté.
