- 8th–6th c. BCE (Phoenician & Punic Era) - Colonized by Phoenician maritime traders who named the natural harbors Maleth ('haven' or 'place of refuge'), establishing a key commercial hub in the central Mediterranean.
- 3rd c. BCE – 4th c. CE (Greco-Roman Era) - Known as Melita / Melitē in Ancient Greek and Latin (popularly associated with Greek meli 'honey' due to the island's endemic bees and limestone flora), celebrated in the Acts of the Apostles for the shipwreck of Saint Paul in 60 CE.
- 9th–11th c. CE (Arab Conquest & Rule) - Conquered by the Aghlabids of Ifriqiya; renamed Mālita, which established the Semitic grammatical and basic lexical substrate that evolved into modern Maltese (Malti).
- 1530–1798 CE (Order of Saint John / Knights of Malta) - Ceded to the Knights Hospitaller by Holy Roman Emperor Charles V; transformed into the premier maritime fortress of Christian Europe, famously repelling the Ottoman Empire during the heroic Great Siege of 1565.
- 1798–1814 CE (Napoleonic Wars & British Intervention) - Captured by Napoleon Bonaparte en route to Egypt in 1798, followed by a Maltese rebellion and British naval blockade; Britain's refusal to evacuate Malta under the Treaty of Amiens precipitated the resumption of the Napoleonic Wars, leading to formal British sovereignty under the 1814 Treaty of Paris.
- 19th–20th c. (British Crown Colony & WWII) - Served as Britain's pivotal Mediterranean naval and air base; subjected to intense Axis bombardment during the Siege of Malta (1940–1942), earning the George Cross for the collective bravery of its populace.
- 1964–Present (Independence & European Integration) - Achieved independence in 1964, became a republic in 1974, joined the European Union in 2004, and today thrives as a sovereign, multicultural maritime republic.
