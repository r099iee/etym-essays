PIE *per- "forward, through" + *bʰuH- "to grow, become, be" → reconstructed compound *pro-bʰu-o-s "being in front, excelling, good" (debated — see note) → Latin probus "good, honest, upright, virtuous" (attested from Plautus, early 2nd century BCE) → Latin probāre "to test, try, prove, deem good" (Classical Latin) → Latin approbāre (ad- "to, towards" + probāre) "to assent to, approve, regard as good" (Classical Latin) → Latin approbātiō, -ōnis "approval, assent, sanction" (Classical Latin, 1st century BCE) → Old French approbacion / approbation "approval" (12th–13th century) → Middle English approbacioun "approval, sanction" (14th century) → Modern English approbation Note on debate: the derivation of Latin probus from a PIE compound *pro-bʰu-o-s "being in front / excelling" (comparing Sanskrit prabhū "mighty, lord", from pra- "forth" + bhū- "to be") is the standard but not universally accepted view; the Sanskrit link is plausible rather than certain and is treated as debated by specialists. Zero guesswork: the Latin-to-English stages are the attested record; only the reconstructed PIE compound is flagged as hypothetical.
