- Archaic burden in Middle English - Burthen is the -th- spelling of burden inherited from Middle English, once an ordinary variant of the word for a load or weight.
- Scriptural and nautical registers - The spelling retreats into solemn contexts — the King James Bible and Prayer Book (the burthen of sin) — while in naval law it names a ship's carrying capacity in tons.
- Romantic revival and archaism - The Romantic poets (Wordsworth, Coleridge, Keats, Shelley) deliberately revive burthen for a weight that sounds ancient and moral; today it survives as a conscious archaism in poetry and liturgical prose.
