• Soiled: Similar because both mean dirtied and stained; differs because soiled is the plain domestic word (soiled linen, soiled nappies), while sullied is literary and moral, reaching honour as well as cloth.
  • Stained: Similar because both describe a mark that spoils whiteness, physical or moral; differs because stained is the neutral general term (stained glass is even beautiful), while sullied always judges the mark as a fall.
  • Tarnished: Similar because both dim former brightness, especially of reputations and metal; differs because tarnished pictures slow dulling of shine, while sullied pictures active dirtying by contact with filth.
  • Besmirched: Similar because both are literary words for dishonouring a fair name; differs because besmirched is rarer and more ornate, stressing smearing with mire, while sullied is the standard epic term.
  • Defiled: Similar because both mean polluted, especially what is sacred or pure; differs because defiled is stronger and often religious or sexual, while sullied can be as light as a dusty hem.
  • Contaminated: Similar because both describe purity ruined by contact; differs because contaminated is scientific and harms health or evidence, while sullied harms beauty and honour.
  • Dishonoured: Similar because both name a lost good reputation; differs because dishonoured states the social result plainly, while sullied paints it as a visible stain spreading across whiteness.
  • Degraded: Similar because both describe a fall to a lower, baser state; differs because degraded stresses rank and function lowered (degraded land, degraded office), while sullied stresses brightness dimmed.
  • Smirched: Similar because both are archaic-literary terms for dirtying what was fair; differs because smirched survives almost only in poetry for physical smudges, while sullied still works daily duty in moral judgment.
