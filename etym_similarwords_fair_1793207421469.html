• Market: Similar because both are regular public gatherings for buying and selling; differs because a market is usually weekly and everyday (butchers, greengrocers), while a fair is periodic, larger, and festive.
  • Mart: Similar because both name centres of trade, especially livestock (auction mart); differs because mart is the commercial building or business proper, while fair is the event with its crowds and customs.
  • Bazaar: Similar because both mix shopping with spectacle and sociability; differs because a bazaar is Eastern or charitable (church bazaar, charity stalls), while a fair is the chartered English country tradition.
  • Fête: Similar because both are festive outdoor gatherings with stalls and sideshows; differs because a village fête is genteel fundraising (tombolas, teas), while a livestock fair is hard commerce in animals.
  • Carnival: Similar because both are periodic public festivities with crowds and booths; differs because carnival is processional revelry (Rio, Notting Hill), while a fair is anchored in trade, however merry.
  • Exposition: Similar because world’s fairs literally are expositions of industry and art; differs because an exposition is grand, state-staged display, while a country fair is farmers dealing in a field.
  • Show: Similar because agricultural shows continue the livestock-fair tradition (prize bulls, sheepdogs); differs because a show exhibits and competes, while a fair primarily buys and sells.
  • Auction: Similar because both dispose of livestock to the highest bidder on the day; differs because an auction is the single mechanism of sale, while a fair is the whole day-long gathering around many sales.
  • Horse fair: Similar because horse fairs (Appleby, Stow) are the most famous surviving English fairs; differs because a horse fair trades only horses among travellers, while Cobbett’s Chertsey fair took cattle and pigs besides.
