- Physical rising in early use - Mountings, the plural verbal noun of mount, originally carries the concrete sense of climbing a mountain or rising onto a horse.
- Wordsworth's abstract coinage - In The Prelude (1805) 'mountings of the mind' transfers the image to repeated, involuntary surges of aspiration and imaginative flight, a distinctively Romantic use.
- Modern technical and figurative senses - Today mountings names mechanical frames or brackets in which parts are fixed, while the related participle mount attaches to steadily increasing pressure, anger, or cost.
