• Command: Similar because both can mean an authoritative order to do something; differs because “command” is fully current and strictly authoritative, while “bid” in this sense is archaic and literary.
  • Order: Similar because both express direction to act; differs because “order” is the everyday, neutral word with no auction or attempt senses.
  • Direct: Similar because both mean to give instructions; differs because “direct” is formal but milder, and lacks the imperious archaism of “bid”.
  • Instruct: Similar because both convey telling someone what to do; differs because “instruct” implies teaching or formal briefing, not the commanding ring of “bid”.
  • Enjoin: Similar because both mean to urge or order emphatically; differs because “enjoin” is legal-formal and often negative (“enjoin from”), while “bid” is literary.
  • Charge: Similar because both can mean to give a solemn duty or order; differs because “charge” implies responsibility entrusted, and has many unrelated senses.
  • Ask: Similar because the oldest sense of “bid” was “to ask”; differs because “ask” is soft, modern and neutral, with no command force.
  • Request: Similar because both can mean to ask for something politely; differs because “request” is courteous-formal, while “bid” is stronger and older in register.
  • Invite: Similar because “bid” once meant to summon or invite to a feast; differs because “invite” is the modern word for the social act, while “bid” retains only the archaic flavour.
  • Entreat / Beseech: Similar because both belong to the praying-asking lineage of “bid”; differs because they are more emotional and suppliant, with no command or auction senses.
  • Offer: Similar because “bid” at auction is literally an offer of a price; differs because “offer” is general (offers of help, goods, jobs), while “bid” implies competitive pricing.
  • Tender: Similar because a tender is a formal offer for a contract, as a bid is; differs because “tender” is restricted to business and legal procurement.
  • Attempt / Strive: Similar because “a bid for” is an attempt to gain something; differs because “attempt” is neutral effort, while “bid” implies rivalry and high stakes.
  • Propose: Similar because both can mean to put forward an offer or plan; differs because “propose” is about putting ideas forward, not prices or commands.
