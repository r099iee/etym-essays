• Puff: Similar because both derive from the same imitative *puf- base and name a quick blast of breath or air; differs because puff is the noun and verb for the blast itself, while poof is the sound-name used as an interjection, more theatrical, abrupt, and single-syllabled.
  • Pop: Similar because both are sudden, explosive little sounds; differs because a pop is sharper, harder, and more metallic (the sound of a cork or bubble), while poof is a soft, breathy burst of air.
  • Bang: Similar because both are sudden explosive exclamations; differs because a bang is a heavy, loud, violent report, far heavier than poof's light, almost comical puff.
  • Whoosh: Similar because both are sudden rushing air-sounds; differs because a whoosh is heavier and more drawn-out, a sustained flow of air, while poof is a single abrupt syllable of release.
  • Whiff: Similar because both involve a quick movement of air; differs because a whiff is a faint smell or brief inhalation, without poof's audible, explosive, interjectional force.
  • Pouffe: Similar because both descend from the same imitative p-f sound-cluster; differs because pouffe is a borrowed noun for an over-stuffed cushion or low seat, while poof is a sound-symbolic interjection.
  • Boom: Similar because both can mark a sudden explosion or disappearance; differs because a boom is deep, loud, and resonant, while poof is light, soft, and breathy — the difference between a detonation and a candle being blown out.
