- [1815 French politics] - [Enters English from French as a noun — a member of the French constitutional party of the Restoration; opponents hurl it as a reproach against abstract theory-makers, so the contemptuous overtone is there from birth]
- [19th century the theory-monger] - [Shifts to describe one who applies a fixed doctrine to every case regardless of practical consequences; connotation pejorative — the doctrinaire fits evidence to doctrine rather than doctrine to evidence]
- [20th-21st century adjective of rigidity] - [Now chiefly an adjective — doctrinaire communism, doctrinaire liberalism — naming theory hardened into dogma; connotation cool, systematic, and principled, the ideology-driven form of inflexibility]
