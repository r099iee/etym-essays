• Often: Similar in meaning "on many occasions"; differs because often is the standard modern adverb, utterly neutral and everyday, while oft-times is archaic, poetic, and heavy with old-world gravity.
  • Frequently: Similar in meaning "at close intervals, again and again"; differs because frequently is formal and quasi-statistical, suggesting a regular cadence, while oft-times stresses repeated recurrence with a tolling, oracular rhythm.
  • Repeatedly: Similar in meaning "again and again"; differs because repeatedly is mechanical and factual, stressing the recurrence of the same act, while oft-times is lyrical and fated - not merely repetitive but habitual and doomed.
  • Oft: Similar in being the clipped archaic form of "often"; differs because oft alone survives only in set phrases such as oft-repeated, oft-told, and oft-quoted, whereas oft-times stands as a full archaic adverb of frequency.
  • Sometimes: Similar in sharing the -times adverbial structure; differs because sometimes means "on some occasions, occasionally", pointing at alternation rather than recurrence, while oft-times insists on "many, frequent times".
  • Betimes: Similar in being an archaic -times adverb of time; differs because betimes originally meant "early, in good time" and later "soon", whereas oft-times is purely a marker of frequency.
  • Time and again: Similar in meaning "repeatedly, on many occasions"; differs because time and again is a modern idiom of ordinary prose, without the antique, ballad-like ring of oft-times.
  • Again and again: Similar in meaning "frequently, many times"; differs because again and again is colloquial and emphatic repetition without any poetic register, while oft-times is dignified, archaic, and literary.
  • Oftener: Similar in expressing comparative frequency; differs because oftener is the comparative form of the modern adverb often, lacking the compound archaic shape and oracular weight of oft-times.
