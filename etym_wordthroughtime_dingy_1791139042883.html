- 1670s slang novelty - soiled, sullied linen and dusky colour; low colloquial tone, vivid barnyard edge
- 1700s dictionary word - foul, tarnished; accepted as standard for dirty cloth, walls, weather; neutral descriptive
- 1800s urban gloom - Dickensian dingy lodgings, counting-houses, fog; strong association with poverty, gaslight, London squalor; pity and disapproval
- Early 1900s moral extension - dingy joke, reputation, affair; from physically unclean to slightly sordid or compromised
- Contemporary usage - shabby, poorly lit, depressing place or faded garment; everyday neutral-informal; historic racist noun sense obsolete and condemned
