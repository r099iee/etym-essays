- 13th Century - [Feudal Manor & Rural Estate] - Denoted the private country manor house and agricultural lands of the Anglo-Norman knight William de Blemund, located well outside the medieval walls of London.
- 17th–18th Century - [Fashionable Aristocratic Expansion] - Perceived as an elegant, airy West End residential retreat laid out in formal Georgian squares for the nobility and upper gentry by the Dukes of Bedford.
- 19th Century - [Scholarly & Reformist Epicenter] - Marked by the construction of the British Museum and the foundation of University College London; societal perception shifted toward serious scholarship, academic philanthropy, and professional intellectual pursuits.
- Early 20th Century - [Modernist Vanguard & Bohemian Elite] - Associated with the radical Bloomsbury Group; perceived simultaneously as the cutting edge of English literary and artistic innovation and as a somewhat precious, insular intellectual clique.
- Late 20th–21st Century - [Global Cultural, Educational & Publishing Landmark] - Universally recognized as a historic symbol of world-class academia, literary history, museum tourism, and publishing prestige.
