• Almost: Similar because both express near-completeness, everything short of the final step; differs because almost is a neutral measure of degree, while all-but keeps the paradoxical 'all except' tension and a more emphatic, formal dignity.
  • Nearly: Similar because both mean 'very close to, not quite'; differs because nearly is the colourless everyday quantifier, while all-but frames the outcome as a totality that is only marginally incomplete.
  • Virtually: Similar because both approach totality; differs because virtually points to effect rather than fact — true 'in essence' — whereas all-but names a literal near-miss on the final step.
  • Practically: Similar because both mean 'in effect, to all intents'; differs because practically grounds the nearness in practical outcome, while all-but is more abstract, formal and paradoxical.
  • Effectively: Similar because both say a state amounts to the condition named; differs because effectively focuses on the result achieved, without the one-step-short image that all-but preserves.
  • Well-nigh: Similar because both are literary near-synonyms of 'almost'; differs because well-nigh is archaic and poetic, while all-but survives in current formal and journalistic prose.
  • Nigh on: Similar because both mean 'almost'; differs because nigh on is a dialectal, rustic idiom, whereas all-but is a formal literary compound with legal overtones.
  • Close to / near: Similar because both express approximation; differs because close to and near are prepositional and casual, lacking the totality-minus-one-step paradox of all-but.
  • Just about: Similar because both mean 'not quite, almost'; differs because just about is conversational and vague, while all-but is measured, formal and precise.
  • Recurrent (for recrudescent): Similar because both mean returning after dormancy; differs because recurrent merely states that something happens again, while recrudescent images the tearing open of a healed wound.
  • Relapsing (for recrudescent): Similar because both describe a return to an earlier active state; differs because relapsing is clinical, of an illness regressing after improvement, while recrudescent emphasises the raw, painful reopening of what had healed over.
  • Resurgent (for recrudescent): Similar because both mean rising again; differs because resurgent suggests a powerful, often triumphant renewal, while recrudescent is reserved for the painful reopening of healed ills.
  • Recurring (for recrudescent): Similar because both mean happening again; differs because recurring is neutral repetition across time, while recrudescent specifies the morbid, raw breaking out after a period of dormancy.
  • Reawakened (for recrudescent): Similar because both mean stirred into activity again; differs because reawakened may be benign or neutral, while recrudescent is specifically pathological or threatening.
