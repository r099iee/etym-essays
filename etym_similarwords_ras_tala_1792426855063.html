• Pātāla: Similar because both refer to the subterranean netherworld in Hindu cosmology; differs because Pātāla serves both as an overarching umbrella term for all seven subterranean spheres collectively and as the name of the seventh, nethermost realm, whereas Rasātala is specifically the sixth sphere situated just above Pātāla.
  • Naraka: Similar because both are realms situated beneath the earthly plane; differs because Naraka is specifically the purgatorial hell of retributive punishment, torments, and suffering ruled by Yama (the lord of death), whereas Rasātala is an opulent, pleasure-filled subterranean paradise (bila-svarga) inhabited by titans and serpents.
  • Talātala: Similar because both are members of the seven subterranean spheres (sapta-pātāla); differs because Talātala is the fourth sphere, historically ruled by the architect-titan Maya, whereas Rasātala is the deeper, moisture-rich sixth sphere.
  • Sutala: Similar because both are subterranean Purāṇic realms; differs because Sutala is the third sphere, celebrated as the sacred subterranean kingdom granted by Viṣṇu's dwarf avatar Vāmana to the righteous titan king Bali.
  • Mahātala: Similar because both are adjacent deep netherworld spheres; differs because Mahātala is the fifth sphere, specifically characterized as the primary domain of the colossal, multi-hooded descendants of the serpent Kadrū.
  • Adhobhuvana: Similar because both denote subterranean cosmological regions; differs because Adhobhuvana is a general descriptive Sanskrit compound meaning "the lower world / down-realm," lacking the specific mythic architecture and narrative resonance of Rasātala.
  • Tartarus: Similar because both represent the deep mythological subterranean abyss in ancient mythologies; differs because Tartarus in Greek myth is a grim, dismal dungeon of torture and cosmic imprisonment for defeated Titans, whereas Rasātala features shining jeweled citadels and gardens.
  • Hades: Similar because both represent realms beneath the surface of the earth; differs because Hades in Greek tradition is the somber realm of discarnate human souls and shadows, whereas Rasātala is teeming with vibrant living titans, serpents, and earth spirits.
  • Abyss: Similar because both convey immense, terrifying depths and figurative ruin; differs because abyss is an abstract bottomless void or chasm, whereas Rasātala has a definite cosmological floor, architecture, and mythic population.
  • Netherworld: Similar because both describe the world beneath the earth; differs because netherworld is a broad, generic Germanic-derived English concept lacking the specific Vedic moisture etymology and seven-tiered Purāṇic structure of Rasātala.
  • Chasm: Similar because both involve deep vertical plunges into the earth; differs because chasm is a physical geological fracture or gorge, whereas Rasātala is a cosmic dimension and literary archetype.
  • Inferno: Similar because both are subterranean mythic realms; differs because inferno (from Latin infernus) carries an overwhelming connotation of raging volcanic fire, brimstone, and torment, completely contrasting with the cool, moisture-rich atmosphere of Rasātala.
