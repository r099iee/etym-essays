Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE; reconstructed)
*skrinkwaną
"to shrink, draw together, contract"
↓
Old English (9th–11th c.)
scrincan
"to draw together, contract, wither"
↓
Middle English (15th–16th c.; frequentative in -el)
schryvelen / shrivelen
"to cause to contract and wrinkle"
↓
Modern English (present)
shrivelled
Note on the debate: The ancestry of shrivel is obscure and disputed. The traditional account derives it as a frequentative built on the stem of Old English scrincan "to shrink" (cf. shrink, shrunken), via the Middle English suffix -el-/-le-; but the vowel and the date make this uncertain, and the OED treats the word's origin as unknown. A rival suggestion points to a Scandinavian source akin to Norwegian skryvla "to wrinkle, shrivel". No ancestor before Proto-Germanic can be securely reconstructed.
