ROOT 1: ROCK
Old French (11th–12th c.)
roche
"rock, stone, cliff"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
rokke
"rock, large mass of stone"
↓
Modern English (present)
rock
"solid mineral mass"

ROOT 2: HEWN
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*hawwaną
"to hew, cut"
↓
Old English (before 10th c.)
heawan
"to cut, strike, shape with a blade"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
hewen
"to cut, carve"
↓
Modern English (present)
hewn
"past participle: cut, carved"

COMPOUND
Modern English (19th–20th c.)
ROCKHEWN
"cut or excavated out of solid rock"

Note: Rock entered English from Old French (ultimately of uncertain, possibly Celtic or Gallo-Romance, origin — no PIE etymon is secure); hewn is the native Germanic participle of hew. The compound is a modern formation, popularised in its single-word form by Tolkien.
