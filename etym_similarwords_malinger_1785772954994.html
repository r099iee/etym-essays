• Shirk — Similar because both mean avoiding work or duty. Differs because shirk requires no pretense of illness — one can shirk by absence, delay, or passing the task on — while malinger specifically involves feigning or exaggerating incapacity as the excuse.
  • Feign — Similar because both describe pretending something false. Differs because feign is the broad verb of pretense (feign sleep, feign surprise, feign interest) with no built-in motive, while malinger is a specialized kind of feigning: pretending illness to avoid duty, and it carries an accusation of shirking.
  • Simulate — Similar because both mean to produce a false appearance, especially of symptoms (to simulate illness). Differs because simulate is neutral and clinical (a simulator, simulation), while malinger is pejorative and implies a moral charge of avoiding duty or extracting benefits.
  • Goldbrick — Similar because both (in U.S. usage) mean dodging work by excuses or idleness. Differs because goldbrick is informal military/college slang for any loafing or shirking and need not involve faking sickness, while malinger is the formal term for the specific sick-pretense fraud.
  • Skive — Similar because both mean avoiding work or school. Differs because skive (chiefly British informal) covers any sly avoidance — skiving off means absenting oneself — while malinger specifically involves the performance of illness or incapacity.
  • Fake — Similar because both mean to pretend or counterfeit. Differs because fake is the broad, informal verb (fake a smile, fake a headache) with no specific context, while malinger is formal and restricted to feigned incapacity used to escape duty or gain benefits.
  • Dodge / evade — Similar because both mean to avoid something unwanted. Differs because dodge and evade stress clever or active avoidance of pursuit, duty, or questions without necessarily staging an illness, whereas malinger always involves the sick-role performance.
  • Fabricate — Similar because both involve inventing something false. Differs because fabricate is used of documents, stories, or evidence and is neutral about motive, while malinger is used of symptoms and implies the specific purpose of dodging obligation.
