Note: The English word is a compound built on two PIE roots — *weid- 'to see' (the base of Latin vidēre; also of English wit and wise, Greek idein, Sanskrit veda) and *pro- 'forward, before' (the base of Latin prō-) — joined in the Latin compound prōvidēre 'to see ahead'. The abstract sense 'foresight, prudence' is the earliest in English; the concrete plural 'supplies of food' develops from the 15th century. 'Providence' (from Latin prōvidentia) is a parallel doublet that took the theological sense.
