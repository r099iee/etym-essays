Drat is not the product of a regular sound change but of deliberate, jocular erosion: it is a minced oath. The chain below traces the two contributing words of the underlying phrase, God and rot, and then the phrase-level contraction that produced drat. Chain 1 — "God" (the divine element of the underlying phrase): - The prehistory of English god is debated and not securely connected to a PIE root; the Germanic form is reconstructed as Proto-Germanic *gudą "a god, an idol" (reconstructed; attested through Old English god, Old Norse goð, Gothic guþ), and its deeper etymology is uncertain — the traditional connection to PIE *ǵʰuto- "that which is invoked" (from *ǵʰew- "to call, to invoke") is conventional but not universally accepted → Old English god "a god, the supreme being" (attested from the earliest records, c. 8th century, already Christianised) → Middle English god → Modern English God. Chain 2 — "rot" (the verb of the underlying phrase): - PIE *ret- "to rot, to decompose, to become putrid" (traditional reconstruction; attested through the Germanic family and possibly the Celtic; the exact Indo-European distribution is debated) → Proto-Germanic *rutōną "to rot, to decay, to decompose" (reconstructed; cognate with Old Norse rotna, Old High German rozzōn) → Old English rotian "to rot, to decay, to become putrid" (weak verb, attested from the Old English period) → Middle English roten "to rot, to decay, to decompose" → Modern English rot "to decay, to decompose; to degenerate." Chain 3 — the imprecation and its erosion to "drat": - English phrase "God rot (him)!" — an imprecation of the 17th–18th centuries calling divine putrefaction upon the offender (attested in this form in the early modern period) → colloquial contraction "'od rot" / "God-ro't" — the profane subject clipped in everyday speech (aphesis of God-, 18th century) → "drat" — the reduced form, first recorded in writing c. 1800–1815 in the OED's earliest citations, as an interjection and verb "to curse mildly" → Modern English DRAT "an exclamation of mild anger or annoyance; to curse mildly." Note on debate: the derivation of drat from "God rot" is the standard etymology (OED, Etymonline), but the intermediate aphetic forms are imperfectly attested; the chain is a reasonable and widely accepted reconstruction of a spoken contraction rather than a documented sequence of written forms. The PIE ancestry of both God (uncertain, possibly *ǵʰuto-) and rot (*ret-, distribution debated) involves reconstruction and should be treated as probable rather than certain. The modern vowel of drat is a short /æ/, matching a short-a re-spelling of the contracted form.
