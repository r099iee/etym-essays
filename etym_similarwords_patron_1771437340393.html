• Sponsor: Similar because both give financial or formal support to a person or project; differs because a sponsor's backing is often contractual and specific (sponsoring an event or athlete), while a patron gives enduring, protective support with a personal relationship.
  • Benefactor: Similar because both are people who give money or help; differs because benefactor stresses the act of doing good and is emotionally cooler, while patron implies an ongoing, almost paternal relationship with the supported person.
  • Backer: Similar because both fund a venture or person; differs because backer is informal and purely financial, while patron carries dignity, cultural authority, and the old art-world resonance.
  • Supporter: Similar because both give help to a cause or person; differs because supporter is the broadest, least hierarchical term, usable of causes, teams, and people, while patron implies elevated status and a protective, quasi-familial role.
  • Protector: Similar because both shield a person or group; differs because protector stresses defence and safety, while patron stresses support, sponsorship, and advancement.
  • Angel: Similar because both fund projects or people; differs because angel is a modern informal term for a generous financial backer (an "angel investor"), without the historical patron's paternal or cultural authority.
  • Godfather: Similar because both are powerful, paternal figures who back and protect; differs because godfather is either the baptismal sponsor or a mafia metaphor, and lacks patron's formal, institutional status.
  • Advocate: Similar because both speak and act for a person or cause; differs because advocate stresses verbal defence and public promotion, while patron stresses material or financial support.
