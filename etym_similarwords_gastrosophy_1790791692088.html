• Gastronomy: Similar because both deal with the art and culture of fine food and drink; differs because gastronomy focuses primarily on the practical skills, culinary styles, recipes, and gourmet appreciation of good food, whereas gastrosophy is a broader, more abstract philosophical and social science linking culinary pleasure to human nature, ethics, and social harmony.
  • Epicureanism: Similar because both pursue refined sensory pleasure and the enjoyment of the table; differs because epicureanism is an entire classical philosophical system advocating tranquility (ataraxia) through moderate, pain-free living, whereas gastrosophy specifically centers on food and table culture as its primary lens.
  • Gourmandism: Similar because both involve a deep enthusiasm for eating; differs because gourmandism frequently carries connotations of excess, gluttony, or obsessive devotion to rich delicacies, whereas gastrosophy emphasizes intellectual wisdom, harmony, and philosophical refinement.
  • Connoisseurship: Similar because both require educated discernment and aesthetic appreciation; differs because connoisseurship applies broadly across arts, wines, and antiques, whereas gastrosophy is specifically a philosophical doctrine of food and social dining.
