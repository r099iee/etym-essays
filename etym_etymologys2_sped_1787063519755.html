PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000–3500 BCE)
*spē- / *speh-
"to thrive, prosper, succeed"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*spōdiz
"success, prosperity; swiftness"
↓
Old English (c. 700–1000)
spēd (noun)
"success, prosperity, wealth, advancement"
↓
Old English (c. 900–1000)
spēdan (verb)
"to prosper, succeed, attain; to hasten"
↓
Middle English (12th–14th c.)
spēden (verb)
"to prosper, succeed; to go quickly, hasten"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
sped (past tense)
"prospered; hastened, went quickly"
(formed by analogy with strong verbs)
↓
Modern English (from c. 1500)
sped
"past tense of 'speed': moved quickly, went at speed"

Note on nuance: the Old English verb spēdan was originally a weak verb whose past was spēdde; the form sped arose in Middle English by analogy with strong verbs and ousted it. For the specialised transitive senses "to exceed the speed limit" and "to hasten a process", Modern English prefers the regular past speeded; sped is standard for simple motion.
