Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*puk- / *pukk-
"to thrust, prod, knock"
↓
Middle Low German / Middle Dutch (c. 12th c. CE)
poken
"to prod, prick, thrust with a point"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300 CE)
poken
"to jab, thrust, prod, push forward"
↓
Early Modern English (16th c. CE)
poke / poked
"thrust with a finger/stick; meddle; search"
↓
Modern English (16th c. CE–present)
poked
"prodded with a finger or stick; nudged; pried"

Note: The verb poke is of Low German / Dutch Germanic origin (*poken*), distinct from the homophonous noun poke ("bag/sack", as in "a pig in a poke"), which derives from Old Northern French poque.
