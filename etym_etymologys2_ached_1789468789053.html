Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*ag- / *ak- (imitative / somatic root)
"to cry out in pain; sharp, driving pain"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*akanan
"to ache, suffer continuous pain"
↓
Old English (c. 9th c.)
acan (strong verb: ōc, acen)
"to suffer pain, hurt continuously"
↓
Middle English (c. 13th c.)
aken (weakened: akede / ok)
"to ache, suffer bodily or spiritual pain"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
ache / aked (spelling influenced by Greek achos)
"to feel continuous dull pain or yearning"
↓
Modern English (present)
ACHED
"suffered a continuous dull pain or intense longing"
