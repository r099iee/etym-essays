• lifted: similar because both mean moving something upward with effort; differs because lift is gentler and more neutral — you lift a cup but heave a boulder; heave implies mass and strain.
  • raised: similar upward motion; differs because raise is deliberate and controlled (raise a flag, raise a hand), while heave is effortful, heavy, and less precise.
  • hauled: similar effortful moving of heavy things; differs because haul emphasises pulling toward you over a distance (haul a net, haul cargo), while heave emphasises the initial upward lift or throw.
  • hoisted: similar lifting of heavy loads; differs because hoist is mechanical and systematic (hoist a sail with tackle), while heave is raw bodily effort.
  • shoved: similar pushing with effort; differs because shove pushes away from the body while heave lifts or throws — the pair appears together in the source text, complementing each other.
  • threw: similar propelling through the air; differs because throw is general and can be light and precise, while heave-as-throw is heavy, clumsy, and effortful.
  • flung: similar violent, careless throwing; differs because fling is wild and sweeping, while heave is heavy and straining.
  • tossed: similar throwing; differs because toss is light and casual — the exact opposite register of heave.
  • retched: similar the visceral sense; differs because retch is exclusively the vomit reflex, while heave (vomit sense) is colloquial and includes the whole body's rise-and-fall motion.
  • surged: similar rising motion of water; differs because surge is powerful and forward, while heave is slow, rhythmic rise-and-fall.
  • swelled: similar rising; differs because swell is expansion from within, while heave is an external strain lifting the mass.
  • strained: similar muscular effort; differs because strain names the internal muscular state, while heave is the completed outward action of lifting.
