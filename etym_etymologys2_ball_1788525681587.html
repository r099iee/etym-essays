Ancient Greek (5th–4th c. BCE)
βαλλίζειν (ballízein)
"to dance, jump about, frolic"
↓
Late Latin (4th–6th c. CE)
ballāre
"to dance"
↓
Old French (12th–13th c.)
baler (verb) / bal (noun)
"to dance; a dancing festival, dance assembly"
↓
French (16th–17th c.)
bal
"a formal social gathering for dancing"
↓
English (1630s)
ball
"a formal social dance; grand evening dancing assembly"

Note: This word is an etymological homonym completely distinct from the Germanic noun ball ("spherical toy or projectile"), which traces to Proto-Germanic *balluz ("sphere", from PIE *bʰel- "to swell"). The dance term entered English via French bal, ultimately stemming from Greek βαλλίζειν (ballízein).
