- c.1300 — gut string: Middle English corde for bowstring, hangman's rope, and lyre string; practical-perilous perception, music and execution share one word.
- Late 14th century — body and bond: tendons, and figurative cords that bind the soul; perception moralised — sin and love both tie.
- 15th–17th century — trade and measure: sash cord, bell cord, and cord of wood measured by rope; perception standardised, cord becomes unit.
- 18th–19th century — fabric and power: corduroy ribs, electrical and detonating cord; industrial perception, cord carries current and fashion.
- Contemporary — connection metaphor: power cord, umbilical cord, to cut the cord from parents or cable TV; perception as lifeline to be kept or cut.
