- [18th-century French import] - Enters English as the fashionable name for the low coiled knot at the nape, bringing the chic of Parisian dressmaking with it.
- [19th-century Victorian peak] - The chignon becomes a defining Victorian silhouette - large, dressed and hairpiece-augmented - a symbol of elaborate, respectable femininity.
- [20th-century simplification] - After the bob and modern easy styles, the chignon narrows to a sleek, structured option for formal or professional occasions; still reads as refined and deliberately dressed.
- [Contemporary register] - Now chiefly a bridal and evening-fashion word, carrying an aura of polished elegance that the plain 'bun' never commands.
