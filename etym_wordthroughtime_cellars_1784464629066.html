“Cellar” entered English from medieval French and Latin naming a storeroom — from the word for a room where provisions and wine were kept.
“Cellar” through the centuries fixed on the underground sense: a vault or room below ground, cool and dark, used for storing wine, food, and fuel.
“Cellar” from the 17th century onward became the domestic undercroft of respectable houses — and by extension any below-ground storage volume, from wine cellars to root cellars.
“Cellars” today still means the cool underground storage rooms of a house — but in hobbit architecture the joke is that even the cellars sit on the same level as the kitchen, multiplying underground-storage comfort inside a hillside smial.
