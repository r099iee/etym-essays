Proto-Indo-European *yeug- “to join, yoke, bind” (attested across the family: Sanskrit yu-, Latin iungere, Greek zeugnunai, all from the zero-grade *yug- with a nasal infix) → Latin iungere “to join, yoke, bind together” (from the same root, with y- → i- and the -ng- infix) → prefix dis- (“apart, asunder”) + iungere → Latin disiungere “to unyoke, separate, disconnect” → disiūnctus, past participle, “separated, set apart” (classical Latin) → English disjunct (adjective, 1580s) “separated, disconnected” → technical extensions: logic, a disjunct = one alternative of a disjunction (19th c.); linguistics, a disjunct = a sentence adverb standing outside the clause (20th c., following Quirk and others) → disjunct (the exact form of this card). Cognate demonstration: the same PIE root through Sanskrit yu- gave yóga “yoking, union” (whence English yoga) and yuga “age” (a “yoked” span of years, preserved in Hindi yuga and jugal “paired”).
