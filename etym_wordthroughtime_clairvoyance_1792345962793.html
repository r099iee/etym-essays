- [Late 17th–Early 18th c. Enlightenment France] — coined in French philosophy and lexicography (1718 Académie française) as a purely rationalist, secular virtue denoting sharp mental discernment, shrewd penetration, and clear-sightedness free from prejudice — [perception: celebrated as the supreme intellectual mark of enlightened reason and mental acuity]
- [Late 18th c. (1784) Mesmeric Era] — radically transformed by the Marquis de Puységur and practitioners of animal magnetism to describe the purported faculty of entranced somnambulists who could see through opaque barriers or diagnose internal bodily ailments without eyes — [perception: created an international medical sensation across European salons, mingling scientific fascination with occult wonder]
- [Mid-19th c. (1830s–1850s) Victorian Era] — borrowed into English during the Victorian Spiritualist craze, becoming inextricably linked with public séances, table-rappers, mediumship, and crystal gazers claiming to view the spirit world — [perception: treated with intense popular enthusiasm, yet condemned by mainstream Victorian scientists as fraudulent charlatanism]
- [Late 19th–Early 20th c.] — formalized by the Society for Psychical Research (SPR) and later J. B. Rhine at Duke University as a distinct category of extrasensory perception (ESP), rigorously separated from telepathy — [perception: elevated into a subject of serious statistical laboratory testing and academic controversy]
- [Late 20th c. Cold War Era] — co-opted into military espionage through government-funded "remote viewing" programs (Project Stargate) conducted by the CIA, DIA, and SRI — [perception: viewed through the lens of Cold War parapsychological defense and classified intelligence]
- [21st c. Contemporary Era] — ubiquitous in pop-culture fiction and supernatural media, while retaining a polished figurative life in political and business journalism for extraordinary strategic foresight and uncanny prescience — [perception: recognized colloquially as psychic sight, and metaphorically as brilliant intuitive acumen]
