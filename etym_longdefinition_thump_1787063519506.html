Thump (/θʌmp/) is an expressive onomatopoeic verb and noun that describes the act of striking or colliding with a surface in a manner that produces a heavy, dull, muffled, and resonant acoustic impact, as well as the discrete sonic event or blunt physical blow resulting from that contact. Unlike sharp, brittle, or high-frequency impact sounds (such as clacks, snaps, or clicks), a thump is characterized by low-frequency acoustic energy and vibrational resonance, typically generated when a heavy, relatively soft, cushioned, or fleshy mass collides with a solid, yielding, or hollow structure.

In standard English lexicography, thump encompasses several distinct, interrelated categories of physical, sensory, and figurative application:

  • Primary Acoustic and Kinetic Impact: The basic mechanical action of hitting, pounding, or dropping an object so that it yields a dull, solid sound:
    • Dropping heavy loads: A heavy sack of grain, a trunk, or a fallen body hitting a wooden floorboard with a dull thump.
    • Manual percussion: Striking a table, counter, or podium with a closed fist or the flat of the hand to command attention or emphasize an argument.
    • Locomotive impact: The heavy, rhythmically deadened footsteps of boots marching over packed earth or hollow wooden floorboards in a quiet enclosure.
  • Cardiovascular and Visceral Physiology: The rapid, heavy, bounding pulsation of the human heart striking against the thoracic cage (ribs and sternum), induced by acute autonomic arousal, terrifying suspense, intense adrenaline surges, erotic anticipation, or strenuous physical exertion. In this sense, thump conveys the subjective, somatic sensation of one's own circulatory pulse amplified to an audible or tactile extreme.
  • Combat, Pugilism, and Physical Aggression: In informal and colloquial registers, to deliver a blunt, heavy punch, cuff, or bludgeoning blow to another person, or to physically assault an adversary ("he thumped his assailant on the jaw"). By extension, in sports and competitive contexts, to thump means to decisively, overwhelmingly, and brutally defeat an opposing team ("they thumped their rivals 5–0").
  • Acoustics, Bass Frequencies, and Modern Music: In audio engineering, electronic dance music (EDM), and popular acoustic jargon, thump refers to the punchy, low-frequency transient impact generated by kick drums, bass guitars, or subwoofers (typically in the 60–120 Hz frequency band) that can be physically felt in the chest cavity of the listener.
  • Idiomatic and Cultural Collocations:
    • Bible-thumper: A disparaging colloquial epithet for an aggressively zealous, dogmatic, or fundamentalist evangelical preacher or believer, derived from the theatrical habit of forcefully pounding the physical Bible upon the pulpit to emphasize doctrinal tenets.
    • Thumping majority / victory: A British and Commonwealth political idiom denoting an extraordinarily large, decisive, and overwhelming electoral triumph.
    • Thumping headache: A severe, debilitating cephalalgia characterized by rhythmic, pulsing, pounding arterial pressure in the temples.
    • Chest-thumping: Ostentatious, aggressive displays of bravado, dominance, or boastful self-assertion, modeled after the territorial behavior of male gorillas.
    • Things that go bump and thump in the night: An idiomatic reference to eerie, unexplained, frightening nocturnal noises in a dark house.
