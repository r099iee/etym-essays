• Addenda: Similar because both refer to supplementary material added to an existing text to expand or amend it; differs because addenda is a formal, Latinate bibliographic term designating appendices or lists of additions printed at the end of a book, whereas paperoles are physical, handwritten slips of paper glued directly into the body of working manuscript drafts.
  • Marginalia: Similar because both involve notes, reflections, and revisions written alongside a primary text; differs because marginalia consists of annotations inscribed directly into the blank margins of an existing page, whereas paperoles are physical paper extensions glued on when the margins have run out of physical space.
  • Paperasses: Similar because both are French derivations from papier conveying colloquial dismissiveness toward paper clutter; differs because paperasses denotes suffocating, sterile bureaucratic red tape, administrative files, and official forms, whereas paperoles specifically denotes small physical slips or artistic paper strips.
  • Palimpsest: Similar because both describe manuscript surfaces bearing multiple historical layers of inscription; differs because a palimpsest involves scraping away or writing over prior erased text on the same parchment, whereas paperoles achieve layering through physical accretion, accordion pasting, and outward expansion.
  • Insets: Similar because both denote pieces of text or imagery inserted into a larger layout; differs because an inset is a formal design element or printed graphic framed within a page, whereas paperoles are improvised, handmade compositional tools of authorial gestation.
  • Quilling: Similar because paperolle is the historical French technical name for quilling (rolled paper filigree); differs because quilling in modern English refers strictly to the decorative paper craft of rolling strips for art, whereas paperoles in modern literary English refers almost exclusively to Proustian manuscript slips.
