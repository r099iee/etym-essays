• Spun: Similar because both describe rapid circular motion; differs because spinning implies smooth, even rotation about an axis, while swirling is unsteady, eddying motion carried by a current or medium.
  • Whirled: Similar because both denote fast rotary movement, and whirl is the closest synonym; differs because whirl suggests a tighter, faster, more violent spin, while swirl suggests fluid, spiralling drift.
  • Eddied: Similar because both describe circular water or air motion; differs because eddy is restricted to small vortices in fluid (water eddies behind a rock), while swirl applies to any spiralling motion, including deliberate human action.
  • Twirled: Similar because both involve rotating motion; differs because twirl implies a deliberate, playful rotation of an object (twirling a baton) or of oneself, while swirl is usually driven by external force.
  • Churned: Similar because both describe agitated, turbulent motion of liquids; differs because churning implies violent, frothing disturbance (a churning sea), while swirling implies smoother spiral movement.
  • Spiralled: Similar because both describe a twisting, coiling path; differs because spiralling suggests a defined helical or conical path, while swirling is looser, more turbulent and less regular.
  • Coiled: Similar because both involve circular winding; differs because coiling implies a static, layered winding of a rope or snake, while swirling is dynamic motion.
  • Roiled: Similar because both describe disturbed, agitated liquid; differs because roiling implies murky, churning turbidity (roiled water, often with sediment), while swirling is cleaner and more graceful.
  • Circled: Similar because both describe round motion; differs because circling implies following a defined circular path, while swirling is chaotic and multidirectional.
  • Billowed: Similar because both describe flowing movement of smoke, sails or clouds; differs because billowing suggests swelling outward in waves, while swirling suggests rotary spiralling.
  • Gyrated: Similar because both denote circular motion; differs because gyrating implies a wiggling, pivoting motion (often of the body, as in dancing), while swirling describes fluid carried in eddies.
  • Wreathed: Similar because both can describe smoke or mist moving sinuously; differs because wreathing implies curling in rings or garlands, while swirling implies fast spiralling.
