• Baalbek: Similar because it is the same city — the modern standard transliteration of the same Arabic name; differs because "Baalbec" is the older English spelling used by Victorian writers and in Wilde's tale, while "Baalbek" is today's scholarly and cartographic form.
  • Balbec: Similar because it is simply another older English spelling of the very same city (Robert Wood's 1757 book, The Ruins of Balbec); differs only in orthography and period — a graphemic variant of the same name.
  • Heliopolis: Similar because it was the ancient Greek and Roman name of the very same city — "City of the Sun"; differs because it is a translation-name of another era and language, and the same Greek word was also applied to the Egyptian city of On by the Nile — a standing source of confusion between two different places.
  • Palmyra: Similar because it is the other great ruined Roman city of the Syrian world — one of the most spectacular ancient sites of the Levant, ruined, romanticised and excavated in the same decades; differs because it was a different city (ancient Tadmor, in the Syrian desert) with its own gods, queen (Zenobia) and history.
  • Tadmor: Similar because it is the Semitic name of Palmyra, exactly as Ba'al-bakk is the Semitic name of Heliopolis; differs because it belongs to a different city and is never applied to Baalbek — a parallel, not an alias.
  • The Beqaa (Valley): Similar because Baalbek's name is generally explained as "Baal of the Beqaa" — the valley is part of the city's very etymology; differs because the word names the whole alluvial plain of eastern Lebanon rather than the city on its slopes.
  • Anjar: Similar because it is another celebrated ancient ruin in the Beqaa Valley of Lebanon (an Umayyad palace-city of the early 8th century); differs because it is an early Islamic caliphal city — a small rectangular complex in stone and brick — not a colossal Roman sanctuary.
