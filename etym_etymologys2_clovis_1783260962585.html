Derivation chain for CLOVIS (a compound of two Germanic root words; chains given for each root in isolation, then the compound and its reflexes): ROOT 1 — *hludaz / *hlod- "loud, famous": 1. Proto-Indo-European *k'lew- "to hear" (reconstructed root; reflexes include Sanskrit sravas "fame", Greek kleos "glory, fame", Latin clueo "to be reputed"), c. 4th-3rd millennium BCE. 2. Proto-Germanic *hludaz "loud, famous" (reconstructed; attested in Gothic hilums "hearing", Old Norse hlyda "to listen", Old English hlud "loud", Old High German hlut "loud, clear"), c. 2nd-1st millennium BCE. The drift "heard" then "of which one hears" then "famous" is the standard reconstruction. 3. Old Frankish *hlod- "famous" (the royal name element, with the lengthened o-grade used in compounds; attested in the Merovingian onomasticon, e.g., Chlodowech, Chlothar), 5th c. CE. ROOT 2 — *wigą "battle": 1. Proto-Indo-European *weyk- "to overcome, conquer" (reconstructed root; reflexes include Latin vinco "I conquer" and victoria "victory", Old Irish fichid "he fights"), c. 4th-3rd millennium BCE. 2. Proto-Germanic *wigą "battle, war, fight" (reconstructed; attested in Gothic weihan "to fight", Old Norse vig "battle, slaying", Old English wig "war", Old High German wig "battle"), c. 2nd-1st millennium BCE. 3. Old Frankish *wig "battle" (attested as the royal name element), 5th c. CE. THE COMPOUND AND ITS REFLEXES: 4. Old Frankish *Hlodowig / Chlodovech "famous in battle" (two-element compound of the two roots above; attested as the king's own name), 5th c. CE. 5. Latinized Chlodovechus / Clodovicus / Hludovicus (attested in Gregory of Tours, Historia Francorum, 6th c., and in later annals and coins), 6th-10th c. CE. 6. French Clovis (the standard medieval and modern French form of the founder's name, fixed in French historiography and chronicles), c. 11th c.-present; the parallel modern French form Louis descends from the same Latinized stem via syncopation (Clodovicus to Clouis to Louis). 7. English CLOVIS (borrowed from French in the historical and archaeological vocabulary), 19th-20th c. CE. Note on the debate: the exact Proto-Germanic grade of the first element is debated — the royal onomasticon shows *hlod- (lengthened grade) beside the adjective *hludaz "loud" (u-grade); both are used in scholarship, and lengthening in compounds is a normal Germanic pattern. The derivation of modern French Louis from the same base via syncope is secure and uncontested. The modern archaeological "Clovis culture" is a secondary naming from the town Clovis, New Mexico (itself named in 1909 for the Frankish king), not a direct linguistic descent of the name's meaning.
