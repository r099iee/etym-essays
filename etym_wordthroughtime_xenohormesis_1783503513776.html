2004 Scientific Coinage - Xenohormesis is coined by David Sinclair's laboratory as a narrow hypothesis in evolutionary biology: animals sense plant stress molecules (like resveratrol) and mount adaptive stress responses, and it circulates only among gerontologists and molecular biologists.
Late 2000s Popular-Science Diffusion - The term leaks into anti-aging and wellness media as scientists and journalists popularize resveratrol research, so xenohormesis acquires a hopeful, almost therapeutic sheen as "the plant-stress diet theory" of longevity, often over-simplified.
Contemporary Sense - Today xenohormesis remains a technical scientific hypothesis, still debated, but widely known in longevity science and health writing as a key idea linking diet (polyphenols, stress compounds) to adaptive resilience, with its original evolutionary framing as the core of the term.
