• Regalia: Similar because both denote splendid ceremonial dress; differs because regalia is the official, institutional dress and insignia of royalty or office (crowns, robes of state), while finery is any showy personal dress for display, not necessarily official or institutional.
  • Raiment: Similar because both denote clothing; differs because raiment is an archaic, poetic word for garments generally (fine or plain), while finery specifically requires showiness and splendour.
  • Trappings: Similar because both denote ornamental accessories; differs because trappings implies the outward signs, often superficial, of office, wealth, or status (and is often faintly ironic), while finery is the actual brilliant dress or decoration itself.
  • Adornments: Similar because both denote decorative extras; differs because adornments is a neutral, general word for any ornament, while finery carries the specific sense of expensive, showy dress worn for display.
  • Bedecking / Decking out: Similar because both describe the act and result of dressing up splendidly; differs because these are verbal nouns of the action, while finery is the collection of fine things worn.
  • Accoutrements: Similar because both denote the accessories that complete an outfit; differs because accoutrements often has a military or practical cast (equipment, gear), while finery is purely festive and decorative.
  • Garb / Apparel: Similar because both mean clothing; differs because garb and apparel are neutral words for any dress, while finery is restricted to the fine, showy kind.
  • Pomp: Similar because both denote splendid display; differs because pomp is the ceremonial grandeur of an occasion, while finery is the personal dress and ornament that contribute to it.
  • Glad rags: Similar because both colloquially mean one's best clothes for a celebration; differs because glad rags is informal, humorous slang, while finery is standard, slightly literary English.
  • Sunday best: Similar because both denote the best clothes worn for display; differs because Sunday best names the specific "best outfit" idiom of church-going and formal wear, while finery covers any festive splendour and includes jewellery and ornament.
  • Ornaments: Similar because both denote decorative objects; differs because ornaments are objects of decoration generally (also on trees, buildings, and garments), while finery is especially the brilliant dress and trimmings worn by a person.
  • Pageant (sense of display): Similar because both suggest brilliant, showy display; differs because pageant names the spectacle or procession itself, while finery names the fine clothing worn within it.
