• Acheron: Similar because it is another river of the Greek Underworld, often associated with woe and sorrow; differs because Styx is more famously associated with divine oaths and invulnerability, and often serves as the primary boundary.
  • Lethe: Similar because it is a river of the Greek Underworld, related to the journey of the dead; differs because Lethe's primary function is to induce forgetfulness in the souls who drink from its waters, rather than being a boundary or oath-binder.
  • Cocytus: Similar because it is one of the five rivers of Hades, associated with lamentation and the wailing of the dead; differs because it lacks the specific symbolic weight of divine oaths and invulnerability attributed to the Styx.
  • Phlegethon: Similar because it is one of the five rivers of Hades, known as the river of fire; differs because its fiery nature gives it a distinct character, separate from the Styx's role as a boundary and oath-binder.
  • Rubicon: Similar because it represents a point of no return, an irreversible decision or commitment; differs because the Rubicon is a historical river (crossed by Julius Caesar) used metaphorically, while Styx is a mythological river with divine and eschatological implications.
  • Threshold: Similar because it signifies a boundary or a point of entry/exit, marking a transition; differs because "threshold" is a general term for any boundary, whereas Styx is a specific, mythological, and highly symbolic boundary between life and death, imbued with sacred power.
  • Abyss: Similar because it denotes a deep, immeasurable chasm or void, often associated with the underworld, hell, or profound darkness; differs because "abyss" is a more general term for a bottomless pit or profound depth, lacking the specific mythological context of a river and its associated rituals and divine powers.
