• Boreas: Similar because both are figures associated with wind in Greek mythology; differs because Boreas is specifically the personification of the North Wind, often depicted as fierce and cold, whereas Aeolus is the overall keeper of all winds.
  • Zephyr: Similar because both are wind deities; differs because Zephyr is the personification of the gentle West Wind, often associated with spring and mild breezes, while Aeolus commands all winds, both gentle and destructive.
  • Anemoi: Similar because this is the collective term for the Greek wind gods; differs because Anemoi refers to the individual personifications of the four cardinal winds (Boreas, Notus, Eurus, Zephyr), while Aeolus is their master and keeper, not one of the winds himself.
  • Storm God: Similar because both control powerful atmospheric phenomena; differs because 'Storm God' is a broader, often more primal archetype found across many mythologies (e.g., Zeus, Thor), whereas Aeolus's domain is specifically the winds, and he often acts under the authority of greater gods.
  • Wind Spirit: Similar because both represent an ethereal or divine entity associated with wind; differs because 'Wind Spirit' is a more general term for various mythological or folkloric beings, while Aeolus is a specific, named figure with a defined role in classical Greek mythology.
