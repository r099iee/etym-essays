• Crockery: Similar because both denote earthenware and kitchen-table vessels of the same ceramic family; differs because crockery is the collective term for the whole range of china and earthenware tableware, while crocks singles out the coarse, heavy, pot-like vessels, especially when dirty.
  • Dishes: Similar because both name the vessels of kitchen and table awaiting the wash-up; differs because a dish is a flat or open serving or eating vessel, whereas a crock is a deep, coarse pot for storing or cooking.
  • Pots: Similar because both are broad terms for cooking and storage vessels; differs because pot covers metal and ceramic cookware alike, while crock specifically means the earthenware, low-fired kind.
  • Jugs: Similar because both are hollow earthenware vessels used to hold liquids; differs because a jug is a handled vessel with a spout for pouring, while a crock is typically a wide-mouthed storage or cooking pot without a spout.
  • Pitchers: Similar because both are earthenware containers for liquid; differs because a pitcher is designed for pouring with a lip and handle, while a crock is a storage or cooking vessel, not a pouring one.
  • Earthenware: Similar because both refer to the same low-fired ceramic tradition; differs because earthenware names the material class itself, whereas crocks names the actual coarse vessels made from it.
  • Stoneware: Similar because both are utilitarian ceramic wares baked in a kiln; differs because stoneware is fired hotter until vitrified and is denser and stronger, while crockware is softer, more porous, and coarser.
  • Vessels: Similar because both name hollow containers; differs because vessel is the general, formal word for any container, while crock is the homely, specific word for a coarse earthenware pot.
  • Kitchenware: Similar because both belong to the utensils and containers of the kitchen; differs because kitchenware covers pans, cutlery, and tools of every material, while crocks are specifically earthenware vessels.
  • Jar: Similar because both are storage containers with a wide body and mouth for preserving; differs because a jar is often of glass or with a screw lid and a narrower mouth, while a crock is of coarse clay and wider-mouthed.
  • China / Porcelain: Similar because both are fired ceramic tableware; differs because china and porcelain are fine, translucent, vitrified ware of refinement, whereas a crock is coarse, opaque, low-fired ware of the kitchen and larder — the two stand at opposite ends of the ceramic hierarchy.
  • Crock-Pot / slow cooker: Similar because both derive from the earthenware cooking pot and its slow-cooking function; differs because Crock-Pot is a modern electric kitchen appliance named for its ceramic insert, while a crock is the traditional unglazed vessel itself.
