- 12th c. — Frankish origin in chivalric France: Old French arreier emerges to describe the outfitting of knights and the tactical organization of chivalric cavalry.
- Early 14th c. — Anglo-Norman importation: enters Middle English as araien, immediately serving both military needs (ordering troops) and courtly needs (dressing royalty in rich apparel).
- Late 14th c. — Chaucerian and chivalric romance: in Geoffrey Chaucer’s Knight’s Tale, both armies poised for combat and royal wedding guests are regularly described as richly and gorgeously arrayed.
- 16th – 17th c. — Biblical and military canonization: established in Tudor military treatises and the King James Bible, where Christ contrasts the lilies of the field with Solomon ‘arrayed in all his glory.’
- 18th c. — Augustan philosophical rhetoric: Samuel Johnson, Edward Gibbon, and Edmund Burke utilize arrayed figuratively to describe political factions, public prejudices, and social classes mobilized against reform.
- 19th c. – Present — Technological and mathematical divergence: while maintaining its grand literary and martial stature, the root expands into mathematics, optics, and computer science to designate structured tables and physical sensor matrices.
