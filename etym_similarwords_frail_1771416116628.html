• Fragile: Similar because both derive from Latin fragilis “easily broken” and denote easy damage; differs because fragile centres on the brittleness of the object (it shatters), while frail centres on weakness, especially of a living person or thin, delicate thing.
  • Weak: Similar because both mean lacking strength; differs because weak is the broad, neutral umbrella term for any lack of power (physical, moral, intellectual), whereas frail specifically implies thinness, delicacy, and vulnerability.
  • Delicate: Similar because both describe fineness and fragility; differs because delicate can describe fine, elegant things that are quite strong, while frail always carries the implication of vulnerability and insufficiency.
  • Feeble: Similar because both mean lacking strength; differs because feeble often sounds clinical or contemptuous and stresses ineffectiveness, while frail carries pathos, delicacy, and even grace.
  • Brittle: Similar because both describe easy breaking; differs because brittle applies to hard materials that shatter sharply and has no physical-weakness-of-a-person sense, whereas frail describes thin, weakened, soft things and people.
  • Infirm: Similar because both describe bodily weakness, often of the old or sick; differs because infirm is a formal, medical-flavoured term for chronic bodily weakness, while frail adds delicacy, slenderness, and emotional resonance.
  • Slight: Similar because both describe thinness and smallness of build; differs because slight is neutral about strength and can carry no weakness at all, while frail asserts vulnerability explicitly.
