Omaha most commonly denotes a Native American people of the Dhegiha Siouan group and their language, and by extension the major city in Nebraska named after them, as well as Omaha Beach in Normandy named after that city. As an ethnonym it refers to an enrolled member of the Omaha Tribe, to the community's history, culture and reservation in northeastern Nebraska and western Iowa. As a toponym it refers to the city of Omaha, Nebraska, the state's largest city and a regional economic hub. The meaning in any given sentence is disambiguated by context: tribal, linguistic, municipal, or historical-military.

Core senses in contemporary use:

  • Member of the Omaha Tribe: A person of Omaha ancestry, enrolled in the Omaha Tribe of Nebraska and Iowa. Example: "She is Omaha on her mother's side." This is the primary human sense, carrying sovereign political meaning under U.S. federal Indian law.
  • The Omaha people collectively: The tribal nation as a collective historical actor. Example: "The Omaha ceded millions of acres in 1854 but retained their reservation." Used in histories, treaties and anthropology.
  • Omaha-Ponca language (Umoⁿhoⁿ ie): The Dhegiha Siouan language spoken traditionally by Omaha and Ponca, now critically endangered with a handful of fluent first-language speakers and revitalization programs. Linguists classify it as a Dhegiha dialect continuum.
  • City of Omaha, Nebraska: Founded 1854, on the west bank of the Missouri River. Major meatpacking, railroad, insurance and finance center, home to Berkshire Hathaway, Union Pacific, Creighton University. Population ~480,000 city / ~1 million metro. Example: "They flew to Omaha for the Berkshire meeting."
  • Omaha Beach: Code-name for one of five Allied landing beaches in Normandy on 6 June 1944, named after the U.S. city under the American practice of naming beaches after cities. Site of the bloodiest D-Day fighting, now synonymous with sacrifice and heroism. Example: "Omaha was the deadliest sector on D-Day."
  • Attributive / adjectival use: Omaha kinship system, Omaha poker, Omaha steaks. In anthropology, "Omaha kinship" denotes a specific patrilineal classificatory kinship terminological system first described among the Omaha. In cards, "Omaha hold'em" is a poker variant where players get four hole cards. In commerce, "Omaha Steaks" is a famous mail-order meat brand leveraging the city's stockyards heritage.

Secondary and transferred senses arise by metonymy: people → language → place → product. Thus Omaha can evoke the Great Plains, beef and railroads, Midwestern pragmatism, Warren Buffett's "Oracle of Omaha," or D-Day valor, depending on discourse. No pejorative sense attaches; the term is respectful when referring to the tribe, though care is required to distinguish tribal Omaha from municipal Omaha to avoid erasure.

Grammatically Omaha is a proper noun, capitalized, with plural Omahas or Omaha for tribal members (both attested; Omaha prefer Omaha as plural). Pronunciation in RP is /ˈəʊməhɑː/, in General American /ˈoʊməhɑː/. It takes definite article only in historical collective phrasing ("the Omaha") but not for the city (*"the Omaha").
