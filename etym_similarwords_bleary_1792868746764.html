• Bleary-eyed: Similar because it is the same condition spelled out, dim watery eyes; differs because bleary-eyed names the eyes themselves, while bleary roams free to mornings, faces, photographs, and dawns.
  • Blear: Similar because both mean dimmed and filmed over; differs because blear is the older, harsher verb and adjective of rheumy eyes, while bleary is its softer modern child, comic where blear is clinical.
  • Blurry: Similar because both describe unfocused watery vision; differs because blurry belongs to images, photos, and outlines, while bleary belongs to the tired owner of the eyes behind them.
  • Bloodshot: Similar because both describe reddened sleepless eyes; differs because bloodshot is precise and alarming, veined red, while bleary is watery, dazed, and forgiving.
  • Groggy: Similar because both follow sleep, drink, or concussion with dazed befuddlement; differs because groggy stresses the staggering mind and body, while bleary stresses the filmed unfocused eyes.
  • Drowsy: Similar because both hover near sleep with heavy lids; differs because drowsy is the heaviness pulling toward sleep, often pleasant, while bleary is the filmed aftermath, usually comic and dishevelled.
  • Watery: Similar because bleary eyes brim and film with water; differs because watery is general and neutral, suiting soups, colours, and sunshine, while bleary is specifically the tired filmed eye.
  • Unfocused: Similar because both fail to settle the gaze sharp; differs because unfocused is cool and technical, suiting cameras and strategies, while bleary is warm and human, smelling of pillows and night shifts.
