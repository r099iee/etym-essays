• Hellas: Similar because Thessaly is an integral, historically celebrated territory of Greece; differs because Hellas encompasses the entirety of the Greek cultural, linguistic, and modern national realm, whereas Thessaly is a specific geographic plain and administrative periphery in north-central Greece.
  • Pelasgia: Similar because Pelasgia was the archaic, pre-Homeric mythical name applied to the Thessalian territory prior to the Thessalian tribal migration; differs because Pelasgia specifically denotes the shadowy, indigenous Bronze Age Pelasgian inhabitants of early Greek myth rather than the historical regional entity.
  • Phthia: Similar because Phthia is a prominent historic district located in southern Thessaly; differs because Phthia specifically designates the Homeric kingdom ruled by Peleus and his son Achilles (homeland of the Myrmidons), whereas Thessaly encompasses the vast expanse of both the northern and southern plains.
  • Boeotia: Similar because both are fertile, agricultural geographic regions of central Greece that played prominent roles in classical military history; differs because Boeotia lies directly to the south of Thessaly (centered on Thebes) and was famous for heavy hoplite infantry, whereas Thessaly was renowned for its elite horse cavalry and expansive plains.
  • Macedonia: Similar because both share a historic northern mountainous border along Mount Olympus and developed formidable cavalry forces; differs because Macedonia emerged as a distinct kingdom and subsequent world empire under Philip II and Alexander the Great, whereas Thessaly was an aristocratic federal tetrarchy.
  • Larissa: Similar because Larissa has functioned continuously as the historic, cultural, and modern administrative capital of Thessaly; differs because Larissa designates the specific urban municipality situated along the Pineios River, while Thessaly denotes the entire surrounding geographic basin and regional population.
  • Magnesia: Similar because Magnesia forms the coastal eastern mountainous district of the Thessalian region; differs because Magnesia is characterized by the rugged maritime terrain of Mount Pelion and the Pagasetic Gulf, whereas Thessaly proper is dominated by expansive, inland agricultural flatlands.
