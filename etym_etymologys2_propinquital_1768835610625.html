PIE *pro- "before, forth, in front" (earliest reconstructable stage of the root) to Latin pro "before, forward, in front of, on behalf of" (classical Latin, 1st c. BCE) to Latin prope "near, close at hand" (generally analysed as *pro-kwe, literally "and in front", i.e. "what lies before one" - the "before" becomes the "near"; early and classical Latin, 3rd-1st c. BCE) to Latin propinquus "neighbouring, adjacent; related by kin" (prope + -inquus; 2nd-1st c. BCE) to Latin propinquitas "nearness, vicinity; kinship, relationship" (1st c. BCE) to Old French propinquite (learned borrowing; 12th-13th c.) to English propinquity "nearness; relationship" (15th c.) to English propinquital "of, relating to, arising from propinquity" (propinquity + adjectival suffix -al; a rare, chiefly 19th-20th c. dictionary formation; the rootWord). Note: the analysis of Latin prope as *pro-kwe is the standard scholarly account but is not beyond dispute; some authorities see prope as built on the base of proximus with an unexplained vowel alternation, or as a separate formation of uncertain shape. The remainder of the chain follows the attested Latin and Romance record without gaps.
