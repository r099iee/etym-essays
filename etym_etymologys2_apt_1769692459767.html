Linear derivation chain (real attested forms with dates; 5 stages): 1. PIE root *ap- (also *ep-) "to reach, touch, grasp, fit" (reconstructed, c. 4500-2500 BCE; Hittite epmi "I take, seize", Sanskrit apnoti "he reaches, obtains", Greek hapto "to fasten, touch"). 2. Latin apere "to fasten, join, tie, attach, bind" (attested in early / archaic Latin, from PIE *ap-; preserved mainly in derivatives and compounds). 3. Latin aptus - past participle of apere - "fastened, joined, fitted; suitable, appropriate" (attested Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE; Caesar, Cicero). 4. Old French apte "fitting, suitable" (attested c. 12th c.; a learned borrowing, part of the clerical and scholarly vocabulary). 5. Middle English / Modern English apt "suitable, appropriate; inclined, prone; quick to learn" (attested from the 14th c.; the "inclined" sense from the 16th c.; in Jane Austen, Pride and Prejudice, 1813, "you are a great deal too apt... to like people in general") - APT. Note: the same Latin aptus gave English adapt (ad- + aptare), inept (in- "not" + aptus), and aptitude (Latin aptitudo). The PIE root *ap- is reconstructed; the Latin stage and all later stages are attested.
