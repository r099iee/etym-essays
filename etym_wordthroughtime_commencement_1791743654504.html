- 13th to 14th c. – The act of beginning or starting; initiation of an action - In Anglo-Norman and early Middle English legal and narrative texts, denoted the initial moment, origin, or start of a period or endeavor.
- Late 14th c. – Formal university inception / conferral of degrees - Specialized in medieval universities (Paris, Oxford, Cambridge) for the ceremony wherein a graduate incepted, or "commenced" teaching as an authorized Master or Doctor.
- 16th to 17th c. – Legal terminology & contractual starting points - Fixed in common law statutes and deeds to demarcate the effective start date of covenants, leases, or parliamentary acts ("from the commencement of this act").
- 18th to 19th c. – Grand American collegiate festival - Became the premier intellectual and social festival of American colleges (Harvard, Brown, Yale), marked by Latin valedictories, civic parades, and alumni donations.
- 20th c. to Present – Universal North American graduation ritual - While retaining its broader sense of "beginning," the word predominantly evokes high school and university degree conferral, symbolized by caps, gowns, and honorary doctorates.
