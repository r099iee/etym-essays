- 16th century - from Medieval Latin 'remonstrare' ('to point out, show again'): at first simply to demonstrate or set forth
- 17th century - shifts to its modern sense: to urge strong reasons against a course of action, to protest earnestly by argument
- 18th-19th century - institutional and religious use; 'remonstrance' becomes a formal written protest or petition
- Contemporary - a formal, literary verb for reasoned, forceful objection, often of one without coercive power relying on argument and feeling
