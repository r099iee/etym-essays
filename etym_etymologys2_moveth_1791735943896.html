Note: The word 'moveth' represents a fascinating morphological hybrid combining a Latinate verbal base with a native Germanic grammatical inflection. The root 'move' traces via Anglo-Norman French from Latin 'movēre' (from PIE *mew- 'to push away, move'). The third-person singular present indicative suffix '-eth' descends directly from Old English '-eþ', which traces back through Proto-Germanic '*-iþi' to the primary Proto-Indo-European verbal ending '*-eti'. While colloquial spoken English in London gradually replaced '-eth' with Northern English '-s' ('moves') during the 15th and 16th centuries, '-eth' was deliberately retained in liturgical texts and the 1611 Authorized King James Bible for sacred solemnity and metric cadence.
