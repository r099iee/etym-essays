• corner: Similar in being a recess or angle of a space; differs because corner is the colourless generic term, carrying none of the seclusion, shelter, and tucked-away secrecy of a nook.
  • recess: Similar in being a space set back from a line of wall; differs because recess is architectural and neutral, while a nook is small, cosy, and implies being hidden or protected.
  • cranny: Similar in naming a tiny hiding place; differs because cranny is a narrow crack or fissure and almost always appears in the alliterative pair "nook and cranny," together meaning every conceivable hiding place, however small.
  • niche: Similar in being a recess in a wall; differs because niche has wandered from architecture to a person's appointed "place" or role in life, while nook stays put in snug, physical shelter.
  • alcove: Similar in being a recessed space; differs because an alcove is a recess large enough to hold a bed or seat — bigger and more furnished than a nook.
  • cubbyhole: Similar in being a small, snug compartment; differs because a cubbyhole is an enclosed pigeon-hole or storage box, domestic and functional, while a nook is an open sheltered corner.
  • inglenook: Similar in naming a snug corner by a hearth; differs because inglenook is specifically the recess beside a fireplace, a subset of nook's wider meaning.
  • hollow: Similar in being a sheltered concave place; differs because a hollow is a natural depression in the ground or a tree, without the implication of an angle or corner.
  • cleft: Similar in being a small sheltered fissure in rock; differs because a cleft is a narrow split or opening, while a nook is an angle or corner, though in wild landscape the two may meet.
  • crevice: Similar in naming a narrow hiding place; differs because crevice stresses the crack itself in rock or wall, while nook stresses the cornered, sheltered space.
  • den: Similar in being a small private shelter; differs because a den is a dwelling or lair, usually of an animal or a person's private study, and is enclosed, whereas a nook is an open corner.
  • hideaway: Similar in being a place of concealment; differs because a hideaway is a whole retreat or refuge, often a building, while a nook is a small corner within a larger place.
  • retreat: Similar in being a sheltered place; differs because a retreat is a place of withdrawal, often grand or distant, while a nook is small, near, and cosy.
  • cove: Similar in naming a small sheltered place; differs because a cove is a small bay or inlet of the sea, while a nook belongs to rooms and inland landscape.
  • angle: Similar in being a corner where surfaces meet; differs because angle is a mathematical, neutral term for any corner, without any of nook's shelter or secrecy.
  • bay: Similar in being a recessed space in a building; differs because a bay is a wide, often windowed recess, much larger and more open than a nook.
