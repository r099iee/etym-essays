PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*(s)kewH- / *(s)keu-
"to cover, conceal, hide, protect"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*op-skūros
"covered over, darkened, dim"
Note: Compound formed from prefix *op- ('over, against') + root adjective *-skūros ('covered', cognate with scūtum 'shield').
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
obscūrus
"dark, dim, shadowy; hidden, unintelligible, enigmatic"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
obscūritās (stem obscūritāt-)
"darkness, gloom; indistinctness, obscurity, unintelligibility"
Note: Formed from adjective obscūrus + abstract quality suffix -tās.
↓
Old French / Middle French (c. 12th–14th c.)
obscurité
"darkness, gloom; unclarity, mystery"
↓
Middle English (c. late 14th c.)
obscurite
"physical darkness, spiritual blindness; unclear passage"
↓
Early Modern English to Modern English (c. 16th c. – present)
obscurity (plural: obscurities)
"instances or qualities of being ambiguous, enigmatic, or shrouded in darkness"
