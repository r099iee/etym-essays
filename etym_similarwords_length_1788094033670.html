• Extent: Similar because both name how far something reaches in space or scope; differs because extent is broader and abstract (extent of damage, extent of knowledge), while length specifically measures the long dimension or a duration.
  • Span: Similar because both measure distance or duration (span of a bridge, attention span); differs because span implies the full stretch between two endpoints, often a reach or spread rather than a single dimension.
  • Duration: Similar because both cover time (“the length of the concert”); differs because duration is exclusively temporal, while length is spatial first and temporal by extension.
  • Distance: Similar because both measure space; differs because distance is the gap between two points along any path, while length is the extent of a single object or dimension.
  • Stretch: Similar because both denote a continuous extent (“a stretch of road”); differs because stretch is a concrete piece or segment, while length is the measure of that piece.
  • Reach: Similar because both can mean how far something extends; differs because reach implies capability of touching or spanning (arm's reach, market reach), not a measured dimension.
  • Measure / measurement: Similar because both involve quantifying size; differ because these name the act or system of measuring, while length is the specific property measured.
  • Size: Similar because both describe magnitude; differs because size is the general overall magnitude (volume, area), while length isolates one dimension.
  • Period / term: Similar because both denote stretches of time; differ because period and term are calendar or contractual units, while length is the mere measure of a duration.
  • Eventually / at last: Similar because these gloss the idiom “at length” in its temporal sense; differ because they are adverbs of outcome, not nouns of dimension.
