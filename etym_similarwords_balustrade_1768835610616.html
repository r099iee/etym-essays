• Parapet: Similar in being a protective barrier at the edge of a roof, balcony, or wall-top; differs because a parapet is a low solid wall with no gaps, whereas a balustrade is open and skeletal, letting light and air pass between its posts.
  • Railing: Similar in being a fence-like barrier along an edge; differs because railing is the generic catch-all term for any barrier of bars or posts, while balustrade specifically names a row of turned baluster posts beneath a continuous rail.
  • Banister: Similar in being the rail and posts along a staircase; differs because banister is the everyday folk-evolved word for the stair rail, whereas balustrade is the formal architectural term covering balconies and terraces as well as stairs.
  • Handrail: Similar in being a horizontal rail that guides and supports; differs because a handrail is a single bar grasped for support with no posts beneath it, while a balustrade is the whole assembly of posts and rail.
  • Baluster: Similar in belonging to the same architectural family; differs because the baluster is the individual turned post, while the balustrade is the completed row of balusters carrying the rail — the part as opposed to the whole.
  • Coping: Similar in being the capping member along a wall-top; differs because coping is the plain top course of a solid wall or parapet, without the open rhythm of posts that defines a balustrade.
