PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*ter- / *terh₁-
"to cross over, pass through, overcome"
Note: Ancestor of Sanskrit tarati ('crosses over') and Latin trans ('across').
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
trānsīre
"to go across, pass over, cross, pass away"
Note: Compound of trāns ('across') + īre ('to go').
↓
Old French (c. 1100–1250)
transir
"to pass over, depart from life, swoon, freeze with fear"
↓
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 1200–1300)
transe
"passage, departure from life, state of swoon or extreme fear"
↓
Middle English (c. 1350–1400)
traunce / trance
"state of suspended animation, stupor, spiritual ecstasy"
↓
Early Modern English (Verb Formation) (c. 1580–1600)
entrance (verb)
"to put into a trance, throw into a swoon, ravish with delight"
Note: Formed with English prefix en- ('put into') + trance.
