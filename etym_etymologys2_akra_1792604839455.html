Note on historical debate: Philologists across comparative Indo-European studies have analyzed whether the verbal base *ḱek- / *ḱak- possesses secure cognates outside Indo-Iranian. While Manfred Mayrhofer (*Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindoarischen*) notes that the root is overwhelmingly productive in Indo-Aryan (giving Sanskrit śakti "power, energy", śakya "possible", and śakra "mighty"), potential external cognates have been suggested in Celtic (e.g., Middle Irish cacht "maidservant, captive" via the sense of "that which is mastered/bound") and Baltic, though these remain tentative. Within Sanskrit grammar, the morphological formation of śakra from root śak- via the standard primary adjectival suffix -ra (governed by Pāṇini’s rule sphāyatāṅkalanipatitrapinadibhyaḥ kan-ran-tran-vā) is universally recognized by ancient and modern lexicographers alike as entirely regular and transparent.
