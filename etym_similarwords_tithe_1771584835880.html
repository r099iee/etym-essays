• Tenth: Similar because both denote one part in ten; differs because tenth is the neutral, purely numerical ordinal fraction, whereas tithe adds religious and historical weight—a tenth paid as church dues.
  • Share: Similar because both are a portion of a whole; differs because share has no fixed size and is the neutral everyday allotment word, while tithe is fixed at exactly one tenth and carries a formal, sacred register.
  • Portion: Similar because both are a part or allotment of something; differs because portion is general and variable in size, whereas tithe is precise, fixed, and institutional.
  • Fraction: Similar because both mean a part of a whole; differs because fraction is the modern mathematical term for any part, wholly neutral, whereas tithe is the archaic, churchly tenth-part.
  • Mite: Similar because both are small but sacred or dutiful contributions; differs because a mite is a tiny and humble offering of any size, while a tithe is a specific and substantial tenth.
  • Modicum: Similar because both name a small amount; differs because a modicum is a small but sufficient quantity of no fixed proportion, while a tithe is exactly a tenth.
  • Iota: Similar because both name a very small part; differs because an iota is the least possible amount (from the Greek letter), with no fraction sense and no religious or fiscal weight.
  • Due / Levy / Tax: Similar because both tithe and these are obligatory payments; differs because a tithe is specifically the tenth-part ecclesiastical levy, while a tax, due, or levy is a general exaction of any size by any authority.
  • Offering / Contribution: Similar because both may denote religious giving; differs because an offering or contribution is voluntary and of any amount, whereas a tithe is the prescribed tenth.
