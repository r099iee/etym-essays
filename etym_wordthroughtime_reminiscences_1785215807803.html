- 16th century philosophical memory - enters English as the philosophical and psychological faculty of recollection, recalling what the mind once knew; the word is learned and inward.
- 17th-18th century mental act - used for the act of summoning past experience back into consciousness, often in a nostalgic or retrospective key.
- 19th century published genre - the plural becomes the title and genre of a book of retrospective portraits: Carlyle's Reminiscences (1881) records late-in-life impressions of the dead and the living, giving the word an elegiac, anecdotal, and sometimes querulous tone.
- Contemporary use - the plural suggests an accumulated, often published collection of memories devoted to people and scenes, while "reminisce" keeps the everyday act of recalling; the genre-sense remains literary.
