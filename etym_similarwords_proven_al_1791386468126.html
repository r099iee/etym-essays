• Occitan: the wider Romance language of southern France, of which Provençal is a variety; differs because Occitan is the superordinate cover-term, while Provençal is the regional variety centred on Provence.
  • Languedocien: a sister Occitan dialect, similar in structure and literary heritage; differs because it belongs to Languedoc rather than Provence and has its own local norms.
  • Gascon: another Occitan variety, similar in being a southern Gallo-Romance speech; differs by its distinctive Basque-influenced phonology and its south-western territory.
  • Auvergnat: an Occitan dialect of the Massif Central, similar in Romance ancestry; differs geographically and in its northern, upland dialect features.
  • Limousin: an Occitan dialect and an important medieval literary centre; differs because Limousin was the home of many troubadours, yet is not Provençal proper.
  • Vivaro-Alpine: the Alpine Occitan variety, similar in being part of the same continuum; differs by its mountain vocabulary and its spread into the French and Italian Alps.
  • Catalan: a close sister of Occitan within Gallo-Romance, historically influenced by the troubadours; differs because Catalan became a national language with its own standard and state-level status.
  • French: the northern langue d'oïl that politically overshadowed Provençal; differs by its oïl phonology and its role as the centralising national standard.
  • Midi: a geographic and cultural term for southern France, similar in evoking the sunny South; differs because it is a looser regional label and not a language name.
  • Meridional: a formal adjective meaning "southern", similar in referring to the south; differs because it is not specific to Provence and carries no linguistic content.
  • Prouvençau: the Provençal-language name for the language and its people; differs only by being the insider endonym rather than the English exonym.
  • Félibrige: the literary revival movement and its spelling tradition, closely associated with Mistral; differs because it names an institution and orthographic school, not the language itself.
