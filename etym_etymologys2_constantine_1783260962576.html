PIE root *steh₂- (traditional notation *stā-), "to stand, to set upright, to be firm" — the root of Sanskrit sthā- "to stand", Greek hístēmi "I stand", Latin stāre "to stand", Germanic *standan "to stand" ↓ Latin stāre, "to stand" — the base verb; source of the family status, statua, statio, stabulum (attested from the earliest Latin literature, 3rd c. BCE) ↓ Latin cōnstāre, "to stand firm, to hold together, to be consistent" — compound of con- "together, with" + stāre "to stand"; literally "to stand together"; attested classical (Cicero, Caesar) ↓ Latin cōnstāns (genitive cōnstantis), "standing firm, steadfast, constant" — present participle of cōnstāre, used as a Roman cognomen of moral praise (with the abstract cōnstantia "steadfastness"); attested from the 1st c. BCE ↓ Latin Cōnstantīnus, "belonging to Constans" — possessive/gentilic formation on the stem Constant- with the suffix -īnus "belonging to"; the name of Flavius Valerius Constantinus, the emperor (c. 272–337 AD); the Greek form is Kōnstantĩnos (Κωνσταντῖνος) ↓ Old French Constantin — from Latin Cōnstantīnus; taken into Middle English as Constantyn / Constantin ↓ Constantine — the emperor's name in modern English (also the base of Constantinople, Greek Kōnstantinoúpolis "city of Constantine") ◄ TARGET DEBATED / NUANCED: The internal formation of the imperial name is transparent and uncontested: Cōnstantīnus is the suffixal derivative (in -īnus) of the well-attested cognomen/adjective cōnstāns "steadfast". The only theoretical element is the PIE laryngeal: modern notation reconstructs *steh₂- "to stand" (with the *h₂ laryngeal), and the older form *stā- is simply the traditional pre-laryngeal rendering of the same root — there is no genuine dispute about the underlying PIE etymon, only about its notational representation.
