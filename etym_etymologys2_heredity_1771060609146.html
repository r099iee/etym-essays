Single root word: heredity (from the English noun heredity). Linear derivation chain: 1. Proto-Indo-European root *gʰeh₁- (also reconstructed *ghe-, *gʰē-), "to leave, leave behind, abandon, forsake, let go; to be empty" — reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE. The same root yields Vedic Sanskrit jahāti "he leaves, abandons," Ancient Greek khōra "empty space, land" and khēros "bereft, widowed." NOTE: the precise PIE shape behind Latin hērēs is debated; some scholars instead propose a root *gʰedʰ- "to take, seize" or a distinct *ǵʰer-; the "leave behind" derivation is the most widely accepted but not beyond dispute. 2. Latin hērēs, genitive hērēdis, "heir, heiress; the person who takes what is left" — attested from the early Roman Republic (Law of the Twelve Tables, 5th century BCE); derived from the PIE root with a suffix -ēs/-ēd- whose exact preform is not directly attested. 3. Latin hērēditās, hērēditātis, "heirship, inheritance, the condition and rights of an heir; an inherited estate" — classical Latin, attested from the 2nd–1st century BCE (Cicero, Caesar); formed from hērēs + abstract suffix -tāt-. 4. Old French eredité / hérédité, "inheritance, hereditary succession, heritage" — by regular sound change from Latin hērēditāte(m) (accusative), attested from the 11th–12th centuries (Chanson de Roland era). 5. Middle English heredite, "inheritance, hereditary succession; the passing of property or privileges to heirs" — borrowed from Old French, attested from the early 15th century (c. 1420–1475). 6. Modern English heredity (early spellings heredite, hereditie), "the transmission of physical and mental characteristics from parents to offspring" — the biological sense crystallised in the mid-19th century (used by Herbert Spencer, c. 1862–1863, and Darwinian writers); the legal sense "inheritance" was the earlier English meaning, attested from the 15th century onward. TARGET: heredity. Debated / nuanced note: the semantic leap from "inheritance of property" to "inheritance of traits" occurred in 19th-century scientific English; the PIE root *gʰeh₁- "to leave behind" is the standard reconstruction for Latin hērēs, but the intermediate suffixation (*gʰeh₁r-?) is not attested and is reconstructed by inference.
