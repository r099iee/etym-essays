• Shouted: Similar because both raise the voice loudly; differs because shouting is the plain, general term for loud speech, while a whoop is a shaped, rising, exultant cry rather than words.
  • Yelled: Similar because both are loud vocal outbursts; differs because yelling often springs from fear, anger, or effort, whereas whooping is rhythmic, triumphant, and festive.
  • Screamed: Similar because both are shrill; differs because screaming is agonized and thin, the cry of pain or terror, not the exultant wail of a whoop.
  • Shrieked: Similar because both are high-pitched; differs because a shriek is higher still, thin, panicked, and usually involuntary, while a whoop is a deliberate rising shout of joy or derision.
  • Hollered: Similar because both mean calling out loudly across a distance; differs because hollering is the informal American word for a plain, flat call, lacking the hunt-like rhythmic exuberance of a whoop.
  • Hallooed: Similar because both are cries meant to be heard at a distance; differs because halloo is the archaic hunter's cry of pursuit, older and more specifically tied to the chase than the general whoop.
  • Hooted: Similar because both are derisive or resonant calls; differs because hooting names the owl's round, low note and by extension booing derision, not the high, rising excited whoop.
  • Crowed: Similar because both are exultant cries of triumph; differs because crowing is the raucous call of the rooster and, figuratively, gloating over a victory, while a whoop is a human shout of joy.
  • Whooped it up / celebrated: Similar because both involve boisterous festivity; differs because celebrating is the broad term for any rejoicing, while whooping names the specific shouted noise of the revel.
