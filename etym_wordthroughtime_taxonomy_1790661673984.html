- 1813 coinage as botanical theory - technical and prestigious, confined to naturalists debating laws of plant classification
- 1820s–1850s adoption in English natural history - neutral scientific term for ranked classification of plants and animals, companion to system and nomenclature
- 1860s–1900s Darwinian reinterpretation - same ranks but newly genealogical in connotation; taxonomy becomes the record of descent, more ambitious and contested
- 1930s–1970s institutional and quarrelsome phase - professionalized with codes, museums, and feuding schools (phenetics vs. cladistics); connotation of dry, expert, museum-bound labour
- 1956–1990s escape into education and management - Bloom's Taxonomy makes the word familiar to teachers; businesses and librarians adopt it for any category tree; connotation broadens from biological to bureaucratic-organizing
- 2000s–present everyday information-architecture word - a website menu, product catalogue, or chart of prose types is casually called a taxonomy; prestigious yet slightly jargonistic, promising completeness and scientific order even for modest lists
