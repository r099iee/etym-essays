needlewoman means a woman who sews, especially one skilled with the needle or employed to sew for pay: a seamstress, dressmaker, embroiderer, or plain sewer. In the source sentence — Ruskin in Praeterita recalling his mother's schooling at Mrs Rice's establishment, where my mother was taught evangelical principles, and became the pattern girl and best needlewoman in the school — it is the schoolgirl-accomplishment sense: among middle-class Regency girls, plain and fancy needlework — hemming, seaming, marking, darning, embroidering samplers — was examined as strictly as grammar, and to be best needlewoman was to be first in industry, neatness, and obedience. Joined to pattern girl (model pupil), the phrase paints Ruskin's mother as the evangelical ideal: pious, diligent, and dexterous with her fingers.

In wider Victorian use a needlewoman is above all a working woman who lives by the needle: the seamstress stitching shirts at twopence an hour, the dressmaker fitting gowns, the milliner's hand, the army contractor's widow. Thomas Hood's Song of the Shirt (1843), Punch cartoons of the distressed needlewoman, and parliamentary reports on sweated labour made her the emblem of respectable female poverty — industrious, underpaid, often consumptive, sewing through the night by a guttering candle. To call a woman a needlewoman rather than a lady or servant therefore located her precisely: genteel enough to be skilled, poor enough to sell the skill.

  • Professional sense: paid sewer, seamstress: one who makes, mends, or alters clothes or linen for money; e.g. to employ a needlewoman, a needlewoman's wages, the Society for the Relief of Distressed Needlewomen. This is the dictionary head sense. It covers plain sewing (shirts, sheets, underclothing), custom dressmaking, and fine embroidery, and it implies hand skill rather than machine operation, though later it extends to machinists.
  • Accomplishment sense (source sense): maid or schoolgirl skilled at needlework: e.g. the best needlewoman in the school, a neat needlewoman, she was a good needlewoman. Here no wages are involved; the word praises feminine training. The bridge is dexterity made moral: a girl whose seams are straight is assumed to be truthful, patient, and chaste — the evangelical equation Ruskin's mother embodied.
  • Craft-specialist senses: embroiderer, lace-maker, tapestry worker, sampler-maker; e.g. an artistic needlewoman, a crewel needlewoman. In museums and guilds needlewoman can honour high art — the medieval women who stitched the Bayeux Tapestry are sometimes called needlewomen — lifting the word from drudgery to heritage.
  • Figurative and institutional uses: prisons, workhouses, and schools appointed a needlewoman to teach and supervise sewing; novels contrast the lady with the needlewoman to dramatise fallen gentility (Dickens's Little Dorrit sews; Gaskell's Ruth sews). Adjectives sort the trade: plain needlewoman, fancy needlewoman, slop needlewoman (cheap ready-made), upper needlewoman (finishing).
  • Grammar, gender, and modern fate: needlewoman is frankly gendered: the male equivalent is tailor (prestigious, cutting) or seamster (rare), while sewer is gender-neutral but homographic with sewer (drain). Feminist and labour history now often prefers seamstress, dressmaker, or garment worker; needlewoman survives in historical writing, school reports, and craft circles, carrying a period perfume of muslin, samplers, and evangelical prize-givings.
  • What it does not mean: it never means a woman who merely wears needles (a nurse giving injections is not a needlewoman), nor a dealer in needles, nor — despite the school context — a teacher of doctrine. The needle is always the sewing needle, and the woman is always its mistress.

To be best needlewoman in 1810s Peckham, as Ruskin's mother was, meant straight hems, invisible stitches, and a sampler without a dropped cross — small triumphs on which a whole ideology of womanhood was hung. The word keeps that entire vanished curriculum folded inside it, alongside the later pathos of the sweated shirt-maker singing through the night.
