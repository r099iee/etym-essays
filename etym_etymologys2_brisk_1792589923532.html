Note on historical debate: The exact immediate donor language of English brisk is the subject of scholarly nuance. Philologists (such as the Oxford English Dictionary and Walter Skeat) regard brisk as an anglicized phonetic adaptation of French brusque, which entered English in the late 16th century alongside the direct loan brusque. Other Celticists have proposed a direct influence or convergence with Scottish Gaelic and Irish briosc ("brittle, active, lively, quick"). Both branches ultimately converge semantically on ideas of physical sharpness, snapping vitality, and sudden energetic movement.
