- Ancient Greece (c. 8th century BCE - 4th century CE) - Revered as the supreme deity, king of the gods, god of sky, thunder, law, and justice. Central to religious worship, civic life, and moral order. Connotation: awe-inspiring, powerful, sometimes capricious, patriarchal.
- Roman Era (1st century BCE - 5th century CE) - Syncretized with Jupiter. Maintained cultural significance as a literary and artistic archetype, though direct worship waned with the rise of Christianity. Connotation: classical, symbolic of divine authority.
- Medieval & Renaissance Europe (5th - 17th centuries) - Primarily known through classical texts (Homer, Hesiod, Ovid). Viewed as a pagan deity, but his myths were studied for their literary and allegorical value. Connotation: mythological, literary, a figure from antiquity.
- Enlightenment & Modern Era (18th century - Present) - Continues as a foundational figure in Western culture, art, and literature. Used metaphorically to denote supreme power or authority. Connotation: archetypal, powerful, a cultural reference point for leadership and divine might.
