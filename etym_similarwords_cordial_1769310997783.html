• Genial: Similar because both describe warm, friendly manner; differs because genial implies cheerful, easygoing, sunny warmth, while cordial is polite, sincere, heartfelt warmth from the heart.
  • Affable: Similar because both describe approachable friendliness; differs because affable emphasises conversational ease and willingness to be addressed, while cordial emphasises genuine warmth of feeling.
  • Amicable: Similar because both describe friendly relations; differs because amicable names peaceable, good-humoured dealings between parties without implying deep personal warmth, while cordial implies warmth felt and shown.
  • Warm: Similar because both name genuine friendliness; differs because warm is the broad, informal everyday word, while cordial is more formal and carries the "from the heart" imagery.
  • Friendly: Similar because both name goodwill toward others; differs because friendly is the plain general term, while cordial adds a flavour of formal, sincere, heartfelt expression.
  • Heartfelt: Similar because both derive from the heart as the seat of feeling; differs because heartfelt modifies emotions and expressions (heartfelt thanks), while cordial modifies manner and relations.
  • Sincere: Similar because both exclude falseness; differs because sincere is a general moral term for genuineness, while cordial specifically names warmth that is felt and conveyed.
  • Hearty: Similar because both are built on the heart and mean warm and sincere; differs because hearty adds vigour, enthusiasm, and often loudness ("a hearty laugh"), while cordial is quieter and more refined.
  • Warm-hearted: Similar because both locate warmth in the heart; differs because warm-hearted is a character trait (kind and sympathetic by nature), while cordial is a manner or feeling expressed on an occasion.
  • Gracious: Similar because both describe pleasant, courteous warmth; differs because gracious emphasises courtesy, dignity, and generosity of manner, while cordial emphasises sincere feeling.
  • Convivial: Similar because both describe warmth among people; differs because convivial specifically belongs to feasting and merry company, while cordial can describe any warm interaction.
  • Hospitable: Similar because both describe welcoming warmth toward others; differs because hospitable names the action of receiving guests generously, while cordial names the warm feeling itself.
  • Welcoming: Similar because both describe a warm reception; differs because welcoming is concrete and behavioural, while cordial is about the manner and tone of the welcome.
  • Sociable: Similar because both describe friendliness toward others; differs because sociable is a general disposition to enjoy company, while cordial is a quality of a specific manner or feeling.
  • Congenial: Similar because both describe pleasant agreeableness; differs because congenial refers to compatibility of tastes and temperaments ("a congenial companion"), while cordial refers to warmth of manner.
  • Liqueur (noun sense): Similar because both name a sweet alcoholic drink; differs because liqueur is the standard term for a sweetened, flavoured, often strong spirit, while cordial in American usage is such a drink and in British usage a non-alcoholic concentrated fruit syrup.
  • Tonic (noun sense): Similar because both historically name a health-restoring drink; differs because tonic emphasises general strength and vigour, while cordial specifically names a heart-restoring or sweet drink.
  • Restorative (noun sense): Similar because both name something that revives; differs because restorative is a general medical term for anything that renews strength, while cordial kept the specific heart-reviving and drink senses.
  • Elixir (noun sense): Similar because both can name a sweet medicinal or magical drink; differs because elixir is fantastical and universal ("the elixir of life"), while cordial is mundane and specific.
  • Chummy / Pally: Similar because both describe warm friendliness; differs because these are informal, intimacy-flavoured terms, while cordial is formal and does not imply intimacy.
