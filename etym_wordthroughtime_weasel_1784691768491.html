- Old English - "weasel" is one of the oldest animal names in English, barely changed in over a thousand years: "wesole/wesle" descends from a Germanic name meaning literally "the foul-smelling animal", after the pungent musk the weasel discharges when alarmed.
- Middle Ages - the creature's darting movement and ability to vanish into the smallest hole were read as evidence of cunning, and folklore and literature made it the type of slyness — a reputation that grew as the word's sound stayed constant.
- Modern English - the animal sense still dominates, while the figurative sense of a cunning, sneaking, evasive person flourishes in "to weasel out" of a difficulty and in the 20th-century idiom "weasel words", deliberately vague and misleading language.
