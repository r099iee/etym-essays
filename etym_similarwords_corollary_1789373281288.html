• Consequence: Similar because both denote an outcome or result that follows from an antecedent action or condition; differs because consequence is broad, neutral, or frequently associated with physical, temporal, or negative practical outcomes (such as facing the consequences of a crime), whereas corollary specifically emphasizes logical, deductive inevitability and direct conceptual derivation.
  • Outgrowth: Similar because both describe a secondary development emerging from a primary source; differs because outgrowth emphasizes organic, biological, or physical growth and uncontrolled branching development over time, whereas corollary emphasizes tight, deductive, and intellectual necessity.
  • Repercussion: Similar because both represent downstream developments generated by an initial event; differs because repercussion carries strong connotations of indirect, chaotic, violent, or widespread negative shockwaves reverberating through a system (like the economic repercussions of war), whereas corollary denotes a direct, predictable, and orderly logical progression.
  • Byproduct: Similar because both refer to incidental secondary results; differs because byproduct emphasizes an unintended, incidental material residue created during a manufacturing or chemical process, whereas corollary is an inherent, logically linked proposition or systemic outcome.
  • Sequel: Similar because both designate something that follows after an earlier entity; differs because sequel primarily denotes chronological succession, narrative continuation (such as a sequel to a film or book), or an event occurring later in time without requiring strict logical deduction.
  • Implication: Similar because both involve logical relationships between premises; differs because an implication is something that is hinted, presupposed, or latent within a premise without being explicitly stated, whereas a corollary is an explicit, distinct conclusion or separate proposition drawn out from that premise.
