• Axioms: Similar because both denote foundational, unproven starting points for logical deduction and theoretical systems; differs because axioms are traditionally regarded as self-evident, undeniable universal truths (such as the whole being greater than the part), whereas postulations are specific demands or operational assumptions accepted without claiming universal self-evidence.
  • Premises: Similar because both serve as the preliminary propositions from which an argument or conclusion is drawn; differs because premises can be empirical observations, factual statements, or temporary hypotheses within a syllogism, whereas postulations are deep, structural, foundational assumptions underpinning an entire intellectual or aesthetic doctrine.
  • Hypotheses: Similar because both are proposed explanations or conjectures formulated to explain observed phenomena; differs because hypotheses are explicitly provisional and formulated to be tested, confirmed, or falsified by empirical experiment, whereas postulations are foundational starting demands accepted as the ground upon which an entire theory is constructed.
  • Presuppositions: Similar because both are implicit or explicit assumptions taken for granted prior to inquiry; differs because presuppositions are often unconscious, unexamined, or tacit biases embedded within language and culture, whereas postulations are formally articulated, conscious, and systematically declared theoretical starting points.
  • Dogmas: Similar because both represent core doctrinal tenets that guide belief and practice; differs because dogmas are authoritative, non-negotiable religious or ideological decrees demanded by an institutional authority on pain of heresy, whereas postulations are intellectual or aesthetic claims subject to logical and philosophical scrutiny.
  • Assumptions: Similar because both denote taking something for granted without immediate proof; differs because assumptions is a broad, everyday, and often careless term describing unexamined beliefs, whereas postulations represents formal, rigorous, and deliberate theoretical framing.
  • Theorems: Similar because both are central propositions within mathematical, philosophical, and logical systems; differs because theorems are derived conclusions that must be rigorously demonstrated and proved from underlying axioms, whereas postulations are the primary, unproven starting points that make such proof possible.
