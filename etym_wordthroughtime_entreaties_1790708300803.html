- c. 1300–1450: Middle English *entraiten* meant 'to manage, negotiate, handle, or treat someone well/badly' - General term for social and diplomatic handling.
- c. 1450–1600: Specialized into 'beseeching, pleading, and soliciting favors' - Associated with courtly petitions and Renaissance theatrical rhetoric.
- 1600s–1800s: The noun *entreaty* / *entreaties* became a standard literary trope in high literature (Shakespeare, Defoe, Austen) for passionate familial and romantic pleas - Expressed tender, desperate persuasion.
- Modern Era: Retains an elevated, elegant, and literary register, evoking serious moral and emotional appeals rather than casual requests.
