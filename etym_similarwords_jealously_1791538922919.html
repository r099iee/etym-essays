• Vigilantly: Similar because both denote heightened, unrelenting alertness to ward off danger or intrusion; differs because vigilantly emphasizes careful attentiveness and safety without the possessive, territorial, or emotionally proprietary edge inherent in jealously.
  • Possessively: Similar because both describe an intense desire to retain sole control over something cherished and keep others at a distance; differs because possessively focuses on dominance and ownership, whereas jealously can also denote envious coveting or fierce defensive vigilance.
  • Enviously: Similar because both involve emotional discontent triggered by another person's possessions, status, or success; differs because enviously specifically denotes longing for what another enjoys, whereas jealously primarily fears losing what one already claims (though secondarily expressing bitter resentment of rivals).
  • Zealously: Similar because both trace to the exact same Greek root (zēlos) denoting passionate, burning ardor; differs because zealously implies enthusiastic, devoted promotion of an ideal or cause, whereas jealously emphasizes anxious protection, defensive vigilance, or suspicion.
  • Suspiciously: Similar because both can describe an uneasy, distrustful scrutiny of others' motives; differs because suspiciously highlights a lack of trust and expectation of deceit, while jealously arises specifically from perceived rivalry or emotional threat.
  • Protective: Similar because both convey an active defense of an entity against harm; differs because protective carries an altruistic, nurturing connotation, whereas jealously implies a selfish, exclusive, or hyper-vigilant monopoly.
  • Covetously: Similar because both involve acute desire centered on desirable goods; differs because covetously signifies an insatiable craving to acquire what belongs to someone else, whereas jealously primarily centers on defending what is already one's own against competitors.
