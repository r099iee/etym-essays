- Classical Latin (ruminatio, c. 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE) - Exclusively described the biological regurgitation and re-chewing of cud by ruminant cattle (*rumen*).
- Late Antiquity & Medieval Latin (c. 400–1200 CE) - Monastic and theological writers (such as St. Augustine and St. Gregory) developed the metaphorical sense of spiritually 'digesting' scriptures through repetitive prayer and contemplation.
- Early Modern English (16th Century, c. 1530–1600) - Borrowed into English to describe both biological cud-chewing and deep, solitary intellectual reflection or pondering.
- 17th to 19th Century - Flourished in literature and philosophy as a dignified term for melancholic, poetic, or solemn interior reflections.
- Late 20th to 21st Century - Adopted as a formal psychiatric and clinical term in cognitive behavioral psychology for maladaptive, repetitive, negative thought cycles in depression and OCD.
