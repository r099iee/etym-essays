- Old & Middle English everyday sense - afore is the common compound of a- and fore meaning 'before', in time and in place alike; it is a standard preposition and adverb of ordinary speech and writing
- Early Modern English standard sense - afore flourishes in the language of law, scripture and plain prose, fixed in formulas such as aforementioned and aforethought, and in nautical speech as 'afore the mast'
- 18th-19th century retreat - as 'before' wins out in educated speech, afore withdraws from the standard language; it survives in the regional dialects of England and Scotland, in traditional American dialect speech, and in storytellers' rustic voices
- Contemporary sense - the word is archaic or dialectal, kept alive by legal and nautical formulas and by any tale that wants an old-fashioned, fireside, rural flavour
