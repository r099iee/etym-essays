• Intrigued: Similar because it also describes curiosity that has been aroused; differs because intrigued is purely fascinated interest with no hint of stinging or irritation, whereas piqued retains the "pricked" edge.
  • Nettled: Similar because it names irritation of wounded pride; differs because nettled is specifically stung into annoyance as by contact with nettles, more petty and defensive than piqued.
  • Provoked: Similar because both describe stirred-up feeling; differs because provoked typically leads toward anger or retaliation, while piqued stays at the level of smarting irritation.
  • Riled: Similar because both mean irritated or annoyed; differs because riled is informal, hotter, and angrier, while piqued is cooler, prouder, and more refined.
  • Irritated: Similar because both name vexation; differs because irritated is general annoyance with no pride or sting imagery, while piqued implies wounded self-regard.
  • Annoyed: Similar because both describe mild displeasure; differs because annoyed is broad and everyday, while piqued is more literary and pride-focused.
  • Offended: Similar because both involve feeling slighted; differs because offended implies a stronger, more moralized sense of wrong, whereas piqued is a lighter, sulkier resentment.
  • Stimulated: Similar because both can mean excited into activity or interest; differs because stimulated is clinical and neutral, losing the sharp "prick" imagery entirely.
  • Spurred: Similar because both mean goaded into action; differs because spurred (from horse-spurs) suggests driving forward, while piqued suggests a sharp mental touch.
  • Titillated: Similar because both describe aroused interest; differs because titillated carries an erotic or teasing lightness, whereas piqued is intellectual and pride-laden.
  • Vexed: Similar because both name provoked irritation; differs because vexed is more fretful and worried, while piqued is proud and stinging.
  • Wounded (in pride): Similar because both describe a damaged self-regard; differs because wounded is the literal image of harm, while piqued is the specific stinging subset of wounded pride.
  • Slighted: Similar because both follow an insult or snub; differs because slighted focuses on the act done to one, while piqued focuses on the resulting irritated state.
  • Roused: Similar because both mean awakened or stirred; differs because roused suggests arousal to vigorous action, while piqued names a lighter, keener mental stirring.
