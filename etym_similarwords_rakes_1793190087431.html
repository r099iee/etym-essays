• Pitchforks: Similar because both are long-handled hayfield tools swung in the same harvest rhythm; differs because a pitchfork lifts and tosses with its few long tines, while rakes drag, spread, and level with many short teeth.
  • Harrows: Similar because both level and break soil with teeth; differs because a harrow is a heavy dragged frame behind horse or tractor, while rakes are hand tools worked stroke by stroke.
  • Hoes: Similar because both are long-handled garden tools of tillage; differs because a hoe chops weeds and opens drills with a blade, while rakes gather and smooth with teeth.
  • Brooms: Similar because both sweep loose material into heaps; differs because a broom brushes with bristles across floors and yards, while rakes comb with teeth through grass, soil, and gravel.
  • Spades: Similar because both are essential digging-tillage hand tools; differs because a spade cuts and inverts earth with a blade, while rakes dress and gather the surface.
  • Tedders: Similar because both turn cut grass to dry it into hay; differs because a tedder is the mechanised rotary successor, while rakes are the hand implement of the source sentence’s age.
  • Forks: Similar because both lift and loosen garden material with tines; differs because a garden fork digs and aerates compacted ground, while rakes finish and level the surface.
  • Combs: Similar because both draw teeth through a mass to order it; differs because a comb orders hair or flax, while rakes order fields and gardens — the same toothed logic at different scales.
