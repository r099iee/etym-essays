• Notch: Similar because both mean a small V-shaped cut or indentation in a surface; differs because notch is more precise and technical (often deliberate, as a marker or tally), while nick is usually accidental and slighter.
  • Cut: Similar because both denote a wound or incision; differs because cut is far broader (any size and depth), while nick is a tiny, shallow, often trivial cut.
  • Snip: Similar because both are small cuts made with a blade; differs because snip is specifically a quick scissor-like clip, while nick is a scrape or notch not necessarily made with scissors.
  • Gash: Similar because both are wounds; differs because gash is a deep, long, severe cut — the opposite end of the scale from nick's triviality.
  • Scratch: Similar because both are minor surface marks; differs because a scratch is a thin line made by dragging a point across a surface, while a nick is a small notch or cut made by a blade.
  • Incision: Similar because both are cuts; differs because incision is formal and medical (a deliberate surgical cut), while nick is casual and accidental.
  • Steal: Similar because "to nick" = to steal, sharing the sense of taking something away; differs because nick is exclusively informal British slang, whereas steal is neutral and universal.
  • Pinch: Similar because in British slang both mean "to steal"; differs because pinch also means to squeeze painfully (its primary sense) and is older slang, while nick adds the "arrest" and "prison" senses.
  • Snatch: Similar because both can mean to seize quickly; differs because snatch implies grabbing with the hand, while nick implies cutting away or sneaking off with something.
  • Moment: Similar because "the nick of time" names a precise point in time; differs because nick is not a time-word outside the idiom, while moment is a general time noun.
  • Jiffy: Similar because "in a jiffy" also expresses a very short time; differs because a jiffy is a short span, while "the nick of time" is the exact critical boundary moment — the last possible instant.
