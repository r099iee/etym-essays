• Treasure: Similar because both are accumulations of valuables; differs because treasure is the broad cover term for riches, while hoard stresses the hidden, guarded, secret quality of the store.
  • Cache: Similar because both are stored supplies; differs because a cache stresses hidden storage, often temporary or tactical, rather than an accumulation of wealth.
  • Trove: Similar because both are deposits of valuables; differs because a trove (as in treasure-trove) is a found hidden deposit, not one kept by its owner.
  • Stockpile: Similar because both are large stored amounts; differs because stockpile is modern and often strategic or military, with none of hoard's treasure-and-dragon romance.
  • Stash: Similar because both are secret stores; differs because stash is informal and often small or illicit, while hoard is weighty, large-scale, and, in its epic sense, treasure.
  • Wealth: Similar because both concern valued possessions; differs because wealth is abstract total riches, while a hoard is a concrete store in a place.
  • Reserve / Stock: Similar because both are supplies kept for future use; differs because these are neutral, prudent terms, while hoard implies secrecy, excess, or greed.
  • Store / Storehouse: Similar because both are places or amounts of accumulated goods; differs because a store is openly kept for use and trade, not hidden from others.
  • Reservoir: Similar because both can mean a large accumulated supply; differs because reservoir is chiefly a body of stored water or an abstract supply, without treasure or secrecy.
  • Collection: Similar because both can be an accumulated set of valued things; differs because a collection is usually systematic, displayed, or shared, while a hoard is hidden and possessive.
