- Latin overflow 1st c. BCE - superfluus as "overflowing river / redundant word" - neutral descriptive + rhetorical critique
- Monastic vice 500-1300s - "sinful luxury violating poverty" - strongly negative, superfluity belongs to the poor by natural law
- Chaucer / Wyclif moral warning 1300s-1500s - "excess of meat, drink and speech" - pulpit condemnation, still theological
- Shakespeare / King James literary image 1600s - "Lear's superfluity vs need" - philosophical reflection on need vs luxury, high literary prestige
- Political economy surplus 1700s-1800s - "shareable surplus" - neutral-to-positive capacity for liberality and trade
- Modern formal redundancy - "cut the superfluity" - bureaucratic-literary for surplus staff, words, evidence; everyday speech prefers surplus/excess
