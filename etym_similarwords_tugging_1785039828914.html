• Pulling: Similar because both draw something toward oneself; differs because pulling is the neutral general term without strain or repetition, while tugging always carries visible, repeated effort against resistance.
  • Dragging: Similar because both involve pulling something along; differs because dragging hauls a resisting object over a surface with the agent moving, while tugging is a jerky, repeated pull often made in place.
  • Hauling: Similar because both mean pulling with effort; differs because hauling implies heavy loads over long distances, while tugging suggests smaller, more urgent jerks.
  • Yanking: Similar because both are sharp pulls; differs because a yank is a single violent, almost contemptuous jerk, whereas tugging is straining and repetitive.
  • Wrenching: Similar because both are forceful pulls; differs because wrenching adds twisting and the sense of tearing something free, while tugging is straightforward straining.
  • Towing: Similar because both involve pulling; differs because towing is pulling by rope or chain, typically of a boat or vehicle, without the jerky effort of tugging.
  • Jerking: Similar because both are sudden pulls; differs because a jerk is abrupt and spasmodic, often involuntary, while tugging is deliberate, insistent, and repeated.
  • Straggling: Similar because both suggest laboured progress behind others; differs because straggling is about lagging behind without active pulling, while tugging is active straining against resistance.
  • Heaving: Similar because both involve great effort; differs because heaving means lifting or throwing with a powerful, sweeping motion, while tugging is a repeated horizontal pull.
  • Nudging: Similar as a light contact; differs because nudging is a gentle, deliberate prod, while tugging is hard and effortful.
