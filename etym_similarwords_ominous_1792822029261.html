• Sinister: Similar because both warn of evil to come; differs because sinister implies active wickedness or conspiracy already present, while ominous implies fate signalling trouble ahead, not yet arrived.
  • Menacing: Similar because both threaten harm; differs because menacing is direct and near — a raised fist, a growling dog — while ominous works at a distance through signs and atmosphere.
  • Foreboding: Similar because both name dread of what is coming; differs because foreboding is the inward feeling (a foreboding heart), while ominous is the outward sign that causes it (an ominous sky).
  • Portentous: Similar because both descend from the Roman reading of signs; differs because portentous is grander and often pompous (a portentous announcement), while ominous stays lean towards threat.
  • Inauspicious: Similar because both judge beginnings by their signs; differs because inauspicious is formal and calendrical (an inauspicious start to the talks), while ominous is atmospheric and felt in the nerves.
  • Threatening: Similar because both promise trouble; differs because threatening is the plain general word for any coming harm, while ominous adds the supernatural flavour of fate dropping hints.
  • Fateful: Similar because in the source sentence fifty feels both fateful and ominous; differs because fateful is neutral about good or evil (a fateful meeting can bless), while ominous leans dark.
  • Uncanny: Similar because both describe a chill of wrongness beyond explanation; differs because uncanny is strange and inexplicable (an uncanny likeness), while ominous is pointed toward a bad future.
  • Gloomy: Similar because ominous skies and gloomy skies overlap; differs because gloomy describes present darkness of mood or weather, while ominous describes what that darkness predicts.
