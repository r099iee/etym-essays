• Whispered: Similar because both describe quiet, forced-breath speech; differs because a whisper is deliberate, articulate, quiet speech that still forms words, whereas a hiss is a sharper, unvoiced sibilant sound, often of menace.
  • Murmured: Similar because both are soft, non-loud vocal sounds; differs because a murmur is a low, voiced, continuous, indistinct sound, while a hiss is sharp, unvoiced, and sibilant.
  • Muttered: Similar because both describe low, indistinct speech under the breath; differs because muttering is mumbling and closed-mouthed, barely intelligible, whereas hissing is a focused sibilant expulsion.
  • Sibilated: Similar because both denote producing an s-like sound; differs because "sibilate" is a rare technical term for any sibilant articulation, with none of the menace or anger of "hissed".
  • Rasped: Similar because both are harsh, strained vocal sounds; differs because a rasp is a dry, rough, grating noise, while a hiss is a smooth, breathy sibilance.
  • Snarled: Similar because both convey animal-like menace in speech; differs because a snarl is a voiced growl with bared teeth, while a hiss is unvoiced breath forced through the teeth.
  • Sizzled: Similar because both are hissing sounds of heat; differs because sizzling is specifically the spitting hiss of heat on fat or moisture, not a speech sound.
  • Fizzed: Similar because both are sharp, bubbly sibilant noises; differs because fizzing is the light, sparkly sound of gas in liquid, without the force or menace of a hiss.
  • Seethed: Similar because both can describe suppressed, boiling anger; differs because "seethe" describes the state of boiling rage (or a crowd's agitation), not the emission of the sound itself.
  • Booed: Similar because both are public sounds of derision; differs because "boo" is a voiced, hollow call, whereas a hiss is unvoiced and sibilant — the theatre crowd's two instruments of rejection.
  • Whistled: Similar because both are sounds made by forcing breath through a narrow opening; differs because a whistle is a sustained pitched tone, while a hiss is unpitched friction noise.
  • Catcalled: Similar because both express audience disapproval; differs because a catcall is a shouted, articulate or semi-articulate jeer, not a sibilant sound.
