• Portion: Similar because both denote a part or share of a whole; differs because portion emphasizes an allotted or measured share (often of food), while part is the general, neutral word.
  • Piece: Similar because both denote a separated part of a whole; differs because piece suggests a physically detached fragment, while part can be an integral component that remains in place.
  • Segment: Similar because both denote a division of a whole; differs because segment is more technical (a section cut or marked off, as of a circle or a market), while part is everyday.
  • Section: Similar because both denote a subdivision; differs because section implies a deliberate division of a document, building, or area, while part is broader and less formal.
  • Share: Similar because both can mean a portion allotted to someone; differs because share emphasizes distribution among people (a fair share), while part is a neutral division without the fairness connotation.
  • Component: Similar because both denote a constituent of a larger assembly; differs because component is technical and implies function within a system, while part is general.
  • Fraction: Similar because both denote a portion of a whole; differs because fraction is mathematical or implies a small, often imprecise portion, while part is neutral in size.
  • Separate: Similar because both mean to divide or move apart; differs because separate is the general word for division of any kind, while part (as a verb) specifically implies people or things that were joined moving away from each other — and, used of people, means "to leave, to take leave".
  • Divide: Similar because both mean to split into parts; differs because divide is stronger and more systematic (partitioning a whole), while part suggests a gentle or mutual separation.
  • Leave: Similar because "to part from" = "to leave"; differs because leave is neutral and everyday, while part is formal, literary, and ceremonial, often implying a mutual farewell.
  • Depart: Similar because both mean to go away; differs because depart is formal and official (a departure board, a departing train) and does not necessarily imply leaving another person, while part implies separation from someone.
  • Role: Similar because "a part" in a play = "a role"; differs because role is the modern standard word for a function or character, while part is the older theatrical term and is far broader (a part of a machine, a part of a whole).
  • Apportion: Similar because both involve dividing into shares; differs because apportion is purely distributive and formal, while part's noun and verb senses span far wider.
