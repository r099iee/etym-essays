• Begged: Similar because both mean asking with great earnestness; differs because begged is more colloquial and can suggest abject or habitual asking, while implored keeps a formal, tear-stained dignity.
  • Besought / Beseeched: Similar in the intensity of entreaty; differs because besought is archaic and biblical in flavour, while implored is current, formal English.
  • Entreated: Similar in earnest request; differs because entreated implies polite, sustained persuasion rather than the desperate crisis of imploring.
  • Pleaded: Similar in asking with feeling; differs because pleaded often takes a legal or argumentative cast ("pleaded for mercy") and need not carry the image of weeping that implored retains.
  • Supplicated: Similar in humble, urgent request; differs because supplicated stresses kneeling, humility, and address to a superior or deity, a more abject posture than implore.
  • Petitioned: Similar in formal asking; differs because petitioned implies a formal, often written request to an authority, without the emotional urgency of imploring.
  • Appealed: Similar in calling on someone for help; differs because appealed is broader and can be legal or logical ("appeal to reason"), while implored is purely emotional entreaty.
  • Importuned: Similar in pressing a request; differs because importuned stresses troublesome, repeated pressing or harassment, whereas implored is a single earnest cry.
  • Adjured: Similar in solemnly urging; differs because adjured carries the force of an oath or command, almost threatening, while implored is pure entreaty.
  • Craved: Similar in longing for something; differs because craved concerns the thing wanted intensely, not the act of begging a person for it.
  • Solicited: Similar in seeking something; differs because solicited is a neutral, often businesslike request or canvass, lacking implore's desperation.
  • Prayed: Similar in earnest petition; differs because prayed is addressed to God or a higher power and belongs to religious speech, while implored addresses any person.
  • Urged: Similar in pressing someone to act; differs because urged is less emotional and can be encouragement or advice, whereas implored is tearful, desperate begging.
  • Pressed: Similar in pressing a request; differs because pressed stresses insistence and persistence rather than the emotional intensity of imploring.
