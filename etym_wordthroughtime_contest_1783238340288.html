- [Medieval legal origin] - In its current form since Middle English, 'contest' began as a legal verb meaning to call in question and dispute a claim in court; formal and procedural.
- [Broadened struggle] - By the 17th and 18th centuries it became the noun for any strenuous rivalry or struggle for superiority - the 'contest' between crown and parliament - carried in dignified constitutional prose.
- [Sporting sense] - From the 19th century it settled into athletics and games, a 'contest' being any competitive match, losing much of its legal gravity.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today it is the neutral word for competition in sport, politics and entertainment, while the verb 'to contest' (an election, a decision) keeps the older legal flavour of challenging.
