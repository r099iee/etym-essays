Note on morphological compounding: In Sanskrit epic syntax, the genitive relation is frequently expressed through tatpuruṣa or bahuvrīhi compounds such as Śakra-sama ("equal to Śakra"), Śakra-tulya ("comparable to Śakra"), or Śakra-kārmuka ("Śakra's bow, the rainbow"). English translators, beginning in the 19th and 20th centuries, routinely render these Sanskrit compounds into analytic English possessives using the Anglicized proper noun with the genitive apostrophe-s (Śakra's). The Indo-European root *ḱek- also generated the Sanskrit abstract noun śakti ("power, energy, capability"), which became the central theological concept of divine feminine cosmic energy in Shaktism.
