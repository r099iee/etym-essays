• gesture: Similar because both are bodily movements that convey meaning; differs because gesture is a neutral, general term for any meaningful movement, while a beck is acutely directional and commanding, expecting an immediate response.
  • nod: Similar because both are silent movements of the head; differs because a nod signals agreement, acknowledgement, or greeting, and lacks the imperative 'come here' or 'do this' pull of a beck.
  • signal: Similar because both convey information by a visible sign; differs because signal is a broad, neutral term for any transmitted sign (including lights, flags, or radio), whereas a beck is specifically a small body-gesture of summons between people.
  • sign: Similar because both are silent means of communication; differs because sign is the fully general word (a sign can be a symbol, a portent, or a notice), while a beck is a live gesture of command.
  • summons: Similar because both are calls to appear; differs because a summons is formal, often vocal or written and legally or officially weighted, whereas a beck is informal, silent, and depends on personal authority.
  • wave: Similar because both are hand-signals; differs because a wave can greet, farewell, or attract attention across a distance, while a beck is intimate, close-range, and authoritative.
  • wink: Similar because both are small, coded gestures of the face; differs because a wink is usually conspiratorial, playful, or flirtatious, not a summons to obedience.
  • call: Similar because both command someone's presence; differs because a call is audible (a voice or a shout, or figuratively a vocation), whereas a beck is wordless — the two are set in contrast inside the very idiom 'beck and call.'
