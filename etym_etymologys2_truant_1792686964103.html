*trugos

Note on historical debate: Historical lexicographers and Celticists (such as Thurneysen and Joseph Vendryes) trace French truand and English truant directly to Celtic *trugos ("miserable, pitiful"), surviving in Old Irish trúag and modern Welsh tru. In Gallo-Roman society, the term was adopted into Vulgar Latin as a pejorative designation for the wretched Celtic peasant class and professional mendicants. In medieval Paris, the grand district of thieves and beggars was famously dubbed the Cour des Miracles where truands gathered. When the word entered English in the 13th century, it denoted sturdy vagabonds. Its specialization to school-age children skipping class developed in the mid-16th century, likely encouraged by the severe regime of Tudor grammar school headmasters who treated schoolboy wanderers as juvenile vagrants.
