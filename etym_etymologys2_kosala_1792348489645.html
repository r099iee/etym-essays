Note on etymological debate: Traditional Sanskrit etymologists and grammarians (Pāṇini, Aṣṭādhyāyī 4.1.171; Yāska's Nirukta; *Śabdakalpadruma*) attempted internal Indo-Aryan etymologies for Kosala, either deriving it from the verbal root kuś- ("to tear, embrace, draw together") or connecting it with kuśa (the sacred ritual grass Desmostachya bipinnata, or Prince Kuśa, the son of Rāma). However, modern comparative historical linguists (such as Michael Witzel, Colin Masica, and F. B. J. Kuiper) demonstrate that many ancient eastern Gangetic toponyms and ethnonyms (including Kosala, Magadha, Videha, Kāśī, and Aṅga) belong to a non-Indo-European substrate stratum—specifically an ancient Austroasiatic (Para-Munda) or pre-Vedic indigenous Gangetic language family. As Indo-Aryan speech spread eastward down the Ganges, these indigenous geographical names were phonologically adapted, received regular Sanskrit case endings, and were retrospectively rationalized through mythic genealogies and Brahmanical folk etymologies.
