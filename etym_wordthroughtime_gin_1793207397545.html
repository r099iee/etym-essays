- Antiquity — the berry: juniper flavours medicines and wines from Egypt to Rome, but no distilled gin exists yet.
- 17th c. — the Dutch invention: genever distilled in Holland as a juniper cordial; English soldiers in the Low Countries bring the taste home.
- 1689–1720 — the floodgate: William III’s tariffs on French brandy and cheap grain excises let anyone distil; London drowns in penny gin.
- 1720–1751 — the Craze: drunk for a penny, dead drunk for twopence; Hogarth’s Gin Lane (1751) and the Gin Act try to dam the deluge.
- Victorian — the officer’s drink: gin and tonic (quinine for malaria), the officers’ mess, Pimm’s and the cocktail’s dawn restore a veneer of class.
- 1920s — bathtub and jazz: Prohibition makes gin the bootlegger’s easiest spirit; the martini becomes America’s cocktail.
- Today — the craft resurrection: hundreds of small stills, foraged botanicals, and cocktail culture turn mother’s ruin into the connoisseur’s darling.
