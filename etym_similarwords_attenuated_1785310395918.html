• diluted: Similar because both mean reduced in strength; differs because dilution works by adding a solvent or foreign element from outside, whereas attenuation implies thinning by stretching, drawing out, or damping from within.
  • debilitated: Similar because both describe a loss of force; differs because debilitated implies loss of physical strength or structural integrity with clinical, bodily overtones, while attenuated is a more general, often quantitative reduction of magnitude or intensity.
  • enervated: Similar because both describe a weakened condition; differs because enervated stresses drained vitality, morale, or nerve — a withering of energy — whereas attenuated stresses a graded reduction of force, intensity, or thickness without destruction.
  • mitigated: Similar because both mean made less severe; differs because mitigation presupposes an active agent softening an outcome such as a harm or penalty, while attenuated is descriptive, naming a state or process of reduction rather than an intervention.
  • weakened: Similar because both mean reduced in power or effectiveness; differs because weakened is the plain, general word for any loss of strength, whereas attenuated adds the specific idea of thinning or graded reduction, usually in technical or scientific contexts.
  • reduced: Similar because both mean lessened in amount or degree; differs because reduced is wholly generic and covers quantity, size, and cost alike, while attenuated is narrower, implying a loss of force, intensity, or thickness with the essential form retained.
  • thinned / thinned out: Similar because both convey the literal idea of making something less dense or less thick; differs because thinned is the ordinary concrete word, while attenuated formalises the same idea and extends it to signals, responses, and arguments.
  • tenuous: Similar because both carry the idea of “thin” and of slightness; differs because tenuous describes a thing that is itself flimsy or insubstantial, while attenuated describes something that has been made thin or weak by reduction.
  • blunted / dampened: Similar because both describe reduced force or sharpness of effect; differs because blunted and dampened are more concrete (an edge, a sound, an emotion), while attenuated is the scientific term for a measurable loss of amplitude or intensity.
  • extenuated: Similar because both derive from the Latin root tenuis “thin” and mean reduced in degree; differs because extenuated (from extenuāre) means “made thin or slight” or, of guilt, “lessened in severity,” and today survives chiefly in the legal phrase “extenuating circumstances,” whereas attenuated is general and scientific.
