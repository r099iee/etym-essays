• Bass Rock: Similar because it is the volcanic islet facing Tantallon across the water; differs because Bass is a natural prison-rock and gannetry, while Tantallon is a built fortress.
  • Stirling (Castle): Similar because Ruskin pairs them as empty Scott castles; differs because Stirling is a royal palace-fortress on a crag, while Tantallon is a baronial curtain-wall on a cliff.
  • Edinburgh Castle: Similar because it is the great Lothian fortress of crown and artillery; differs because Edinburgh is the capital citadel still garrisoned, while Tantallon is a roofless ruin.
  • Dunbar Castle: Similar because it is the neighbouring East Lothian sea-fortress of the earls of March; differs because Dunbar guards a harbour town, while Tantallon guards an open headland.
  • Bothwell Castle: Similar because it is another vast Douglas red-sandstone fortress; differs because Bothwell stands on the Clyde inland, while Tantallon stands on the North Sea cliff.
  • Hermitage Castle: Similar because it is a Douglas border stronghold of grim repute; differs because Hermitage is a lonely Liddesdale tower-house, while Tantallon is a coastal wall-castle.
  • Alnwick / Bamburgh: Similar because they are Northumbrian sea-castles of parallel scale and romance; differs because they are English Percy fortresses across the border, while Tantallon is Scottish Douglas.
  • Marmion (the poem): Similar because Scott fixes Tantallon in Canto 5; differs because Marmion is the narrative poem, while Tantallon is the place it describes.
  • Archibald of Angus: Similar because he is the earl who held Tantallon against James V; differs because Archibald is the man, while Tantallon is his house.
  • Hailes Castle: Similar because it is the nearby East Lothian predecessor of the Hepburns and Scotts; differs because Hailes is a smaller river-valley ruin, while Tantallon is the great sea-fortress.
