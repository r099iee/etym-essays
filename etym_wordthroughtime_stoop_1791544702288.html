- Old English Period (c. 10th c.) - Native verbal sense: bodily bending, leaning forward, or walking with an infirm, bowed posture.
- Tudor & Elizabethan Falconry (c. 16th c.) - Aerodynamic expansion: a falcon's breathtaking, near-vertical downward dive upon aerial prey.
- 17th Century Colonial Contact - In New Netherland / New York: adoption of Dutch 'stoep' for the open front steps and entrance platforms of townhouses.
- 18th & 19th Century Literature - Moral figurative expansion of the verb: 'stooping' to unworthy or degrading behavior; while 'stoop' becomes a fixture of Knickerbocker Dutch-American lore.
- 20th Century Urban Culture - The brownstone stoop becomes the quintessential symbol of New York City and Brooklyn communal street culture and working-class neighborhood life.
