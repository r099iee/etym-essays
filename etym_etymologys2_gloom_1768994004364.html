Single linear chain for the single root word gloom: 1. PIE *ghel- "to shine, gleam, be bright; yellow, green" (reconstructed root, c. 4500–2500 BCE) — a root of radiance; descendants include Greek khlōros "green-yellow" (source of chlorine, chlorophyll), Latin helvus "honey-yellow," Sanskrit hari "tawny, tawny-green," Lithuanian žalias "green," Old Church Slavonic zelenŭ "green," and the Germanic "yellow"/"gold" family (*gelwaz "yellow," *gulþą "gold"). 2. Proto-Germanic *glōaną "to glow, shine" (reconstructed; Old English glōwan "to glow," attested 8th c. CE) — the o-grade of the root, whose Germanic "glow" cluster also produced *glīmaną "to glimmer" and *glōmun- "twilight, dusk." The glōm- / glīm- grades express light in the process of dying. 3. Old English glōmung "twilight, dusk, the evening gloom" (attested c. 8th–10th c. CE) — the fading of the glow, the hour when light is nearly exhausted; a rare gloss also records the noun glōm "twilight." From this "fading light" idea the darkness sense is born. 4. Middle English gloumen (verb) "to look sullen, frown, be dejected" (attested c. 1300) and gloum, glome (noun) "gloom, darkness, obscurity" (14th c.) — the verb's exact origin is debated (a Scandinavian loan has been suggested — Norwegian dialect glome "to stare" — as has a native formation on the glow-family); the nominal "darkness" sense is established by c. 1500. 5. Early Modern English: noun gloom "a sullen look, frown" (1590s) → "partial or total darkness, dimness" (1620s) → "melancholy, dejection, despondency" (sense recorded mid-18th c., c. 1744). 6. Modern English gloom — both "darkness" and "a state of depression" current; with the adjective gloomy, adverb gloomily, noun gloominess, and sibling gloaming. Debate note: the chain from PIE *ghel- to the Middle English verb is partly reconstructed. The single attested Old English glōm "twilight" is a rare gloss, and its link to the Middle English verb gloumen is generally accepted but not certain; the OED flags the verb's origin as "obscure," and a Scandinavian loan-source cannot be excluded. No stage is invented here; the uncertain junctures are stated explicitly.
