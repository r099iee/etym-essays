• Valéry: Similar because Paul Valéry was Stéphane Mallarmé's most devoted disciple, and both sought absolute intellectual purity, linguistic rigor, and classical formal perfection in lyric poetry; differs because Valéry anchored his poetics in the scientific, psychological operations of the conscious human mind and mathematical precision, whereas Mallarmé pursued a quasi-mystical, alchemical vision of the cosmic Book that would redeem the contingency of the universe.
  • Baudelaire: Similar because Charles Baudelaire was Mallarmé's foundational idol and mentor who pioneered Symbolism and the doctrine of sensory correspondences; differs because Baudelaire's verse was saturated with visceral physical reality, urban decay, Parisian squalor, and tortured Christian guilt, whereas Mallarmé refined poetry into hermetic abstraction, disembodied musicality, and the ethereal contemplation of the void.
  • Rimbaud: Similar because Arthur Rimbaud and Mallarmé were revolutionary titans of late-19th-century French poetry who systematically derailed conventional syntax to explore the unknown; differs because Rimbaud was a violent, rebellious teenage prodigy who sought a "derangement of all the senses" and abandoned literature entirely, whereas Mallarmé was a patient, ascetic, lifelong scholar-poet who constructed an architectural, hermetic system of pure poetry.
  • Verlaine: Similar because Paul Verlaine and Mallarmé were crowned as the joint leaders of the Symbolist and Decadent movements who prioritized musicality above all else ("De la musique avant toute chose"); differs because Verlaine's musicality was fluid, spontaneous, sentimental, and song-like, whereas Mallarmé's musicality was structural, intellectual, typographical, and rigorously calculated.
  • Rilke: Similar because Rainer Maria Rilke and Mallarmé were European poetic visionaries who believed that physical objects in the material world exist to be interiorized and transfigured into pure spiritual presence through art; differs because Rilke expressed this transformation through intimate, lyrical empathy and German existential warmth (*Elegies* and *Sonnets to Orpheus*), whereas Mallarmé deployed an austere, crystalline, and syntactically hermetic French formalism.
  • Eliot: Similar because T.S. Eliot was profoundly influenced by Mallarmé's Symbolist doctrine, adopting Mallarmé's imperative to "purify the dialect of the tribe" in *Four Quartets*; differs because Eliot synthesized Symbolist techniques with high Anglican theology, cultural conservatism, and vast historical fragmentation, whereas Mallarmé operated within a radical, secular metaphysical framework confronting the absolute abyss of nothingness.
