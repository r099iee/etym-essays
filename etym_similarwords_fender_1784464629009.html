• Fireguard: Similar because it is another protective device placed at the fireplace to contain fire and embers; differs because a fireguard is usually a taller freestanding mesh or decorative screen, while a fender is the low rail or kerb at floor level.
  • Firescreen: Similar because it shields a room from the fireplace; differs because a firescreen is a screen set before the fire (often folding or decorative) rather than a fixed low rail at the hearth's edge.
  • Grate: Similar because it belongs to the fireplace and contains the fire; differs because the grate is the metal basket that holds coals up off the floor, while the fender stops coals from escaping the fireplace.
  • Hearth: Similar because it is the physical focus of the fireplace area; differs because the hearth is the floor or surround of the fireplace itself, not a protective rail in front of it.
  • Buffer: Similar because it is a general word for a protective device that absorbs impact; differs because buffer is the abstract, generic term without fender's specific hearth-rail or wheel-guard identity.
  • Bumper: Similar because it is a protective fitting on a vehicle; differs because a bumper guards the front or rear of a car, while an automotive fender frames the wheel.
  • Mudguard: Similar because it is the British term for the panel over a car wheel; differs because mudguard foregrounds the stopping of mud and spray, while fender stresses general protection.
  • Wing: Similar because in British English a wing is the panel over a car wheel; differs because wing is the British vehicle-body term and carries none of the nautical or hearth senses of fender.
  • Guard: Similar because it names any protective barrier or device; differs because guard is the broadest term and lacks fender's historical tie to the aphetic shortening of defender and to specific hearth, ship, and wheel objects.
  • Defender: Similar because fender is historically a shortening of defender, both meaning ‘one who or that which wards off harm’; differs because defender is the full agent word used of people, causes, and teams, while fender is the concrete object name.
