• Collapsed: Similar because both describe a sudden giving way and falling; differs because 'collapsed' is the broader, more catastrophic word (buildings collapse, systems collapse), while 'slumped' emphasises limp heaviness rather than shattering.
  • Sank: Similar because both describe downward movement; differs because 'sank' is slow and often into a fluid or into the depths, while 'slumped' is sudden and heavy, losing posture.
  • Sagged: Similar because both describe downward yielding under weight; differs because 'sag' is gradual and soft (a sagging mattress, sagging skin), while 'slump' is a sudden drop.
  • Dropped: Similar because both describe falling; differs because 'dropped' is neutral and could be controlled, while 'slumped' implies limpness and lack of control.
  • Folded: Similar because both describe the body giving way; differs because 'fold' is the sudden doubling-over (knees folding) often from pain or laughter, while 'slump' is the collapse of whole posture.
  • Flopped: Similar because both describe a graceless fall; differs because 'flop' is loose and often comedic (flopping onto a sofa), while 'slump' is heavier and often tragic.
  • Toppled: Similar because both describe falling from an upright position; differs because 'topple' is a tipping over of something tall and rigid, while 'slump' is the collapse of something that loses its own support.
  • Subsided: Similar because both describe sinking down; differs because 'subside' is technical-geological or of swelling reducing (the flood subsided), while 'slump' is about a loss of stance.
  • Lapsed: Similar in the sense of falling from a state; differs because 'lapse' is mental or moral (a lapse of memory) and figurative, while 'slump' is first physical.
  • Wilted: Similar because both describe loss of vigour and uprightness; differs because 'wilt' is the slow droop of plants and spirits, while 'slump' is the sudden heavy fall.
  • Flagged: Similar because both describe weakening and drooping; differs because 'flag' is the tiredness of diminishing energy, while 'slump' is a decisive collapse.
  • Crumbled: Similar because both describe falling apart; differs because 'crumble' is the breaking into pieces, while 'slump' is the sinking of a whole mass.
  • Caved (in): Similar because both describe a structure giving way; differs because 'caving in' is the inward collapse of an enclosed thing, while 'slump' is an outward or bodily sinking.
  • Buckled: Similar because both describe giving way under pressure; differs because 'buckle' is the bending and folding of a structure (knees buckle), often with a sound, while 'slump' is the whole-body collapse.
  • Declined / Fell: Similar in the economic sense (prices slumped/fell/declined); differs because 'decline' is gradual and dignified, while 'slump' is sudden, heavy and undignified.
  • Plunged / Plummeted: Similar because both describe steep falls in prices; differs because 'plunge' and 'plummet' are fast vertical dives with momentum, while 'slump' is a heavy settling into a trough.
  • Drooped: Similar because both describe downward hang; differs because 'droop' is soft and gradual (drooping eyelids), while 'slump' is a sudden weighty collapse.
  • Swooned / Fainted: Similar because both describe loss of consciousness with collapse; differs because these specify the medical cause, while 'slumped' describes the fall itself regardless of cause.
  • Depressed: Similar in the economic sense (a slump is a depression); differs because 'depression' is the extreme form of a slump, now a distinct clinical and economic term.
