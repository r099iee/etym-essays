- [Archaic kingdom name] - 'Macedon' is the old nominative form of the Greek ethnonym, and in English it has long served as the elevated, archaizing name for the ancient kingdom - the form of heroic and classical prose.
- [Heroic register] - Through the 18th and 19th centuries it was the standard choice for epic and historical narrative ('the phalanx of Macedon'), carrying a commemorative, almost Homeric dignity that the territorial 'Macedonia' lacked.
- [Contemporary usage] - Today 'Macedon' survives almost exclusively in classical-historical and literary contexts - Philip of Macedon - while modern affairs use the wider form, leaving Macedon frozen as the poetic name of the vanished kingdom.
