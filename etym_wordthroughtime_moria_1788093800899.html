- (Tolkien's invented history) - “Moria” is the Elvish nickname that replaces the Dwarves' proud Khazad-dûm after the fall — a name of awe and loss, never used by the Dwarves themselves.
- (1937) - The Hobbit introduces the word to the world in a single sentence of blood-feud — the place where Dáin slew Azog — making it a byword for the ancient Dwarven world and its wars.
- (1954–55) - The Lord of the Rings deepens the perception: Moria becomes the haunted threshold of the quest where Gandalf falls with the Balrog — the name now resonates with dread, sacrifice and rebirth.
- (1975) - The roguelike computer game Moria borrows the name, launching its second life as gaming slang for any vast, monster-filled, procedurally generated dungeon.
- (Today) - In popular culture and journalism “Moria” means any sprawling, dangerous underground complex — or the hubris of digging too deep — a cultural meme still faintly echoing its Elvish meaning, “the Black Chasm”.
