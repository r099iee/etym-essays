- Old English mæst - the sailing ship’s pole - a plain technical word shared by all Germanic sea peoples
- Middle Ages – early modern - the mast as the tallest timber made by man, symbol of voyage, trade, and war fleets - the word rides every great voyage narrative
- 19th c. - “before the mast” (Richard Henry Dana’s Two Years Before the Mast, 1840) - emblem of the common sailor’s hard life
- 19th–20th c. - “masthead” passes from the top of the mast to the top of a newspaper page - journalism borrows the ship’s word
- 20th c. - “radio mast”, “television mast” - the word climbs down from the sea and stands on the land of every town
- today - yachting nostalgia, mourning at “half-mast”, and the anonymous antenna masts of the wireless age
