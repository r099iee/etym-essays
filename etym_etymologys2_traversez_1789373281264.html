PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*terh₂- / *treh₂- + *wer- / *wert-
"to cross over, pass through" + "to turn, bend"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*trānts + *wersō
"across, beyond" + "turned"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
trānsversus (contracted trāversus)
"turned across, lying athwart, transverse"
↓
Late / Vulgar Latin (c. 4th–7th c.)
trāversāre
"to cross over, pass across, go athwart"
Note: Frequentative verb formed from the participial stem transversus/traversus.
↓
Old French (c. 11th–13th c.)
traverser
"to pass across, cross over, penetrate"
↓
Old / Middle French (c. 12th–15th c.)
traversez
"cross ye!, pass across! (2nd pers. pl. imperative)"
Note: Inflected with 2nd person plural ending -ez (from Latin -ātis).
↓
Modern French / Literary Loan-form (present)
TRAVERSEZ
