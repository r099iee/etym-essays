- [Late Vedic Era (c. 800–500 BCE)] — emerged in Vedic lineage lists and priestly traditions as a descendant of Kaśyapa associated with ancient animal symbolism, ritual fertility, and magical rainmaking charms
- [Classical Epic Period (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)] — developed in the Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata into a full-scale dramatic literary masterpiece of erotic seduction, famine relief in Aṅga, and the crucial Putrakāmeṣṭi sacrifice of Rāma's birth
- [Early Buddhist Jātaka Era (c. 3rd c. BCE–5th c. CE)] — moralized in the Pali canon as the Bodhisatta Isisiṅga in the Alambusā and Nalinikā Jātakas, serving as an allegorical warning to Buddhist monks regarding the seductive hazards of female sensuality
- [Silk Road & East Asian Transmission (c. 6th–14th c. CE)] — migrated across the Himalayas and Central Asian trade routes as Ekaśṛṅga / Yijiao Xianren into China and Japan, transforming into the iconic horned hermit of the Japanese Noh classic Ikkaku Sennin
- [Medieval Global Synthesis & Unicorn Diffusion (c. 12th–17th c.)] — absorbed into Middle Eastern and European bestiaries through traveler accounts and Hellenistic transmission, establishing the foundational narrative archetype for the Western unicorn tamed by a virgin maiden
- [Modern Comparative Mythological Era (19th–21st c.)] — celebrated by global folklorists, Indologists, and structural mythologists as a profound pan-Eurasian study in sacred celibacy, ecological balance, and the dialectic of wilderness versus urban civilization
