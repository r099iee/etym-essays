- 17th-century coinage - to bash is to strike violently and smash, an echoic blunt-impact verb of cudgels, fists and falls; the word pictures something hard coming down on something softer
- 18th-19th century - the noun a bash takes shape as the heavy blow itself and, by natural extension, the visible mark it leaves — a bruise, swelling or contusion on the flesh; a bash is heavier than a rap, cruder than a knock
- 20th century British extension - bash gains friendly senses: 'have a bash' means make an attempt, and a bash is a lively party; the word's violence softens into sociability
- Contemporary sense - the physical blow and bruise remain, alongside the jocular 'have a bash'; bash keeps the vigour of schoolboy scuffles and swelling knuckles
