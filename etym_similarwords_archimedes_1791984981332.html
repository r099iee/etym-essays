• Euclid: Similar because he is the other canonical Greek geometer and his name stands for systematic mathematical proof; differs because Euclid taught at Alexandria and built the deductive framework of geometry, while Archimedes worked at Syracuse and combined proof with mechanics and numerical approximation.
  • Pythagoras: Similar because his name too has become an eponym (the theorem) and a byword for mathematical mystique; differs because Pythagoras is a semi-legendary religious teacher of the sixth century BCE, known mainly through later anecdotes, whereas Archimedes is a documented author whose treatises survive.
  • Apollonius of Perga: Similar because he was a contemporary Greek geometer of comparable technical power, famous for the conic sections; differs because Apollonius is read almost exclusively by specialists, while Archimedes acquired a popular reputation through the bath, the lever and the siege engines.
  • Hero of Alexandria: Similar because he too joined mathematics to machinery and left descriptions of devices; differs because Hero was an inventor and textbook writer of the first century CE whose automata were entertainment and wonder, whereas Archimedes' engineering was derived from, and subordinate to, his geometry.
  • Eratosthenes: Similar because he was a Hellenistic polymath who measured the earth and corresponded with the great mathematicians; differs because Eratosthenes is remembered for one brilliant measurement and for his literary criticism, not for a body of rigorous proofs.
  • Hipparchus: Similar because he was a Hellenistic scientist of the first rank who used geometry on nature; differs because Hipparchus worked on astronomy and the calendar and left no popular legends, while Archimedes is inseparable from the anecdotal tradition.
  • Ptolemy: Similar because his name also became an adjective and a system (Ptolemaic); differs because Ptolemy of Alexandria was an astronomer and geographer whose authority lasted into the Renaissance, whereas Archimedes' reputation is mathematical and mechanical rather than cosmological.
  • Thales: Similar because he is the traditional first Greek scientist and the subject of famous anecdotes; differs because Thales is a shadowy figure of the sixth century BCE credited with isolated insights, while Archimedes is known through complete, difficult treatises.
