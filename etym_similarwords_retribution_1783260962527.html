• Vengeance: Similar because both denote a return of harm for a wrong; differs because vengeance is personal, emotional, and private, with no guarantee of desert, whereas retribution claims to restore justice impersonally.
  • Revenge: Similar because both involve paying back injury; differs because revenge is the act of an aggrieved party driven by feeling and often excess, while retribution is measured, deserved, and morally framed.
  • Retaliation: Similar because both are a return for injury; differs because retaliation is tit-for-tat reciprocity, justified or not, without retribution's claim that consequence fits crime.
  • Punishment: Similar because both are penalties for wrongdoing; differs because punishment is the general term imposed by any authority and may be just or arbitrary, while retribution specifies a deserved, balanced return.
  • Penalty: Similar because both involve a sanction for wrongdoing; differs because penalty is the concrete fine or loss fixed by law or rule, with no implication of moral balance.
  • Requital: Similar because both mean a repayment; differs because requital is archaic and neutral — it can repay good as well as evil, whereas retribution is limited to deserved harm.
  • Reprisal: Similar because both are a return for injury; differs because reprisal is a state or legal act of retaliation, especially in war (seizing property or hostages), and need not be just.
  • Nemesis: Similar because both name divine retribution; differs because nemesis is the Greek personification of justice's pursuit, mythological and literary, not a legal or moral term.
  • Comeuppance: Similar because both mean deserved punishment; differs because comeuppance is informal and colloquial, suggesting a satisfying downfall, far lighter in register than retribution.
  • Payback: Similar because both mean a return for harm; differs because payback is slangy and personal, with no moral or legal weight.
  • Redress: Similar because both involve repairing a wrong; differs because redress seeks compensation or correction for a victim, whereas retribution punishes the wrongdoer.
  • Restitution: Similar because both share the notion of giving back; differs because restitution is the restoration of what was lost (via Latin restituere, a different root), while retribution is deserved punishment (via Latin retribuere).
  • Just deserts: Similar because both mean deserved consequence; differs because just deserts is an idiom (from "deserve"), blunter and less Latinate than retribution.
  • Atonement: Similar because both involve repair of wrong; differs because atonement is the wrongdoer's own voluntary reparation for sin, religious in colour, while retribution is imposed from outside.
