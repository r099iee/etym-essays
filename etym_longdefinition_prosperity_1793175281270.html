Prosperity is the state of flourishing: good fortune made durable, success that holds its shape across seasons. In its dominant modern sense it means material well-being — wealth, thriving trade, full employment, rising harvests — but the word has always carried a wider benediction, covering health, family increase, fair winds, and the general smile of fortune, so that to wish a man prosperity is to wish him everything that grows.

In the source sentence — the greeting Prosperity to thee, Tsarevitch Alexei — the word is a salutation, a fairy-tale good luck to you addressed to the questing hero. This is the optative, wishing use: the speaker does not describe the prince's bank balance but invokes fortune itself to walk beside him beyond the blue sea-ocean, where sisters marry hawks and hosts are routed. The capital P of the tale's diction makes it a formal blessing, the wonder-tale equivalent of Godspeed.

Core meaning in detail: The noun is built on the adjective prosperous, literally according to hope, from Latin pro spes, and it names both the condition of thriving and, by metonymy, the wealth that evidences it. Economists distinguish its shades: prosperity is cyclical and collective — a nation, a town, a trade enjoys prosperity — where wealth can sit idle in one man's vault. To be prosperous is to be in motion, growing, succeeding; the word dislikes the miser and loves the merchant, the farmer, and the returning hero.

All senses, primary and secondary:

  • Flourishing material well-being (primary modern sense): wealth, success, booming business, the prosperity of the northern towns. The bridge runs from Latin prosperus, favourable, hopeful: what accords with hope becomes what fills the purse.
  • A formal blessing or toast (the tale's sense, optative): prosperity to thee, I drink to your prosperity — the wish of good fortune upon another. The bridge is performative: naming the good state is thought to summon it, as in every toast ever raised.
  • Good fortune in general, luck's smile (older, broader sense): the 1611 Bible's in the day of prosperity be joyful means not GDP but God's favour. The bridge is the pre-economic world where fortune, health, children, and harvest were one undivided blessing.
  • National economic boom (technical, 20th c.): post-war prosperity, the measurable expansion of output and employment. The bridge narrows the general blessing into the economist's indicator, the good years between slumps.
  • Theological prosperity, health-and-wealth doctrine (religious jargon, modern): the prosperity gospel teaching that faith compels riches. The bridge is literalism: the Bible's agricultural blessings read as bank statements, a bridge many theologians refuse to cross.
  • Botanical and poetic flourishing (figurative): the prosperity of the garden, vines and roses thriving. The bridge returns the word to its root-sense of growing things, spes being hope embodied in seed and shoot.

Register and connotation: Prosperity is warm, respectable, and faintly formal — the diction of toasts, sermons, inaugural addresses, and fairy-tale greetings. It praises without envying: unlike riches, which can sneer, or wealth, which can accuse, prosperity wishes well even when it describes. Its shadow is complacency — prophets from Amos to the present have warned that prosperity rots vigilance — but the word itself never stops smiling.
