• Villain: Similar in denoting an unprincipled, malicious person who harms others; differs because villain implies grander malevolence, deliberate calculated wickedness, or a primary antagonistic role in a dramatic plot, whereas miscreant frequently designates petty delinquents, unruly troublemakers, or disreputable lawbreakers.
  • Scoundrel: Similar in expressing moral indignation toward a dishonorable person; differs because scoundrel emphasizes knavery, deceit, and ungentlemanly underhandedness, whereas miscreant carries heavier historical and legal overtones of societal deviance.
  • Rogue: Similar in describing someone who defies conventional societal rules; differs because rogue often possesses a playful, dashing, or endearingly rebellious charm, whereas miscreant is thoroughly pejorative and censorious.
  • Caitiff: Similar in describing a wretched, despicable individual; differs because caitiff heavily connotes cowardly baseness and pitiful spiritual captivity (from Latin captivus), while miscreant centers on behavioral misconduct and transgression.
  • Reprobate: Similar in describing a morally depraved, unrepentant wrongdoer; differs because reprobate carries theological undertones of being predestined for damnation or hardened in incorrigible vice, whereas miscreant secularized into civil delinquency.
  • Heretic: Similar in historical lineage where miscreant originally meant an unbeliever or false believer; differs because heretic remains firmly tethered to doctrinal dispute and ideological schism within religion, while modern miscreant has no necessary religious meaning.
  • Malefactor: Similar in denoting an evildoer or criminal; differs because malefactor is a formal, clinical, juristic term for one who commits statutory crimes, whereas miscreant blends behavioral misconduct with sharp moral disdain.
  • Ne'er-do-well: Similar in referring to a disreputable, troublesome character; differs because ne'er-do-well highlights chronic laziness, financial shiftlessness, and general incompetence rather than active malice or criminality.
  • Blackguard: Similar in denoting a scoundrel devoid of integrity; differs because blackguard emphasizes foul-mouthed vulgarity, lack of breeding, and abusive behavior, whereas miscreant focuses on antisocial lawbreaking.
  • Transgressor: Similar in denoting one who breaches rules; differs because transgressor is an analytical, formal term emphasizing the boundary-crossing nature of the act itself rather than the personal degeneracy of the actor.
