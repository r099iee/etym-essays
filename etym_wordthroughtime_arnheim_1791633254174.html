- 1930s: Weimar Avant-Garde & Classic Film Theory - Rudolf Arnheim gained international renown in Berlin with *Film als Kunst* (1932), establishing formalist film theory by demonstrating that cinema is art precisely because it diverges from mechanical reality.
- 1940s–1950s: Gestalt Aesthetic Revolution in American Exile - After escaping European fascism, Arnheim joined Sarah Lawrence College and published *Art and Visual Perception* (1954), revolutionizing visual arts pedagogy by anchoring balance, shape, and tension in Gestalt psychology.
- 1960s–1970s: The 'Visual Thinking' Epistemological Paradigm - At Harvard University, Arnheim dismantled the Cartesian dichotomy between sensation and thought with *Visual Thinking* (1969) and *The Dynamics of Architectural Form* (1977), proving that vision is itself a sophisticated mode of reasoning.
- Late 20th–21st Century: Canonical Pillar of Cognitive Aesthetics - Established as a timeless touchstone across cognitive psychology, art education, graphic design, and neuroaesthetics, celebrated for bridging the gap between empirical science and humanistic art.
