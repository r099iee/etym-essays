• Grasshoppers: Similar because locusts are grasshoppers — the same family Acrididae in the solitary phase; differs because “grasshopper” names the harmless solitary insect, while “locust” specifically denotes the swarming, crop-devouring phase and its biblical dread.
  • Crickets: Similar because both are familiar hopping, chirping orthopterans; differ because crickets (family Gryllidae) are a different family, nocturnal, and never swarm destructively.
  • Cicadas: Similar because American usage calls cicadas “locusts” and both emerge in vast numbers; differ because cicadas are true bugs (Hemiptera) that feed on tree sap and do not strip crops as locust swarms do.
  • Swarm: Similar because it names the collective form in which locusts act; differs because a swarm is any dense moving mass (bees, locusts, people) and is a count noun for the group, not the insect.
  • Plague: Similar because locusts are proverbially one of the biblical plagues and “plague” names the disaster they cause; differs because plague denotes calamity or epidemic disease rather than the insect itself.
  • Horde: Similar because both evoke a vast, destructive multitude (the metaphorical “locusts”); differs because horde is a neutral-to-pejorative word for any large group of people or animals, without the insect's devouring specificity.
  • Carob / St John's bread: Similar because carob pods were historically misidentified as the Baptist's “locusts” and the tree is still called a locust tree; differ because these are plant names (Ceratonia siliqua) that share only the misidentification, not the insect sense.
  • Parasites / vultures: Similar because all three are stock metaphors for people who consume others' resources; differ because “parasites” implies attachment to a host and “vultures” scavenging the dead, while “locusts” implies wholesale devastation of the living landscape.
