• gathered: Similar because both mean bringing items together; differs because gathered is neutral and casual, naming the action without judging the discrimination behind it, while culled stresses selective, quality-based choice.
  • compiled: Similar because both assemble material from sources; differs because compiled is the dry, bookish act of building a list, and it cannot hint at pruning or rejection the way culled does.
  • gleaned: Similar because both keep the farming metaphor of collection; differs because gleaning stresses picking up scattered remnants left behind after a harvest, whereas culling stresses positively picking out the choicest specimens.
  • winnowed: Similar because both separate the good from the bad; differs because winnowing images the removal of chaff by wind, closer to rejection, while culling can be purely acquisitive selection.
  • selected: Similar because both mean choosing from a larger set; differs because selected is neutral and procedural, carrying no hint of ruthless removal or of agricultural harvesting.
  • harvested: Similar because both imply reaping from a source; differs because harvesting is large-scale agricultural gathering without the fine discrimination or the cull’s edge of removal.
  • picked: Similar because both mean choosing by hand; differs because picked is casual and lacks the connotations of deliberate removal and quality-screening that culled carries.
  • pruned: Similar because both cut away the unwanted; differs because pruning is specifically about plants and shaping growth, while culled (as removal) applies to populations and collections.
  • weeded: Similar because both remove undesirable members; differs because weeded implies removing nuisance growth from a cultivated setting, lighter and less formal than a cull.
  • curated: Similar because both involve careful, expert choice; differs because curating is modern, museum- or media-flavoured and foregrounds the expert’s taste, while culling has older agricultural and animal-stock roots.
