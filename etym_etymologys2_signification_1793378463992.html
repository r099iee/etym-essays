Note: Latin significātiō is a compound derivation: the base noun signum (‘mark, token’) combines with the root of facere (‘to make, do’, from PIE *dʰeh₁-, ‘to put, place’), yielding the denominative verb significāre (‘to make a sign’), which then received the action-noun suffix -tiō (genitive -tiōnis). Some historical linguists debate whether signum derives from PIE *sek- (‘to cut’, i.e., an incised carving or notch) or from PIE *sekʷ- (‘to point out, say, follow’), but modern consensus (de Vaan 2008) favors the incision root *sek- as the most phonologically and culturally plausible origin for early Roman boundary marks.
