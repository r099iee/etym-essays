- Old English - blǣtan, an imitative verb for the sheep's cry, kin to other old words for crying out.
- Middle English to 18th centuries - bleat extends from the flock to human speech: anything feeble, plaintive, and trembling.
- 19th-20th centuries - prose uses bleating for weak excuses and helpless victims, dehumanizing them into livestock.
- Contemporary - bleat compresses weakness, trembling, and a silly helpless note into one verb — the cry of sheep and of the pitiable.
