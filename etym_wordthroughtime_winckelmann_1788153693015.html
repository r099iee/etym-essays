- [17th–18th c.: an ordinary German surname, "Winkel + Mann", the man from the corner village] - [Perception: provincial, plebeian, forgettable]
- [1755: "Thoughts on the Imitation of Greek Works" makes his name European] - [Perception: the obscure librarian becomes the manifesto-writer of Greek revival]
- [1754–1768: Rome, the Albani library, the History of the Art of Antiquity] - [Perception: reverence, scholarly glamour, the convert and the cardinal’s favourite]
- [1768: murdered at Trieste] - [Perception: instant myth — the martyr of archaeology, mourned by Goethe as the sire of a new sense of life]
- [19th c.: neoclassicism institutionalises him] - [Perception: the name becomes a method and a school; "Winckelmann" = art history itself]
- [20th–21st c.: academic revision] - [Perception: honoured founder, but his idealising Greek image is filed under historiography; the name now denotes a moment of European consciousness rather than a man]
