Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*plewd- / *plew-
"to float, fly, flow, beat, clap"
[Tentative PIE root; or Proto-Italic substrate *slaud- / *laud- "praise"]

Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*laud-
"praise, merit, glory, fame"
[Nominal root underlying early Italic civic commendation]
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
laus (genitive laudis)
"praise, commendation, renown, eulogy"
[Core Roman forensic and rhetorical concept]
↓
Classical Latin Verb (c. 1st c. BCE)
laudāre (participial stem laudāt-)
"to praise, extol, commend, eulogize"
[First conjugation frequentative verb]
↓
Late / Imperial Latin (c. 1st–2nd c. CE)
laudātōrius
"of or belonging to praise, commendatory"
[Adjective formed with suffix -ōrius denoting purpose/quality]
↓
Middle French (c. 15th c.)
laudatoire
"expressing praise, commendatory in speech"
[Scholarly borrowing from Latin legal and rhetorical registers]
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1560s)
laudatorie
"tending to praise, containing commendation"
[Anglicized via suffix -y replacing French -toire / Latin -torius]
↓
Modern English (present)
laudatory
"expressing, containing, or conveying warm praise"
