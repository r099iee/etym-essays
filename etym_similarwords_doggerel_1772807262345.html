• Jingle: Similar because both denote catchy, sing-song, metrically light verse or rhyme; differs because "jingle" stresses mechanical sound and is often neutral or approving in advertising contexts, whereas "doggerel" always carries a pejorative judgement of craft.
  • Ditty: Similar because both name short, simple, light compositions; differs because "ditty" is usually affectionate or neutral and may be a proper song, while "doggerel" is contemptuous about clumsy verse.
  • Light verse: Similar because both belong to the broad zone of comic or recreational poetry; differs because "light verse" names a legitimate, often highly skilled genre (Lear, Belloc, Nash), while "doggerel" names failure or deliberate crudeness of craft.
  • Hudibrastic verse: Similar because both denote comic octosyllabic rhyme of irregular tone; differs because "Hudibrastic" names one specific burlesque tradition derived from Butler's Hudibras, whereas "doggerel" is the broader, purely pejorative category.
  • Nursery rhyme: Similar because both cover simple, memorable, irregular children's verse; differs because "nursery rhyme" is a neutral genre term, while "doggerel" judges the verse as crude or comic.
  • Lampoon: Similar because both are used for comic, mocking composition; differs because "lampoon" is a satire of a specific person, usually in prose or verse of any quality, whereas "doggerel" says nothing about target and everything about metrical clumsiness.
  • Ballad: Similar because both involve rhymed, stanzaic, often popular verse; differs because "ballad" is a serious traditional narrative genre with its own conventions, while "doggerel" is an insult about bad verse.
  • Burlesque: Similar because both are comic, mocking, and low-register forms; differs because "burlesque" is a deliberate style of parody with an object, while "doggerel" may be unintentionally bad and is a quality judgement rather than a mode.
  • Rhymer's cliche / forced rhyme: Similar because both describe mechanical, predictable rhyme; differs because the cliche is a defect inside a poem, while "doggerel" labels the whole composition as inferior verse.
  • Drivel: Similar because both dismiss a text as worthless; differs because "drivel" attacks sense, coherence, and intelligence (in verse or prose) rather than metrical clumsiness.
  • Verse: Similar because both denote metrical composition; differs because "verse" is completely neutral and descriptive, whereas "doggerel" injects contempt for quality.
  • Epigram: Similar because both are short, pointed, often witty compositions; differs because "epigram" is a polished, respected classical form, the opposite of the carelessness doggerel implies.
