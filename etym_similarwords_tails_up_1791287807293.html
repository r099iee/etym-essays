• chin up: Similar because it is an encouragement to stay brave; differs because it refers to the human chin, not an animal tail, and is more widely used.
  • buck up: Similar because it means cheer up and take courage; differs because it is more brisk and sometimes means hurry up.
  • cheer up: Similar because it is the plain equivalent urging happier mood; differs because it lacks the sporting, tail-wagging image.
  • keep smiling: Similar because it urges outward optimism; differs because it focuses on the face rather than posture.
  • take heart: Similar because it urges courage; differs because it is more literary and solemn, less breezy.
  • keep your pecker up: Similar because it is vintage British slang for stay cheerful; differs because pecker means spirits and is now often avoided for its other meaning.
  • look on the bright side: Similar because it urges optimism; differs because it is a full clause about perspective, not a snappy imperative.
  • perk up: Similar because it means become livelier; differs because it describes the recovery itself rather than ordering it.
  • brace up: Similar because it means pull oneself together; differs because it stresses fortitude and effort rather than jauntiness.
  • heads up: Similar because in its encouragement sense it means keep head high; differs because today it more often means warning or alert.
