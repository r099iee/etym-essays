• prayers: The plainest synonym, naming any address to a deity; supplications are narrower, always petitionary and always humble, and belong to a more formal register.
  • entreaties: Similar in denoting earnest asking; they differ in being addressed to anyone and in lacking the religious and liturgical colour that supplications carry.
  • petitions: Similar as formal requests, especially written ones to an authority; they differ by being institutional and procedural, whereas supplications stress humility and emotional urgency.
  • pleas: Similar in urgency; they differ by being more colloquial and more forensic, and by suggesting a defendant's request rather than a worshipper's prayer.
  • appeals: Similar in asking for a decision to be reversed or a need met; they differ by presupposing a right or a procedure and by carrying no suggestion of kneeling humility.
  • invocations: Similar in being addressed to a deity; they differ by naming the act of calling a god or saint to be present, rather than asking for a favour.
  • litanies: Similar as corporate, repeated prayer; they differ by being a fixed liturgical form with responses, where supplications may be spontaneous.
  • orisons: Similar as an archaic literary word for prayers; they differ by being simply "prayers" without the special note of petition and abasement, and they are now poetic or humorous.
  • intercessions: Similar in praying on behalf of another; they differ by focusing on the mediation (a saint or priest praying for someone), not on the petitioner's humility.
  • rogations: Similar as public, formal prayer for a need; they differ by being a specific liturgical observance (the Rogation Days) rather than a general term.
  • beseechings: Similar in humble urgency; it differs by being a rare nominalisation, more archaic still than supplications, and confined to literary prose.
