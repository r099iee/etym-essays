• hee-haw: Similar because it is the onomatopoeic noun/verb for the donkey cry itself; differs because it imitates the two syllables directly, while bray is the standard dictionary term.
  • bellow: Similar because it means a loud deep animal cry (bull, stag); differs because it is lower and more powerful, not nasal and comic like bray.
  • blare / blat: Similar because they mean loud harsh sounds of brass or animals; differs because they stress amplified harshness, not specifically donkey timbre.
  • honk: Similar because it is a loud nasal cry (goose, car horn); differs because it is shorter and higher, while bray is prolonged hee-haw.
  • trumpet (verb): Similar because elephants and brass are said to trumpet loudly; differs because it implies piercing brilliance rather than grating comedy.
  • bleat: Similar because it is the thin cry of sheep/goats; differs because it is higher, tremulous, and less loud than bray.
  • neigh / whinny: Similar because they are equine cries (horse); differs because they are higher, more musical, and signal horse not donkey.
  • bawl / roar: Similar because they mean to cry out loudly (human or animal); differs because they are generic about volume, not species-specific.
  • cackle (for braying laugh): Similar because a cackling laugh is loud and harsh; differs because it evokes hens/witches, more high-pitched than braying.
  • snort / grunt: Similar because they are harsh animal/human noises of effort or contempt; differs because they are brief expulsions, not prolonged cries.
