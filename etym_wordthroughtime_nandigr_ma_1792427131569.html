- [Epic Composition Era (c. 800–200 BCE; Rāmāyaṇa)] — immortalized as the austere village northeast of Ayodhya where Prince Bharata governed the Kosala realm as an ascetic viceroy dedicated to Rama’s sandals — [perception: sacred symbol of selfless fraternal love, ascetic kingship, and moral integrity]
- [Classical & Purāṇic Pilgrimage Age (c. 2nd c. BCE – 10th c. CE)] — recognized as an essential tīrtha in the sacred landscape of Kosala (modern Bharatkund), frequented by pilgrims performing ancestor rites and honoring Bharat Milap — [perception: holy pilgrimage site sanctified by the footprints of Bharata and Rama]
- [Early Medieval Epigraphic & Land-Grant Era (c. 6th–13th c. CE)] — adopted as an auspicious toponym (*Nandigrāma*, *Nandgaon*) across Western India and Bengal for settlements granted to Vedic scholars and monasteries — [perception: celebrated toponym designating joyous, consecrated agrarian communities]
- [Bhakti Movement & Regional Literature (c. 15th–18th c.)] — emotionally celebrated in Tulsīdās’s *Rāmacaritamānasa* as the ultimate crucible of unshakeable devotion and moral rectitude — [perception: the pinnacle of self-abnegation and fraternal loyalty]
- [Modern Historical & Political Era (19th–21st c.)] — preserved as a major archaeological and religious site near Ayodhya, while the homophonous Bengal town emerged in 2007 as a global emblem of grassroots agrarian resistance — [perception: revered epic heritage landmark and contemporary political symbol of land and autonomy]
