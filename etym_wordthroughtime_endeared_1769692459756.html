- Late 16th c. 'To make dear, loved, or precious' — entered English as a transitive verb meaning to render someone beloved, with a warm, affectionate register.
- 17th–18th c. 'To bind by affection, to attach' — used of people and qualities that win lasting love; the phrase 'endear oneself to' becomes established, implying natural or deliberate charm.
- 19th c. 'Made beloved through gentle or attractive qualities' — in Regency usage a precise, analytical term for how temperament earns another's affection.
- Contemporary 'Held in high emotional esteem' — most often the past participle (endeared / endearing), describing warm attachment won through kindness rather than passion.
