- 13th to 14th Century - Enters Middle English (as *galeie* or *galie*) via Old French from Medieval Latin and Byzantine Greek; utilized exclusively in military and maritime contexts to denote Mediterranean oared warships.
- 15th to 16th Century - Expands into shipboard domestic architecture to designate the cookroom or cooking galley of seafaring sailing vessels; simultaneously, Mediterranean states expand the penal sentence of rowing "in the galleys."
- 17th Century - First adopted into letterpress printing workshops during the Early Modern period; compositors name their shallow wooden trays "galleys" after the vessel's hull shape, subsequently calling the pulled proof sheets "galley proofs."
- 18th to 19th Century - Solidifies across the publishing industry as the standard term for unpaged proof sheets; in French and British literary circles, galley revision becomes an indispensable rite of authorial refinement.
- 20th to 21st Century - Retains its technical status in commercial book publishing (often abbreviated as digital galleys or e-galleys sent to critics), while in modern aviation, "galley" becomes the universal standard term for an aircraft's compact culinary galley.
