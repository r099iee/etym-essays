• Fog: Similar because both represent meteorological suspensions of condensed liquid water droplets near the ground that obstruct human vision; differs because fog is scientifically denser and more opaque, reducing horizontal visibility to less than one kilometer, whereas mist is lighter and more diffuse, allowing visibility between one and two kilometers with a softer visual quality.
  • Haze: Similar because both produce a diffuse atmospheric veil that obscures distant landmarks and horizon lines; differs because haze is caused entirely by dry, microscopic solid particulates (such as dust, industrial smoke, pollen, or sea salt aerosols) under lower humidity, whereas mist consists strictly of liquid water droplets suspended in nearly saturated air (relative humidity $>95\%$).
  • Smog: Similar because both create a murky, ground-level airborne blanket that severely degrades visibility; differs because smog is an anthropogenic, noxious combination of industrial pollutants, sulfur dioxide, ozone, and vehicle exhaust (originally a blend of smoke and fog), whereas mist is a clean, natural meteorological phenomenon of condensed water vapor.
  • Vapor: Similar because both involve the presence of water in the atmosphere; differs because vapor in physics denotes water in its invisible, gaseous state (or visible hot steam above boiling water), whereas mist consists of already condensed, visible liquid droplets suspended in cool air.
  • Obscurity: Similar because both describe a condition wherein things cannot be clearly seen, known, or understood (as in "the mists of history"); differs because obscurity is an entirely abstract noun designating the conceptual state of being hidden, dark, or unintelligible, lacking the physical, meteorological imagery and visual beauty inherent to mist.
  • Shroud: Similar because both metaphorically and visually envelop, cover, and conceal objects from sight; differs because a shroud originates from a heavy burial sheet or funeral winding-cloth, conveying grim, heavy, or fatal concealment, whereas mists suggest an ethereal, shifting, semitransparent, and naturally dissolving veil.
