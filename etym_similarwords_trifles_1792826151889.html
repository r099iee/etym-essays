• Trivialities: Similar because both name matters of slight importance; differs because trivialities stresses commonplace emptiness and is cooler and more Latinate, while trifles stresses lightness and often carries affection or weary dismissal.
  • Bagatelles: Similar because both mean light, unimportant things or amusements; differs because bagatelles is French, elegant, and literary, often decorative miniatures, while trifles is the plain English everyday word.
  • Trinkets: Similar because both can name small inexpensive objects; differs because trinkets is concrete and usually ornamental jewellery or souvenirs, while trifles ranges over abstract matters, worries, and delays as well.
  • Knick-knacks: Similar because both name small fanciful household objects; differs because knick-knacks is cosy and domestic shelf-decoration, while trifles is broader and more judgmental about worth.
  • Baubles: Similar because both dismiss things as showy but worthless; differs because a bauble glitters and deceives with cheap shine, while a trifle is merely small and may not glitter at all.
  • Fripperies: Similar because both scorn unnecessary small adornments; differs because fripperies attacks ostentatious frills of dress or style with moral disapproval, while trifles dismisses with a shrug rather than a sermon.
  • Minutiae: Similar because both name tiny details; differs because minutiae is neutral or scholarly precision-work (the minutiae of a manuscript), while trifles declares the details unworthy of attention.
  • Peccadilloes: Similar because both minimise faults as small; differs because peccadilloes names minor sins or offences specifically, while trifles covers any small matter, innocent or not.
  • Nothings: Similar because Shakespearean nothings and trifles both breed jealousy and quarrels out of air; differs because nothings denies existence itself, while trifles admits the thing exists but denies it weight.
