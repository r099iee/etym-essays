• Persistent: Similar because both describe continuing in the face of difficulty; differs because persistent is the neutral term for going on over time, with no image of a clinging or grasping hold, whereas tenacious makes the grip itself the point.
  • Persevering: Similar because both imply continuing effort; differs because persevering is positive and suggests moral effort against setbacks, while tenacious names the sheer adhesive strength of a thing that sticks.
  • Dogged: Similar because both describe refusal to give up; differs because dogged suggests grim, slogging determination, while tenacious evokes a physical, vine-like clinging that will not let go.
  • Determined: Similar because both imply firm purpose; differs because determined stresses a settled decision of the will, while tenacious stresses the unyielding grip itself.
  • Resolute: Similar because both describe firmness in purpose; differs because resolute is nobler and more deliberate, suggesting a fixed resolution, whereas tenacious is instinctive and physical, a hold that will not relax.
  • Steadfast: Similar because both describe constancy; differs because steadfast implies loyalty and unwavering allegiance, while tenacious implies a clinging hold that may be directed at anything.
  • Unyielding: Similar because both describe resistance to force; differs because unyielding describes the refusal to bend under pressure, while tenacious describes the active holding on of a grip.
  • Obstinate: Similar because both involve firm refusal to yield; differs because obstinate implies unreasonable stubbornness in the face of persuasion, whereas tenacious celebrates firmness of hold without condemning it.
  • Stubborn: Similar because both describe a refusal to change; differs because stubborn is the everyday word for inflexible resistance, often with mild censure, while tenacious carries a more admiring, physical image.
  • Pertinacious: Similar because both derive from the same Latin root (pertinax, tenax) and both mean clinging persistence; differs because pertinacious is tenacity taken to a fault - annoying, relentless insistence.
  • Adhesive: Similar because both describe a thing that sticks; differs because adhesive is technical and physical, naming actual stickiness or glue, while tenacious is primarily about a firm hold rather than stickiness.
  • Clinging: Similar because both describe holding on; differs because clinging is more passive and needy - hanging on for support - while tenacious suggests active, forceful grip.
