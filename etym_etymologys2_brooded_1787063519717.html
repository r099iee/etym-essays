PIE (reconstructed)
*bhre-
"to burn, to heat,
to warm"

Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–1st c. CE)
*brōd-
"brood, offspring —
what is warmed into life"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
brēdan
"to hatch, to warm eggs
and sit upon them"
↓
Middle English (13th–15th c.)
broden
"to incubate; to keep a
thing warm and close"
↓
Modern English (1500s–present)
brood
"to sit on eggs; to think
moodily and at length"
↓
English (preterite)
BROODED
"sat brooding; hung over
oppressively"

Note on the nuance: the PIE root is reconstructed, and the derivation through Proto-Germanic is standard, though the exact sense of *brōd- (“brood” as the warmed young versus “heat”) is debated among scholars. The verb “brood” itself is a later formation built on the noun “brood”, and its modern “anxious thought” sense is a metaphorical extension of the hen’s long motionless sitting, attested clearly from the 18th century.
