- [Vedic & Epic Genesis: c. 1st millennium BCE] — Emerged in the ancient Sanskrit epic tradition (*Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*) as the primary patronymic epithet for Prince Rāma, celebrating his royal descent from the legendary conquering King Raghu and invoking ancestral chivalry, filial piety, and dharmic righteousness — [perception: celebrated heroic patronymic signifying solar royal descent, supreme chivalry, and dharmic kingship]
- [Classical Sanskrit Golden Age: c. 4th–8th c. CE] — Immortally codified in Kālidāsa’s monumental court epic *Raghuvaṃśa* and Bhavabhūti’s tragic dramas (*Mahāvīracarita*, *Uttararāmacarita*), expanding into a high-register literary epithet for the ideal monarch who balances imperial conquest with ascetic renunciation — [perception: pinnacle of classical aesthetic nobility, royal genealogy, and poetic refinement]
- [Medieval Bhakti Devotional Movement: c. 12th–17th c. CE] — Transformed by poet-saints (such as Tulsīdās, Kabīr, and Tyāgarāja) from a formal courtly patronymic into an intimate, spiritually electrifying name of the divine savior (*Raghupati Rāghava*), chanted across temples and pilgrim roads — [perception: tender object of personal surrender, emotional prayer, and divine salvation]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era: 19th–21st c.] — Cemented in global consciousness through Mahatma Gandhi’s favorite hymn (*Raghupati Rāghava Rājā Rām*), universalized as a common given name and cultural touchstone representing selfless ethical leadership and communal harmony — [perception: ubiquitous pan-Indian personal name and enduring symbol of universal ethical harmony and righteousness]
