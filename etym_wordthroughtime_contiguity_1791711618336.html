- 16th to early 17th Century: Latinate legal and territorial emergence - Signified physical touching, border demarcation, and real-property borders between adjoining parcels of land.
- Late 17th to 18th Century: Epistemological and associationist psychology adoption - Championed by John Locke and David Hume to explain how ideas experienced closely in time and space become linked in the human mind.
- 19th Century: Geopolitical, topological, and imperial consolidation - Vital in colonial border treaties, international maritime law (territorial contiguity doctrine), and geometric spatial mathematics.
- 20th to 21st Century: Computational topology, network architecture, and urban planning - Expanded to graph theory, memory addressing in computer science, and ecological habitat corridor connectivity.
