ROOT WORDS: steepening = steep (adjective) + -en (verbal suffix). Two lexical components require two chains, each in isolation. The participle -ing is a grammatical suffix shared by all verbs and is not part of the lexical etymology.
CHAIN 1 — steep (adjective):
Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European (tentative, reconstructed): the adjective has no securely reconstructible PIE ancestor; some handbooks connect the Germanic base *staup- “to project, rise up, stand out” with the Indo-European root *(s)teup- “to strike, knock; to cut off” (in the sense of something standing out against the sky), but this is tentative and not settled, and the OED records the adjective’s origin as obscure.
Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *staupaz “high, lofty, towering, steep” (reconstructed): a Germanic word of verticality before it was a word of inclination.
Stage 3 — Old English stēap “high, lofty, towering; deep (of water); steep” (attested 8th–10th c.; Beowulf’s stēap hof “lofty hall” and the stēap stānhlīðu “steep stone-cliffs”): inherited from Proto-Germanic *staupaz.
Stage 4 — Middle English steep / stepe “high; sharply inclined” (attested 12th–15th c.): the sense narrows from “lofty, towering” toward “hard to climb, sharply inclined.”
Stage 5 — Modern English steep (adjective) “sharply inclined, nearly vertical”: the base adjective on this card, ready to receive the verbal suffix.
CHAIN 2 — the verbal suffix -en (as in steepen, deepen, lengthen):
Stage 1 — PIE *-nó- “having the quality of X” (reconstructed; the adjectival suffix visible in Latin plēnus “full” and English golden, wooden): supplies the -n- element.
Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *-inōn “to make X, to become X” (reconstructed; a denominative verbal suffix forming causatives and inchoatives from adjectives and nouns): the direct ancestor of the English suffix.
Stage 3 — Old English -nian (attested; the suffix of the class II weak verbs, e.g. hālnian “to heal”): productive throughout the Old English period.
Stage 4 — Middle English -nen / -en (attested 12th–15th c.; e.g. hardenen “to harden,” gladdenen): the suffix continues unchanged in function.
Stage 5 — Modern English -en; combined with steep in the verb steepen “to make or become steep” (first recorded 16th c.), whose present participle is STEEPENING (the form on this card): a late, fully productive coinage of the ancient suffix.
Notes on debate and nuance: The identity of the verb steep “to soak, immerse” (Old English stīepan, from a different Germanic base with a different vowel) is a separate word that has converged in spelling with the adjective; it does not belong to this chain. The “-en” suffix historically forms both causatives (“to make steep”) and inchoatives (“to become steep”); steepen is attested in both uses, the intransitive “to become steep” being the sense on this card. The PIE reconstruction *(s)teup- for the steep-cluster is tentative and is not accepted by all authorities.”
