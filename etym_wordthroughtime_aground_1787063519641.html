- c. 1500 (Maritime Coining) - Formed in late Middle / Early Modern English as a prepositional compound (*on ground*) to denote ships striking the seabed or shoreline.
- 16th–17th c. (Navigational Regularization) - Becomes a standard technical term in British Admiralty courts, merchant logs, and naval charts, distinguishing temporary strandings from total shipwrecks.
- 18th c. (Literary Metaphorization) - Broadens into political, philosophical, and moral discourse to describe stalled ambitions, bankrupt ventures, and failed arguments.
- 19th–21st c. (Global Modernity) - Retains dual vitality: precise nautical/ecological terminology for marine groundings alongside ubiquitous metaphorical usage in business, diplomacy, and everyday language.
