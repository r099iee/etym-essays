- The Roman guardian (classical Latin) - a curator is one appointed to care for another's affairs - a minor, an estate, an inheritance
- The English guardian & curate (14th - 17th c.) - entering English via Old French curateur, the word names a legal guardian and, through the same Latin cura, a parish priest (curate) - care of souls
- The museum officer (17th c. onward) - the decisive shift: a curator becomes the official in charge of a museum, library or collection, care of objects replacing care of persons
- The widened modern sense (20th - 21st c.) - spreads from museums to zoos, archives and finally digital data; 'to curate' a playlist or feed makes care itself the essence of the office even where nothing physical is kept
