Wickerwork is material woven from pliable twigs and the ware made from it. As a mass noun it means open lattice weaving of willow withies, cane, rattan, or similar osiers; as a count noun it means articles, structures, or textures so woven.

In Durant's sentence, Clay placed upon wickerwork to keep the latter from being burned, wickerwork means a basketry frame or mat serving as mold and fuel-guard. Clay smeared over it hardened on heating into a fireproof shell that kept its shape after the wicker burned away. The repetition of the word underscores its role as temporary scaffold for permanent pottery. This is the technological-origins sense.

Principal senses today:

  • Woven material and texture: interlaced rods and splints forming an open lattice: chair seats of wickerwork, fences of wickerwork, wickerwork pattern. Emphasizes visible weave, airiness, lightness, and flexibility before firing or finishing.
  • Articles made of wicker: baskets, hampers, cradles, chairs, tables, screens, lobster pots: a shop selling wickerwork, colonial wickerwork. Here nearly synonymous with wicker furniture, but broader to include fences and hurdles.
  • Craft and construction method: the technique of weaving osiers around stakes: to teach wickerwork, traditional wickerwork hurdles. Wattle-and-daub walls, for example, are hazel wickerwork plastered with clay, exactly the Durant process on architectural scale.
  • Imitation pattern: by extension, any weave-like surface recalling wicker: brick wickerwork, concrete with wickerwork impression, wickerwork stitch in knitting. Archaeologists speak of wickerwork impressions on early potsherds.

Technical distinctions:

  • Material specificity: true wickerwork uses round rigid withies for stakes and thinner weavers, unlike basketry in general which includes coiling and twining with grasses, and unlike cane work which uses split rattan peel for chair seats.
  • Structure: warp stakes held by weft weavers in plain, twill, or open weave. The result is strong yet light, springy, ventilated, ideal for carrying, storing, sitting.
  • Durability and care: vulnerable to fire, rot, and brittleness, hence historically lined with clay or cloth. Modern resin wicker mimics the look in plastic for outdoor use.

Figurative and cultural resonances: wickerwork evokes rusticity, colonial verandas, cottage gardens, craft fairs, and pre-industrial ingenuity. It collocates with willow, cane, rattan, woven, lattice, hurdle, hamper. Register is standard domestic and technical, slightly nostalgic. Unlike industrial metalwork or brickwork, wickerwork feels handmade and warm. The Durant use adds deep-time prestige: humble fence-weaving as ancestor of Chinese porcelain.

In short, wickerwork names both a humble lattice of bent shoots and a deep technological principle of shaping space by interlacing. Its lightness, ventilation, repairability, and human scale keep it alive from Neolithic fish-traps to contemporary pavilions and gallery sculpture.
