• Stolid: Similar because both refer to an unexcitable, calm, and unemotional disposition; differs because stolid suggests a heavy, immovable, almost wooden impassivity that can imply dullness or lack of imagination, whereas phlegmatic emphasizes an innate coolness, steady rational balance, and peaceful equanimity.
  • Imperturbable: Similar because neither individual can be easily upset, startled, or provoked; differs because imperturbable highlights an active, polished mental serenity and supreme poise under intense pressure, while phlegmatic is a broader, deeper, constitutional trait of natural slowness to react.
  • Impassive: Similar because both involve showing no outward sign of emotion; differs because impassive focuses primarily on facial expression and physical demeanor (the unreadable mask, which may conceal inner turmoil), whereas phlegmatic describes a genuine internal emotional tranquility and low arousal threshold.
  • Stoic: Similar because both endure hardship, pain, or chaos without crying out or complaining; differs because stoic represents a conscious, disciplined philosophical fortitude that masters and suppresses passion, while phlegmatic is a natural, uncalculated temperament that simply does not feel turbulent passion in the first place.
  • Placid: Similar because both convey an outward aura of peaceful quiet and gentle contentment; differs because placid has a softer, sweeter, and more tranquil connotation (like a calm country pond), whereas phlegmatic has more psychological weight, detachment, and emotional cool.
  • Unflappable: Similar because both describe someone who keeps their head when everyone else is in a panic; differs because unflappable is a more modern, informal term conveying sharp, agile confidence and swagger under crisis, whereas phlegmatic evokes a slow, steady, pipe-smoking deliberateness.
  • Serene: Similar because both remain untroubled by life's tempests; differs because serene carries a radiant, luminous, and often spiritual or aesthetic quality of profound inner peace, while phlegmatic is more mundane, grounded, and coolly pragmatic.
  • Equable: Similar because both signify an even temper that avoids extreme emotional peaks and valleys; differs because equable specifically describes a uniform, cheerful consistency of mood, whereas phlegmatic can tilt toward inertia, sluggishness, or apathy.
  • Unruffled: Similar because both maintain composure when confronted with unexpected difficulties; differs because unruffled emphasizes smooth surface elegance and the effortless preservation of dignity, whereas phlegmatic refers to a deep, constitutional metabolic pacing.
  • Apathetic: Similar because both can exhibit a lack of passionate reaction or motivation; differs because apathetic is distinctly negative, denoting a total absence of care, moral indifference, or depressive withdrawal, while phlegmatic is often a positive, stable virtue of unshakeable composure.
  • Torpid: Similar because both involve slow movement and reduced responsiveness; differs because torpid implies physical numbness, hibernation, illness, or sleep-induced lethargy, whereas phlegmatic is a permanent, healthy personality disposition.
  • Composed: Similar because both exhibit control over emotional reactions; differs because composed often implies an effort of self-control or social decorum, while phlegmatic requires no deliberate effort—the person is naturally cool and unaffected.
  • Nonchalant: Similar because both appear casual and unconcerned in serious situations; differs because nonchalant often involves an air of affected carelessness, breezy nonchalance, or fashionable detachment, whereas phlegmatic is completely authentic, unpretentious, and earnest.
