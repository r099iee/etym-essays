• Harfoots: Similar because both represent the two most populous ancestral breeds of Hobbits who migrated west across the Misty Mountains into Eriador; differs because Harfoots were smaller, browner-skinned, beardless, dwelt in burrows on hillsides, and had an intense traditional dread of deep water and boats, whereas Stoors were broader, heavier, dwelt in flat river valleys, and loved swimming and boating.
  • Fallohides: Similar because both are foundational ancestral kindreds of the Hobbit race; differs because Fallohides were taller, slimmer, fairer of skin and hair, and favored forests and hunting rather than riverlands and fishing, serving as aristocratic leaders rather than rustic boatmen.
  • Hobbits: Similar because Stoors constitutes one of the three primary historical branches that merged to form the unified Hobbit population of the Shire; differs because Hobbits is the comprehensive overarching racial designation for the entire halfling species, whereas Stoors specifically denotes this particular river-loving genetic and cultural lineage.
  • Halflings: Similar because both terms identify the diminutive mortal race standing roughly three to four feet in height; differs because Halflings is an external exonym primarily employed by Men and Elves, whereas Stoors is an internal genealogical and historical tribal subdivision.
  • Periannath: Similar because both refer to the small-statured folk of the Shire and Eriador; differs because Periannath is the formal Sindarin Elvish collective noun for the Halfling people, carrying an elevated linguistic register, while Stoors is a rustic Germanic-style tribal endonym.
  • River-folk: Similar because both describe communities dwelling along the banks of major waterways and sustaining themselves through fishing and watercraft; differs because River-folk is a broad descriptive category encompassing any riparian culture (including human settlements along the Anduin), whereas Stoors identifies a specific Hobbit breed.
  • Bucklanders: Similar because the hobbits of Buckland are the direct geographical and cultural descendants of the Stoors who migrated into the Eastfarthing; differs because Bucklanders denotes the late Third Age geopolitical population dwelling east of the Brandywine River behind the High Hay, whereas Stoors is the ancient racial and philological breed.
  • Dunlendings: Similar because the Stoors dwelt in peace among the Dunlendings in the southern foothills of the Misty Mountains for several centuries and adopted some of their vocabulary; differs because Dunlendings are a tall, dark-haired race of mortal Men with an ancient grievance against the Rohirrim, whereas Stoors are Halflings.
  • Marish-folk: Similar because the hobbits of the Marish in the Shire’s Eastfarthing preserve heavy Stoor ancestry, mud-boots, and agricultural riverside traditions; differs because Marish-folk is a regional Shire designation, whereas Stoors represents the ancestral racial stock.
  • Dwarves: Similar because both are characterized by thick-set, heavy bodily frames, deep physical stamina, and a willingness to wear sturdy boots; differs because Dwarves are an entirely separate humanoid race created by Aulë, possessing dense stone-like constitution, long braided beards, and underground mining traditions, whereas Stoors are mortal Halflings closely related to Men.
