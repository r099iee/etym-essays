- 1st c. BCE — Pompeius Magnus, the general; the name is a political asset and, for his sons, a hereditary claim.
- Middle English — “Pompey” enters through translations of Roman history, trimmed to a familiar English shape.
- 1599 — Shakespeare's Julius Caesar fixes the name on the stage as the shadow behind the conspiracy.
- 17th–18th c. — “Pompey the Great” becomes a schoolroom example of military glory undone by civil war.
- 19th c. — the name acquires a monument misnomer (Pompey's Pillar) and a British place nickname (Portsmouth).
- 20th–21st c. — chiefly historical and literary, with the Portsmouth nickname the only everyday use.
