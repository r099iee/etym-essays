• Beam: Similar because a lintel is a kind of horizontal load-bearing beam; differs because beam is generic (any long timber or metal support, such as a ceiling joist), whereas lintel is specifically the member spanning an opening such as a door or window.
  • Header: Similar because a header is a beam spanning an opening in timber framing; differs because header is carpentry jargon for the beam over rough openings in frame construction, while lintel is the general architectural term covering stone and masonry.
  • Jamb: Similar because both are structural parts of a doorway; differs because jambs are the vertical side posts of an opening, whereas the lintel is the horizontal top member.
  • Threshold: Similar because both frame a doorway; differs because the threshold is the horizontal sill at the foot of a door, while the lintel is at the top.
  • Architrave: Similar because both are horizontal members above an opening; differs because an architrave is the ornamental moulding/beam resting on columns in classical architecture, not necessarily a load-bearing member over an opening.
  • Epistyle: Similar because it is the Greek name for the same class of horizontal member above columns; differs because it belongs to classical columnar architecture and is not specifically the beam over a door or window.
  • Arch: Similar because both support the weight above an opening; differs because an arch distributes load in a curve and can span wider openings, whereas a lintel is a straight horizontal member of limited span.
  • Transom: Similar because both are horizontal members in an opening; differs because a transom sits across a window or above a door as a crossbar, usually not load-bearing in the structural sense of a lintel.
  • Sill: Similar because both are horizontal members of an opening; differs because a sill is the bottom member of a window frame, while the lintel is the top.
