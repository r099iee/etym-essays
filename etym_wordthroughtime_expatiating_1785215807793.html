- 16th–17th century - “Expatiate” enters English (from the Latin for roaming beyond bounds) meaning to range freely in speech or writing — to wander at large over a subject; also literally to roam widely in space.
- 18th century - Becomes the standard rhetorical term for speaking at great length: “to expatiate upon” a topic is to dilate over it with freedom and relish, a positive performance of expansive discourse.
- 19th century - Common in biography and essay prose; Mill “was never weary of expatiating on all the advantages of so singular a relationship” — a tireless, obsessive ranging over a beloved theme.
- 20th–21st century - Retreats from everyday speech, surviving in formal and literary registers; its spatial image of free roaming (the opposite of digressing) still distinguishes it from mere elaborating or discoursing.
