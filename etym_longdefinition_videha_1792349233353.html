Videha (proper noun & philosophical concept) — An ancient kingdom of late Vedic and epic India located across northern Bihar and the southeastern plains (Terai) of modern Nepal, celebrated as one of the most prominent early realms (*mahājanapadas*) of northern South Asia, the royal domain of King Janaka, and a radiant epicenter of Upanishadic metaphysics. Simultaneously, in classical Sanskrit and Vedantic philosophy, videha functions as an adjective and compound noun literally meaning "bodiless, devoid of physical form, or unembodied" (from the privative prefix vi- + deha, "body"), providing the conceptual foundation for the doctrine of videha-mukti (liberation attained upon the dissolution of the mortal physical frame).

Geographical and Historical Identity: The Eastern Vedic Frontier. Geographically, the kingdom of Videha was naturally demarcated by formidable topographical boundaries: bounded on the west by the Sadānīrā River (modern Gandaki), on the east by the Kauśikī River (modern Kosi), on the south by the sacred Gaṅgā (Ganges), and on the north by the forested foothills of the Himalayas. Its historic capital was the famed city of Mithilā (modern Janakpur and surrounding regions of Tirhut). As recorded in the foundational narrative of the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, Videha represented the easternmost vanguard of early Indo-Aryan agricultural settlement. Under the leadership of King Māthava Videgha and his family priest Gotama Rāhūgaṇa, the marshy, uncultivated woodlands east of the Sadānīrā were cleared, drained, and consecrated by sacrificial fire, transforming the fertile alluvial plains into one of the richest and most densely populated civilizational centers of ancient India.

Political Evolution: From Monarchy to the Vajjian Republic. The political trajectory of Videha reflects the broader constitutional transformations of ancient India. During the late Vedic period (c. 9th–7th centuries BCE), Videha was governed as an illustrious hereditary monarchy under the Janaka dynasty. However, around the 6th century BCE—coinciding with the lifetimes of Gautama Buddha and Vardhamāna Mahāvīra—the monarchy collapsed or was peaceably restructured into an aristocratic republic. Videha joined with its neighbors, including the Licchavis of Vaiśālī, the Jñātrikas of Kuṇḍagrāma, and the Mallas, to form the formidable Vajjika League (the Vṛji Confederacy). This confederacy operated under an elective council of elders and represented one of the world's earliest documented republican and democratic governing experiments, fiercely resisting the imperialistic expansion of the neighboring kingdom of Magadha until its eventual annexation by King Ajātaśatru.

Philosophical Dimension: The Doctrine of Videha-Mukti. Beyond its geopolitical reality, Videha occupies an elevated station in Hindu metaphysics and Yogic psychology. In Advaita Vedānta and the Yoga Vāsiṣṭha, a crucial distinction is drawn between two modalities of spiritual liberation:

  • Jīvanmukti (Liberation While Living): The state of an enlightened sage who continues to inhabit an active physical body, acting in the world without egoic attachment or karmic accumulation, experiencing phenomenal existence like an actor playing a scripted role.
  • Videhamukti (Bodiless Liberation): The final, absolute emancipation attained when the physical body (*sthūla-śarīra*) perishes, the subtle body (*sūkṣma-śarīra*) dissolves, and all remaining *prārabdha karma* is completely exhausted. The individual consciousness merges irrevocably into the infinite, unmanifest ocean of Brahman, leaving no trace of individuality.
  • In Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras (1.19): The term videha refers to celestial beings or disembodied spiritual entities (*videha-prakṛti-layānām*) who, having transcended gross physical incarnation through profound meditative absorption, experience a subtle, discarnate level of cosmic awareness without having achieved ultimate liberating insight (*prajñā*).

Mythological Origin of the Name: In the Purāṇas, the kingdom's name is inextricably linked to the legend of King Nimi. When Nimi died from a mutual curse exchanged with sage Vasiṣṭha, he refused to have his spirit re-housed into a gross physical body when the deities offered to resurrect him, preferring to dwell invisibly as an unembodied spiritual presence (*vi-deha*) in the eyes and breaths of living beings. His domain and descendants were consequently named Videha, symbolizing an entire royal lineage dedicated to spiritual transcendence over physical form.

Register and Scholarly Context: In Indological, historical, and philosophical discourse, Videha carries profound academic prestige. It evokes both a golden era of ancient Indian political and intellectual independence and the highest metaphysical aspiration of the human soul to transcend physical embodiment.
