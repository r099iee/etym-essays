CLOVIS is a two-element Germanic compound; the two elements have separate chains from separate Indo-European roots. CHAIN 1 — THE FIRST ELEMENT (Hlōd-, "fame, famous"): Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *ḱlew- "to hear" (3rd millennium BCE; also the root of Greek kléos "glory", Vedic Sanskrit śrávas "fame", Latin cluēre "to be renowned", English loud). Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *hlūdaz "loud; famous, renowned" (reconstructed; literally "that about which much is heard"; cf. Old English hlūd "loud, famous"). Stage 3 — Frankish *Chlōd(o)wīg "famous in battle" — first element *hlōd- "fame, famous" (6th century CE; the name of Clovis I, founder of the Merovingian dynasty). CHAIN 2 — THE SECOND ELEMENT (-wig, "battle, war"): Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *weyk- "to conquer, overcome, fight" (3rd millennium BCE; also the root of Latin vincere "to conquer" and English victory, victor, invincible). Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *wīgą / *wīgaz "battle, war, fighter" (reconstructed; cf. Old Norse víg "battle", Old English wīg "war"). Stage 3 — Frankish *Chlōd(o)wīg "famous in battle" — second element *-wīg "battle" (6th century CE). COMPOUND PATH: Stage 1 — Frankish *Chlōdowīg / Chlodovech "famous in battle" (6th century CE; the name of Clovis I). Stage 2 — Latinized Chlodovechus / Clodovicus (6th century onward in Gallo-Roman and ecclesiastical sources; Gregory of Tours writes Chlodovechus). Stage 3 — Old French Clovis (attested in French historiography; the form shows loss of internal -d- and reduction of the -ove- group). Stage 4 — English CLOVIS (modern English historical spelling). FINAL NODE. NOTE ON SISTER FORMS: the same Germanic name, carried through different channels, produced the French royal Louis (via Chlodovech → *Chlodowich → Clodovicus → Louis) and the German Ludwig — each a later refraction of the same "famous in battle" compound.
