PIE *ker- "to burn, heat" (reconstructed, c. 4500–3000 BCE; per Pokorny — debated, see note) → Proto-Germanic *herþaz "hearth, fire-place" (c. 500 BCE–200 CE; reconstructed) → Old English heorþ "hearth, fireplace floor, fireside" (attested 8th–11th c.) → Middle English herth, herthe "hearth" (12th–15th c.) → Modern English HEARTH (16th c. onward) Note on the debated stage: the link of Proto-Germanic *herþaz to PIE *ker- "heat, burn" is traditional (Pokorny, Indogermanisches Etymologisches Wörterbuch), supported by the root's sense "glow, burn" and by related Germanic fire-words such as Gothic haúri "coal". But secure extra-Germanic cognates are few, and some scholars treat *herþaz as an isolated Germanic etymon whose deeper ancestry cannot be safely reconstructed; the PIE root should therefore be read as a probable but not fully certain reconstruction.
