Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*an- + *mey-
"on, in" + "to change, exchange, go astray"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*ana + *missaz
"on, in" + "divergent, failed, erroneous"
(*ana "on" + *missaz "diverging/lost")
↓
Old Norse / Old English (c. 9th-10th c.)
á mis / on mis-
"in error, wrongly, in a missed manner"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
a mis / amisse
"in error, off the mark, improperly, astray"
↓
Modern English (c. 1550)
amiss
"wrong, faulty, out of proper order (pred. adj. / adv.)"
Note: Fused from prepositional proclitic *a-* (< OE *on*) + noun/adverb *mis*, heavily reinforced by Scandinavian *á mis*.
