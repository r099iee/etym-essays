shoptalk
"informal vocational conversation among colleagues"

Note: The compound shoptalk was nominalized in 19th-century American English from the British verbal idiom to talk shop (first recorded in the 1850s). In this idiom, shop metaphorically stood for one's business, trade, or profession. The two root elements are purely Germanic: shop derives from the Proto-Germanic root for a shed/booth, and talk is an East Midland frequentative formation from the root of tell and tale.
