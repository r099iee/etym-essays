• Trippest: Similar because it is the same archaic second-person grammar applied to the nearest synonym, to trip; differs because trippest stresses the light caught-foot moment itself, while stumblest stresses the heavier lurch and near-fall that follows.
  • Falterest: Similar because both describe broken rhythm under a thou subject, in step or speech; differs because falterest stresses hesitation and loss of confidence, while stumblest stresses the physical or moral trip.
  • Staggerest: Similar because both name lurching, unsteady motion addressed to thou; differs because staggerest suggests reeling sway, as with drink or a blow, while stumblest suggests a checked trip over an obstacle.
  • Totterest: Similar because both picture unsteadiness on one's feet; differs because totterest is the trembling sway of the weak or aged, while stumblest is the sudden catch and pitch of the tripped.
  • Slippest: Similar because both are archaic thou-forms for losing one's footing; differs because slippest is the slide on smooth ground, while stumblest is the catch against raised ground.
  • Errest: Similar because in scripture to stumble is to err, and both take the -est ending; differs because errest names the moral or factual wrong itself, while stumblest names it through the body-image of tripping.
  • Blunderest: Similar because both can describe clumsy graceless failing; differs because blunderest stresses the foolish mistake and confusion, while stumblest stresses the interrupted stride.
  • Hobblest: Similar because both describe impaired gait in old diction; differs because hobblest is the ongoing limp of the lame, while stumblest is the single accidental trip.
