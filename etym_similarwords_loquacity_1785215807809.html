• Garrulity: Similar because both name excessive talking; differs because garrulity stresses rambling, trivial, tedious chatter that wearies the hearer, while loquacity stresses sheer volume and fluency of speech and may be neutral or even admirable in its energy.
  • Volubility: Similar because both describe easy, rapid, fluent speech; differs because volubility is a quality of delivery — smoothness and quickness — rather than quantity, and is often a compliment, while loquacity is a disposition to talk a great deal and is more often mildly critical.
  • Verbosity: Similar because both involve an excess of words; differs because verbosity names the use of too many words, chiefly in writing or formal speech, whereas loquacity is a personal quality of the talker and not limited to writing.
  • Prolixity: Similar because both denote wordiness; differs because prolixity emphasizes drawn-out, tedious length and extension, especially in formal written works, while loquacity points to abundant spoken fluency regardless of length.
  • Wordiness: Similar because both mean an over-abundance of words; differs because wordiness is the plain, everyday, neutral term for using more words than needed, while loquacity is its formal literary cousin tied to the act of speaking.
  • Talkativeness: Similar because both mean the habit of talking a lot; differs because talkativeness is the ordinary, largely neutral everyday word, while loquacity is the learned, stylistic variant with a hint of censure or irony.
  • Chatter: Similar because both denote plentiful talk; differs because chatter is light, casual, inconsequential small talk, whereas loquacity can be earnest, sustained, and substantial speech, not mere triviality.
  • Prattle: Similar because both suggest a flow of words; differs because prattle is childish or foolish babbling, while loquacity implies fluent, articulate talk that merely overflows.
  • Eloquence: Similar because both involve fluent, copious speech; differs because eloquence is a compliment praising persuasive, graceful, effective expression, while loquacity is at best neutral and at worst critical, and carries no guarantee of persuasiveness or grace.
  • Logorrhea: Similar because both denote excessive speech; differs because logorrhea is a clinical or jocular term for uncontrollable, pathological word-flow, often a symptom of disorder, whereas loquacity is an ordinary human trait.
