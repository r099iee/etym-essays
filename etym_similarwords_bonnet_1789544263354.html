• Cap: Similar because both refer to soft, close-fitting headgear generally lacking a wide circular brim; differs because a cap typically features a front visor or peak (e.g., a baseball cap or flat cap) and is worn predominantly by men and athletes, whereas a bonnet historically tied under the chin or refers to specific Scottish woollen headwear and automotive hoods.
  • Hat: Similar because both denote protective and decorative headcoverings; differs because a hat generally possesses a structured crown and a continuous brim encircling the entire circumference (e.g., a fedora, bowler, or top hat), whereas a bonnet frames the face without a rear brim.
  • Hood: Similar because both cover the head and can protect against the elements; differs because a hood is typically attached directly to a cloak, jacket, or sweatshirt covering the entire head and neck, whereas a bonnet is an independent headpiece. In British English, bonnet is the car engine cover, while in American English that same panel is called the hood.
  • Tam o' Shanter: Similar because both designate traditional Scottish woollen flat hats; differs because a Tam o' Shanter is a specific round, floppy Scottish bonnet with a pompom (toorie) in the center, named after Robert Burns's poem, whereas bonnet is the broader Scottish generic term.
  • Glengarry: Similar because both are traditional Scottish highland military and civilian headwear; differs because a Glengarry is a specific boat-shaped, creased woollen cap with trailing ribbons at the back.
  • Balmoral: Similar because both are traditional Scottish bonnets; differs because the Balmoral is a broad, flat-crowned bonnet with a diced or plain band, whereas bonnet is the overarching generic class.
