PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed)
*dʰerebʰ-
"to curdle, thicken, make turbid"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*drab- / *drablijaną
"dregs, thick liquid; to slobber"
↓
Old English (c. 1000)
dreflian
"to slaver, drool, run at the nose"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
drivelen / drevelen
"to drool; to babble foolishly"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
drivel
"to speak nonsense, play the fool"
↓
Modern English (19th c.–present)
drivel
"foolish nonsense; to babble"
Note: Proto-Germanic *drablijaną is an iterative/frequentative formation related to *drab- (dregs, sediment, whence English draff and Old Norse draf). Indo-Europeanists connect this to PIE *dʰerebʰ- (to curdle, become thick or murky). The semantic path travelled from thick, murky liquid to physical drool/mucus, and finally to muddy, turbid, nonsensical speech.
