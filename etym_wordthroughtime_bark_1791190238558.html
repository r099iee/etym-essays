- c.1300 — borrowed tree-skin: Middle English bark from Old Norse börkr replaces native rind for woody covering; neutral forestry perception.
- 14th–16th century — craft material: bark for tannin, roofing, baskets, and writing; perception as useful harvest, cork and cinnamon trades grow.
- 16th century — verb to bark: to strip bark, then to graze skin; perception shifts to wounding, girdling kills tree as scraping wounds knee.
- 19th century — industrial and colonial botany: bark-bed heating, bark beetle pests, quinine and rubber bark; scientific-economic perception, empire botany.
- Contemporary — ecological armour: bark as fire shield, habitat, carbon store, and sustainable textile; positive conservation perception plus homonym confusion with dog bark kept apart by context.
