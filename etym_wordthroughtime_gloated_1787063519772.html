- 14th century: "to stare, gaze" (Middle English glouten) - a rude, fixed, leering look, with no moral weight of its own.
- 15th–17th centuries: "to gaze at greedily or with desire" - the satisfied stare of covetousness; "gloat over" is still about the eyes.
- 18th century: "to dwell on with self-satisfaction" - the moral turn; satisfaction begins to attach to another's loss or one's own victory.
- 19th century: modern sense fixed - "to savour another's misfortune with smug, spiteful relish"; the word hardens into a negative judgement of character.
- Present: the everyday English word for schadenfreude; standard in politics, sport, and gossip - "the press gloated," "he gloated over his rival's defeat."
