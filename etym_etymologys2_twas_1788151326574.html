Note: The verb “was” comes from the Indo-European root that meant “to dwell, remain” (*h₂wes-), which supplied the past tense of “to be” across the Germanic and Indo-European family; the pronoun “it” descends from the deictic *ḱó- via Germanic *hit. The two elements fused into the single written contraction ’twas in the sixteenth century, when the unstressed vowel of it was regularly elided before a following vowel-initial or h-initial word.
