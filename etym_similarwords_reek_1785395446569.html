• Stench: Similar because both mean a strong unpleasant smell; differs because stench refers only to odour, while reek also carries the old sense of thick smoke or vapour and is slightly more literary.
  • Stink: Similar because both are strong foul smells; differs because stink is cruder, more colloquial, and bluntly physical, while reek is more sensory-literary and extends to smoke and to figurative corruption.
  • Smoke: Similar because reek historically means thick smoke and the words overlap in burning scenes; differs because smoke is a neutral technical word, while reek adds foulness, density, and moral charge.
  • Fumes: Similar because both are pungent gases or vapours; differs because fumes suggests irritant or chemical gases from a process, while reek is thicker, more pervading, and more primal.
  • Vapour: Similar because both name visible gas or steam; differs because vapour is neutral and scientific, without the foulness or the literary menace of reek.
  • Steam: Similar because both are hot rising vapour; differs because steam is heat without foulness and is physically specific, while reek is about blinding density and bad smell.
  • Odour: Similar because both concern smell; differs because odour is neutral and general, requiring a qualifier, while reek is inherently strong, unpleasant, and overwhelming.
  • Whiff: Similar because both name a smell; differs because a whiff is faint and passing, while reek is a thick, pervading cloud that cannot be escaped.
  • Smack: Similar because "to reek of" and "to smack of" are both figurative; differs because smack of suggests a trace or suggestion (smacks of insincerity), while reek of implies a saturated, unmistakable, overwhelming quality.
  • Effluvium: Similar because both are unpleasant emissions; differs because effluvium is a learned, technical term for a discharge, while reek is a strong, everyday sensory word uniting smoke and stink.
