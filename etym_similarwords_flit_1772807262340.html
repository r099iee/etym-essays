• Flee: Similar because both mean leaving a place, often in a hurry; differs because flee stresses flight from danger or pursuit and lacks the light, secretive "pack-up-and-vanish" colour of "do a flit".
  • Abscond: Similar because both can mean departing secretly; differs because abscond is formal and legal (especially with funds or bail), without the breezy British idiom and the domestic rent-dodging flavour of flit.
  • Decamp: Similar because both mean moving away suddenly from a place; differs because decamp is more bookish and military, with none of flit's connotation of lightness or of a specific vanishing act.
  • Bolt: Similar because both denote sudden rapid departure; differs because bolt suggests a panicked, headlong dash (often of an animal startled into flight), not a quiet, calculated vanishing.
  • Scarper: Similar because both are British informal terms for running away; differs because scarper is slangier and implies hurried flight after trouble, without the light-motion sense or the debt-dodging idiom.
  • Dart: Similar because both mean moving very quickly; differs because dart is a single sharp movement in a direction, while flit suggests repeated light fluttering or hovering motion.
  • Flutter: Similar because both describe light airborne motion; differs because flutter emphasizes trembling, wavering wings rather than swift darting travel from place to place.
  • Migrate: Similar because both can mean moving from place to place; differs because migrate implies regular, seasonal movement of populations and is neutral and formal, with no secrecy, lightness or evasion.
