• Cutting down: Similar because both denote removing standing trees; differs because “cutting down” is the general everyday phrase for any cutting at the base, while felling is the precise, technical term of forestry.
  • Chopping: Similar because both involve striking with a blade; differs because chopping is the physical act of hacking with an axe (any object), while felling names the completed operation of bringing a tree or being down.
  • Hewing: Similar because both involve cutting with an axe; differs because hewing means cutting or shaping (often trimming a log square), while felling means bringing down whole.
  • Logging: Similar because both describe the timber industry's work; differs because logging is the whole industry and its vocabulary (harvesting, hauling, milling), while felling is specifically the act of cutting trees down.
  • Lumbering: Similar because both are timber-trade terms; differs because lumbering is North American industry jargon for the whole business, while felling names the single operation.
  • Timbering: Similar because both name the work of getting timber; differs because timbering can include felling, hauling and sawing, while felling stops at the point the tree hits the ground.
  • Clearing: Similar because both remove trees; differs because clearing means removing all growth from an area (the result is a clearing), while felling is about the individual trees, not the land.
  • Toppling: Similar because both bring something tall down; differs because toppling suggests losing balance and falling over (structures, statues), while felling is deliberate cutting or striking down.
  • Uprooting: Similar because both remove standing trees; differs because uprooting pulls out the roots (by storm or machine), while felling cuts the trunk and leaves the stump.
  • Knocking down / striking down: Similar because both mean causing a person or thing to fall by a blow; differs because these are general and physical, while felling is the dignified, literary word for the same act and carries the tree-image.
  • Laying low / prostrating: Similar because both mean bringing down (often figuratively, of illness or grief); differs because these are more abstract and passive, while felling implies an active blow or cut.
  • Overthrowing / defeating (figurative): Similar because both mean ruining or bringing down a person, regime or reputation; differs because overthrow and defeat are political and strategic, while felling is sudden, physical and often mortal in its image.
