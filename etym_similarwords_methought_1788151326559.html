• I thought: similar because it is the modern content-equivalent (“methought that…” ≈ “I thought that…”); differs because it is an ordinary active construction with subject agreement, lacking the impersonal, perception-from-outside nuance.
  • It seemed to me: similar in meaning — both express an appearance rather than an assertion; differs because the periphrasis is neutral modern prose while methought is a fused poetic fossil.
  • Methinks: the same construction in the present tense; differs in tense only (“it seems to me now” vs. “it seemed to me then”).
  • Meseems / meseemed: the exact parallel fossil built on “seem”; differs in that it is even rarer and built on appear/see rather than think.
  • I fancied: similar in reporting an imaginative perception; differs because it is active, modern, and implies fancy or imagination rather than impersonal appearance.
  • I deemed: similar as a past-tense judgment; differs in being active, formal-judgmental, and implying deliberation rather than sudden appearance.
  • I ween (archaic): similar as an archaic past-tense belief; differs in origin (OE wēnan “to hope, think”) and in being even more obsolete.
  • I bethought me (archaic): similar reflexive archaism; differs in meaning “I reflected, reminded myself” rather than “it seemed to me”.
