• mumble: Similar because both mean low, indistinct speech with the mouth barely open; differs because mumbling stresses inarticulacy — words swallowed past intelligibility — whereas muttering is more intentional, half-private speech often carrying irritation or complaint.
  • murmur: Similar because both describe low, continuous speech-like sound; differs because murmuring is softer, gentler, more flowing and often pleasant (a murmuring brook), whereas muttering is more closed-mouthed, muffled, and coloured by private irritation or unease.
  • grumble: Similar because both describe low, displeased, half-suppressed speech; differs because grumbling adds open complaint and bad temper — the grumbler airs his grievance in low tones — whereas muttering is quieter, more self-contained, and not necessarily addressed to anyone.
  • whisper: Similar because both are quiet speech; differs because whispering is deliberate and articulates distinct words breathily and audibly by design, whereas muttering is more closed-mouthed, muffled, and barely intelligible.
  • drone: Similar because both are low, continuous vocal sounds; differs because droning is a monotonous, sustained, unvarying hum — often of someone speaking at length without modulation — whereas muttering is low, broken, and half-articulate.
  • splutter: Similar because both describe imperfect, suppressed speech; differs because spluttering is choked, spitting, spasmodic sound, broken into bursts, whereas muttering is a steady low mumble with no spitting.
  • stammer: Similar because both describe speech that is not fluently delivered; differs because stammering is a blockage and repetition of sounds in otherwise audible, open speech, whereas muttering is a lowering and muffling of the whole voice.
  • stutter: Similar because both describe imperfect delivery of words; differs because stuttering is an involuntary repetition of initial sounds, clearly audible, whereas muttering is deliberately or habitually low and indistinct.
  • growl: Similar because both are low, throaty, and often hostile vocal sounds; differs because growling is deep and guttural, produced in the throat with vibration, whereas muttering is labial and mumbling, produced behind nearly closed lips.
  • mutterings (noun): Similar because both name low, half-heard speech; differs because the plural noun mutterings specifically denotes accumulated, circulating complaints within a group — political mutterings — whereas the verb mutters describes the act of speaking low itself.
