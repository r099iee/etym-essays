• Sheath: Similar because both are protective coverings for a blade; differs because a sheath is the older, softer, more general word for any close-fitting blade-cover, while a scabbard names the firm, structured, usually ornate case of a sword, dagger, or bayonet.
  • Holster: Similar because both are carrying-cases for a weapon worn on the body; differs because a holster holds a pistol or revolver at the belt or thigh, while a scabbard holds an edged, bladed weapon.
  • Case / Casing: Similar because both denote a protective container; differs because 'case' is a fully generic word for any container or covering, while 'scabbard' is specialised to blades and implies rigidity, fittings, and often ornament.
  • Quiver: Similar because both are purpose-built containers for carrying a weapon at one's side or back; differs because a quiver holds arrows, not a blade, and is typically open or of a very different construction from a rigid scabbard.
  • Cover / Covering: Similar because both protect what they enclose; differs because a cover is soft, generic, and not fitted to a blade's shape, while a scabbard is a rigid, shaped, and structurally defined case.
  • Holder / Housing: Similar because both contain and carry an object; differs because these are colourless, mechanical terms without the martial, historical, and ornamental associations of scabbard.
  • Casket / Coffer (weak resemblance): Similar because both are rigid, often decorated containers for something valuable; differs because a casket or coffer holds treasure or relics rather than a blade, and lacks the sword-specific fittings (locket, chape, rings) of a scabbard.
