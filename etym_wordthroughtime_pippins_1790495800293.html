- Old French pepin - grape or apple seed; small, hard, neutral
- Middle English pipin / pippin (14th c.) - apple grown from seed; novelty, uncertain, hopeful
- Tudor-Stuart pippin orchards - Ribston, Golden, French pippins planted by gentry; choice, grafted, named
- 17th-18th c. colonial Newtown Pippin - Virginia export, Jefferson’s favourite; commercial, keeping, famous
- Early 19th c. slang she’s a pippin - excellent girl, horse, shot; sporting, admiring, jocular
- Victorian Ruskin sweet pippins - living culture versus bitter criticism; moral, horticultural, programmatic
- Modern heritage pippin - orchard societies rescue Cox and Ribston; nostalgic, crisp, conservationist
