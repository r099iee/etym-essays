• spelling: Similar because both relate directly to the arrangement of letters that form words; differs because spelling is the common, everyday Germanic term used in colloquial speech and primary education, whereas orthographical is a formal, Latinate/Greek academic term encompassing the broader systemic rules of graphemics, punctuation, diacritics, and script history.
  • typographical: Similar because both concern the visual appearance and mechanical presentation of written text; differs because typographical pertains to the art, design, spacing, font selection, and mechanical composition of printed type (e.g., kerning, leading, typefaces, printer's typos), whereas orthographical governs linguistic correctness and standard letter choices regardless of font or medium.
  • graphical: Similar because both involve visual markings and written symbols; differs because graphical encompasses all forms of visual representation—including pictorial drawings, digital computer graphics, diagrams, and charts—lacking the specific focus on linguistic spelling laws inherent to orthographical.
  • graphematic: Similar because both belong to the structural analysis of writing systems; differs because graphematic is a highly specialized technical term in theoretical linguistics referring to the functional analysis of graphemes as minimal distinctive units of writing (analogous to phonemes in phonology), whereas orthographical refers to normative spelling conventions and practical script standardization.
  • lexicographical: Similar because both deal with words and language standardization; differs because lexicographical specifically concerns the theory, methodology, and practice of compiling and editing dictionaries, defining meanings, and recording etymologies, whereas orthographical is strictly limited to how those words are spelled and graphically rendered.
  • grammatical: Similar because both represent normative rules governing correct language usage; differs because grammatical regulates the syntax, morphology, parts of speech, and inflectional relationships of words within clauses and sentences, whereas orthographical focuses exclusively on the correct visual spelling of the individual words themselves.
  • orthoepic: Similar because both derive from the Greek root ortho- ("correct") and deal with language correctness; differs because orthoepic relates strictly to the correct pronunciation and vocalization of words in spoken speech, whereas orthographical relates strictly to their correct spelling and inscription in written script.
  • phonetic: Similar because both analyze the relationship between speech and written symbols; differs because phonetic describes the physical, acoustic sounds of human speech and their precise notation in international phonetic alphabets, whereas orthographical describes the often historical, irregular, and conventional spelling system established by social custom or academies.
  • calligraphic: Similar because both describe the physical execution of handwriting and letterforms; differs because calligraphic evaluates the aesthetic elegance, beauty, and artistic flourish of penmanship, whereas orthographical assesses compliance with normative spelling rules regardless of whether the penmanship is beautiful or messy.
  • scribal: Similar because both are frequently applied to textual analysis of ancient manuscripts; differs because scribal pertains to the occupational habits, transmission errors, physical materials, and manual behaviors of historical copyists, whereas orthographical characterizes the abstract spelling system itself.
  • syntactical: Similar because both govern language mechanics; differs because syntactical deals with sentence structure, word order, and clause hierarchy, operating at the macro-level of grammar rather than the micro-level of individual letter spelling.
  • philological: Similar because both are central to textual criticism and historical document analysis; differs because philological denotes the broad historical study of language evolution, literary history, textual authenticity, and cultural meaning across centuries, of which orthographical analysis is merely one diagnostic tool.
