• awakened: Similar because both mean brought out of sleep or unconsciousness; differs because awakened is limited to literal or figurative waking from sleep, whereas roused extends to being stirred from rest, idleness, or apathy and implies movement toward action.
  • stirred: Similar because both describe being set in motion or emotionally moved; differs because stirred is gentler and can be positive (stirred to pity), while roused often carries urgency or abruptness and can edge into provocation or alarm.
  • aroused: Similar because both mean roused into activity or feeling and are historically near-synonyms; differs because aroused has taken on a strong sexual connotation in modern use, while roused has stayed closer to waking, excitement, anger, or action.
  • woken: Similar because both mean having come out of sleep; differs because woken is neutral and purely literal, whereas roused is usually deliberate or forceful (someone roused you) and frequently figurative (roused to anger).
  • provoked: Similar because both can mean stirred into anger or action; differs because provoked implies a specific irritating stimulus deliberately applied, whereas roused can come from an inspiring, alarming, or encouraging cause and is not always hostile.
  • incited: Similar because both mean urged or stirred into action; differs because incited is usually negative and instigatory (inciting a riot, inciting hatred) while roused can be morally neutral or even noble (roused to defend one's home).
  • goaded: Similar because both mean forced or urged into action; differs because goaded stresses persistent, irritating pressure (like a goad-stick on cattle), whereas roused stresses a sudden waking or surge of feeling.
  • animated: Similar because both mean given energy and life; differs because animated emphasises liveliness and enthusiasm in mood, while roused emphasises being called out of passivity, sometimes with agitation or alarm.
  • excited: Similar because both mean stirred into heightened feeling or activity; differs because excited is usually positive anticipation, while roused can be neutral-to-alarming and carries an older sense of being literally roused from rest.
  • wakened: Similar because both are near-literal synonyms for being brought out of sleep; differs because wakened is quieter and purely literal, while roused is more forceful, more formal, and heavily used figuratively.
