QUERIED — linear derivation chain: - (Pre-Latin): The deeper ancestry of Latin quaerere is UNKNOWN / UNCERTAIN. No securely reconstructible PIE root is established for *kʷair- (handbooks note possible connections to a root meaning "to notice, to perceive" but reject all candidates as unproven). This stage is stated as unknown rather than guessed. - Latin quaerere, "to seek, to look for, to strive after, to ask, to demand" (attested from the 3rd–2nd c. BCE, Plautus onward). The core meaning is physical seeking, which slides to verbal seeking (asking) and legal seeking (investigation, prosecution). - Latin quaere, imperative of quaerere, "ask! seek!" — used from late antiquity as a marginal annotation in medieval manuscripts to flag a passage to be looked up (the origin of the English word). The imperative form, not the infinitive, is what entered English. - English query, noun "a question, an inquiry" (1530s) — borrowed from Latin quaere as the name of the marginal question-note, hence also the question mark in early printing. - English query, verb "to ask, to call in question, to doubt" (1550s) — a back-formation/verbalization of the noun. - English queried, past tense and past participle of query (regular -ed inflection) — the form on this card, QUERIED. Modern derivatives: query string, SQL query, query letter, questionnaire (a separate but related French-based loan).
