PIE *leg- “to gather, collect, pick out” (c. 4000 BCE; restricted to Western Indo-European — Greek, Italic, Celtic, Germanic — with no attested Indo-Iranian reflex) → Proto-Italic *legō “to gather, collect” (reconstructed) → Latin legere “to gather, choose, read” (attested from the 3rd c. BCE; the Romans “picked out” letters in order to read) → Latin colligere “to gather together, collect” (com- “with, together” + legere; attested from the 2nd c. BCE; also the ancestor of collect and college) → Vulgar Latin *colligere, with vowel reduction of the prefix (the source of the Romance cuillir family) → Old French cuillir / collir “to gather, pluck, select” (10th–12th c.) → Middle English cullen / collen “to pick out, choose” (c. 1300) → Early Modern English cull “to select from a group; to remove the surplus” (16th c.) → English CULLED “selected and gathered with discrimination” — past participle used adjectivally (modern sense). Debated / nuanced stages: the Germanic branch (Old Norse lesa, Old English lesan “to gather, to read”) is grouped under *leg- in older handbooks, but many modern etymologists (following the zero grade) derive it from a distinct base *les- “to gather”; the two roots are close and possibly related, but the equation is not certain. The Greek reflex legein “to pick out, count, speak” and its noun logos belong to the same *leg-, but they lie outside the direct Italic line that produced English cull.
