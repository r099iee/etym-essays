- Clear liquids first (17th c.) - entering English, limpid is used of water, streams and springs that hold no sediment, a purely physical clarity with no moral colour
- The gaze turns inward (18th-19th c.) - transferred to the eye and the look (a limpid gaze), the word acquires a moral glow: innocence, candour, a soul without concealment
- The style of clear water (19th c.) - the word migrates from water to prose and speech - limpid prose, a limpid style - becoming the critic's term for writing that flows without obstruction
- Contemporary - rare and formal, kept for descriptive and literary registers; its public sense has barely shifted, but it now reads as a decorative, faintly archaic word of luminous serenity, at home in romance and fairy-tale landscapes
