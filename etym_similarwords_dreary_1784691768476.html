• Gloomy: Similar because both describe a depressing state; differs because gloomy emphasises darkness and shadow where light is absent, while dreary stresses dullness and grey monotony.
  • Bleak: Similar because both describe a cheerless prospect; differs because bleak suggests exposure, cold and barrenness, while dreary is a weary, colourless persistence rather than a bare emptiness.
  • Dismal: Similar because both mean gloomy and discouraging; differs because dismal carries a superstitious flavour of ill omen (from medieval Latin dies mali, "evil days"), whereas dreary is merely dull and monotonous.
  • Desolate: Similar because both describe a depressing place; differs because desolate emphasises emptiness and abandonment, while dreary can describe even an ordinary grey day.
  • Cheerless: Similar because both mean lacking comfort and joy; differs because cheerless is a more neutral, direct negative (no cheer), while dreary adds a specific grey, wearying, monotonous colour.
  • Drab: Similar because both suggest colourless dullness; differs because drab describes the visual flatness of a thing (drab colours, drab clothes), while dreary is chiefly atmospheric and emotional.
  • Somber (Sombre): Similar because both are dark and grave; differs because sombre implies seriousness and gravity, often solemn rather than wearying, while dreary implies dullness and monotony.
  • Morose: Similar because both describe a gloomy disposition; differs because morose names a person's sour, sullen temper, while dreary more often describes weather, places and the tone of experience.
  • Depressing: Similar because both make the spirits sink; differs because depressing is a general, active word (it depresses), while dreary is a settled, passive quality of greyness.
  • Joyless: Similar because both mean without happiness; differs because joyless is a stark negative of a single quality, while dreary gathers dullness, bleakness and monotony together.
  • Funereal: Similar because both suggest a heavy, mournful atmosphere; differs because funereal explicitly evokes death and mourning, far darker than the merely wearying grey of dreary.
  • Drear (poetic): Similar because it means exactly dreary; differs because drear is the clipped one-syllable poetic form of the word, used for verse and archaizing effect rather than ordinary speech.
