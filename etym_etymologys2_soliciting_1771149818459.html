Root 1: sollus "whole, entire"
1. Proto-Indo-European *solh2- / *solh2wos "whole, sound, healthy" (reconstructed; attested cognates: Greek holos "whole," Sanskrit sarva- "all, whole"; the exact vocalism is debated — some reconstruct *solh2-wo-s — and this uncertainty is noted rather than hidden).
2. Latin sollus "whole, entire, intact" (attested chiefly in early compounds such as solliferreus "made entirely of iron" and sollistimus "most propitious").
3. Latin sollicitus "thoroughly stirred, agitated, anxious" — compound of sollus "whole" + citus "moved, roused" (2nd-1st c. BCE; cf. Plautus, Cicero).
4. Latin sollicitare "to disturb, agitate, stir up, vex, excite" (Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE; e.g., sollicitare animos "to stir up minds").
5. Old French solliciter "to stir, disturb, urge" (12th-13th c.).
6. Middle English soliciten "to disturb; to urge, entreat, petition" (14th c.).
7. English solicit "to ask earnestly for; to seek to obtain; to approach with a request or offer" (16th c. onward).
8. SOLICITING — present participle and gerund of solicit (17th c. onward).

Root 2: citus "moved, roused" (from ciere)
1. Proto-Indo-European *kei- / *k(e)ih1- "to set in motion, rouse" (reconstructed; attested cognate: Greek kinein "to move").
2. Latin ciere "to set in motion, rouse, stir, summon" (attested; also the base of citare "to summon, arouse," source of English "cite" and "excite").
3. Latin citus "moved, roused; quick, swift" — past participle of ciere; appears as the second element of sollicitus "thoroughly stirred."
4. Latin sollicitus -> sollicitare -> Old French solliciter -> English solicit -> SOLICITING.

Debate note: the reconstruction of the PIE "whole" root and its relationship to Sanskrit sarva- is debated among Indo-Europeanists; the connection of citus to the same root as English "cite" is standard. The Latin spelling sollicit- loses one l in English (solicit-), a change fixed in Middle French and early Modern English orthography.
