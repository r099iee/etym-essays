PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*trep-
"to turn, bend, twist"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th–5th c. BCE)
τρόπος (trópos)
"a turn, direction, manner, musical mode, trope"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
tropus
"metaphor, figure of speech, melody"
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 6th–8th c. CE)
*tropāre
"to compose in tropes, invent poetry, find melodies, find"
Note: Primary consensus derivation (Gaston Paris, Meyer-Lübke, FEW, TLF). An older 19th-century hypothesis deriving it from Frankish *trōpōn ("to encounter") is now discounted.
↓
Old French (c. 11th–12th c.)
re- + trover
"to find again, discover anew, recover"
Note: Prefixed with Latin re- ("again, back"). Cognate with Old Occitan trobar ("to compose poetry, find"), whence 'troubadour'.
↓
Middle French (c. 14th–15th c.)
retrouver (past part. retrouvé)
"found again, regained, recognized, recovered"
↓
Modern French / English literary adoption (1927–present)
RETROUVÉ
