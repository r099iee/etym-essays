• artifacts: Similar because both refer to physical, tangible objects manufactured or used by human beings in cultural settings; differs because artifacts focuses specifically on historical, archaeological, or anthropological antiquity and material workmanship, whereas realia emphasizes the educational, pedagogical, or communicative utility of modern or traditional everyday items brought into instructional environments.
  • manipulatives: Similar because both denote physical objects handled directly by students to facilitate learning; differs because manipulatives is almost exclusively employed in early childhood and mathematics pedagogy (e.g., counting blocks, geometric shapes, Cuisenaire rods) to teach abstract conceptual reasoning, whereas realia refers to authentic cultural items (e.g., foreign newspapers, train schedules, currency) used to contextualize language and culture.
  • specimens: Similar because both denote physical, tangible items examined for study rather than abstract textual descriptions; differs because specimens specifically denotes individual biological, geological, or botanical samples collected for scientific analysis, classification, and taxidermy, while realia covers cultural, linguistic, and everyday human objects.
  • ephemera: Similar because both frequently consist of everyday paper and material items such as bus tickets, pamphlets, menus, and receipts; differs because ephemera highlights the transient, short-lived, disposable nature of the printed materials collected primarily by hobbyists and historical archivists, whereas realia highlights their active didactic role in teaching or cultural representation.
  • props: Similar because both are physical objects utilized within an artificial setting to evoke a realistic environment; differs because props belongs to theatrical, cinematic, and dramatic production where items are handled by actors to advance a narrative illusion, whereas realia belongs to educational pedagogy, translatology, and library classification.
  • culturemes: Similar because both designate culture-specific elements that resist facile intercultural translation; differs because culturemes is an abstract semiotic term covering immaterial customs, gestures, linguistic taboos, and behavioral codes alongside physical items, whereas realia is overwhelmingly anchored in tangible physical entities and specific lexical terms.
  • facsimiles: Similar because both provide students or researchers with direct access to materials beyond standard printed textbooks; differs because a facsimile is an exact photographic or physical replica of an original rare book, manuscript, or map, whereas realia consists of genuine, authentic, three-dimensional real-world articles rather than photocopied duplicates.
  • sensory aids: Similar because both serve as pedagogical tools designed to stimulate sensory perception beyond purely auditory or textual instruction; differs because sensory aids is a broad instructional category that includes digital audio-visual projections, tactile relief maps, and mechanical models, whereas realia refers to authentic everyday real-world objects themselves.
  • paraphernalia: Similar because both encompass diverse collections of physical articles associated with a specific activity, trade, or cultural practice; differs because paraphernalia often carries a colloquial or chaotic connotation of miscellaneous gear, equipment, or personal belongings, whereas realia is a formal academic and bibliographical descriptor.
  • tangibilia: Similar because both etymologically emphasize physical, touchable reality over intangible discourse; differs because tangibilia is an obscure legal and metaphysical term referring broadly to anything capable of being touched, whereas realia possesses codified definitions in library science, second-language acquisition, and classical philology.
