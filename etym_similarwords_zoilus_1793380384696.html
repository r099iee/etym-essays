• Thersites: Similar because both are ancient Greek archetypes of vulgar, insolent, and malicious fault-finding directed against heroic greatness; differs because Thersites is a comic, physically deformed, and cowardly soldier in Homer's Iliad who vulgarly rails against military commanders like Agamemnon, whereas Zoilus is an educated, pedantic rhetorician and literary scholar who systematically attacks the poetry of Homer itself.
  • Momus: Similar because both personify ungenerous, relentless fault-finding and ridicule; differs because Momus is a Greek mythological deity of mockery, censure, and satire who was expelled from Mount Olympus for criticizing the creations of the gods, whereas Zoilus was a historical human grammarian whose infamy derives from specific literary polemics.
  • Caviler (Caviller): Similar because both engage in petty, irritating, and trivial objections; differs because a caviler is an everyday term for anyone raising frivolous complaints in legal, domestic, or political debates, whereas Zoilus carries profound literary and historical weight, specifically implying malicious, envious criticism of artistic genius.
  • Carper: Similar because both describe someone who peevishly and continually finds fault; differs because carper is a common, informal noun describing a querulous disposition, while Zoilus is a formal classical antonomasia denoting an ambitious, bad-faith critic seeking personal fame by attempting to tear down acknowledged masterpieces.
  • Detractor: Similar because both actively work to diminish, subtract from, and belittle the legitimate merit and reputation of others; differs because a detractor often operates quietly through gossip, slander, or backbiting, whereas Zoilus mounts a noisy, pseudo-scholarly public offensive against creative greatness.
  • Hypercritic: Similar because both subject texts and works of art to exaggerated, mercilessly severe criticism; differs because a hypercritic may be motivated by an excessively rigid, genuine devotion to impossible objective standards, whereas Zoilus is motivated by sour envy, personal malice, and the desire to degrade greatness.
  • Smelfungus: Similar because both are literary archetypes of sour, miserable, and ungenerous critics; differs because Smelfungus (coined by Laurence Sterne in A Sentimental Journey to satirize Tobias Smollett) is a perpetually dissatisfied traveler who complains about every road, inn, and meal, while Zoilus focuses his malice exclusively on artistic, literary, and poetic creation.
