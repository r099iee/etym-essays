• New Guinea: Similar because both terms designate the vast southwestern Pacific island and its Indigenous peoples; differs because New Guinea (coined in 1545 by Spanish navigator Ortiz de Retes due to the perceived physical resemblance of the locals to the inhabitants of African Guinea) is primarily a physical geographic label for the island landmass, whereas Papua carries specific administrative, national, and historical connotations (as in Papua New Guinea or the Indonesian provinces of Tanah Papua).
  • Melanesia: Similar because both refer to the cultural and geographic realm of dark-skinned Indigenous Pacific islanders; differs because Melanesia is a broader geographic macro-region that includes not only New Guinea/Papua, but also the Solomon Islands, Vanuatu, New Caledonia, and Fiji.
  • Irian: Similar because Irian was the official Indonesian state name applied to western Papua (as Irian Barat and Irian Jaya) from 1963 until 2001; differs because Irian is an Indonesian acronym (historically interpreted as Ikut Republik Indonesia Anti-Nederland) and an indigenous Biak word meaning "hot climate rising from the sea," which was replaced in 2001 by the restored name Papua to satisfy local indigenous demands.
  • Australasia: Similar because both describe landmasses situated in the southwestern Pacific/Oceanic sphere; differs because Australasia is an overarching continental region encompassing Australia, New Zealand, and New Guinea, dominated culturally and politically by the Australian landmass.
  • Polynesia: Similar because both represent major cultural subregions of the Pacific Ocean; differs because Polynesia (meaning "many islands") encompasses the eastern Pacific triangle bounded by Hawaii, New Zealand (Aotearoa), and Easter Island (Rapa Nui), characterized by Austronesian-speaking seafaring populations with hereditary chieftainships, sharply distinct from the Papuan languages and clan-based institutions of Papua.
  • Micronesia: Similar because both are adjacent island zones in the western Pacific; differs because Micronesia ("small islands") designates the archipelagos north of the equator (such as the Carolines, Marshalls, and Marianas), consisting of small coral atolls and volcanic islands inhabited predominantly by Austronesian peoples.
  • Oceania: Similar because both denote Pacific geographic divisions; differs because Oceania is the vast continental macro-region encompassing Australia, Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia combined.
