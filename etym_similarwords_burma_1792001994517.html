• Myanmar: Similar because both are names for the exact same sovereign state in Southeast Asia; differs because Myanmar is the formal, literary endonym officially adopted by the government since 1989 and recognized by the United Nations, whereas Burma is the traditional English name derived from colloquial Bama, carrying colonial history and long favored by democratic opposition movements.
  • Siam: Similar because both were powerful, rival Theravada Buddhist kingdoms in mainland Southeast Asia; differs because Siam (now Thailand) successfully preserved its national independence through diplomatic maneuvering, while Burma was conquered and colonized by the British Empire.
  • Indochina: Similar because both designate geographic regions in mainland Southeast Asia heavily influenced by Indian and Chinese cultural crosscurrents; differs because Indochina typically refers to the former French colonial territories of Vietnam, Laos, and Cambodia, whereas Burma was part of the British imperial sphere.
  • Malaya: Similar because both were British colonial dependencies in Southeast Asia; differs because Malaya was centered on the Malay Peninsula with a Muslim-majority population and tin/rubber plantation economy, whereas Burma was an ancient Buddhist agrarian empire initially governed from Calcutta.
  • Bamar: Similar because both share the exact same ethnolinguistic root; differs because Bamar specifically designates the majority ethnic group (comprising roughly 68% of the population), whereas Burma designates the entire multi-ethnic sovereign nation.
  • Ayeyarwady: Similar because both are central to the cultural and geographic identity of the country; differs because Ayeyarwady (the Irrawaddy) is the primary river basin and economic lifeline of Burma, rather than the nation as a whole.
  • Raj: Similar because both relate directly to nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century British imperial governance; differs because the Raj refers to the entire British imperial regime across South Asia, under which Burma was governed as a subordinate province until 1937.
