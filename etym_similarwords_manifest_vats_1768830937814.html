• Evident: Similar because both mean plainly visible or obvious to the senses or mind; differs because evident focuses on what is clear from signs or circumstances, while manifest emphasizes something so open it needs no proof — almost physically graspable.
  • Obvious: Similar because both mean easily perceived or understood; differs because obvious is the everyday, neutral word for what is clear, while manifest is formal and legalistic, carrying the idea of evidence seized in the act.
  • Apparent: Similar because both describe what is visible or plainly seen; differs because apparent can also mean "seeming" (possibly deceptive), while manifest implies genuine, undeniable openness.
  • Patent: Similar because both mean plainly evident, and both have legal registers (letters patent, patent defect); differs because patent is more technical and now more often a noun for an exclusive right, while manifest stays primarily an adjective of perception.
  • Self-evident: Similar because both name truths that require no demonstration; differs because self-evident is a philosophical term for axioms, while manifest is broader, covering any openly visible thing or flaw.
  • Palpable: Similar because both derive from the image of touching — manifest is "seized by the hand," palpable is "touchable"; differs because palpable insists on the physical, touchable quality, while manifest can be purely intellectual.
  • Conspicuous: Similar because both mean attracting attention by being clearly visible; differs because conspicuous implies standing out from a background (conspicuous consumption), while manifest is about the certainty of perception, not prominence.
  • Transparent: Similar because both mean "allowing full seeing-through" — an open quality; differs because transparent has modern figurative uses (transparent government) and literal optical ones (transparent glass), while manifest has no literal sense.
  • Overt: Similar because both mean open and not hidden; differs because overt is usually opposed to covert (overt vs. covert operations), while manifest is opposed to doubtful or hidden on the plane of evidence.
  • Container: Similar to vat in the shared core meaning of a receptacle for holding things; differs because container is a broad, modern, all-purpose term, while vat is specifically a large open vessel for processing liquids.
  • Tank: Similar because both are large containers for holding liquids; differs because tank is usually closed, often metallic, and associated with vehicles, fuel, or pressure, while vat is open-topped and associated with brewing, dyeing, and processing.
  • Cask / Barrel: Similar because both are large containers for liquids, especially alcoholic ones; differs because a cask or barrel is a curved, closed, staved vessel rolled on its side, while a vat is an open tub that need not be sealed.
  • Tub: Similar because both are open vessels for holding liquids; differs because tub is smaller and domestic (bath-tub, washing-tub), while vat implies industrial scale.
  • Cauldron: Similar because both are large vessels for heating or mixing contents; differs because cauldron is associated with witches' magic and fires beneath, while vat is associated with industrial processing and fermentation.
  • Basin: Similar because both are open receptacles for liquid; differs because basin is usually shallow, wide, and often natural (a river basin) or domestic (a washbasin), while vat is deep and industrial.
