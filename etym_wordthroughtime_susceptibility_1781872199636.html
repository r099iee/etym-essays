- Receptivity in early English - Since the 17th century susceptibility has meant the capacity or liability to be affected by impressions, influence, or agency, a neutral philosophical and psychological term.
- Medical sense - In the 18th-19th centuries it becomes clinical: susceptibility to disease, a state of openness to harm.
- Paterian virtue and modern uses - In aesthetic criticism (Pater) susceptibility is re-valued as a positive, educable power — the trained openness of the cultivated mind — while in modern usage it still carries both the neutral 'openness to influence' and the medical 'liability to be affected' senses.
