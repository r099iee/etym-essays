Ancient Occupational Word - Porter first means "carrier" or "gatekeeper," from Latin portare ("to carry") through Old French; for centuries the word belongs to the person, not the drink, with the "one who carries" sense dominant.
Early 18th-Century London Metonymy - Around the 1720s dark, well-hopped, aged ales become the staple of London's street and river porters; the drink is called "porter's ale," then shortened to porter, the occupational name transferring to the liquid by metonymy.
19th-Century Style Evolution - Porter spreads from London trade English across the brewing world, and later brewing history folds it into the overlapping family of stouts, so the style-name widens and partly merges with its dark cousins.
Contemporary Sense - Today porter is a distinct, revived dark beer style (roasted, brown-black, bitter, nourishing), while the carrier/gatekeeper sense still exists alongside it as a separate meaning of the same word.
