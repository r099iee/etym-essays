• Punch: Similar because both are the standard informal verbs for striking with a closed fist; differs because punch is the normal boxing term covering any fist-blow (jab, hook, uppercut), while sock is more colloquial and specifically suggests a single heavy, thudding blow with real weight.
  • Thump: Similar because both describe heavy blows with a dull impact sound; differs because thump can be a lighter or even affectionate pat delivered with the flat hand and emphasises the sound, while sock is a deliberate forceful fist-punch.
  • Wallop: Similar because both are informal verbs for a forceful beating blow; differs because wallop has a comic, boisterous flavour of sound thrashing and can mean "to defeat utterly," while sock is plainer, blunter, and focused on a single hard punch.
  • Clobber: Similar because both are slang for hitting hard; differs because clobber suggests repeated beating or battering and can also mean "to defeat decisively" or "to afflict," while sock names one decisive blow.
  • Belt: Similar because both are informal verbs for striking hard; differs because belt can mean to hit with an instrument or to move fast ("belting along"), while sock is specific to delivering a heavy blow.
  • Slug: Similar because both are boxing slang for a heavy punch; differs because slug is prizefighting jargon for a slow, powerful, swinging punch, while sock is the more general street word for a hard blow.
  • Whack: Similar because both are informal strike verbs with an echoing, percussive feel; differs because whack can be delivered with any object and has a sharper crack sound, while sock is specifically a heavy fist-punch with a duller thud.
  • Bash: Similar because both describe striking hard; differs because bash implies a violent, possibly repeated battering and can be used of smashing objects, while sock is a cleaner, single decisive blow.
  • Smack: Similar because both are abrupt strike words; differs because smack is usually open-handed or lighter and produces a sharp, stinging report, while sock is the closed fist and a heavy, dull impact.
  • Clout: Similar because both are informal words for a hard blow; differs because clout is older, can be delivered by a blow of any kind, and also means "influence," while sock is specifically a punch.
  • Biff: Similar because both are slang for a punch; differs because biff is a lighter, almost comic tap, the sort of blow in a cartoon, while sock carries genuine weight.
  • Strike: Similar because both name the action of hitting; differs because strike is the formal, neutral, all-purpose verb covering every kind of hit, while sock is informal, physical, and specifically a forceful punch.
  • Hit: Similar because both describe making forceful contact; differs because hit is the broadest neutral verb for contact of any force, while sock singles out a heavy, informal, closed-fist blow.
  • Stocking: Similar because both are knitted leg-and-foot garments; differs because stocking extends up the leg and thigh, while the garment sock covers only the foot and ankle — the two are relatives, not synonyms, in the garment sense of the word.
  • Slipper: Similar because both are light footwear; differs because a slipper is a soft indoor shoe, while the garment sock is the knitted foot-covering worn inside a shoe — the historical ancestor of the sock, since Latin soccus was a light slipper.
