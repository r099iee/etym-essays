• Bow: Similar because it is a bodily gesture of greeting or respect that lowers the performer before a superior; differs because a bow is performed by either sex from the waist with the head inclined, may be casual, and carries no gender-marking, while a curtsey is the knee-bending dip specific to women and girls.
  • Bob: Similar because it is a quick dip of the body used as a feminine greeting or courtesy; differs because a bob is a small, brisk, informal curtsey — a shallow, hasty dip — whereas a curtsey is a full, controlled, deeper bend.
  • Genuflect: Similar because it involves bending the knee in reverence or submission; differs because genuflection drops the performer onto one knee, carries religious or quasi-royal weight, and is not a gendered social greeting.
  • Kneel: Similar because it lowers the body as an act of deference or worship; differs because kneeling goes down onto both knees to the ground, implying longer, deeper submission than the curtsey's momentary dip.
  • Prostrate: Similar because it signals submission before a superior; differs because prostration lays the whole body flat on the ground and implies abject or extreme deference, far beyond the curtsey's composed bend.
  • Salaam: Similar because it is a formal gesture of greeting and respect; differs because a salaam is a low bow with the right hand placed on the forehead — an Islamic greeting shared by men and women — not a knee-bend.
  • Kowtow: Similar because it is a ritual gesture of deep respect and submission; differs because kowtow involves kneeling and touching the forehead to the ground repeatedly before a superior, a far more extreme and now archaic Chinese obeisance.
  • Dip: Similar because it names a lowering of the body used in greeting; differs because a dip is a loose, informal verb for a slight sinking or bow, without the fixed form, gender, or etiquette of a curtsey.
  • Obeisance: Similar because it is a gesture of respect or submission, of which the curtsey is one example; differs because obeisance is a general cover-term for bow, curtsey, salaam, and so on, whereas curtsey names one specific gendered gesture.
  • Reverence: Similar because it names a bow or curtsey made as a mark of respect; differs because reverence is a broader term covering bows, curtsies, and devotional inclinations, and in church use it has a specifically religious colour.
