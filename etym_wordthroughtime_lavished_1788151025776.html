- [14th–15th c. English lavas(a), "extravagance, profusion"] - [borrowed with the sense of a great outpouring, literally a flood; lavish = like a bursting downpour]
- [16th c. verb "to spend or give without stint"] - [perception largely negative at first: prodigality, waste, the vice opposite to frugality]
- [18th–19th c. moral re-ballasting] - [lavish praise and lavish affection begin to read as generous rather than corrupt; motive, not amount, decides whether lavishness is vice or virtue]
- [20th–21st c. prestige sense] - [lavish weddings, lavish productions, lavish lifestyles — the adjective glows with wealth and excess; to lavish care on something is almost always admired]
- [Modern usage] - [still judgment-laden: the same act can be "generously lavished" or "shamelessly squandered" depending on the speaker’s stance]
