Note on vowel alternation and prefix semantics: The Latin base verb tenēre underwent characteristic Latin apophony (vowel weakening) in unstressed closed syllables when combined with prefixes, shifting from short -e- to short -i- (hence per- + tenēre -> pertinēre; compare con- + tenēre -> continēre -> English contain). The prefix per- carries a spatial sense of "throughout, thoroughly, from end to end." Thus, pertinēre originally depicted a physical expanse or boundary line that "reaches all the way through to" another geographic landmark (e.g., ager ad ripam pertinet, "the land extends all the way to the riverbank"), before developing the abstract legal and cognitive sense of "reaching or extending to someone as a rightful claim, concern, or duty".
