• Rash: Similar because both describe acting without caution; differs because rash usually condemns the act, while impetuous can admire the force behind it.
  • Hasty: Similar because both mean done quickly; differs because hasty stresses speed with insufficient deliberation and is milder, lacking the forward-rush image.
  • Impulsive: Similar because both describe acting on a sudden inner urge; differs because impulsive points to psychological spontaneity, while impetuous keeps the image of a driving onset.
  • Violent: Similar because both involve extreme force; differs because violent marks force without the specific sense of headlong forward drive.
  • Precipitate: Similar because both describe action taken before considering consequences; differs because precipitate stresses falling headlong into a result, while impetuous stresses the energetic rush itself.
  • Headlong: Similar because both describe rushing forward; differs because headlong is more physical and spatial, while impetuous extends to temperament and emotion.
  • Vehement: Similar because both describe intense force; differs because vehement stresses strength of feeling or opinion, while impetuous stresses sudden, unchecked motion.
  • Spontaneous: Similar because both describe unplanned action; differs because spontaneous is neutral and often positive, lacking the recklessness or force of impetuous.
  • Hot-headed: Similar because both describe quick action or anger; differs because hot-headed is colloquial and stresses anger, while impetuous need not involve anger at all.
  • Reckless: Similar because both involve acting without care; differs because reckless emphasises indifference to danger, while impetuous emphasises speed of impulse.
  • Ardent / fervent: Similar in intensity of feeling; differs because these stress warmth and devotion, not the headlong forward rush of impetuous.
