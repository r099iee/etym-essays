• Prevarication: Similar because it is evasive straying from plain truth; differs because prevarication wanders and delays, while equivocation balances two precise meanings.
  • Evasion: Similar because it avoids a direct answer; differs because evasion can be silent or tangential, while equivocation answers in double sense.
  • Quibbling: Similar because it plays on words to escape; differs because quibbling is petty verbal nicety, while equivocation can be grave and systematic.
  • Double-talk: Similar because it says two things at once; differs because double-talk is colloquial and often playful, while equivocation is formal and blaming.
  • Doublespeak: Similar because it obscures with language; differs because doublespeak (Orwell) is bureaucratic euphemism, while equivocation is personal ambiguity.
  • Ambiguity: Similar because it is open to two readings; differs because ambiguity can be innocent, while equivocation intends to mislead.
  • Mental reservation: Similar because it silently adds a qualification to mislead lawfully; differs because it is the Jesuit theological device, while equivocation is the general act.
  • Casuistry: Similar because it reasons about doubtful cases; differs because casuistry is the method, while equivocation is one sin it debated.
  • Tergiversation: Similar because it shifts ground to deceive; differs because tergiversation changes sides or excuses, while equivocation holds one sentence with two faces.
  • Lying: Similar because it deceives about truth; differs because lying asserts outright falsehood, while equivocation hides behind technical truth.
