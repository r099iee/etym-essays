Stage 1 — PIE *steh₂- (traditionally *stā-) "to stand, be firm, set up" (Sanskrit sthā- "to stand", Avestan stā-, Greek histēmi "I set up"). Stage 2 — Proto-Italic *stāō "to stand". Stage 3 — Latin stāre "to stand" (stō, stāre). Stage 4 — Latin statuō, statuere "to set up, place, establish, decide" (the causative "make to stand", built on the same root; classical). Stage 5 — Latin dēstituō, dēstituere "to set down, put away, abandon, forsake, deprive" — dē- "down, away from" + statuere (attested from the 1st century BC: Cicero, Caesar). Stage 6 — Latin dēstitūtus "abandoned, forsaken, deprived" (past participle; classical). Stage 7 — Old French destitut "deprived, abandoned" (passed on through Anglo-French, 12th–13th centuries). Stage 8 — Middle English destitute "forsaken, abandoned" (15th century) → Modern English destitute "entirely lacking; impoverished". FINAL NODE: DESTITUTE.
