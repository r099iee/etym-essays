• carried: Similar because both mean moving something from one place to another. Differs because carried is fully generic and makes no claim about the medium or a water crossing, whereas ferried implies conveyance across water or between two fixed points.
  • transported: Similar because both denote moving people or goods over a distance. Differs because transported is broader and more formal, covering any vehicle and any distance, while ferried is more specific — usually short, repeated, and historically boat-based.
  • conveyed: Similar because both mean to carry from one place to another. Differs because conveyed is more formal and often abstract or legal (conveyance of goods, of ideas, of meaning), while ferried is concrete, physical, and water- or route-bound.
  • shuttled: Similar because both describe repeated movement back and forth between two points. Differs because shuttled is medium-neutral and modern (buses, aircraft, trains), while ferried keeps a nautical tint and implies a bounded crossing between shores.
  • rowed: Similar because both describe crossing water in a boat. Differs because rowed specifies oars and human effort in a small craft, whereas ferried names the act of conveying (by a ferry operator) without specifying propulsion.
  • sailed: Similar because both involve crossing water by vessel. Differs because sailed describes the vessel's own motion and voyage rather than the act of carrying passengers, while ferried is transitive and passenger- or cargo-centred.
  • freighted: Similar because both involve carrying goods across water. Differs because freighted emphasizes cargo, usually commercial and bulk, while ferried may carry people or a few items on a short run and has no commercial-weight requirement.
  • brought: Similar because both mean causing something to arrive somewhere. Differs because brought is direction-neutral and unmarked, while ferried specifies the crossing of water or a fixed-route shuttle.
  • chauffeured: Similar because both describe driving someone between places. Differs because chauffeured implies a professional driver and land travel, while ferried can be water, air, or road and connotes a route rather than hired service.
  • transshipped: Similar because both involve moving goods across water. Differs because transshipped is a technical logistics term for transferring cargo between vessels, while ferried is the everyday word for conveying people or goods across.
