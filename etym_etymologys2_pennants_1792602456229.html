Note on lexical blending and nautical phonology: Historical lexicographers and the Oxford English Dictionary classify pennant as an early 17th-century maritime portmanteau or lexical blend of Middle English pennon (the small pointed, feather-like flag, from Latin penna "feather") and pendant (the hanging cord, tackle, or streamer, from Latin pendere "to hang"). English seafarers assimilated the two distinct nautical terms, attaching the excrescent dental stop -t from pendant onto pennon. In the Royal Navy, the official written spelling was long maintained conservatively as pendant while being pronounced by sailors as /'penənt/. By the late 18th and early 19th centuries, the phonetic spelling pennant triumphed across both maritime, architectural, and sporting spheres.
