• Brocade: Similar because both are rich, heavy patterned fabrics used for luxury dress and furnishing; differs because brocade's raised pattern is woven with additional gold or silver threads — richer, more metallic, and more ceremonial — while damask uses a single colour and achieves its design by contrasting weave structures and the play of light.
  • Satin: Similar because both are smooth, luxurious fabrics with a glossy surface used for dress and lining; differs because satin is a plain, unpatterned weave with a glossy face, while damask is defined by its figured, reversible pattern of light and shadow.
  • Tapestry: Similar because both are decoratively patterned textiles; differs because a tapestry is a pictorial wall-hanging woven as a picture, meant to be seen flat and from a distance, while damask is a figured cloth for furnishings and dress with a repeating, reversible pattern.
  • Silk: Similar because both name luxurious fabrics associated with fine dress; differs because silk names the fibre itself and any cloth made from it, while damask names a specific weave structure and its patterned effect, whatever the fibre.
  • Velvet: Similar because both are rich, heavy, luxurious furnishing and dress fabrics; differs because velvet has a soft, dense cut-pile surface that traps colour and light, while damask is a flat weave whose pattern appears through contrasting sheen.
  • Jacquard: Similar because both describe figured woven textiles; differs because jacquard names the loom mechanism (and the machine-woven cloths it produces) that can create complex figures including damask patterns, while damask names the specific reversible, single-colour weave regardless of the loom used.
  • Damassé: Similar because both are French-derived terms for figured silk fabrics; differs because damassé is the specific French technical term for a damask-like satin-figured cloth, while damask in English is the broader, older word rooted in the city's name.
  • Chintz: Similar because both are patterned furnishing fabrics used for interiors; differs because chintz is a glazed, printed cotton with colourful floral designs, while damask is woven, reversible, and typically one-coloured, its pattern structural rather than printed.
