- [Vedic & Early Sanskrit Era (c. 1500–800 BCE)] — functioned primarily as a botanical noun denoting hollow aquatic reeds, bamboo stalks, and tubular water conduits (*Arundo donax*) — [perception: natural botanical term for buoyant riverbank vegetation and utilitarian pipes]
- [Epic Sanskrit Composition Period (c. 800–200 BCE; Rāmāyaṇa & Mahābhārata)] — crystallized into two immortal proper names: the celestial monkey engineer of the oceanic bridge in the Rāmāyaṇa and the virtuous, suffering horse-master king in the Mahābhārata — [perception: celebrated heroic paradigm of engineering brilliance, supreme royal honor, and romantic endurance]
- [Classical Mahākāvya & Campū Era (c. 5th–12th c. CE)] — elevated by courtly poets such as Śrīharṣa (*Naiṣadhīyacarita*) into the quintessential archetype of refined aesthetics, tragic nobility, and profound psychological complexity — [perception: supreme literary hero of regal elegance, rhetorical brilliance, and philosophical drama]
- [Medieval Vernacular Bhakti Period (c. 14th–18th c.)] — retold across regional Indian languages (Tulsīdās’s *Rāmacaritamānasa*, regional Nalavenbas) emphasizing divine grace, unshakeable devotion, and the sanctity of Rāma Setu — [perception: holy devotee-craftsman of Lord Rāma and pious exemplar of karma and surrender]
- [Modern Archaeological & Literary Age (19th–21st c.)] — celebrated in Indian comparative literature, stage drama, and Kathakali performances, while giving its name to geological studies and debates surrounding the Palk Strait causeway — [perception: enduring cultural symbol of legendary architecture, literary romance, and epic heritage]
