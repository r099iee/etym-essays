• Pounding: Similar because both mean repeated heavy blows falling on one target; differs because pounding stresses rhythmic hammering sound and motion, while battering stresses the damage and relentlessness of the assault.
  • Pummelling: Similar because both describe fists or blows raining down continuously; differs because pummelling (from pommel, a saddle-knob used as a club) pictures fists on flesh, while battering ranges from doors to reputations to coastlines.
  • Beating: Similar because batter is historically the frequentative of beat; differs because a beating can be brief or ceremonial (a beating of drums, a heart-beat), while a battering is always prolonged and damaging.
  • Hammering: Similar because both picture sustained crushing blows; differs because hammering keeps the tool in view (literal hammers, or a hammering in debate), while battering keeps the victim's suffering in view.
  • Buffeting: Similar because both describe winds and waves striking repeatedly; differs because buffeting stresses being tossed about (a buffeted traveller), while battering stresses being broken down.
  • Bombardment: Similar because both mean a sustained assault from a distance; differs because bombardment rains projectiles or questions from afar (a bombardment of shells, of emails), while battering pounds one surface up close.
  • Assault: Similar because both name violent attacks on persons or places; differs because assault is the lawyer's and general's formal term (assault charges, the assault on the ridge), while battering is the hammer's-eye account of blows landing.
  • Thrashing: Similar because both describe one-sided beatings, literal or sporting; differs because a thrashing pictures flailing strokes (originally flail-strokes in threshing) and humiliation, while a battering pictures siege-like persistence.
  • Drubbing: Similar because both mean a sound beating, especially a sporting defeat; differs because a drubbing is colloquial and scoreboard-minded (a 5–0 drubbing), while a battering can be solemn — storms, sieges, and courtrooms included.
