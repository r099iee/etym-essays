- [c. 1200s–1400s] Anglo-Latin peta / Middle English pete — primarily a cut piece of bog turf for burning; perception practical and local, tied to northern British and Irish fuel economies.
- [1500s–1700s] Scots & Northern English domestic staple — peat as household heating fuel; cultural smell of peat smoke; word common in regional speech and travel accounts; still concrete and material.
- [18th–19th c.] Natural history & geology — peat redefined as organic deposit and early coal stage; educated readers meet the word in science alongside folk fuel sense; horticultural use begins to rise.
- [20th c.] Dual life — rural fuel declines in many areas as coal/oil spread; garden and greenhouse demand for peat moss soars; "peat bog" fixed in school geography.
- [Late 20th–21st c.] Environmental reframing — peatlands as carbon sinks and endangered habitats; "peat-free" products signal ethical consumerism; the word gains policy and climate-news frequency while traditional hearth associations survive in heritage and whisky contexts.
