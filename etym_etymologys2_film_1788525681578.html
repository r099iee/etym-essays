PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500 BCE)
*pel- / *pel-no-
"skin, hide, covering, cloth"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*filminją
"membrane, thin skin, caul" (diminutive of *fellą "skin, hide")
↓
Old English (c. 9th–10th c.)
filmen
"membrane, thin skin, prepuce, husk"
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
filme
"thin skin, membrane, pellicle, film"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
film
"pellicle, delicate layer, cataract or veil over eyes"
↓
Modern English (Present)
film
"thin layer, membrane; ocular veil; photographic strip; motion picture"

Note: Proto-Germanic *filminją is an instrumental/diminutive extension formed on *fellą ("skin, pelt"), cognate with Latin pellis ("skin"), Greek πέλλα (pélla), and English fell ("animal skin"), pelt, and felt.
