• Swallow: Similar because both describe taking something wholly inside a larger devourer; differs because swallow stresses passage down a throat or channel, while engulf stresses surrounding on every side until nothing shows.
  • Devour: Similar because both mean to consume completely and destructively; differs because devour implies tearing, chewing, or greedy eating, while engulf implies drowning or folding over in one smooth closing motion.
  • Overwhelm: Similar because both describe a force too great to resist, especially of emotion; differs because overwhelm is the general word for defeating by excess, while engulf adds the specific picture of being swallowed inside the force.
  • Inundate: Similar because both picture drowning under floodwater, literal or metaphorical; differs because inundate stresses the rising level of water or of demands, while engulf stresses disappearance inside the mass.
  • Submerge: Similar because both mean to put or sink under liquid; differs because submerge is technical and neutral (submerged cables, submerged evidence), while engulf is dramatic and total, with menace or awe.
  • Envelop: Similar because both mean to wrap or surround completely; differs because envelop can be gentle and protective (enveloped in a blanket, in mist), while engulf almost always threatens to destroy or erase.
  • Consume: Similar because both describe fire, passion, or spending that eats things up; differs because consume stresses using up by degrees until nothing remains, while engulf stresses sudden surrounding and hiding from view.
  • Drown: Similar because both evoke death by water and being covered by feeling; differs because drown names the fatal outcome for a living sufferer, while engulf names the surrounding act, which villages and roads can suffer without lungs.
  • Bury: Similar because both hide the patient out of sight beneath a mass; differs because bury pictures covering from above with earth or snow, while engulf pictures closing in from every side at once.
