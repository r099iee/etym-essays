• Durée: Similar because both denote the temporal dimension of reality and the persistence of phenomena; differs because durée (especially as formulated by philosopher Henri Bergson) emphasizes the subjective, qualitative, unbroken, and flowing psychological experience of inner time, whereas temps can denote objective chronological intervals, metric beats, atmospheric weather, or segmented procedural stages (such as pedestrian phases).
  • Délai: Similar because both relate to periods or intervals of time allocated for actions; differs because délai specifically denotes an allotted deadline, grace period, or legally mandated time limit within which an obligation must be fulfilled, whereas temps represents the general concept of duration or a natural phase of motion.
  • Moment: Similar because both characterize segments within the flow of time; differs because moment denotes an instantaneous, fleeting, or point-like flash of high intensity or critical significance, whereas temps encompasses broad historical epochs, ongoing duration, metric beats, and multi-stage sequences.
  • Époque: Similar because both designate historical expanses in human chronology; differs because époque is reserved for monumental historical eras characterized by distinctive cultural, political, or intellectual conditions, whereas temps is the universal, everyday word for both small minutes and entire millennia.
  • Cadence: Similar because both involve the rhythmic measurement of actions, steps, or music across time; differs because cadence highlights the repetitive harmonic flow, pacing, or rhythmic fall of sound, whereas temps refers specifically to the structural beat, the individual phase of an action, or cosmic duration itself.
  • Tempo: Similar because it is an Italian cognate directly descended from Latin *tempus* used across global music and athletic performance; differs because tempo refers to the overall speed, pace, or velocity at which an entire piece or event unfolds, whereas temps refers to the individual metric beat or a staged phase within a sequenced action.
  • Saison: Similar because both can describe atmospheric and cyclical periods of the year; differs because saison denotes one of the four formal astronomical and meteorological divisions of the calendar year (spring, summer, autumn, winter), whereas temps can describe the immediate, changing weather of a single morning (*le temps qu'il fait*).
