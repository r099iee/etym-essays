CHAIN — THE RIVER NAME Elbe: Stage 1 — PIE *albh- "white, bright" (reconstructed color-adjective root, the source of Latin albus "white" and Greek alphós "white leprosy"; widely proposed as the ultimate base of the river name — see debate note). Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *albi- / *albī "river" (reconstructed Germanic word for a river, plausibly "the bright or white one" from the same *albh- root, or an independent Germanic water-word meaning "river"; the base of the recorded Latin and later Germanic forms of the name of the Elbe; not directly attested). Stage 3 — Latin Albis "the Elbe" (attested from the 1st century CE: Pliny the Elder's Natural History 4.100, and Tacitus's Germania 41, where the Albis is described as rising among the Hermunduri; the river is also the target of the campaigns of Drusus and Tiberius in the Roman sources). Stage 4 — Old High German Elba and Middle High German Elbe "the Elbe" (attested from the 9th century in OHG sources such as Otfrid of Weissenburg and later chronicles; the modern German form Elbe is fixed from the Middle High German). Stage 5 — English Elbe "the river Elbe" (the English name, taken from the modern German form and replacing the older Latin Albis; in use from the 16th–17th centuries in geography and travel writing, and naturalized in historical prose, as on this card, by the 18th century). FINAL NODE: ELBE. DEBATE NOTE: two schools dispute the ultimate meaning of the name. The majority connects the Germanic and Latin forms with PIE *albh- "white", so that the Elbe is "the white or bright river" (water gleaming among dark forests). A minority sees an independent Germanic water-word *albiz "river" with no color sense, perhaps from a different root meaning "to flow". The chain from Albis to Elbe is itself secure and regular, but the pre-Roman semantic value is genuinely debated. The Czech name Labe is the same Germanic name in Slavic guise, adapted by regular Slavic sound changes.
