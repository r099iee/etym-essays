1. Proto-Indo-European *der- “to split, flay, peel, tear apart” (reconstructed root; cf. Vedic Sanskrit dṛṇāti “he splits, tears asunder”; Ancient Greek derō “I flay, skin” and dérma “skin, hide”) 2. Proto-Germanic *teran- “to tear, rend, destroy” (Grimm’s Law: PIE *d- → Germanic *t-) 3. Old English teran “to tear, rend” — strong Class IV verb; past tense tær, past participle toren / getoren (attested from c. 8th century) 4. Middle English toren “torn, ripped” → contraction to torn (participle loses medial vowel and final -en; attested from c. 13th–14th c.) 5. Modern English torn (past participle of tear: “lacerated, split, pulled apart”; figurative “torn between,” 18th c.) Note: Sanskrit dāru “wood” resembles *der- but belongs to the separate PIE root *deru- “tree, wood,” so it is not a cognate. The past tense tore was reshaped on analogy with bore and swore; the participle torn keeps the older strong form.
