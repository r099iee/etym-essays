- [Antiquity to Early Medieval Era] — Evolved across Eurasia from primitive vertical warp-weighted looms to horizontal floor-treadle timber looms — [perception: universal and indispensable domestic appliance of household self-sufficiency and feminine domestic mastery]
- [17th–18th c. Cottage Industry Golden Age] — Became the economic foundation of rural families across Britain, Flanders, and South Asia ("the golden age of the handloom weaver") — [perception: celebrated symbol of independent, respectable artisan status, rural prosperity, and self-directed labor]
- [Early 19th c. Industrial Revolution & Luddism] — Catastrophically undercut by coal- and steam-driven mechanical power looms, driving weavers into starvation, machine-wrecking rebellions (Luddism), and radical politics — [perception: tragic emblem of exploited, displaced craftsmanship crushed by the brutal march of mechanized industrial factory labor]
- [20th c. Indian Independence & Swadeshi Movement] — Adopted by Mahatma Gandhi as the central icon of Indian anti-colonial liberation (the spinning of Khadi), rejecting foreign British mill textiles — [perception: revolutionary emblem of national self-reliance, moral purity, spiritual dignity, and nonviolent economic resistance]
- [Late 20th–21st c. Slow Fashion & Artisanal Luxury] — Celebrated globally as an ethical, carbon-neutral alternative to fast fashion, prized for bespoke luxury, tactile irregularity, and living cultural heritage — [perception: prestigious hallmark of master craftsmanship, sustainable design, and conscious eco-luxury]
