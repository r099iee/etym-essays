• courage: Similar because both describe the mental or moral strength to face danger, fear, or difficulty; differs because courage is a broad, universal virtue applicable to quiet domestic trials, disease, or routine hardship, whereas valor specifically evokes heroic, high-stakes confrontation, often in martial or grand dramatic settings.
  • bravery: Similar because both denote an absence of fear or willingness to confront danger; differs because bravery can describe a spontaneous or innate temperament (even reckless or unreflective), whereas valor carries a stately, noble connotation of honorable and deliberate self-sacrifice.
  • intrepidity: Similar because both highlight unflinching fearlessness in perilous circumstances; differs because intrepidity emphasizes psychological coolness and unshakeable composure in the presence of terror, whereas valor emphasizes aggressive, proactive exertion and gallantry.
  • gallantry: Similar because both evoke chivalric heroism; differs because gallantry strongly emphasizes dash, courtly elegance, high spiritedness, and courteous nobility toward others, whereas valor focuses strictly on the grim fortitude and prowess of combat.
  • prowess: Similar because both are celebrated martial virtues; differs because prowess highlights exceptional technical skill, physical capability, and battlefield effectiveness, whereas valor denotes the moral and spiritual fortitude that motivates heroic action.
  • fortitude: Similar because both involve resolute strength in the face of suffering or danger; differs because fortitude is primarily passive and enduring—the quiet, steadfast capacity to bear pain, grief, or adversity without breaking—whereas valor is active, outward, and dynamic.
  • daring: Similar because both involve venturing into dangerous territory; differs because daring emphasizes boldness, risk-taking, and audacity, which may be adventurous or reckless rather than guided by duty or noble honor.
  • heroism: Similar because both encompass extraordinary acts of courage; differs because heroism describes the overarching quality or monumental narrative achievement of a hero's life, whereas valor is the specific martial or moral mettle demonstrated in a critical encounter.
  • fearlessness: Similar because both describe an attitude untamed by dread; differs because fearlessness is merely the negative condition of feeling no fear (which can stem from ignorance or psychopathy), whereas valor masters and transcends real fear for a higher cause.
  • pluck: Similar because both denote spirited resilience in difficult situations; differs because pluck is an informal, colloquial term evoking scrappy, stubborn, underdog determination, lacking the majestic, epic register of valor.
  • mettle: Similar because both describe the quality of a person's character under pressure; differs because mettle refers to general temperament, resilience, and stamina, whereas valor specifically denotes illustrious bravery in dangerous trials.
  • dauntlessness: Similar because both signify inability to be intimidated; differs because dauntlessness stresses unbroken defiance and perseverance against intimidating foes, whereas valor combines that defiance with exalted honor and noble execution.
