• corundum: Similar because ruby is a variety of corundum, its mineral species; differs because corundum names the whole mineral family, including industrial emery and blue sapphire, while ruby picks out only transparent red gem-grade material.
  • sapphire: Similar because sapphire is ruby's sister gem, the other precious variety of corundum with identical chemistry; differs because sapphire is usually blue (coloured by iron and titanium) while ruby is red (coloured by chromium), and the trade grades them entirely separately.
  • spinel: Similar because fine red spinel is the historic "balas ruby" and was long sold as ruby; differs because spinel is a different mineral (magnesium aluminium oxide) and modern gemology separates the two; true rubies are rarer and costlier.
  • garnet: Similar because red garnets were once sold as "Cape rubies" and share ruby's deep red glamour; differs because garnet is a different, much softer mineral family (Mohs 6.5-7.5) and far cheaper, with brownish and purplish tones.
  • carbuncle: Similar because the ancient name for ruby-like red gems was carbunculus, the "little coal"; differs because carbuncle is obsolete as a gem term and today names a skin boil, so the resemblance is purely historical.
  • crimson / scarlet: Similar because ruby red is a deep red colour bordering on crimson; differs because ruby names the gem first and the colour second, and ruby-red is darker, denser and more saturated than either scarlet or crimson.
  • carnelian: Similar because carnelian is another red gemstone, a chalcedony quartz; differs because carnelian is translucent, brownish-orange and semi-precious — a soft, cheap stone with none of ruby's hardness or price.
