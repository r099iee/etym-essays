*ten- (PIE, reconstructed)
"to stretch, extend"
(ancestor of Latin tenēre "to hold")

▼

tenēre (Latin, 1st c. BCE)
"to hold, keep, grasp"

▼

continēre (Latin, Classical)
com- "together" + tenēre
"to hold together, contain, restrain"

▼

continentia (Latin, 1st c. BCE)
"self-restraint, moderation, composure"
(abstract noun; Stoic virtue of self-control)

▼

contenance (Old French, 12th c.)
"bearing, demeanour, composure"
(meaning slid from self-control to outward bearing)

▼

countenaunce (Middle English, 13th c.)
"appearance, face, demeanour; favour"

▼

countenance (English noun)
"face, facial expression; composure;
approval, support, sanction"

▼

countenance (English verb, from the noun, 15th c.)
"to approve of, patronize, support"
then "to tolerate, permit" (modern)

▼

COUNTENANCE (modern English)
"to admit as acceptable or possible;
to tolerate or approve"

NOTE: the spelling count- (with the u) reflects a 14th-century
influence from the unrelated verb count (Latin computāre);
the semantic link between "face" and "approval" is the
well-attested bridge — support is shown in the face.
