• manifested: Similar because both describe something hidden becoming observable. Differs because manifested implies deliberate or complete revelation (often spiritual), while symptomatized implies an involuntary, causal, medical link — signs that fall together with an underlying condition.
  • indicated: Similar because both mean that one thing points to another. Differs because indicated is a broad, neutral term (a signpost indicates), while symptomatized is specific and pathological, tying the sign causally to a hidden cause.
  • revealed: Similar because both involve making the hidden known. Differs because revealed suggests an act of uncovering (often divine or deliberate), while symptomatized is diagnostic and involuntary — the sign emerges on its own from the condition.
  • signified: Similar because both concern what a sign means. Differs because signified belongs to language and semiotics (a word signifies), while symptomatized belongs to medicine and diagnosis — a physical sign of an underlying state.
  • evidenced: Similar because both mean shown or proved by outward indications. Differs because evidenced is neutral and evidential (a fact evidenced by documents), while symptomatized implies an organic, pathological relationship between sign and cause.
  • signalled: Similar because both mean communicated by signs. Differs because signalled implies intentional communication (a signal deliberately sent), while symptomatized implies involuntary manifestation of an inner state.
  • betokened: Similar because both mean to indicate by signs. Differs because betokened is archaic and literary (a blush betokens guilt), while symptomatized is clinical and analytic, naming sign-cause structure rather than mere portent.
  • displayed / exhibited: Similar because both mean to show outwardly. Differs because these imply conscious or public display, while symptomatized implies that the hidden condition itself produces the signs without volition.
  • characterized: Similar because both describe how a thing is marked by certain features. Differs because characterized is static (a portrait of typical features), while symptomatized is dynamic and diagnostic (a hidden condition actively surfacing in signs).
  • expressed: Similar because both mean to make inner states outward. Differs because expressed usually implies the subject's own communication or outward form, while symptomatized attributes the outward sign to the underlying condition itself.
