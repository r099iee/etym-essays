• Stubs: Similar because both denote short blunt remnants of something cut or broken; differs because a stub is generally smaller and suggests being worn or broken close to the surface, while a stump is the thicker projecting base of a tree or limb.
  • Remnants: Similar because both mean what remains after destruction; differs because remnant is broad, abstract, and often figurative, while stump is concrete and specifically organic.
  • Trunks: Similar because both belong to the tree's main stem; differs because a trunk is the full standing body of the tree, while a stump is only the truncated base left behind after felling.
  • Stubble: Similar because both describe what is left of cut vegetation; differs because stubble refers to short stalks of grain left in fields, while stumps are the cut bases of trees.
  • Ruins: Similar because both denote remains of destruction; differs because ruins typically implies buildings or structures of scale and age, while stumps are the cut remains of trees.
  • Debris: Similar because both can describe scattered remains; differs because debris is broken scattered fragments, while stumps are larger, intact-but-truncated masses.
  • Clubs: Similar because both are short thick blunt objects; differs because a club is a weapon or tool, while a stump is a natural remnant.
  • Butts: Similar because both denote the thicker end of something; differs because butt usually means the end of a tool, cigarette, or body part, while stump specifically suggests a tree base.
