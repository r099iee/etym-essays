- [14th-17th century shoot sense] - 'Sprig' names a small twig or shoot; 'sprigged' describes things bearing or adorned with such shoots.
- [18th-19th century textile heyday] - Sprigged muslins and cottons - cloth scattered with small floral sprays - become the fashionable dress and curtain fabrics of the Georgian and Regency era, fresh and delicate.
- [19th-century craft extension] - The technique extends to pottery (sprigged ceramics with applied clay motifs); the word stays in the textile and crafts lexicon.
- [Contemporary register] - A period-flavoured textile term evoking old-fashioned floral dress; also describes anything ornamented with small sprig-like motifs.
