• Servants: Similar because both denote household employees; differs because servants is broader and lacks the feudal flavor of long personal attachment that retainers carries.
  • Followers: Similar because both mark allegiance to a person; differs because followers can be political or ideological adherents, not necessarily household staff.
  • Dependents: Similar because both highlight economic reliance on a patron; differs because dependents lacks the honorific or long-standing service sense of a retained household follower.
  • Minions: Similar because both name dependents of a powerful person; differs because minions imply active participation in a master's schemes and a sinister tone, whereas retainers can be uninvolved bystanders.
  • Henchmen: Similar because both are men attached to a powerful master; differs because henchmen are actively involved in violence or schemes, while the retainers in the card are explicitly not involved in the crime.
  • Attendants: Similar because both denote people who serve or accompany; differs because an attendant is a personal waiter or companion, not necessarily one retained long-term.
  • Aides: Similar because both can mean helpers of a powerful person; differs because an aide is a staff assistant or military officer, a modern role without feudal attachment.
  • Domestics: Similar because both are household workers; differs because domestics is a modern, class-neutral word for indoor servants without the loyalty of retainers.
  • Staff: Similar because both denote employed people in a household; differs because staff is modern, contractual, and impersonal compared with retained loyalty.
  • Household: Similar because both evoke the whole body of a great house's people; differs because household can include family, while retainers are specifically the kept servants and followers.
  • Retinue: Similar because both name a noble's accompanying people; differs because a retinue is the group in attendance on a journey, while retainers are the fixed household core.
  • Entourage: Similar because both denote a powerful person's circle; differs because entourage is modern and casual, often famous people's hangers-on, without feudal service.
  • Liegemen: Similar because both are sworn followers of a lord; differs because a liegeman owes feudal allegiance and military service, a more solemn bond than household service.
  • Vassals: Similar because both are dependent on a lord; differs because vassals hold land in return for service, while retainers are paid or kept household men.
  • Lackeys: Similar because both are subservient attendants; differs because lackey is contemptuous and implies servile flattery rather than loyal, honorable service.
  • Flunkies: Similar because both name obsequious followers; differs because flunky is purely derogatory and implies social climbing, not faithful domestic service.
  • Hangers-on: Similar because both are people who attach themselves to the powerful; differs because hangers-on are uninvited parasites, while retainers are formally kept.
  • Courtiers: Similar because both move in a powerful person's circle; differs because courtiers attend a royal court and pursue favor, not domestic service.
  • Bodyguards: Similar because both are men kept for a powerful person's protection; differs because bodyguards are trained for defense, while retainers serve generally and may be uninvolved in crime.
  • Employees: Similar because both denote people paid to serve; differs because employees is the modern contractual term without feudal or long-standing loyalty.
