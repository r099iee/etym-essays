• Satya Yuga: Similar because both are synonymous designations for the primordial Golden Age of cosmic purity and righteousness; differs because Satya Yuga highlights the moral and philosophical essence of the age ("the Age of Truth"), whereas Kṛta Yuga emphasizes its complete four-pointed perfection and accomplished fulfillment derived from ancient numerological and dice-play terminology.
  • Golden Age: Similar because both represent the mythic primordial era of universal harmony, longevity, and absence of suffering across world mythologies; differs because the Golden Age is a Greco-Roman metallic concept (Hesiodic ages: Gold, Silver, Bronze, Iron), whereas Kṛta Yuga is deeply rooted in cyclical Vedic cosmology, dice metrics, and the four-legged stance of Dharma.
  • Tretā Yuga: Similar because both are consecutive cyclical epochs in the Hindu Mahāyuga system; differs because the Kṛta Yuga possesses four full parts (100%) of virtue and requires no external sacrifices, whereas Tretā Yuga experiences the loss of one-quarter of Dharma (retaining 75% virtue) and introduces formal ritual sacrifice, kingship, and moral effort.
  • Dvāpara Yuga: Similar because both belong to the cosmic world cycle; differs because Dvāpara Yuga retains only half (50%) of original righteousness, marked by increasing disease, moral conflict, and the division of the single Veda into four compilations by Vyāsa.
  • Kali Yuga: Similar because both mark the boundary epochs of a Mahāyuga; differs because Kṛta Yuga is the zenith of light, longevity, and virtue, whereas Kali Yuga is the nadir of darkness, corruption, discord, and moral degradation, where Dharma clings to only a single unstable leg.
  • Millennium: Similar because both denote an extended idealized era of peace and righteous governance; differs because a millennium is a literal thousand-year period often tied to Western apocalyptic or messianic expectations, whereas the Kṛta Yuga spans 1,728,000 years in an eternal cosmic wheel of time.
  • Utopia: Similar because both describe an idealized societal state free from famine, violence, and institutional corruption; differs because utopia (Greek "no-place") is an imagined, fictional socio-political construct, whereas the Kṛta Yuga was regarded in traditional Indian thought as a concrete historical and cyclical reality.
  • Primordial State: Similar because both designate the original uncorrupted baseline of the cosmos before moral decay; differs because the primordial state is an abstract philosophical concept, whereas Kṛta embodies detailed chronological, cosmological, and moral metrics.
