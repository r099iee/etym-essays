- Plain plant-name - For centuries ivy simply named the hardy evergreen climber Hedera helix, a practical plant of cottage walls, hedging, and herbal remedy, carrying little symbolic freight.
- Classical and medieval emblem - Classical habit loaded the word with meaning: ivy became the emblem of Bacchus and of fidelity (evergreen constancy), its wreaths decorating poets, lovers, and churchyard memorials alike.
- Gothic and Romantic ruin - The 18th-19th century Gothic Revival recast ivy as the clinging companion of decay: ivy-clad ruins became the standard image of melancholy, tenacity, and slow ruin - exactly the morbid atmosphere this passage demands.
- Modern double life - Today the word splits between the horticultural (often an invasive nuisance that strips and disfigures walls) and the elite (the 1930s Ivy League borrowing the ivied masonry of old campuses), keeping its vertical grip while losing its innocence.
