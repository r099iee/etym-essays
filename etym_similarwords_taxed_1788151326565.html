• burdened: similar because both mean weighed down by a heavy load or obligation; differs because "taxed" may be purely fiscal and institutional, while "burdened" is chiefly physical or emotional and more literary.
  • strained: similar because both convey resources stretched to the limit; differs because "strained" suggests mechanical or relational tension, whereas "taxed" emphasises an externally imposed demand.
  • overtaxed: similar because both refer to levies or demands; differs because "overtaxed" adds the prefix "over-", meaning beyond capacity — an intensification of "taxed".
  • charged: similar because both involve obligations or accusations; differs because "charged" is far broader (electricity, prices, duties, enthusiasm) and lacks the fiscal-strain focus.
  • levied (upon): similar because both describe the imposition of a compulsory exaction; differs because "levied" is dryly technical and legal and describes the act rather than the subjected state.
  • tasked: similar because both involve assigned obligations; differs because "tasked" implies purposeful delegation, "taxed" implies weight, extraction, or punishment.
  • harassed: similar because both convey oppressive, unrelenting demands; differs because "harassed" is emotional and vexatious, "taxed" more neutral, resource-based, or fiscal.
  • stretched thin: similar because both express resources spread to the breaking point; differs because the idiom is colloquial and spatial, "taxed" formal and institutional.
  • drained: similar because both describe depletion by demands; differs because "drained" is resultative (the resource is gone), "taxed" impositive (the demand is on).
  • exhausted: similar because both describe energy or means used up; differs because "exhausted" signals completion of the process, while "taxed" may still leave room to cope.
