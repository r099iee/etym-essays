- [Old English martyr: witness] - 'Martyr' enters English from Greek via Latin, meaning a witness, especially one who suffers death for the faith; connotation is religious and heroic from the start.
- [Medieval to Reformation: the martyr cult] - To be 'martyred' becomes the supreme Christian honour - dying as a witness to truth and being vindicated by the very act of dying; connotation is sacred, sacrificial, and glorified.
- [Modern secular extension] - 'Martyred' extends to anyone killed for their beliefs, religious or otherwise, and metaphorically to anyone who suffers self-sacrificingly; connotation is tragic and hallowed, though it can slide into irony (the 'martyr complex') when the suffering is merely felt rather than real.
