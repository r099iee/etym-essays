- 12th–13th c. — the name appears in charters as a personal name of Frankish descent, still interchangeable with the name of the county in the Low Countries.
- 14th–15th c. — hereditary surnames settle among robe and merchant families, and the name can still mean simply “a man from that county”.
- 16th c. — the accented spelling settles, and the word becomes unambiguously a family name, no longer a mere label of origin.
- 1723–1744 — the president’s name becomes a salon and academic label, then a European one with the Abrégé chronologique.
- 1837 — Carlyle prints the name without the accent, and it becomes English shorthand for sleek official history.
- 20th–21st c. — confined to historical scholarship, book catalogues and genealogy; rare as a living surname outside France.
