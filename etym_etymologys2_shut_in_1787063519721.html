ROOT 1 — SHUT

PIE (reconstructed; debated)
*skeud-
"to shoot, throw, push"

↓

Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*skutjaną
"to push, bolt, make fast"

↓

Old English (c. 900 CE)
scyttan
"to shut, close, fasten"

↓

Middle English (12th–15th c.)
shutten
"to close, enclose, shut"

↓

Modern English (19th c. compound)
SHUT-IN
"enclosed; homebound person"


ROOT 2 — IN

PIE (reconstructed)
*h₁én
"in, inside"

↓

Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*in
"in, inside"

↓

Old English (attested c. 700 CE)
in
"in, into, within"

↓

Middle English (12th–15th c.)
in
"in, within"

↓

Modern English (19th c. compound)
SHUT-IN
"enclosed; homebound person"


Debate note: the PIE ancestor of scyttan is not secure; the link to *skeud- "to shoot" is a hypothesis, not a certainty. The Germanic stage (*skutjaną) and Old English scyttan are solidly attested. The second root in is uncontroversial.
