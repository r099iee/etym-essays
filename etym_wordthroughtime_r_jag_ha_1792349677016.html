- [Late Vedic & Early Epic Era (c. 1000–600 BCE)] — functioned as an active Sanskrit compound designating the physical fortified manor, residence, or citadel of a consecrated sovereign ruler (*rājan*) — [perception: revered as a sacrosanct hearth of dynastic authority, sacrificial rites, and tribal protection]
- [Epic Itihāsa Period (c. 500–200 BCE; Rāmāyaṇa & Mahābhārata)] — crystallized into renowned toponyms for legendary fortified cities, notably King Ashvapati’s mountain-girt capital of Kekeya in the northwest and Jarasandha’s fortress-valley in Magadha — [perception: celebrated as an impregnable bastion of royal bloodlines, hero-kings, and strategic imperial defense]
- [Classical Śramaṇic & Magadhan Age (c. 6th–4th c. BCE)] — historical apex as the prime imperial metropolis of northern India under Kings Bimbisāra and Ajātaśatru, frequented by Gautama Buddha and Vardhamāna Mahāvīra, and hosting the First Buddhist Council — [perception: venerated across the subcontinent as the intellectual, monastic, and political heart of early Indian civilization]
- [Classical Sanskrit & Vāstuśāstra Codification (c. 1st millennium CE)] — memorialized in architectural treatises as the archetypal master-plan for a royal palace precinct, while functioning metaphorically in spiritual poetry as the human body housing the sovereign soul — [perception: admired as a classical architectural paradigm and a symbol of venerable antiquity]
- [Modern Archaeological & Heritage Era (19th–21st c.)] — identified with the excavated archaeological complex at Rajgir in Bihar and the epic geography of Punjab, preserved as major pilgrimage and cultural heritage landmarks — [perception: viewed globally as an evocative monument of ancient Indian urbanization, Buddhist philosophy, and epic literature]
