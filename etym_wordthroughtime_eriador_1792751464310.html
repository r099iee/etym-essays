- [First Age / Elder Days] — Known to wandering Nandorin and Sindarin Elves as a boundless, primeval green wilderness covered in dense primeval forests of oak and pine between the Blue and Misty Mountains — [perception: perceived as a serene, untouched primeval garden of Arda]
- [Second Age / c. S.A. 600–1700] — Transformed into a site of massive industrial timber exploitation by Númenórean shipbuilders and subsequently scorched into open plains during the devastating War of the Elves and Sauron — [perception: understood as a war-torn, deforested continental expanse stripped of its ancient canopy]
- [Third Age / Golden Age of Arnor c. T.A. 1–1300] — United under Elendil and his heirs as the magnificent North-kingdom of Arnor, crisscrossed by royal paved highways, great fortresses, and thriving agricultural estates — [perception: revered as the imperial northern heartland of the Dúnedain and high civilization]
- [Late Third Age / c. T.A. 1400–3019] — Fractured by Angmar's wars, ravaging plagues, and cold ruin into the haunting "Lone-lands," dotted with ancient barrows and decaying towers, while nurturing the idyllic sanctuary of the Shire — [perception: feared as a perilous, haunted wilderness by ordinary folk, and guarded with quiet sacrifice by the Rangers]
- [Fourth Age & Modern Literary Era / 1954–present] — Re-established as the United Kingdom of Arnor and Gondor under King Elessar (Aragorn), and recognized in world literature as Tolkien's poignant master-setting for the nostalgic, melancholic beauty of lost realms — [perception: celebrated by literary scholars and readers worldwide as the quintessential fantasy landscape of wandering heroism and quiet rural safety]
