Middle English (13th c.)
cobbe
‘rounded lump, mass’
also ‘head, chief’
↓
Middle English (extended) (14th–15th c.)
cobbe-nut, cobble
‘round nut, round stone’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
cob (horse)
‘short stout riding horse’
↓
Modern English (current)
cob
‘stout horse; round lump’
‘corncob, loaf, swan’

Note: the chain before Middle English cobbe is unknown: the word has no certain cognates and no agreed deeper root — it may be native expressive formation (lump-words are often sound-symbolic, compare bob, knob, nob), or possibly of Celtic origin, but neither is proven. Old English coppe (‘spider’, surviving in cobweb) is a separate word and not an ancestor, despite the spelling resemblance.
