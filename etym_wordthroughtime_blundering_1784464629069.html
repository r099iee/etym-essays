“Blunder” entered English meaning to move blindly or clumsily — from a Scandinavian word for shut-eyed, a blunderer is one who goes forward without seeing.
“Blunder” from the physical stumble widened to the figurative error: to blunder is to make a stupid, careless mistake, and a “blunder” is a serious miscalculation.
“Blunder” through the 18th–19th centuries kept both senses entwined — the clumsy big person crashing through a quiet world and the fool making careless errors — often comic in tone.
“Blundering” today is the participle of that clumsy, careless mode of being: moving and acting blindly, making noise and mistakes through lack of care — the elephantine tread that a quiet people can hear a mile off.
