• Suppose: Similar because both express a mild assumption of probability (I daresay ≈ I suppose); differs because suppose is plainer and more uncertain, without daresay's genteel, assertive-hedge flavour.
  • Guess: Similar because both express uncertainty about a probability; differs because guess is informal and implies little basis, while daresay is polite and lightly confident.
  • Reckon: Similar because both can mean 'think probable' (I reckon); differs because reckon is regional and informal (British and American colloquial), while daresay is genteel and dated.
  • Presume: Similar because both mean to take something as likely; differs because presume is formal and can sound assertive or even arrogant, while daresay is modest and hedged.
  • Assume: Similar because both mean to accept as true without proof; differs because assume implies taking for granted, while daresay merely ventures a probability.
  • Suspect: Similar because both express a belief without certainty; differs because suspect hints at doubt or distrust, while daresay is neutral and pleasant.
  • Expect: Similar because both imply reasonable grounds for belief; differs because expect is more confident and forward-looking, while daresay is tentative.
  • Figure: Similar because both can mean 'suppose' (I figure); differs because figure is American informal, with none of daresay's British gentility.
  • Opine: Similar because both mean to state an opinion; differs because opine is formal and often pompous, while daresay is colloquial and self-effacing.
  • I should think: Similar because both are British hedged assertions of probability; differs because I should think is a full clause of several words, while daresay is a fixed fused formula.
  • Venture to say: Similar because both mean 'I am bold enough to say'; differs because venture to say is the formal, self-deprecating phrase, while daresay is the everyday contracted version.
