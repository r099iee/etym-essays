- [Late 19th century · philological borrowing] - Adopted into English from Late Latin peiorātus ('made worse', from peior, 'worse'); began as a technical label for words or senses that depreciate.
- [Early 20th century · lexicography] - Standardized in dictionaries ('the word has acquired a pejorative sense'); becomes the precise tool for marking semantic drift toward insult.
- [Modern · public critical discourse] - Widens beyond dictionaries to describe remarks, reviews, and everyday speech that belittle; still neutral as a label, applied to language whose function is to lower the status of its object.
