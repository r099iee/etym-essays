Aaron is in the Hebrew Bible the elder brother of Moses and Miriam, spokesman to Pharaoh, and consecrated first high priest whose descendants form the Aaronic or Levitical priesthood. His rod — paired with the rod of Moses in the source passage — buds with almonds as a sign of chosen priesthood, turns into a serpent, and with Hur steadies Moses's arms in battle. The passage cites rod of Moses or Aaron as the Hebrew example of staff-as-authority alongside Roman lituus and consular cane.

Beyond the person, Aaron names an office, a lineage, and botanical metaphors: Aaronic blessing, Aaronic priesthood, Aaron's beard for hairy St John's wort, Aaron's rod for mullein and garden ornamentals.

  • Core biblical sense — brother and mouthpiece: Exodus 4:14 — Is there not Aaron your brother the Levite? I know that he can speak well; eloquent where Moses is slow of speech; performs signs, stretches rod, addresses Pharaoh.
  • Priestly sense — first high priest: Exodus 28–29, Leviticus 8 investiture with ephod, breastplate, Urim and Thummim; offers Day of Atonement rites; sons Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, Ithamar; all kohanim claim descent; bridge from kinship to institution.
  • Ambivalent narrative sense: fashions the Golden Calf in Exodus 32 while Moses is on Sinai, yet is forgiven and confirmed; complains with Miriam in Numbers 12; strikes rock with Moses and is barred from land, dying on Mount Hor; humanity and mediation intertwined.
  • Interfaith senses: in Judaism Aharon lover of peace pursuing peace; in Christianity Aaronic priesthood superseded by Melchizedek; in Islam Harun helper and deputy of Musa, honoured prophet; each keeps brother-helper core.
  • Onomastic sense — given name: Aaron, Aharon, Aron, Harun, Haroun in English since Reformation, in Hebrew, Arabic, and European forms; connotes eloquence, priestliness, and peacemaking; borne by Aaron Burr, Hank Aaron, Aaron Copland.
  • Botanical senses — Aaron's beard and Aaron's rod: from 1540s hairy plants deemed bearded, from 1834 tall spires like a staff; e.g. Hypericum calycinum, Verbascum thapsus, Thermopsis; metaphor from body and staff to plant.
  • Figurative senses — Aaronic: Aaronic blessing May the Lord bless you from Numbers 6; Aaronic order in Latter-day Saint priesthood; Aaron's serpent for rhetoric that swallows rivals.

The unity is MEDIATED AUTHORITY. Aaron never acts alone: he speaks for Moses, sacrifices for people, and inherits through sons. Secondary plant names borrow his beard and staff by visual fancy; secondary oaths are rarer than for Moses. Where Moses draws out and legislates, Aaron speaks well and reconciles — glad in heart when he sees his brother — which is why tradition makes brightness and eloquence the inner meaning of his otherwise obscure name.

In art Aaron is robed in blue, scarlet, and gold with bells and pomegranates, bearing censer or blossoming rod, always beside Moses: the visual shorthand for priesthood supporting prophecy, ritual sustaining word, and peace-making sustaining law.
