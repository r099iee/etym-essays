evasively is the adverb of evasive, and it describes doing something in a manner that slips aside from the point, refusing to meet a question, duty, or pursuer head-on. To answer evasively is to circle the question without landing on it, to act evasively is to keep one’s intentions hidden while appearing to cooperate, and to move evasively is to dodge literally, the way a ship weaves or a boxer sidesteps.

In the source sentence Frodo answers evasively when asked what he knows, offering only hints and riddles. He is not lying outright, and that is the point. The adverb marks the moral middle ground Tolkien loves, a good person withholding truth under pressure, loyal to a secret he cannot share, polite enough not to refuse yet resolved not to tell, so the sentence itself sidesteps exactly as Frodo does.

Core meaning in detail: The stem is evade, from Latin for getting out of the way, and the suffixes build the character study step by step. Evasion names the act of getting out, evasive names the person or answer that practises it, and evasively names the manner, the how of the dodge. Scope is wide, from courtrooms to playgrounds, and tone is mildly disapproving, since honest people are expected to be direct, yet often sympathetic, since everyone understands the need to hide a pain, a plan, or a Ring.

All senses, primary and secondary:

  • Answering without answering, withholding the point (primary sense, Frodo’s sense): she replied evasively about the missing money. The bridge is the dodge made verbal, words deployed as a screen rather than a window, hints and riddles instead of facts.
  • Behaving so as to avoid commitment or responsibility (social sense): he smiled evasively when asked to volunteer. The bridge extends the verbal dodge to conduct, promises deferred, eyes lowered, shoulders turned, the whole body voting present while the will votes absent.
  • Moving so as to avoid capture or attack (physical sense): the pilot flew evasively through the flak, the hare ran evasively through the grass. The bridge returns to the Latin root of getting out of the way, now at speed, weaving and doubling back.
  • Glancing or looking aside to avoid contact (gestural sense): she looked at him evasively over her cup. The bridge narrows the dodge to the eyes alone, the oldest signal of shame, fear, or secrecy in the human repertoire.
  • Legally and politically stonewalling (institutional sense): the minister answered evasively at the inquiry. The bridge carries the private dodge into public life, where evasion becomes strategy, talking clocks out and truth is filibustered.

Register and connotation: evasively is cool, watchful, and faintly censorious, the word of journalists, judges, parents, and narrators who notice what is not being said. It rarely praises, yet in Tolkien it protects, Frodo’s evasion being loyalty rather than deceit. The adverb always implies an audience kept waiting, a question hanging unanswered in the air between speaker and hearer.
