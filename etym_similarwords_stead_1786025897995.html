• Place: Similar because both refer to the position or role occupied by another; differs because place is the universal, modern spatial and functional word, whereas stead is an archaic, literary, and formal noun restricted to fixed substitution idioms (e.g., 'in his stead').
  • Room: Similar because both historically denoted physical and functional space occupied; differs because room in substitution phrases (e.g., 'in his room') is largely obsolete in modern English, whereas in his stead remains active in formal and literary registers.
  • Behalf: Similar because both relate to representing another; differs because on behalf of means for the interest, benefit, or as the representative of someone, while in someone's stead specifically denotes replacing them in their actual physical post, role, or duty.
  • Proxy: Similar because both involve substitution; differs because a proxy is the specific authorized agent, document, or legal vote cast for another, whereas stead denotes the role or position itself.
  • Surrogate: Similar because both involve acting as a substitute; differs because a surrogate is a person or object appointed or engineered to take the place of another in a specific functional capacity.
  • Lieu: Similar because both are used in prepositional phrases meaning 'in place of'; differs because in lieu of is a loan from French used primarily in legal, administrative, and financial contexts (e.g., 'time off in lieu of overtime pay'), whereas in someone's stead is Germanic and more commonly applied to persons occupying roles.
