- 17th-century anatomical naming - Early neuroanatomists christen the cluster corpus striatum, “the striped body”, for its zebra-like alternation of grey cell bodies and white myelinated fibre bundles; purely descriptive, location-only status in the brain.
- 19th–early 20th century (clinicopathological neurology) - Folded into the basal ganglia and tied to motor disorders such as chorea and Parkinsonism, giving the striatum a strictly motor identity as a “movement centre”.
- Mid–late 20th century (neurochemistry and reward) - Dopamine research recasts it as a motivation- and reward-processing hub, especially the ventral striatum and nucleus accumbens, not merely a motor station.
- Contemporary (computational neuroscience) - Cast as the brain's prediction-error engine, issuing a micro-dose of dopamine when expectations are met; the striatum becomes familiar even outside neuroscience as the “Good job, you predicted right” reward circuit.
