• you: Similar because both function as second-person pronouns addressing an interlocutor; differs because you is the universal modern standard pronoun (singular and plural, subject and object), whereas thou is strictly archaic/dialectal, singular, and nominative.
  • thee: Similar because both belong to the exact same second-person singular pronominal paradigm; differs because thee is the objective case (accusative/dative) form ("I see thee"), whereas thou is the nominative subject form ("thou art").
  • ye: Similar because both are older English second-person subject pronouns; differs because ye was historically the second-person plural nominative pronoun ("ye shall know the truth") and formal singular, whereas thou is exclusively singular and informal/intimate.
  • thine: Similar because both share the same second-person singular root; differs because thine is a possessive pronoun or archaic possessive determiner before vowels ("thine eye"), expressing ownership rather than subject agency.
  • thy: Similar because both derive from the same Germanic second-person base; differs because thy functions as a possessive adjective preceding consonants ("thy hand"), whereas thou functions as an independent clause subject.
  • one: Similar because both can refer to an individual human subject; differs because one is an impersonal, generalized third-person pronoun ("one must try"), whereas thou is direct, personal, and second-person.
  • tha: Similar because it is the direct phonetic descendant and regional dialectal variant of thou in Northern English; differs because tha is a contemporary regional vernacular form rather than classical literary English.
