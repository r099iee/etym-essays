• Cauldron: Similar because both are large metallic vessels used historically to boil water and liquids over an open fire; differs because a cauldron is typically a massive, spoutless, open-topped hemispherical pot suspended by chains or resting on three iron feet (often associated with witchcraft, brewing stews, or medieval feasting), whereas a kettle is smaller, enclosed, and specifically fitted with a pouring spout.
  • Pot: Similar because both are common kitchen vessels used for cooking and heating liquids; differs because a pot is a broad, flat-bottomed, open-mouthed cooking container designed primarily for solid and liquid food preparation (soups, sauces, boiling vegetables), whereas a kettle is specialized for heating and pouring water.
  • Teapot: Similar because both are vessels with a handle, lid, and spout used in tea preparation; differs because a teapot is an infusing vessel made of ceramic, porcelain, or glass where tea leaves steep in already-boiled water, whereas a kettle is a heating vessel placed upon a heat source to bring cold water to a boil.
  • Urn: Similar because both are vessels designed to supply hot water or tea for beverages; differs because an urn (especially an electric hot water urn or samovar) is a large, stationary, high-capacity cylindrical tank equipped with a dispensing spigot or tap at the bottom, whereas a kettle is a portable vessel lifted and tilted by hand.
  • Saucepan: Similar because both are stovetop vessels used to heat liquids and food; differs because a saucepan has a single long straight handle, a flat base, and an open top without a fixed dedicated pouring spout, designed for stirring sauces and cooking food rather than cleanly pouring boiling water.
  • Samovar: Similar because both are metal containers used for heating water to brew tea; differs because a samovar is a traditional Russian and Middle Eastern metal urn containing a central internal vertical pipe or charcoal firebox, equipped with a tap and a top ring to hold a teapot, whereas a kettle is an externally or bottom-heated vessel.
  • Boiler: Similar because both are devices designed to heat water to high temperatures; differs because a boiler is typically a heavy industrial, domestic heating system, or closed mechanical apparatus providing steam or hot water for central heating and power generation, whereas a kettle is a small portable kitchen utensil.
