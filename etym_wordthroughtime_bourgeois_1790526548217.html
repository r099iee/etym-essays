- 1560s–1650s: A citizen or freeman of a fortified French borough (bourg) holding legal municipal privileges - neutral civic and legal descriptor of urban burghers.
- late 17th–18th c.: The emerging mercantile and professional urban middle class distinct from both feudal nobility and manual peasantry - recognized as the economic engine of trade and commerce.
- 1789–1848: The revolutionary French third estate that overturned feudal privileges - briefly associated with political reform, Enlightenment liberalism, and constitutionalism.
- mid-19th c.–20th c. (Marxist Critique): The ruling capitalist class owning the means of production, juxtaposed against the proletariat - acquired severe political, ideological, and socio-economic opprobrium.
- 20th c.–Present (Cultural & Aesthetic): Pejorative descriptor for mediocre, conformist, conservative, materialistic tastes and conventional philistinism ('bourgeois morality', 'bourgeois tastes').
