- Blood relation phrase - of kin, a kin means sharing parents or clan; concrete genealogy, legal and familial weight.
- Fused adjective of kinship - By 1600 akin as one word still means blood-related, used for cousins and in-laws; perception is factual and domestic.
- Metaphorical broadening - 17th-18th centuries extend to ideas, passions, languages akin in spirit; now signals intellectual kinship, admired as insightful comparison.
- Academic relation marker - 19th-20th centuries become staple of history, biology, linguistics for common descent; connotation is scholarly and evolutionary, as in Durant.
- Modern figurative staple - Today overwhelmingly means similar in feeling or nature; literal blood sense fades, leaving a formal elegant synonym for similar-with-depth.
