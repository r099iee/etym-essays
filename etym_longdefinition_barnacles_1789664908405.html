BARNACLES are small sessile marine crustaceans of infraclass Cirripedia that cement themselves permanently to hard surfaces such as rocks, piers, whale skin and the hulls of boats, forming hard calcareous shells. They feed by sweeping feathery cirri through water to filter plankton. To landsmen they are emblems of stubborn adhesion, unsightly encrustation and slow accumulation: once settled, a barnacle cannot move, and hulls left uncleaned gather colonies that increase drag and require scraping.

In the sentence myths and half-truths have added themselves to the Baker story like ______ catching on the hull of a boat, the literal animal becomes a precise literary simile. Baker biography, lacking hard fact because its subject was reclusive, has accreted parasitic stories the way a hull accretes crustaceans. The comparison implies passivity of the hull, opportunism of the settlers, gradual uglification, and the labour needed to scrape truth clean.

  • Primary sense: the animal. Acorn barnacles Balanus and Semibalanus on intertidal rocks, goose barnacles Lepas with fleshy stalks on driftwood and hulls. Lifecycle includes free-swimming nauplius and cypris larvae that glue themselves headfirst and metamorphose into sessile adults, a celebrated Darwinian study. Semantic core is tenacious clinging filter-feeder with shell plates.
  • Secondary sense: metaphor of encrustation and parasitism. To call rumours barnacles is to say they are sticky, secondary, hard to remove and extrinsic to the true hull. Organisations gather bureaucratic barnacles, reputations gather anecdotal barnacles, texts gather exegetical barnacles. Bridge: functional analogy from marine fouling to informational fouling, preserving stickiness, accretion over time and need for scraping.
  • Tertiary senses and idioms. To cling like a barnacle is to cling obnoxiously to person or place; barnacle-brained is dense and encrusted. In naval history, careening and dry-docking exist largely to remove barnacles and weed. Darwin 1851-1854 monographs made barnacle a byword for painstaking taxonomy. Bridge: behavioural analogy extends zoology into character judgement and institutional critique.
  • Why this metaphor fits Baker. Baker left few letters, gave no interviews and died childless; biographers face a clean, absent hull. Speculation about his sexuality, illness, driving and death rushes into the vacuum, each retelling adding another plate. Like hull fouling, the process is incremental, underwater and unnoticed until the boat slows. The image also faintly echoes the barnacle-goose myth, itself a medieval half-truth about spontaneous generation, doubling the theme of myth-making.

Learning barnacles therefore means holding both zoology and rhetoric together: picture the white volcano-like shell on a rock, feel its razor edge, then transfer tenacity and accretion to stories that will not let go of a life.
