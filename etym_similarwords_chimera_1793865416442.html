• Illusion: Similar because both refer to something that is perceived or imagined but does not exist in reality; differs because an illusion can be an optical trick, a sensory misperception, or an erroneous belief rooted in real sensory stimuli, whereas a chimera is an elaborate, wildly improbable conceptual fabrication or unattainable longing.
  • Delusion: Similar because both involve believing in something false; differs because a delusion is a persistent, fixed false belief held despite incontrovertible proof to the contrary, carrying a clinical psychiatric pathology, whereas a chimera is a fanciful, poetic, or utopian pipe dream rather than a psychiatric illness.
  • Phantasm: Similar because both denote mental fabrications without external physical reality; differs because a phantasm evokes a spectral, ghostlike apparition or shadowy visual hallucination of the mind, whereas a chimera evokes an incongruous, monstrous composite of disparate, irreconcilable ideas.
  • Mirage: Similar because both describe an enticing but unattainable vision; differs because a mirage is a literal physical atmospheric phenomenon (refraction of light through heated air) that symbolizes deceptive hopes that vanish upon closer approach, whereas a chimera is entirely a conceptual fabrication from the outset.
  • Pipe dream: Similar because both denote an impossible, unrealistic hope or ambition; differs because pipe dream is an informal, colloquial American idiom originating from opium-smoking hallucinations, whereas chimera is an elevated, literary, and classical term with profound mythological and scientific depth.
  • Figment: Similar because both exist purely within the imagination; differs because a figment (as in 'a figment of the imagination') is typically a fleeting, trivial, or petty suspicion, whereas a chimera is an elaborate, grand, or monstrous intellectual construct.
  • Mosaic: Similar because both describe biological organisms containing distinct cellular populations; differs because in genetics, a mosaic organism arises from a single fertilized zygote through subsequent mutation or chromosomal non-disjunction, whereas a genetic chimera arises from the combination of two or more distinct zygotes or embryos.
  • Daydream: Similar because both involve imaginative reverie; differs because a daydream is a pleasant, conscious, and harmless indulgence of romantic or aspirational thoughts, whereas a chimera involves pursuing an impossible, incongruous goal that leads to futile waste and disillusionment.
