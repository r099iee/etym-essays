• Scowl: Similar because both are expressions of displeasure on the face; differs because a scowl is a dark, aggressive, often threat-filled grimace of anger, while a frown is the milder, brows-focused expression of displeasure, worry, or thought.
  • Glower: Similar because both express hostility on the face; differs because glowering implies an intense, fixed, angry stare, while frowning centers on knitting the brows without necessarily staring.
  • Grimace: Similar because both involve contorting the face; differs because a grimace is a twisted, exaggerated distortion expressing pain, disgust, or effort, while a frown is a relatively controlled contraction of the brow.
  • Glare: Similar because both are looks of anger or hostility; differs because a glare is directed through the eyes — a fierce, fixed stare — while a frown is a brow expression that need not involve staring at anyone.
  • Knit the brows: Similar because both name the same contraction of the brow muscles; differs because 'knit the brows' is a phrase emphasizing concentration or puzzled thought as much as displeasure, while 'frown' leans toward disapproval.
  • Pout: Similar because both are wordless expressions of displeasure; differs because a pout involves pushing the lips forward (childish sulking or flirtation), while a frown involves only the brows.
  • Lower / lour: Similar because both can mean to look sullen or threatening; differs because to lower is to look dark and menacing (also of sky and weather), a heavier, more ominous word than frown.
  • Moue: Similar because both are wordless facial signals of distaste; differs because a moue is a delicate French-loaned pout or grimace of the lips, whereas a frown is a brows-focused expression of English currency.
