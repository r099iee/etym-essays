• Volumes: Similar because it denotes bound books; differs because volumes is a neutral bibliographical term, whereas tomes highlights massive size, weight, and scholarly or arcane authority.
  • Codices: Similar because it denotes ancient bound books; differs because codices specifies hand-written manuscript books as opposed to scrolls, whereas tomes emphasizes imposing physical size and weight.
  • Grimoires: Similar because it denotes magical books; differs because grimoires focuses on spell formulas and demonology, whereas tomes describes the physical volume itself.
  • Folios: Similar because it denotes large-format books; differs because folios names a specific printing format (sheets folded once), whereas tomes stresses heaviness and learned weight regardless of format.
  • Pushed: Similar because it means applying force; differs because pushed is general, whereas thrust implies sudden, forceful, stabbing, or jamming motion.
  • Shoved: Similar because it means rough pushing; differs because shoved implies clumsy or rude movement, whereas thrust implies directed, sudden, or piercing drive.
  • Jammed: Similar because it means forcing something in tightly; differs because jammed implies wedging with friction, whereas thrust implies a sudden directed push.
  • Propelled: Similar because it means driving forward; differs because propelled is mechanical and continuous, whereas thrust is sudden and hand- or blade-driven (and is the technical noun for propulsive force).
  • Plunged: Similar because it means driving forcibly into something; differs because plunged emphasizes descent into a substance, whereas thrust emphasizes directed horizontal force.
  • Stabbed: Similar because it denotes a sudden piercing drive; differs because stabbed is confined to blades and wounds, whereas thrust extends to shelves, rockets, and arguments.
  • Pell-mell: Similar because it denotes confused, disorderly haste; differs because pell-mell emphasizes confused crowding of people or things, whereas helter-skelter emphasizes disorderly scattering and tumbling headlong motion.
  • Topsy-turvy: Similar because it denotes disorder; differs because topsy-turvy emphasizes upside-down inversion, whereas helter-skelter emphasizes chaotic motion and hurry.
  • Haphazardly: Similar because it means without order or plan; differs because haphazardly stresses lack of plan and randomness, whereas helter-skelter stresses headlong speed and tumbling disorder.
  • Hurly-burly: Similar because it is a rhyming-reduplication word for noisy disorder; differs because hurly-burly emphasizes noisy confusion and uproar, whereas helter-skelter emphasizes rushing, tumbling motion.
  • Willy-nilly: Similar because it denotes disorderly or indiscriminate action; differs because willy-nilly stresses being forced or happening whether one likes it or not, whereas helter-skelter stresses headlong physical scramble.
