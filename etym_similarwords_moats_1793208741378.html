• Ditch: Similar because a ditch is likewise a long excavated trench in the ground; differs because ditch is the plain everyday word for drainage or boundary channels, while moat is specifically defensive and architectural, ringing something worth protecting.
  • Trench: Similar because both are deep defensive excavations; differs because a trench is a narrow linear shelter that soldiers fight from, while a moat is a broad empty barrier that attackers must cross, with nobody stationed inside it.
  • Fosse: Similar because a fosse is an ancient defensive ditch, the Roman ancestor of the moat; differs because fosse belongs to archaeology and ancient history such as Roman camps and hill forts, while moat belongs to medieval castles and living metaphor.
  • Ha-ha: Similar because a ha-ha is a sunken boundary ditch that bars passage while preserving the view, much like a dry moat; differs because the ha-ha serves polite landscape gardening by keeping livestock off the lawn unseen, while the moat serves war.
  • Dike: Similar because dikes are water-engineered earthworks of embankment and channel; differs because a dike holds water back or drains land for farming, while a moat holds attackers out for defence — opposite purposes dug with similar spades.
  • Canal: Similar because great wet moats are water-filled channels crossed by bridges; differs because a canal is built for transport and navigation from place to place, while a moat goes nowhere — it circles, guarding rather than conveying.
  • Gully: Similar because the source sentence's moats are essentially gully-like folds in the ground; differs because a gully is carved by rainfall and erosion with no human purpose, while a true moat is dug on purpose, to a plan.
  • Ravine: Similar because both are deep steep-sided hollows that stop travellers; differs because a ravine is a large natural valley worn by water over ages, while a moat is artificial, straight-sided, and comparatively narrow.
