• ask: Similar because it denotes posing a question to gain information; differs because it is neutral, casual and general, while inquire is formal, polite and often procedural.
  • enquire: Similar because in British English it is the same verb for seeking information; differs because convention reserves enquire for general asking and inquire for formal investigation, though the distinction is fading.
  • question: Similar because it means to interrogate or elicit answers; differs because it can imply doubt, challenge or cross-examination, while inquire is neutral and courteous.
  • interrogate: Similar because it means to ask systematically; differs because it implies intense, often hostile or official grilling, while inquire implies polite or neutral investigation.
  • investigate: Similar because to inquire into something is to examine it methodically; differs because investigate emphasizes evidence-gathering and detective work rather than simply asking.
  • probe: Similar because it means to examine deeply; differs because it suggests penetrating, persistent or pointed inquiry, often journalistic or medical, with more intensity.
  • query: Similar because it means to ask or raise a question about accuracy; differs because it is more technical and written, often about data, bookings or doubts, and shorter in scope.
  • request: Similar because an inquiry often frames a polite request for information; differs because request denotes asking for something to be given or done, not only for knowledge.
  • explore: Similar because intellectual inquiry explores a topic; differs because explore is broader and metaphorical, suggesting open searching rather than direct questioning.
  • delve into: Similar because it means to examine a subject in depth; differs because it is idiomatic and suggests deep research rather than asking a person.
  • examine: Similar because a formal inquiry examines facts; differs because examine stresses close inspection or testing, not necessarily by question and answer.
