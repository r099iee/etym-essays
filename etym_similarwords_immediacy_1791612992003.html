• Directness: Similar because both denote an absence of intervening agents, detours, or intermediaries; differs because directness often emphasizes a straightforward, blunt, or unswerving path of action, speech, or connection, whereas immediacy highlights the sensory vividness, presentness, and temporal urgency of an experience.
  • Instantaneity: Similar because both involve the absence of delay; differs because instantaneity refers strictly to the temporal speed of something occurring in a single flash or microsecond, whereas immediacy encompasses deep sensory presence, emotional connection, and unmediated perception.
  • Presence: Similar because both evoke the feeling of being intensely in the here and now; differs because presence focuses on the commanding physical or charismatic aura of a person or object in space, while immediacy emphasizes the lack of conceptual filters separating the observer from the phenomenon.
  • Spontaneity: Similar because both convey an uncalculated, natural freshness of feeling; differs because spontaneity refers to actions performed on impulse without premeditation, whereas immediacy is an objective condition of directness or an intense quality of perception.
  • Proximity: Similar because both denote close connection without a wide gap; differs because proximity refers primarily to spatial or temporal physical closeness, whereas immediacy describes an unmediated psychological, phenomenological, or aesthetic impact.
  • Vividness: Similar because both describe impressions that strike the mind with sharp clarity and life; differs because vividness focuses on the bright, distinct, and picturesque qualities of imagery or memory, while immediacy underscores the sense that the event is unfolding directly before one's eyes.
  • Urgency: Similar because both convey a pressing demand for attention; differs because urgency arises from high stakes, danger, or tight deadlines that compel prompt response, whereas immediacy is a structural or experiential state of direct contact that may or may not involve peril.
