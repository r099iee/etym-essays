*wer- / *wōr- (PIE, reconstructed; the link is DEBATED)
"to tread down, wear out, exhaust"
(no secure non-Germanic cognate is agreed)

▼

*wōrigaz (Proto-Germanic, reconstructed)
"tired, worn out, exhausted"

▼

wērig (Old English, attested from the 8th c.)
"weary, tired, exhausted, dispirited"
(used in Beowulf and the Old English elegies)

▼

wery / weri (Middle English, 13th c.)
"tired in body or mind; causing fatigue;
impatient, dissatisfied"

▼

weary (Early Modern English, 16th-17th c.)
"exhausted; tired of something"
(transitive verb "to exhaust" develops c. 1600)

▼

WEARY (modern English)
"feeling or showing extreme tiredness, exhaustion,
or impatience resulting from hard work, stress,
or prolonged exposure"

NOTE ON DEBATED ORIGIN: the pre-Germanic ancestry of *wōrigaz
is not secure. The traditional link to *wer- "to tread/wear
down" is plausible and widely cited, but Kroonen and other
modern comparativists treat the West Germanic form as of
unclear Indo-European etymology; no attested cognate
outside Germanic settles the question.
