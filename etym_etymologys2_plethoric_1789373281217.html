PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*pleh₁-
"to fill, be full, abound"
↓
Proto-Hellenic (c. 2000–1200 BCE)
*plē- / *plēthos
"multitude, crowd, great quantity"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th–5th c. BCE)
plḗthein (πλήθειν) ("to be full, swell, teem") / plêthos (πλῆθος) ("great number, mass")
"to be full, teem"
↓
Ancient Greek (Hippocratic / Galenic Medicine) (c. 5th–2nd c. BCE)
plēthṓrē (πληθώρη)
"fullness, satiety, abnormal excess of blood or bodily humors"
↓
Late Latin / Medical Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
plethōra
"superabundance of blood in the vascular channels"
↓
Medical Latin / Middle French (c. 16th c.)
plethoricus / pléthorique
"affected by a plethora, congested with blood, overfilled"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1620–1660)
plethoric / plethorical
"having a fullness of bodily vessels; bloated with superabundance"
↓
