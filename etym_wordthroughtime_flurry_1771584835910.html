- [17th century: a gust of wind] - 'Flurry' first names a sudden, brief rush of wind, often carrying snow - a squall-like gust; connotation is meteorological, transient, and weather-flecked.
- [18th-19th century: from weather to bustle] - The word broadens to any sudden commotion of activity - a flurry of work, a flurry of excitement; connotation becomes human and social: an agitated but passing moment, scattered and many-parted.
- [Contemporary sense] - A short, scattered burst of activity, excitement, or snow; connotation is busy but brief - many small things happening at once, like snowflakes driven by a gust, then gone as suddenly as they came.
