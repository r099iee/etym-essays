Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*steip- / *steyp-
"to be stiff, packed close, compressed, pointed post"
(source of Latin stīpāre "to pack tight", stipes "tree trunk, post", English stiff)
↓
Old Latin / Italic Adjective (c. 4th–3rd c. BCE)
stipulus
"firm, solid, stable, fixed"
(linguistic note: Roman grammarians derived it from stipula "straw", claiming a ritual breaking of straw sealed contracts; modern philologists reject this folk etymology in favor of stipulus "firm")
↓
Classical Latin Deponent Verb (3rd c. BCE–1st c. CE)
stipulārī / stipulor
"to exact a formal verbal promise, bargain solemnly under law"
↓
Latin Past Participle (classical period)
stipulātus
"having bargained, having exacted a covenant"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1620s)
stipulate
"to demand as an indispensable condition in an agreement"
↓
Modern English (present)
STIPULATE
"to specify a requirement; insist on conditions in a contract"
