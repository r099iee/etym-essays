PIE *wen- 'to desire, strive for, love' - Latin venus 'love, desire; loveliness, beauty, charm' (genitive veneris; classical Latin — the noun underlying the goddess's name) - Latin venerari 'to worship, revere, hold in awe' (denominative verb built on venus; classical Latin) - Latin venerabilis 'worthy of reverence or respect' (adjective in -bilis, 'worthy of'; classical to late Latin, applied to elders, church fathers, and holy things) - Old French venerable 'revered, esteemed' (learned borrowing from Latin) - Middle English venerable 'worthy of deep respect; of Christ, worthy of worship' (c. 1400) - Modern English venerable 'commanding great respect; hallowed' — in general use from the late 15th century; also the ecclesiastical title 'the Venerable'
