- **Ancient Greece (Homeric Era)** - The name 'Achilles' referred to the preeminent warrior of the Achaeans, a figure of immense strength, pride, and tragic fate, whose story was central to the *Iliad*. His character evoked awe for his prowess and pity for his doomed glory.
- **Classical Antiquity (Post-Homeric)** - The legend of his vulnerable heel gained prominence, transforming 'Achilles' into a symbol of powerful strength undermined by a single, specific weakness. The tragic aspect of his character deepened, emphasizing the inevitability of fate.
- **Renaissance to 19th Century** - 'Achilles' continued to represent the archetypal warrior hero, but the 'Achilles' heel' idiom began to solidify in literary and common usage, signifying a critical vulnerability. The focus shifted from the hero himself to the universal concept of a fatal flaw.
- **20th Century to Present** - The name 'Achilles' is primarily recognized through the idiom 'Achilles' heel,' which is widely used in everyday language, business, and politics to denote a crucial weakness. The mythological figure remains a cultural touchstone, but his name's most common contemporary usage is metaphorical, representing the inherent fragility even in great strength.
