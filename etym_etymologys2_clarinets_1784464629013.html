=== ROOT WORD: CLARINET (single root, French instrument-name) === Stage 1 — PIE *kel- / *kla- (sometimes set up as *kelh- or *kleu-) ‘to shout, call, resound’ (reconstructed; also behind Latin calāre ‘to call, summon’ and clamāre ‘to shout’) — root shape debated, see note Stage 2 — Latin clārus ‘clear, bright, loud, distinct, famous’ (classical Latin; from the notion of something that rings out clearly) Stage 3 — Old French clair ‘clear, bright’ and clarine ‘little bell; high clear-sounding signal instrument’ (medieval French) Stage 4 — French clarinette ‘little clarine; the single-reed woodwind’ (diminutive -ette; early 18th c. — the instrument was developed c. 1690–1700 in Central European workshops, associated with Johann Christoph Denner of Nuremberg, under a French-style name) Stage 5 — English clarinet ‘the single-reed cylindrical woodwind’ (borrowed from French; first recorded 1732–1733) Stage 6 — English clarinet + plural -s → clarinets => clarinets (modern English plural noun). DEBATE / NUANCE: The PIE root is usually given as *kel- ‘to call/shout’, but some reconstructions set it up as *kelh- or *kleu-; the Latin and Romance history is secure. The instrument itself matured in German-speaking lands even though the name is French — a cultural wrinkle, not an etymological one. The plural is a straightforward English inflection of the borrowed noun.
