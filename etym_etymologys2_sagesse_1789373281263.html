PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*seh₁p- / *sep-
"to taste, perceive; have flavor"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*sapiō
"to taste, be sensible, perceive"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
sapere
"to taste of; have discernment, be wise"
↓
Classical / Imperial Latin (c. 1st–4th c. CE)
sapidus
"savory, tasty; (fig.) discerning, wise"
Note: Developed figurative sense of 'judicious, wise' in Late Latin (e.g. Ausonius).
↓
Vulgar / Spoken Latin (c. 5th–7th c.)
*sabius (or *sapius)
"wise, prudent, sensible"
Note: Romance alteration of sapidus influenced by sapere, with intervocalic betacism/voicing.
↓
Old French (c. 10th–11th c.)
sage + -esse
"wise, prudent" + "abstract state suffix"
Note: Formed from adjective sage with abstract noun suffix -esse (from Latin -itia).
↓
Old / Middle French (c. 1100 – 14th c.)
sagesse
"wisdom, prudence, sound judgment"
↓
Modern English (Loanword) (present)
SAGESSE
