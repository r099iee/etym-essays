• scattered: Similar because both describe items distributed loosely and irregularly over an area; differs because scattered emphasizes separation, distance between individual elements, or flight, whereas strewn highlights the physical surface upon which items lie layered.
  • dispersed: Similar because both indicate the spreading out of elements across a wider space; differs because dispersed is a more formal, analytical term frequently applied to crowds, gases, or abstract populations thinning out into oblivion.
  • littered: Similar because both describe objects lying casually or untidily across a ground or floor; differs because littered carries a strongly negative, messy connotation of unwanted trash, neglect, and unkempt clutter, whereas strewn can describe beautiful festive petals or starry skies.
  • broadcast: Similar because both involve scattering particles or objects broadly across a field; differs because broadcast is an agricultural or technological verb emphasizing the active outward casting motion (of seeds or radio waves) rather than the resulting resting state.
  • sprinkled: Similar because both refer to loose items distributed across a surface; differs because sprinkled denotes tiny, fine droplets or minimal, sparse particles applied lightly and delicately rather than voluminous coverage.
  • carpeted: Similar because both depict a surface covered by a layer of materials (such as autumn leaves or moss); differs because carpeted implies a dense, uniform, unbroken, and thick textile-like blanket, whereas strewn implies loose, intermittent, or irregular dispersal.
  • dappled: Similar because both describe surfaces exhibiting varied visual coverage; differs because dappled refers strictly to optical patterns of rounded spots, sunlight, or shadow rather than physical objects physically distributed upon the ground.
  • stippled: Similar because both suggest discrete points covering a backdrop; differs because stippled is a technical fine-art term referring to intentional patterns of fine dots, flecks, or engraver's marks.
  • peppered: Similar because both describe random distribution across an area; differs because peppered implies sharp, stinging impacts or sudden, aggressive distributions (such as shrapnel, gunshot, or relentless questions).
  • disseminated: Similar because both signify the spreading of elements over a territory; differs because disseminated is predominantly used for abstract diffusion—such as doctrines, ideas, news, or metastatic biological pathogens.
