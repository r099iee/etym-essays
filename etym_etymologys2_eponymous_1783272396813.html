Derivation chain for EPONYMOUS (single root word): 1. Proto-Indo-European *nō-mn- (or *h₃néh₃mn̥) "name" (reconstructed root; c. 4th millennium BCE; cognates Latin nōmen, English name, German Name, Sanskrit nā́man, Old Irish ainm). 2. Ancient Greek ónyma "name" (also ónoma in Attic; the ónyma form is Aeolic/Doric and later standard for the combining element). 3. Ancient Greek epṓnymos (ἐπώνυμος) "giving one's name to; named after" — epí "upon, after, in succession to" + ónyma "name" (attested from the 5th c. BCE, e.g. of the eponymous archon and eponymous heroes). 4. Late Latin eponymus "eponymous" — learned Latinization of the Greek adjective (attested in later/medieval Latin; rare). 5. English eponymous "giving one's name to; named after" — borrowed from Greek via Latin; the noun eponym is first attested 1844, the adjective eponymous in 1846 (in George Grote's History of Greece, the very work of the card's context). 6. English eponymous — modern form with the "self-titled" extension from the mid-20th century. Note: The root *nō-mn- "name" is one of the most securely reconstructed PIE roots, reflected in nearly every branch of the family; the Greek ónyma/ónoma variation is dialectal (Aeolic/Doric ónyma vs. Attic ónoma), and the combining form -onym (as in synonym, homonym, acronym) is the same Greek element.
