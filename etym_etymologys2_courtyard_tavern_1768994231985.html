COURTYARD — a compound of two elements
element 1: court
↓
PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*ǵʰer-
"to enclose"
↓
Latin (2nd c. BCE)
cohors, cohortis
"an enclosed courtyard, a retinue"
↓
Old French (9th c. CE)
cort
"a court, a courtyard"
↓
Middle English (12th c. CE)
court
"a court, a courtyard"

element 2: yard
↓
PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*gʰerdʰ-
"to enclose"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 1st–5th c. CE)
*gardaz
"an enclosure, a yard"
↓
Old English (c. 5th–11th c. CE)
geard
"an enclosure, a yard"
↓
English (11th c. CE)
yard
"a yard, an enclosure"

compound: court + yard
↓
English (16th c. CE)
courtyard
"an unroofed area enclosed by walls"

TAVERN

taberna

taverne

↓
Middle English (13th c. CE)
tavern

Debate: the link of court to PIE *ǵʰer- is uncertain; and taberna may derive from trabs ("a hut of beams") — both debated.
