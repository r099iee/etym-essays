• Previously: Similar because both mean "before now"; differs because previously is neutral and common, while hitherto is formal and marks a firm boundary with the present.
  • So far: Similar because both mean "up to now"; differs because "so far" is informal and open-ended, while hitherto is formal and final in tone.
  • Up to now: Similar because both mean "until the present moment"; differs because "up to now" is neutral spoken English, while hitherto is bookish.
  • As yet: Similar because both relate to the present moment; differs because "as yet" is typically used with negatives ("as yet unproven"), while hitherto states positive prior fact.
  • Thus far: Similar because both are formal markers of past-to-present; differs because "thus far" is used in argumentative or narrative summing-up, while hitherto typically precedes a change or reversal.
  • Heretofore: Similar because both are archaic formal words for "until now"; differs because heretofore is even more legalistic and rarer.
  • Ere now: Similar in meaning "before this time"; differs because "ere now" is poetic and archaic, while hitherto is standard formal prose.
  • Before this: Similar because both locate something in the earlier period; differs because "before this" is plain and may refer to any earlier point, while hitherto means specifically up to the present.
  • Until recently: Similar because both push a state into the recent past; differs because "until recently" implies the state has now changed, while hitherto simply ends at now.
  • To date: Similar because both mean "up to the present"; differs because "to date" is used in commercial and reportorial summaries ("sales to date"), while hitherto is narrative and scholarly.
  • Up to this point: Similar because both draw a line at the present moment; differs because "up to this point" is conversational and can be spatial or temporal, while hitherto is strictly temporal and formal.
