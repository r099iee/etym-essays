• Glamdring: Similar because it is Orcrist's companion blade from the same troll-hoard, also forged in Gondolin and named in Elvish (it means "Foe-hammer"); differs because it belongs to Gandalf, appears again throughout The Lord of the Rings, and survives, whereas Orcrist is buried with Thorin.
  • Sting: Similar because it is the third blade of the hoard, Elvish-made, and famous for glowing when orcs are near; differs because it is a small dagger-like sword with a plain English name (chosen by Bilbo) rather than an Elvish one, and its fame belongs to Bilbo and Frodo.
  • Narsil / Andúril: Similar because both are ancient Elvish-forged blades of renown with meaningful names; differs because Narsil was forged for Elendil in the Elder Days, was broken and reforged as Andúril for Aragorn, and symbolizes the kingship of Gondor — a political symbol, where Orcrist is a purely martial relic.
  • Ringil: Similar because it is also a blade of Gondolin (Fingolfin's sword, whose name means "cold star"); differs because it belongs to the High King of the Noldor and is confined to Silmarillion lore rather than the novel's storyline.
  • Anglachel / Gurthang: Similar because it is a First-Age sword with a legendary epithet — Túrin's black blade, "Iron of Death"; differs because it was made by the Dark Elf Eöl from meteoric iron and is cursed and malevolent in the tale — the dark counterpart of the bright Gondolin blades.
  • Anguirel: Similar because it is the companion blade to Anglachel, also forged by Eöl; differs because it is marginal even within the legendarium — named once and never wielded in the story — so it lacks Orcrist's narrative presence.
  • Aeglos: Similar because it is a famous weapon of the Elder Days with a crisp Elvish name ("snow-thorn"); differs because it is a spear, not a sword, and belongs to Gil-galad, the last High King.
  • "Goblin-cleaver" itself: the English translation of the name; similar as a full paraphrase of Orcrist's meaning; differs because it is a description, not a name — it lacks the Elvish coinage, the runes, and the prestige.
  • Arondight: Similar as a real-world comparison — Lancelot's named sword in Arthurian romance, likewise an iconic blade with a poetic name; differs because it comes from medieval romance tradition, not from an invented language, and has no orc-slaying function.
