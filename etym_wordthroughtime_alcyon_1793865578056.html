- Homeric & Archaic Greek (c. 8th c. BCE) - Recorded in epic verse as ἀλκυών (alkyōn) without an initial aspirate; perceived as a sorrowful, solitary seabird whose plaintive cry symbolized enduring grief and marital fidelity.
- Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE) - Adopted by Virgil and Ovid directly as alcyōn; perceived as an emblem of sacred marital devotion, miraculous transformation, and the 14-day winter solstice maritime peace.
- Medieval Bestiaries & Scholasticism (c. 12th–14th c.) - Recorded as alcyon or alcyoun; allegorized in Christian moral treatises as a symbol of divine providence and spiritual calm amidst the tempestuous sea of worldly sin.
- Early Modern English & Renaissance Humanism (c. 1560–1650) - Championed by scholars and translators (e.g., Arthur Golding, George Chapman) who sought to restore pure classical orthography free of pseudo-etymological French corruptions.
- 18th Century to Modern Scientific Taxonomy - Shifted decisively from mythical ornithology into marine biology, naming the soft coral genus Alcyonium and the taxonomic order Alcyonacea.
