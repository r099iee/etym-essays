• mooring: Similar because it means securing a vessel in place; differs because it stresses tackle and anchors or buoys, while berthing stresses allotted space plus payment.
  • docking: Similar because it means bringing a vessel into dock; differs because it is the manoeuvre and harbour basin, narrower than berthing which includes bunks and marina contracts.
  • anchoring: Similar because it means securing by anchor; differs because it is open-water and tackle-specific, while berthing is usually pontoon or quay plus fees.
  • accommodation: Similar because berth can mean sleeping place; differs because it is generic lodging, not nautical.
  • billeting: Similar because it means assigning sleeping quarters, military; differs because it is personnel ashore, not vessels afloat.
  • housing: Similar because to berth can mean to house crew or vessels; differs because it is land-general and permanent.
  • parking: Similar because marina berthing is often glossed as parking the boat; differs because it is land-vehicle colloquial, and misses the sleeping-bunk sense.
  • wharfage: Similar because it is a harbour charge for lying alongside; differs because it is the fee for the wall itself, while berthing is the space and act.
  • dry-docking: Similar because it is a yard cost-item alongside outfitting and berthing; differs because it means lifting out for hull work, not ordinary mooring.
