• Niche: Similar because both denote a hollowed space in a wall; differs because niche is a shallow, usually decorative alcove, often for a statue, implying human design, while recesses are deeper and can be natural or merely functional.
  • Alcove: Similar because both are recessed spaces; differs because alcove is a larger, often vaulted and deliberately architectural recess, while recesses may be irregular natural hollows.
  • Cavity: Similar because both are hollowed spaces; differs because cavity is the neutral physical term for any hollow (teeth, body, rock), without architectural or hidden connotation, while recesses suggest depth and inaccessibility.
  • Hollow: Similar because both are empty spaces in a surface; differs because hollow is the plain generic word, while recesses implies a cut-back, often shadowy interior.
  • Bay: Similar because both are spaces set back between projections; differs because bay is specifically a space between pillars or columns, larger and architectural, while recesses is the general hollow.
  • Indentation: Similar because both are dents or set-backs in a surface; differs because indentation is a shallow impression made by pressure, while recesses are deeper spaces cut back from a plane.
  • Cove: Similar because both are sheltered hollows; differs because cove is a rounded, sheltering recess (a cove in a coastline or ceiling), while recesses emphasises hidden depth.
  • Cranny: Similar because both are small hidden gaps; differs because cranny is a small, narrow crack, while recesses are larger hollows or alcoves.
  • Nook: Similar because both are small sheltered corners; differs because nook is cosy, informal, and inviting, while recesses in the plural are dark, deep, and often menacing.
  • Corner: Similar because both are interior spaces away from the open; differs because corner is a neutral angle where surfaces meet, while recesses are hollows set back into a surface.
  • Cubbyhole: Similar because both are small enclosed spaces; differs because cubbyhole is a tiny, homely storage space, while recesses suggests architectural or natural hollows of depth.
  • Cell: Similar because both are small enclosed spaces; differs because cell implies a sealed or confined room, while recesses are open set-backs, not enclosed chambers.
  • Hideaway: Similar because both are places of concealment; differs because hideaway is a retreat chosen by a person, while recesses are hollows that exist independently and hide things by their depth.
  • Retreat: Similar because both derive from “going back” to a hidden place; differs because retreat is a deliberate refuge, while recesses is the physical or mental hollow itself.
  • Grotto: Similar because both are natural cave-like hollows; differs because grotto is a cave or artificial cave, usually with a specific character, while recesses are niches and set-backs in a surface.
  • Opening: Similar because both are gaps in a surface; differs because opening is an entrance or aperture into something, while recesses are blind hollows that do not necessarily lead anywhere.
