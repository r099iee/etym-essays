ROOT 1: su- (सु-)
PIE (reconstructed)
*h₁su- / *su-
"good, well, noble, healthy"
(source of Greek eu- "good, well", Celtic su-)
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (reconstructed)
*Hsu-
"good, well, excellent"
(cognate with Avestan hu- / hū- "good, well")
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1500 BCE; Rigveda)
su- (सु-)
"well, good, beautiful, noble, very, strongly"

ROOT 2: bāhu (बाहु)
PIE (reconstructed)
*bʰāǵʰu-s / *bʰeh₂ǵʰu-s
"arm, forearm"
(source of Greek pēkhus "forearm", English bough)
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (reconstructed)
*bʰaHȷ́ʰúš
"arm, forearm"
(cognate with Avestan bāzu- "arm")
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1500 BCE; Rigveda)
bāhu (बाहु)
"the arm, especially forearm; front leg; strength"

SYNTHESIS & COMPOUND MORPHOLOGY:
Vedic Sanskrit (Pāṇinian bahuvrīhi compound)
su- + bāhu → Subāhu
"having handsome or mighty arms; strong-armed"
↓
Epic Sanskrit (c. 500 BCE; Valmiki Ramayana)
Subāhu (सुबाहु)
"name of the rakshasa warrior slain by Rama's Agneyastra"
↓
Modern Indological Loan / English (19th c.–present)
Subāhu
"the demonic antagonist slain by Rama at Siddhāśrama"

Note on comparative morphology: The compound Subāhu is a precise morphological cognate to ancient Greek personal names formed with eu- + pēkhus (e.g. Eupēkhus, "fair-armed"), demonstrating the shared Indo-European heritage of constructing heroic patronymics and warrior epithets combining the particle "good/mighty" with bodily limbs.
