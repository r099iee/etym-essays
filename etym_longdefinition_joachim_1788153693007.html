Joachim — A masculine given name of Biblical Hebrew origin, meaning "Yahweh raises up" or "Yahweh establishes" (from Yəhō-, the divine name, and yāqīm, "he raises", hiphil of qūm "to rise, stand"). In the reading context the word is the given name of Joachim du Bellay (c. 1522–1560), the French Renaissance poet, member of the Pléiade, and author of the manifesto La Défense et illustration de la langue française (1549) and of the sonnet collections Les Regrets and Les Antiquitez de Rome.

The biblical name. The Hebrew Yəhōyāqīm was the name of a king of Judah (late 7th–early 6th c. BCE) who reigned when Babylon rose to supremacy, the father-pleaser of 2 Kings 23–24; the name appears in the Greek Old Testament as Iōakeim. In Christian tradition the Greek-Latin form Joachim became the name traditionally given to the father of the Virgin Mary (from early apocryphal gospels), making it a saint’s name of enormous medieval popularity.

Joachim du Bellay, the poet of the context. Born near Liré, Anjou, du Bellay came to Paris, joined the circle around Ronsard, and in 1549 published the Défense, which argued — against the Latinist scholars — that the French tongue could and should be enriched and made worthy of great poetry. Sent to Rome as secretary to his cousin the cardinal, he wrote Les Regrets (1558), whose sonnets of exile, homesickness, and sly satire are among the finest of the French Renaissance, and Les Antiquitez de Rome, meditations on the ruins of Rome. Pater’s essay makes du Bellay the type of the Renaissance’s "refined and comely decadence" — a late flowering of delicate sweetness.

  • Fame: with Ronsard, co-founder and active member of the Pléiade; the ablest prose-minds regard his sonnets as the summit of early French poetry.
  • Legacy: the Défense is a founding document of French literary modernism; Heureux qui, comme Ulysse remains one of the most anthologised poems in French.
  • Other famous bearers — Joachim of Fiore (12th-century Calabrian visionary abbot), Gioacchino Rossini (Italian composer), Joachim Murat (Napoleonic marshal), Saint Joachim (the Virgin’s father).
  • Modern use: rare but dignified as a first name across Europe; Joaquim (Portuguese), Gioacchino (Italian), Joaquín (Spanish), Akim (Russian) are cognate forms.

Du Bellay’s work in outline.La Défense et illustration de la langue française (1549) argues not for Latin imitation but for the illustration of French: borrowing, grafting, and exercise to make the vernacular rich enough for great poetry — the founding text of the Pléiade programme. Les Regrets (1558), written in Rome, blends the sombre sonnet of exile with satirical portraits of curial life and the famous homesick lyric Heureux qui, comme Ulysse, a fait un beau voyage. Les Antiquitez de Rome sets the ruins of the imperial city against the enduring art made from them — a theme Pater’s Renaissance continues. His early death in 1560, at about thirty-seven, cut short a career that had already fixed the shape of French sonnet practice for two centuries.

Pronunciation and forms. In English the name is commonly /ˈdʒoʊəkɪm/ (JO-uh-kim); in French, Joachim du Bellay is /ʒɔ.a.ʃɛ̃/ + dy bɛlɛ/. The spelling has been stable since Middle French; the chief cognate forms are the Italian Gioacchino, Spanish Joaquín, Portuguese Joaquim, and Russian Akim.
