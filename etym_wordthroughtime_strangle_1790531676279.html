- 5th c. BCE Ancient Greek strangalein - referred specifically to execution, murder, or wrestling combat by twisting cords or squeezing hands around the neck; physical and lethal
- 1st c. BCE-1st c. CE Classical Latin strangulare - codified in Roman law and medicine for homicidal throttling and respiratory choking, with early poetic metaphors of weeds choking crops; legal and forensic
- 1300s Middle English stranglen - borrowed via Anglo-Norman for murder, hunting deaths (Chaucer), and capital punishment by the halter; vivid and visceral
- 1500s-1600s Shakespearean & Early Modern expansion - expanded into figurative realms of smothering light (Macbeth), silencing voices, and suppressing nascent love or rebellion; poetic and dramatic
- 1790s-1800s Counter-Revolutionary & Napoleonic rhetoric - widely used in political discourse to denote violently crushing rebellions and "strangling the hydra of revolution" (Tolstoy, Burke); ideological and intense
- 1900s-present Modern multidisciplinary use - standard in criminal forensics, botanical parasitism (strangler fig), and economic commentary (strangling growth or supply chains); systemic and emphatic
