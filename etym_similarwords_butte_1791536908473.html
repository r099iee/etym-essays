• Mesa: Similar because both are isolated, steep-sided, flat-topped geological formations created by the differential erosion of horizontal sedimentary strata in arid environments; differs because a mesa represents an earlier erosional stage with a broader summit surface area whose width is substantially greater than its vertical cliff height, whereas a butte has eroded into a narrower remnant whose top diameter is smaller than or equal to its vertical relief.
  • Plateau: Similar because both feature elevated, relatively flat terrain bounded by sheer escarpments or cliffs; differs because a plateau is a vast, regional-scale upland extending across thousands of square kilometers, whereas a butte is an isolated, localized remnant hill left behind after the surrounding plateau has been dissected by millennia of erosion.
  • Knoll: Similar because both denote prominent small hills rising above surrounding lower terrain; differs because a knoll is typically a gentle, rounded, low hillock formed by varied glacial, volcanic, or fluvial processes and often covered in soil and vegetation, whereas a butte is characterized by sheer vertical rock cliffs and a distinct flat caprock summit.
  • Pinnacle: Similar because both are isolated, dramatic rock formations produced by advanced weathering and erosion of tablelands; differs because a pinnacle represents the terminal stage of erosion where the protective flat caprock has completely worn away, leaving a sharp, pointed, needle-like spire or tower rather than a flat table.
  • Escarpment: Similar because both feature sheer vertical rock cliffs formed by tectonic faulting or differential erosion; differs because an escarpment is a continuous, linear ridge or cliff face separating two distinct land surfaces of different elevations, whereas a butte is an entirely detached, freestanding solitary hill.
  • Bluff: Similar because both are steep, prominent cliffs overlooking a valley, river, or coastal plain; differs because a bluff is a broad, rounded, or precipitous headland often composed of soft sediments or river loam overlooking a watercourse, lacking the distinct flat caprock and isolated freestanding perimeter of a desert butte.
