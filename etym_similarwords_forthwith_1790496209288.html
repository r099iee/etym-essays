• immediately: Similar because it means at once with no delay; differs because immediately is the neutral everyday word, while forthwith is formal, legal, and commanding.
  • instantly: Similar because it denotes the very same instant; differs because instantly stresses temporal exactness, while forthwith stresses obedience and procedure.
  • at once: Similar because it means right away; differs because at once is conversational and plain, while forthwith is stiff and official.
  • directly: Similar because it means with no detour or interval (especially British I'll come directly); differs because directly retains a spatial sense of straightness, while forthwith is purely temporal-legal.
  • straightaway: Similar because it means without hesitation; differs because straightaway is colloquial and energetic, while forthwith is solemn and written.
  • promptly: Similar because it denotes quick willing action; differs because promptly praises alacrity, while forthwith commands it.
  • without delay: Similar because it is the standard paraphrase of forthwith in orders; differs because it is a transparent phrase, while forthwith is a single archaic lexical item carrying institutional weight.
  • pronto: Similar because it means very quickly, now; differs because pronto is slangy and military-jocular, while forthwith is dignified and judicial.
  • presently (archaic sense): Similar because in older English presently meant immediately; differs because modern presently has drifted to mean soon, while forthwith has never weakened.
  • instanter (legal Latinism): Similar because it is a lawyer's synonym meaning instantly; differs because instanter is an overt Latin borrowing used in writs, while forthwith is native Germanic dressed in legal robes.
