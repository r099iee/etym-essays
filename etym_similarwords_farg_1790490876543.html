• Glendearg (fictional): Similar because the child confuses Glen Farg with Scott's Glendearg by rhyme and scenery; differs because Glendearg is invented for The Monastery, while Glen Farg is the real Ochil valley.
  • glen: Similar because Glen Farg is one instance of the landform; differs because glen is the generic Gaelic valley-term, while Farg is the unique specific naming this one water.
  • Farg Water / River Farg: Similar because it is the burn that gives the glen its name; differs because the Water names the stream, while Farg in the card names the valley-shorthand.
  • Glenfarg (village): Similar because the village takes the glen's name; differs because the village is the settlement with church and houses, while the glen is the landform.
  • Ochil glen (e.g. Glendevon, Glenearn): Similar because they are neighbouring parallel valleys with burns and braes; differs because each has its own water-name and character, while Farg is this notch alone.
  • burn / streamlet: Similar because the Farg is the glen's streamlet where the White Lady supposedly dwelt; differs because burn is the Scots common noun, while Farg is the proper hydronym.
  • Loch Leven (contrast): Similar because both are Scott-tour localities on the same holiday (Abbot at Kinross, Monastery in Glen Farg); differs because Loch Leven is open water with a castle, while Farg is a narrow inland valley.
  • pass / notch / defile: Similar because Glen Farg functions as the road-pass through the Ochils; differs because these are generic route-terms, while Farg is the proper name of this pass.
  • White Lady's glen: Similar because the child believes she dwells by the Farg streamlet; differs because that role belongs fictionally to Glendearg, while Farg merely lends it scenery.
  • water-name / hydronym: Similar because Farg survives only as a water-name; differs because hydronym is the linguistic category, while Farg is the single obscure member.
