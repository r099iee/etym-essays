• retinue: Similar because both refer to a group of attendants accompanying a distinguished leader; differs because retinue has a strictly aristocratic, feudal, and historical character denoting servants and retainers under a lord's direct service, whereas entourage encompasses informal companions, advisors, media staff, and personal friends.
  • cortege: Similar because both denote a ceremonial procession of attendants; differs because cortege specifically implies a formal, solemn, and usually mournful procession (such as a funeral procession) moving in stately procession, whereas entourage is an active, living circle of companions in daily affairs.
  • suite: Similar because both refer to a staff of personal assistants and officials attending an important personage; differs because suite emphasizes the functional, administrative, and diplomatic hierarchy of assigned personnel (e.g., an ambassador's suite), whereas entourage carries a broader social, psychological, and protective presence.
  • train: Similar because both denote an extended following accompanying a leader during transit; differs because train is an older, elevated literary term highlighting physical linear procession and voluminous numbers of followers trailing behind, whereas entourage emphasizes an encircling, surrounding human ring.
  • escort: Similar because both provide companionship and physical accompaniment during journeys; differs because escort centers specifically on armed security, tactical protection, or ceremonial honor guards assigned for safety, whereas entourage includes personal friends, stylists, and informal advisors.
  • coterie: Similar because both describe an exclusive circle of associates; differs because coterie denotes a small, intimate clique bound by shared intellectual, artistic, or social interests without necessarily serving or traveling with a singular focal leader.
  • posse: Similar because both denote an accompanying band of loyal associates; differs because posse originated as a legal term for armed citizens deputized by a sheriff (*posse comitatus*) and survives today in colloquial slang for a tight-knit peer group, whereas entourage remains more formal and centered on a leader's status.
  • following: Similar because both describe admirers and adherents attached to a figure; differs because following is an abstract, dispersed collective of fans, supporters, or disciples who do not physically travel in the leader's immediate physical proximity.
  • camp: Similar because both represent the supportive network of a public figure; differs because camp refers to an ideological faction, political base, or campaign staff in a broader conceptual sense rather than a traveling personal escort.
  • hangers-on: Similar because both can describe people clustered around a wealthy or famous individual; differs because hangers-on is inherently pejorative, denoting parasitic sycophants seeking personal advantage, whereas entourage is fundamentally descriptive and includes essential professional aides.
