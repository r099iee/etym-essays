• Transcendental: Similar because both refer to realities beyond empirical sensory observation; differs because transcendental carries rigorous technical epistemological definitions (concerning the a priori mental conditions that structure experience), whereas supersensuous directly highlights the contrast between physical bodily senses and higher intellect/spirit.
  • Suprasensible: Similar as an exact philosophical synonym derived from identical conceptual roots; differs because suprasensible is favored in dry academic translations of Kantian critique, whereas supersensuous carries a richer, more aesthetic and poetic resonance favored by Romantic essayists.
  • Incorporeal: Similar because both describe entities lacking material bodily substance; differs because incorporeal is an ontological term signifying the physical absence of a body or flesh, whereas supersensuous is primarily an epistemological and perceptual boundary.
  • Metaphysical: Similar because both concern truths beyond physical nature; differs because metaphysical encompasses the entire systematic discipline of ontology and cosmology, while supersensuous specifically emphasizes the cognitive leap above sensory data.
  • Spiritual: Similar because both denote non-material, elevated domains; differs because spiritual is heavily freighted with religious devotion, sacred liturgy, and moral sanctity, whereas supersensuous can describe abstract mathematical realities, philosophical ideals, or aesthetic intuitions.
  • Intelligible: Similar because in Platonic philosophy the intelligible world stands opposed to the sensible world; differs because intelligible emphasizes the capacity to be grasped by pure reason, while supersensuous focuses on the transcendence over the sensory organs.
  • Sensuous (Antonym): The direct polar opposite, signifying that which appeals directly, richly, and vividly to the physical senses.
