- Working earth-dog: In medieval and early modern England, a 'terrier' was a functional dog that went to earth after foxes, badgers, and rats. Register: plain, utilitarian.
- Plucky sporting dog: Through the 18th-19th centuries terriers were prized on sporting estates for grit and stamina, and the word took on an aura of scrappy courage and tenacity.
- Companion and show dog: From the late 19th century, selective breeding produced small companion terrier breeds; 'terrier' softened into a household-pet word, yet the stereotype of the stubborn, spirited, feisty little dog endures.
