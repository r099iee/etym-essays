- Late 16th to Early 17th Century (Tudor & Stuart Eras) - Originates as an energetic sailor's hauling shanty or working cheer (*huzza / hussa*) shouted aboard British sailing vessels while hoisting sails or hauling heavy ropes.
- Mid-17th Century (Civil War & Commonwealth) - Transitions from a purely nautical hauling cry into an overt public shout of political and military acclaim; crowds shout it for returning generals and monarchs during the Restoration.
- 18th Century (Georgian & American Revolutionary Eras) - Reaches its cultural zenith as the universal British and American patriotic shout; standardized in the ritual of "three huzzas" by naval crews, infantry charges, and festive crowds at fireworks displays.
- 19th Century (Victorian Era) - Gradually supplanted in everyday popular speech by its phonetic variant *hurrah* (and later *hooray*); remains common in historical prose, naval tradition, and Oscar Wilde's fairy tales.
- 20th to 21st Century - Preserved as a colorful historical archaism in period drama and maritime history; playfully revived in modern fantasy role-playing and internet pop culture as an ironic, flamboyant cheer of triumph (*huzzah!*).
