• Hazelnut: Similar because it is the edible kernel of this very shrub; differs because hazelnut names only the nut as food, while hazel names the tree, the wood, and the colour too.
  • Cobnut: Similar because both are cultivated large hazelnuts of English orchards; differs because cobnut is the fat selected variety (Kentish cobs), while hazel covers the wild shrub itself.
  • Filbert: Similar because both name hazel-type nuts; differs because filbert is the long continental variety, while hazel is the whole native species.
  • Alder: Similar because both are catkin-bearing shrubs of damp woods in the birch family; differs because alder loves riverbanks and fixes nitrogen, while hazel prefers drier slopes and gives edible nuts.
  • Hawthorn: Similar because both are classic hedgerow shrubs of the English lane; differs because hawthorn is thorny, white-blossomed, and berried, while hazel is thornless, catkined, and nut-bearing.
  • Coppice: Similar because hazel is the archetypal coppiced tree; differs because coppice is the managed system or its product, while hazel is the living species within it.
  • Wattle: Similar because hazel rods woven between stakes are the classic wattle; differs because wattle is the woven fence or wall itself, while hazel is the material it is woven from.
  • Auburn: Similar because both name reddish-brown colouring of hair; differs because auburn is rich red-brown and chiefly of hair, while hazel is green-brown and chiefly of eyes.
  • Birch: Similar because both are slender pale-barked pioneers of the same family; differs because birch is a true single-trunked tree with peeling bark, while hazel is multi-stemmed coppice scrub.
