• Lacquer: Similar because both name the glossy resinous finish and the objects bearing it; differs because lacquer is the simple word for the substance and the coating, while lacquer-work adds the craft/object sense and the compound’s hand-made, connoisseurial weight.
  • Varnish: Similar because both are glossy resinous coatings on wood; differs because varnish is the generic clear coat laid on in a coat or two for protection and sheen, whereas lacquer-work is thick-bodied and built up in many polished, cured layers to a deep, semi-translucent lustre and names a whole craft.
  • Japanning: Similar because both name the technique of giving objects a hard, glossy Asian-style finish; differs because japanning is specifically the 18th-century European imitation of Asian lacquer using shellac baked onto wood or metal, whereas lacquer-work names the genuine, often Eastern, craft.
  • Enamel: Similar because both are hard, glossy decorative surfaces; differs because enamel is pigmented glass fired onto metal—vitreous and brittle—whereas lacquer is organic resin applied and cured in layers.
  • Gilding: Similar because both are decorative finishes applied to objects; differs because gilding is the application of gold leaf or gold paint to a surface, while lacquer-work is a whole built-up resinous coating of accumulated craft.
  • Lacquerware: Similar because both denote objects finished in lacquer; differs because lacquerware is the modern collective noun for such objects, while lacquer-work is the older, craft-flavoured term that can also name the technique and the finish themselves.
  • Shellac: Similar because both are resinous coatings derived from insect secretion; differs because shellac is a specific spirit-soluble resin (the base of japanning and French polish), whereas lacquer-work traditionally used the thicker, slower-curing sap varnish of the East.
  • Patina: Similar because both are lustrous surface qualities developed by craft and time; differs because patina is the natural film of age and oxidation, unplanned and valued for its history, whereas lacquer-work is the deliberately built and polished finish.
  • Veneer: Similar because both concern the decorated surface of furniture; differs because a veneer is a thin sheet of fine wood glued over a cheaper base, while lacquer-work is a coating of resin, not of wood, applied as many layered films.
