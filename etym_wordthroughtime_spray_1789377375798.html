- Late 13th Century (Middle English) - Botanical noun *sprai* / *spray* enters written English, denoting a slender leafy twig, shoot, or flowering branchlet, celebrated in early courtly nature lyrics and pastoral poetry.
- 16th Century (Tudor Maritime Era) - Borrowed from Middle Dutch *spraeyen*, the nautical noun and verb *spray* enters seafaring English to describe fine water droplets whipped from ocean whitecaps by stormy winds.
- 18th to 19th Century (Romantic Literature & Victorian Floristry) - Botanical spray reaches peak aesthetic prestige in Victorian poetry (such as Oscar Wilde's fairy tales) and ornamental jewelry (diamond floral brooches); simultaneously, early mechanical scent atomizers and garden syringes popularize domestic liquid sprays.
- Mid-20th Century (Aerosol Revolution & Military Ballistics) - The invention of the commercial aerosol can revolutionizes consumer products, insecticides, and pharmaceuticals (asthma inhalers); military jargon coins *spray and pray* for automatic machine-gun fire.
- Early 21st Century (Digital Analytics & Esports) - Permanently integrated into sports data science (baseball *spray charts*) and competitive video gaming (*spray patterns* and weapon recoil mechanics).
