• Cliff: Similar because both name a steep rock face; differs because cliff is the neutral, general word for any steep face of rock, while precipice insists on great height and on the danger of falling headlong.
  • Crag: Similar because both name steep rock formations; differs because crag suggests a rugged, broken projection or outcrop of rock, while precipice is a sheer drop, smooth and vertiginous rather than broken.
  • Bluff: Similar because both name steep banks or headlands; differs because bluff suggests a broad, rounded, often seaside bank of moderate height, while precipice is a great, sheer, inland or mountain drop.
  • Escarpment: Similar because both name steep rock faces; differs because escarpment is the technical geological term for a long slope formed by faulting or erosion, while precipice is the literary word for a single sheer drop.
  • Drop: Similar because both can name a vertical descent; differs because drop is vague and can mean any falling height, while precipice specifies a sheer, dangerous cliff face conceived from the standpoint of the fall.
  • Abyss: Similar because both evoke depth and danger; differs because abyss names the fathomless depth below the edge - the void - while precipice names the edge itself from which one falls.
  • Chasm: Similar because both are great openings in the ground; differs because chasm is a deep fissure or gorge between rock walls, while precipice is a vertical face descending from a height.
  • Brink: Similar because both name the edge of something catastrophic, literal or figurative; differs because brink is any edge at all (of a river, of tears), while precipice specifies a high sheer drop and carries more menace.
  • Edge: Similar because both name the boundary of a dangerous place; differs because edge is the general word for any margin, while precipice names a specific kind of dangerous edge - a sheer cliff.
  • Precipitousness: Similar because both derive from the same Latin source and describe steepness; differs because precipitousness is the abstract noun for the quality of being precipitous, while precipice names the physical cliff itself.
  • Overhang: Similar because both are parts of a steep face; differs because an overhang projects outward and leans over the void, while a precipice may be perfectly vertical or undercut only at its base.
  • Ledge: Similar because both involve vertical rock; differs because a ledge is a narrow horizontal shelf that offers a footing, the opposite of the falling-place a precipice is.
