• Dissimulate: Similar because it shares the same Latin base (dissimulāre) and the same core of concealing true motives; differs because it is a learned doublet formed directly from Latin and stays more technical and colder, while dissemble is the more idiomatic, theatrical literary verb for hiding feelings.
  • Conceal: Similar because both mean to keep something from being known or seen; differs because conceal is broader and neutral — you may conceal a gift or a fact without any falseness of face — whereas dissemble specifically involves a misleading outward appearance.
  • Hide: Similar because both imply keeping true feelings or facts out of sight; differs because hide is the everyday, morally neutral verb, while dissemble adds deliberate deception through a contrived seeming.
  • Mask: Similar because both describe covering a true self with a false surface; differs because mask is a noun-based metaphor often used figuratively (to mask one's anger) and need not imply sustained performance, while dissemble implies an active strategy of false seeming.
  • Cloak: Similar because both picture covering or concealing something; differs because cloak suggests screening behind a pretext or outward show rather than the specific craft of acting like something one is not.
  • Disguise: Similar because both involve concealing identity or appearance; differs because disguise usually means altering one's appearance or identity (a disguise), while dissemble is about hiding inner states behind behavior.
  • Feign: Similar because both involve presenting a false appearance; differs because feign means to pretend something IS the case (to feign illness, feign sleep) — a positive invention of a fiction — whereas dissemble emphasizes making what IS true look unlike itself.
  • Simulate: Similar because both derive from simulāre "to make like" and involve producing a likeness; differs because simulate is technical (simulation, simulator) and stresses reproducing or imitating something, not hiding a genuine feeling.
  • Pretend: Similar because both involve not being genuine; differs because pretend is far wider and often playful, childish, or momentary, without dissemble's ethical charge of deliberate social hypocrisy.
  • Affect: Similar because both mean to assume a manner or appearance; differs because affect stresses putting on a style or air (to affect indifference) and is usually narrower, while dissemble stresses concealing what genuinely lies beneath.
  • Counterfeit: Similar because both involve falsity and imitation; differs because counterfeit focuses on forging objects or documents (or, archaically, persons) — an external fraud — while dissemble is an inward concealment of feeling.
  • Playact / Act: Similar because both liken deception to theater, hiding feeling behind a performed role; differs because act is casual and neutral and playact often implies melodrama, while dissemble keeps the deliberate, ethical-political force of hiding a true self.
