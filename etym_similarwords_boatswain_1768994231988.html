• Bosun: Similar because it is the same office, written as pronounced; differs only in spelling — "bosun" is the phonetic spelling of the sailor's one-syllable pronunciation, while "boatswain" preserves the etymology.
  • Petty officer: Similar because it is the naval rank to which a boatswain belongs; differs because it is the general rank without maritime-gear specialisation, while the boatswain is specifically charged with the hull, rigging, sails, and deck crew.
  • Boatswain's mate: Similar because it shares the boatswain's trade and name; differs because it is the rating that assists the boatswain — the modern petty-officer title in the U.S. Navy — whereas the boatswain proper is the principal warrant officer of the deck.
  • Mate: Similar because both are subordinate ship's officers; differs because a mate commands navigation and crew duties with a more respectable, quasi-officer ring, whereas the boatswain's domain is the physical tackle of the deck.
  • Coxswain: Similar because it shares the "-swain" suffix and nautical command; differs because a coxswain steers a boat or rowing crew — a humbler, more specialised post — while the boatswain commands the whole ship's gear.
  • Deckhand: Similar because both work the deck of a ship; differs because a deckhand is the unskilled labourer beneath the boatswain, who is the skilled officer giving the orders.
  • Quartermaster: Similar because both are senior enlisted or warrant posts on deck; differs because a quartermaster handles steering, signals, and stores, while the boatswain is the foreman of rigging and the physical gear.
  • Warrant officer: Similar because both name the commissioned-by-warrant class between officers and ratings; differs because it is the general class, of which the boatswain is one specific technical member.
  • Boatswain's call: Similar because both belong to the boatswain's office; differs because the call is the small whistle-pipe used to pass orders, not the man — a metonymic relative of the word rather than a synonym.
