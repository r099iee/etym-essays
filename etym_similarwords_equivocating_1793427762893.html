• Prevaricating: Similar because both involve avoiding the straightforward truth; differs because prevaricating often involves straying into indirect deceit, quibbling, or creating false impressions through irrelevance, whereas equivocating specifically relies on ambiguous words with double meanings.
  • Paltering: Similar because both mislead while technically uttering true words; differs because paltering is the active deployment of truthful statements specifically to deceive regarding the overall reality, while equivocating focuses on verbal indeterminacy and dual interpretations.
  • Hedging: Similar because both refuse to commit to a clear position; differs because hedging is primarily defensive risk-mitigation—qualifying statements with caveats (‘perhaps’, ‘to some extent’)—whereas equivocating carries stronger connotations of bad faith and intentional duplicity.
  • Weaseling: Similar because both involve squirming out of commitments; differs because weaseling is informal and colloquial, evoking sneakiness and evasion through slippery phrasing (‘weasel words’), whereas equivocating is a formal, dignified term of rhetorical and ethical critique.
  • Evading: Similar because both dodge the central issue; differs because evading is a broad category encompassing changing the subject, remaining silent, or physically walking away, while equivocating works strictly within language through double-meaning constructs.
  • Quibbling: Similar because both avoid the core truth through verbal craft; differs because quibbling focuses on trivial technicalities, petty distinctions, or minor flaws in phrasing to derail a point, while equivocating targets major substance through calculated ambiguity.
  • Shuffling: Similar because both denote evasive, slippery behavior; differs because shuffling emphasizes clumsy, shifting pretexts, awkward evasions, or changing one's story under pressure, whereas equivocating suggests a cooler, more intellectualized linguistic game.
  • Tergiversating: Similar because both involve abandoning clear positions; differs because tergiversating specifically implies repeated apostasy, deserting a cause, or completely reversing one's stance, rather than merely speaking with double tongue.
  • Vacillating: Similar because both show indecision; differs because vacillating stems from genuine psychological wavering, doubt, or weakness of will, while equivocating is an intentional rhetorical strategy aimed at an audience.
  • Obfuscating: Similar because both prevent clear understanding; differs because obfuscating aims to make a subject dense, muddy, and incomprehensible, whereas equivocating offers a superficially clear statement that hides a second, conflicting interpretation.
