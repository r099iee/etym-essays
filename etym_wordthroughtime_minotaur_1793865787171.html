- [c. 17th c. - 18th c.] - [Interpreted by Enlightenment and neoclassical moralists as an emblem of bestial savagery rightly subdued by civilizing human reason and law]
- [c. 19th c. - early 20th c.] - [Reimagined by psychoanalysts (Freud, Jung) and modernist authors as an archetype of the shadow, the unconscious mind, and suppressed primal drives]
- [Mid-20th c. - Present] - [Celebrated as an iconic surrealist emblem of existential anguish and artistic struggle (Picasso), and widely codified as a standard formidable maze-dwelling fantasy creature]
