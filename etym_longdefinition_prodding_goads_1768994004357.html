The two root words are prodding and goads — the vocabulary of the passage: "Prodding their vortlups with the iron-pointed goads which alone could elicit any increase of speed from these sluggish monsters, the earthmen started off toward the cavern-mouth descried by Chivers."

1. PRODDING

Core sense: poking or jabbing repeatedly with a pointed instrument; urging a reluctant creature or person forward by means of small, insistent, slightly irritating thrusts. In the passage the earthmen are "prodding their vortlups," and the participle does double work: it names the mechanical action (the repetitive jab of the goad) and the equation it implies — the beasts are so sluggish that only physical irritation can elicit speed from them. The word's own sound, the clipped monosyllable prod, is stubby and insistent, an echoic little blow of a word, apt for the motion it names. Polysemy:
  • physical: to poke or thrust repeatedly with a pointed thing (to prod a fire with a stick);
  • figurative: to urge, stimulate, or provoke a person into action (to prod someone along, to prod a memory, a prod of conscience);
  • instrumental: a prod as a pointed stick, and in the 20th century the electric cattle prod.
Differentiating terms: poking is a single, casual, often aimless thrust (a finger into a hole); nudging is gentle, rounded, usually an elbow, with no point at all; jabbing is sharp, sudden, and hostile; pricking uses a fine point and barely breaks the skin; spurring applies the horseman's wheeled heel-spur. Prodding sits between the casual poke and the violent jab: repeated, pointed, businesslike — the routine nudge of the drover rather than the fury of the fight.

2. GOADS

Core sense: pointed sticks — often tipped with iron — used for driving cattle and other beasts of burden; by extension, anything that stings or urges a person into action. In the passage the goads are explicitly "iron-pointed": they are the tools of command that bridge human urgency and animal inertia, the harsh mechanical link between the earthmen's need for haste and the vortlups' monumental sluggishness. The detail makes the instrument vivid and dangerous — a weapon aimed at a beast's hide rather than at an enemy. Polysemy:
  • concrete: the drover's pointed stick, historically iron-tipped; in modern form the electric cattle prod;
  • figurative: anything that stings or urges a person into action — the goad of ambition, a goad to the conscience, a taunt that goads;
  • verb: to drive or urge with a goad; to goad someone into doing something; goaded, goading.
Differentiating terms: spurs are worn on the heel and press rather than stab; whips strike with a flexible lash and punish from outside; prods are blunter and more insistent; pricks have a finer point. A goad is the pointed stick wielded in the hand — the harshest and most direct link between the driver's will and the driven beast.
