• Spitefully: Similar because both describe acting from ill will; differs because spitefully emphasises petty personal resentment and retaliatory smallness, while maliciously covers broader, more deliberate intent to harm.
  • Malevolently: Similar because both describe intending harm; differs because malevolently is heavier and more abstract or gothic ("wishing evil" generally), while maliciously can describe a specific knowing act or gesture.
  • Viciously: Similar because both involve harm; differs because viciously stresses cruelty or a savage manner, usually in physical or verbal attack, not a composed, knowing smile.
  • Wickedly: Similar because both denote moral badness; differs because wickedly can be playful or moralising and lacks the precise legal-moral weight of maliciously.
  • Nastily: Similar because both describe unkind manner; differs because nastily is colloquial and thinner, lacking the formal intent-to-harm component of maliciously.
  • Cruelly: Similar because both involve hurting; differs because cruelly stresses the infliction of suffering, while maliciously stresses the ill-will motive behind even small gestures.
  • Venomously: Similar because both describe poisonous ill feeling; differs because venomously suggests bitter, vitriolic hostility in speech, while maliciously covers cold, calculated ill will.
  • Vindictively: Similar because both involve harming from ill will; differs because vindictively emphasises revenge for a perceived wrong, while maliciously need not involve prior injury.
  • Hostilely: Similar because both mark an unfriendly manner; differs because hostilely is more open and adversarial, lacking the covert, knowing quality of maliciously.
