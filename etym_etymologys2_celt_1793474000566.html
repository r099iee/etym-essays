Note: The ultimate etymology of the native Celtic autonym *Kelto- is debated among Indo-Europeanists. The three leading hypotheses are: (1) from PIE *ḱel- ('to hide, cover, conceal', cognate with Latin celāre and English hall, hell), meaning 'the hidden ones' or 'the clothed/armoured ones'; (2) from PIE *kelh₅- ('to rise, be prominent, high', cognate with Latin celsus 'lofty' and collis 'hill'), meaning 'the tall ones' or 'the noble/elevated ones'; and (3) from PIE *kelh₂- ('to strike, beat'), meaning 'the warriors' or 'the strikers'. The Greeks borrowed the name directly from native Celts in southern Gaul (near Massalia/Marseille) as Κελτοί, which was subsequently transcribed into Latin as Celtae.
