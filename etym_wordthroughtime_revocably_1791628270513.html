- 16th to 17th c. – Common law grants & royal prerogatives - Employed in Tudor and Stuart legal charters, letters patent, and court commissions held conditionally at the monarch's pleasure.
- 18th c. – Equity jurisprudence & testamentary law - Formalized in the Court of Chancery and probate law to govern revocable trusts, ambulatory wills, and conditional powers of appointment.
- 19th c. – International treaties & diplomatic alliances - Extended to the political sphere to critique fragile interstate peace pacts that retained unilateral sovereign exit mechanisms.
- Early 20th c. – League of Nations & collective security - Featured prominently in political debates (such as Will Durant's historical writings) contrasting modern states' revocable peace treaties with ancient sacred peace oaths.
- Late 20th c. to Present – Administrative licensing, digital security & contract theory - Widely utilized in administrative law (revocable permits), software licensing, and cybersecurity (revocably granted cryptographic access tokens).
