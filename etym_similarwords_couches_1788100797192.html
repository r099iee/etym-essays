• Lies down: Similar because both name assuming a recumbent posture; differs because "couch" in its animal use implies settling, rest or concealment rather than the mere posture.
  • Crouches: Similar because it lows the body as an animal does; differs because crouching stresses bent limbs and readiness to spring, while couching is a settled, resting posture.
  • Reclines: Similar because resting in a reclined attitude; differs because reclining is human, deliberate and elegant (sofas, banquets), without the lurking of beasts.
  • Squats: Similar because a low animal posture; differs because squatting keeps the animal alert on its haunches — the opposite of couched rest.
  • Lurks / hides: Similar because couching in cover includes half-concealment; differs because lurking stresses hostile or guilty intent, which a couching beast need not have.
  • Rests / reposes: Similar because the quiet-lying sense; differs because they are general and indoor-human, without hunting or lair colouring.
  • Phrases / frames / clothes (in words): Similar because all mean expressing something in a specified way; differs because "couched in" is the formal, rather stiff passive idiom of documents and diplomacy.
  • Lairs (verb, of animals): Similar because a beast settling in its resting place; differs because lairing is tied to the den itself, not to posture.
  • Sofa / settee / divan / chesterfield: Similar because the modern noun-sense's near-synonyms for an upholstered seat; differs by dialect and shape: sofa is common to UK and US, settee more formal-British, divan backless and low, chesterfield a tufted leather sofa — while "couch" is the preferred American everyday word.
