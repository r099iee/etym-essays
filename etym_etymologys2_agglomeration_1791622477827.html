Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*glem- / *gleb-
"to ball up, compress, round mass"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*glomos-
"ball, clew of yarn"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
glomus
"ball of yarn or thread, skein, mass"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. CE)
agglomerāre
"to wind onto a ball, gather into a mass"
↓
Late Latin / Medieval Latin (c. 6th–12th c.)
agglomerātiō
"a winding into a ball, heaping together"
↓
French (late 18th c.)
agglomération
"aggregation, clustered population"
↓
Modern English (late 18th c.–present)
agglomeration
"cluster, mass, urban coalescence"

Note: The base noun glomus ('ball of yarn') shares deep Indo-European roots with Latin globus ('sphere, globe') and gleba ('clod of earth'), both stemming from the expressive PIE root *gel- / *gleb- denoting compacting or rounding matter into a ball.
