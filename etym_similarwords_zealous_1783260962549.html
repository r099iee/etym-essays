• Ardent: Similar because it denotes burning passionate intensity; differs because ardent emphasizes fiery emotional warmth, while zealous stresses active, sustained, and dutiful effort toward a goal or cause.
  • Fervent: Similar because it denotes strong, heated feeling; differs because fervent carries the image of boiling or glowing emotion, whereas zealous implies energetic advocacy and commitment that shows in action.
  • Passionate: Similar because it denotes strong emotion; differs because passionate is broader, more personal, and may be passive, while zealous is specifically directed and energetic devotion to a cause, faith, or duty.
  • Eager: Similar because it denotes keen willingness; differs because eager is lighter, often momentary anticipation, whereas zealous implies deep, sustained commitment with real intensity.
  • Enthusiastic: Similar because it denotes lively interest and support; differs because enthusiastic is general and can be casual, while zealous is stronger, more serious, and often carries religious or moral weight.
  • Devoted: Similar because it denotes dedicated loyalty; differs because devoted emphasizes faithfulness and affection, while zealous emphasizes energetic, active fervour in pursuit.
  • Fanatic(al): Similar because it denotes extreme, intense devotion; differs because fanatical is pejorative, implying irrational, uncritical, or dangerous obsession, whereas zealous can still be approving or neutral.
  • Overzealous: Similar because it is built on the same word; differs because overzealous marks the excess that zealous itself usually avoids — fervour that has tipped into officiousness or excess.
  • Militant: Similar because it denotes aggressive, committed advocacy; differs because militant implies combative, confrontational methods, often political or religious struggle, while zealous need not be aggressive.
  • Jealous: Similar because it is the historical doublet of zealous, from the same Greek zēlos; differs because jealous has drifted to possessive suspicion, envy, or protectiveness, while zealous retains the older sense of devoted ardor.
