Note on historical sound shifts and semantic drift: The base of disfigurement is Latin figūra, derived from the PIE verbal root *dʰeyǵʰ- ("to knead, mold, form from clay"). Through regular Italic sound laws (PIE voiced aspirate *dʰ becoming voiceless labiodental fricative f- initially, and velar *ǵʰ leniting to g-), this root generated Latin fingere ("to fashion, devise, invent") and figūra ("shape, statue, portrait"), as well as English fiction and feign. In Germanic, the same PIE root retained its original agrarian craft meaning: through Grimm's law, *dʰeyǵʰ- became Proto-Germanic *daigaz, producing Old English dāg (modern English dough), and the Old English compound hlāfdīġe ("bread-kneader", modern English lady). Thus, etymologically, disfigurement and dough share the primordial Indo-European concept of kneading and shaping form.
