• Hissing: Similar because both are high-pitched air sounds through narrow openings; differs because hissing is a harsh, sibilant noise without musical pitch (snakes, steam jets), while whistling is clearer, pitched, and more musical.
  • Shrieking: Similar because both are shrill, piercing sounds; differs because shrieking is loud, panicked, and human (a cry), often higher and wilder than a whistle, which is controlled and tonal.
  • Shrilling: Similar because both describe high, piercing continuous tones; differs because shrilling implies grating loudness (a siren, a cicada), while whistling implies a pitched, tunable tone.
  • Piping: Similar because both describe clear, high musical notes; differs because piping is reedy and birdlike or instrument-made (a pipe, a piccolo), whereas whistling is breath-through-a-hole and can be done by lips alone.
  • Buzzing: Similar because both are continuous sounds of air or vibration; differs because buzzing is low, ragged, and mechanical (a bee, a wire), the opposite end of the pitch range from a whistle.
  • Wheezing: Similar because both are breath-forced sounds; differs because wheezing is laboured, asthmatic, and unmusical (a sick lung), while whistling is free, clear, and controlled.
  • Singing (of wind, kettle): Similar because both describe the wind or vessels producing sustained tones; differs because "singing" is lyrical and pleasant, while "whistling" is sharper, more cutting, and more technical-sounding.
  • Sizzling: Similar because both describe hot escaping steam or fat; differs because sizzling is a crackling, sputtering noise (frying), while whistling is a single sustained shrill tone.
