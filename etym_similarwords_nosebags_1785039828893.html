• Feedbag: Similar because both name a sack for feeding a harnessed animal; differs because feedbag is chiefly North American, can also mean a human meal in slang, and is less evocative of the muzzle-fitted shape than nosebag.
  • Muzzle: Similar because both are items fitted over an animal's muzzle; differs because a muzzle's function is the opposite — preventing biting or eating — while a nosebag delivers feed to the mouth.
  • Noseband: Similar because both are nose-gear of the bridle family; differs because a noseband is a strap for steadying the head and carries no idea of feeding at all.
  • Pannier: Similar because both are bags used with pack animals; differs because a pannier is slung on either side of the body for carrying loads, not fitted to the head for feeding.
  • Morral: Similar because both are head-mounted feedbags for horses or mules; differs because morral is the Spanish/Mexican term for the same object, used in Latin American horsemanship, while nosebag is the English term.
  • Halter-bag: Similar because both are feeding sacks attached to the horse's head; differs because a halter-bag is a subtype fixed to the halter, while nosebag is the general word for the muzzle-fitted feed sack.
  • Grain sack: Similar because both hold grain; differs because a grain sack is a general storage sack for feed, not a device fitted to an animal's muzzle for feeding while harnessed.
