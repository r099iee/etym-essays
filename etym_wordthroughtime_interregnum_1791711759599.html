- Roman Antiquity (c. 6th c. BCE to 1st c. BCE): Constitutional mechanism of the Roman Republic - An official legal period between the death of a king or consul, governed by an interrex to hold elections.
- 16th to 17th Century: English adoption and the Cromwellian Commonwealth - Naturalized in English historiography, most famously applied to the English Interregnum (1649–1660) between Charles I and Charles II.
- 19th Century: Anthropological and political theory generalization - Used by sociologists (Spencer, Durant) to denote the volatile breakdown of civic order when sovereign authority vanishes.
- 20th to 21st Century: Gramscian cultural crisis and institutional leadership gaps - Famously re-theorized by Antonio Gramsci ('the old is dying and the new cannot be born; in this interregnum a great variety of morbid symptoms appear') and widely used for corporate and geopolitical transitions.
