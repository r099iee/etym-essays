• Theories: Similar because both denote bodies of intellectual claims; differs because theories are provisional explanatory frameworks open to testing and revision, while doctrines are established teachings asserted as valid by an authority.
  • Dogmas: Similar because both are fixed teachings of a group; differs because dogmas carry the added sense of rigid, unquestionable, usually theological pronouncements and are often pejorative in modern use.
  • Principles: Similar because both are foundational points of belief; differs because principles are the fundamental starting points of reasoning, while a doctrine is a codified body of instruction with recognised authority behind it.
  • Tenets: Similar because both are items of belief held by a group; differs because a tenet is a single article of belief, while a doctrine is the organised body or system of such articles.
  • Creeds: Similar because both are statements of belief; differs because a creed is a formal, usually short confession of religious faith, while a doctrine is the broader teaching system of which creeds are only summaries.
  • Teachings: Similar because both are what a master imparts; differs because teachings is the general, informal word, while doctrines implies a codified, authoritative body of instruction.
  • Ideologies: Similar because both describe systematic sets of beliefs; differs because an ideology is a comprehensive, often politicised worldview of which a doctrine is only a narrower, more formal component.
  • Dogmatics: Similar because both concern fixed religious teaching; differs because dogmatics names the theological discipline that studies doctrine, not the doctrines themselves.
  • Canons: Similar because both denote authoritative bodies of rule; differs because a canon is the official list or rule of a church or literary tradition, while a doctrine is the teaching content itself.
  • Beliefs: Similar because both are what people hold to be true; differs because beliefs are private and can be held informally, while doctrines are public, codified, and institutionally asserted.
