DERIVATION CHAIN FOR "RECOMMENCED" — PREFIX + VERB BASE:

ROOT — the verb base "commence":
Stage 1 — PIE root: *h₁ey- / *ei- "to go" (reconstructed root of motion; attested in Sanskrit éti "he goes," Greek eîmi "I go," Lithuanian eĩti "to go," Old Church Slavonic iti "to go," Latin īre "to go").
Stage 2 — Latin: īre "to go" (eō, īvī, itum).
Stage 3 — Latin: inīre (in- "into" + īre) "to go in, to enter upon."
Stage 4 — Latin: initium "a going in; a beginning, an entrance" (verbal noun); the Romans conceived beginning as entering upon a road.
Stage 5 — Latin: initiāre "to begin, to initiate" (denominative verb from initium).
Stage 6 — Vulgar Latin: *cominitiāre (com- "with, together" + initiāre) "to begin, to commence" (reconstructed; the source of the Romance verbs).
Stage 7 — Old French: comencier "to begin" (attested; ancestor of modern French commencer).
Stage 8 — Middle English: commencen (c. 1300) "to begin," borrowed from Old French comencier.
Stage 9 — English: commence "to begin" (formal verb).

THE PREFIX "re-":
Stage 10 — Latin: re- "back, again" (prefix of repetition). The ultimate origin of re- is itself uncertain: the OED labels its source obscure, so no PIE root is asserted here; it is conventionally analysed as an old Italic directional element meaning "back," sometimes doubtfully connected with the root behind Latin retro "backwards," but this is not securely reconstructed.

THE COMPOUND:
Stage 11 — English verb: to recommence (16th century) — re- + commence, "to begin again."
Stage 12 — English: recommenced (16th century) — past tense and past participle of "to recommence."
Stage 13 — English: RECOMMENCED.

NOTES / DEBATED POINTS (explicit):
The earlier condensed tree invoked PIE *wert- "to turn" as the root of this word; that is a mistake — the verb's core is the "go" root *h₁ey-, and no "turn" root belongs anywhere in its ancestry. The Latin prefix com- (from PIE *kom "with, beside") is a separate element, and re- (origin obscure per the OED) is a further separate element; both are morphological building blocks of the word rather than roots in the derivation chain. Hindi "to go" is jānā, from the different PIE root *gʷem-, so the old *h₁ey- root survives in modern Indo-Aryan only in frozen derivatives and no Hindi branch belongs here.
