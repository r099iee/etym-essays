- Ancient Greek Mythology (Homer, post-Homeric tradition) - Literal physical vulnerability of the hero Achilles, a specific anatomical point left unprotected by the River Styx. - Connotation of a singular, fated weakness.
- 17th-18th Century (Emergence in English) - Gradual adoption of the phrase in scholarly and literary contexts, primarily referring to the mythological story itself. - Connotation of classical allusion and erudition.
- 19th Century (Popularization as an Idiom) - Widespread figurative use, especially after its popularization in English literature (e.g., by Coleridge and Byron), to denote any single, critical weakness in an otherwise strong entity. - Shift from literal to metaphorical, becoming a common idiom signifying a crucial point of failure.
- 20th-21st Century (Ubiquitous Idiomatic Use) - Fully integrated into common parlance across various domains (business, politics, personal development, sports). - Connotation of a crucial, often overlooked, point of failure that can lead to downfall, applicable to individuals, organizations, and systems.
