PIE *terkʷ- "to turn, twist" (proposed deeper connection — debated; see note) → Proto-Germanic *þwerhaz "transverse, crosswise, contrary, perverse" (reconstructed) → Old High German twerah / twerh "crosswise, oblique, contrary" (attested c. 8th–9th c.) → Middle High German twer / twër "crosswise, oblique, perverse" → German quer "transverse, diagonal, crosswise; contrary" (modern form, c. 13th c. onward) → Middle Low German quer / dwe(e)r "oblique, crosswise" → English queer "strange, odd, peculiar, eccentric" (borrowed from the German/Low German area, attested from c. 1500) → Modern English queer (this card: "odd, peculiar"). Debated/nuanced stage: The immediate source of English queer is debated between German quer and Low German quer/dweer, both from the same Germanic *þwerh- family; the two are so close that the precise route cannot be settled. The PIE link *terkʷ- "turn, twist" (via "twisted" → "crosswise" → "odd") is attractive and often proposed, but it is not universally accepted, and some handbooks prefer to leave *þwerh- without a secure Indo-European etymon. The modern identity senses and idioms such as "queer the pitch" are English-internal developments of the modern period and are not stages of this derivation chain.
