• Ayodhyā: Similar because both were primary administrative centers of the ancient Kosala kingdom during the epic period; differs because Ayodhyā was the grand, fortified imperial capital city with royal palaces, broad boulevards, and treasuries, whereas Nandigrāma was a modest rural outpost chosen specifically for ascetic regency outside the urban center.
  • Citrakūṭa: Similar because both served as sacred ascetic dwellings during the period of epic exile; differs because Citrakūṭa is an elevated sacred mountain and hermitage where Rāma, Sītā, and Lakṣmaṇa resided during the initial phase of their banishment, whereas Nandigrāma was the flat rural encampment of Bharata near the kingdom’s borders.
  • Hermitage (Āśrama): Similar because Nandigrāma was transformed by Bharata into an austere sanctuary of prayer, simplicity, and tapas; differs because an āśrama is primarily a spiritual retreat for ascetics, sages, and Vedic students, whereas Nandigrāma remained a fully functioning seat of imperial statecraft and civil government.
  • Encampment (Śibira): Similar because both designate an administrative or military outpost situated outside an imperial capital; differs because a śibira is typically a temporary military bivouac for troops on campaign, whereas Nandigrāma served as a permanent political residence for fourteen uninterrupted years.
  • Rājadhānī: Similar because Bharata exercised royal authority from Nandigrāma over the entire empire; differs because rājadhānī is the formal sovereign capital housing the monarch and throne, whereas Nandigrāma was deliberately an anti-capital created to avert royal luxury.
  • Outpost: Similar because both refer to a settlement positioned at a distance from the main center of power; differs because an outpost implies a fortified military or commercial border station, whereas Nandigrāma was a sacred village dedicated to fraternal devotion and ascetic penance.
  • Sanctuary: Similar because Nandigrāma offered a peaceful, sacred haven away from the political intrigue of the Ayodhyā court; differs because a sanctuary is an abstract or religious asylum, whereas Nandigrāma was a physical geographic village and administrative hub.
  • Panchavati: Similar because both are famous rural locations in the Rāmāyaṇa marked by ascetic huts and profound epic events; differs because Panchavati was deep inside the wild, demon-haunted Daṇḍaka forest where Sītā was abducted, while Nandigrāma was in civilized Kosala near Ayodhyā.
