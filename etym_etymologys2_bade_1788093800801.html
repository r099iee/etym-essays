Old English (attested c. 8th–11th c.)
bēodan — past sing. bēad

Middle English (12th–15th c.)
bād / bad / bade
"commanded, invited, said (greeting or farewell)"
↓
Early Modern / Modern English
bade
"(archaic) past of bid: commanded, invited; bade farewell"

Note: modern "bid" fuses two Old English verbs: bēodan "to command, offer"
(the source of this form, chain above) and biddan "to ask, entreat, pray"
(class V, PIE *gʷʰedʰ- "to ask", cf. Gothic bidjan "to pray"). Both merged
in Middle English "bidden". Pronunciation: traditional RP /bæd/; /beɪd/ is a
spelling-pronunciation from the final -e.
