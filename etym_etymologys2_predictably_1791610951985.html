PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*prey- / *per- + *deyḱ-
"before, in front" + "to show, point out, pronounce"
↓
Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
dicere
"to say, speak, declare"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
praedicere (prae- + dicere)
"to mention beforehand, foretell, advise in advance"
↓
Latin (past participle) (c. 1st c. CE)
praedictus
"foretold, mentioned in advance"
↓
English (verb) (c. 1620s)
predict
"to foretell, declare in advance"
↓
English (adjective) (c. 1850s)
predictable
"capable of being foretold or anticipated"
↓
English (adverb / sentence adverb) (20th c.–modern)
Predictably
"in a manner that can be anticipated beforehand"

Note: Latin praedicere ('to foretell', with short /e/ in dicere) must be distinguished from praedicare ('to proclaim publicly, preach', whence English preach and predicate). The modern adverb Predictably was constructed in English by compounding the verb predict with the Romance suffix -able and the native Germanic adverbial suffix -ly (Old English -lice, from PIE *līg-, 'body, likeness'). It is presented here capitalized to preserve the sentence-initial form from the source text.
