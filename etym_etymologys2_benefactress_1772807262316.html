Chain for the first element “bene / bonus” (the “good, well” half): - PIE *du̯eno- / *dwe- “fitting, due, worthy; good” (reconstruction after de Vaan; the exact shape and extent of attestation are debated). - Old Latin duenos / duonos “good” (attested c. 7th–5th century BCE, famously on the Duenos inscription of central Italy). - Latin bonus “good” (attested from c. 3rd century BCE onward; classical form). - Latin bene “well” (adverb, from *duenē; attested classical). - English bene- (first element of benefactor / benefactress, borrowed c. 15th century from Latin and Old French). - In benefactress, bene “well” is the first element. Note on the debate: Germanic has no transparent cognate of this “good” word—the ordinary Germanic words (English good, German gut, Dutch goed, Gothic gods) descend from a different root *gʰedʰ- “to unite, fit together”—so do not invent false “good” cognates. Greek agathos “good” is unrelated. Chain for the second element “facere” (the “do, make” half): - PIE *dʰeh₁- “to put, place; to do, make” (attested across the family; sisters: Sanskrit dadhāti “puts, places,” Greek títhēmi “I put, place” → English thesis, theme; Germanic *dōną “to do” → English do, deed, German tun). - Latin facere “to do, make” (attested from c. 3rd century BCE onward). - Latin benefacere “to do well, to do good to” (attested; prefix bene- + facere). - Latin benefactor “one who does good; a patron” (post-classical / ecclesiastical Latin, c. 3rd–6th century CE; feminine benefactrix also exists in Latin). - English benefactor (borrowed c. 15th century, via Old French bienfaiteur and Latin). - English benefactress = benefactor + -ess “female benefactor” (attested 17th century). Chain for the third element “-ess” (the feminine suffix): - Greek -ισσα (-issa) feminine agent and role suffix (attested classical; e.g. basilissa “queen”). - Late Latin -issa (borrowed from Greek; e.g. abbatissa “abbess”). - Old French -esse (e.g. abesse; the pattern of feminine role nouns). - Anglo-French / Middle English -esse > English -ess (attested c. 13th century: goddess, huntress). - English -ess added to benefactor (17th century) → benefactress “a female doer of good.” Composite gloss: bene “well” + factor “doer” + -ess “female” → benefactress “a woman who does good.”
