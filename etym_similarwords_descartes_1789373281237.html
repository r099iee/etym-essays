• Spinoza: Similar because both were monumental 17th-century Continental rationalists who sought mathematical certainty in metaphysics; differs because Descartes maintained strict substance dualism (separating immaterial mind *res cogitans* from material body *res extensa*) and a transcendent personal God, whereas Spinoza rejected dualism in favor of substance monism and pantheism (*Deus sive Natura*), holding mind and matter to be two attributes of one infinite reality.
  • Leibniz: Similar because both were preeminent rationalist philosophers and mathematical geniuses who transformed modern logic; differs because Descartes viewed the physical world as a continuous, inert material extension governed by mechanical impact, whereas Leibniz posited that reality consists of infinite, non-material, dynamic spiritual units of energy called monads operating under pre-established harmony.
  • Francis Bacon: Similar because both are venerated as co-founders of modern scientific methodology during the early 17th century; differs because Bacon championed empirical observation, systematic experimentation, and inductive reasoning from physical facts, whereas Descartes championed deductive rationalism from indubitable innate first principles of the mind.
  • Montaigne: Similar because both were supreme French prose stylists whose autobiographical reflections revolutionized Western consciousness; differs because Montaigne embraced personal skepticism, bodily imperfection, contradiction, and the open-ended, unsystematic wanderings of the essay (*Essais*), whereas Descartes sought to overcome all skepticism to construct an infallible, systematic rational discourse.
  • John Locke: Similar because both addressed foundational epistemology regarding how human minds acquire valid knowledge; differs because Locke was an empiricist who rejected innate ideas, arguing that the mind begins as a blank slate (*tabula rasa*) inscribed solely by sensory experience, whereas Descartes held that our knowledge of God and geometry is innate.
  • Cartesian: Similar because it is the direct adjectival and nominal eponym derived from Descartes; differs because Cartesian describes the methodology, philosophical school, coordinate mathematics, or cultural temperament of clear logical deduction, whereas Descartes is the concrete historical person, thinker, and author.
  • Rationalist: Similar because both denote an epistemological commitment to human reason as the primary source of truth; differs because rationalist is a broad philosophical category encompassing diverse figures from Plato to Spinoza and Kant, whereas Descartes specifically founded modern Continental rationalism centered on methodical doubt and substance dualism.
