• Proto-Indo-European *splend-/*pel- "to shine, gleam" (uncertain; the exact PIE shape is debated and some etymologists regard the Latin family as of unclear pre-Italic origin): hypothesized as the ultimate source of the "shine" semantics.
  • Latin splendēre "to shine, gleam, be bright" (attested Classical Latin, 1st century BCE).
  • Latin splendor (gen. splendoris) "brightness, brilliance, radiance" — noun formed from splendēre with the suffix -or (Classical Latin, 1st century BCE).
  • Old French splendour/splendeur "brightness, brilliance" (11th–12th century), inherited from Latin.
  • Middle English splendour/splendure "magnificence, brilliance" (borrowed c. 1400–1450 via Anglo-French).
  • Modern English SPLENDOUR (British orthography), with American splendor standardized from the 19th century.

Debate note: The pre-Latin ancestry is disputed: there is no securely attested PIE root for the Latin splend- family, and standard reference works either leave it of unclear origin or connect it to a tentatively reconstructed root meaning "to shine, spread light". The chain from attested Latin onward, however, is secure.
