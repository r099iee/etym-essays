- [5th–4th c. BCE] - askēsis means “training”, above all athletic: diet, exertion, the gymnasium - [perception: bodily excellence through discipline]
- [4th c. BCE onward] - Socrates, the Cynics, and the Stoics turn askēsis into “training of the soul”: exercises in virtue and endurance - [perception: self-mastery as philosophy]
- [1st–4th c. CE] - Christian ascetics adopt the term for fasting, vigil, and celibacy - [perception: the word crosses from the gymnasium to the desert]
- [19th c.] - Pater and the aesthetic critics revive ascesis for the discipline of art: the “austere and serious girding of the loins in youth” - [perception: the charm of renunciation within beauty]
- [20th c.] - Foucault reads askēsis as the ancient “practice of the self”: freedom through self-formation - [perception: intellectual prestige; the word of theory]
