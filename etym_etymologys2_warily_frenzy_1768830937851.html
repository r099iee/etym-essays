Root 1 — WARILY:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*wer-
"to watch, guard"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*waraz
"watchful"
↓
Old English (c. 700)
wær
"aware, wary, watchful"
↓
English (16th c.)
wary
"cautious, watchful"
↓
English (16th c.)
warily
"cautiously; with a watchful
eye for danger"

Note: wary (16th c.) is a survival or revival of Old English wær "aware, cautious"; the same PIE *wer- yields aware, beware, and guard (via Germanic and French).

Root 2 — FRENZY:

Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE)
φρήν (phrēn)
"the mind"
(orig. 'midriff, seat of emotion')
(PIE origin uncertain)
↓
Late Latin (3rd c. CE)
phrenēsis
"madness, frenzy"
(from Greek)
↓
Medieval Latin (12th c.)
phrenesia
"frenzy, madness"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
frenesie
"frenzy"
↓
English (14th c.)
frenzy
"a state of wild excitement
or derangement"

Note: Greek phrēn originally meant "midriff, diaphragm" — thought to be the seat of emotion — before "mind". Its PIE origin is uncertain; a link to *gʷʰren- "to think" is speculative.
