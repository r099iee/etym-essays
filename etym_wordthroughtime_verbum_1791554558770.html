- Classical Antiquity (c. 1st c. BCE): Primary Latin word for spoken speech and grammatical verb - Signified both any spoken vocable and the supreme functional word of a predicate in Quintilian and Varro.
- Early Christian & Patristic Era (c. 4th c. CE): Theological elevation as the divine Logos - Jerome's Vulgate translation of John 1:1 cemented Verbum as the second person of the Trinity, imbued with cosmic creative potency.
- Medieval Scholasticism & Renaissance (c. 12th–16th Century): Quintessential grammatical and philosophical term - Universal scholastic currency across European universities for logic, semantics, and grammar treatises.
- Modern English (17th–21st Century): Preserved Latinate technical term, theological concept, and legal/etymological anchor - Retained in scholarly, theological, and philological discourse, as well as idiomatic Latin maxims.
