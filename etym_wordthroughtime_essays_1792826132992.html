- Latin weighing — the seed: exigere means to drive out, weigh, and examine; exagium is the balance itself — trying as measuring.
- Old French trial — the word takes shape: essai means a trying, a test, a sample — the craftsman's cupel and the volunteer's attempt.
- Middle English assay — the laboratory years: assay tests metals and ores; miners and mint-masters essay gold while poets have not yet heard the word.
- 1580 — Montaigne invents the genre: his Essais (Attempts — at himself, at knowing) turn trying into literature, and Europe follows with Bacon's essays of counsel.
- 18th–19th c. — the periodical age: Addison, Steele, Lamb, and Hazlitt make the essay the English reader's daily bread — short, personal, and everywhere.
- Today — classroom and craft at once: students write essays for grades while makers call prototypes essays — the trial piece and the prose piece, twins again, as in Gandalf's judgement on the lesser rings.
