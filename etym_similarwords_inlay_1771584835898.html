• Marquetry: Similar because it is decorative work set into a surface; differs because marquetry is specifically the veneering of wood with pieces of wood to form patterns on furniture, whereas inlay may use ivory, mother-of-pearl, gold or silver set into wood, metal or stone.
  • Intarsia: Similar because it is an inlay technique; differs because intarsia is the Italian art of inlaying wood in a mosaic manner, staying within furniture and panel-work, whereas inlay is broader and spans many materials.
  • Mosaic: Similar because it is a pattern built into a surface; differs because mosaic covers a surface with many small cubes or pieces of glass, stone or tile, building a picture from a field of fragments, whereas inlay sets a few pieces flush into a cut-out bed.
  • Damascening: Similar because it is the art of decorating a metal surface with patterns in another metal; differs because damascening is a metal-on-metal specialisation — gold or silver hammered into steel — while inlay spans wood, stone, ivory, shell and metal alike.
  • Veneer: Similar because it is a decorative layer applied to a surface; differs because a veneer is a thin sheet glued over a surface — applied, not embedded — whereas inlay is set flush into a prepared cavity and cannot be removed without damage.
  • Inset: Similar because it means something set into something else; differs because an inset is a general piece set into a larger whole and need not lie flush, while inlay specifically denotes material set level with the surrounding surface.
