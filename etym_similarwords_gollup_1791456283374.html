• Gulp: the standard word for a large single swallow; similar in meaning, but gulp is standard and neutral, whereas gollup is dialectal, warmer, and more strongly imitative of the noise.
  • Swig: informal for a quick drink, often straight from a bottle; similar casual register, but swig stresses drinking from a container rather than the size and sound of the swallow.
  • Draught: a quantity drunk at one time; more formal and literary, and it measures the drink without suggesting greed or noise.
  • Swallow: the neutral physiological act; it lacks the emphasis on haste, quantity, and audible effort that gollup carries.
  • Guzzle: to drink greedily over a period; stronger and more disapproving, implying excess rather than one hearty mouthful.
  • Quaff: literary or archaic “to drink deeply”; dignified and celebratory, the opposite register to homely gollup.
  • Gobble: to eat fast and noisily; usually of solid food, and even more frankly onomatopoeic.
  • Bolt: to swallow food without chewing; stresses speed and lack of ceremony, typically with solids.
  • Wolf: to eat ravenously; implies hunger and crude haste, almost always of food.
  • Chug: informal, to drink quickly and steadily, often a whole container; modern and casual, without the rural dialect flavour.
  • Slug: a small draught of spirits; colloquial and dialectal, but it names a measure rather than the swallowing action.
  • Sup: to take liquid in small mouthfuls; archaic and rustic, suggesting slow, modest drinking — the reverse of gollup.
  • Mouthful: the quantity held in the mouth at once; a neutral measure, not an act of greedy drinking.
