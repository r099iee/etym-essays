• fosse: Similar because both refer to an excavated ditch or trench serving defensive fortification purposes; differs because fosse is a formal, classical, or technical military term (derived from Latin fossa) frequently applied to dry trenches or Roman earthworks, whereas moat is the standard everyday term commonly associated with medieval water-filled castle ditches.
  • trench: Similar because both are linear excavations dug into the earth; differs because a trench is generally a narrow channel excavated for field warfare, irrigation, or utility pipes, lacking the grand, permanent perimeter defense of a fortress characteristic of a moat.
  • ditch: Similar because both designate an excavated channel in the ground; differs because a ditch is an informal, generic term primarily used for agricultural drainage or roadside runoff, carrying none of the monumental military or architectural dignity inherent to a moat.
  • ha-ha: Similar because both are sunken barrier ditches designed to prevent physical crossing; differs because a ha-ha is an aesthetic landscape feature built into an English garden to keep cattle out while remaining invisible from a manor house, whereas a moat is an overt, intimidating defensive barrier.
  • rampart: Similar because both are core elements of defensive fortification perimeters; differs because a rampart is a raised embankment of earth or stone constructed upward to provide an elevated firing platform, whereas a moat is an excavation dug downward into the earth.
  • barbican: Similar because both are defensive structures safeguarding an entrance or perimeter; differs because a barbican is an elevated fortified gatehouse or forward tower positioned over a drawbridge, whereas a moat is the aqueous or dry trench underneath it.
  • palisade: Similar because both serve to impede hostile foot soldiers from rushing a stronghold; differs because a palisade is an upright fence of sharpened wooden stakes, whereas a moat is a sunken excavation.
  • circumvallation: Similar because both are encumbering continuous perimeter works in siegecraft; differs because circumvallation refers to an entire concentric line of ramparts and trenches built by the besiegers around an entire city to trap it, whereas a moat is built by the defenders to protect their own castle walls.
  • berm: Similar because both are architectural features situated between a fortification wall and the terrain; differs because a berm is a narrow, flat ledge of land left between the parapet and the ditch to prevent earth from slumping into the excavation.
