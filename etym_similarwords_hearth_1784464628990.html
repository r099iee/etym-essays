• Fireplace: Similar because both name the structure for a fire; differs because fireplace is the whole architectural unit (surround, chimney, firebox), while hearth names specifically the floor of the fire — the part the fire touches.
  • Fireside: Similar because both evoke domestic warmth; differs because fireside is the area around the fire and an image of home comfort, while hearth is the physical fire-floor and a stronger symbolic noun for the home itself.
  • Home: Similar because both can mean the dwelling and its warm centre; differs because home is fully abstract and general, while hearth grounds home in the physical fire and retains a poetic, archaic warmth.
  • Chimney: Similar because both belong to the fireplace structure; differs because the chimney is the flue that carries smoke away, while the hearth is the floor where the fire burns below it.
  • Grate: Similar because both are parts of a fireplace; differs because a grate is the metal frame holding the coals, a modern contrivance, while the hearth is the older, primal stone floor.
  • Inglenook: Similar because both are cosy spaces by the fire; differs because the inglenook is a recessed seating nook beside a large fireplace, while the hearth is the fire-floor itself.
  • Furnace: Similar because both hold fires for heating and industry; differs because a furnace is an enclosed industrial or domestic heat-chamber, while the hearth is the open fire-floor and its symbolic home-value.
  • Focus: Similar because both name the centre of the household; differs because focus is the Latin name of the hearth-fire (today "centre of attention" generally), while hearth is the native English word with the domestic emotion still attached.
  • Household: Similar because both denote the domestic unit; differs because household is the socio-economic group of the dwelling, while hearth is the symbolic fire-centre around which that group gathers.
  • Oven: Similar because both are places where food is heated; differs because the oven is a closed chamber for baking, while the hearth was the open fire-floor that cooked, warmed, and lit the whole home.
