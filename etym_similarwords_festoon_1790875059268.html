• Garland: Similar because both are decorative arrangements of flowers, leaves, or greenery; differs because a garland is often a circular wreath worn on the head or placed around the neck, whereas a festoon is specifically an elongated strip or chain hung suspended between two points in a drooping curved loop.
  • Swag: Similar because both refer to a piece of fabric, foliage, or floral arrangement hung in a sagging curve; differs because swag in drapery specifically emphasizes heavy fabric folded or draped gracefully, whereas festoon encompasses sculpted stone motifs, lighting chains, and traditional floral strings.
  • Adorn: Similar because to festoon something is to adorn it; differs because adorn is a general, formal verb meaning to make something beautiful or enhance appearance by adding ornaments, whereas festoon specifically implies draping across surfaces or heaping abundantly.
  • Deck: Similar because both mean to decorate lavishly for celebration (e.g., "deck the halls"); differs because deck is an older Germanic verb denoting general dressing up or furnishing, while festoon evokes the specific visual silhouette of curved, hanging garlands.
  • Bunting: Similar because both are festive decorations draped across public venues; differs because bunting consists specifically of rows of triangular fabric flags or pleated fabric banners (often in national colors), whereas festoon comprises flowers, lights, leaves, or carved reliefs.
  • Wreath: Similar because both use foliage or flowers for celebration; differs because a wreath is strictly bound into a closed circular ring (e.g., advent wreath, memorial wreath), whereas a festoon is an open, hanging horizontal loop.
