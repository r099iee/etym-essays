• Charisma: Similar because both denote a power of personal attraction; differs because charisma derives from a Greek theological term for divine grace and suggests a mystique of authority, even an aura of the extraordinary, while magnetism is a more earthy, physical "pull."
  • Charm: Similar because both make a person attractive to others; differs because charm implies smoothness, agreeableness, and deliberate pleasantness that can be switched on and off, lacking the force of attraction implicit in magnetism.
  • Allure: Similar because both attract others; differs because allure suggests something actively seductive or entrancing, often with a hint of the dangerous or mysterious.
  • Appeal: Similar because both draw others toward one; differs because appeal points to the rational or emotional content of what is said — the argument itself — whereas magnetism resides in the speaker, independent of the message.
  • Fascination: Similar because both hold attention powerfully; differs because fascination implies the object's power to engross the mind, often with wonder or curiosity, while magnetism implies an active pull of presence.
  • Presence: Similar because both describe the effect a person has on a room; differs because presence is a quality of bearing and self-command, while magnetism specifically implies the power to attract others to oneself.
  • Persuasiveness: Similar because both move an audience; differs because persuasiveness is a property of argument and rhetoric, while magnetism operates before a word is spoken.
  • Gravitas: Similar because both confer authority; differs because gravitas is weight, seriousness, and dignity, while magnetism is an active, attractive force.
  • Pull / draw: Similar because both name the attractive force of a person or thing; differs because pull and draw are colloquial and general, without the quasi-scientific physical metaphor of magnetism.
  • Aura: Similar because both describe an intangible field around a person; differs because an aura is a perceived radiance or emanation, more mystical and passive than magnetism's directed attraction.
