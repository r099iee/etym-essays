Note on historical debate: The etymology of German Bildung involves a profound semantic shift from concrete visual reproduction to abstract spiritual and philosophical cultivation. Ancient Germanic *biliþi designated a concrete likeness, idol, or physical image (cognate with Old English bilewit, "simple, innocent", literally "gracious countenance"). In medieval Christian mysticism (Meister Eckhart), Bildung was defined through the theology of Gottebenbildlichkeit—the spiritual restoration of man's soul into the true image (Bild) of God. During the 18th-century German Enlightenment, Johann Gottfried von Herder, Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, and Wilhelm von Humboldt secularized this mystical concept, transforming Bildung into the harmonious, all-round cultivation of an individual's innate intellectual, aesthetic, and moral capacities within human civilization.
