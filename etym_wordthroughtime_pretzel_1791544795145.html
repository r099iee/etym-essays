- 8th–12th c. – Monastic devotional origin: Born in medieval European monasteries (likely Carolingian Franco-German or northern Italian) as a symbolic Lenten bread shaped like arms crossed over the chest in prayer.
- 14th–16th c. – Guild emblem and civic symbol: Adopted across southern German lands, Switzerland, and Austria as the official heraldic emblem of bakers' guilds, associated with good fortune, weddings, and Christian festivals.
- 18th c. – Hard-baking innovation: The emergence of dry, hard, shelf-stable pretzels alongside traditional soft lye-dipped pretzels (Laugengebäck).
- Mid-19th c. – Transatlantic commercialization: Brought to North America by Pennsylvania Dutch and German immigrants, establishing commercial bakeries (notably Julius Sturgis in Lititz, PA, in 1861) that transformed the pretzel into an American mass-market snack food.
- 20th c. to Present – Figurative and cognitive expansion: Expanded from a savory snack into widespread metaphorical usage to describe human physical contortion, gymnastic maneuvers, and tortured, self-contradictory logic ("pretzel logic").
