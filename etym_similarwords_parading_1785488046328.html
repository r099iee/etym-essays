• FLAUNT: Similar because both mean showing something off conspicuously; differs because flaunting adds aggressive, shameless, morally charged exhibition — it accuses vulgarity — while parading has the lighter, ceremonial note of display for an audience.
  • SHOWCASE: Similar because both mean presenting something for attention; differs because showcasing is modern, neutral, and commercial, suggesting benign presentation of a product, while parading implies proud, self-conscious self-display.
  • DISPLAY: Similar because both mean showing something; differs because display is colourless and merely perceptual, unable to carry the procession-and-audience pageantry of a parade.
  • BRANDISH: Similar because both mean flourishing something in view; differs because brandishing is limited to a weapon or hand-held object and adds menace rather than showmanship.
  • STRUT: Similar because both involve walking or moving to show off; differs because strutting is the exaggerated, self-important gait itself, while parading is the whole ceremonial display of something before an audience.
  • BOAST: Similar because both mean drawing attention to one's merits; differs because boasting is verbal, claiming excellence in speech, while parading is demonstrative, marching something out for inspection rather than merely claiming it.
  • EXHIBIT: Similar because both mean showing something publicly; differs because exhibiting is neutral and formal, describing display in a museum or a court, without the note of proud vanity that parading carries.
  • FLASH: Similar because both mean showing something briefly and showily; differs because flashing is quick and often vulgar (flashing a wad of cash), while parading is a sustained, ceremonial display.
  • TROT OUT: Similar because both mean producing something for display, often with an air of showing off; differs because trotting out is informal and dismissive, presenting something repeatedly or predictably, while parading adds the ceremonial procession.
  • FLOURISH: Similar because both mean displaying something with a showy gesture; differs because flourishing emphasizes the sweeping, triumphant gesture itself (a flourish of the pen), while parading emphasizes marching something out before an audience.
