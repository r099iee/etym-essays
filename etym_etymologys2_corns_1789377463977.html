PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*ḱer- / *ḱerh₂-
"horn, head, upper part of the body; hard substance"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*kornu-
"horn, hard projection"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
cornū (neut. noun "horn, hoof, hard horn-like projection") → Vulgar Latin *corna (fem. collective)
"horn, horn-like hardness, callosity"
↓
Old French (c. 11th–13th c.)
corne (diminutive cornet)
"horn; callous skin, localized thickening of the epidermis on the foot"
↓
Middle English (c. 13th–15th c.)
corne (plural cornes)
"horny growth on the foot or toe, callus"
Note: Distinct from native Germanic 'corn' (grain/cereal, from PIE *g̑r̥hₐ-nóm). Podiatric corn is a Romance borrowing from Latin cornū ('horn'). Popular association between the two words arose due to phonetic convergence and the kernel-like appearance of foot corns.
↓
Early Modern to Modern English (c. 16th c. – present)
corn (plural corns)
"painful localized hyperkeratotic lesion on the foot"
↓
