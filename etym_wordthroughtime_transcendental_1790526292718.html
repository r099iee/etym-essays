- 1200s–1500s: Medieval Scholastic metaphysics - Coined in Scholastic Latin to designate universal attributes of Being (Truth, Goodness, Unity) that transcend Aristotle's ten categories.
- 1780s–1830s: The Kantian Epistemological Revolution - Redefined by Immanuel Kant to designate the necessary a priori cognitive structures (space, time, causality) that make human experience possible.
- 1830s–1860s: American Transcendentalism - Reinterpreted by Emerson and Thoreau as a literary and spiritual philosophy emphasizing intuition, individual divinity, and communion with nature.
- 1870s–1920s: Mathematical rigorous formalization - Codified in number theory for numbers (like $\pi$ and $e$) and functions that cannot be expressed as roots of rational polynomial equations.
- 1960s–Present: Popular culture, meditation, and general prose - Broadened through Transcendental Meditation (TM) and everyday language to describe deeply elevating, sublime, or consciousness-expanding experiences.
