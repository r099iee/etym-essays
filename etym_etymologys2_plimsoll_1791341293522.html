Note: The family surname Plimsoll (with historical orthographic variants including Plymsole, Plymstoll, and Plemstall) is an English habitational surname. Onomastic scholars trace it to the parish of Plemstall (or Plemstowe) near Chester in Cheshire. The toponym is an Old English compound formed from the personal name of the venerable 9th-century Archbishop of Canterbury, St. Plegmund (from Old English plegan "to play, strive, or dance" + mund "protection, guardian"), combined with stōw ("holy place, place of assembly, or sanctuary"). Phonetic attrition, dialectal reduction of the medial consonant cluster, and assimilation over several centuries softened Plegmundestow through Plemstow into the distinctive surname Plimsoll, which achieved worldwide renown solely through the moral crusade of Samuel Plimsoll.
