- Old English "drēorig" - a word with blood on it: it meant "gory, blood-stained", the grief of a lord who had watched his retainers fall being felt as a kind of bleeding; physical gore and emotional sorrow were never far apart.
- 14th century - the bloody sense faded and Middle English "drery" meant simply sorrowful, dismal, gloomy — a mood of the person rather than of the battlefield.
- 16th-17th century to modern - "dreary" made its great shift from a person's grief to a place's atmosphere: a landscape, road, or sky could now be dreary, cheerless, dull, and monotonous, and today it is the grey, wearying, colourless word for draining weather, scenes, and lives.
