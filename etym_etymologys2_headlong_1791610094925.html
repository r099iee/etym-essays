PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*kaput- + *-ling-
"head" + adverbial suffix
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*haubudą
"head"
↓
Old English (c. 9th c.)
hēafod + -ling
"head" + directional suffix
↓
Middle English (c. 1250)
hedlyng
"head foremost, headfirst"
↓
Middle English (c. 1400)
hedlong
"headfirst, rash, precipitate"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–present)
headlong
"with the head foremost; rash, impetuous"

Note: The terminal element was originally the Germanic adverbial suffix -ling (denoting direction or condition, seen in darkling, sideling, and flatling). In late Middle English, the unstressed suffix was altered by folk etymology through association with the adjective/adverb long, producing the modern form headlong parallel to sidelong.
