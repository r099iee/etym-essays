Note on debate. The Proto-Germanic form *bōsm- is entirely secure, with an unbroken and well-distributed set of cognates in Old English, Old Saxon, Old Frisian, Old Norse, Middle Dutch and German. Everything before it is not. It is conventionally carried back to PIE *bheh2- ‘to be warm, to heat’, on the general ground that a word for the chest may have arisen from the warmth of the body or of the hearth, but this root is known otherwise only from a thin set of forms, and the semantic route to a word meaning ‘breast’ is not demonstrable. Competing proposals have connected the word with Latin bustum and Greek buston ‘breast’, or with other roots entirely, and no derivation has won general acceptance. The one thing that can be said with confidence is that the word is genuinely Germanic in its form and that its prehistory is open.
