• Holiness: Similar because both name the state of being set apart as sacred and pure; differs because holiness is the native Germanic word rooted in the divine presence of God (Old English halig), warm and devotional, whereas sanctity is Latinate, formal, and heavily extended to secular legal and ethical inviolability (sanctity of life, of contracts).
  • Sacredness: Similar because both denote the quality of being consecrated and beyond violation; differs because sacredness centres on being set apart for worship or divine service, while sanctity carries an additional stress on moral purity and inherent, unconditional value.
  • Inviolability: Similar because both convey protection from violation or infringement; differs because inviolability is a technical, largely secular legal and political term (inviolability of diplomats, of borders), whereas sanctity retains spiritual and moral resonance even inside legal phrases.
  • Piety: Similar because both belong to the religious sphere; differs because piety describes a person's devout behaviour, reverence, and duty toward God, while sanctity is the holy state itself - the quality, not the practice.
  • Saintliness: Similar because both derive from sanctus and denote the quality of being holy; differs because saintliness applies specifically to the personal moral perfection attributed to a saint, whereas sanctity generalizes to things, institutions, and principles.
  • Purity: Similar because both suggest moral cleanness and freedom from taint; differs because purity is a broader, neutral term (pure water, pure motives, pure science) with no built-in claim of being set apart, while sanctity always implies consecration and inviolability.
  • Consecration: Similar because both grow from the same Latin stem and concern the sacred; differs because consecration is the act of making something holy, a rite or event, whereas sanctity is the resulting state or quality.
