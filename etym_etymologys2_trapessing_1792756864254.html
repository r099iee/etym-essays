Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*trapp- / *tramp-
"to tread, stamp, walk heavily"
↓
Old French / Middle French (c. 13th c.)
treper / traper
"to stamp the feet, hop, trudge about"
↓
Middle Low German / German Dialect (c. 15th c.)
trapsen / trappsen
"to walk noisily, trudge heavily, pad about"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1590)
trapes / traipse
"a slovenly trailing woman; to trudge through mud"
↓
Dialectal English (18th–19th c.)
trapesing / trapessing
"wandering aimlessly, trudging wearily through dirt"
↓
Modern English (20th c.–present)
trapessing
"wandering carelessly or intrusive foot-slogging"
