[ROOT 1: KING]
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*ǵenh₁-
"to beget, produce, give birth"
[ultimate root of kin, lineage, and generation]
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*kunją
"family, kin, race, noble lineage"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 200 BCE)
*kuningaz
"leader of the kin, monarch, chieftain"
[formed with patronymic/associative suffix *-ingaz]
↓
Old English (c. 700–900 CE)
cyning / cyng
"king, sovereign ruler, chieftain"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200–1400 CE)
king / kyng
"king, sovereign monarch; preeminent exemplar"

[ROOT 2: FISHER]
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*peysk- / *pisk-
"fish"
[ancient European root for fish; cognate with Latin piscis]
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*fiskaz
"fish"
[Grimm's Law: PIE *p- shifts to Germanic *f-]
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 300 BCE)
*fiskōną
"to catch fish, to fish"
↓
Old English (c. 750–950 CE)
fiscere
"fisherman, one who catches fish"
[agent noun formed with agentive suffix -ere]
↓
Middle English (c. 1200–1400 CE)
fischere / fisher
"fisher, angler, catcher of fish"

[COMPOUND SYNTHESIS]
Late Middle English (c. 1400–1450 CE)
kinges fisshere
"the king's fisher; preeminent avian angler"
[genitive compound honoring the bird's supreme diving mastery]
↓
Early Modern to Contemporary English (16th c.–present)
kingfisher
"plunge-diving coraciiform bird of the family Alcedinidae"
[coalesced compound noun replacing Old English isfugol and Latin alcedo]
