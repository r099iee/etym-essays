- Falling away, disapproving - in sixteenth- and seventeenth-century English decadent names persons and ages sunk from former virtue; connotation stern, pulpit and historian’s gloom.
- Imperial diagnosis, portentous - through the eighteenth century, under Gibbon’s shadow, it becomes the grand term for Rome-like decline; perception learned, anxious, prophetic.
- Aesthetic badge, defiant - in late nineteenth-century Paris and London the Décadents invert the insult into style; connotation glamorous, perfumed, deliberately scandalous.
- Notorious and prosecuted, anxious - around the Wilde trials decadence signals criminalised desire; perception fearful and prurient, glamour curdling into evidence.
- Playful indulgence, affectionate - in contemporary consumer prose decadent desserts and weekends keep only a wink of guilt; connotation yummy, guilt as garnish, the fall reduced to calories.
