• streamers: Similar because both are long, narrow ribbons or bands of fabric that flutter freely in the wind; differs because streamers can be informal paper or ribbon decorations used for parties and celebrations without heraldic or nautical meaning, whereas pennants are triangular, tapered fabric flags carrying military, naval, or sporting significance.
  • pennons: Similar because pennon is the direct historical ancestor of pennant, representing a triangular or swallow-tailed flag; differs because a pennon refers specifically to the medieval knight's lance-flag bearing his personal heraldic badge, whereas pennants encompass naval commissioning flags, sports championship banners, and rigging cords.
  • banners: Similar because both are cloth emblems displaying heraldry, allegiances, or slogans; differs because a banner is typically large, rectangular or square, suspended from a crossbar or two poles, and historically denoted high noble rank (such as a knight banneret or sovereign), whereas pennants are long, slender, and tapered.
  • standards: Similar because both are military and heraldic flags flown to identify a commander; differs because a standard is a large, elaborate ceremonial flag tapered slightly toward a rounded split end, bearing the personal crest, badge, and motto of a monarch or supreme military commander, kept stationary at headquarters rather than carried into hand-to-hand combat.
  • ensigns: Similar because both are official maritime flags flown by ships at sea; differs because an ensign is a large rectangular national flag flown at the stern (the flagstaff or taffrail) to denote the vessel's nationality, whereas a pennant is a long, thin streamer flown from the masthead to signify naval commission or signaling codes.
  • flags: Similar because both are fabric devices attached to a staff or halyard for visual communication and symbolism; differs because flag is the broad generic superordinate category encompassing all shapes, sizes, and functions, while pennants are specifically triangular or tapering streamers.
  • buntings: Similar because both involve decorative or signaling maritime cloth; differs because bunting refers either to the lightweight, open-weave wool fabric from which flags are made, or to strings of festive festive draperies and multicolored semicircles, rather than individual tapering flags.
  • guidons: Similar because both are small military flags used by tactical units; differs because a guidon is a specialized swallow-tailed or pointed flag carried by a military company, battery, or cavalry troop to serve as a marker and rallying unit point in tactical formation.
  • burgees: Similar because both are small, pointed or swallow-tailed maritime flags flown on boats; differs because a burgee is specifically the identifying triangular pennant of a recreational yacht club or sailing organization, flown on private pleasure crafts rather than sovereign warships or castle towers.
  • pendants: Similar because pendant is the direct etymological twin and historical British naval spelling of pennant; differs because pendant today usually refers to a hanging piece of jewelry worn around the neck, or in strict nautical engineering, to a short wire rope tackle rather than the symbolic cloth flag.
  • vexilla: Similar because both are ancient military flags flown by troops; differs because a vexillum (plural vexilla) was an ancient Roman military standard consisting of a square cloth hanging vertically from a horizontal crossbar mounted on a spear, the Latin origin of the term vexillology.
  • colors: Similar because both are consecrated flags representing military units; differs because military colors (the regimental and national flags of an infantry battalion) are sacred rectangular emblems carried under armed escort and presented with formal honors, unlike everyday masthead pennants.
