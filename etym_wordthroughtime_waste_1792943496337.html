- Roman — vastus: empty, desolate, immense — the Latin adjective for unfilled space and untilled ground.
- 13th c. — Norman gift: Old French wast gives Middle English waste for untilled commons and the verb to despoil.
- Medieval — the king's waste: unenclosed commons where peasants grazed and gathered; lawyers argue over waste while poets ride across it.
- 16th–17th c. — moralising spreads: to waste time, substance, and youth becomes the preacher's and the proverb's theme.
- 19th c. — empire and industry: colonial wastes await the plough while factories invent industrial waste — the word fills at both ends.
- Today — green and guilty: waste means landfill, emissions, and food waste on one side, and wasted chances on the other — geography turned into ethics.
