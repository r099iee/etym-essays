• Emanate: Similar because it means flowing out from a source; differs because emanate is a neutral streaming-forth with no body, whereas exude keeps the sweaty, pore-level intimacy — a quality seeping from a thing's very substance.
  • Radiate: Similar because it means giving off from a centre; differs because radiate implies light or energy streaming outward with force — brighter and more deliberate — while exude is slow, passive, involuntary.
  • Ooze: Similar because it means slow, gradual seepage; differs because ooze is the colloquial, earthy cousin — thick, visible liquid — while exude is formal and can be wholly figurative, governing charm and darkness as well as sap.
  • Seep: Similar because it means passing slowly through a surface; differs because seep describes the movement of a liquid through pores or cracks, without the sense of the thing's inner state, while exude makes the quality a secretion of the being.
  • Secrete: Similar because it means producing and releasing a substance; differs because secrete is the technical, biological word for a gland's product, while exude is the general word for passive emission, physical or figurative.
  • Emit: Similar because it means sending forth; differs because emit is neutral and general, active or passive, carrying no bodily image, while exude is always the body's confession rather than its speech.
  • Transpire: Similar because it means passing off through a surface; differs because transpire is technical (of plants giving off moisture) or now chiefly means 'to become known,' while exude keeps the sense of a quality seeping from a being.
  • Perspire: Similar because it is the physiological act of sweating; differs because perspire is the polite word for the body's sweat itself, while exude generalises the image to anything oozing through a surface — and, figuratively, to qualities.
