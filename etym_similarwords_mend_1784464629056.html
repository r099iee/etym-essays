• Repair: Similar because both mean to restore something broken to sound condition; differs because repair is more formal and mechanical, often professional, while mend is plainer and more domestic handwork.
  • Fix: Similar because both mean to make good something broken; differs because fix is the broad modern verb covering any remedy, attachment, or arrangement, while mend keeps the older craft-and-fabric flavour of making whole.
  • Patch: Similar because both address damage; differs because patching covers a hole partially or temporarily with a piece, while mending restores to sound condition more fully.
  • Restore: Similar because both return something to good condition; differs because restore implies a fuller, sometimes original-state overhaul, while mend is the simple immediate repair.
  • Amend: Similar because both descend from Latin ēmendāre "to correct"; differs because amend kept the fuller form and shifted toward revising texts, laws, and behaviour, while mend shortened and kept the concrete repair sense.
  • Remedy: Similar because both mean to set right a defect; differs because remedy is Latinate, formal, and usually about curing a problem or disease, while mend is plain and often about hands-on repair.
  • Heal: Similar because both can mean to make healthy or whole again; differs because heal is restricted to bodies, wounds, and figurative harm, while mend freely applies to objects and tools.
  • Correct: Similar because both remove an error or fault; differs because correct is intellectual and evaluative, fixing mistakes, while mend restores broken physical or moral wholeness.
  • Rectify: Similar because both put right what is wrong; differs because rectify is formal and technical, righting an error or an electrical circuit, while mend is homely and concrete.
  • Darn: Similar because both are needle-and-thread repairs of cloth; differs because darning is specifically the weaving of threads across a hole in fabric, while mending covers all repairs.
  • Refurbish / Renovate: Similar because both make worn things good again; differs because they imply extensive cleaning, repair, and renewal of whole rooms or buildings, while mend is a small, single repair.
  • Cure: Similar because both can mean to heal; differs because cure is about treating an illness to health, while mend extends to objects, relationships, and habits.
