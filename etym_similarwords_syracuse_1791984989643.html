• Siracusa: Similar because it names exactly the same city and is increasingly used in English travel writing; differs because Siracusa is the Italian official form and signals the modern municipality, while Syracuse is the English and historical form that evokes the Greek and Roman city.
  • Ortygia: Similar because it is the name of the island that formed the original settlement and is often used by metonymy for the old town; differs because Ortygia denotes only that island and its quarter, not the whole city or its territory.
  • Corinth: Similar because it was Syracuse's mother city and shared its Dorian institutions and its commercial character; differs because Corinth is the metropolis on the Isthmus, the founder rather than the colony, and its own history followed a different arc.
  • Athens: Similar because it was Syracuse's great rival in the fifth century and the two cities are permanently linked by the expedition of 415–413; differs because Athens is the Ionian, imperial, naval democracy, while Syracuse was Dorian, land-based and ruled for long periods by tyrants.
  • Carthage: Similar because it contested Syracuse for control of Sicily across three centuries and shared its Mediterranean trading world; differs because Carthage was a Phoenician colony in Africa, an overseas empire rather than a Greek polis, and it was destroyed in 146 BCE while Syracuse survived as a provincial capital.
  • Messina: Similar because it is the other great harbour city of Sicily and shared the island's Greek, Roman and Norman fate; differs because Messina, ancient Zancle, sits at the strait and looks to Italy, whereas Syracuse faces the open sea and the Greek east.
  • Acragas: Similar because it was a great Greek city of Sicily, famous for its temples and its wealth, and also fell to Rome; differs because Acragas, modern Agrigento, was a Doric foundation of Gela with no Archimedes and no siege legend, and it declined earlier.
  • Sparta: Similar because Syracuse was Dorian and allied with Sparta during the Peloponnesian War, and Athenian propaganda treated the two as kindred; differs because Sparta was a land power of the Peloponnese with a unique social system, while Syracuse was a commercial port city with a mixed population.
