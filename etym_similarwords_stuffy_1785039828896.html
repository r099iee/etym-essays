• Close: Similar because both describe air that is dead, heavy, and unventilated; differs because close is the older, subtler, more formal word for the same airlessness, while stuffy adds the vivid image of being plugged or crammed up.
  • Airless: Similar because both describe a space without circulating fresh air; differs because airless is a neutral, scientific statement of fact, while stuffy carries the sensory and moral weight of stale, oppressive enclosure.
  • Stifling: Similar because both describe air that is hard to breathe; differs because stifling is a degree stronger — it suggests active suffocation and overwhelming heat or oppression, while stuffy is milder, mere unpleasant closeness.
  • Suffocating: Similar because both describe air that makes breathing difficult; differs because suffocating is extreme and frightening — a genuine struggle for air — while stuffy is a homely word for mild discomfort.
  • Muggy / Sultry: Similar because both describe uncomfortable, oppressive air; differ because they specifically add heat and humidity (tropical or thunderstorm warmth), while stuffy emphasizes staleness and lack of ventilation rather than heat alone.
  • Musty: Similar because both describe stale, unpleasant indoor air; differs because musty adds the specific odor of damp, mold, and age (old books, cellars), while stuffy is about lack of fresh air rather than dampness.
  • Fusty: Similar because both describe stale, sour-smelling enclosures; differs because fusty is archaic and dialectal and leans toward rank, moldy staleness, while stuffy is the standard modern word.
  • Oppressive: Similar because both describe a heavy, uncomfortable atmosphere; differs because oppressive widens beyond air to moral, emotional, or political weight — a regime or a gloom can be oppressive, but only a space can be stuffy in the literal sense.
  • Stiff / Prim / Pompous: Similar because these describe a stuffy (formal, rigid) person; differ because they name the formality directly without the airless metaphor — they lack stuffy's image of a mind sealed against fresh air.
  • Congested: Similar because both can describe a blocked, stuffy nose; differs because congested is the medical/technical term for swelling or blockage, while stuffy is the everyday word and also applies to rooms and manners.
