• Inuit: Similar because it denotes the same Arctic peoples in Canada and Greenland; differs because it is the self-designation meaning the people and excludes Yupik peoples who are not Inuit.
  • Yupik: Similar because it denotes western Arctic peoples also once called Eskimo; differs because it refers specifically to Yupik/Cupik groups of Alaska and Siberia, not to Inuit.
  • Inupiat: Similar because it denotes northern Alaskan Inuit peoples; differs because it is a specific regional nation, not a pan-Arctic umbrella.
  • Yup'ik / Cup'ik: Similar because they are Central Alaskan peoples formerly grouped as Eskimo; differs because each is a distinct linguistic-cultural group with its own name.
  • Alutiiq / Sugpiaq: Similar because Pacific Gulf Yupik peoples were historically labelled Eskimo; differs because they have a distinct identity and language on Kodiak and the Alaska Peninsula.
  • Unangan / Aleut: Similar because Aleut peoples are close relatives within the Eskimo-Aleut family and were sometimes loosely called Eskimo; differs because they are culturally and linguistically separate and never accept Eskimo for themselves.
  • Inuvialuit: Similar because Western Canadian Inuit were once called Eskimo; differs because it is the specific modern land-claim group of the western Arctic.
  • Kalaallit: Similar because Greenlanders were historically called Eskimo; differs because it is the Greenlandic self-term for Greenland Inuit.
  • Arctic peoples: Similar because it covers the same circumpolar population inclusively; differs because it is a neutral geographic umbrella avoiding the exonym problem.
  • Circumpolar peoples: Similar because it denotes Indigenous northern peoples around the pole; differs because it is broader, also including Sami, Chukchi and others.
  • Alaska Native: Similar because it includes Eskimo peoples in Alaska; differs because it is broader, also covering Athabaskan, Tlingit, Haida and others.
  • Northerner: Similar because it can refer to inhabitants of the far north; differs because it is generic and non-ethnic, lacking any cultural specificity.
