• Wail: Similar because both are long, loud cries of grief or distress; differs because a wail is plaintive and human, a lament, whereas a howl is the more animal, uncontrolled and throaty cry — a wolf howls, a mourner wails.
  • Scream: Similar because both are loud, piercing cries; differs because a scream is high-pitched, piercing, and distinctly human or terrified, while a howl is lower, prolonged, and wolfish or wind-like.
  • Shriek: Similar because both are sharp, loud cries; differs because a shriek is even higher and more piercing than a scream, while a howl is a long, low, sustained open-mouth cry.
  • Yell: Similar because both are loud cries; differs because a yell is loud and aggressive, often with words or anger behind it, while a howl is wordless, doleful and animal in register.
  • Bellow: Similar because both are deep, loud animal cries; differs because a bellow is a throaty roar of a bull or a shouting man, full of force and threat, while a howl is a prolonged, mournful, open-mouthed wail.
  • Roar: Similar because both are powerful animal or human cries; differs because a roar is deep, throaty and imposing, expressive of fury or a lion's power, while a howl is long, rising, and expressive of pain or distress.
  • Bay: Similar because both describe the prolonged cry of hounds and wolves; differs because baying is specifically the deep, sustained cry of hunting dogs in pursuit, while howling is the more general, mournful, wind-like cry.
  • Cry: Similar because both are emotional vocal sounds; differs because cry is the general word for any tearful or distressed sound, while howl specifies the long, open-throated, wordless, animal character of the cry.
  • Ululate: Similar because both describe long, wailing, open-throated cries — ululate is, in fact, the Latin-rooted cousin of howl's imitative family; differs because ululate is technical and exotic, usually of ritual wailing, while howl is the plain everyday English word.
  • Screech: Similar because both are harsh, prolonged sounds; differs because a screech is grating, high and shrill (of tyres, owls, nails), while a howl is lower, smoother and doleful.
  • Sob: Similar because both can express grief; differs because sobbing is broken, gasping weeping, while howling is a single long, loud, open-mouthed cry — a person can howl with grief but not sob with it in the same way.
  • Hoot: Similar because both are prolonged vocal sounds (also of owls and sirens); differs because a hoot is short, hollow and repetitive, while a howl is a long rising wail.
