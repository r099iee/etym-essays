• Handle: Similar because both are the part by which a tool or weapon is gripped; differs because handle is the general term for any grip, while hilt is specifically the handle-complex of a sword, dagger, or bladed weapon.
  • Haft: Similar because both are weapon or tool handles; differs because haft is more often used of axes, knives, or spears, not the sword-default hilt.
  • Grip: Similar because both name the holding part; differs because grip names only the holding surface, while hilt includes guard and pommel.
  • Pommel: Similar because both are parts of a sword's handle end; differs because the pommel is only the counterweight knob at the end of the hilt, not the whole handle.
  • Guard / Crossguard: Similar because both are parts of the hilt assembly; differs because the guard is the bar protecting the hand, only one component of the hilt.
  • Blade: Similar because both are parts of a sword; differs because the blade is the steel cutting edge, the opposite end of the weapon from the hilt.
  • Shaft: Similar because both are the long holding part of a weapon; differs because shaft is used of spears, arrows, and tools, not of swords.
  • Stock: Similar because both are the handle-like part of a weapon; differs because stock is the wooden body of a firearm or crossbow, a different weapon family.
  • Sword / Blade (as metonym): Similar because both can stand for the whole weapon; differs because hilt names only the handle, used metonymically, while sword and blade name the whole weapon or its cutting edge.
  • Scalp (of a knife) / grip (as noun): Similar because both are the holding part of a knife; differs because these are more technical or narrower terms, while hilt covers the whole handle assembly of a sword.
