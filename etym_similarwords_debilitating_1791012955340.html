• weakening: Similar because it denotes loss of strength; differs because it is generic and milder, without the clinical severity of debilitating.
  • enervating: Similar because it means draining energy and vigour; differs because it is more literary and suggests nervous exhaustion rather than medical incapacity.
  • exhausting: Similar because it describes extreme tiredness; differs because exhaustion can be temporary and recoverable, while debilitating implies impaired capacity.
  • draining: Similar because it evokes sapping of energy; differs because it is informal and often emotional, less clinical than debilitating.
  • crippling: Similar because it means severely disabling; differs because crippling suggests total disablement or lameness, stronger and blunter than debilitating.
  • incapacitating: Similar because it means rendering unable to function; differs because it implies complete inability, while debilitating allows painful continued functioning.
  • paralysing: Similar because it denotes loss of ability to act, especially from fear; differs because it stresses immobility and sudden onset.
  • prostrating: Similar because it means laying low through illness or grief; differs because it is literary and suggests collapse.
  • sapping: Similar because it means gradually undermining strength; differs because it emphasises slow erosion rather than the resulting weak state.
  • undermining: Similar because it weakens foundations of health or confidence; differs because it is more gradual and often covert.
  • enfeebling: Similar because it is a near-synonym for making feeble; differs because it is rarer, more formal, and often age-related.
  • devastating: Similar because it describes overwhelming harmful effects; differs because it stresses destruction and emotional shock, not specifically weakness.
