• Devoid of: Similar because both state total absence of something; differs because devoid of is equally absolute but more neutral and cerebral ('devoid of interest'), without destitute's weight of loss and formality.
  • Bereft of: Similar because both mean completely deprived; differs because bereft implies sorrowful, violent loss (bereft of a child), while destitute need not be emotional and is often material or abstract.
  • Lacking: Similar because both denote deficiency; differs because lacking is plainer and allows degree ('lacking in taste'), whereas destitute asserts absolute, utter emptiness.
  • Wanting: Similar because both can mean 'not having'; differs because wanting is more archaic-literal and softer in force, while destitute is absolute and formal.
  • Deficient in: Similar because both concern insufficiency; differs because deficient is the clinical, comparative term admitting some is present but not enough, not the absolute emptiness of destitute.
  • Impoverished: Similar because both can describe poverty; differs because impoverished is economic and can be relative, while destitute insists on complete, absolute want.
  • Penniless: Similar because both can mean without means; differs because penniless is concrete and colloquial about money alone, while destitute covers all resources and support.
  • Indigent: Similar because both denote poverty; differs because indigent is the legal-administrative term for the needy, without destitute's absolute or abstract senses.
  • Needy: Similar because both describe those without means; differs because needy implies a need for help that may be partial, whereas destitute is total.
  • Poor: Similar because both describe limited means; differs because poor is the general, relative word for poverty, while destitute is poverty at the extreme bottom.
  • Forlorn: Similar because both can describe abandonment and desolation; differs because forlorn stresses sadness and spirit, not the material fact of having nothing.
  • Forsaken / Abandoned: Similar because both preserve the obsolete literal sense of destitute; differs because they are active verbs about being left behind, while modern destitute is an adjective of lack or poverty.
  • Destitution: Similar because it is the abstract noun of the same idea; differs because it names the state of extreme want itself, whereas destitute is the adjective describing a person or quality.
