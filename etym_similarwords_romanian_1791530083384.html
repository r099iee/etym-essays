• Rumanian: an older English spelling of the same language and people; identical in reference, but archaic and now largely replaced, and it can look old-fashioned or careless in modern prose.
  • Roumanian: a nineteenth-century spelling reflecting French roumain; similar in meaning, but obsolete in English and associated with older scholarship and travel writing.
  • Daco-Romanian: the precise linguistic name for standard Romanian as opposed to Aromanian and the other Balkan Romance varieties; similar in reference, but narrower and technical, and it foregrounds the Dacian continuity question.
  • Moldovan: the variety of Romanian spoken in the Republic of Moldova; similar because it is mutually intelligible with standard Romanian, but the label is politically contested and historically tied to Soviet language policy.
  • Vlach: the traditional exonym for Romanian-speaking populations in the Balkans; similar in reference to Romanian-speaking communities, but it is an external, often archaic label and can cover Aromanians and other groups, not just Romanians.
  • Aromanian: a close Balkan Romance relative spoken in Greece, Albania and North Macedonia; similar because it descends from the same Latin base and is often called "Vlach", but it is a distinct language with its own standard and diaspora.
  • Wallachian: the adjective of Wallachia, the southern Romanian principality; similar as a regional label, but it names one historical province and its elite, not the modern nation or language.
  • Romance: the family of languages descended from Latin; similar as a classificatory term, but it is far broader and includes French, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese.
  • Latin: the ancestor language; similar because Romanian is its direct descendant and the comparison is central to Romanian identity, but Latin is the source, not the modern language.
  • Romani: the Indo-Aryan language of the Roma; similar only in sound and spelling, and a frequent confusable, but it is unrelated to Romanian and the confusion is offensive to both communities.
  • Roman: "of Rome"; similar as the etymological root of the national name, but it refers to ancient Rome or to the Roman people, not to the modern country.
  • Balkan Romance: the branch comprising Romanian and its southern relatives; similar as a classification, but it is a comparative-linguistic category, not an ethnic or national name.
