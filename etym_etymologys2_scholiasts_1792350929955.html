Note on semantic derivation: Historical linguists (e.g. Pierre Chantraine, Robert Beekes) trace Greek skholḗ back to the PIE root *seǵʰ- ("to hold, possess"), with the specialized Greek semantic shift toward "holding back, cessation from labor, leisure". In aristocratic Greek society, leisure (*skholḗ*) was the essential prerequisite for philosophical contemplation and debate. From "leisure" the word shifted to "places where leisure is spent in learned discussion" (the ancestor of modern school). In the Hellenistic era, the diminutive noun skhólion was coined to mean "a learned exercise produced during leisure", specifically a marginal note or commentary. The agent noun skholiastḗs ("one who writes scholia") subsequently emerged in late antiquity to denote professional commentators.
