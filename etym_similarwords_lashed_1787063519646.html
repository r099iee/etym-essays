• bound: similar because both mean tying firmly; differs because bind is general (bind a book, be bound by rules) while lash specifically means rope or flexible-cord binding, usually on ships or outdoors.
  • tied: similar fastening; differs because tie is everyday and light (tie shoelaces, tie a ribbon), while lashed is heavy-duty, storm-proof binding.
  • fastened: similar securing; differs because fasten covers every means (buttons, hooks, glue) while lash specifies rope-work.
  • secured: similar making firm; differs because secure is abstract and often metaphorical (secure a loan, secure a border), while lash is physical rope-work.
  • strapped: similar binding with flexible material; differs because a strap is a flat band while a lashing is a round cord or rope.
  • roped: similar tying with rope; differs because rope is an everyday word, while lash is the sailor's technical verb.
  • tethered: similar tying an animal or object to a fixed point; differs because a tether is single-point restraint of a living thing, while a lashing is multi-point binding of objects.
  • hitched: similar attaching; differs because hitch implies a knot or hook (hitch a trailer), while lash means wrapping cord around and around.
  • moored: similar securing a vessel; differs because moor anchors a boat to shore or a buoy, while lash fastens it to another object.
  • whipped: similar the striking sense; differs because whip names the instrument and is freely metaphorical (whipped cream), while lash is the physical blow of a flexible thong or weather.
  • flogged: similar beating with a whip; differs because flog is formal, legal punishment vocabulary, while lash is plainer and also names wind and rain.
  • thrashed: similar violent beating; differs because thrash is wild and uncontrolled, while lash is deliberate and rhythmic.
  • beat: similar striking; differs because beat is the most general strike-word, while lash specifies a whip-like flexible instrument or storm-force weather.
  • attacked: similar the "lash out" sense; differs because attack is general aggression, while lash out is sudden, verbal, whip-like — a single cracking outburst.
