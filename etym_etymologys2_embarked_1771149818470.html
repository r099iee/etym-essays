Root 1: barque / barca "boat" (origin debated)
1. Origin of Late Latin barca debated: (a) possibly from Latin barga "a kind of boat" (attested), itself possibly of Gaulish/Celtic origin (Gaulish *barga "boat," on one theory a reflex of PIE *bher- "to carry" through the notion of a "carrier"); or (b) possibly from Greek baris "Egyptian river boat" (attested; itself from Egyptian bar / baare "boat"). The exact path is uncertain, and both routes are presented without forcing a choice.
2. Late Latin barca "a boat, small vessel" (attested from the 6th c. CE onward; also the source of Romance words for "boat": Italian barca, Spanish barca, French barque).
3. Old French / Middle French barque "a small ship, bark, boat" (attested 12th-13th c.).
4. Middle French embarquer "to put on board a ship" — compound of em- "in, into" + barque (attested 16th c.).
5. English embark "to go on board a ship" — borrowed from Middle French embarquer (1540s).
6. English embarked — past tense and past participle, and by extension "having begun a venture" (16th-17th c.).
7. EMBARKED — present-day form.

Root 2: em- "in, into" (from Latin in-)
1. Proto-Indo-European *h1en "in, into" (reconstructed; attested cognates: Greek en "in," Latin in "in," Gothic in, English "in").
2. Latin in "in, into, on" — preposition and prefix (attested).
3. Old French en- / em- "in, into" — preposition and prefix, with em- used before b, p, and m (attested; from Latin in-).
4. em- prefix in Middle French embarquer "to put on board" -> English embark -> EMBARKED.

Debate note: the deeper ancestry of Latin barca (Gaulish vs. Greek/Egyptian) is genuinely uncertain; English "bark" (a small ship), "barque," and "barge" all descend from this contested family. The prefix stage from PIE *h1en to Old French en-/em- is standard and uncontroversial.
