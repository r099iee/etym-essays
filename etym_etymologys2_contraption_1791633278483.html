Note: Contraption arose in early 19th-century northern English and Scottish dialect as a humorous portmanteau. Etymologists identify it as a blend of contrive / contrivance (from Old French controver) with trap (a mechanical snare or fitting, from Old English træppe) or trappings, modeled with the Latinate nominal suffix -tion (as in deception, confection) to bestow an amusing, mock-scholarly air upon an odd device.
