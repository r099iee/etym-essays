SHOULDER
"shoulder; projecting part; to push or bear"

NOTE: The word has no certain cognates outside Germanic (OED). A connection with a PIE root *skel- "to bend, curve" (the shoulder as the bending joint) has been proposed by some scholars but is debated and unproven; other proposals link the word to the Germanic shield-word. The Proto-Germanic form *skuldraz is a reconstruction, not an attested form. The -ou- spelling appears in the 16th century (on the analogy of could, would, should), and the sc- > sh- spelling reflects the normal Old English palatalisation of /sk/ before front vowels, as in ship, shall, sheep.
