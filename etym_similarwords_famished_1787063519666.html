• Starving: Similar because it means suffering extreme hunger; differs because in modern speech starving is the more everyday word for 'very hungry', while famished is more literary and, at full strength, suggests hunger unto death.
  • Ravenous: Similar because it describes intense, wolfish hunger; differs because it stresses the fierceness of the appetite and the eagerness to devour rather than the suffering of want.
  • Hungry: Similar because it is the general word for the need for food; differs because it covers every degree from mild to extreme, while famished is restricted to the extreme end of the scale.
  • Voracious: Similar because it describes a huge appetite; differs because it belongs to the eater's character or habit (a voracious reader, a voracious appetite), not to a temporary state of starvation.
  • Underfed / Malnourished: Similar because both describe a body deprived of food; differ because they are clinical and factual terms about diet, while famished is vivid and emotional about the hunger itself.
  • Peckish: Similar because it occupies the same scale of appetite; differs because it means only slightly hungry — the exact opposite intensity of famished.
  • Emaciated / Gaunt: Similar because a famished person looks emaciated; differ because they describe the wasted appearance produced by hunger rather than the feeling of hunger itself.
