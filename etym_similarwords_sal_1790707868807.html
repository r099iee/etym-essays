• Salé Rovers: Similar because it specifically designates the maritime corsairs operating from Salé; differs because 'Salé' is the proper toponym of the Moroccan city itself, whereas 'Salé Rovers' (or 'Sallee Rovers') refers to the pirate crews and privateer captains who sailed from its harbor.
  • Corsairs: Similar because the mariners of Salé were Muslim corsairs engaged in authorized commerce raiding; differs because 'corsairs' is a general noun for state-authorized privateers in the Mediterranean and Atlantic (both Christian and Muslim), whereas 'Salé' is a specific geographic urban base.
  • Barbary pirates: Similar because Salé was a prominent Atlantic port of the Barbary Coast; differs because 'Barbary pirates' is an overarching regional term encompassing corsairs from Algiers, Tunis, Tripoli, and Morocco.
  • Privateers: Similar because Salé rovers operated with letters of marque and sanction from local governors or the Corsair Republic; differs because 'privateers' is a general naval term for private vessel owners authorized by any government to attack enemy shipping during wartime.
  • Buccaneers: Similar because both were seventeenth-century maritime marauders; differs because 'buccaneers' specifically operated in the Caribbean and tropical Americas, primarily attacking Spanish treasure fleets, whereas Salé corsairs operated in the eastern Atlantic and Mediterranean.
  • Moorish: Similar because Salé was a major Moorish (Andalusian Muslim) coastal citadel; differs because 'Moorish' is a broad historical, architectural, and cultural adjective for the Islamic civilization of North Africa and Andalusia.
  • Freebooters: Similar because both engaged in maritime plundering; differs because 'freebooters' is a broad, often lawless term for pirates operating without state sanction or legal framework.
