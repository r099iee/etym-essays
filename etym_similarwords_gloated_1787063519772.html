• exulted: Similar because both mean to express or feel triumphant joy; differs because exult can be a noble, legitimate triumph with no malice and no loser to gloat over, while gloat is always tinged with smugness at another's expense.
  • rejoiced: Similar because both describe feeling great pleasure; differs because rejoice is general, innocent joy (one can "rejoice with" someone), whereas gloat is self-satisfied pleasure over a failure or victory and is disapproved of.
  • crowed: Similar because both describe triumphant, boastful self-satisfaction; differs because crow stresses the noisy, vocal boasting (like a cock's cry) and can be merely proud (a parent "crowed with delight"), while gloat centres on inward or visible savouring with a mean edge.
  • triumphed: Similar because both involve winning or success; differs because triumph is the neutral/positive verb of achieving victory, with no spite toward the loser; "gloated" adds unkind relish to the winning.
  • sneered: Similar because both show contempt and superiority; differs because sneer is a facial/verbal expression of contempt aimed at a person, while gloat is the satisfaction felt or shown over a misfortune and need not be directed as an insult.
  • relished: Similar because both mean to savour something with pleasure; differs because relish applies to food, sights, and positive experiences without malice, while gloat specifically savours another's loss or one's own advantage over others.
  • savoured: Similar because both describe lingering, enjoyable contemplation; differs because savour is morally neutral (you can savour a meal), while gloat is morally charged and targeted.
  • gratified: Similar because both involve satisfaction; differs because gratified is the satisfaction of having a desire met and can be entirely positive, with no schadenfreude component.
  • revelled: Similar because both mean to take great pleasure in something; differs because revel emphasises boisterous enjoyment and celebration, not the quiet, spiteful dwelling of gloating.
