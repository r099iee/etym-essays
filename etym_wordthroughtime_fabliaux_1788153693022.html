- [12th c. - fabliau (Picard) appears in the hands of Jean Bodel: a ‘little fable’, short, merry, rhymed] - [Perception: a low, urban, laughable genre, opposed to the courtly romance]
- [13th–14th c. - the golden age: c. 150 survive from Flanders to Champagne; Rutebeuf and Gautier le Leu sign their tricks] - [Perception: tavern and fair repertory, anticlerical and cheerfully obscene]
- [15th–16th c. - the genre dissolves into farce, nouvelle and jest-book; the word fades from ordinary speech] - [Perception: old-fashioned and worn out]
- [18th–19th c. - antiquarians publish the manuscripts; Bédier’s Les Fabliaux (1893) turns the word into a scientific genre label] - [Perception: precious documents of medieval realism]
- [20th c. - ‘Chaucer’s fabliaux’ enters English criticism; the word crosses into world-literature departments] - [Perception: a portable term for bawdy comic verse anywhere]
- [Today - the plural fabliaux is standard in literary history; the singular keeps its French flavour; the genre stands for laughter that survives censorship] - [Perception: scholarly, slightly scandalous, indispensable]
