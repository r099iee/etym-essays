CONSTITUENT 1: TOM (Generic Proper Name)
Aramaic (c. 1st c. CE)
T'ōmā
"twin"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 1st c. CE)
Thōmâs
"Thomas (biblical apostle)"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th c. CE)
Thomas
"Thomas (Christian personal name)"
↓
Old French / Middle English (c. 12th c.)
Tomme / Tom
"pet form of Thomas; generic label for everyman or fool"
(as in Tom Fool, Tom Thumb, Tom-boy)

CONSTITUENT 2: NODDY (Simpleton, Fool)
Middle English (c. 14th c.)
nodden (verb)
"to bow, bob the head, fall asleep"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1530)
noddy
"a simpleton, one whose head bobs in vacant agreement; card game"

COMPOUND SYNTHESIS:
Early Modern English (c. 1700)
Tom Noddy / Tomnoddy
"a foolish person, dunce, blockhead; also puffin"
Note: Compounded from archetypal male name Tom + noddy (simpleton).
