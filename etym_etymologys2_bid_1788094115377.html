Lineage 1 — “to offer, command” (from OE bēodan):

PIE (c. 4500–3500 BCE)
*bʰewdʰ-
“to be awake, perceive;
to make known, announce”

↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*beudaną
“to offer, proclaim, command”

↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
bēodan
“to command, offer, announce”

↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
bēden / bidden
“to command, offer, ask”
(merges with biddan)

↓
Modern English
BID
“to command; to offer
(a price); to attempt”


Lineage 2 — “to ask, entreat, pray” (from OE biddan):

PIE (c. 4500–3500 BCE)
*bʰedʰ-
“to bend”

↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*bidjaną
“to ask, entreat, pray”
(lit. “to bend oneself”
in supplication)

↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
biddan
“to ask, entreat, pray”

↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
bidden
“to ask, pray, invite”
(conflated with bēden)

↓
Modern English
BID
“to ask, invite, greet (archaic);
to pray (in ‘bidding prayer’)”

Note on the merger: Modern bid conflates the two Old English verbs bēodan and biddan, whose forms fell together in Middle English; the past forms bade and bid still coexist. The derivation of *bidjaną from PIE “bend” (Ringe, Kroonen) is the standard modern view but is still marked as uncertain in some handbooks.
