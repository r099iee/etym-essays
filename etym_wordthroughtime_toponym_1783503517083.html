Late 19th-Century Scholarly Coinage - Toponym enters English in 1899 as a back-formation from toponymy (1876), a technical term of geography and place-name studies, used only by philologists and surveyors.
20th-Century Academic Spread - The word becomes standard in linguistics, cartography, archaeology, and onomastics, where it names a prized kind of evidence, and appears in compounds like toponymic research and hydronym, still specialist.
Late 20th-21st Century Widening - Toponym moves into popular history, travel writing, and cultural journalism as a vivid word for place-names that preserve lost languages and memories, gaining a reverent, literary aura (as "a sacred vessel of history") while remaining distinctly bookish.
