• Brevity: Similar because both name shortness of speech; differs because brevity is neutral and even admirable — the quality of using few words well — while curtness adds rudeness and cold dismissal; a telegram has brevity but no curtness.
  • Terseness: Similar because both name economical speech; differs because terseness is neat, polished, and graceful, while curtness is angular and unpolished, a brevity that refuses courtesy.
  • Abruptness: Similar because both describe a sudden, jarring break in conversation; differs because abruptness stresses the snap in the thread of talk, while curtness stresses the refusal of expansion and courtesy itself.
  • Brusqueness: Similar because both describe rough, blunt brevity; differs because brusqueness adds an offhand gruffness of manner carried by tone of voice, while curtness is the intellectual coldness of a clipped reply.
  • Bluntness: Similar because both describe direct, unsoftened speech; differs because bluntness foregrounds uncomfortable frankness of content — saying something unpleasant directly — while curtness says little and simply refuses to engage.
  • Succinctness / conciseness: Similar because both name brevity in expression; differs because both are neutral or positive, praised qualities of prose, while curtness is their negative shadow — brevity turned cold and dismissive.
  • Shortness: Similar because both name brevity; differs because shortness is plain and physical, without the pejorative social judgement of curtness.
  • Rudeness / incivility: Similar because both name behaviour that breaches social courtesy; differs because rudeness is general, while curtness names the specific manner of clipped, peremptory brevity.
