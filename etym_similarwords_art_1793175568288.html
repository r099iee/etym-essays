• Are: Similar because both are second-person present forms of be meaning the same thing; differs because are is the living modern form for all persons and numbers, while art is the fossil singular reserved for thou and archaic style.
  • Be: Similar because both belong to the same verb's paradigm of existence; differs because be is the infinitive and base (to be or not to be), while art is one finished inflection of it, usable only with thou.
  • Is: Similar because both state present being of a single subject; differs because is serves the third person (he/she/it is), while art serves only the second singular (thou art).
  • Am: Similar because both assert present existence of the speaker's party; differs because am is first singular (I am), while art addresses the single familiar thou.
  • Wast / wert: Similar because both are archaic thou-forms of be (thou wast); differs because wast and wert are past tense (thou wast there), while art is present (thou art here).
  • Beest: Similar because both are archaic second-singular forms used in old Bibles and spells; differs because beest is subjunctive or future-leaning (that thou beest saved), while art is plain present indicative.
  • Dost: Similar because both are archaic thou-verbs giving period flavour; differs because dost is the thou-form of do (thou dost ask), while art is the thou-form of be.
  • Hast: Similar because both complete the Shakespearean trio with thou; differs because hast is the thou-form of have (thou hast my sword), while art equates and identifies (thou art my shield).
  • Shalt: Similar because both sound the trumpet of old law and prophecy; differs because shalt commands futurity (thou shalt not steal), while art declares present being.
