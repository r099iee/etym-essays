• Go bust: Similar because both denote commercial failure and sudden collapse; differs because go bust is explicitly financial and mercantile (bankruptcy), whereas go phut applies equally to machines, plans, romances, and gadgets with lighter comedic understatement.
  • Fizzle out: Similar because both express an anticlimactic failure; differs because fizzle out describes a gradual, hissing loss of momentum or enthusiasm over time, while go phut connotes an immediate, punctual breakdown or blowout.
  • Collapse: Similar because both describe systems or enterprises falling to ruin; differs because collapse is formal, dramatic, and grave, lacking the humorous bathos and onomatopoeic levity of phut.
  • Flop: Similar because it denotes a conspicuous failure; differs because flop is primarily used for creative or public performances (a play, movie, or product launch that fails to attract an audience), whereas phut focuses on internal functional breakdown.
  • Conk out: Similar because both describe engines or equipment suddenly ceasing to work; differs because conk out is informal twentieth-century slang also widely applied to exhausted people falling asleep, without the colonial Anglo-Indian etymological pedigree of phut.
  • Break down: Similar because both designate mechanical or systemic failure; differs because break down is standard, neutral, and devoid of stylistic or historical colour.
  • Peter out: Similar because both signify an end; differs because peter out implies a slow tapering away to nothingness, like a dwindling vein of ore, whereas go phut is a sudden rupture.
  • Fold: Similar because both denote the closing down of a business or publication; differs because fold is standard corporate and journalistic terminology, lacking the sensory, echoic pop of phut.
  • Go belly-up: Similar because both describe total collapse; differs because go belly-up draws on the visceral imagery of a dead fish floating to the surface, carrying a harsher, more informal American business slang register.
  • Kaput: Similar because both describe something that is completely finished, dead, or broken; differs because kaput is a predicate adjective derived via German from the card game term capot, expressing final devastation rather than an onomatopoeic verbal idiom.
  • Poop out: Similar because both indicate sudden mechanical or physical quitting; differs because poop out is informal American colloquialism centered heavily on bodily exhaustion.
  • Miscarry: Similar because both denote an enterprise failing to reach its intended outcome; differs because miscarry is an elevated, formal, or archaic legal and biological term devoid of colloquial intimacy.
