• disheveled: Similar because both describe an untidy, disordered personal appearance; differs because disheveled (historically meaning "with disarranged hair or clothes") emphasizes rumpled, mussed disruption caused by wind, haste, or distress, whereas unkempt suggests prolonged neglect or failure to comb and groom.
  • tousled: Similar because both refer to mussed or tangled hair; differs because tousled often carries an endearing, playful, or charming connotation (such as a child's bedhead or windblown curls), whereas unkempt is more broadly disordered and neglected.
  • slovenly: Similar because both describe a lack of neatness and order; differs because slovenly carries a severe moral condemnation of habitual laziness, personal dirtiness, and careless negligence.
  • scruffy: Similar because both denote a rugged, unshaven, and informal look; differs because scruffy is an informal, colloquial term often associated with youthful counterculture or deliberately casual style.
  • untidy: Similar because both indicate an absence of neatness; differs because untidy is a generic, everyday term lacking the specific historical imagery of uncombed hair or overgrown wildness.
  • bedraggled: Similar because both depict a ruined or messy appearance; differs because bedraggled specifically implies being soaked with rain, dragged through mud, or limp from wetness.
  • rumpled: Similar because both describe disordered surfaces; differs because rumpled applies primarily to creased and wrinkled fabrics or papers rather than uncombed hair or wild gardens.
  • shaggy: Similar because both describe long, untrimmed, thick hair or vegetation; differs because shaggy focuses on the physical texture and heavy density of hair or wool rather than the lack of grooming.
  • frowzy: Similar because both describe an unkempt, slovenly appearance; differs because frowzy adds a strong implication of musty odor, stale air, and unwashed neglect.
  • dilapidated: Similar because both apply to neglected physical environments; differs because dilapidated refers strictly to structures in a state of architectural ruin, decay, and crumbling stone.
  • unpolished: Similar in the figurative sense of lacking refinement; differs because unpolished relates to manners, education, or prose style rather than visible physical disarray.
  • unkempt vs kempt: Differs because kempt is a rare, conscious back-formation meaning "neatly combed and tidy", whereas unkempt is the ancient, uninterrupted historical survivor.
