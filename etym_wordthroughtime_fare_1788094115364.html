- Old English faran 'to go, travel, journey' — the plain everyday verb of motion (cognate with travel for travel's sake)
- Development of 'how one proceeds' → 'how one thrives': to fare well/badly as the outcome of one's going
- The meal sense: to fare = 'to be fed, to have food before you' (we fared sumptuously) — nourishing the noun 'fare' = food
- The journey-cost sense: 'fare' = the price paid to travel, born of the travelling verb
- 'Farewell' and 'fare thee well' freeze into a blessing salutation, preserving the old verb
- Modern divide: the pure motion sense fades into poetry and fantasy (Tolkien), while the condition sense 'how did you fare?' stays fully alive in ordinary speech
