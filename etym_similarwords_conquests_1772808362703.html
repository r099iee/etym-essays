• Victory: Similar because both name winning in war; differs because victory marks a single battlefield outcome and need not yield lasting territory, while a conquest ends in possession.
  • Subjugation: Similar because both involve overcoming a people; differs because subjugation stresses the breaking of the enemy's will and their subordination, while conquest focuses on the taking and holding of territory.
  • Annexation: Similar because both are the acquisition of territory; differs because annexation is cool and bureaucratic (formal incorporation into a state), without the bloody process that conquest evokes.
  • Acquisition: Similar because both are gains; differs because acquisition is the neutral general term for obtaining anything, stripped of the military force and glory of conquest.
  • Domination: Similar because both concern exercising power over others; differs because domination is continued control and supremacy, while conquest is the act of winning that control.
  • Capture: Similar because both are takings by force; differs because capture can be a single small seizure (a city, a prisoner), whereas conquest implies territory and peoples won.
  • Occupation: Similar because both involve holding land; differs because occupation is the physical presence of troops in a territory and may be temporary or purely military, not the permanent transfer of sovereignty.
  • Invasion: Similar because both involve entering a country by force; differs because invasion is the hostile incursion itself, which may fail, while conquest is the successful outcome that secures possession.
  • Triumph: Similar because both celebrate winning; differs because triumph is the celebration or personal glory of a victory, not the gaining of territory.
  • Takeover: Similar because both are seizures of control; differs because takeover is the modern informal or figurative word (companies, organizations), while conquest is concrete and military.
