Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*dʰgʷʰei- / *dʰwey-
"to perish, decline, waste away, fade"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*dwīnaną
"to vanish, fade away, waste away, pine"
↓
Old English (c. 700–1050 CE)
dwīnan
"to waste away, languish, fade, disappear"
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–15th c.)
dwinen / dwine
"to pine away, shrivel, waste away, decline"
↓
Early Modern English (Frequentative Innovation) (c. 1590s)
dwindle
"to shrink gradually, waste away step by step"
(simplex dwine + frequentative suffix -le)
↓
Modern English (17th–21st c.)
dwindling
"gradually shrinking, diminishing, or fading away"
