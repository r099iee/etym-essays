- Aristocratic parlor banque in 1847 - three-person zero-sum salon pastime; perceived as exclusive, secretive, French.
- English borrowing in 1848 as baccarat - fashionable foreign vice; perceived as smart and slightly naughty.
- Railway chemin de fer in the 1880s - faster two-person game; perceived as modern, sporting, Bond-like.
- Club and casino game to 1907 - commission on Banker; perceived as commercialised luxury.
- Havana punto banco and Vegas 1959 - house-banked, skill-free; perceived as democratic high-roller thrill.
- Macau dominance today - billions in revenue, mini-baccarat everywhere; perceived as the ultimate whale game, with side argot baccara = ruin.
