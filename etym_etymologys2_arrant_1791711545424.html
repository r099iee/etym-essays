arrant thief / arrant knave
"notorious, confirmed, thorough scoundrel"
↓
Modern English (c. 1600)
arrant
"utter, unmitigated, notoriously bad"

Note: Arrant is an orthographic and phonetic variant of errant, arising from the regular Middle English lowering of -er- to -ar- before consonants (as in clerk/clark, person/parson, and starve from sterven). In Old French, the verb errer ('to travel', from Latin iterare) merged phonetically with errer ('to stray, err', from Latin errare). The pejorative sense arose from set legal phrases like arrant thief (originally 'roving bandit').
