- 8th–7th c. BCE: epic hero — the lost Aethiopis tells of his coming and fall; the Odyssey remembers him as the son of the Dawn; a Greek name for a great foreign prince.
- 5th–1st c. BCE: the name migrates to Egypt — Greeks identify the Theban colossi of Amenhotep III as Memnon, and the nearby temple becomes the "Memnonium".
- 1st–3rd c. CE: the vocal colossus — Strabo, Tacitus on Germanicus, Pausanias, the graffiti of Roman officers, Julia Balbilla's verses for Hadrian; the dawn-song is a wonder of tourism; after Severus's repair it falls silent.
- Middle Ages: the name survives in Latin learning; the Egyptian statue is largely forgotten in the West.
- 17th–19th c.: Egyptomania revives him — travellers and savants fix the "Colossi of Memnon" on the Nile route.
- Today: a classical allusion, an art-history subject, a tourist landmark — and, in Wilde, a god enthroned over the Second Cataract who cries out at the morning star.
