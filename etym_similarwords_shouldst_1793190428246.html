• Wouldst: Similar because both are thou-forms of great modal verbs with conditional force; differs because wouldst voices will, wish, and habit (thou wouldst wander), while shouldst voices duty and ought.
  • Couldst: Similar because both are archaic thou-modal pasts in -st; differs because couldst grants ability and permission (thou couldst lift it), while shouldst imposes obligation.
  • Hadst: Similar because both pair with thou in scripture and Shakespeare; differs because hadst is possession and past circumstance (thou hadst two sons), while shouldst is modal futurity and duty.
  • Didst: Similar because both are the familiar second-person pasts of the old grammar; differs because didst records completed action (thou didst go), while shouldst counsels or supposes.
  • Shalt: Similar because both descend from the same Old English verb sceal; differs because shalt (thou shalt) commands the future absolutely, while shouldst softens toward ought and supposition.
  • Oughtest: Similar because both lay duty on thou with the -st ending; differs because oughtest is duty pure and present, while shouldst ranges wider across condition, advice, and reproach.
  • Mightst: Similar because both stage uncertain or wished scenes for thou; differs because mightst voices mere possibility, while shouldst adds the weight of fittingness and debt.
  • Wert: Similar because both belong to the lost thou-paradigm of solemn style; differs because wert is being itself (thou wert weary), while shouldst is modal attitude toward action.
