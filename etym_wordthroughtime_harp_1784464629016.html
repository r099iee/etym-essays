The harp is one of the oldest of stringed instruments, and in English it has always named the framed, plucked instrument of kings, bards, and hall-song.
“Harp” through the Middle Ages and Renaissance carried high status — the instrument of minstrels and poets, later a national emblem of Ireland and Wales, a symbol of a people and their song.
“Harp” in the 18th–19th centuries acquired the concert pedal harp, and the word also bred the idiom “to harp on” about something — talking about it tediously and repeatedly, a figurative branch from the constant plucking of strings.
“Harp” today is both the orchestral instrument of many strings and the mythic emblem of bardic music, still faintly royal in flavour — as Thorin’s green-wrapped harp in the story suggests.
