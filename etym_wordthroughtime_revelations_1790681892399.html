- Divine unveiling, awe and dread - in Late Latin and Middle English the word names God’s disclosure of hidden truth and the Apocalypse; connotation solemn, fearful, authoritative, received by prophets and saints.
- Doctrinal technical term, reverent and contested - through the Reformation and early modern theology it hardens into revealed religion versus natural reason; perception respectful but polemical, fought over by divines.
- Inward illumination, wonder - with Romanticism it softens toward personal insight and aesthetic epiphany; connotation marvelling and intimate rather than dogmatic.
- Journalistic sensation, excitement and suspicion - in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the plural revelations becomes press currency for scoops and scandals; perception thrilling but tinged with sensationalism.
- Scholarly and scientific payoff, prestige - in contemporary academic and popular-science prose revelations mark paradigm-shifting findings; connotation prestigious, evidential, the respectable descendant of the veil lifted.
