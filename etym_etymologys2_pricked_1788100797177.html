*prikōn

“to prick, jab, pierce”

Old English (c. 9th–11th c.)

prician

Middle English (13th–15th c.)

prikken · pryken

“to pierce; to spur a horse”



↓

Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)

to prick

Modern English (present)

Note: no Indo-European ancestors are agreed for this Germanic word; the root *prik- is shared with Middle Dutch prikken and Low German pricken. A 19th-century guess linking it with “break” is rejected by modern scholarship. The chain therefore begins honestly at Proto-West Germanic, whose form itself is a reconstruction.
