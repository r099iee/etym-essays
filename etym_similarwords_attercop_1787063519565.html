• Spider: Similar because both refer literally to the eight-legged, web-spinning arachnid; differs because spider (from Old English spīþra, "spinner") is the universal, neutral standard term focusing on thread production, whereas attercop is archaic, dialectal, and focuses etymologically on poison and venom.
  • Arachnid: Similar because both denote the zoological class of spiders; differs because arachnid is a formal, scientific Latinate taxon derived from Greek mythology (Arachne), lacking the gritty Anglo-Saxon bite of attercop.
  • Shrew: Similar because both function as animal metaphors for ill-tempered, spiteful humans; differs because shrew derives from a small aggressive mammal and is gendered historically toward scolding women, while attercop emphasizes venomous malice regardless of gender.
  • Termagant: Similar because both are archaic insults for quarrelsome, venomous individuals; differs because termagant emphasizes loud, overbearing, and violent turbulence rather than creeping, toxic spite.
  • Spitfire: Similar because both describe fiery, aggressive personalities; differs because spitfire denotes quick-tempered, explosive passion, whereas attercop suggests cold, bitter, venomous malevolence.
  • Vixen: Similar because both are pejorative animal metaphors; differs because vixen (female fox) carries connotations of cunning, seductive deceit, or sharp-tongued bickering.
