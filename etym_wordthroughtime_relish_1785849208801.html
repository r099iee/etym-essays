- Old French — relais as remainder, residual trail/scent; aftertaste as one concrete reading among “things left behind”
- 14th–15th c. Middle English — reles perceived mainly as taste or aftertaste; still close to sensory residue
- 16th c. — Shift toward pleasing flavor and appetizing quality; early condiment and “gives relish to” uses; enjoyment still closely food-bound
- 17th c. — Figurative boom: relish as psychological zest and keen pleasure in non-gustatory experiences; verb of enjoying firms
- 18th–19th c. — Dual standard: moralists and novelists use relish for character appetite (sometimes sinful); cookery fixes relish as named condiments
- 20th–21st c. — Everyday “with relish” for eager enjoyment; supermarket relish jars; verb common in speech (“I'd relish the chance”); Latin/OF origin unknown to most users; mild risk of ironic or dark relish noted in careful style
