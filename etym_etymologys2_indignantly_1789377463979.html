PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*deḱ- (root "to take, accept; acceptable, becoming, fitting") + *ne- (negative prefix "not")
"not acceptable, not fitting, not worthy"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*dek-nos → *dignos ("worthy") + *en- ("not")
"unworthy, undeserving"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
in- ("un-, not") + dignus ("worthy, deserving") → indignus ("unworthy, shameful") → indignārī ("to deem unworthy, be displeased, take offense") → present participle indignāns (stem indignant-)
"regarding as unworthy, filled with righteous anger or resentment"
↓
Middle French (c. 14th–16th c.)
indignant
"feeling or showing anger at what is unworthy or unjust"
↓
Early Modern English (c. late 16th c.)
indignant + -ly (adverbial suffix, from Old English -līce)
"in an indignant manner, with righteous anger or offended pride"
↓
Modern English (present)
INDIGNANTLY
