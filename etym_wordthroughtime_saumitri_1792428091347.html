- [Vedic & Pre-Epic Era (c. 1500–800 BCE)] — the constituent roots *su-* (well/auspicious) and *mitra* (friend/covenant/solar deity) flourished as foundational sacral concepts in Vedic hymns, personifying divine friendship, social contracts, and cosmic order — [perception: revered as sacred divine principles of cosmic harmony and alliance]
- [Epic Itihāsa Period (c. 500–200 BCE; Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa)] — crystallized as the celebrated matronymic patronymic (*apatya-pratyaya*) for Prince Lakṣmaṇa in the Sanskrit epic, used by sages, gods, and Rāma himself during council, forest exile, and fierce martial combat — [perception: celebrated as an honorable hero-epithet evoking fierce loyalty, maternal nobility, and peerless martial valor]
- [Classical Sanskrit Golden Age (c. 4th–8th c. CE)] — immortalized in classical kāvya poetry and dramatic literature by Kālidāsa (*Raghuvaṃśa*), Bhavabhūti (*Uttararāmacarita*), and Bhaṭṭi, serving as the textbook standard of grammatical patronymic derivation and brotherly self-abnegation — [perception: admired as a classical literary model of selfless fraternal duty and unyielding righteousness]
- [Medieval Bhakti Renaissance (c. 11th–16th c.)] — elevated in Vaishnava theology (Rāmānuja, Madhva) and vernacular epics (Kamban, Tulsīdās) where Saumitri is venerated as the direct avatāra of Ādi Śeṣa (the cosmic serpent) and the supreme exemplar of *śeṣatva* (eternal selfless servitude to God) — [perception: worshiped across India as the supreme deity of unwavering devotional surrender and protective guardianship]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era (19th–21st c.)] — universally recognized in South Asian literature, classical music, dance drama (Kathakali, Yakshagana), and Southeast Asian traditions (Ramakien, Wayang) as the evocative epic designation for Lakṣmaṇa's fearless, fiery devotion — [perception: venerated globally as a timeless symbol of sacrificial loyalty, chivalric honor, and filial devotion]
