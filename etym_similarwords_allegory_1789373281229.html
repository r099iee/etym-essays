• Parable: Similar because both are didactic narrative fictions conveying moral, ethical, or spiritual instruction through figurative events; differs because a parable is typically a short, simple, realistic, and compact story focused on a single moral or spiritual lesson (such as the Parable of the Prodigal Son), whereas an allegory is an extended, intricate, and multifaceted work containing comprehensive systems of personification and layered correspondence.
  • Fable: Similar because both utilize fictitious scenarios and non-literal figures to instruct and entertain; differs because a fable specifically features anthropomorphized animals, plants, or inanimate forces acting out humorous or cautionary episodes concluding with an explicit maxim or moral, whereas an allegory can encompass serious metaphysical, political, and philosophical epics without animal protagonists.
  • Metaphor: Similar because both assert a figurative relationship between two disparate realms of experience; differs because a metaphor is an isolated, concentrated rhetorical figure of speech compressed into a single word or phrase (e.g., "time is a thief"), whereas an allegory is an expansive, sustained narrative architecture spanning chapters, cantos, or entire volumes.
  • Symbolism: Similar because both employ concrete objects, figures, and motifs to evoke deeper, intangible meanings beyond literal reality; differs because a symbol is polyvalent, evocative, open-ended, and emotionally resonant without demanding a strict one-to-one conceptual decoding, whereas an allegory relies on a structured, coherent, and decipherable parallel scheme of correspondences.
  • Analogy: Similar because both establish a cognitive comparison between two different systems to facilitate comprehension; differs because an analogy is a logical or explanatory cognitive tool designed to clarify an unfamiliar mechanism by linking it to a familiar one, whereas an allegory is primarily an imaginative, narrative, or aesthetic work of art.
  • Myth: Similar because both weave fantastical narratives to communicate fundamental truths about the human condition and the cosmos; differs because a myth is a traditional, sacred, and communal narrative regarded by its culture as expressing ancient, foundational sacred reality, whereas an allegory is a consciously crafted, authored literary or artistic composition designed for systematic interpretation.
  • Apologue: Similar because both are narrative devices invented to inculcate a moral truth through indirect representation; differs because an apologue is a brief, formal moral fable or apologetic tale whose moral is immediately didactic and often prefixed or appended, whereas an allegory embeds its complex philosophical, political, or psychological implications throughout the narrative fabric.
