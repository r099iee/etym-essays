↓
Sanskrit (c. 1st millennium BCE)
jaṅgala
"dry, barren,
uncultivated land"

↓
Hindi / Urdu (from Sanskrit)
jangal
"wilderness, forest"

↓
English (18th c., via Hindi)
jungle
"dense, tangled wild forest"

↓
PIE (reconstructed)
*upo
"under, over, up from below"

↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*upp
"up, upward"

↓
Old English (c. 8th c.)
upp
"upward, aloft"

↓
English (slang coinage, 20th c.)
jungle-up
"to set up a makeshift camp
or rough bed outdoors"

Note: jungle reaches English via Hindi/Urdu jangal from Sanskrit jaṅgala "dry, barren, uncultivated land"; no secure PIE ancestor is known. jungle-up is a loose American slang compound of jungle + up meaning to throw together a rough camp.
