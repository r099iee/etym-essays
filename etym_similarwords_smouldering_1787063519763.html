• smoldering (American spelling): Similar because it is the identical word in its US spelling, meaning burning slowly without flame; differs only in orthography — smouldering is British, smoldering American, with no difference in meaning.
  • burning: Similar because both describe active combustion or intense emotion; differs because burning covers all degrees of fire including visible flame, whereas smouldering specifically means flameless, smoky, and slow.
  • glowing: Similar because both describe heat emitted without full flame; differs because glowing emphasises the light (a glowing coal) and is often positive, while smouldering emphasises smoke and dangerous containment.
  • smoking: Similar because both involve giving off smoke; differs because smoking merely means emitting smoke and can describe chimneys or engines, whereas smouldering specifies a slow, flameless, interior burn.
  • seething: Similar because both describe intense suppressed feeling (seething anger); differs because seething suggests boiling, agitated activity barely contained, while smouldering suggests slow, deep, patient burning.
  • simmering: Similar because both mean kept below the point of eruption (simmering resentment); differs because simmering is a cooking metaphor suggesting constant low heat, while smouldering is a fire metaphor suggesting smoke and the risk of flare-up.
  • festering: Similar because both describe something bad kept alive and growing below the surface (a festering wound or grudge); differs because festering suggests decay and infection, while smouldering suggests heat and the threat of ignition.
  • suppressed: Similar because both mean held down and prevented from showing; differs because suppressed is a neutral, general word for anything held back, while smouldering specifically pictures the held-back thing as a live fire.
  • latent: Similar because both describe a state of existing but not yet active (latent rage); differs because latent is a clinical, neutral term implying dormancy, while smouldering is vivid and implies heat, smoke, and imminent danger.
  • scorching: Similar because both are fire-related heat words; differs because scorching means burning the surface with intense visible heat, while smouldering means slow interior burning with little or no flame.
