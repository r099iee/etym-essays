• Barred: Similar because both mean shut out, prevented, or obstructed; differs because barred is the simpler, more general verb and often refers to physical blocking (barred from entering a room), whereas debarred adds formality and the sense of an institutional, legal, or principled exclusion.
  • Excluded: Similar because both mean left out of a group, category, or place; differs because excluded merely means omitted or not included, whereas debarred is actively prohibitive — the person is not simply left out but formally shut out, with the image of the bar behind the exclusion.
  • Prohibited: Similar because both involve forbidding; differs because prohibited emphasizes the rule, law, or command that forbids the action, while debarred emphasizes the person who is shut out by that rule — one prohibits an act, one debars an agent.
  • Disqualified: Similar because both mean made ineligible; differs because disqualified implies the removal of an eligibility or right that the person once possessed, whereas debarred often blocks from the start, before the right was ever exercisable.
  • Prevented: Similar because both mean stopped from doing something; differs because prevented is neutral and general, naming any cause or obstacle that stops an action, while debarred is formal and implies authority, a barrier, or a principle as the obstacle.
  • Precluded: Similar because both mean made impossible or shut out; differs because precluded stresses the logical or practical impossibility (the possibility was precluded by the facts), while debarred stresses an active, usually authoritative exclusion of a person.
  • Banned: Similar because both mean formally forbidden; differs because banned typically refers to a decree or law forbidding something (banned from the country, banned book), while debarred is narrower, naming the exclusion of a person from a right, office, or place by a formal barrier.
  • Shut out: Similar because both mean excluded from entry or participation; differs because shut out is the plain, physical idiom, while debarred is the formal, legal counterpart carrying institutional weight.
  • Outlawed: Similar because both mean placed outside the protection or enjoyment of something; differs because outlawed is archaic and dramatic, historically meaning placed beyond the law (outlawry), while debarred is administrative and sober, meaning formally excluded from a specific right or place.
  • Blacklisted: Similar because both mean formally excluded from participation; differs because blacklisted suggests the compilation of a list of the excluded and a ban imposed on a group, whereas debarred can apply to a single person or a class and stresses the barrier rather than the record.
