• anthropoid: Similar because both denote monkey- or ape-like physical characteristics; differs because anthropoid literally means "human-shaped / resembling man" (from Greek ánthrōpos + eîdos) and specifically designates the tailless great apes (family Hominidae) that physically mirror human morphology, whereas simian encompasses all monkeys (both New World and Old World) as well as apes.
  • pithecoid: Similar because both pertain to primates and monkeys; differs because pithecoid (from Greek píthēkos, ape) is an archaic, highly technical morphological term primarily used in paleontological fossil descriptions, whereas simian is the standard living zoological and literary descriptor.
  • primate: Similar because both refer to the biological order containing monkeys, apes, and humans; differs because primate is the overarching mammalian order that also includes the prosimians (strepsirrhines such as lemurs, lorises, and tarsiers), whereas simian historically and taxonomically isolates the higher primates (infraorder Simiiformes).
  • apish: Similar because both describe traits resembling primates; differs because apish is an informal, almost purely figurative adjective carrying pejorative connotations of foolish mimicry, grotesque behavior, or clumsy imitation, whereas simian is a neutral, scientific, and dignified literary term.
  • cynocephalic: Similar because both describe animalistic facial profiles in mythology and historical zoology; differs because cynocephalic means literally "dog-headed" (from Greek kýōn + kephalē), applied specifically to baboons with elongated muzzles or mythical dog-headed races, whereas simian denotes monkey or ape anatomy in general.
  • quadrumanous: Similar because both describe anatomical features of monkeys and apes; differs because quadrumanous is an eighteenth- and nineteenth-century taxonomic term (pioneered by Georges Cuvier) meaning "four-handed" (referring to opposable big toes on hind feet), whereas simian originates from facial morphology (the snub nose).
  • hominid: Similar because both belong to primate evolutionary lineage; differs because hominid refers strictly to members of the family Hominidae (modern humans, chimpanzees, gorillas, orangutans, and their fossil ancestors), excluding all New World and Old World monkeys.
  • monkeyshine: Similar because both derive from monkey behavior; differs because monkeyshine is colloquial American slang for mischievous pranks or playful tomfoolery, completely lacking the biological or literary dignity of simian.
