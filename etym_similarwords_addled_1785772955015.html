• Befuddled: Similar because both name mental unclarity; differs because befuddled often feels temporary, milder, even charmingly puzzled, while addled suggests spoilage or breakdown of thinking quality with a stronger rotten-egg undertone.
  • Muddled: Similar because both imply disorderly thought; differs because muddled focuses on mixture and tangle (muddled plans, muddled accounts), while addled stresses that the mind itself is unfit or "gone off," not only that its contents are mixed.
  • Confused: Similar because it is the broad neutral umbrella term for mental unclarity; differs because confused is less imagistic and more clinical-neutral, while addled is concrete, earthy, and faintly old-fashioned or literary.
  • Disoriented: Similar because both describe loss of a clear grasp; differs because disoriented targets bearings — place, time, identity — while addled targets reasoning quality and mental spoilage rather than spatial-temporal loss.
  • Scatterbrained: Similar because both criticize unreliable cognition; differs because scatterbrained implies chronic flightiness and inattention, while addled can be situational (heat, drink, shock) and carries the putrid-egg metaphor rather than mere distraction.
  • Demented: Similar because both can describe seriously impaired thinking; differs because demented is far stronger and clinical/pathological (dementia), while addled need not imply disease and is milder, comic, or temporary.
  • Rotten (of eggs): Similar because addled eggs literally means rotten or barren eggs; differs because rotten is general decay applied to food and fruit, while addled is specialized to eggs and, figuratively, to minds.
