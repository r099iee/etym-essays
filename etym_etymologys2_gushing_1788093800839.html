Note: The immediate source is debated at the margins: the OED and Etymonline link English gush to Middle Low German guschen/gussen "to pour" (probably via Hanseatic and Flemish trade contacts — the English form's -u- and -sh- cannot come from Old English gēotan by regular sound change), with reinforcement from onomatopoeia. Old English gēotan "to pour" is a sibling, not the ancestor, surviving in the dialect noun gote "drain, watercourse"; Greek khéō "I pour" and Sanskrit juhóti "pours (an offering)" show the same PIE root farther afield.
