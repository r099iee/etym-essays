Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed, prehistoric)
*slend-
to slide, slip
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, prehistoric)
*slintaną
to slope, to slip
↓
Old Norse (c. 9th-13th c. CE)
*sletta
to throw carelessly, to cause to slip
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
slenten
to slope, to slip or glide sideways
↓
Early Modern English (1520s)
slant
to strike or fall obliquely
↓
English (present)
slanting
leaning or moving at an angle

Note: The origin is debated. The chain relies on reconstruction: *sletta is not directly attested in Old Norse texts in this sense, and some etymologists derive the word from a different Germanic base meaning to hang loosely. The PIE stage *slend- is conjectural, linking the word to the family of slide and slip.
