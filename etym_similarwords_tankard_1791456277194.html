• mug: similar because it is a large handled drinking cup; differs because it is the generic, usually unlidded term and lacks the tankard’s historical association with pewter and ale.
  • stein: similar because it is a German beer mug, often stoneware with a pewter lid; differs because of its German origin and its typical stoneware material.
  • flagon: similar because it is a large vessel for holding drink, often with a narrow neck and stopper; differs because it is a serving vessel rather than an individual drinking cup.
  • pitcher: similar because it is a large container with a lip for pouring; differs because of its pouring function and its lack of a lid or drinking rim.
  • jug: similar because it is a deep vessel with a handle and a lip, used for serving liquids; differs because it is a serving vessel and because of its wide range of sizes.
  • goblet: similar because it is a stemmed drinking cup; differs because of its stem, its lack of a handle and its more ceremonial or wine-oriented use.
  • cup: similar because it is a small open drinking vessel; differs because of its size, its usual lack of a handle and its generality.
  • beaker: similar because it is a tall drinking cup without a handle; differs because of its simple cylindrical shape and its laboratory or historical uses.
  • chalice: similar because it is a large ceremonial cup, especially in religious ritual; differs because of its sacred function and its lack of a lid.
  • can: similar because it is a metal container for drink; differs because of its industrial, sealed form and its modern association with soft drinks and beer.
  • pot: similar because it is a rounded container, sometimes used for ale; differs because of its broader culinary range and its lack of a fixed drinking shape.
  • horn: similar because it is a drinking vessel made from an animal horn; differs because of its material and its archaic, Viking or medieval flavour.
  • quaich: similar because it is a shallow Scottish drinking cup with two handles; differs because of its Scottish ceremonial use and its wide, shallow shape.
  • taster: similar because it is a small cup used to sample drink; differs because of its small size and its evaluative function.
  • pint pot: similar because it is a vessel holding a pint, especially of beer; differs because of its precise measure and its informal British pub context.
