• Balkanic: a neutral adjective meaning "of the Balkans", used in linguistics and geology; differs by being technical and free of the pejorative colour that attaches to the metaphor.
  • Balkans: the plural noun for the peninsula and its states; differs because it names the region as a whole rather than qualifying a noun.
  • Southeastern Europe: the preferred neutral geographic term; differs by avoiding the cultural baggage of "Balkan" and by including Romania and sometimes Croatia more readily.
  • Haemus: the classical Greek name of the Balkan Mountains; differs because it is the ancient name, restricted to the range, and now purely historical.
  • Stara Planina: the Bulgarian name of the same range, meaning "Old Mountain"; differs because it is the local endonym and is used chiefly inside Bulgaria.
  • balkanisation: fragmentation into small hostile units; differs because it names the process or result, not the region.
  • balkanise: to fragment in that manner; differs by being a verb and by extending the metaphor to institutions, industries and software.
  • Balkanism: the Western discourse that stereotypes the region; differs because it names a mode of representation, not the place.
  • powder keg: a metaphor for a volatile region; differs by being a general image of imminent explosion rather than a geographic name.
  • Near East: an older geopolitical term that once overlapped with the Balkan space; differs because it centres on the Ottoman and Levantine world rather than on south-eastern Europe.
  • Danube–Sava line: the conventional northern boundary of the peninsula; differs because it is a geographical limit, not a name for the region.
  • Rumelia: the Ottoman name for the Balkan lands of the empire; differs because it is a historical administrative term with a Turkish and Islamic frame of reference.
