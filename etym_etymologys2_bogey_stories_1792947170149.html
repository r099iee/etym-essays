First element — bogey:
Middle English (14th c.)
bugge
‘frightening spectre, scarecrow’
↓
Scots and Northern English (16th c.)
bogle, boggart
‘goblin, sprite, haunting thing’
↓
Modern English (19th c.)
bogey, bogy
‘nursery goblin, scare’
↓
Modern English (current, plural compound)
bogey-stories
‘nursery scare-tales’

Second element — stories, plural of story:
Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE)
historía
‘inquiry, knowledge, account’
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
historia
‘history, narrative, tale’
↓
Anglo-Norman (13th c.)
estorie
‘story, chronicle, tale’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
storie
‘narrative of marvels’
↓
Modern English (current, plural)
stories
‘told tales’

Note: the deeper origin of bugge and bogle is uncertain, with Welsh bwg sometimes proposed but unproven, so scholars leave the goblin family without a secure PIE root.
