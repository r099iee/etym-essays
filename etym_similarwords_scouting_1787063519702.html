• reconnaissance (recon): Similar because both mean the systematic gathering of information about an area or enemy; differs because "reconnaissance" is formal military jargon for a planned operation, while "scouting" is broader and also names the youth movement.
  • reconnoitering: Similar because both mean surveying a place for military or practical information; differs because "reconnoitering" is more formal, bookish, and almost exclusively military, while "scouting" extends to sports, film, and youth activities.
  • exploration: Similar because both involve going into unfamiliar territory to find things out; differs because exploration is open-ended and aims at discovery or mapping, while scouting is goal-directed information-gathering with a report in view.
  • patrolling: Similar because both involve moving through an area observing it; differs because patrolling is a routine, protective sweep of a known area, while scouting is a targeted look into the unknown.
  • spying: Similar because both mean gathering secret or hidden information; differs because spying is covert, deceptive, and usually hostile or illegal, while scouting may be perfectly open.
  • prying: Similar because both involve looking into things one needs information from; differs because prying is intrusive, nosy curiosity about others' private affairs, while scouting is purposeful and legitimate.
  • surveying: Similar because both mean examining an area systematically; differs because surveying is technical (measuring land) or statistical, while scouting is a broader, less formal look.
  • ranging: Similar because both mean moving over a wide area observing it; differs because "ranging" is older poetic and military language for wandering and scanning, while scouting implies a defined mission and a report.
  • sleuthing: Similar because both mean investigating to find facts; differs because sleuthing suggests detective work on a mystery or crime, while scouting looks at places and prospects, not crimes.
  • talent-hunting: Similar because both are professional searches for promising people; differs because talent-hunting is restricted to sports and entertainment recruitment, while scouting covers territory, film locations, and markets too.
  • looking around / casing: Similar because both mean inspecting a place casually; differs because "casing" is slang for criminally checking a place for a robbery, while scouting is lawful; "looking around" is fully informal with no mission sense.
