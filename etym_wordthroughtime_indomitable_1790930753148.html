- 15th–16th Century - Borrowed into English from Late Latin *indomitabilis* (untamable), initially describing wild beasts, fierce stallions, and raging elemental tempests.
- 17th–18th Century - Shifted from zoological and elemental forces to human moral character, martial heroism, and spiritual fortitude ('an indomitable spirit').
- 19th Century (Victorian Era) - Elevated as a premier moral virtue in literature and biographies celebrating explorers, reformers, and abolitionists.
- 20th–21st Century - Universally applied to courageous civil rights advocates, independent institution builders, visionary artists, and survivors facing catastrophic adversity.
