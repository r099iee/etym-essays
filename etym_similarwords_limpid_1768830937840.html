• clear: Similar because both mean free from cloudiness or obstruction in a physical or figurative medium; differs because clear is the plain, everyday, register-neutral word covering weather, skin, conscience, and prose, while limpid is rarer, more poetic, and always adds a luminous, liquid brilliance.
  • transparent: Similar because both mean that objects can be seen through the medium; differs because transparent is a neutral technical term with no aesthetic colouring (a pane of glass is transparent but never limpid), while limpid implies purity, stillness, and beauty in the medium itself.
  • pellucid: Similar because both name perfect transparency, admitting the passage of light; differs because pellucid leans toward the scientific and cool, describing a medium that light passes through, while limpid leans toward the poetic and serene, describing a liquid at rest.
  • lucid: Similar because both descend from light-clarity and can mean easily understood; differs because lucid has settled into the intellectual register (a lucid explanation, a lucid mind), while limpid stays with the physical and aesthetic clarity of water.
  • crystalline: Similar because both mean perfectly clear, as if pure; differs because crystalline names a hard, brilliant sparkle like cut glass or ice, while limpid names a soft, still luminosity like still water.
  • translucent: Similar because both concern the passage of light; differs because translucent transmits light while diffusing forms (frosted glass), the very opposite of limpid's perfect, undistorted transparency.
  • unclouded: Similar because both mean not obscured; differs because unclouded chiefly emphasizes the absence of cloud or shadow in sky, face, or judgment, while limpid adds the glowing clarity of a clear liquid.
  • glassy: Similar because both describe smooth, clear stillness; differs because glassy suggests a hard, reflective, sometimes lifeless surface (glassy eyes), while limpid is warm, alive, and inviting.
  • bright: Similar because both carry a positive quality of light; differs because bright is generic luminosity, while limpid specifies clarity-through-purity rather than intensity of shine.
  • pure: Similar because both imply freedom from admixture and stain; differs because pure is broad (air, motives, metals) and often moral, while limpid is anchored to transparency and liquid clarity.
  • sparkling: Similar because both describe attractive, lively water; differs because sparkling implies movement, bubbles, and glints of reflected light, while limpid implies perfect stillness and depth-seeing clarity.
  • serene: Similar because both connote untroubled calm; differs because serene is a state of mind or scene, while limpid is the physical transparency that serenity is often compared to.
