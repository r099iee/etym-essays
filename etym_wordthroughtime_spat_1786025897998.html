- Late 18th c. - Echoic slap & water drop: Emerged colloquially in British and American dialects denoting a light slap, a smack with the palm, or the audible impact of a heavy falling raindrop.
- Early 19th c. (c. 1804) - Metaphorical transfer to verbal quarrels: Transferred in American and British colloquial registers to a short, petty verbal dispute or tiff between intimates.
- Victorian Era to Mid-20th c. - Domestic & matrimonial trope: Solidified in popular literature, theater, and journalism as the definitive lighthearted term for romantic frictions ("lovers' spat", "marital spat").
- Late 20th–21st c. - Broadened journalistic shorthand: Widely adopted by modern media to characterize low-stakes political debates, celebrity feuds, diplomatic squabbles, and social media clashes.
