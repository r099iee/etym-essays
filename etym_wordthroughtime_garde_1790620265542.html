- Germanic wardon: to watch, guard - concrete tribal sense of keeping watch against danger
- Frankish into Old French garder / garde: to keep, guard; custody and watch - feudal and courtly register; garde-robe, garde du corps
- Middle English guard / ward: the same root returns with Normans - doubled vocabulary; guard as soldier, ward as district and keeping
- Early modern avant-garde: advance guard - technical military term in both French and borrowed English vanguard
- 19th–20th century avant-garde: artists as guards of the new - heroic then institutional connotation; from barricades to biennales
- Present garde fragment: self-conscious Gallicism - signals cultural literacy; affectionate or gently satirical depending on tone
