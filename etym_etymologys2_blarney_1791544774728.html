Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*bʰel-
"to shine, flash; white, pale"
↓
Proto-Celtic (c. 800 BCE)
*blāros
"pale, gray, spotted"
↓
Old Irish (c. 700 CE)
blár
"pale field, clearing, battle-ground"
↓
Middle / Early Modern Irish (c. 1200–1400)
An Bhlarna
"the little field; the clearing"
↓
Hiberno-English (c. 1600)
Blarney
"Blarney Castle near Cork, Ireland"
↓
Modern English (c. 1790–present)
blarney
"smooth, persuasive flattery; cajolery"

Note: The Irish toponym An Bhlarna derives from blár ("clearing, open plain") with the diminutive/topographical suffix -na. The shift from geographical proper noun to common noun arose through Queen Elizabeth I's exasperation with Cormac Teige MacCarthy's flattering evasion, later immortalized by the ritual kissing of the Blarney Stone.
