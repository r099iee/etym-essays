• Forge: Similar because both are workshops where metal is worked; differs because a forge shapes solid, heated metal by hammering and pressing, while a foundry melts the metal and pours it into moulds — forging never liquifies the metal, founding always does.
  • Smeltery / Smelting works: Similar because both process metal at high temperature; differs because a smeltery extracts pure metal from its ore (converting rock into metal), while a foundry takes already-produced metal and casts it into shapes — metal flows out of a smeltery and through a foundry.
  • Factory: Similar because both are places of industrial production; differs because factory is the general modern term for any manufacturing plant, while foundry names a specific technology (melting and casting metal); a factory may assemble, a foundry always pours.
  • Plant: Similar because both name an industrial installation; differs because plant is corporate-administrative language for any facility (a power plant, an assembly plant), while foundry names the trade and its craft, with the furnace at its centre.
  • Works (ironworks, steelworks): Similar because both name heavy metal installations; differs because works usually covers whole integrated sites (often including smelting, rolling, and forging), while a foundry is the casting shop as such.
  • Mill: Similar because both are industrial workplaces; differs because a mill in the metal trades rolls, presses, or machines metal (a rolling mill), while a foundry casts it molten — mills shape by pressure, foundries by pouring.
  • Workshop: Similar because both are places where things are made by craft; differs because a workshop is small, general, and unmechanised in implication, while a foundry is purpose-built around furnace and mould, large or small.
  • Atelier: Similar because both name the maker's working place; differs because an atelier is the artist's or couturier's studio — refined and artistic — while a foundry is the industrial or craft casting-house, hot, dusty, and functional.
  • Casting house: Similar because both name the place where molten metal becomes shapes; differs because casting house is a descriptive technical synonym (used of ironworks' pouring halls), while foundry is the everyday and legal word for the whole establishment.
  • Manufactory: Similar because both name a place of manufacture; differs because manufactory is a dated, general word for any factory (18th–19th c.), while foundry specifies metal-casting and remains current technical usage.
  • Fab: Similar because both can name chip-production facilities in the semiconductor world; differs because fab (fabrication plant) is modern silicon-industry jargon for a wafer factory, while foundry in that world specifically means a contract manufacturer making other companies' chips, TSMC-style.
