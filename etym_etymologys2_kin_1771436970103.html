Stage 1 — PIE *ǵenh₁- "to beget, produce, give birth" (reconstructed; the great "birth" root of Indo-European). Stage 2 — PIE *ǵn̥h₁-yo- "the begotten ones, the family" (zero-grade derivative) → Proto-Germanic *kunją "family, kin, kind" (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–500 CE); the same base yields *kuningaz "king," literally "scion of the kin." Stage 3 — Old English cynn "family, race, kind, species" (attested in Old English verse and prose from the 8th–9th c.; the range covers both the blood-group and the category). Stage 4 — Middle English kyn / kin "relatives, family" (12th–15th c.). Stage 5 — English KIN "family and relations collectively; blood relationship" (this card's word; modern narrowing to blood relatives, the categorical sense passing to the cognate kind). NOTE ON SISTER FORMS: the same "beget" root produced Latin gens ("clan" → gentle, gentleman), Greek genos ("race" → gene, genesis), and Germanic *kunją (kin → cynn, king, kind, German Kind "child," "the begotten one"). These are cognates, not intermediate stages of this card's chain; the card's direct line runs through the Germanic branch only.
