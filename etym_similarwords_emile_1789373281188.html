• Emil: Similar because both names are direct descendants of the ancient Roman patrician name Aemilius, sharing the identical meaning of 'rival' or 'eager to excel'; differs because Emil is the standard Germanic, Scandinavian, and Slavic spelling/pronunciation (stressing the first syllable), whereas Émile is the French form, intimately associated with French literature, Jean-Jacques Rousseau, and Émile Zola.
  • Emilio: Similar because both derive from Latin Aemilius; differs because Emilio is the Italian and Spanish form, possessing a vocalic three-syllable sonority and distinctive Mediterranean cultural associations.
  • Emily: Similar because both derive from the same Roman clan root (Aemilia, the feminine form of Aemilius); differs because Emily is an anglicized feminine personal name (immortalized by Emily Brontë and Emily Dickinson), whereas Emile is a masculine personal name.
  • Emulation: Similar because both words trace their ultimate etymology to Latin aemulus ('rival, striving to equal'); differs because emulation is an abstract common noun denoting the ambition or effort to equal or surpass another, whereas Emile is a proper personal name.
  • Anatole: Similar because in the history of the Dreyfus Affair and French literature, both names represent prominent French authors who stood together in defense of Alfred Dreyfus; differs because Anatole (France) derives from Greek roots meaning 'sunrise' or 'the East' and was noted for classicist irony, while Émile (Zola) derives from Latin roots meaning 'striving rival' and was the champion of passionate, unsparing Naturalism.
