Derivation chain for ONTOLOGICAL (first root word): 1. Proto-Indo-European *h₁es- / *es- (to be, exist) — reconstructed, 4th–3rd millennium BCE. 2. Ancient Greek eimí (I am); present participle ōn, genitive óntos (being, that which is) — attested 8th c. BCE (Homer). 3. Neo-Latin ontologia (the study or doctrine of being), coined from Greek ón/óntos "being" + -logia "study of" — coined 1606 CE by Jacob Lorhard in his Ogdoas Scholastica (also used by Rudolf Goclenius, 1613). 4. English ontology (the branch of metaphysics concerned with the nature of being) — borrowed via Latin/French, first attested 17th century. 5. English ontological (pertaining to ontology or being; formed with the adjectival suffix -ical) — 18th century. 6. ONTOLOGICAL — the modern English adjective. Derivation chain for METAPHOR (second root word): 1. Proto-Indo-European *bher- (to carry, bear) — reconstructed; the base of Greek phérō "I carry." 2. Ancient Greek metá (across, over, between; with, after; expressing change) — prefix; attested 8th c. BCE; of debated earlier origin. 3. Ancient Greek phérō (I carry, bear) — attested 8th c. BCE (Homer). 4. Ancient Greek metapherō (to carry over, transfer) — attested 5th c. BCE; used by Plato in the sense of transferring a term. 5. Ancient Greek metaphorá (a carrying over, transference; a figure of speech transferring a name) — codified as a technical term by Aristotle in the Poetics (4th c. BCE). 6. Latin metaphora (transference of a word to a new sense; trope) — rhetorical term, 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE. 7. English metaphor (a figure of speech; transference of meaning) — 16th century (via Latin and French métaphore). 8. METAPHOR — the modern English noun. Note on the compound: ONTOLOGICAL METAPHOR is a modern technical compound of the 20th century (cognitive linguistics), joining two words of independent ancient Greek descent. The Greek prefix metá is of debated pre-Greek origin — several PIE sources have been proposed (e.g., *meth₂- "with, among" or *med- "middle") — and this stage is marked as uncertain rather than reconstructed with certainty.
