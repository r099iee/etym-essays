• defiled: Similar because both mean making foul or impure; differs because defile is almost wholly moral and religious (desecration of the sacred), while befoul covers physical dirt as well.
  • polluted: Similar because both describe contamination; differs because pollute is the technical, environmental term (water, air, land), while befoul is more visceral and keeps its moral senses.
  • contaminated: Similar because both mean making impure; differs because contaminate is clinical and specific (bacteria, toxins, radiation), while befoul is broader, disgust-laden, and moral.
  • tainted: Similar because both name a corruption of purity; differs because taint suggests a subtle or partial stain, while befoul implies thorough and gross soiling.
  • sullied: Similar because both mean to soil or stain; differs because sully is gentler and often figurative (a sullied reputation), while befoul is coarser and more physical.
  • soiled: Similar because both mean to make dirty; differs because soil is mild and everyday, while befoul is intense, deliberate, and literary.
  • smirched: Similar because both mean to stain or dishonour; differs because smirch is mostly figurative (character, name), while befoul covers literal filth too.
  • desecrated: Similar because both describe the violation of the sacred; differs because desecrate applies only to holy things and places, while befoul is far wider in range.
  • corrupted / debased: Similar because both describe moral ruin; differs because corrupt and debase suggest a long process of moral decay, while befoul emphasizes staining and soiling, often at a single stroke.
