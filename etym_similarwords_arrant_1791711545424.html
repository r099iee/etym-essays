• Utter: Similar because both serve as absolute degree intensifiers signifying total completeness; differs because utter is emotionally neutral and can modify positive, negative, or neutral nouns (e.g., 'utter joy', 'utter silence', 'utter ruin'), whereas arrant is strictly pejorative, reserved almost entirely for blameworthy or foolish qualities.
  • Unmitigated: Similar because both describe an absolute negative condition unrelieved by any positive element; differs because unmitigated is formal, analytical, and Latinate (meaning 'unsoftened' or 'unmoderated', e.g., 'an unmitigated disaster'), while arrant carries a biting, colorful literary register emphasizing shameless notoriety.
  • Notorious: Similar because both imply being widely known for bad behavior; differs because notorious focuses specifically on widespread public reputation and fame for vice (Latin notus, 'known'), whereas arrant emphasizes the consummate, unadulterated nature of the negative trait itself.
  • Flagrant: Similar because both describe brazen, undeniable wrongdoing; differs because flagrant derives from the Latin for burning or blazing (flagrare), emphasizing an offense that is glaring, scandalous, and inflammatory in its open defiance of law, while arrant emphasizes thoroughbred scoundrelism.
  • Out-and-out: Similar because both denote thorough, complete scoundrels or fools; differs because out-and-out is an informal, colloquial Germanic compound phrase, whereas arrant is a dignified, historical literary adjective.
  • Blatant: Similar because both convey shameless openness; differs because blatant emphasizes noisy, vulgar obtrusiveness and offensive visibility (coined by Spenser from barking beasts), while arrant emphasizes incorrigible, dyed-in-the-wool villainy.
  • Brazen: Similar because both describe shameless behavior; differs because brazen emphasizes metallic hardness of face (brass-like audacity) and total lack of personal modesty or shame in confrontation, while arrant is an intensifying modifier.
  • Downright: Similar because both express straightforward, plain completeness; differs because downright is direct, blunt, and plain-spoken (often modifying honest speech or simple folly), whereas arrant possesses high-register theatrical and literary weight.
  • Consummate: Similar because both indicate supreme, finished mastery; differs because consummate can describe supreme positive skill (e.g., 'a consummate artist') as well as negative skill (e.g., 'a consummate liar'), while arrant cannot be used in a positive sense.
  • Egregious: Similar because both characterize shocking, stand-out badness; differs because egregious (literally 'standing out from the flock', Latin ex grege) focuses on an action or error that is extraordinarily conspicuous, shocking, and offensive.
