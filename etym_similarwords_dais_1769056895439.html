• Platform: Similar because both are raised horizontal surfaces for standing or seating; differs because platform is the generic word with zero implication of honour — a railway platform or an oil rig is a platform, but nobody presides from it.
  • Stage: Similar because both are raised surfaces in a hall; differs because stage implies performance and spectacle — actors, music, speeches — while a dais is not for performing but for presiding.
  • Podium: Similar because both raise a person above the surrounding floor; differs because a podium is small and functional, made for a single speaker or conductor, and never for seating a king and great lords.
  • Rostrum: Similar because both are raised platforms for addressing an assembly; differs because a rostrum is the Roman beaked prow, hence a speaker's platform in a political assembly — a word of oratory and debate, not of banquets.
  • Pulpit: Similar because both are raised platforms within a building; differs because a pulpit is the enclosed desk for preaching in a church, with unmistakably clerical overtones, while a dais is secular and seats its dignitaries at a table.
  • Throne: Similar because both belong to the furniture of royal authority; differs because the throne is the chair itself, while the dais is the whole raised block that raises the room's hierarchy around it.
  • High table: Similar because both name the seat of authority at a feast; differs because the high table is the table, while the dais is the platform that carries it — the one may exist without the other in modern ceremony.
