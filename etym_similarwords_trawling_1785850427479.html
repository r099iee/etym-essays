• Trolling: Similar because both are fishing techniques of dragging gear behind a moving boat and both have internet senses; differs because trolling trails a baited line or lure (and on the internet means provoking others), whereas trawling drags a wide net (and, digitally, means harvesting data).
  • Dredging: Similar because both drag a heavy apparatus through water and both can mean a broad search of the bottom; differs because dredging scrapes mud, silt, and wreckage from the physical seabed or riverbed, while trawling catches swimming organisms or sweeps data.
  • Scouring: Similar because both mean searching exhaustively; differs because scouring emphasizes intense, abrasive scrubbing of a surface or terrain, while trawling implies drawing a continuous net through a fluid medium.
  • Sifting: Similar because both examine large amounts of material for something valuable; differs because sifting shakes material through a sieve to separate fine from coarse, while trawling gathers everything first and sorts it later.
  • Combing: Similar because both are methodical searches across an area; differs because combing moves in neat parallel lines over a territory, while trawling is a single broad sweep of a net-like drag.
  • Rummaging: Similar because both involve searching through many things; differs because rummaging is disorderly, hands-on digging through containers, while trawling is systematic and catch-all.
  • Sweeping: Similar because both express a wide, continuous pass through an area or set; differs because sweeping is a clean single pass (as with a broom or radar sweep), while trawling specifically borrows the fishing net's mesh-and-cod-end image of gathering bulk.
  • Hunting: Similar because both seek a target; differs because hunting is purposeful, often patient pursuit of a known quarry, while trawling is an indiscriminate broad-net operation for an unspecified yield.
  • Grubbing through: Similar because both imply digging through quantities of material for what is wanted; differs because grubbing suggests messy rooting in earth or piles, while trawling is a wide, mechanical drag.
  • Foraging: Similar because both mean searching widely; differs because foraging is opportunistic gathering of food or useful items by individuals, while trawling is industrial or computational bulk collection.
