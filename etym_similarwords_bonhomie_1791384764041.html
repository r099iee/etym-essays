• Geniality: Similar because both describe warm, cheerful, and pleasant friendliness; differs because geniality is native Latinate English and focuses on sunny disposition, while bonhomie retains French flair and implies unpretentious good-hearted simplicity.
  • Affability: Similar because both refer to approachable, pleasant manners; differs because affability emphasizes ease of being spoken to and gracious condescension (especially in a superior), whereas bonhomie is democratic, hearty, and uncalculated.
  • Conviviality: Similar because both evoke pleasant social warmth; differs because conviviality specifically implies feasting, drinking, and festive table revelry, whereas bonhomie is a personal disposition that exists even in quiet moments.
  • Camaraderie: Similar because both denote warm interpersonal rapport; differs because camaraderie requires mutual fellowship among comrades who share experiences or hardships, while bonhomie can be radiated by a single individual toward anyone.
  • Joviality: Similar because both denote good humor; differs because joviality is boisterous, hearty, and loud (full of laughter), while bonhomie can be gentle, quiet, and mellow.
  • Amiability: Similar because both describe a pleasant, likeable nature; differs because amiability focuses on mild agreeable compliance and sweetness of temper, lacking the hearty, expansive warmth of bonhomie.
  • Cordiality: Similar because both convey heartfelt warmth; differs because cordiality emphasizes earnest, sincere politeness and hospitality, often in formal or professional contexts.
  • Good-nature: Similar because it is the exact literal translation of bonhomie; differs because good-nature is plain, homely Anglo-Saxon English and can suggest simple-minded gullibility, whereas bonhomie is sophisticated and cultured in English usage.
  • Comity: Similar because both involve pleasant social relations; differs because comity is formal, diplomatic, and legal (comity of nations), describing mutual courtesy rather than warm personal cheer.
  • Goodwill: Similar because both denote benevolent feeling toward others; differs because goodwill is an ethical or legal attitude of benevolence, devoid of the social charm and warmth of bonhomie.
