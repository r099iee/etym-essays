Expressive Germanic base (pre-1500)
*tw- + *-le
"small twisting / fiddling"
"frequentative fidget"
↓
Early Modern English (1540s)
twiddle
"to trifle, toy with"
↓
Modern English (17th-19th c.)
twiddle
"to turn lightly between fingers"
"to twiddle thumbs = idle"
↓
Modern English (19th-20th c.)
twiddly
"fiddly, fussy, fidgety"
"with small intricate movements"

Note: Origin debated. Etymonline and OED call twiddle of uncertain origin, probably expressive formation on tw- as in twist, twirl, tweak plus frequentative -le as in fiddle, diddle. No secure PIE root; -y is the regular adjectival suffix.
