- 14th – 15th Century: A type of fine woolen or silk cloth used for headwear, and the cap made from it - Borrowed from Old French *bonet* (short for *chapel de bonet*, "hat of bonnet-cloth"), worn by men of all classes.
- 16th – 17th Century: Solidified as the standard Scottish woollen cap for men (the "Scotch bonnet") - Retained in Scotland as the national headgear (Highland and Lowland bonnets), while in England it evolved into various brimless caps.
- 18th – 19th Century: The quintessential women's fashion headwear (tied with ribbons under the chin) - Dominated Regency and Victorian women's fashion (poke bonnets, straw bonnets), while remaining the standard military and civilian cap of Scottish regiments.
- 20th – 21st Century: British automotive terminology (car engine cover) and historical/cultural costume - Universally used in Commonwealth English for the hinged front hood of a motor car, while preserving its historical fashion and Scottish cultural identities.
