In heroic and Old English poetry, “hoard” meant the treasure of a king or dragon — a guarded, glittering accumulation of gold and gems, the moral heart of dragon-legends.
“Hoard” in later general English broadened to any hidden or carefully kept store of valuables, but kept a note of secrecy and guarded accumulation.
“Hoard” darkened psychologically in the 20th century: “hoarding” came to name the compulsive accumulation of things beyond need, and a “hoarder” became a figure of disorder rather than of wealth.
“Hoard” today carries both senses at once — the legendary dragon-guarded gold-heap and the modern, slightly clinical word for obsessive stockpiling — its old glitter now tinged with unease.
