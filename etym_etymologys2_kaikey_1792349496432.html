Note on historical and grammatical formation: In Sanskrit onomastics, Kaikeyī is a relational ethnonymic and patronymic adjective converted into a proper name, formed according to the strict morphological rules of Pāṇinian grammar (Aṣṭādhyāyī 4.1.86). The base noun is Kekaya (the territory and royal dynasty). By applying vṛddhi (vocalic strengthening of the first syllable from e to the diphthong ai) and affixing the secondary patronymic/matronymic feminine suffix -ī (ṅīp), the word literally translates as "the woman of Kekaya" or "princess of the Kekaya lineage." Just as King Janaka's daughter is known as Jānakī and King Drupada's daughter is known as Draupadī, Queen Kaikeyī's primary epic designation marks her ancestral and political allegiance to the powerful frontier realm of her father, King Aśvapati.
