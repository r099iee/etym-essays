- Late 18th c. – Natural philosophy & particle mechanics - Denoted the physical coalescing or gathering of matter, fluids, and fine corpuscles into spherical lumps or chaotic masses.
- Early 19th c. – Geological petrology - Adopted by Sir Charles Lyell and early geologists to classify chaotic pyroclastic rock formed of fused volcanic bombs and ejecta.
- Mid-19th c. – Urban explosion & sociological critique - Emerged in descriptions of rapidly industrializing Victorian cities absorbing surrounding rural villages into sprawling, unregulated urban bodies.
- Early to Mid-20th c. – Spatial economics & industrial clustering - Formalized into positive regional planning terminology by economists studying Marshallian agglomeration economies and labor market pooling.
- Late 20th c. to Present – Metropolitan demography & materials technology - Universally employed to designate megacities exceeding ten million residents (urban agglomerations) as well as advanced industrial powder granulations and cognitive sensory masses.
