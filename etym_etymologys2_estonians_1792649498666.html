Note on historical debate: Historical linguists and classical philologists widely agree that the Aestii described by Tacitus in 98 CE—who inhabited the southeastern coast of the Baltic Sea and gathered amber—were ethnically and linguistically Baltic (Indo-European ancestors of Old Prussians and Lithuanians), not Finnic. Scandinavian Viking-Age seafarers and German crusading knights subsequently transferred this geographic term (*Eistland*, *Estland*) northward across the Gulf of Riga to encompass the Finnic-speaking tribes who called themselves maarahvas ("people of the land"). The Finnic population subsequently embraced this exonym as their national autonym (eestlased) during the 19th-century National Awakening.
