• Completely: Similar because both mean to the full extent. Differs because completely is the neutral everyday word, with no emotional weight; utterly adds extremity and often finality.
  • Totally: Similar because both mean wholly, entirely. Differs because totally is colloquial and can be a mild filler ("totally awesome"); utterly is formal and absolute in force.
  • Absolutely: Similar because both assert full degree. Differs because absolutely is also an interjection of agreement and can be emphatic rhetoric; utterly is used only as a degree adverb.
  • Entirely: Similar because both mean without remainder. Differs because entirely is precise and neutral, often of extent or membership; utterly implies intensity, not just completeness.
  • Wholly: Similar because both mean completely. Differs because wholly is formal and chiefly of whole-part relations; utterly is about degree and extremity.
  • Fully: Similar because both mean to the full extent. Differs because fully suggests capacity or completeness of content; utterly suggests the exhaustion of all degree.
  • Thoroughly: Similar because both mean completely. Differs because thoroughly implies careful penetration into every part; utterly implies absolute extremity of the whole.
  • Perfectly: Similar because both can intensify absolutes. Differs because perfectly also means flawlessly and is used in polite minimising ("perfectly fine"); utterly brooks no minimising.
  • Altogether: Similar because both mean entirely. Differs because altogether is collective and slightly archaic ("altogether different"); utterly is intensive and emphatic.
  • Outright: Similar because both mean without qualification. Differs because outright is used of actions and statements (refused outright); utterly modifies states and qualities.
  • Categorically: Similar because both deny qualification. Differs because categorically is formal and logical, of denials and assertions; utterly is general and emotional.
  • Unreservedly / Unconditionally: Similar because both mean without exception. Differs because these are formal and social (accepted unreservedly); utterly is adjectival and experiential.
  • Dead / Stone / Stark (informal intensifiers): Similar because dead wrong, stone cold, stark raving all intensify absolutely. Differs because these are colloquial and restricted to fixed phrases; utterly is standard and general.
  • Plainly / Positively / Simply: Similar because all can intensify ("plainly impossible," "positively radiant," "simply dreadful"). Differs because these carry their own literal senses (clearly, affirmatively, merely); utterly has only the one intensive job.
