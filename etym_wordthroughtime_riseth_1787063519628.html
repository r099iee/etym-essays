- Old English (c. 800–1100): Rīseþ / rīst — Third-person singular present of strong verb rīsan, denoting rising from sleep, ascending, and celestial motion.
- Middle English (c. 1100–1500): Riseth / rysith — Standard southern and midland English verb inflection, ubiquitous in Chaucer, Gower, and religious mystery plays.
- Early Modern English (c. 1500–1700): Riseth — Immortalized in the King James Bible (1611) and Book of Common Prayer, though competing with Northern dialectal rises.
- Modern English (c. 1700–present): Riseth — Preserved as a deliberate archaic, liturgical, and poetic aesthetic device, while standard daily speech transitioned to rises.
