• Wondering: Similar because both mean "being filled with curiosity and astonishment"; differs because wondering is the broader, everyday word (wondering about a question), while marvelling implies delighted admiration of something remarkable.
  • Amazed: Similar because both mean "greatly surprised"; differs because amazed can be neutral or even negative (amazed at the damage), while marvelling is admiring and positive.
  • Astonished: Similar because both mean "struck with surprise"; differs because astonished implies shock or disbelief, a stronger jolt, while marvelling is a gentler, longer-lasting wonder.
  • Astounded: Similar because both mean "overwhelmed with surprise"; differs because astounded suggests being stunned into speechlessness, while marvelling keeps an active, delighted attention.
  • Awed: Similar because both describe wonder before the remarkable; differs because awe implies reverence and fear (awe of nature's power), while marvelling is lighter and more curious.
  • Awestruck: Similar because both describe being overwhelmed by the impressive; differs because awestruck implies being silenced and stilled by grandeur, while marvelling is a lively, expressive wonder.
  • Spellbound: Similar because both describe being held by something remarkable; differs because spellbound implies enchantment, an inability to move or look away, while marvelling is an active response.
  • Dumbfounded: Similar because both mean "rendered speechless with surprise"; differs because dumbfounded implies confusion and shock, while marvelling implies appreciation.
  • Staggered: Similar because both mean "greatly shocked"; differs because staggered suggests being knocked off balance by news, negative or positive, while marvelling is admiring.
  • Flabbergasted: Similar because both mean "extremely surprised"; differs because flabbergasted is colloquial and comic, while marvelling is literary and warm.
  • Boggled: Similar because both describe the mind's astonishment ("the mind boggles"); differs because boggle suggests the mind reeling at the incomprehensible, while marvelling suggests delighted appreciation of the wonderful.
  • Agog: Similar because both describe eager, excited attention; differs because agog implies excited anticipation of what comes next (agog with curiosity), while marvelling is wonder at what is present.
  • Intrigued: Similar because both describe fascinated attention; differs because intrigue implies curiosity about the hidden or unknown, while marvelling implies admiration of the visible.
  • Fascinated: Similar because both mean "held by keen interest"; differs because fascination can be cold or intellectual (fascinated by the mechanism), while marvelling is warm and admiring.
