• Pleas: Similar because both denote urgent, emotional requests for help or mercy; differs because a plea is more raw, abrupt, and frequently used in formal legal contexts (such as entering a plea of guilty or not guilty), whereas entreaties implies a more sustained, eloquent, and deferential supplication.
  • Supplications: Similar because both involve deeply humble and urgent petitioning; differs because supplications carries a distinctively religious, ritualistic, or deeply submissive posture (often accompanied by kneeling or physical prostration), whereas entreaties frequently occurs in personal, secular, and familial disputes.
  • Petitions: Similar because both represent formal or earnest requests; differs because a petition is typically a structured, written, and often collective legal or civic document signed by multiple individuals, whereas entreaties are personal, passionate, and emotive appeals.
  • Appeals: Similar because both involve asking someone for a decision, reconsideration, or aid; differs because an appeal can be an intellectual, judicial, or public request (such as an appeal for charitable funds or a legal appeal to a higher court), whereas entreaties focuses on direct, heartfelt persuasion.
  • Solicitations: Similar because both involve asking someone for something; differs because solicitation often carries a business, transactional, or legalistic tone (such as soliciting business, votes, or donations), and in modern law can carry pejorative connotations, lacking the tender emotional gravity of entreaties.
  • Importunities: Similar because both denote persistent requests; differs because importunities highlights the troublesome, annoying, and unrelenting persistence of the asker, often bordering on harassment, whereas entreaties highlights the earnest emotional sincerity of the plea.
