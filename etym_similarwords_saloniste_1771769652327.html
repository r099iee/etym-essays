• Hostess: Similar because both receive guests in a home; differs because a hostess provides hospitality without necessarily directing high intellectual traffic, while a saloniste orchestrates the conversation and the guest list.
  • Patron / Patroness: Similar because both support artists and intellectuals; differs because a patron funds or protects artists, whereas a saloniste primarily steers talk and social chemistry, though the roles may overlap.
  • Bluestocking: Similar because both denote a learned woman of the salon world; differs because bluestocking names erudition (sometimes mockingly) without implying the office of salon-keeper.
  • Doyenne: Similar because both mark a senior, influential woman at the centre of a social or intellectual circle; differs because doyenne means simply "most senior or esteemed woman in a group" and does not imply hosting a salon.
  • Maven: Similar because both describe an expert who shapes a community's taste; differs because maven is informal, American, and general, with no salon institution behind it.
  • Curator: Similar because both select and present material for a public; differs because a curator manages objects and exhibitions, while a saloniste manages people and conversation.
  • Impresario: Similar because both organize and promote cultural events; differs because an impresario runs theatrical or musical enterprises for profit, which a saloniste does not.
  • Socialite: Similar because both denote a woman prominent in high society; differs because a socialite's prominence is primarily glamorous and social, not intellectual, whereas a saloniste's is intellectual.
  • Convener / circle leader: Similar because both gather a group regularly; differs because these are generic and informal, lacking the saloniste's historical and French institutional weight.
  • Salonnière: Similar because both name a woman who keeps a salon; differs because salonnière is the specifically feminine French form, while saloniste is the more general form (the one used in the passage).
