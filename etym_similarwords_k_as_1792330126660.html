• Cantos: Similar because both refer to major structural units or overarching narrative sections within a long epic poem; differs because cantos originates in the European poetic tradition (Italian canto, 'song') as an explicit marker of an oral singing performance or recitation session (such as in Dante or Byron), whereas kāṇḍas derives from botanical and sacrificial metaphors in ancient India, possessing formal theological codification and ritual divisions.
  • Books: Similar because both function as the primary, high-level textual containers dividing a massive literary masterpiece into autonomous thematic segments; differs because books is a generic, ubiquitous Anglo-Saxon term rooted in physical medium (Old English bōc, related to beech wood or tablets) applied universally across prose, history, and poetry, lacking the specific organic and liturgical resonance of kāṇḍas.
  • Sargas: Similar because both are indigenous structural divisions utilized in classical Sanskrit poetic compositions (mahākāvya); differs because a sarga represents a subordinate chapter, song, or canto (literally a 'surge,' 'emission,' or 'flow' of verse) nested inside a larger kāṇḍa, such that a single kāṇḍa contains many individual sargas.
  • Parvans: Similar because both designate the grand, monumental primary books of ancient Indian epic literature; differs because parvans (literally 'knots,' 'joints,' or 'nodes' of cane, from Sanskrit parvan) is the specific canonical term reserved for the eighteen major books of the Mahābhārata, whereas kāṇḍas is the formal designation for the seven major books of the Rāmāyaṇa.
  • Tomes: Similar because both designate expansive, weighty portions or physical volumes of an extensive written corpus; differs because tomes emphasizes physical heaviness, tangible codex binding, and scholarly heft (from Greek tomos, 'a cut piece of papyrus roll'), whereas kāṇḍas denotes an intrinsic, conceptual, and compositional division regardless of how physical manuscripts were bound.
  • Fascicles: Similar because both denote discrete parts or serial installments into which an extensive literary work is divided; differs because a fascicle refers to a temporary, sequentially issued paper-bound installment distributed during gradual publication before collective binding, whereas a kāṇḍa is an eternal, integral compositional division of epic poetry.
  • Sections: Similar because both segment a continuous written discourse into distinct, organized portions; differs because sections is an abstract, utilitarian administrative or academic label that lacks the sacred narrative rhythm, dramatic pacing, and ancestral performance traditions inherent in kāṇḍas.
