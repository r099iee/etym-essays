Note: The PIE root *kelH- (‘to rise, project, be high’) is the source of a vast family of high-elevation words across Indo-European languages, including Latin collis (‘hill’), columna (‘pillar, column’), and excellere (‘to rise above, excel’), as well as English hill and holm. In Latin, the archaic noun columen underwent syncopation of the unaccented medial short vowel -u- and assimilation, yielding culmen. The verb culmināre was formed in Late Latin from the stem culmin-, entering English first as an astronomical technicality before broadening into its familiar metaphorical senses.
