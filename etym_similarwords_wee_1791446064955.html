• Little: small in size or amount; similar in being the basic everyday adjective, but more neutral and standard, without the Scottish or affectionate colour of wee.
  • Small: of limited size; similar in core meaning, but more formal and objective, and less likely to imply endearment.
  • Tiny: extremely small; similar in size, but stronger in degree and often more emphatic or clinical.
  • Minute: extremely small, almost microscopic; similar in scale, but learned and emphatic, and pronounced /maɪˈnjuːt/ in this sense.
  • Diminutive: unusually small, often charmingly so; similar in affective colour, but formal and often used of people or objects in a descriptive, almost technical way.
  • Petite: small and daintily built, usually of a woman or garment; similar in delicacy, but French-derived, fashion-oriented and gender-specific.
  • Slight: small in degree or build; similar in suggesting thinness or modest size, but often implies weakness or insignificance.
  • Mini: miniature or very short; similar in informal diminutive force, but modern, commercial and often attached to nouns (miniskirt, minibus).
  • Minuscule: extremely small; similar in degree, but formal, often figurative (a minuscule chance) and sometimes misspelled as miniscule.
  • Infinitesimal: immeasurably small; similar in scale, but mathematical and hyperbolic, far beyond everyday wee.
  • Short: of small length or duration; similar in the temporal phrase a wee while, but measures length rather than overall size.
  • Young: of little age; similar when wee means a small child, but specifies age rather than size.
  • A wee bit: a small amount; similar in being the common phrasal use, but it is a phrase rather than a synonym and functions as a minimiser.
