Old Norse (c. 1200)
búask
‘to prepare oneself, get ready’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
busk
‘to prepare, dress; hasten’
↓
Early Modern English (1540s)
bustle
‘to hurry busily, make a stir’
↓
Modern English (current, past tense)
bustled
‘hurried fussily; fussed over’

Note: the standard account treats bustle as a frequentative of busk, but an imitative origin (busyness made audible) is also proposed; scholars agree the dress-pad noun bustle (1780s) is probably a separate word of unknown origin that converged in form.
