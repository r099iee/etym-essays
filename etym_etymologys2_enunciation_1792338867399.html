Note on phonological assimilation: The Classical Latin verb ēnūntiāre is an exemplary demonstration of regular Latin prefix assimilation. The prepositional prefix ex- regularly dropped its final consonant -x- /ks/ and lengthened the preceding vowel to ē- before voiced consonants (such as the nasal -n- in nūntiāre), yielding ēnūntiāre rather than *exnūntiāre. In Renaissance Late Latin and Early Modern French/English orthography, the Classical ligature -ti- before a vowel was alternately spelled with -ci- (yielding enunciation), which became the standardized English spelling.
