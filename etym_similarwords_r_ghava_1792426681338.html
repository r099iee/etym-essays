• Dāśarathi: Similar because both serve as high-register patronymic epithets for Rāma in epic Sanskrit literature; differs because Dāśarathi is a direct patronymic derived from his father King Daśaratha, whereas Rāghava traces lineage more broadly to his illustrious great-grandfather King Raghu, carrying greater dynastic and mythic weight.
  • Rāmabhadra: Similar because both are sacred honorific titles bestowed upon Rāma; differs because Rāmabhadra combines his personal name with bhadra ("auspicious, gentle, blessed"), highlighting his benevolent, gracious countenance rather than his royal ancestral descent.
  • Kakutstha: Similar because both are patronymic titles referring to ancient kings of the Solar Ikṣvāku line; differs because Kakutstha refers specifically to descent from King Purañjaya (who fought demons while seated on the hump, kakud, of Indra's bull), representing an older, more archaic layer of dynastic myth than Rāghava.
  • Aikṣvāka: Similar because both identify Rāma's celebrated royal dynasty; differs because Aikṣvāka derives from the primordial progenitor King Ikṣvāku, encompassing the entire Solar dynasty across cosmic ages, whereas Rāghava specifies the celebrated sub-branch founded by King Raghu.
  • Raghupati: Similar because both celebrate Rāma's connection to the clan of Raghu; differs because Raghupati is a tatpuruṣa compound meaning "Lord or Sovereign of the Raghus," emphasizing executive rulership, whereas Rāghava is a morphological patronymic meaning "son or scion of Raghu."
  • Raghunātha: Similar because both associate Rāma with the Raghu lineage; differs because Raghunātha incorporates nātha ("protector, master, guardian"), emphasizing Rāma's pastoral role as the supreme preserver and refuge of his devotees.
  • Raghuvīra: Similar because both honor Rāma through his ancestral name; differs because Raghuvīra explicitly celebrates martial heroism and battlefield valor (vīra), whereas Rāghava is an all-encompassing familial and royal appellation.
  • Kausalyānandana: Similar because both identify Rāma through familial kinship; differs because Kausalyānandana is a matronymic title meaning "the delight of Queen Kausalyā," highlighting tender maternal affection rather than patriarchal royal lineage.
  • Sītāpati: Similar because both identify Rāma in personal relationships; differs because Sītāpati designates his marital status as the faithful husband and consort of Sītā, whereas Rāghava links him back to his royal dynastic ancestors.
  • Maryādā Puruṣottama: Similar because both are supreme titles applied to Rāma in Hindu thought; differs because Maryādā Puruṣottama is an elevated theological and philosophical epithet meaning "the Supreme Being who upholds the limits of righteousness and duty," rather than a genealogical patronymic.
  • Raghukula-tilaka: Similar because both honor his standing within the Raghu lineage; differs because Raghukula-tilaka is a metaphorical compound meaning "the forehead-mark or crowning glory of the Raghu clan," used poetically to celebrate his preeminence.
  • Janārdana: Similar because both are divine names for Viṣṇu and his avatars; differs because Janārdana is a cosmic name meaning "he who is beseeched by the living" or "destroyer of the wicked," lacking any specific historical or dynastic association with Ayodhyā.
