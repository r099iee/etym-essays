• Nest: Similar in being a bird’s home; differs because a nest is the general, affectionate word for any bird’s home and can sit in a hedgerow at shoulder height, while an eyrie is specifically a raptor’s nest, always high, exposed, and built on bare rock or a commanding branch.
  • Roost: Similar in a place where birds settle; differs because a roost is where birds sleep or gather — often sheltered, indoor, or communal — and is never a breeding structure like an eyrie.
  • Perch: Similar in a high resting place; differs because a perch is a bare branch or ledge where a bird alights to rest, while an eyrie is a fortress-like nesting platform built for rearing young.
  • Lair: Similar as an animal’s retreat; differs because a lair is the ground-level den of a mammal predator such as a wolf or lion, and carries no element of height or the sky.
  • Den: Similar as a wild animal’s hidden dwelling; differs because a den is underground or enclosed, whereas an eyrie is open and elevated, exposed to the air.
  • Fastness: Similar in suggesting a secure, defensible retreat; differs because a fastness is any stronghold — mountain or otherwise — while an eyrie is specifically the nest of a bird of prey or a high perch.
  • Citadel: Similar as a fortified place of refuge and power; differs because a citadel is a human-built fortress in a city, whereas an eyrie is natural, wild, and airborne.
  • Aerie: Similar as the identical word; differs only in spelling — aerie is the American and older English form, eyrie the modern British respelling.
