Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*gʰh₁bʰ- / *gʰabʰ-
"to give, receive, hold, take in the hands"
Note: Widespread PIE root denoting physical possession and exchange; ancestral to Germanic *gebaną ('to give') and Italic *habē-.
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–600 BCE)
*χaβē-
"to have, hold, possess"
↓
Old / Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE–1st c. CE)
dēbēre (from dē- 'away from' + habēre 'to have')
"to owe, to have something from another; to be bound by debt or duty"
Note: Contraction of *dē-habēre ('to hold from someone else'); past participle dēbitus yields English 'debt' and 'debit'.
↓
Vulgar Latin / Old French (c. 9th–12th c.)
deveir / deure (past participle: deu / deü)
"to owe, to be obliged; (as participle/adjective) owed, proper, due"
↓
Anglo-Norman / Middle English (c. 1300–1380)
dewe / due (adjective) + -ly (adverbial suffix) → deuly / dewely
"in a manner owed by obligation, fittingly, rightfully, lawfully"
Note: Formed in late 14th-century Middle English by compounding the Norman-French loanword 'due' with the native Old English suffix -līce.
