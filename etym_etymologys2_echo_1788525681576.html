PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed onomatopoeic root)
*swagh- / *wekʷ- / expressive *ēkh-
"to sound, resound, roar, cry out"
↓
Proto-Hellenic (c. 1500 BCE)
*wākʰā́ / *ēkʰā́
"sound, noise, ringing"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th–5th c. BCE)
ἠχή (ēkhḗ, "sound, roar") / ἠχώ (ēkhṓ, "reflected sound, nymph Echo")
"sound, noise; reflected sound, Echo"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE; Ovid, Cicero)
ēchō
"echo, reflected sound; the nymph Echo"
↓
Old French (c. 13th–14th c.)
echo / esco
"reflected sound, echo"
↓
Middle English (c. late 14th c.; Chaucer)
echo / ecco
"echo, sound reflection, mythological nymph"
↓
Modern English (Present)
echo
"a reflected sound; secondary resonance; personified mountain nymph"

Note: The Greek noun ἠχή is generally classified by historical linguists (Beekes, Chantraine) as an expressive or onomatopoeic formation imitating reverberating sound, cognate with Greek ἦχος (êkhos, "sound") and ἠχεῖν (ēkheîn, "to ring, resound").
