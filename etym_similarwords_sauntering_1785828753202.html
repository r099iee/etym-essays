• Strolling: Similar because both mean walking slowly and leisurely without haste; differs because 'strolling' is relaxed and aimless but without the confident, casual swagger that 'sauntering' implies — a stroll is a gentle walk, a saunter is a walk with an air.
  • Ambling: Similar because both describe unhurried, relaxed walking; differs because 'ambling' (from the horse's amble) is a smooth, easy, even gait and is neutral about attitude, while 'sauntering' adds the careless, self-possessed confidence.
  • Wandering: Similar because both describe moving without a fixed destination; differs because 'wandering' emphasizes aimlessness and straying from a path, while 'sauntering' emphasizes a deliberate, confident leisure rather than being lost.
  • Moseying: Similar because both mean moving slowly and lazily, especially in American English; differs because 'moseying' is folksy and suggests drifting along with no plan, while 'sauntering' is more elegant and suggests an air of confident unconcern.
  • Dawdling: Similar because both describe slow, unhurried motion; differs because 'dawdling' is slower and implies wasting time or being deliberately slow to someone's annoyance, while 'sauntering' is a graceful, stylish leisure without the negative charge.
  • Loitering: Similar because both mean lingering or moving slowly without purpose; differs because 'loitering' is the suspicious, negative word (loitering with intent), while 'sauntering' is positive and elegant.
  • Rambling: Similar because both describe walking without a fixed course; differs because 'rambling' suggests walking over rough country with no fixed path, and is also used of speech, while 'sauntering' is smooth, easy walking in town or garden.
  • Roaming: Similar because both describe wandering over an area; differs because 'roaming' implies covering a wide territory freely (roaming the hills), while 'sauntering' is a short, relaxed, confident stroll.
  • Traipsing: Similar because both describe walking about, often idly or casually; differs because 'traipsing' implies walking a long way with effort, often wearily or pointlessly, while 'sauntering' is effortless and elegant.
  • Perambulating: Similar because both mean walking about at leisure; differs because 'perambulating' is a formal, even jocular word for strolling, while 'sauntering' is the vivid, ordinary word with the confident air.
  • Swaggering: Similar because both can describe a confident, self-assured walk; differs because 'swaggering' emphasizes a boastful, arrogant strut, while 'sauntering' is easy and unconcerned rather than aggressive.
