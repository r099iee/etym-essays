ROOT 1 — “waist”:
*h₂weg-
PIE (traditional; see note)
"to grow, increase, wax"
↓
*wahsijaną
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–1st c. CE)
"to grow, increase" (source of English "wax")
↓
wæst
Old English (attested c. 9th c.)
"growth, increase; build, stature, figure"
↓
wast
Middle English (attested 14th c.)
"the middle part of the body; the figure, waist"
↓
waist
English (from 14th c.)
"the narrow part of the body between the ribs and hips"

Note on “waist”: the standard etymology connects Old English wæst “growth, build” with the PIE grow-verb *h₂weg-, giving the odd sense “the place where the body “grew” (narrowed),” but the semantic link is not fully transparent and some scholars regard the derivation as uncertain. Note also: Late Latin cotta itself has a debated origin (possibly Germanic or Gaulish); the chain from Old French cote onward is secure.
