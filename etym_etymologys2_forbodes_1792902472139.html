PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*bheudh-
‘to be awake, aware, make aware’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*budōn-
‘messenger, herald’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
bodian
‘to announce, proclaim, foretell’
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
forboden, foreboden
‘to portend beforehand’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
forbode, forebode
‘to presage evil, feel coming ill’
↓
Modern English (current, 3rd singular)
forbodes
‘feels beforehand that ill is coming’

Note: Tolkien’s forbodes preserves the older for- spelling beside standard fore-; both prefixes were attested in Middle English before fore- won the sense of before in time. The deeper link of the bode family to PIE *bheudh- is the standard handbook connection through the Germanic words for messenger, bidding, and command.
