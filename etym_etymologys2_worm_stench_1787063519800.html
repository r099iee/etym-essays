WORM chain:
PIE
(c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*wrm̥is
"a worm, a serpent"
↓
Proto-Germanic
(c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*wurmiz
"serpent, dragon, worm"
↓
Old English
(before 700 CE)
wyrm
"serpent, snake, dragon;
also earthworm"
↓
Middle English
(12th–15th c.)
worm
"snake, dragon; creeping insect
or worm"
↓
English
(modern)
worm
"invertebrate; also an archaic
poetic word for dragon"

STENCH chain:
PIE
(root origin debated)
*stengw-
"to push, press" (possibly;
the idea of a piercing odour)
↓
Proto-Germanic
(c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*stinkwanan
"to emit a smell, to stink"
↓
Old English
(before 1000 CE)
stincan / stenc
"to give off an odour;
a smell, good or bad"
↓
Middle English
(14th c.)
stench
"an offensive, foul smell"
↓
English
(modern)
stench
"a strong and very unpleasant
smell"

Fused compound:
↓
English
(1937, The Hobbit)
worm-stench
"the foul smell of a dragon
(a worm)"

Note: the pre-Germanic ancestry of *stengw- is debated; some scholars connect it to a root meaning "to be sharp", others to "to push, thrust". No single reconstruction is universally accepted.
