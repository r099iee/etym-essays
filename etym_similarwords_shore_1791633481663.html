• prop up: Similar because both denote placing a support beneath or against something to prevent falling; differs because prop up is a plain, informal native phrasal verb that can describe casual physical actions (propping up one's chin), whereas shore up is a technical engineering term with significant structural and institutional gravity.
  • buttress: Similar because both signify reinforcing an existing structure or argument; differs because a buttress is typically an exterior, permanent masonry projection designed into architectural plans, whereas shoring is characteristically a temporary, remedial timber or steel intervention in emergencies.
  • bolster: Similar because both describe strengthening or supporting something that is weakening; differs because bolster (originally a pillow or cushion) primarily connotes uplifting morale, self-confidence, or abstract enthusiasm, lacking the rigid, angular mechanical bracing of shore.
  • brace: Similar because both involve providing rigid mechanical support against lateral stress; differs because brace implies steadying or preparing oneself against an impending impact or shock, whereas shore emphasizes carrying vertical or tipping loads to stop subsidence.
  • underpin: Similar because both provide structural support to prevent collapse; differs because underpin specifically means inserting a permanent masonry or concrete foundation beneath an existing wall from below, whereas shoring braces from the side or surface.
  • reinforce: Similar because both describe making something stronger; differs because reinforce is a broader, neutral term that encompasses adding internal material (such as rebar to concrete) or sending military reinforcements, rather than external propping.
  • sustain: Similar because both involve bearing weight or keeping something functioning; differs because sustain emphasizes prolonged, continuous endurance and nourishing support over time rather than remedial bracing.
  • stay: Similar because both act as structural supports; differs because a stay is specifically a tensile guy-wire, cable, or rope holding something upright under tension (as on a sailboat mast), whereas a shore is a compressive timber or strut.
  • fortify: Similar because both strengthen a position against danger; differs because fortify emphasizes defensive military protection, ramparts, or nutritional enrichment rather than mechanical stabilization against gravity.
  • truss: Similar because both are carpentry terms for structural support; differs because a truss is a rigid framework of assembled triangles supporting a roof or bridge, rather than an external temporary prop.
