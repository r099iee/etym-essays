• choleric: Similar because both denote an innate predisposition toward severe anger and volatile outbursts; differs because choleric specifically invokes the classical humoral framework of excess yellow bile and implies an intensely fiery, driving, authoritative irritability, whereas irascible focuses more broadly on how easily one's temper is ignited by external friction.
  • petulant: Similar because both describe irritable, disagreeable behavioral reactions; differs because petulant carries a distinct connotation of childishness, pouting sulkiness, and unjustified whininess over trivial frustrations, whereas irascible conveys formidable, adult anger capable of genuine ferocity.
  • peevish: Similar because both denote an easily upset disposition; differs because peevish implies persistent, petty discontent, fretful complaining, and mild sourness, lacking the explosive kinetic fury inherent in irascible.
  • cantankerous: Similar because both describe difficult, bad-tempered individuals; differs because cantankerous emphasizes stubborn uncooperativeness, argumentative contrariness, and crotchety friction (frequently associated with old age), whereas irascible highlights quickness to ignite into hot wrath.
  • bilious: Similar because both connect ill temper with physical and emotional volatility; differs because bilious evokes gastric distress, nauseous sourness, and a sickly, jaundiced spitefulness, while irascible centers on sudden combustion.
  • fractious: Similar because both refer to persons who cause conflict due to unstable tempers; differs because fractious suggests rebelliousness, unruliness, and difficult resistance to discipline (like a restive horse), whereas irascible describes a personal affective trigger.
  • testy: Similar because both denote a short-fused, snappish reaction to questions or interruptions; differs because testy is relatively mild and colloquial, describing temporary touchiness, whereas irascible denotes a deep-seated constitutional inclination toward wrath.
  • touchy: Similar because both describe extreme sensitivity to perceived offenses; differs because touchy emphasizes hypersensitivity, over-defensiveness, and thin-skinned vulnerability, whereas irascible emphasizes the outward aggressive blast of anger.
  • splenetic: Similar because both trace back to historical organ-based theories of temperamental rage; differs because splenetic implies bitter, venomous malice, sarcastic melancholy, and brooding spite, whereas irascible denotes instant combustibility.
  • waspish: Similar because both describe prickly, sharp-tongued interactions; differs because waspish implies stinging, snappy, venomous verbal jabs, whereas irascible suggests an overwhelming storm of temper.
  • dyspeptic: Similar because both describe chronic irritability; differs because dyspeptic roots the foul temper in indigestion, gloomy pessimism, and morose depression, whereas irascible is energized by active fire.
  • pugnacious: Similar because both lead to interpersonal hostility; differs because pugnacious denotes an eager appetite for physical fighting, brawling, and aggressive confrontation, while irascible centers on internal emotional flashpoints.
