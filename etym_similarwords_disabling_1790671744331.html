• crippling: Similar because both mean severely impairing function; differs because crippling is more vivid and physical, evoking lameness, while disabling is broader including technical deactivation.
  • incapacitating: Similar because both mean rendering incapable; differs because incapacitating is formal, often medical or legal, while disabling is common across medicine and technology.
  • debilitating: Similar because both mean weakening; differs because debilitating stresses draining strength over time, while disabling stresses removing ability or function.
  • paralysing: Similar because both mean making unable to move or act; differs because paralysing suggests total freeze by fear or nerve loss, while disabling allows partial impairment.
  • hamstringing: Similar because both mean hindering effectiveness; differs because hamstringing is metaphorical restriction, while disabling is fuller deprivation of power.
  • undermining: Similar because both mean weakening from below; differs because undermining is gradual and insidious, while disabling is direct loss of function.
  • neutralising: Similar because both mean making ineffective; differs because neutralising is tactical and chemical, cancelling effect, while disabling is functional shutdown.
  • deactivating: Similar because both mean switching off; differs because deactivating is technical and reversible, while disabling can be medical and permanent.
  • immobilising: Similar because both mean preventing movement; differs because immobilising focuses on motion, while disabling focuses on ability broadly.
