• Stocking: Similar because both are close-fitting leg coverings; differs because “stocking” is the modern knitted garment for the foot and lower leg, while “hose” historically covered the whole leg and could be made of cloth, leather or mail.
  • Sock: Similar because a sock is a short knitted leg covering; differs because it covers only the foot and ankle and never means a tube or a piece of armour.
  • Tights: Similar because tights are close-fitting legwear covering leg and foot; differs because tights are a modern stretch garment worn as outerwear or dancewear, with no tube or armour sense.
  • Leggings: Similar because leggings cover the leg closely; differs because leggings are loose or tight leg garments without feet, and have no conduit sense.
  • Chausses: Similar because chausses are the technical term for mail or plate leg defences in armour studies; differs because “chausses” is purely specialist armour terminology, while “hose” is a general word with many senses.
  • Tube: Similar because both name long hollow cylinders; differs because “tube” is generic, may be rigid or flexible, and has no garment history.
  • Pipe: Similar because a pipe conveys liquids; differs because a pipe is usually rigid and permanently installed, while a hose is flexible and portable.
  • Conduit: Similar because both are channels for conveying fluid; differs because “conduit” is a formal, generic term for any channel, including non-flexible structures.
  • Pantyhose: Similar because it descends directly from “hose” and names legwear; differs because it is a specific modern garment (tights with integrated brief), not the historical leg covering.
  • Hosiery: Similar because it is the collective trade term for leg coverings built on the same root; differs because it names the category and its manufacture, not a single object.
  • Hosepipe: Similar because it is the reinforced compound for the water tube; differs because it is a tautological compound used mainly in British English for garden watering.
