• Revelry: Similar because both name noisy joyful celebration; differs because revelry is wilder and more unrestrained, while merrymaking sounds warmer and more traditional.
  • Festivity: Similar because both describe celebratory proceedings; differs because festivity is the formal collective noun for the event, while merrymaking stresses the cheerful activity itself.
  • Celebration: Similar because both mark joyous observance; differs because celebration is neutral and modern, while merrymaking is nostalgic and stresses hearty noise.
  • Carousing: Similar because both involve drinking and merriment; differs because carousing centres on heavy drinking, while merrymaking centres on general joy with drink optional.
  • Jollity: Similar because both convey high-spirited cheer; differs because jollity names the mood of cheerfulness, while merrymaking names the festive doing.
  • Merriment: Similar because both come from merry and mean cheer; differs because merriment is the atmosphere or sound of mirth, while merrymaking is the active celebrating.
  • Partying: Similar because both mean enjoying festivities with others; differs because partying is contemporary and casual, while merrymaking is old-fashioned and literary.
  • Conviviality: Similar because both describe warm sociable enjoyment; differs because conviviality is formal and stresses friendliness, while merrymaking stresses audible boisterous fun.
