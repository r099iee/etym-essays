• Fastidious: Similar because both describe someone with exacting, discriminating standards; differs because fastidious stresses refined aesthetic or hygienic taste and is largely admiring, whereas fussy is mildly critical, warm, and about anxious attention to trifles.
  • Meticulous: Similar because both describe careful, thorough attention to detail; differs because meticulous is largely admiring and calm, stressing exact, well-organised work with no hint of anxiety, whereas fussy suggests nervous, disproportionate concern.
  • Pedantic: Similar because both describe excessive attention to minor correctness; differs because pedantic is intellectual and condemnatory, a rigid bookish insistence on rules and trivia, whereas fussy is warm, informal, and often applied to domestic matters.
  • Finicky: Similar because both describe a person hard to please about details (a finicky eater); differs because finicky is narrower, applied chiefly to tastes and preferences, whereas fussy also extends to things and processes (a fussy design).
  • Picky: Similar because both describe being hard to please; differs because picky is informal and applied to preferences and choices, with less of fussy's implication of restless, anxious bustle.
  • Pernickety: Similar because both describe an excessively fastidious, finicky person; differs because pernickety (chiefly British) adds an edge of contemptuous dismissal of the fussiness itself.
  • Exacting: Similar because both describe demanding standards; differs because exacting is respectful, describing a legitimate high requirement, whereas fussy implies the requirement is disproportionate or neurotic.
  • Particular: Similar because both describe someone with specific, hard-to-satisfy preferences; differs because particular is neutral or mildly positive, whereas fussy carries the negative tone of disproportionate bother.
  • Over-particular: Similar because both describe excessive attention to detail; differs because over-particular is more formal and clinical, while fussy is colloquial and carries the image of physical bustle and agitation.
  • Punctilious: Similar because both describe careful attention to small formal details; differs because punctilious is high-register and admiring of correctness in etiquette and procedure, whereas fussy is informal and mildly critical.
