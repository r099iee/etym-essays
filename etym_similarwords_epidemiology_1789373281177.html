• Pathology: Similar because both are foundational medical disciplines that study disease mechanisms, manifestations, and consequences; differs because pathology examines structural, cellular, tissue, and biochemical changes in individual specimens and corpses to establish diagnoses, whereas epidemiology evaluates population-level statistical patterns, risk factors, and environmental distributions across groups of living people.
  • Public Health: Similar because both strive to prevent illness, extend human life, and promote community well-being; differs because public health is a sweeping umbrella field encompassing healthcare policy, sanitation infrastructure, health education, and administrative intervention, whereas epidemiology is the specific quantitative, empirical, and scientific methodology that supplies public health with evidence.
  • Etiology: Similar because both investigate why and how illnesses originate; differs because etiology focuses on the specific biological, chemical, or genetic causative agents (pathogens, toxins, mutations) that initiate disease in an organism, whereas epidemiology investigates the demographic, behavioral, and environmental conditions governing how that disease behaves across populations.
  • Epizootiology: Similar because both analyze disease occurrence, vector dynamics, and outbreak trajectories across host populations; differs because epizootiology deals strictly with non-human animal populations (veterinary disease dynamics), whereas epidemiology focuses on human health (though contemporary One Health initiatives combine both).
  • Biostatistics: Similar because both employ sophisticated quantitative modeling, probability theory, and data analytics to evaluate health data; differs because biostatistics is a branch of mathematics concerned with statistical methodology, study design, and computational data analysis, whereas epidemiology is a substantive medical science that applies these statistical tools to answer biological and clinical hypotheses.
  • Immunology: Similar because both contribute critically to controlling infectious outbreaks and preventing pandemics; differs because immunology focuses on the molecular and cellular defense mechanisms of the host immune system against pathogens, whereas epidemiology tracks disease transmission dynamics and immunity rates at the population level (herd immunity).
