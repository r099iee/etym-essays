- Danger itself - Old English faer, sudden peril and ambush, the objective threat abroad
- The feeling - Middle English fear, inward dread; the emotion named after the danger
- Full of fear (both ways) - fearsome as timid (easily frightened) and as terrible (frightening)
- Standardised menace - modern fearsome settles on fear-causing; formidable beasts, foes, and reputations
- Colloquial magnitude - a fearsome load, bill, or workload; dread becomes a measure of scale
