PIE (reconstructed, imitative root)
*dhun-
‘to sound, roar, boom’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*duniz
‘loud noise, din, thunder’
↓
Old Norse (9th c., cognate)
dynr
‘din, noise, thunder’
↓
Old English (before 12th c.)
dyne (also dynn)
‘loud noise, crash’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
dine (also dyn)
‘loud confused noise’
↓
Modern English (16th c. onward)
din
‘prolonged loud confused noise’

Note: the deeper PIE connection is the standard handbook proposal for an imitative Germanic sound-word; because such echoic roots arise independently in many languages, some scholars treat the Germanic family as essentially expressive rather than inherited, and the pre-Germanic stage should be read with that caution.
