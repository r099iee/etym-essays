• Invasion: A large-scale entry aimed at conquest or occupation; it is similar in crossing a border but far grander and usually permanent, whereas an incursion is limited and temporary.
  • Raid: A sudden attack, often for plunder; it is similar in speed and scale but is more concrete and less formal.
  • Foray: A sudden excursion into enemy territory, especially for supplies; it is literary and archaic, with a hint of adventure.
  • Sortie: A sally by besieged defenders against besiegers; it differs in starting from a defensive position.
  • Inroad: A hostile entrance, now usually figurative; it suggests gradual encroachment rather than a single incursion.
  • Encroachment: A gradual advance beyond proper limits; it is often peaceful and incremental, lacking the violence of an incursion.
  • Intrusion: An unwelcome entry into a place or privacy; it is broader and less military, and can be merely social.
  • Trespass: An unlawful entry onto land; it is a legal wrong, not a military operation, and is usually individual.
  • Penetration: The act of passing into or through; it is neutral and technical, with no implication of hostility.
  • Assault: A violent attack; it is directed at a person or position rather than at territory, and it is more personal.
  • Offensive: A sustained military campaign of attack; it is larger and strategic, unlike a single incursion.
  • Advance: Forward movement of an army; it need not meet resistance and carries no suggestion of violation.
  • Onslaught: A fierce, overwhelming attack; it is more intense and less limited than an incursion.
  • Thrust: A concentrated forward push, often figurative; it is narrower and more tactical.
  • Sally: A sudden sortie from a defended place; it is close to sortie and usually smaller.
  • Expedition: An organised journey or campaign; it may be peaceful and is not defined by border violation.
  • Maraud: To roam in search of plunder; it describes the activity rather than a single entry.
  • Depredation: An act of plundering or ravaging; it focuses on damage rather than movement.
  • Infiltration: A covert, gradual penetration; it is secret and often by individuals, unlike an open incursion.
  • Violation: A breach of a border, law or right; it names the offence rather than the military action.
