curtness — The quality or state of being curt: rudely brief or abrupt in manner, speech, or behaviour; the disposition that disposes of a matter with clipped, peremptory brevity and dispenses with courtesy, cushioning, and elaboration. The word is the noun built from curt with the abstract suffix -ness, and it names not merely brevity but a chosen, sometimes contemptuous brevity — the manner of a person who treats talk as a transaction to be minimized.

In the passage the word performs a precise act of character portraiture. Bellman "dismissed the query with his usual curtness": he does not merely answer briefly; he disposes of the other man's question as one would dispose of a trivial object, and the framing tells us this is a habit ("his usual curtness"), a fixed behavioural signature rather than a one-off rudeness. The tone is clipped, peremptory, and faintly contemptuous: the italicised challenge "Not likely?" is turned back with no expansion, no courtesy, and no willingness to entertain the hypothesis his companion has raised. In this Martian-adventure frame, where the party hunts for gold and nerves are raw, curtness functions as a defensive wall — an efficiency-driven mind that applies a blade to conversation and shears away all social nicety, all cushioning, all politeness. The word is built, etymologically and metaphorically, on cutting: Latin curtus means "shortened, cut short, docked," and curtness is conversation cut short, lopped like a docked tail, sheared of every courtesy.

Among its near-synonyms, curtness is sharply distinct:

  • Brevity is neutral and even admirable — the quality of using few words well; a telegram has brevity but no curtness.
  • Terseness is neat and economical, marked by polish and grace, whereas curtness is angular and unpolished.
  • Abruptness stresses the sudden, jarring break in a conversation — a reply that snaps the thread of talk.
  • Brusqueness adds a rough, offhand gruffness of manner, a blunt dismissal delivered as much through tone of voice as through words.
  • Bluntness foregrounds an uncomfortable frankness of content, saying something unpleasant directly; curtness, by contrast, says little and refuses to engage.
  • Succinctness and conciseness are neutral or positive — praised in prose; curtness is their negative shadow, brevity turned cold.

Register: the word is formal-literary, mildly abstract; it describes a characterological quality rather than a single act. It collocates with possessive adjectives (his usual curtness, with her characteristic curtness) and with verbs of dismissal and reply. In modern usage the word retains the blade: to behave with curtness is to refuse warmth, to impose distance, to treat the other person's words as an interruption. The passage's oddly placed punctuation ("Not likely?.") registers the same abrupt, dismissive rhythm as the word itself — a sentence cut short before it can be a question, a manner that clips conversation the way a knife clips a thread.
