TWO SEPARATE CHAINS — the compound WERE-WORMS is a modern (Tolkienian) literary formation built from two independent native Germanic elements; no single PIE compound "man-worm" is reconstructed or attested. CHAIN 1 — WERE- ("man"), the first element: → PIE *wiH-ró- / *wī-ró- ("man, freeman"), reconstructed; cognate Latin vir (man), Old Irish fer, Lithuanian vyras, Sanskrit vīrá- (man, hero) → Proto-Germanic *weraz (man), late 2nd–1st millennium BCE → Old English wer (man, male person), attested from the 8th century CE; its modern survival is chiefly the combining form in werewolf and similar compounds → Modern English combining form were- ("man; man-shaped; shape-shifting") — the first element of this card's compound. CHAIN 2 — WORMS ("serpent, dragon"), the second element: → PIE *wŕ̥mis / *wrm̥i- ("worm, serpent"), reconstructed; cognate Latin vermis (worm), Greek rhómos (woodworm), Lithuanian var̃mas (a worm or maggot) → Proto-Germanic *wurmiz (worm, serpent, dragon), late 2nd–1st millennium BCE → Old English wyrm (serpent, dragon, reptile, worm), attested from the 8th century CE; in heroic verse wyrm frequently means "dragon" (the dragon of Beowulf is a wyrm) → Middle English worm (worm; serpent; dragon), 12th–15th centuries CE, keeping the dragon sense beside the everyday small-creature sense → Modern English worm; plural worms — the second element of this card's compound → WERE-WORMS — the card's full target compound (were- + worms), Tolkien's coinage for the fabulous desert dragon-serpents of the "Last Desert." NOTE on the compound: the name follows the were- + beast template of werewolf; as a whole it is a modern literary creation (first in Tolkien's The Hobbit), not an attested medieval word — but both of its element chains are historically secure.
