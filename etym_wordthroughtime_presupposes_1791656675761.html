- 1420s–1500s: Late Middle English Scholastic Dialectics - Borrowed directly from Old French *presupposer* and Medieval Latin *praesupponere* into English scholastic philosophy, functioning as a technical term in university syllogisms to signify laying down a prior premise before proceeding with a formal proof.
- 16th–17th Century: Humanist Rhetoric & Scientific Rationalism - Expanded beyond monastic logic into natural philosophy (Francis Bacon, René Descartes, Thomas Hobbes), describing the fundamental epistemological assumptions that must be true before empirical observation or political sovereignty can be justified.
- 18th–19th Century: German Idealism & Kantian Epistemology - Deepened immensely under the influence of Immanuel Kant's critical philosophy, where *presupposition* (*Voraussetzung*) came to denote a 'condition of possibility' (*Bedingung der Möglichkeit*)—the inescapable cognitive structures without which human experience cannot occur.
- Late 19th–20th Century: Analytic Philosophy & Formal Linguistics - Rigorously formalized by Gottlob Frege, Bertrand Russell, and P. F. Strawson into linguistic theory, defining 'presupposition' as a distinct semantic condition where a proposition's truth-value depends entirely on the prior truth of an unstated background statement.
- Late 20th–21st Century: Social Theory & Institutional Economics - Widely applied across sociology, historical anthropology, and institutional analysis to uncover the hidden cultural, legal, and infrastructural scaffolding that modern systems (such as capitalism or representative democracy) tacitly take for granted.
