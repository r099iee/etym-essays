• Traps: Similar because both denote devices or strategies intended to capture animals or people; differs because 'traps' is a broad, overarching category including cages, pits, spring-traps, and deadfalls, whereas 'snares' specifically refer to noose-based or cord-based entanglements.
  • Nooses: Similar because both consist of running loops of cord or rope that tighten upon pulling; differs because 'nooses' are typically associated with hangman's executions or simple knots, whereas 'snares' encompass complete trapping mechanisms with triggers and anchor points.
  • Pitfalls: Similar because both represent hidden dangers or traps; differs because a 'pitfall' is a concealed hole in the ground or a structural danger into which one falls, rather than a tightening loop.
  • Toils: Similar because both describe entangling nets or traps; differs because 'toils' (derived from French toile, cloth/net) specifically evokes heavy woven hunting nets used to enclose large quarry, often used in poetic plural.
  • Ambushes: Similar because both involve surprise capture; differs because an 'ambush' is a tactical military or physical assault launched from concealment by living attackers.
  • Entanglements: Similar because both involve being caught or snarled in complex obstacles; differs because 'entanglements' describes the state of general messiness or confusion (like barbed wire or legal complications) without an explicit trigger mechanism.
  • Lures: Similar because both attract prey; differs because a 'lure' is a deceptive bait or decoy used to attract the target toward the trap, whereas the snare is the capturing mechanism itself.
