PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*webʰ- / *wab-
‘to weave; to move to and fro, waver’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*wapp- / *wabb-
‘to move back and forth, oscillate, waver’
↓
Old English Personal Name (c. 6th–7th c.)
*Wæppa / *Wappa
‘personal hypocoristic name (lit. agile or bustling one)’
↓
Old English Patronymic Suffix (c. 7th–8th c.)
-ingas
‘the people of, sons or followers of’
↓
Old English Toponym (c. 8th c.)
*Wappingas
‘the settlement of Wæppa’s people’
↓
Middle English Records (c. 1220–1231)
Wappinges → Wapping
‘settlement and marshland along the Thames’
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th–17th c.)
Wapping-on-the-Woze / Wapping
‘riverside maritime sailortown of London’
↓
Modern English (current)
Wapping
‘historic riverside district in East London’

Note: Modern place-name scholarship (including Eilert Ekwall’s Concise Oxford Dictionary of English Place-Names and the English Place-Name Society) establishes that Wapping belongs to the ancient stratum of archaic Anglo-Saxon -ingas group names. The personal name *Wæppa is an unrecorded but phonologically regular hypocoristic (short form) based on a Germanic verbal root signifying movement or agility, or related to Old English wæpen (‘weapon’).
