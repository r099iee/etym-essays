• Dissatisfied: Similar to DISCONTENTED because both name a lack of satisfaction; differs because dissatisfied is a neutral, focused displeasure with a specific result, while discontented is a chronic, restless mood about one's general circumstances.
  • Disgruntled: Similar to DISCONTENTED because both describe displeasure with one's situation; differs because disgruntled implies sulky, grumbling, bad-tempered annoyance, while discontented can be a quieter but more pervasive unhappiness.
  • Restless: Similar to DISCONTENTED because both involve an inability to settle; differs because restless emphasizes physical and mental agitation and fidgeting, while discontented centres on dissatisfaction with one's lot.
  • Uneasy: Similar to DISCONTENTED because both describe a lack of ease; differs because uneasy is anxiety or discomfort without a definite object, while discontented specifically targets one's circumstances.
  • Malcontent: Similar to DISCONTENTED because both describe a person ill-pleased with the existing order; differs because malcontent is stronger and more political — one actively hostile or rebellious toward authority.
  • Unhappy: Similar to DISCONTENTED because both denote absence of happiness; differs because unhappy is the broad, general word, while discontented adds the specific sense of dissatisfaction with one's situation.
  • Disaffected: Similar to DISCONTENTED because both describe alienation from a situation or authority; differs because disaffected is chiefly used of populations, workers, or groups estranged from an institution or regime — a political rather than personal mood.
  • Restive: Similar to DISCONTENTED because both imply restlessness under constraint; differs because restive means impatient under control or discipline, while discontented is a general unhappiness with circumstances.
  • Whim: Similar to CAPRICE because both name a sudden, unaccountable desire or change of mind; differs because whim is the lighter, everyday word for a passing fancy, while caprice carries more weight of unpredictability and unreasonableness.
  • Fancy: Similar to CAPRICE because both denote an unreasoned inclination; differs because fancy can be a mild, even pleasant inclination, while caprice is a sharper, more erratic and sudden shift.
  • Vagary: Similar to CAPRICE because both name an unpredictable, eccentric action or notion; differs because vagary (often plural, vagaries) suggests wild or bizarre digressions, while caprice is a sudden change of mood or behaviour.
  • Quirk: Similar to CAPRICE because both describe an odd, unpredictable trait or behaviour; differs because a quirk is a fixed peculiarity of personality, while caprice is a sudden, passing change.
  • Impulse: Similar to CAPRICE because both describe action without deliberation; differs because impulse can be a natural or even healthy urge, while caprice emphasizes the arbitrary, unreasoned, and changeable quality.
  • Volatility: Similar to CAPRICE because both imply rapid, unpredictable change; differs because volatility describes a general disposition to change quickly (of moods, markets), while caprice is the single sudden change or whim itself.
  • Fickleness: Similar to CAPRICE because both denote inconstancy; differs because fickleness is a trait of character (being changeable and unreliable), while caprice is the specific sudden change or whim.
  • Whimsy: Similar to CAPRICE because both belong to the family of whim; differs because whimsy suggests light, playful, often charming oddity, while caprice is more erratic and can be perverse or unreasonable.
  • Crotchet: Similar to CAPRICE because both name an odd, unpredictable notion; differs because crotchet (a fanciful whim) is a milder, quirkier British word, while caprice is the general and stronger term.
  • Changeability: Similar to CAPRICE because both involve the disposition to change; differs because changeability is a neutral capacity to change, while caprice is the sudden, unaccountable change itself, with a suggestion of unreason.
