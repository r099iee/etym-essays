- Original sense - Meant to eat the final evening meal, historically a light repast of broth or soaked bread, taken quietly at day's end.
- Expanded sense - Broadened to any evening meal and, by association, to drinking by small sips; carried a simple, homely, communal register.
- Figurative use - Turned up in proverbs like 'he that sups with the devil needs a long spoon,' giving the word a wary, cautionary flavor.
- Contemporary usage - Largely archaic and literary today, kept for rustic charm or historical flavor, while everyday speech prefers 'ate dinner' or 'had supper.'
