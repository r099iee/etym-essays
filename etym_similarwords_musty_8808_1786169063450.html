• Moldy: Similar because it denotes a damp-decay odor; differs because moldy implies visible fungal growth, whereas musty describes the stale, unventilated air odor even when mold is invisible.
  • Fusty: Similar because it denotes a stale, damp smell; differs because fusty emphasizes the smell of old wooden casks or stuffy, unventilated rooms, whereas musty emphasizes dampness and slow decay.
  • Stale: Similar because it denotes a loss of freshness; differs because stale applies to dry old bread or flat, tired air, whereas musty specifically requires damp, moldy odor.
  • Mildewed: Similar because it denotes fungal dampness; differs because mildewed specifies surface discoloration and fungus on cloth or paper, whereas musty names the smell of the air and the objects in it.
  • Dank: Similar because it denotes a cold, damp atmosphere; differs because dank emphasizes disagreeable cold wetness and darkness, whereas musty emphasizes the stale, moldy smell.
  • Rank: Similar because it denotes an offensive odor; differs because rank implies strong, foul overgrowth or stench, whereas musty is a softer, drier cellar dampness.
  • Fetid: Similar because it denotes a bad smell; differs because fetid implies actively stinking rot or putrefaction, whereas musty is the quieter smell of long-closed age.
  • Mephitic: Similar because it denotes a foul, noxious air; differs because mephitic suggests poisonous, sickening vapors, whereas musty is mild, stale, and associated with archives and cellars.
  • Humid: Similar because it denotes dampness; differs because humid is a neutral meteorological description of moisture, whereas musty carries the smell and staleness of damp decay.
  • Outdated: Similar to the figurative sense of old and stale; differs because outdated is a neutral temporal judgment, whereas musty is a contemptuous metaphor implying the smell of neglect.
  • Antiquated: Similar to the figurative sense of old; differs because antiquated implies old-fashioned or obsolete in style, whereas musty suggests physically and intellectually stale, dust-covered, and damp.
  • Earthy: Similar because both relate to soil-like smells; differs because earthy (geosmin, petrichor) is often positive and fresh, whereas musty is stale, enclosed, and negative.
  • Moldering: Similar because it denotes decaying matter; differs because moldering is the process of slow decay itself, whereas musty is the quality of smell produced.
  • Stuffy: Similar because it denotes stale, unventilated air; differs because stuffy emphasizes closeness and lack of fresh air, whereas musty emphasizes the damp moldy smell within that air.
