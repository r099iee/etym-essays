- [17th-century emergence] - Entering English prose in the 1600s as the adjective companion of 'decision', it first described arguments, rulings and causes that settle a question - a formal, near-technical word of law and logic.
- [Military and political register] - In the 18th and 19th centuries it attached itself to battles, victories and crises - a 'decisive moment' that turns history - gaining a confident, teleological ring (Macaulay's 'decisive crisis').
- [Personal and modern sense] - In the 20th century the word moved inward to describe a person's character - a decisive leader, a decisive temperament - meaning resolute and quick to choose, so that today it straddles the event that settles a matter and the person who settles it.
