• hooting: Similar because both are loud, carrying cries of pursuit raised together in the same sentence; differs because hooting is the cruder, owl-like, derisive sound — half animal, half jeer — whereas hallooing is the articulate, formulaic call of the huntsman, a ceremonial cry with a pedigree rather than a mere noise.
  • whooping: Similar because both are loud, wordless outcries of excitement or pursuit; differs because whooping is high, triumphant, and spontaneous (the war-whoop, the whoop of joy), whereas hallooing is a stylized, repeatable cry that carries the discipline and pageantry of the hunt.
  • hailing: Similar because both are calls raised aloud to reach someone at a distance; differs because hailing is a neutral, polite, or maritime summons for attention, whereas hallooing specifically incites hounds, announces a chase, and carries the theatrical thrill of the hunt.
  • shouting: Similar because both involve raising the voice loudly; differs because shouting is the generic, unmarked word for loud speech, without any ritual, hunting-field, or theatrical associations — anyone can shout, but only a huntsman (or someone playing at one) halloos.
  • yelling: Similar because both are loud, energetic vocalizations; differs because yelling stresses raw volume and emotional intensity (anger, pain, fear), whereas hallooing stresses form, repetition, and purpose — it is loudness deployed as a signal of sport and pursuit.
  • calling: Similar because both direct a cry toward someone or something; differs because calling is neutral attention-seeking, whereas hallooing is the specialized, archaic cry of the chase, dignified and ceremonial rather than merely functional.
  • baying: Similar because both describe the loud, persistent noise of pursuit; differs because baying is properly the deep, resonant cry of hounds at the quarry, an animal sound, whereas hallooing is the human huntsman's articulate shout that directs those hounds.
  • hello-ing: Similar because both derive from the same ancient syllable of summons and share the base form; differs because hello-ing is a modern, casual, telephone-age greeting, stripped of all hunting and theatrical associations, whereas hallooing is archaic, ceremonial, and belongs to the field, not the phone.
  • hollering: Similar because both are American-dialect descendants of the same interjection family meaning loud shouting; differs because hollering is plain, vigorous, everyday yelling, with no hint of the hunt or of ceremony, whereas hallooing keeps the old-world pageantry of the chase.
  • clamouring: Similar because both denote loud, insistent collective noise; differs because clamouring implies a confused, demanding uproar of many voices, whereas hallooing is a coordinated, rhythmic, almost ritual cry — noise with a plan and a purpose.
