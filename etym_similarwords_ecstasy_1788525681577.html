• rapture: Similar because both denote an overwhelming state of exalted, transcendent delight; differs because rapture etymologically implies being violently seized or carried off by force (Latin rapere), whereas ecstasy emphasizes the state of standing outside of oneself or mental elevation beyond ordinary bodily consciousness.
  • bliss: Similar because both represent supreme happiness and joy; differs because bliss implies serene, untroubled, peaceful, and enduring heavenly contentment, whereas ecstasy is dynamic, turbulent, electrifying, and intensely physical in its tremors.
  • euphoria: Similar because both describe an elevated, intensely positive emotional state; differs because euphoria is a clinical, medical, or psychological term denoting an exaggerated, often artificial or chemically induced sense of well-being, lacking the poetic depth and mystical grandeur of ecstasy.
  • transport: Similar because both signify being carried away by intense emotion; differs because transport is a broader, slightly formal term that can apply to transports of grief or rage as well as joy, whereas modern ecstasy is almost exclusively reserved for peak delight and sublime passion.
  • elation: Similar because both involve a heightened, joyful mood; differs because elation emphasizes feelings of pride, triumphant accomplishment, and high spirits (being "lifted up"), without the dissolution of self-boundaries characteristic of ecstasy.
  • exaltation: Similar because both denote emotional and spiritual elevation; differs because exaltation stresses reverence, noble dignity, moral praise, and elevated status, whereas ecstasy captures intense emotional and sensual fervor.
  • frenzy: Similar because historically both involved states of agitated delirium and loss of self-control; differs because frenzy today strongly connotes wild, disordered, violent, or chaotic agitation, whereas ecstasy connotes harmonious, pleasurable, or divine elevation.
  • trance: Similar because both describe an altered state of consciousness detached from external reality; differs because trance focuses on the hypnotic, somnambulistic, or sleep-like state of reduced physical response, whereas ecstasy emphasizes radiant internal joy and emotional intensity.
  • delirium: Similar because both involve ecstatic fever and altered mental states; differs because delirium retains strong medical associations with fever, hallucinations, confusion, and cognitive impairment.
  • rhapsody: Similar because both describe effusive, enthusiastic outpourings of delight; differs because rhapsody is primarily an expressive, musical, or literary performance of praise rather than an internal psychological state.
