In the sentence at hand, FORKED describes beards divided into two branches: 'their beards were forked and plaited' — the classic twin-braided or bifurcated beard of the dwarves. Forked (adjective, past participle of the verb fork) means 'divided into two (or more) branches or prongs; shaped like a fork'. The word is built on the image of the eating or pitchfork: a single stem that splits into two or more tines.

1. Core senses

  • Bifurcated shape: split into two branches — a 'forked tongue' (of a snake, and metaphorically of a liar who speaks with two tongues), a 'forked beard', a 'forked road' or 'forked tree' (a trunk that divides into two limbs), 'forked lightning' (a bolt that branches).
  • Technical use: 'forked' in mechanics — a forked lever, a forked joint, a fork (in a bicycle) holding the front wheel; and in computing (below).
  • Verb 'to fork': to divide into branches — a road 'forks' (splits into two); 'fork out/over/up' (slang, 19th c.) = to pay up, hand over money — an image of reluctantly parting with coins.

2. Domain-specific senses

  • Computing: a 'fork' is a copy of a codebase or project that splits off into an independent line of development (from Unix's fork() system call, 1971, which copies a process into two); 'forked' = branched, no longer identical. GitHub made 'forking' a mainstream act of open-source collaboration after 2008.
  • Biology / anatomy: forked = bifurcate — a forked tail (of a swallow or a fish), a forked tongue (snakes), bifurcate structures; the Latinate synonym is 'bifurcate'.
  • Geography: a 'fork' is the point where a river or road divides, or one of the branches: 'the North Fork of the river'; 'forked' describes such dividing features.
  • Chess / others: a 'fork' in chess is a single piece attacking two enemy pieces at once (the piece 'forks' them — the shape-image again); a 'fork' in a boat's rigging; 'forklift', 'tuning fork', 'pitchfork', 'table fork' — all share the pronged shape.

3. Semantic bridges

  • From the tool (pitchfork, table fork) to the shape: a fork is defined by its prongs, so 'forked' = 'having prongs or branches' — the tool's name becomes the general word for any Y-shape (the verb is 13th–14th c.; the adjective 'forked' is recorded from c. 1300).
  • From shape to division: a road that 'forks' is a road that splits like a fork's prongs; from the road, any division into two lines of development — genealogies, discussions ('the conversation forked'), software projects.
  • From division to deception: the snake's forked tongue — two tongues — becomes the emblem of duplicity: 'to speak with a forked tongue' (a phrase popularised in 19th-century American frontier writing, though the exact idiom is a literary creation of that era).

4. Register and connotation

  • 'Forked' is an ordinary, vivid, shape-descriptive word — concrete and visual; it has no marked formality and is used of beards, lightning, roads, tongues, tails, and code.
  • The sinister tinge comes entirely from the snake association ('forked tongue' = deceit); otherwise the word is neutral.
