- Antiquity (Gallo-Roman Avennio) - A modest town of the Cavares tribe on the Rhône, recorded as Avennio; a local toponym of no wider fame.
- 14th century (Avignon Papacy, 1309–1377) - The name becomes world-famous as the seat of seven popes and two rival claimants; Avignon is synonymous with papal power and the “Babylonian captivity” of the Western Church, crowned by the Palais des Papes.
- 16th–18th century - A papal enclave (Comtat Venaissin) inside France, known for its bridge (Pont Saint-Bénézet) and its Rhône trade; annexed to France in 1791 during the Revolution.
- 19th century (Mill's Avignon) - Gains a literary identity: Harriet Taylor dies there in 1858 and John Stuart Mill retires to a modest house near her grave, writing his later works in the shadow of the cemetery — the emblem of a philosopher's devoted, grieving retirement.
- Contemporary - A celebrated tourist city of Provence — Palais des Papes, Pont d'Avignon, summer theatre festival — its layers of papal seat, Rhône port, and Millian sanctuary fused into one famous name.
