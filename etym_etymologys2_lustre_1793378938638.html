Note: The ultimate source is the PIE root *leuk- (‘light, bright’), which generated Latin lūx (‘light’), lūceō (‘to shine’), and lūcidus (‘clear, lucid’). The formation of Latin lūstrum involves a nominal suffix *-tro-. In ancient Rome, a lūstrum was the solemn purificatory sacrifice (suovetaurilia) performed by the censors every five years after completing the census; through association with ceremonial lustration (sprinkling with purifying water in daylight) and the verb lūstrāre (‘to shed light upon, illuminate, review’), the semantic thread shifted toward brightness, polish, and radiance in Renaissance Italian lustro.
