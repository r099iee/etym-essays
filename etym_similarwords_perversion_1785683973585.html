• CORRUPTION: Similar because both name damage done to something sound and both carry strong moral disapproval; differs because corruption implies decay, rot, or depravity arising from within the thing itself, while perversion specifically images a forcible turning or twisting of the thing away from its true course — the edict in the card’s sentence is wrenched, not rotted.
  • DISTORTION: Similar because both mean twisting something out of its proper shape or meaning; differs because distortion is narrower and morally lighter — the bending of facts, shapes, sounds, or appearances, with no necessary charge of evil — whereas perversion insists on a wrong purpose and a violation of nature, as when the ancient laws are twisted to serve a bad end.
  • SUBVERSION: Similar because both turn an established order against itself; differs because subversion is political and aims at the overthrow or undermining of an institution from without, while perversion turns a thing against its own proper nature from within and need not aim at any political overturn.
  • MISAPPLICATION: Similar because both describe using something in the wrong way; differs because misapplication is neutral, technical, and bureaucratic — a rule applied to the wrong case — while perversion is indignant and moral, accusing the user of actively perverting the law’s meaning.
  • ABUSE: Similar because both mean wrongful use; differs because abuse is broader and covers mistreatment, misuse, or insulting language, while perversion is more precise — it names a turning against proper nature, with the concreteness of a direction being reversed.
  • DEPRAVITY: Similar because both belong to moral condemnation and describe what is bad in character or conduct; differs because depravity is a state of the person — a corrupted, wicked character — while perversion is an act or effect — the turning of a law, text, or institution; the minister attacks the edict, not a man.
  • DEBASEMENT: Similar because both lower the value or quality of something; differs because debasement cheapens, degrades, or corrupts (of currency, standards, or taste) without the image of twisting, and is far less morally volcanic than perversion.
  • CONTORTION: Similar because both involve twisting; differs because contortion is chiefly physical and neutral — the bending of a body or shape into unnatural positions — while perversion is abstract and moral, a twisting of meaning, law, or nature.
