• Abating: Similar because both denote a progressive reduction in intensity, violence, or force; differs because abating emphasizes the weakening or lessening of dynamic energy (such as a storm, wind, or pain) rather than the physical downward settling or gravitational leveling inherent in subsiding.
  • Receding: Similar because both describe something withdrawing or retreating to a lower or distant state (especially floodwaters); differs because receding emphasizes horizontal withdrawal, backward movement, or optical fading into the distance, whereas subsiding denotes vertical downward settling or settling toward baseline equilibrium.
  • Diminishing: Similar because both indicate a quantitative or qualitative reduction; differs because diminishing is an abstract, general term for becoming smaller or fewer in degree, size, or importance, lacking the specific dynamic of settling after turbulent agitation.
  • Waning: Similar because both capture a gradual decline from a peak state; differs because waning conveys an organic, cyclical, or periodic fading (like the moon, influence, or enthusiasm) rather than an agitated disturbance returning to calmness or a physical surface sinking.
  • Dwindling: Similar because both convey a marked decrease; differs because dwindling specifically implies a progressive reduction toward depletion, exhaustion, or insignificance (e.g., dwindling resources, dwindling hope), whereas subsiding implies a return to a tranquil, normal, or stable baseline.
  • Sinking: Similar because both denote downward vertical movement; differs because sinking often connotes submergence, descent below a liquid surface, or catastrophic failure, whereas subsiding denotes either geological settling into place or an active agitation dying down into stillness.
  • Settling: Similar because both denote moving downward into a stable resting position; differs because settling is broader and can apply to dust, legal disputes, domestic habitation, or deliberate agreement, whereas subsiding carries a stronger connotation of previous turbulence, swelling, or elevated pressure giving way.
  • Easing: Similar because both denote a reduction in discomfort, severity, or strain; differs because easing emphasizes relief from tension or physical pain and often implies active intervention or gentle relaxation, whereas subsiding describes the natural, autonomous dying down of an event or state.
  • Lulling: Similar because both describe a shift toward temporary quietude or calmness; differs because a lull implies a deceptive, transient pause in activity or storminess before renewed agitation, whereas subsiding suggests a genuine, enduring descent into equilibrium.
  • Tapering: Similar because both describe a gradual lessening toward an end; differs because tapering (or tapering off) emphasizes a geometric, narrow, step-by-step thinning or reduction in rate, whereas subsiding emphasizes the dissipation of energy, height, or violent intensity.
  • Placating: Similar because both relate to the attainment of calm; differs because placating is strictly transitive and psychological, requiring an external agent actively pacifying an offended or hostile party, whereas subsiding is predominantly intransitive, describing the internal process of an emotion or phenomenon cooling down.
  • Abating: Similar because both denote a drop in violent activity; differs because abating carries a distinct legal and formal nuance (e.g., abating a nuisance or writ) alongside its physical application.
