Onomatopoeic formation (no attested PIE ancestor)
“imitative of rapid repeated cries and rattles”
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
chateren
“to twitter, jabber, talk rapidly”
↓
English (14th–15th c.)
chatter
“rapid trivial talk; bird cries”
↓
English (present participle form)
CHATTERING
“making rapid clicking or talkative sounds”

Note: the word has no Indo-European etymology — it is a native English imitative formation (with its twin chitter, Middle English chiteren, and its relative clatter, Old English clatrian). Latin garrīre “to chatter” (source of garrulous) is an independent, parallel imitation of the same sounds, not an ancestor. No stages are missing: the attested record begins in Middle English.
