PIE *kwo- "who? what?" (interrogative pronominal stem; reflexes across the family — Sanskrit kaḥ "who", Hindi kaun "who", Latin quī / quae / quod "who, which", Greek poû "where?", Old English hwā "who") → (DEBATED LINK) Latin quaerere "to seek, ask, inquire" — the traditional hypothesis derives this verb from the *kwo- stem, the idea being that to seek is to ask "who?" or "what?" of the world; but the verb has no certain Italic cognates and the derivation, though traditional, is not beyond dispute (see DEBATE) → Latin inquirere "to seek into, search for" (from in- "into" + quaerere; attested in classical Latin prose) → Latin inquisit- (perfect passive participle stem of inquirere; the stem used in derived nouns and adjectives) → Late Latin inquisitivus "given to questioning, searching" (adjectival formation with the suffix -ivus) → Old French inquisitif "given to inquiry" (medieval French, 12th–14th c.) → Middle English inquisityf / inquisitive "given to asking questions; eager to learn; prying" (attested late 14th c., via Old French) → Modern English inquisitive "curious, enquiring; prying, meddlesome" (with the derived forms inquisitively, inquisitiveness). DEBATE / NUANCE: The deep link to PIE *kwo- is traditional but debated: Latin quaerere has no sure Italic cognates, and some scholars instead derive it from the related root *kwei- "to pay, value, seek", which also gives Greek poinē "penalty, blood-price" and, through it, Latin poena, ancestor of English pain and penalty. Neither the *kwo- nor the *kwei- derivation is provable. The chain from Latin inquirere onward, however, is solid and well attested. The spelling inquisitive reflects the Latin route, while the parallel pair enquire/enquiry preserves the Old French route, and both spellings are current in modern English.
