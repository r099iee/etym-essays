*bhos-
"bare, naked, empty"

Frankish / West Germanic (c. 5th–8th c. CE, assumed)
*bar(r)-
"bare, naked"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
baraigne, barain
"sterile, unfruitful"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
barein, barain
"sterile (of women); unfruitful (of land)"
↓
Modern English (spelling 'barren' from 16th c.)
barren

Note: The origin is debated. The Oxford English Dictionary calls Old French baraigne 'of obscure origin'; the commonest hypothesis links it to a Germanic base meaning 'bare' (cf. Old English bær, Old High German bar), ultimately from PIE *bhos- 'bare', but the exact route through Frankish is assumed, not attested. Alternative proposals derive the word from a pre-Roman substrate source. The chain above follows the mainstream hypothesis and marks the doubtful stages.
