• Lifted: Similar because both describe moving an object from a lower to a higher position; differs because 'lifted' is the basic, ubiquitous term that requires no special mechanism or intense struggle, whereas 'hoisted' emphasizes significant weight, vertical hauling, or the use of mechanical leverage (ropes, pulleys, cranes).
  • Elevated: Similar because both involve raising something upward; differs because 'elevated' is formal, abstract, or clinical, often referring to raising rank, social status, moral tone, or medical angles, while 'hoisted' is physical, nautical, and mechanical.
  • Hauled: Similar because both involve strenuous pulling of heavy loads; differs because 'hauled' usually denotes horizontal dragging or transporting over distance, whereas 'hoisted' is strictly vertical ascent.
  • Heaved: Similar because both imply tremendous physical exertion to move something heavy; differs because 'heaved' emphasizes the rhythmic strain, swelling, or throwing motion (e.g., heaving a boulder), whereas 'hoisted' focuses on vertical lifting and securing at a higher elevation.
  • Winched: Similar because both refer to raising loads via mechanical devices; differs because 'winched' specifically requires a winch (a revolving spool and crank/motor cable system), whereas 'hoisted' can be done with ropes, cranes, or raw human arms.
  • Jacked: Similar because both describe raising heavy objects using mechanical aid; differs because 'jacked' (as in jacked up) specifically implies lifting from beneath using a hydraulic or screw jack, typically for short vertical distances (e.g., lifting a car).
  • Raised: Similar because both denote upward movement; differs because 'raised' is a broad general hypernym that encompasses physical lifting as well as breeding children, asking questions, or increasing prices, while 'hoisted' is specialized for heavy vertical lifting.
  • Craned: Similar because both involve mechanical upward lifting or stretching; differs because 'craned' either specifically refers to using an industrial crane or stretching one's neck to see, while 'hoisted' applies to any upward lifting effort or tackle.
