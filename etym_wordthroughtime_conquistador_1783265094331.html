- Spanish origin - conquistador, the Spanish agent noun for conqueror (from conquista, conquest), names the sixteenth-century Spanish soldiers and adventurers who led the conquest of the Americas, from Cortés in Mexico to Pizarro in Peru.
- Romantic historiography - in older Western histories the conquistadors were heroic figures of audacious enterprise, men who toppled vast indigenous empires through daring, alliance, and firepower.
- Modern reassessment - contemporary scholarship and public memory have recast the conquistador as the armed colonizer whose conquest brought colonial violence and demographic catastrophe, so the word now carries both admiration for daring and condemnation of colonial brutality.
- Contemporary extension - beyond the Americas, conquistador is extended metaphorically to any audacious conqueror, corporate or military, while retaining its unmistakable Spanish historical flavour in English.
