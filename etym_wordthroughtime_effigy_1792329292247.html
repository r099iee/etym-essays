- 16th Century — Elite High Art: A formal, dignified artistic representation or sculpted monument, particularly a stone or bronze funerary sculpture laid atop a tomb.
- Early 17th Century — Judicial & Symbolic Execution: Transitioned into continental and British legal ritual (execution in effigie), where an absent, condemned criminal or heretic had their painted portrait or mock figure publicly hanged or burned.
- 18th to 19th Century — Popular Protest & Mob Ridicule: Secularized into radical street protests and holiday festivities (notably Guy Fawkes Night in Britain and Stamp Act protests in the American colonies), turning the word into a symbol of crude stuffed straw dummies and political contempt.
- 20th to 21st Century — Vitriolic Street Demonstration: Almost universally evokes an inflammatory political demonstration, effigy burning, or ritual burning of foreign leaders and mythic antagonists (such as Ravana during Dussehra/Vijayadashami).
