PIE (reconstructed; debated)
*speh₁-
"to stretch, extend, spread out"

Latin (2nd c. BCE)
spatium
"space, extent, room, distance"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
spatiārī
"to walk about, take a stroll"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
expatiārī (ex- "out" + spatiārī)
"to spread out, extend;
to range, roam at large"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
expatiātus
"spread out; roamed at large"
↓
English (17th c.)
expatiate
"to speak or write at length;
enlarge upon a topic"
↓
English (17th c.)
expatiating
"speaking or writing at length;
enlarging upon a topic"

Debate note: The PIE root of spatium is uncertain; *speh₁- "to stretch, spread" is proposed (cf. Greek spáo "to draw, pull") but the cognate set is small. The "speak at length" sense comes from the Latin figurative use of expatiārī, "to roam at large", applied to speech.
