Latin spatium 'room, open space, extent; distance; interval of time; racecourse, walking-place' (attested from Plautus, 3rd–2nd c. BCE; no secure Indo-European etymology — traditional dictionaries suggest PIE *spe(i)- 'to spread, expand', but this is uncertain and must be marked as such; see note) → Latin spatiari 'to walk about, take a walk, stroll' (deponent verb of the colonnade and the park; from spatium) → Latin exspatiari / expatiari 'to wander out of bounds, stray from the course; (of a river) to overflow; (in rhetoric) to digress, expatiate' (ex- 'out' + spatiari; attested from Cicero, 1st c. BCE) → Latin expatiātus (past participle) → Late Latin expatiare → English expatiate (c. 1650, 'to roam, wander'; figurative 'to speak or write at length' from the 1750s) → English expatiating (present participle, 'speaking or writing at length'). Note: Latin spatium has no secure Indo-European etymology; the traditional root *spe(i)- 'to spread, expand' is proposed by the older dictionaries but is not universally accepted. Note also that expatriate 'to drive out of one's country' is a DIFFERENT Latin compound (ex- + patria 'fatherland') and must not be merged with expatiate.
