Old French (Imitative) (c. 13th c.)
groucier / grocier
"to grumble, murmur, mutter in discontent"
↓
British Army Slang (c. 1880s)
grouse
"to grumble, complain over petty grievances"
↓
Modern English (19th c.)
GROUSE
"to complain or grumble discontentedly"

Note on etymology: The verb "grouse" (complain) arose in 19th-century British military slang from Old French "groucier" and is etymologically distinct from the game bird "grouse".
