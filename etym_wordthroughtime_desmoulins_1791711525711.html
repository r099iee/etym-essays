- 1789 to 1791: Radical revolutionary hero, catalyst of the Bastille, and populist journalist - Celebrated across Paris as the fearless young orator of the Palais-Royal and the razor-sharp satirist of the Ancien Régime in Les Révolutions de France et de Brabant.
- 1792 to 1793: Montagnard deputy, ally of Danton, and combatant against the Girondins - Voted for the execution of Louis XVI; wielded his satirical pen to crush the moderate Girondin faction in the National Convention.
- Late 1793 to 1794: Conscience of the Revolution, prophet of clemency, and martyr of the Terror - Rebounded against the bloodbath of the Reign of Terror in Le Vieux Cordelier, becoming an apostate to the Jacobin government and dying on the guillotine.
- 19th Century: Romantic icon of passionate idealism and revolutionary tragedy - Immortalized by Jules Michelet, Thomas Carlyle, and Georg Büchner (in Danton's Death) as the artistic, humane soul of 1789 crushed by ideological fanaticism.
- 20th to 21st Century: Foundational historical symbol of the power and perils of political rhetoric - Analyzed in historiography and political theory as an archetype of the intellectual whose literary genius ignites a revolution that ultimately destroys him.
