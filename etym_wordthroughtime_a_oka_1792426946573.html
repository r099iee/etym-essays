- [Vedic & Epic Antiquity: c. 1000–500 BCE] — Applied as an elevated adjective ("sorrowless") in the Upaniṣads and established as a sacred tree in the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, where the *Aśokavāṭikā* in Lan̄kā forms the emotional heart of Sītā's captivity and Hanumān's solace — [perception: sacred flowering haven of protection, deep longing, and divine comfort]
- [Classical Sanskrit Kāvya Golden Age: c. 4th–8th c. CE] — Immortally celebrated in Kālidāsa's poetry (*Mālavikāgnimitra*, *Meghadūta*, *Kumārasambhava*) as the premier tree of romantic poetics, blossoming exclusively via the tender kick of a beautiful maiden's foot (*dohada*) — [perception: quintessential aesthetic symbol of spring passion, feminine beauty, and courtly romance]
- [Mauryan & Historical Buddhist Era: c. 3rd c. BCE–Medieval] — Immortalized as the royal throne name of Emperor Aśoka the Great, transforming the word into a worldwide monument of Buddhist moral governance, non-violence (*ahiṃsā*), and ethical statecraft — [perception: imperial archetype of repentance, moral authority, and universal welfare]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era: 19th–21st c.] — Universally recognized across botanical medicine (Ayurvedic *Aśokāriṣṭa*), state symbolism (India's national emblem, the Lion Capital and Aśoka Chakra on the flag), and popular personal names — [perception: supreme national emblem of moral triumph, pharmaceutical healing, and cultural pride]
