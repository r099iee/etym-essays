• Brentano: Similar because Franz Brentano was Ehrenfels's revered philosophy professor in Vienna whose act psychology and doctrine of intentionality inspired Ehrenfels's work; differs because Brentano focused on the intentional relationship between mental acts and objects within a descriptive scholastic framework, whereas Ehrenfels isolated the specific perceptual emergence of holistic 'form-qualities' (Gestaltqualitäten).
  • Meinong: Similar because Alexius Meinong was Ehrenfels's mentor and collaborator at the University of Graz who developed the Graz School's theory of objects (Gegenstandstheorie); differs because Meinong formulated complex hierarchical orders of higher-order objects (Fundierungsrelation) within speculative metaphysics, whereas Ehrenfels's 1890 melody criterion provided the direct empirical bridge into experimental Gestalt psychology.
  • Mach: Similar because the Austrian physicist and philosopher Ernst Mach directly anticipated Ehrenfels with his 1886 analysis of spatial and acoustic sensations (noting that we perceive melodies and geometric shapes directly); differs because Mach retained a radical sensationalist, positivistic reductionism (claiming space-forms are physiological sensations of eye movements), whereas Ehrenfels recognized form-quality as an emergent relational mental reality.
  • Wertheimer: Similar because Max Wertheimer was Ehrenfels's student in Prague who took Ehrenfels's concept and founded the Berlin School of Gestalt psychology; differs because Ehrenfels maintained that elemental sensory tones still exist as the foundation upon which the mind builds a form-quality, whereas Wertheimer made the radical step of asserting that the Gestalt is primary, immediate, and does not depend on prior elemental sensations.
  • Köhler: Similar because Wolfgang Köhler built his entire physical field theory upon the foundational dilemma of wholeness first articulated by Ehrenfels; differs because Köhler was an experimental physicist and primatologist who investigated cortical electrical fields, whereas Ehrenfels was a philosopher who worked primarily through philosophical phenomenology, ethics, and value theory.
  • Koffka: Similar because Kurt Koffka systematically articulated the principle of non-summative wholeness first popularized by Ehrenfels; differs because Koffka applied holistic theory to child development, developmental psychology, and general perception in English textbooks, whereas Ehrenfels formulated the initial philosophical concept within the nineteenth-century Austrian epistemological milieu.
  • Husserl: Similar because Edmund Husserl was Ehrenfels's fellow student under Franz Brentano who addressed similar problems of part-whole relations and categorical intuition in his Logical Investigations; differs because Husserl developed transcendental phenomenology, whereas Ehrenfels focused on empirical Gestalt qualities, musicology, and evolutionary value theory.
