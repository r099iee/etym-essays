- 15th–16th Century: Legal acquisition and physical obtaining - entered English from Anglo-French to designate the formal procurement of titles, estates, or privileges, closely tied to the verb attain.
- 17th Century: Scholarly discovery and scientific advancement - embraced during the Scientific Revolution by figures like Francis Bacon to describe the hard-won intellectual victories and proven doctrines of natural philosophy.
- 18th to Early 19th Century: Polite cultivation and elegant personal graces - celebrated in the Georgian and Regency eras (as in Jane Austen) as the essential refined skills—music, languages, drawing, and literature—of a cultivated lady or gentleman.
- Late 19th to Mid-20th Century: Established scientific milestones and institutional truths - employed by thinkers like Carl Menger and John Stuart Mill to denote the verified, definitive principles established by a scientific discipline.
- Contemporary 21st Century Usage: Standardized educational metrics and developmental milestones - predominantly encountered today in institutional phrases like "educational attainments," quantifying academic degrees, literacy rates, and professional proficiencies.
