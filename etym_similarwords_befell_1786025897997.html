• Overtook: Similar because both describe an event catching up to and affecting someone; differs because overtook emphasizes the suddenness of catching up from behind or surpassing someone's capacity to escape or prepare, whereas befell emphasizes the gravity, fate, and downward descent of the occurrence.
  • Transpired: Similar because both describe occurrences coming to pass; differs because transpired literally means "leaked out through pores" and is properly used when a secret or unknown fact becomes gradually known, or in formal register for general occurrence without any tragic connotation.
  • Occurred: Similar because both mean happened; differs because occurred is a completely neutral, objective, matter-of-fact term devoid of poetic weight, destiny, or inherent tragedy.
  • Happened: Similar because both denote an occurrence; differs because happened is the default conversational, everyday verb that carries no inherent stylistic elevation, dramatic tension, or ominous tone.
  • Betided: Similar because both are archaic verbs for fateful happenings; differs because betided (from tide / betide) specifically emphasizes the rolling in of time and fortune (e.g., "woe betide him"), whereas befell emphasizes the striking impact of the event itself.
  • Struck: Similar because both denote an adverse event impacting someone; differs because struck emphasizes violent, instantaneous physical or emotional impact (e.g., lightning struck, disaster struck), while befell conveys a narrative unfolding of destiny over time.
  • Visited: Similar because both can describe calamity falling upon a people; differs because visited carries a biblical, theological connotation of deliberate divine judgment or chastisement (e.g., "the sins of the fathers visited upon the children").
