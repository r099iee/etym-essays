• Rascals: Similar because both name rogues of low principle; differs because a rascal is often mischievous and half forgiven, while a scoundrel is condemned as genuinely base.
  • Knaves: Similar because both denounce deceitful villains; differs because knave is the older card-pack word for a cheating servant, while scoundrel is the broader modern scolding.
  • Rogues: Similar because both brand dishonest tricksters; differs because rogue can be playful or even admiring, while scoundrel keeps its moral frown.
  • Villains: Similar because both name wicked wrongdoers; differs because a villain may be grand and powerful, while a scoundrel is petty, shabby, and contemptible.
  • Swindlers: Similar because both cheat for gain; differs because a swindler is defined by financial fraud, while a scoundrel may wrong by lying, betrayal, or cowardice.
  • Blackguards: Similar because both are old-fashioned terms of utter contempt for base men; differs because blackguard stresses foul-mouthed ruffianism, while scoundrel stresses deceit and dishonour.
  • Miscreants: Similar because both condemn wrongdoers from a moral height; differs because miscreant is formal and legalistic, while scoundrel is vivid popular denunciation.
  • Reprobates: Similar because both declare someone morally lost; differs because a reprobate is hardened beyond shame, while a scoundrel still knows the decency he betrays.
  • Cads: Similar because both scorn dishonourable men, especially to women; differs because a cad is specifically the gentleman who behaves basely, while a scoundrel needs no rank at all.
