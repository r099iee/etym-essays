• Claim: Similar because both are assertions of a right or quality; differs because claim is neutral and may be perfectly legitimate (a claim to one's inheritance), whereas pretension is always a claim regarded as unearned.
  • Presumption: Similar because both involve taking more than one is entitled to; differs because presumption means overstepping, taking liberties beyond one's place, while pretension is the specific assertion of a standing one does not have.
  • Affectation: Similar because both present a false front to others; differs because affectation is the mannered outward behaviour adopted to impress, whereas pretension is the underlying assertion of merit of which affectation is the visible garb.
  • Pretentiousness: Similar because both name the display of unearned self-importance; differs because pretentiousness is the quality of being showily self-important (from the adjective pretentious, 1852), while pretension is the underlying claim itself.
  • Arrogance: Similar because both inflate one's own worth; differs because arrogance is an overbearing attitude of superiority toward others, whereas pretension claims a social or intellectual position one has not earned.
  • Conceit: Similar because both involve an exaggerated opinion of oneself; differs because conceit is vanity about one's own merits, while pretension asserts a standing or skill one lacks in the eyes of others.
  • Ostentation: Similar because both involve showiness; differs because ostentation displays what one actually has (wealth, finery), whereas pretension claims what one does not have.
  • Airs: Similar because both mean affected superiority; differs because airs is the informal, spoken-language plural ("putting on airs"), while pretension is the formal abstract noun for the claim itself.
  • Aspiration: Similar because both can name a reaching toward something; differs because aspiration is an honourable ambition, while pretension is an ambition or claim regarded as unearned or excessive.
  • Pretense: Similar because both descend from the same Latin family of "putting forward"; differs because pretense (BrE pretence) is the act of feigning in a particular instance ("under false pretences"), whereas pretension is the standing claim of worth.
