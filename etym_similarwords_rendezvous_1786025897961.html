• Meeting: Similar because both involve people coming together; differs because meeting is the plain, universal noun for any coming together, while rendezvous implies a specific, pre-arranged time and place, often military, secretive, or charged with purpose.
  • Tryst: Similar because both are pre-arranged meetings; differs because tryst strongly implies a romantic, illicit, or secret lovers' meeting, whereas rendezvous can be entirely military, professional, or casual.
  • Appointment: Similar because both are arranged meetings; differs because appointment is the professional, institutional word (doctor's, business), while rendezvous has a more dramatic, atmospheric, or military colouring.
  • Assignation: Similar because both denote a planned meeting; differs because assignation is heavily weighted toward illicit romantic encounters and is far rarer, while rendezvous is neutral and widely used.
  • Date: Similar because both are arranged meetings between people; differs because date is informal and almost exclusively romantic in English, while rendezvous is formal and neutral.
  • Gathering: Similar because both involve people assembling; differs because a gathering implies a larger group and less precise coordination, while a rendezvous is a tight, point-in-time meeting of specific participants.
  • Get-together: Similar because both are informal meetings; differs because get-together is casual, domestic, and social, whereas rendezvous carries formal, military, or dramatic resonance.
  • Rallying point: Similar because both designate a place where forces assemble; differs because rallying point stresses the regrouping of scattered forces after dispersal, while rendezvous is a neutral meeting place agreed in advance.
  • Encounter: Similar because both involve people coming into contact; differs because an encounter can be unplanned or hostile, while a rendezvous is by definition arranged and mutually agreed.
  • Meeting point: Similar because both name a location of assembly; differs because meeting point is the plain, modern, logistical term (often found on maps), while rendezvous is the same idea with atmosphere and history.
  • Confluence: Similar because both can describe things coming together at one place; differs because confluence is normally used of rivers or figurative currents, while rendezvous is used of people and vehicles that meet by arrangement.
