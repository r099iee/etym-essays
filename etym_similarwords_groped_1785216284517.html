• Fumbled: Similar because both involve awkward, uncertain handling by touch; differs because fumbling is clumsy manipulation of objects already in hand, while groping reaches out to search for something unseen.
  • Searched: Similar because both mean looking for something; differs because searching is deliberate and methodical, whereas groping is blind, hesitant, and without direction.
  • Pawed: Similar because both involve touching with the hands clumsily; differs because pawing suggests grosser, coarser contact, while groping is a delicate, uncertain probing of unseen surroundings.
  • Felt: Similar because both use the sense of touch; differs because feeling is neutral and calm, while groping carries helplessness, fear, and the desperation of blindness.
  • Probed: Similar because both mean examining by touch; differs because probing is systematic and investigative (as a doctor probes a wound), while groping is blind and undirected.
  • Blundered: Similar because both imply movement without clear sight; differs because blundering is clumsy walking with the whole body and implies noisy error, while groping is quiet, cautious hand-searching.
  • Rummaged: Similar because both mean searching through things by hand; differs because rummaging is a vigorous turning-over of a mass of objects, while groping is a gentle, blind probing of unseen space.
  • Stumbled: Similar because both imply uncertain movement in darkness; differs because stumbling is a tripping of the feet, while groping is a reaching of the hands.
  • Grasped / Grabbed: Similar because both derive from the same root of reaching and seizing; differs because grasping seizes something already known, while groping reaches uncertainly for something not yet found.
  • Ransacked: Similar because both can describe searching; differs because ransacking is violent and destructive plundering, while groping is delicate and helpless, without force or intent to damage.
