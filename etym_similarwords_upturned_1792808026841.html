• Raised: Similar because both describe lifting upward (raised faces, raised hands); differs because raised stresses upward motion from below, while upturned stresses rotation — the surface itself now faces the sky.
  • Uplifted: Similar because both picture faces, eyes, and hands directed heavenward; differs because uplifted is loftier and often spiritual (‘uplifted in prayer’), while upturned is plain physical description.
  • Upcast: Similar because both mean thrown or turned upward (upcast eyes, upcast soil); differs because upcast is literary and slightly archaic, often with scorn (‘an upcast lip’), while upturned is neutral and current.
  • Overturned: Similar because both describe flipping upside down (boats, carts, verdicts); differs because overturned stresses toppling past the tipping point, often violently, while upturned can be a gentle deliberate rotation.
  • Capsized: Similar because both describe boats flipped keel-up; differs because capsized is specifically nautical disaster (the vessel overturned in the water), while an upturned boat may simply be beached for storage.
  • Inverted: Similar because both mean turned upside down; differs because inverted is technical and precise (inverted image, inverted commas), while upturned is everyday and visual.
  • Retroussé: Similar because both describe the charmingly tilted-up nose; differs because retroussé is the French loanword of fashion and novels (noses only), while upturned applies to faces, boats, soil, and all.
  • Supine: Similar because both can describe faces turned skyward (lying supine, face upturned); differs because supine describes the whole body's lying posture (and, figuratively, passivity), while upturned names only the rotated surface.
  • Keel-up: Similar because both picture the capsized boat's exposed bottom; differs because keel-up is salty nautical slang for the wreck itself, while upturned is the general word for anything flipped.
