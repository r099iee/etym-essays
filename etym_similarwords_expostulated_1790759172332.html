• Remonstrated: Similar because both mean to present reasoned arguments in opposition or protest against someone's actions; differs because 'remonstrate' often carries a slightly more formal, stern, or official connotation of presenting grievances, whereas 'expostulate' frequently conveys earnest, heartfelt, and affectionate personal pleading.
  • Protested: Similar because both express objection or disagreement; differs because 'protested' can refer to a simple, declarative expression of dissent or public demonstration without engaging in bilateral, reasoned persuasion.
  • Admonished: Similar because both involve counseling someone against undesirable behavior; differs because 'admonish' emphasizes cautionary reproof, warning, or mild reprimand from a position of authority rather than mutual argumentative reasoning.
  • Pleaded: Similar because both involve intense personal appeal to change someone's mind; differs because 'pleaded' emphasizes emotional supplication, begging, or desperation rather than structured rational argument.
  • Demurred: Similar because both involve raising objections to a proposal; differs because 'demurred' signifies a polite, quiet, or hesitant expression of doubt or reluctance rather than an active, impassioned verbal confrontation.
  • Rebuked: Similar because both involve confronting another about their conduct; differs because 'rebuked' signifies sharp, direct censure or criticism, lacking the discursive reasoning and persuasive dissuasion inherent in expostulation.
  • Exhorted: Similar because both represent earnest verbal appeals; differs because 'exhorted' focuses on passionately urging someone *to take* a positive, heroic, or righteous action, whereas 'expostulated' focuses on dissuading them *from taking* an unwise or destructive one.
