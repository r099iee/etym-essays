Cadenced describes language, sound or movement that has been given a clear, measured rhythm — that rises and falls in regular, pleasing beats like music settling toward a close. In the source phrase “lovely, cadenced phraseology,” it praises prose whose sentences move with deliberate musicality rather than flatly or jerkily.

At its core the word belongs to the family of cadence: a falling of the voice, a rhythmic flow, a closing pattern. To call speech cadenced is to say it has been shaped in time: stressed and unstressed syllables alternate, pauses breathe, clauses balance, and the whole period comes to rest satisfyingly. It is rhythmic but not mechanical, musical but still speech.

  • Primary sense — rhythmic, well-modulated speech or writing: prose, verse, oratory or conversation with perceptible rise-and-fall. E.g. cadenced prose, cadenced periods, a cadenced voice. The emphasis is aesthetic: clarity, balance, memorability. Semantic bridge: directly from musical cadence as a pattern of close; speech borrows the idea of timed fall and return.
  • Secondary sense — musical: furnished with or marked by cadences: a cadenced melody, cadenced harmony, a cadenced close. In older technical use, a passage that moves to a formal cadence or that contains many cadential formulae. Bridge: literal music theory transferred to general rhythm.
  • Tertiary / extended senses — measured movement and manner: cadenced steps, cadenced oars, cadenced breathing, cadenced work. Any bodily or mechanical action made regular and flowing. In literary criticism it can shade into “formal, stately, ritualised,” sometimes with a hint of artificiality if overdone. In modern usage it can describe even non-auditory rhythm, such as cadenced editing in film or cadenced posting online.
  • Connotations and register: almost always positive — graceful, controlled, soothing, authoritative. Unlike monotonous or jerky, cadenced implies variety within order. Unlike merely rhythmic, it implies a fall toward closure, a sense of arrival. In praise of style it suggests the writer hears language as music.
  • Domain notes: in rhetoric and stylistics, cadenced prose (Latin numerus) is studied as clausulae, cursus, parallelism; in poetry, cadenced free verse keeps ghostly metre without strict count; in acting and preaching, a cadenced delivery aids memory and emotion; in sport or military drill, cadenced movement aids coordination.

The force of lovely, cadenced phraseology is thus double: lovely gives affection, cadenced gives the reason — the wording delights the ear because its rhythms fall and lift like waves around Aran, cradling thought as the islands cradled the writer.

For learners, cadenced rewards attention to pause and stress: read a cadenced sentence aloud and mark where the voice naturally falls; imitate that fall in your own prose by balancing long and short clauses, and by ending periods on a stressed, resonant word rather than a trailing particle. Avoid confusing it with candenced misspellings or with cadenza (the solo flourish); cadenced is the settled, repeatable rhythm, not the showy exception. In examination or essay praise, use it precisely for rhythm, not for mere beauty of image.
