PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*h₁wenh₁-
"to lack, be deficient,
be empty, be wanting"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*wanaz
"lacking, deficient,
wanting, insufficient"
↓
Proto-Germanic (denominative verb)
*wanōną
"to diminish, grow less,
decrease, lessen"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
wanian
"to lessen, diminish,
decline, fade away"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
wanen
"to wane, decrease,
grow less, decay"
↓
Modern English (16th c.)
wane
"to decrease in size,
strength, or intensity,
esp. of the moon"
↓
Modern English (+ present-participle suffix -ing)
waning
"decreasing, declining,
growing less"

Note: the PIE root is sometimes reconstructed *h₁wenh₁- with cognates in Latin vānus "empty" and Sanskrit ūnā "deficient"; the exact laryngeal and ablaut shapes are debated, but the sense of deficiency and emptiness is consistent across all branches.
