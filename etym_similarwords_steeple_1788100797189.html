• Spire: Similar because both name the pointed top of a church; differs because a spire is strictly the tapering pyramid or cone, while a steeple is the whole tower-plus-spire ensemble — and a spire can crown a castle tower without a church beneath it.
  • Tower: Similar because both are tall vertical structures; differs because a tower need not be pointed or churchly (Eiffel Tower, watchtower), while a steeple is the church's pointed crown.
  • Pinnacle: Similar because both are tall pointed ornaments on buildings; differs because a pinnacle is a small decorative spike on parapets and buttresses, not a great tower, and "pinnacle" is also abstract ("the pinnacle of success").
  • Minaret: Similar because both are tall towers attached to places of worship; differs because a minaret belongs to a mosque, has balconies and no spire, and calls to prayer rather than ringing bells.
  • Campanile / bell tower: Similar because both house bells in a tall structure; differs because a campanile is usually a detached tower (as in Italy) without a pointed spire — the steeple's function split from its form.
  • Turret: Similar because both are towers, small vs. great; differs because turrets attach to castles and great houses, are defensive or decorative, and never carry a church spire.
  • Belfry: Similar because both are associated with church bells; differs because the belfry is merely the bell-chamber within or atop a tower — a part of the steeple, not the whole — and has an independent idiom ("bats in the belfry").
  • Lantern tower: Similar because both crown church roofs with vertical structures; differs because a lantern is a pierced, windowed turret for light, low and square, not a tapering spire.
  • Needle spire: Similar because both are pointed church summits; differs because a needle spire is specifically the slender, sharp, often timber-framed spire of the 14th–15th centuries — a subtype of spire, hence part of the steeple family.
  • Steep (adjective): Similar because the steeple is literally "the steep one" — both descend from the same root; differs because "steep" describes slopes and prices (precipitous), while the steeple is the noun for the tall tower built from that idea.
