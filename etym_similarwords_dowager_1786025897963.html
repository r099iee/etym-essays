• Widow: Similar because both denote a woman whose husband has died; differs because widow is the general, emotionally neutral term for any bereaved wife, while dowager specifically designates a widow who holds a dower, a title, or property from her late husband.
  • Matriarch: Similar because both suggest an older woman of power and authority within a family; differs because matriarch implies leadership by seniority and character and does not require widowhood or an inherited legal title.
  • Heiress: Similar because both involve a woman receiving wealth or property; differs because an heiress inherits by birthright or by will, while a dowager holds a dower settled on her at her marriage for her late husband's estate.
  • Dame: Similar because both are honorifics for women of status; differs because dame is a general title of respect (and, since 1917, a British knighthood rank), while dowager specifies a widow in possession of a dower.
  • Queen mother: Similar because both denote a widowed mother of a sovereign holding a rank derived from her late husband; differs because queen mother is the specific royal role (mother of the reigning monarch), while dowager is a general legal term applicable to any noble widow.
  • Grand dame: Similar because both suggest a dignified, elderly woman of high social standing; differs because grand dame is an admiring, metaphorical phrase for a distinguished older woman, while dowager is a precise legal-historical designation with property rights behind it.
  • Relict: Similar because both are formal or legal terms for a widow; differs because relict is archaic and purely legal (literally "the one left behind"), while dowager conveys rank, title, and dower rights.
  • Dowager duchess / dowager countess: Similar as the titular compounds in which the word itself appears; differs because the compounds specify the exact rank held, while the bare word dowager leaves the rank general.
  • Endowed widow (paraphrase): Similar because both describe a widow supported by her late husband's settlement; differs because the paraphrase is plain and modern, while dowager compresses law, rank, and social position into one word.
