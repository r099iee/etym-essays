• Tithe: Similar because it is the direct root and near-synonym for the tenth-part payment or the act of giving it; differs because tithe is the concrete tenth itself or the verb/action, whereas tithing is the institutional practice, the process, or the historic ten-household unit.
  • Tenth: Similar because tithing is literally a tenth part; differs because tenth is the pure ordinal fraction, carrying no fiscal, religious, or administrative apparatus at all.
  • Decimation: Similar because both derive from a word for "tenth" and involve the proportion one-in-ten; differs because decimation is the historical Roman punishment of executing every tenth soldier — a destructive act, not a tax or gift.
  • Levy: Similar because both are compulsory exactions; differs because a levy is any general tax or troop-raising imposed by a government, while tithing is specifically a tenth and historically bound to church and manorial law.
  • Assessment: Similar because both are calculated contributions to a public body; differs because assessment is a modern administrative valuation of property for taxation, carrying no fractional or religious meaning.
  • Dues: Similar because both are periodic payments owed to an institution; differs because dues are membership or trade fees of variable amount, while a tithe is the fixed tenth.
  • Contribution: Similar because both denote giving a share; differs because a contribution is voluntary and of any size, whereas tithing implies a proportion, traditionally one tenth.
  • Alms: Similar because both are charitable giving; differs because alms are spontaneous gifts to the poor with no fixed proportion, whereas tithing is an ordered, reckoned tenth.
  • Zakat: Similar because both are fixed proportional religious almsgiving (in Islam, roughly 2.5% of wealth); differs because zakat has its own theology and percentage and is etymologically unrelated.
  • Toll: Similar because both are exactions on produce, goods, or passage; differs because a toll is a fee for a specific service or road, with no tenth, no church, and no ten-man grouping.
