• Idler: Similar because both describe one who avoids work and wastes time; differs because an idler may be merely unoccupied or leisured, while a sluggard is condemned as constitutionally lazy.
  • Loafer: Similar because both name habitual dodgers of honest labour; differs because a loafer lounges and hangs about in public, while a sluggard is defined by slowness and sleep.
  • Layabout: Similar because both are blunt insults for the work-shy; differs because layabout is modern and colloquial slang, while sluggard is biblical and literary in flavour.
  • Laggard: Similar because both describe slow stragglers, and the two words are cousins in the -ard family; differs because a laggard falls behind a group in motion, while a sluggard may never have started.
  • Slacker: Similar because both shirk effort and let others carry the load; differs because slacker suggests deliberate evasion, even countercultural pride, while sluggard suggests drowsy inertia.
  • Shirker: Similar because both dodge assigned duties; differs because a shirker actively evades specific tasks, while a sluggard is slow about everything by nature.
  • Drone: Similar because both live off the labour of others like the stingless bee; differs because drone stresses parasitism on the hive, while sluggard stresses the sin of sleep itself.
  • Slugabed: Similar because both denounce late risers who cling to the mattress; differs because slugabed names only the bed-hugger, while sluggard condemns the whole idle character.
  • Dullard: Similar because both are -ard insults turning a flaw into an identity; differs because a dullard is slow of mind, while a sluggard is slow of body and will.
