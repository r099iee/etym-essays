Root 1 — GLOOM:

Origin uncertain (14th c.; possibly Scandinavian)
gloom
"darkness, dimness of light"
↓
Modern English (present)
gloom
"darkness or dim light"

Debate: first attested in the 14th c. with no certain earlier etymon; perhaps from a Scandinavian word, perhaps related to glum; no PIE root is securely reconstructed.

Root 2 — EQUIVOCAL:

Element 1 — aequus:
PIE (reconstructed; uncertain)
*h₂eykʷ-
"equal"
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE)
aequus
"equal, level, even"

Element 2 — vocare:
PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*wekʷ-
"to speak, call"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
vocāre
"to call, summon"

Compound — aequivocus:
Medieval Latin (4th–5th c. CE)
aequivocus
"of equal sound, ambiguous"
(aequus "equal" + vocāre "to call")
↓
English (16th c.)
equivocal
"uncertain or ambiguous,
open to two meanings"

Debate: the PIE etymon *h₂eykʷ- "equal" is uncertain; aequivocus was formed in Late/Medieval Latin on aequus "equal" + vocāre "to call".

Root 3 — BAFFLED:

Origin uncertain (16th c.; possibly echoic)
baffle
"to confuse, bewilder"
↓
Modern English (past participle)
baffled
"confused or frustrated"

Debate: baffle first appears in the 16th c. of uncertain origin — possibly echoic, or from the Scots verb baff "to abuse, hoodwink"; no reliable PIE etymon.

Root 4 — INCONGRUOUS:

Latin (1st c. BCE)
congruere
"to come together, agree"
(com- "together" + gruere "to fall, rush")
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
congruus
"agreeing, fit, suitable"
↓
Latin (Late Latin)
incongruus
"not agreeing, unsuitable"
(in- "not" + congruus)
↓
English (17th c.)
incongruous
"mismatched or out of place"

Debate: the base gruere "to fall, rush" is of uncertain origin, possibly from a lost PIE root *gʷer- "to fall"; the compound in- "not" + congruus is secure.
