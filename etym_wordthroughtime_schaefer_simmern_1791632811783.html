- 1896–1920s (Weimar Germany) — Born Heinrich Schäfer in Simmern; adopts the hyphenated name Schäfer-Simmern to reflect his regional heritage and establish his professional reputation as an innovative art educator in Frankfurt and Berlin.
- 1930s (Exile from Totalitarianism) — Emigrated to the United States as a refugee scholar fleeing Nazi cultural repression; Anglicized his spelling to Henry Schaefer-Simmern.
- 1940s–1950s (American Acclaim & Theoretical Breakthrough) — Published The Unfolding of Artistic Activity (1948) with John Dewey's foreword, gaining international renown for demonstrating that institutionalized mentally handicapped individuals possessed innate artistic capacities.
- 1960s–1970s (The Berkeley Studio) — Conducted master classes at his studio in Berkeley, California, revered as a pioneer of humanistic visual education and Gestalt-oriented art pedagogy.
- Contemporary Era — Recognized in cognitive science, art therapy, and aesthetic philosophy as a seminal figure who proved that visual-artistic conception is an innate, universal human capacity.
