• Ruled: Similar because both mean exercising sovereign authority; differs because rule is the broader political term for governing, whether by a monarch, an oligarchy, or a law, while reign is specifically the tenure of a monarch and carries the figurative sense of a prevailing condition.
  • Governed: Similar because both mean exercising authority; differs because govern implies day-to-day administration and is used of officials and institutions, whereas reign denotes the sovereign authority of a monarch, which may involve little actual administration.
  • Prevailed: Similar because both can describe a condition dominating a situation; differs because prevail implies winning out or being the strongest through persuasion or force, while reign (figuratively) simply holds unchallenged sway over the whole scene.
  • Predominated: Similar because both mean being most prominent; differs because predominate is descriptive and statistical (most numerous or influential), lacking the personified, sovereign image of reign.
  • Held sway: Similar because the phrase is a near-idiom for ruling or dominating; differs because it applies to abstract forces and persons alike with an older literary flavour, while reigned is the ordinary verb.
  • Occupied the throne: Similar because both describe a monarch’s rule; differs because it is a formal periphrastic idiom stressing the physical seat of power, whereas reigned is the single everyday verb.
  • Exercised control: Similar because both mean exercising power; differs because it is a colourless formal phrase with no royal or dramatic imagery, unlike reign.
  • Sat on the throne: Similar because both describe royal tenure; differs because it is an idiom focusing on the position itself rather than the act of prevailing, and is less natural in figurative uses.
