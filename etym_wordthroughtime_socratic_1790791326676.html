- 4th c. BCE - *Classical Greek Σωκρατικός (Sōkratikós)*: Applied to the direct disciples, dialogues, and philosophical traditions founded by Socrates in classical Athens.
- 1st c. BCE–1500s - *Roman & Renaissance Humanism*: Preserved in Latin (*Socraticus*) to denote philosophical humility, moral self-examination ('know thyself'), and the dialectical art of pursuing ethical virtue.
- 1600s–1700s - *Enlightenment Rhetoric*: 'Socratic irony' emerged as a celebrated literary and conversational device for subverting pedantic dogmatism through feigned simplicity.
- 1870s–1950s - *Legal Education & University Pedagogy*: Institutionalized as the 'Socratic method' in American law schools, transforming into a high-pressure pedagogical technique of cold-calling and intense cross-examination.
- Late 20th–21st c. - *Psychotherapy & Critical Pedagogy*: Adapted into Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and modern inquiry-based education as a gentle, non-judgmental technique for cognitive restructuring and unmasking cognitive distortions.
