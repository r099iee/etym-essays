• Contesting: Similar because both involve formally disputing or challenging a claim, rule, or outcome; differs because contesting can refer to elections, wills, ideas, or athletic competitions without necessarily initiating formal judicial proceedings, whereas litigating primarily centers on legal lawsuits or adopting a strictly adversarial legalistic posture.
  • Disputing: Similar because both involve verbal or conceptual disagreement; differs because disputing is broader, informal, and conversational, whereas litigating specifically invokes courtroom warfare or rigid, point-by-point evidentiary argumentation.
  • Suing: Similar because both denote legal action; differs because suing refers specifically to bringing a civil action against a defendant for damages or relief, whereas litigating describes the entire ongoing, complex procedural conduct of a lawsuit from discovery to appeal, as well as broader figurative rehashing of ideological rifts.
  • Prosecuting: Similar because both represent formal legal engagement in court; differs because prosecuting specifically denotes the state or Crown taking criminal action against an accused party, whereas litigating predominantly applies to civil disputes between private or corporate parties.
  • Arbitrating: Similar because both resolve legal conflicts; differs because arbitrating involves submitting a dispute to an impartial third-party arbitrator outside the public court system for a binding settlement, whereas litigating involves adversarial courtroom battle before a judge and jury.
  • Rehashing: Similar because both describe repeatedly arguing over past disputes (in figurative usage); differs because rehashing implies tired, repetitive rumination without legal structure, whereas litigating the past carries the connotation of aggressively cross-examining motives and assigning formal culpability.
  • Contending: Similar because both involve struggling against an opponent; differs because contending emphasizes the exertion of effort or asserting a proposition against resistance, whereas litigating implies formal rules of evidence, legal briefs, and structured adjudication.
  • Debating: Similar because both involve structured argumentation; differs because debating seeks to persuade an audience through rhetoric within an agreed forum, whereas litigating aims at securing a binding judgment or total vindication against an adversary.
