Note on Indo-European cognates: The Proto-Indo-European verbal root *gʰerdʰ- ("to enclose, encircle") produced one of the most distinguished lexical families across Eurasia. In Germanic, it produced two parallel ablaut series: the verb *gurdijaną (whence English gird, girth, and girdle) and the nominal formation *gardaz ("enclosed place, court, yard"), which yielded English yard, Old Norse garðr (source of English garth), and via Romance borrowing, garden. Beyond Germanic, it gave Slavic *gordъ (whence Russian gorod and grad, "city, fortified town", seen in Petrograd and Belgrade), Baltic Lithuanian gardas ("pen, fold"), and Indo-Iranian Sanskrit gṛhá- ("house, dwelling"). All these cognates share the primordial concept of establishing a protective perimeter around a human space.
