• Squealed: Similar because both are high-pitched cries of the same expressive family; differs because a squeal is louder, longer, and more piercing (and can mean to inform or betray), while a squeak is brief, thin, and small.
  • Shrieked: Similar because both are high, sharp vocal sounds; differs because a shriek is a loud, penetrating scream of fear or pain, while a squeak is a small, thin sound that lacks volume and alarm.
  • Screeched: Similar because both are harsh high sounds of friction or voice; differs because a screech is harsher, more strident, and usually of brakes, birds, or angry voices, while a squeak is thinner and smaller.
  • Creaked: Similar because both are sounds of objects under friction (hinges, doors, floors); differs because a creak is a lower, groaning, grating sound of wood or metal, while a squeak is thin and high-pitched.
  • Squawked: Similar because both are shrill cries; differs because a squawk is raucous and usually of birds (parrots, gulls) or an angry complaint, while a squeak is small and brief.
  • Chirped: Similar because both are short, high sounds, often of small animals; differs because chirping is bird-like and often cheerful, while squeaking is strained, nervous, or merely mechanical.
  • Peeped: Similar because both are tiny high sounds; differs because a peep is a very faint, small sound (of chicks) or a quick glance, while a squeak has more friction and sharpness.
  • Cheeped: Similar because both are small high cries of young birds; differs because cheeping is specifically the weak cry of chicks, while squeaking belongs to mice, hinges, and nervous voices.
  • Piped: Similar because both describe a high-pitched voice; differs because piping may be musical, clear, or fluting, while squeaking is thin, strained, and slightly comical.
  • Whined: Similar because both can be thin, high, plaintive sounds; differs because whining is a continuous, complaining, nasal tone, while a squeak is a single short, sharp note.
  • Gasped: Similar because both are involuntary sounds of the throat and breath; differs because a gasp is a sharp intake of breath (surprise, breathlessness), while a squeak is a voiced thin cry.
  • Cried: Similar because both are vocal utterances; differs because a cry is broader, louder, and stronger, while a squeak is the small, sharp, high note of a thin voice.
  • Yelped: Similar because both are short, sharp cries of pain or alarm; differs because a yelp is a quick bark-like cry (of a dog) with more force, while a squeak is thinner and higher.
  • Eeked: Similar because both are exclamations of a small, high, startled noise; differs because eek is an interjection of fright, while squeaked is a verb describing the actual thin high sound of the voice.
