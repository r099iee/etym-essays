TWO ROOT WORDS IN ISOLATION: CHAIN 1 — "bulbs": Ancient Greek βολβός (bolbós) "bulbous plant, bulb, onion, edible root; also the edible sea-onion / squill" (attested 5th c. BCE; no certain PIE ancestry — Beekes classes it as Pre-Greek / substrate) → Latin bulbus "bulb, onion, edible root" (1st c. BCE; borrowed from Greek) → French bulbe "bulb" (learned borrowing from Latin) → English bulb "an onion or other bulbous root" (16th c., via Latin bulbus and French bulbe) → FINAL: English bulbs (plural; the form used in the Narnia passage). DEBATED / NUANCED NOTES: (1) Greek βολβός has no secure Indo-European etymology; Robert Beekes classes it as Pre-Greek / substrate, for which no PIE ancestry can be demonstrated. Julius Pokorny tentatively linked it to PIE *bʰel- "to swell, to blow, to bloom" — because a bulb is "the swollen root" — but this is debated, and the honest route runs from Greek itself. (2) The English word is a learned borrowing, not an inheritance; its Romance sisters are French bulbe, Italian bulbo, Spanish bulbo, Portuguese bulbo, Romanian bulb, and German Bulbus is a Latinism (the everyday German word is Zwiebel, a different etymology). (3) Anti-merger: the bird bulbul is from Arabic بُلْبُل, unrelated to bulb. CHAIN 2 — "bolsters": PIE *bʰel- "to blow, to swell, to bloom, to thrive" (reconstructed; no date; the "swelling" family that also yields blow "to blast," belly, bellows, bloom, blossom) → Proto-Germanic *bulstrą "a soft stuffed cushion, a pillow" (c. 500 BCE–c. 500 CE; o-grade of *bʰel- with the noun-suffix *-strą — "that which is stuffed and made to swell") → Old English bolster (also bolstre) "a pillow, a cushion, a pad" (attested 9th c.) → Middle English bolster "a long pillow, a support" (12th–15th c.) → English bolster "a long firm pillow; anything that supports" (16th c. onwards; the verb "to support, prop up" is also 16th c.) → FINAL: English bolsters (plural; the form used in the Narnia passage). NUANCE: The kinship is sound: Germanic *bulstrą belongs to the well-attested *bʰel- "swell/blow" family (OE belg "bag, belly" → belly, bellows; OE blōwan "to bloom"; PGmc *blēsaną "to blow"). The reconstruction runs from *bʰel- via an o-grade *bʰol- > *bul- with a noun-suffix; no stage is invented, and the chain from *bʰel- to bolster is uncontroversial within Germanic scholarship.
