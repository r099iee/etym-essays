- 14th–16th c. 'A hollow case, frame, or enclosure' — borrowed from French with the neutral, technical sense of a box-like casing or frame surrounding an opening.
- 17th–19th c. 'A window that opens on hinges' — becomes a standard architectural term for the side-hinged window that swings open like a door, an everyday domestic word with a practical register.
- 20th–21st c. 'Archaic-poetic window' — pushed out of ordinary speech by the generic window, it now survives mainly in poetry and period writing, evoking old houses, romance, and a nostalgic, literary atmosphere.
