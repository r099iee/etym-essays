Derivation chain for PANTRIES (plural of pantry); the card's form is the plural, so the final node is bolded as pantries, with the singular pantry given as the base of the chain. [1] PIE root *peh2- "to feed, protect, guard" — sometimes proposed by handbooks as the deeper base of Latin panis "bread" (bread as "that which nourishes"). DEBATED AND CONJECTURAL: the link is plausible but not universally accepted; Latin panis is securely attested, its pre-Latin ancestry is not. This stage may be omitted in strict accounts and is labelled here as the only uncertain link in the chain. [2] Latin panis "bread" (attested 1st c. BCE; e.g. in Plautus, Terence, and the elder Cato's De Agricultura). [3] Old French paneterie "the office, department, or room of the panetier (the officer who kept and distributed bread)" (11th–13th c.); panetier is formed on panis with the occupational suffix -ier. [4] Anglo-French / Middle English panetrie, pantrie, pantre "pantry, the bread-room of a household" (14th c.). [5] Modern English pantry "a small room or closet where food, tableware, and kitchen supplies are kept" (16th–21st c.), the meaning broadened from bread to provisions. [6] Modern English plural pantries (regular -ies plural; the form on the card). Note on the chain: stages [2]–[6] are the secure, attested core; stage [1] is the only debated link and must not be treated as established. The office-to-room semantic shift happens inside stage [3], as the feudal institution (paneterie = department) transferred its name to the physical room when great households modernized.
