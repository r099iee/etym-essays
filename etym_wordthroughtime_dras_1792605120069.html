- [Late Rigvedic Era (c. 1200–1000 BCE)] - first recorded in the Puruṣasūkta (RV 10.90.12) as the fourth estate born from the feet of the cosmic primeval being (Puruṣa) - [perception: recognized cosmologically as the productive base and physical support of the social order]
- [Dharmaśāstra & Epic Era (c. 500 BCE–500 CE)] - codified into classical legal texts (Manusmṛti) as ekajāti (once-born) barred from Vedic recitation, while in historical reality they transitioned into peasant farmers, skilled artisans, and even imperial dynasty founders (Nandas) - [perception: viewed by conservative priests with ritual ambivalence, but acknowledged by pragmatic rulers as the economic lifeblood of the realm]
- [Medieval Bhakti Revolution (c. 7th–17th c.)] - radically re-evaluated through the rise of devotional Bhakti movements, where great Śūdra and artisan saints (Tukaram, Namdev, Ravidas) were celebrated as supreme spiritual masters whose pure love of God transcended ritual caste - [perception: revered spiritually across the subcontinent as authentic voices of divine grace]
- [Colonial British Indology (late 18th–19th c.)] - rigidified by British orientalist administrators through Anglo-Hindu case law and decennial census classifications; countered by pioneer social reformer Jyotirao Phule (author of Gulamgiri) who mobilized Śūdra and Ati-Śūdra consciousness against Brahmanical orthodoxy - [perception: became the focal point of intense political agitation and anti-caste emancipatory discourse]
- [Modern Indian Republic (20th–21st c.)] - re-analyzed historically by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (Who Were the Shudras?) and transformed into the constitutional powerhouse of the Other Backward Classes (OBCs), achieving dominant political, agrarian, and electoral leadership in modern democracy - [perception: transformed from a traditionally subordinated social class into the decisive democratic majority shaping the nation's political destiny]
