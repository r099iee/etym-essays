• Marcus Cocceius Nerva: Similar because it is the full Roman name of the same emperor; differs because it spells out the praenomen and gens (Marcus + Cocceius), while "Nerva" alone is the short form used in narrative history.
  • Trajan: Similar because Trajan is Nerva's adopted successor and the next name in Gibbon's list; differs because Trajan was a soldier-conqueror and reigned nineteen years, whereas Nerva was an elderly civilian who reigned barely two years and is famed for the adoption rather than for conquest.
  • Hadrian: Similar because Hadrian continues the same chain of adoptive emperors and the same "virtue and abilities" theme; differs because Hadrian consolidated and travelled the empire and built the wall, while Nerva's contribution was the institutional precedent of adoption.
  • Domitian: Similar because Domitian is the immediately preceding emperor and the historical foil of Nerva; differs because Domitian is remembered as a tyrant whose assassination in AD 96 cleared the way for Nerva, whereas Nerva is celebrated as the moderate restorer.
  • The Five Good Emperors: Similar because Nerva is the first of the five; differs because the group label is a collective historiographical tag, not a personal name, and does not single out Nerva's own brief rule.
  • Sinew / nerve (the common noun): Similar because the emperor's name derives from Latin nervus "sinew, vigour"; differs because the common noun is a concrete anatomical and figurative term, while "Nerva" is a frozen proper name of a second-century emperor.
