• Mārīca: Similar because both are sibling rākṣasa commanders, sons of Tāṭakā, who jointly led the assault against Sage Viśvāmitra's sacred hermitage at Siddhāśrama; differs because Subāhu is an uncompromising, aggressive warrior who is incinerated on the spot by Rāma's fiery Āgneyāstra, whereas Mārīca survives the battle, flees into ascetic solitude, and later reappears as the cunning shapeshifting golden deer in the Dandaka forest.
  • Tāṭakā: Similar because she is the monstrous biological mother of Subāhu, sharing his bloodthirsty cruelty, sorcery, and intense hatred of Vedic sacrifices; differs because Tāṭakā is a transformed Yakṣiṇī possessing the strength of a thousand elephants who terrorized the surrounding forest and was killed by Rāma prior to the defense of the sacrificial altar, whereas Subāhu was slain during the climactic sacrifice itself.
  • Khara: Similar because both are ferocious rākṣasa warlords in the Rāmāyaṇa who command large demonic garrisons dedicated to terrorizing sages and hermits in the forest; differs because Khara is a cousin of Rāvaṇa ruling the military outpost of Janasthāna who leads an army of fourteen thousand warriors against Rāma in the Araṇya Kāṇḍa, while Subāhu attacks Viśvāmitra's ashram in the early Bāla Kāṇḍa.
  • Dūṣaṇa: Similar because both are frontline demonic commanders who execute brutal military assaults against Rāma in defense of demonic dominance; differs because Dūṣaṇa serves as Khara's field general in the later forest war, whereas Subāhu is an independent marauder allied with Mārīca at Siddhāśrama.
  • Rāvaṇa: Similar because both represent the demonic forces of adharma seeking to overthrow the spiritual and social order established by the devas and rishis; differs because Rāvaṇa is the sovereign emperor of Laṅkā possessing immense cosmic boons, scholarship, and ten heads, while Subāhu is a local forest marauder serving as an early martial test for the youthful Rāma.
  • Kumbhakarṇa: Similar because both are massive rākṣasa champions celebrated for their immense physical strength and martial violence; differs because Kumbhakarṇa is Rāvaṇa's titanic brother cursed with months of slumber who possesses philosophical misgivings about Rāvaṇa's actions, while Subāhu displays straightforward, unreflective demonic aggression.
  • antagonist: Similar because Subāhu functions as a direct hostile adversary to the epic's protagonist, Prince Rāma; differs because antagonist is a generic literary and dramatic role, whereas Subāhu is a specific mythological actor carrying distinct familial and epic pedigree.
  • marauder: Similar because Subāhu's primary operational mode consists of raiding peaceful forest ashrams, defiling sacred altars, and terrorizing unarmed ascetics; differs because marauder is a modern descriptive noun for any roving bandit, lacking the supernatural demonic traits and mythic significance of an epic rākṣasa.
  • desecrator: Similar because Subāhu's defining criminal act in the Rāmāyaṇa is the deliberate defilement and desecration of Viśvāmitra's Vedic sacrifice using flesh and blood; differs because desecrator highlights only the sacrilegious behavior, whereas Subāhu embodies a martial warrior archetype.
  • adversary: Similar because both denote an opposing force in battle or conflict; differs because adversary is a neutral, formal descriptor of opposition, while Subāhu represents mythic, cosmic defiance of divine righteousness.
