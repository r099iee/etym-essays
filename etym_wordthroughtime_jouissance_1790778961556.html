- 12th–15th centuries: Old French legal term denoting the peaceful usufruct, lawful possession, and harvesting of revenues from property or land.
- 16th–18th centuries: Broadened into Classical French literature and Enlightenment libertine prose to denote sensual delight, amorous pleasure, and worldly happiness.
- 19th century: Colloquially specialized in spoken French as the standard vernacular noun for physical sexual climax (orgasm).
- Mid-20th century: Re-theorized by Jacques Lacan in psychoanalysis as an overwhelming, transgressive limit-pleasure that exceeds the pleasure principle and borders on traumatic pain.
- 1970s–present: Popularized in global literary criticism and feminist theory by Roland Barthes (*The Pleasure of the Text*) and Hélène Cixous as the radical, unnamable textual and bodily bliss that disrupts social and syntactic conventions.
