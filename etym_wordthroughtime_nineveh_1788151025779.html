- [8th–7th c. BCE] - The name Ninua is the Assyrian capital, seat of Sennacherib and Ashurbanipal, terror of the Near East - [perception: power, dread, splendor]
- [612 BCE onward] - The city is destroyed and abandoned; the name survives in chronicles, oracles, and the Bible - [perception: punishment fulfilled, ruins]
- [biblical transmission] - Jonah makes Nineveh the great city of repentance; Nahum and Zephaniah make it the type of a wicked city doomed by God - [perception: moral lesson, divine justice]
- [classical world] - Greeks and Romans remember it through Ctesias's fabulous history of its legendary founder Ninus; Nineveh is 'the city that Ninus built' - [perception: legendary, exotic, half-mythical]
- [Middle Ages] - The site lies unrecognized; pilgrims and scholars identify assorted mounds as the Nineveh of scripture - [perception: lost city of the Bible]
- [19th c.] - Layard's excavations (1845–51) reveal the real city: palaces, winged bulls, the library of Ashurbanipal; Nineveh fills museum galleries and bestsellers - [perception: archaeological wonder, recovered empire]
- [20th c.] - Kipling's 'Recessional' fixes the name as shorthand for any empire's inevitable erasure - [perception: cautionary metaphor of decline]
- [21st c.] - ISIL bulldozes the site's walls and gates (2015), making Nineveh again a headline symbol of heritage annihilated in war - [perception: fragile, endangered memory]
