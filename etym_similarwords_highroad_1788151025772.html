• Highway: Similar because both name the principal public road of a region; differs in register and currency — highway is the standard modern American and official term, while highroad is older and now chiefly British or literary.
  • Main road: Similar because it picks out the road of first importance; differs because it is purely descriptive and colourless, lacking highroad’s figurative charge — nobody says "the main road to success" with the same rhetorical force.
  • Thoroughfare: Similar because it denotes a public way through a place; differs because it stresses through-passage and street-like traffic within towns, whereas highroad implies a long-distance inter-city route.
  • Artery: A metaphorical synonym in phrases like "trade artery" or "arterial road"; similar because both image the route as a channel carrying the lifeblood of commerce; differs because artery is explicitly a metaphor drawn from anatomy and is used as a technical term in traffic planning.
  • Trunk road: Similar as the modern administrative term for a major long-distance route (especially in the UK); differs in being bureaucratic — it designates a road’s official classification, not its cultural or rhetorical weight.
  • Mainline: Similar as "the chief line of traffic or activity," often of railways; differs because it derives from rail terminology (the principal track of a railway) and is less often applied to roads or to moral exhortation.
  • Route: Similar in denoting a way between places; differs in being wholly neutral about size and importance — a footpath is a route; only a road of the first rank is a highroad.
  • Royal road: Similar as a rhetorical synonym for a sure, easy path ("the royal road to knowledge"); differs in connoting effortless advantage rather than the industrious traffic of a working highway.
