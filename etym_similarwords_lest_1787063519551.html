• For fear that: Similar because both introduce a negative condition or danger to be avoided; differs because for fear that is a multi-word colloquial or standard phrase highlighting the emotional fear of the agent, whereas lest is a concise, elevated conjunction requiring the subjunctive or modal 'should'.
  • In order that not: Similar because both express purpose aimed at preventing a specific consequence; differs because in order that not is clumsy and mechanical, whereas lest is elegant, literary, and historically rooted.
  • In case: Similar because both contemplate a possible future risk; differs because in case introduces a contingency to prepare for (e.g., 'take an umbrella in case it rains'), whereas lest introduces a specific adverse outcome that the main action is actively intended to avert.
  • Unless: Similar because both connect conditional consequences; differs because unless introduces an essential prerequisite or exception ('I won't go unless you come'), whereas lest introduces a negative purpose or danger to be averted.
  • Lest that (Archaic): Similar because it is the older redundant variant; differs because the compound form with 'that' is archaic and obsolete in contemporary standard English.
  • To avoid: Similar because both signify preventative intention; differs because to avoid is a prepositional/infinitive phrase taking a noun object or gerund ('to avoid falling'), whereas lest is a subordinating conjunction taking a full finite clause with a verb.
