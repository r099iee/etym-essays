• anuṣṭubh: Similar because both denote the 32-syllable four-quartered metrical scheme; differs because anuṣṭubh designates the broader historical and Vedic liturgical meter, while śloka refers specifically to the classical epic couplet governed by strict alternating cadential rules.
  • couplet: Similar because both refer to a pair of successive poetic lines forming a unit; differs because couplet is a generic Western formal designation (often rhymed, as in heroic couplets), whereas a śloka is quantitative, unrhymed, and precisely subdivided into four octosyllabic quarters.
  • distich: Similar because both denote a two-line stanzaic verse unit; differs because distich in classical Greco-Roman prosody typically alternates hexameter and pentameter lines, whereas a śloka maintains two lines of equal sixteen-syllable measure.
  • sūtra: Similar because both are fundamental mnemonic formats in classical Indian literature; differs because a sūtra is extremely terse, non-metrical prose aphorism stripped of all ornament, whereas a śloka is a complete, metrically flowing poetic verse.
  • mantra: Similar because both are sacred Sanskrit verbal utterances chanted in spiritual practice; differs because a mantra specifically carries esoteric ritual, sonic, and sacramental efficacy revealed in the Vedic canon, whereas a śloka can be entirely secular, narrative, didactic, or humanly authored.
  • strophe: Similar because both describe organized structural divisions of a poem; differs because a strophe usually implies choral movement or irregular free-form lyric division in Western drama, whereas a śloka is a strictly uniform 32-syllable rhythmic block.
  • subhāṣita: Similar because the vast majority of moral maxims are composed in śloka meter; differs because subhāṣita refers to the genre or semantic content ("eloquent saying, proverb"), whereas śloka is the metrical container.
