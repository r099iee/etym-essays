• Modest: Similar because both denote humility and lack of ostentation; differs because modest is the broad internal attitude of self-effacement about one's achievements (one can be modest without being quiet), while demure is a specific quality of outward bearing, softness, and reserve.
  • Coy: Similar because both suggest shyness and reserve; differs because coy foregrounds deliberate, often flirtatious performance — shyness as a strategy to attract — whereas demure may be entirely genuine; the boundary is exactly what novelists exploit.
  • Reserved: Similar because both denote emotional restraint; differs because reserved stresses reluctance to reveal one's feelings and opinions, without the decorous, gentle, behavioural and often feminine coding that demure carries.
  • Retiring: Similar because both suggest shrinking from attention; differs because retiring implies a temperamental, uncalculated withdrawal, while demure implies a poised, deliberate quietness.
  • Prim: Similar because both denote proper, correct modesty; differs because prim stresses neat, conventional, precise correctness with a hint of prudish disapproval, while demure is softer and gentler.
  • Shy: Similar because both suggest timidity of manner; differs because shy implies nervousness or fear of attention, whereas demure implies composed, controlled reserve that may be entirely self-possessed.
  • Proper: Similar because both suggest decorous behaviour; differs because proper is a general judgement of conformity to rules, while demure names the specific quiet, modest manner.
  • Sedate: Similar because both denote calm, composed gravity; differs because sedate implies placid, unhurried calm (possibly dull), while demure adds modesty and self-effacement.
  • Staid: Similar because both denote sober, settled gravity; differs because staid may imply dullness and stuffiness, while demure is gentle and carries the softness of shyness.
  • Meek: Similar because both suggest gentle submissiveness; differs because meek implies actual submissiveness or mildness of temper under pressure, while demure is a decorous manner without necessarily any yielding of will.
