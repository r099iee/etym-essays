Bushmen (singular Bushman) is the historical English collective name for the San peoples — the indigenous hunter-gatherer peoples of southern Africa (Botswana, Namibia, South Africa, Angola, Zimbabwe) — so called by Dutch and English colonists because they lived in the bush (wild uncultivated country). The term is now dated and often considered insensitive; San, or specific group names such as Ju/'hoansi, !Kung, Hai//om, is preferred unless the community itself uses Bushman.

In older literature, as in the source — with the Bushmen of Africa it is always either a feast or a famine — Bushmen means foraging bands who hunt game and gather veld foods, sharing meat immediately rather than storing it, hence alternating glut and hunger. Colonial writers contrasted them with Hottentots (herders) and Bantu farmers, ranking them as most primitive. That tripartite scheme is now rejected as evolutionist, but the ecological observation contains truth: without livestock or crops, San bands relied on immediate sharing, mobility and intimate knowledge of water and plants; large kills were consumed quickly in the heat without preservation.

  • 1. Ethnonym for San peoples: The Bushmen painted rock art, speak click languages, practise persistence hunting and trance healing. Modern anthropology stresses resilience, egalitarianism, detailed botany, not improvidence. Use San in new writing.
  • 2. Australian bushman (lower-case, distinct): In Australia bushman means a white frontiersman skilled in bush life (bushcraft), and bushmen can mean such men generally. Capitalised Bushmen in African context is different; confusion is possible so context matters.
  • 3. Generic wild-dweller (obsolete): Early Cape use could mean any outlaw, runaway slave or mixed-race bandit living in the bush (Boschjesmans), not only San. This wider sense is obsolete.
  • 4. Sporting / cultural reclamation: Some San organisations and South African museums retain Bushman by choice (e.g., Bushman Heritage Museum), insisting on pride and recognition. Respect self-identification: if a community says Bushmen, echo it; otherwise say San.

Connotations: To older readers Bushmen evoked small stature, clicks, poison arrows, rock paintings, desert survival — romanticised noble savage or pitiable primitive depending on author. To modern readers the word itself signals colonial gaze: it defines people by where settlers thought they lived (the wild) rather than who they are. It differs from San (neutral, though itself an outsider Khoikhoi word meaning foragers, also debated), from Khoikhoi (herders, distinct economy), from Aborigine (Australia-specific). In the plural, Bushmen follows English -man > -men, but refers to men, women and children collectively.

Usage guidance for the Anki card: Understand the source sentence as period economics using period terms (Savages, Hottentot, Bushmen) to illustrate feast-or-famine foraging. Do not repeat the framing as fact; modern economics of foraging stresses sharing as rational risk-pooling, storage in the form of social obligations and ecological knowledge, and colonial disruption of hunting grounds. When writing today, say San hunter-gatherers (historically called Bushmen).
