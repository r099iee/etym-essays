PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*mori-
"body of water, sea, lake"

*mari
"sea, lake, still water"

mere
"sea, ocean; lake, pool, pond"

NOTE: PIE *mori- is one of the best-attested water-roots of Indo-European: Latin mare “sea,” Old Irish muir and Welsh môr “sea,” Lithuanian marios “lagoon,” the Slavic family (Russian more “sea”), and all of Germanic (Gothic marei, Old Norse marr, Old High German meri, modern German Meer “sea,” Dutch meer “lake”). The adjective MERE “only, just” is a different word: Old French mer “pure, unmixed” < Latin merus “pure, undiluted” (further origin debated, sometimes linked with PIE *mer- “to rub, polish,” i.e. “unalloyed”). A third, obsolete MERE “boundary” (Old English (ge)mǣre) is yet another homograph. The three must not be conflated.
