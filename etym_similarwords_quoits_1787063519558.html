• Horseshoes: Similar because both are traditional outdoor pitching games where players toss U-shaped or ring-shaped metal objects at an iron stake in the ground; differs because 'horseshoes' uses open, U-shaped iron horseshoes, whereas 'quoits' uses closed, circular annular rings (of iron, rubber, or rope).
  • Bowls / Lawn Bowls: Similar because both are traditional English target games played on manicured lawn greens; differs because 'bowls' involves rolling weighted, biased spherical balls across turf toward a small jack, while 'quoits' involves aerial tossing/hurling of rings through the air onto a peg.
  • Ninepins / Skittles: Similar because both are historic pub and village aiming sports; differs because 'ninepins' involves rolling a heavy ball to knock down standing wooden pins, whereas 'quoits' involves pitching a ring onto a single upright pin.
  • Darts: Similar because both are target-aiming games popular in English pubs; differs because 'darts' involves throwing small, pointed missiles at a vertical wall board with numbered segments, while 'quoits' involves lobbing heavy rings at a ground stake.
  • Bocce / Pétanque: Similar because both are traditional throwing/rolling target sports; differs because 'bocce' and 'pétanque' involve hurling or rolling metal/wood balls as close as possible to a smaller target ball (cochonnet) without an upright peg.
  • Cornhole: Similar because both are recreational lawn tossing games; differs because 'cornhole' involves throwing fabric bean bags into a hole on an angled wooden platform, whereas 'quoits' involves ring-to-pin targeting.
  • Ring-toss: Similar because both describe pitching rings over pegs; differs because 'ring-toss' is a lightweight, informal carnival or children's game, whereas 'quoits' is a formalized historical sport with heavy forged iron or shipboard rope rings.
