Scandinavian (source form uncertain)
skvabba / skvabbel
"to prattle, babble; a dispute, murmur"
↓
English (c. 1600)
squabble
"to engage in a petty, noisy quarrel"
↓
English (c. 1600)
squabble
"a petty, noisy quarrel"
↓
English (modern)
squabbles
"petty, noisy quarrels"

Note on the debate: the origin is genuinely uncertain.
The word is usually classed as imitative (echoic of spluttering,
pointless arguing) and may have been reinforced or borrowed
from Scandinavian (Swedish skvabbel, Norwegian skvabba).
No Indo-European root can be traced with confidence.
