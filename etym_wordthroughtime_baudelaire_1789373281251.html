- 12th to 14th Century - Originates as an Old French metonymic byname derived from a short curved cutlass (*baudelaire*) or a Germanic personal name (*Baldhari*, "bold warrior").
- 1857 to 1860s - Explodes into scandalous Parisian notoriety as Charles Baudelaire publishes *Les Fleurs du mal*, suffering a criminal obscenity conviction for outraging public morality and being vilified as a diseased, decadent provocateur.
- 1870s to 1890s - Canonized by Rimbaud, Verlaine, and Mallarmé as the supreme visionary father of Symbolism and the discoverer of sensory correspondences and modern poetic synesthesia.
- Early to Mid-20th Century - Celebrated by T.S. Eliot, Walter Benjamin, and Marcel Proust as the ultimate prophet of urban modernity, the heroic poet of the metropolis, and the crucial discoverer of involuntary memory.
- Late 20th Century to Present - Universally revered across global comparative literature as an eponymous adjective ("Baudelairean"), designating dark lyrical elegance, metropolitan alienation, melancholic *spleen*, and the alchemical transfiguration of suffering into eternal art.
