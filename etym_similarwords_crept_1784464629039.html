• Crawled: Similar because both mean to move close to the ground at a slow pace; differs because crawling typically implies hands and knees or the belly as the mode of locomotion, while creeping is a slow, cautious advance in any low posture and stresses stealth rather than the specific posture.
  • Sneaked: Similar because both describe secretive, quiet movement; differs because sneaking emphasizes the deliberate intent to avoid notice, while creeping emphasizes the slow, low, gradual motion itself.
  • Edged: Similar because both describe cautious, gradual movement; differs because edging suggests small sideways or incremental shifts, while creeping is a continuous low traverse toward a goal.
  • Tiptoed: Similar because both describe quiet, careful movement; differs because tiptoeing is specifically walking silently on the toes while upright, while creeping is low, close to the ground, in any posture.
  • Slunk: Similar because both describe moving in a low, stealthy, skulking manner; differs because slinking adds a connotation of shame, guilt, or cringing, while creeping is merely slow, nervous, or hesitant.
  • Inched: Similar because both describe very slow, gradual forward progress; differs because inching emphasizes tiny movements measured in small amounts, while creeping adds the low, stealthy, crawling image.
  • Stole: Similar because both describe quiet, stealthy movement; differs because stealing (as in "stole away") emphasizes quiet departure and covertness, while creeping emphasizes the slow low posture of the advance.
  • Shuffled: Similar because both describe slow, dragging movement; differs because shuffling is a feet-dragging motion without lifting the feet, while creeping is low, careful, stealthy progression close to the ground.
