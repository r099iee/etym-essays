Gaulish *wassos / *wasso-

"servant, young man, one who stands beneath"
(Celtic; attested in personal names such as
Vassorix; no secure earlier stage recorded)

↓

Vulgar Latin vassus

"servant, slave, retainer"
(borrowed from Gaulish in Roman Gaul)

↓

Medieval Latin vassallus

"feudal tenant, military retainer, one who holds
land by homage" (Carolingian period, 8th–9th c.)

↓

Old French vassal

"feudal landholder bound to a lord by homage
and fealty" (11th–12th c.)

↓

Anglo-Norman / Middle English vassal

"subject, retainer, feudal tenant owing homage"
(attested c. 1300)

↓

English vassal, plural vassals

"feudal landholding retainers; subordinate states;
servile dependents"

↓

VASSALS

the plural form — "feudal retainers holding land
under obligation of allegiance; subordinate
dependents serving a master"

Note: the ultimate ancestry of Gaulish *wasso- is debated; some scholars connect it with PIE *upo- "under, below" via the idea of "one who stands under (a master)," but this is not securely established. The attested record begins at the Celtic stage.
