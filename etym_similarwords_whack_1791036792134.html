• thwack: Similar because it also denotes a sharp blow with a flat object; differs because thwack is slightly heavier, older and more literary.
  • smack: Similar because it means a sharp slap-like hit with sound; differs because smack suggests open-hand or lip sound, less heavy than whack.
  • slap: Similar because it is a blow with the open hand; differs because slap is specifically open-handed and insulting, whack can use any implement.
  • hit: Similar because it is the generic verb for striking; differs because hit is neutral and broad, whack adds noise, force and informality.
  • strike: Similar because it means to deliver a blow; differs because strike is formal and neutral, whack is colloquial and onomatopoeic.
  • thump: Similar because it is a heavy blow with dull sound; differs because thump emphasizes dull heaviness, whack emphasizes sharp crack.
  • bash: Similar because it means to hit violently; differs because bash suggests crushing damage, whack suggests a single sharp crack.
  • knock: Similar because it means to hit or rap; differs because knock is lighter and often for attention, whack is harder.
  • smash: Similar because it involves violent hitting; differs because smash implies shattering, whack does not imply breaking.
  • attempt: Similar because to have a whack means to try; differs because attempt is formal and general, whack is idiomatic and breezy.
  • try: Similar because it also means an effort; differs because try is neutral, whack adds sporting cheerfulness.
  • shot: Similar because to have a shot also means a try; differs because shot comes from shooting/throwing metaphor, whack from hitting metaphor.
