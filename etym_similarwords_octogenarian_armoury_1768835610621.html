• Arsenal (for armoury): Similar in being a place where weapons are kept; differs because arsenal (from Arabic dar as-sina'a, 'house of manufacture') stresses the production and supply of munitions, while armoury is where arms are fitted, stored and kept.
  • Magazine (for armoury): Similar in being a store of weapons; differs because magazine (from Arabic makhzan, 'storehouse') emphasises the stock itself, especially of powder and ammunition, whereas armoury emphasises the place and its equipment.
  • Depot (for armoury): Similar in being a storage place for military material; differs because depot (from French dépôt, 'a deposit') is a general storage-and-supply installation, while armoury is specifically the house of arms.
  • Weaponry / armament store: Similar in naming collected weapons; differs because weaponry names the weapons collectively as things, while armoury names the place that houses them.
  • Gun-room (for armoury): Similar in being a place where arms are kept; differs because gun-room is a domestic, often informal room for sporting firearms, while armoury implies a whole institutional arsenal.
  • Storehouse (for armoury): Similar in being a place of storage; differs because storehouse is a colourless general term for goods of any kind, while armoury reserves the store for weapons and equipment.
  • Armamentarium (for armoury): Similar in being a complete equipment of tools; differs because armamentarium is the learned medical/technical Latin term for a specialty's instruments, while armoury is the everyday-military place.
  • Centenarian (for octogenarian): Similar in being an age-classification; differs because the centenarian has crossed one hundred years, while the octogenarian sits in the statistical middle of the decade-series.
  • Nonagenarian (for octogenarian): Similar in being an exact age-classification; differs because nonagenarian fixes the nineties, one decade later, and is rarer and even more honorific in effect.
  • Septuagenarian (for octogenarian): Similar in belonging to the same Latinate decade-series; differs because it fixes the seventies, and lacks the ripe, monumental 'grand old survivor' ring of octogenarian.
  • Octogenary (for octogenarian): Similar in meaning 'of eighty years'; differs because octogenary is a rare poetic coinage, while octogenarian is the standard, formal, demographic word.
  • Eightysomething (for octogenarian): Similar in naming a person in the eighth decade; differs because eightysomething is breezy and colloquial with no demographic gravity, while octogenarian is exact, formal and actuarial.
  • Elder (for octogenarian): Similar in naming an aged person; differs because elder names age without precision and carries an honourific leadership sense, whereas octogenarian fixes the exact decade and is demographic, not social.
  • Veteran (for octogenarian): Similar in naming an older, experienced person; differs because veteran implies long service or experience and does not fix any decade, while octogenarian is pure arithmetic.
