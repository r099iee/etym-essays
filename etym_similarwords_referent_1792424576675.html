• denotatum: Similar because both refer to the real-world object or class of entities picked out by a linguistic sign; differs because denotatum (plural denotata) is a technical term originating in Charles Morris's formal semiotics emphasizing the full class of potential objects satisfying a sign, whereas referent is the universal standard term in general linguistics and philosophy of language.
  • designatum: Similar because both designate that which is indicated or symbolized by a word; differs because a designatum can be an abstract condition or fictional property even when no concrete physical entity exists (such as a mythological creature), whereas a referent typically implies an actual entity located in the relevant universe of discourse.
  • extension: Similar because both encompass the real-world entities denoted by language; differs because extension (contrasted with intension) is a formal set-theoretic term in mathematical logic denoting the entire set of all entities that satisfy a predicate, whereas referent can denote a single specific individual object identified in a concrete speech act.
  • signification: Similar because both relate to the meaningful content of a word; differs because signification refers to the internal sense, conceptual definition, or semantic import carried by the sign (the signifié), whereas the referent is the external entity in reality toward which that signification points.
  • connotation: Similar because both are dimensions of lexical meaning; differs because connotation refers to the emotional aura, cultural associations, and stylistic overtones accompanying a word, whereas the referent is the objective external thing denoted.
  • correlative: Similar because both describe paired relational elements in linguistic analysis; differs because a correlative is a reciprocal grammatical relationship between two coordinating words (such as neither...nor), whereas a referent is the asymmetrical external target of symbolic reference.
  • token: Similar because both deal with concrete manifestations in semiotic analysis; differs because a token is an individual physical inscription or spoken utterance of a sign (contrasted with the abstract type), whereas the referent is the external state of reality represented by that token.
  • relatum: Similar because both arise from Scholastic relational ontology; differs because a relatum is any entity that stands in relation to another entity within a formal proposition, whereas a referent specifically denotes the external object of symbolic reference.
