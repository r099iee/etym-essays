• Inalienable: Similar because both words express the identical conceptual state of being impossible to transfer, surrender, or forfeit; differs because inalienable is the standardized Latinate form in modern international treaties and European jurisprudence, whereas unalienable preserves a historic, solemn, and specifically American constitutional register tied directly to the Declaration of Independence.
  • Inviolable: Similar because both describe rights or covenants that must never be broken, attacked, or infringed; differs because inviolable emphasizes absolute immunity from assault, desecration, or violation by external forces, whereas unalienable focuses specifically on the impossibility of the right being transferred, bartered away, or surrendered even by the owner themselves.
  • Indefeasible: Similar because both denote entitlements that cannot be undone, voided, or forfeited; differs because indefeasible is a technical common-law property term describing an estate or legal title that cannot be defeated or revoked by future contingencies or challenges, carrying a more technical procedural connotation.
  • Sacrosanct: Similar because both describe something that is untouchable and immune from interference; differs because sacrosanct emphasizes religious, holy, or deeply revered status that inspires awe or protects against criticism, rather than the intrinsic legal and natural non-transferability of unalienable.
  • Non-negotiable: Similar because neither can be bargained away or altered through negotiation; differs because non-negotiable is a pragmatic, modern transactional term denoting an unbending stance or fixed term in negotiations, lacking the philosophical and sacred natural-law foundation of unalienable.
  • Inherent: Similar because unalienable rights are inherent to human nature; differs because inherent merely describes an essential, intrinsic characteristic naturally belonging to something, without explicitly stating that it cannot be transferred, legally severed, or extinguished by outside force.
  • Irrevocable: Similar because both cannot be called back or undone; differs because irrevocable emphasizes that a formal decree, trust, decision, or promise cannot be canceled or repealed once established, whereas unalienable refers to that which could never be legally surrendered in the first place.
