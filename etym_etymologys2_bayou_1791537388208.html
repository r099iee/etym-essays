Proto-Muskogean (c. 1000 BCE)
*p-ayok / *payo-
"flowing water, stream, river"
↓
Western Muskogean (Choctaw) (c. 17th c. CE)
bayuk
"river, creek, stream"
↓
Louisiana French (c. 1699–1758)
bayouc / bayou
"sluggish secondary river channel"
↓
American English (c. 1766)
bayou
"slow stagnant creek or delta inlet"

Note: Choctaw bayuk is an archaic or dialectal form cognate with modern standard Choctaw bok ("creek, river", seen in place names like Bogue Chitto). A persistent nineteenth-century folk etymology erroneously derived bayou from French boyau ("gut, hose, narrow channel"), but historical documentation by early explorers (e.g. Bienville 1699) and linguistic research by William A. Read (1931) proved the Choctaw origin conclusively.
