- 13th–14th c. (Middle English *lyngren / lengeren*) - Dawdling & prolonging time: Formed from *long* as a frequentative verb meaning to loiter, protract time, or delay departure out of sloth.
- 15th–16th c. (Late Middle to Early Modern English) - Protracted sickness & slow death: Widened to describe dying persons who hung on between life and death for long periods.
- 17th–18th c. - Atmospheric & sensory persistence: Extended across literature to describe fragrances, echoes, twilight rays, and emotional memories that slowly fade.
- 19th c. (Romantic Era) - Poetic nostalgia & melancholy: Championed by Romantic poets (Keats, Wordsworth, Shelley) as an aesthetic term for tender reluctance to depart from beauty.
- 20th–21st c. - Atmospheric enchantment & psychological resonance: Universally employed in fantasy literature (Tolkien) and general narrative for sacred auras, enduring aromas, and lingering doubts.
