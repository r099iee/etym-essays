- 12th–13th century (Old French barater) - Meant to deceive, cheat, practice chicanery, or haggle deceitfully in commercial dealings (the source of legal barratry).
- 14th–15th century (Middle English barteren / bartry) - Shifted from fraudulent deception toward the general act of bargaining, haggling, and trafficking in the marketplace.
- 16th century (Tudor Era) - Acquired the specific, neutral technical definition of exchanging commodities directly for other commodities without the use of money.
- 18th century (Classical Economics & Adam Smith) - Formalized in foundational economic theory (e.g., Adam Smith's The Wealth of Nations, 1776) as humanity's natural propensity to 'truck, barter, and exchange.'
- 19th–20th century (Anthropology & Macroeconomics) - Explored in economic anthropology (examining gift economies and market origins) and applied in macroeconomic finance to international corporate countertrade and crisis economies.
- 21st century (Digital Barter & Modern Commerce) - Expanded into sophisticated internet-based B2B barter networks, carbon credit trading, time-banking communities, and metaphorical exchange of ideas.
