• Magistrate: Similar because it denotes a public officer with legal authority; differs because it is generic and can cover judges and local officials without the specifically Roman or diplomatic history of consul.
  • Proconsul: Similar because it is a Roman governor wielding consular imperium outside Rome; differs because it is ex-consul extended command, typically provincial, not the annual city magistracy itself.
  • Praetor: Similar because it was the next great Roman magistracy with imperium and judicial functions; differs because praetors ranked below consuls and specialized more in justice and provincial command.
  • Senator: Similar because it belongs to Roman governance and deliberation; differs because a senator was a member of the council, not the executive magistrate who presided and commanded.
  • Ambassador: Similar because both represent a state abroad today; differs because an ambassador conducts high diplomacy to the host government while a consul handles citizen services, visas, and trade.
  • Diplomat: Similar because consuls belong to the broader foreign-service world; differs because diplomat is the superordinate term covering ambassadors, envoys, and consuls.
  • Consul-general: Similar because it is a grade of consul heading a large consular district; differs because it is higher in consular hierarchy than a plain consul or vice-consul.
  • Vice-consul: Similar because it performs consular functions; differs because it is subordinate, assisting or deputizing for the consul.
  • Honorary consul: Similar because it bears the title and performs limited consular help; differs because the holder is usually a local private person, part-time and unpaid, not a career officer.
  • Attaché: Similar because both serve in missions abroad; differs because an attaché is a specialist assigned to an embassy, not the head of a separate consulate.
  • Envoy: Similar because it denotes a representative sent on mission; differs because envoy implies a diplomatic message or negotiation rather than routine consular administration.
  • Governor: Similar because Roman consuls governed through command and modern consuls oversee a district; differs because governor implies territorial rule, not foreign citizen-services.
  • Prefect: Similar because it was another Roman and later French administrative officer; differs because prefect is appointive bureaucratic administration, without the elective collegial Roman resonance.
