- c. 1530 – "Snap" borrowed from Dutch/Low German: to bite or snatch suddenly — an animal word, coined from the sound.
- 17th c. – Extends to things breaking with a sharp crack; "snap up" (seize eagerly); the snapdragon plant is named for its jaw-like flower (16th c.).
- 18th–19th c. – Sounds and clicks: fingers, locks, fasteners; "snap judgement" (an instant decision).
- 1808/1890 – "Snapshot": a quick gunshot becomes a quick photograph; "to snap a picture" is born.
- 19th c. – "Snap at" = speak sharply; the card game Snap; nerves "snapping" under strain.
- 20th–21st c. – The mental "snap" (breakdown); the football snap; the snap election; Snapchat (2011) — the photographic snap, digitised.
