- 15th c. — Leon Battista Alberti serves as papal secretary, architect, and premier theorist of the early Italian Renaissance; dedicates Della pittura to Brunelleschi, Donatello, and Ghiberti, revolutionizing artistic practice across Italian courts.
- 16th c. — Celebrated by Giorgio Vasari in Le Vite as the supreme exemplar of the scholarly gentleman whose profound theoretical writings bestowed intellectual nobility upon painting and architecture.
- 17th–18th c. — Canonized across European royal academies of art and architecture (in France, Britain, and Italy) as the foundational authority on mathematical perspective, classical orders, and harmonious architectural proportion (*concinnitas*).
- 19th c. — Lionized by the Swiss historian Jacob Burckhardt in The Civilization of the Renaissance in Italy (1860) as the primordial historical realization of the *uomo universale* (universal Renaissance man).
- 20th–21st c. — Recognized in contemporary media theory, cinema studies, and computer graphics (Erwin Panofsky, Lev Manovich, Friedrich Kittler) as the pioneer of the 'Albertian window'—the foundational visual paradigm underlying photography, modern screens, and virtual reality.
