- Late 15th c. — Albrecht Dürer establishes his workshop in Nuremberg; the publication of the Apocalypse woodcut series (1498) achieves immediate, sensational pan-European acclaim, transforming printmaking into fine art.
- 16th c. — Celebrated across Europe by Erasmus of Rotterdam, Raphael, and Giovanni Bellini; serves as court painter to Holy Roman Emperors Maximilian I and Charles V; praised lavishly by Giorgio Vasari in Le Vite for his astonishing graphic inventiveness.
- 17th–18th c. — Revered by collectors, scholars, and print connoisseurs as the 'prince of German artists'; his interlocking monogram (AD) becomes one of the most widely recognized and forged trademarks in art history.
- 19th c. — Deified during the German Romantic revival (by Goethe, Tieck, Wackenroder, and the Nazarene painters) as the quintessential national hero and spiritual embodiment of pure German artistic integrity.
- 20th–21st c. — Cemented in global intellectual history through Erwin Panofsky's landmark 1943 monograph as a universal polymath; recognized worldwide for iconic cultural images (*Praying Hands*, *The Hare*, *Rhinoceros*, *Melencolia I*).
