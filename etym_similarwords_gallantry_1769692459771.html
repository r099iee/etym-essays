• Chivalry: Similar because both describe noble, courtly conduct; differs because chivalry is the medieval knightly code of honor, religion, and fealty, while gallantry is a refined, secular courtliness toward women or spirited bravery.
  • Courtesy: Similar because both denote polite behavior; differs because courtesy is general civility shown to everyone, while gallantry is specifically attentive, flattering deference toward women.
  • Politeness: Similar because both involve good manners; differs because politeness is the basic observance of social rules, while gallantry is an elevated, romantic, stylized performance of those rules.
  • Heroism: Similar because both denote bravery; differs because heroism stresses selfless courage in crisis, while gallantry adds a stylish, courtly flair and is often lighter in tone.
  • Bravery: Similar because both mean courage; differs because bravery is any courage in the face of fear, while gallantry in the military sense implies conspicuous, dashing, memorable courage.
  • Valor: Similar because both denote martial courage; differs because valor emphasizes the inner worth and fortitude of the soldier, while gallantry emphasizes the brilliant, visible conduct.
  • Flattery: Similar because both involve praise and compliments; differs because flattery is insincere praise for personal advantage, while gallantry is ritualized, socially sanctioned attentiveness that may be genuinely courteous.
  • Romance / Courtship: Similar because both involve wooing behavior; differs because courtship is the serious pursuit of a marriage partner, while gallantry is the polished performance of attentiveness that may accompany — but is not identical with — courtship.
