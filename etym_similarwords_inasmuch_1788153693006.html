• insofar as: similar as the modern proportional conjunction; differs by stressing degree/scope alone, losing the causal "since" force.
  • in that: similar for introducing a precise ground; differs as more compact and neutral, common in analytic prose.
  • since: similar for cause with evident reason; differs by informality and by lacking the measured-proportion nuance.
  • because: similar for direct cause; differs by plainness — no extent-limiting, no formality.
  • seeing that: similar for evident justification; differs as conversational and somewhat dated.
  • forasmuch as: similar as the archaic counterpart; differs by being virtually obsolete (legal/King-James register).
  • whereas: similar in formal legal contexts; differs by contrastive force ("while on the other hand").
  • given that: similar for a presupposed ground; differs by presupposition rather than proportionate cause.
  • to the extent that: similar for the limiting sense; differs as a paraphrase rather than a lexical unit.
