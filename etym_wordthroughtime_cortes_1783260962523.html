- [16th-century conqueror] - The name Cortes (Macaulay's anglicised form of Spanish Cortés) entered English with the fall of the Aztec Empire: Hernán Cortés, who destroyed Tenochtitlan in 1521 and made himself master of Mexico.
- [Emblem of strategy] - In 18th and 19th century historical prose the name became the epitome of strategic conquest - a conquistador who exploited alliances and wrote his own justification - set against Pizarro's raw violence.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'Cortes' denotes the historical conqueror, its place in the moral argument of empire fixed as the emblem of conquest won by audacity and the sword.
