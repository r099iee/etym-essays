• Affluent: Similar because an affluent is exactly a stream flowing into another; differs because affluent is formal and chiefly literary or French-flavoured, while tributary is the standard atlas and textbook word.
  • Feeder: Similar because feeder streams and feeder roads pour into greater ones; differs because feeder is functional and modern, feeder flights and feeder cables, while tributary carries two millennia of Roman dignity.
  • Branch: Similar because branches join trunks in tree and river alike; differs because branch suggests a fork splitting off and rejoining, or a division of a firm, while tributary flows one way only, small surrendering to great.
  • Confluent: Similar because confluents meet and merge in one stream; differs because confluent stresses the meeting of near-equals, while tributary marks the hierarchy, lesser honouring greater.
  • Sidestream: Similar because both are lesser waters beside the main flow; differs because sidestream can run parallel and aloof, even metaphorically marginal, while tributary joins and contributes.
  • Contributor: Similar because both pour something into a greater whole; differs because contributor is general and human, writers and donors, while tributary keeps its watery grace even in metaphor.
  • Subsidiary: Similar because subsidiaries serve greater enterprises as tributaries serve rivers; differs because subsidiary is corporate and contractual, while tributary is natural and honoured.
  • Vassal: Similar because vassal states mirror tributary states in owing duty upward; differs because vassal belongs to feudal personal homage, knight to lord, while tributary belongs to Rome's tax-lists and the world's watersheds.
