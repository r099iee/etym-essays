• Halcyon: Similar because both words derive from the identical ancient Greek root referring to the mythical kingfisher and its calm-bringing midwinter brooding period; differs because halcyon is the standard modern English spelling containing the folk-etymological initial 'h-', whereas alcyon is the unaspirated classical variant, literary archaism, and modern biological taxonomic stem.
  • Kingfisher: Similar because both refer to the iconic plunge-diving river bird; differs because kingfisher is the vernacular Germanic compound commonly used in everyday speech and ornithology, whereas alcyon is an elevated classical loanword rich with Ovidian mythology.
  • Alcedo: Similar because both are classical names applied to the kingfisher bird; differs because Alcedo is the formal Latin zoological genus of typical Old World river kingfishers (e.g., Alcedo atthis), while alcyon stems directly from Greek and was historically extended into marine invertebrate taxonomy (the soft coral genus Alcyonium).
  • Petrel: Similar because both are seabirds inextricably linked in historical maritime folklore with oceanic storms and waves; differs because petrels (family Procellariidae) are genuine pelagic seabirds that fly low over tempestuous open oceans, whereas alcyon is an idealized coastal kingfisher mythologized into an open-sea floating nester.
  • Shearwater: Similar because both are marine diving birds whose names trace back to ancient Mediterranean coastal encounters; differs because shearwaters nest in underground burrows on rocky islands and lack the auspicious, storm-quelling symbolism and marital devotion associated with the alcyon.
  • Sea-swallow: Similar because both are poetic vernacular names given to slender, fast-flying marine diving birds; differs because sea-swallow commonly refers to terns (family Sternidae), whereas alcyon is strictly tied to the kingfisher and the Alcyone myth.
