PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*(s)ker-
‘to cut, cut short’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*skurta-
‘short, truncated’
↓
Old Norse (c. 900–1200)
skyrta
‘shirt, skirt, kirtle’
‘a short garment’
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
skirt
‘lower part of a garment’
↓
Early Modern English (1580s, verb)
skirt
‘to border, run along’
‘the edge of’
↓
Modern English (past form)
skirted
‘bordered, passed around’

Note: the chain from PIE *(s)ker- through Germanic *skurta- is the standard handbook link joining skirt, shirt, and short as cut-short garments, but some scholars leave the Germanic root without a certain deeper PIE etymology and treat the cut connection as plausible rather than proven.
