• Smugly: Similar because both describe speaking or acting with irritating self-satisfaction; differs because smugly emphasizes an overt, conceited, and often annoying display of personal cleverness or superiority aimed at an observer, whereas complacently emphasizes a tranquil, lazy, and uncritical lack of concern about potential vulnerabilities.
  • Self-satisfiedly: Similar because both denote an attitude of contentment with one's own actions and situation; differs because self-satisfiedly is an explicit, literal compound describing the direct feeling of being pleased with oneself, whereas complacently adds the crucial connotation of dangerous apathy, negligence, and blindness to external threats.
  • Complaisantly: Similar because both derive from the same Latin ancestor (*complacēre*); differs fundamentally because complaisantly means in a polite, obliging, eager-to-please, and accommodating manner toward others, whereas complacently means in a self-absorbed, smug, and indolent manner pleased solely with oneself.
  • Nonchalantly: Similar because both involve an absence of worry or agitation; differs because nonchalantly suggests an effortless, stylish coolness, poise, or casual indifference without necessary arrogance, whereas complacently carries an underlying moral defect of unearned superiority and uncritical pride.
  • Serenely: Similar because both convey deep calm and an absence of disturbance; differs because serenely describes a noble, tranquil, and dignified peace of mind untainted by conceit, whereas complacently is peace of mind rooted in foolish ignorance and smug vanity.
  • Smirkingly: Similar because both describe expressions of self-congratulation; differs because smirkingly describes a specific facial sneer or half-smile expressing mocking amusement, whereas complacently describes an entire psychological posture and verbal delivery.
  • Insouciantly: Similar because both describe being unbothered by trouble; differs because insouciantly implies a lighthearted, carefree, and cheerful lack of concern (often charmingly bohemian), whereas complacently implies heavy, comfortable, and narrow-minded self-absorption.
