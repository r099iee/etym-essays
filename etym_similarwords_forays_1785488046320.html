• Excursion: Similar because both denote a short trip into other territory; differs because an excursion implies leisure and pleasant wandering with no aggressive purpose, while a foray carries purpose, boldness, and a hint of risk.
  • Expedition: Similar because both enter unfamiliar territory; differs because an expedition is a large, well-organised undertaking with full equipment and a clear goal, while a foray is brief, modest, and limited.
  • Raid: Similar because both derive from the plundering venture; differs because a raid retains the military violence of hostile attack, while a foray has shed that menace in modern intellectual use.
  • Incursion: Similar because both mean a hostile entry into foreign territory; differs because an incursion stresses the breach of a boundary and remains aggressive, whereas a foray is benign in modern use.
  • Digression: Similar because both depart from the main course; differs because a digression implies drifting off and getting lost from a line of thought, while a foray is a purposeful, self-contained expedition that returns home.
  • Sortie: Similar because both name a sudden military venture outward; differs because sortie is a technical military term for a breakout from a besieged position, with no civilian or academic use.
  • Venture: Similar because both denote a risky, uncertain undertaking; differs because a venture is forward-looking and commercial or entrepreneurial, while a foray emphasises brief entry into foreign territory.
  • Jaunt: Similar because both are short outings; differs because a jaunt is light-hearted, aimless, and purely recreational, while a foray is purposeful and faintly risky.
