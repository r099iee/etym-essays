• Wandering: Similar because both mean moving without a fixed route; differs because wandering is aimless movement without any sense of being lost or out of bounds, while straying means crossing beyond a proper limit.
  • Roaming: Similar because both mean moving widely; differs because roaming is deliberate, wide-ranging movement, while straying is an accidental or heedless drift off the path.
  • Roving: Similar because both mean moving widely; differs because roving is restless, purposeful travel, whereas straying is an unwilled departure from where one belongs.
  • Drifting: Similar because both mean passive, directionless motion; differs because drifting is often figurative and lacks straying's pastoral and legal sense of an animal leaving its rightful place.
  • Meandering: Similar because both mean wandering off a straight course; differs because meandering describes a winding, leisurely course (as of a river) with no sense of boundary-crossing.
  • Erring: Similar because both mean deviating from what is right; differs because erring is primarily moral or intellectual error, without straying's literal physical sense.
  • Errant: Similar because both mean wayward wandering; differs as a formal word (from Latin errare) for wandering or wayward behaviour, without straying's specific animal and legal flavour.
  • Deviating: Similar because both mean turning aside from a norm or path; differs because deviating is a neutral, technical word for divergence, while straying implies an animal, a herd, or a moral boundary.
  • Digressing: Similar because both mean wandering from a line; differs because digressing is specifically verbal or written departure from a topic in speech and prose.
