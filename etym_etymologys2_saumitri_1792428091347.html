Note on grammatical derivation and historical semantics: The name Saumitri is formed through one of the most celebrated derivational paradigms in Sanskrit vyākaraṇa. The base noun Sumitrā is a classic bahuvrīhi (possessive exocentric) compound: su- ("good, noble") + mitra ("friend, pact"), meaning "she who possesses noble friendship" or "she who is a benevolent ally". To generate the matronymic denoting her biological child, Pāṇinian grammar (Aṣṭādhyāyī 4.1.95: ata iñ) prescribes the addition of the primary taddhita affix iñ (surface form -i). By the rule of initial vowel strengthening (vṛddhi, Aṣṭādhyāyī 7.2.117: taddhiteṣv acām ādeḥ), the short vowel u of the initial syllable su- strengthens to the diphthong au, yielding Saumitri. Philologists emphasize that the root *mey- in mitra fundamentally denotes reciprocal covenantal binding; thus, Sumitrā and Saumitri embody the sacred, unbreakable bond of righteous alliance that safeguards the cosmic dharma of the Ikṣvāku dynasty.
