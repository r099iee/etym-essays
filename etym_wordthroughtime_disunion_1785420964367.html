- Late 15th century (first English use, from Latin disunio) - plain, general sense: the state of being separated or parted; a neutral word for loss of union.
- Late 18th century (American political rhetoric) - acquires heavy political charge as the feared breakup of the federal union; a menace spoken of in the abstract.
- Mid-19th century (Antebellum and Civil War) - loaded, near-treasonous word: 'disunion' names secession, civil rupture and national catastrophe.
- Early 20th century (psychology and criticism) - shifts inward: disunion of the self, the mind's loss of its own coherence; a diagnostic term.
- Present day - general, often formal word for discord or fragmentation in a group, institution or personality; the fierce political edge has faded.
