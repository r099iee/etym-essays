Non-PIE chain, attested directly in Latin. Stage 1: Latin laburnum 'a tree of the pea family with golden flowers (the golden-chain tree)' — attested in the 1st century CE (Pliny the Elder, Naturalis Historia, and later glossed by Servius in his commentary on Vergil's Georgics). The ultimate origin is unknown: the word is very likely a pre-Roman substrate word of the Alps (perhaps Gaulish or Ligurian) or a Latinized borrowing of a local plant-name, and no secure PIE root can be assigned — the pre-Latin stage is explicitly unknown. Stage 2: English laburnum 'the golden-chain tree', adopted in the 16th century directly from Latin as a learned botanical name (English herbalists of the period, e.g. Henry Lyte's translation of Dodoens, 1578, and Gerard's Herball, 1597). Stage 3: English plural laburnums 'several golden-chain trees' — the regular English plural of laburnum, in use from the 17th century onward. Final node: LABURNUMS. Debate note: because Latin laburnum itself is of obscure, probably pre-Latin substrate origin, any etymology that pushes the chain beyond Latin is speculative; standard authorities (OED, Etymonline) stop at 'unknown pre-Latin word'. The English-to-plural stage is a routine morphological addition and is not in dispute.
