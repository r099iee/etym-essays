- Roman dynasty in use - From the 2nd century AD the Antonine name belongs to a real imperial family (Antoninus Pius, Marcus Aurelius), a technical onomastic label for emperors bearing the cognomen Antoninus.
- Gibbon's golden-age coinage - With The Decline and Fall (1776) 'the age of the Antonines' is recast as the historiographical golden age when the human race was most happy and prosperous, turning a family name into a moral-political ideal.
- Modern scholarly label - Today 'Antonines' survives in historiography as a convenient dynasty-name for the adoptive emperors from Nerva to Marcus Aurelius, carrying a mixed air of period nostalgia and technical precision.
