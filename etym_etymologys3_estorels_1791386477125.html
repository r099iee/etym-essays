Estorels is a place name, and its history is therefore onomastic rather than lexical: it tells us less about a common meaning than about how a particular rocky landscape was named, mapped and imagined. The massif lies between Cannes and Fréjus, and its name has passed through Provençal, Latin charter spelling, French and English.

Earliest attestations. The documented record begins in the late thirteenth century. A charter of 1295–1300 mentions la fontayna d'Estelell, a spring on the old road between La Napoule and the interior; a Latin document of 1301 gives Stelellum. In 1390 we find the bois de l'Esterau or Estereu, and in 1401 the forms de Stelelo and de Sterello. These are not yet the name of the whole mountain: they designate a spring, a wood, a road station and a local hill. The modern cartographic name is a later generalisation.

From microtoponym to massif. Through the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries the name appears in learned and administrative Latin — Michel de l'Hospital writes of Estreli in 1570, and a 1676 document has ad Sterellis. French forms follow: d'Estrelles in 1765, Lestrelle in 1793. Only in the late eighteenth century do cartographers extend the name from a single eminence to the entire range; the SHFR toponymic study notes that the modern extension dates to about 1787. From that moment l'Estérel becomes a regional landmark rather than a village-scale place name.

Competing etymologies. The origin of the name is genuinely disputed, and a responsible account must say so:

  • Pre-Latin *ester: a substrate word for a steep rock, spur or gorge, compared with Basque ezter and with place names such as Estérençuby, Esteribar and the Estergebirge in Bavaria. This is attractive because it describes the terrain, but the comparanda are distant and the reconstruction is hypothetical.
  • The Ligurian Suelteri or Suetri: a Celto-Ligurian tribe said to have occupied the area before the Roman conquest. The phonetic distance is considerable, and the link is speculative.
  • Latin sterilis: “sterile”, referring to the thin, acid, drought-prone soils of the massif. This is a popular explanation and appears in tourist literature, but it requires an irregular phonetic development and is not securely attested.
  • Provençal estello: “star”, with the diminutive or expressive estelell; the idea is that the peaks point to the stars, or that the name encodes a celestial metaphor. This is poetically attractive but etymologically weak.
  • Old Provençal estelell: Mistral noted this form, sometimes glossed as “sun”, which would suit a sun-facing slope; the semantic history is unclear.
  • Latin strata: “paved road”, via via strata and diminutives such as Estrella; one detailed toponymic study argues that the original Estérel was a road, not a mountain. The early attestations near a road and a spring make this hypothesis worth serious consideration.
  • Spanish Sierra Estrella or Portuguese Serra da Estrela: a learned or folk association with “star mountain”, probably secondary rather than the source.

Sound changes and spelling. The Provençal form Esterèu shows the vocalisation of final -l to -u, a characteristic southern change; French Estérel preserves an -l that reflects an older stage or a learned spelling. The vacillation between Estel-, Sterel- and Ester- in the documents reflects the instability of unstressed syllables and the influence of Latinising scribes. English Estorels adds a further layer: the loss of the acute accent and the substitution of o for é, plus an English plural -s.

Folklore and literary colour. Local tradition personifies the massif as the fairy Estérelle, consulted by women hoping for fertility, and the red rocks and strange pinnacles readily invite legend. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the massif became a backdrop for Romantic travel writing, Impressionist and Post-Impressionist painting, and later for film and advertising. The sunset over the Estérels, seen from Cannes, is one of the most reproduced views of the Riviera.

Modern reference. Today the name denotes a protected forest, a tourist destination, a wine-growing area and a brand (Estérel Côte d'Azur). The massif's red rhyolite, its calanques and its Corniche d'Or are central to the identity of the eastern Var. The word has thus completed a long journey from a spring and a road in medieval charters to a synecdoche for the glamour of the Côte d'Azur.
