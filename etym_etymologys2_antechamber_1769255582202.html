Antechamber is a compound of two root words, ANTE- and CHAMBER. Each receives its own complete derivation chain.

Chain 1: ANTE- "before"

1. PIE *h₂énti "in front of, before" — the locative of the noun stem *h₂ent- "front, forehead" (reconstructed; attested in Hittite ḫanti "in front, separately" and Greek antí "against, instead of, opposite"; c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE). 2. Latin ante "before, in front of (place); before, prior to (time)" — preposition and adverb directly continued from PIE *h₂énti (attested in Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE, e.g., in Caesar and Cicero). 3. Latin prefix ante- "before" — used in countless Latin compounds (antecedere, antemeridian, antebellum); the fixed Latin prepositional prefix, unchanged in form and meaning (attested throughout Classical Latin). 4. English antechamber — borrowed as a ready compound in the 17th c. (first recorded 1607) from French antichambre / Italian anticamera; the English prefixal element is Latin ante- "before," highlighted in the modern word antechamber.

Chain 2: CHAMBER "room"

1. PIE *kam- "to bend, curve, arch, vault" (reconstructed; possibly of non-Indo-European or substrate origin; attested through Greek and Latin reflexes, c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE). 2. Greek kamára "anything with an arched cover, a vaulted chamber, a vault" (attested from the 5th c. BCE onward, e.g., in Xenophon and later; the noun from the verb-forming stem of "to arch, curve"). 3. Latin camera "a vault, an arched roof; later a roofed room or chamber" (borrowed from Greek kamára; attested 1st c. BCE onward; also the source of the medieval Latin sense "chamber, room"). 4. Old French chambre "a room, an apartment, a chamber" (from Latin camera; attested from the 10th–11th c. CE; in Old French the word already means an interior room, esp. a bedroom or council room). 5. Middle English chambre / chaumbre "a room, esp. a private room or bedroom" (borrowed from Old French; attested 13th–14th c. CE, e.g., in Chaucer). 6. Modern English chamber "a room, esp. a private or principal one" — the second element of antechamber (attested from the 16th c. onward in the compound; the compound antechamber = ante- "before" + chamber "room"). Debated / nuanced note: The PIE etymon *kam- "to arch, curve" is sometimes suspected of being a loan from a non-Indo-European (possibly Mediterranean substrate) source, since the Greek kamára has no fully certain Indo-European cognates outside its Latin reflex. The Greek k- before a would in strict inherited phonology be expected to show different outcomes; the debate is real but the chain Greek kamára → Latin camera → French chambre → English chamber is agreed at every attested step. Note also that Latin camera independently gives English camera (via camera obscura "dark chamber"), a separate later borrowing.
