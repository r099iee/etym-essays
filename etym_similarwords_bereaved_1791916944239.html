• bereft: Similar because it is the older participle of the same verb and can also mean "deprived of"; differs because bereft is now used mainly of abstract loss (bereft of hope, bereft of reason) and of a desolate emotional state, not of a death in the family.
  • grieving: Similar because it describes someone suffering after a death; differs because grieving names the active emotional process, which may be public or private, while bereaved names the fact of having lost someone and can be used before grief is fully felt.
  • mourning: Similar because it is associated with death and loss; differs because mourning refers to the outward observance, ritual or period (wearing black, sitting shiva), whereas bereaved describes the person's status, not their practices.
  • sorrowing: Similar because it denotes deep sadness after loss; differs because it is literary and general, applicable to any sorrow, and lacks the specific legal and social meaning of having lost a relative to death.
  • stricken: Similar because it suggests someone suddenly overwhelmed by misfortune; differs because stricken is usually compounded (grief-stricken, panic-stricken) and describes the blow's effect rather than the bereavement itself.
  • widowed: Similar because it means deprived of a spouse by death; differs because it is specific to marriage and is a legal status, while bereaved covers any close relative, including a child or parent.
  • orphaned: Similar because it means deprived of parents by death; differs because it is specific to children and to parental loss, and it can also be used figuratively of being abandoned by an institution.
  • deprived: Similar because it is the general sense of being stripped of something; differs because deprived is socio-economic and material (deprived neighbourhoods, sleep-deprived), with no necessary connection to death.
  • desolate: Similar because it can describe the loneliness of loss; differs because it is an emotional and poetic adjective meaning bleak and forsaken, not a statement that a death has occurred.
  • afflicted: Similar because it means struck by suffering; differs because it is broad and somewhat archaic, covering illness, disaster and persecution, and it does not by itself imply bereavement.
