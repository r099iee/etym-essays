Element 1: FRANK
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*frankô
"javelin, spear; freeman, Frank"
(tribal name of the Franks, whose status as conquerors meant freedom from Roman tribute)
↓
Late Latin / Medieval Latin (6th c.)
francus
"free, at liberty, exempt; noble"
↓
Old French (11th–12th c.)
franc
"free, noble, generous, pure, of genuine superior quality"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
frank
"free, noble, pure, superior"

Element 2: INCENSE
PIE (reconstructed)
*kand- / *(s)kand-
"to shine, glow, burn"
(root of Latin candere, candidus, candela)
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*en-kand-ē-
"to cause to glow, kindle"
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE)
incendere
"to set on fire, kindle, burn"
(prefix in- 'into, upon' + candere 'to shine, glow')
↓
Latin (classical / late)
incensum
"that which is burnt; incense, burnt offering"
(neuter past participle of incendere used as noun)
↓
Old French (12th c.)
encens
"aromatic resin burned in worship, incense"
↓
Compound Formation:
Old French (12th c.)
franc encens
"pure incense, high-grade noble incense"
↓
Anglo-Norman / Middle English (13th–14th c.)
frankencens / fraunkencens
"frankincense, pure Boswellia gum resin"
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
frankincense
"aromatic resin burned in religious ceremonies"
↓
Modern English (present)
frankincense
"aromatic Boswellia resin; sweet ritual incense"

Note on Nuance: The compound frankincense is a syntactic calque of Old French franc encens. The initial modifier frank reflects the semantic evolution of the Germanic tribal name *Frankô into an adjective meaning 'free, noble, pure', used specifically by medieval merchants to distinguish genuine, high-grade Arabian gum resin from common or adulterated native incenses.
