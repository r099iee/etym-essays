- [Early 16th c. borrowing] - From Latin alleviare (ad- "to" + levis "light"); meant to lighten a physical weight or burden, often literally lifting something heavy.
- [17th-18th c. extension] - Broadened from physical weight to figurative burdens: pain, grief, taxes, and hardship could now be "alleviated," though still implying only partial lessening.
- [19th-20th c.] - Settled into medical, legal, and social registers; "alleviate symptoms/poverty/suffering" became standard, connoting careful, temporary relief rather than cure.
- [Contemporary Sense] - A formal, compassionate verb: reducing severity without promising full removal, still carrying its root sense of lifting a weight.
