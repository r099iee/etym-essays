“Kindling” began as the act of lighting or setting alight — to kindle a hearth or a candle — the practical verb of starting fire.
“Kindling” slid from the act to the fuel itself: by the 18th–19th century it meant the dry sticks and tinder used to start a fire, and this noun sense became the everyday one.
“Kindling” ran the figurative sense alongside — to kindle hope, desire, or passion is to set it alight — and in the 20th century the word in daily speech is almost entirely the fire-starting wood.
“Kindling” today holds both meanings at once: the innocent bundle of sticks for the hearth and the dramatic, ongoing act of setting something ablaze — as when dragons are kindling a whole Hill to flames.
