• chafe: Similar because both describe the effect of rubbing on skin; differs because chafe names the friction itself — the warming, rubbing action — while galled describes the raw, tender sore that results.
  • abrade: Similar because both denote wearing the skin away by rubbing; differs because abrade is clinical and neutral, used of any surface worn by friction, while galled specifically names the inflamed, painful spot on a living body.
  • fret: Similar because both can mean wearing away gradually and also nagging worry; differs because fret is gentler and more about slow erosion or anxious brooding, while galled is sharper, hotter, and more humiliating.
  • rankle: Similar because both describe deep-seated, festering irritation; differs because rankle evokes a poisoned, suppurating wound and persistent resentment, while galled keeps the physical sore and the sting of humiliation together.
  • vex: Similar because both mean to trouble or distress; differs because vex is about disturbance and agitation — a lighter, more transient annoyance — while galled implies something that rubs against one until it wears the pride raw.
  • irritate: Similar because both mean to annoy or provoke; differs because irritate is far milder and clinical, lacking galled's physical soreness and its note of abraded, humiliated pride.
  • nettle: Similar because both mean to provoke or sting; differs because nettle is more momentary and personal (as by an insult), while galled suggests sustained friction that has worked the feeling in.
  • grate: Similar because both involve rubbing or scraping; differs because grate is used of harsh sound or irritation of nerves, while galled names the raw physical and emotional wound.
  • humiliate: Similar because both carry a sense of wounded pride; differs because humiliate is a deliberate act of another person, while galled may simply be the inward effect of an event on one's feelings.
  • harass: Similar because both can describe persistent pressure that wears a person down; differs because harass implies repeated deliberate attacks or demands, while galled is the effect of steady rubbing, physical or emotional, without the intent of an attacker.
