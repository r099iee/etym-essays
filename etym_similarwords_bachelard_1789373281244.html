• Bergson: Similar because both are towering 20th-century French philosophers who profoundly re-examined time, memory, consciousness, and aesthetic experience; differs because Henri Bergson championed continuous, seamless duration (durée) and biological intuition, whereas Bachelard argued for discontinuous, atomized instants of time and rigorous epistemological breaks with naive intuition.
  • Foucault: Similar because both investigated the historical conditions, discontinuities, and institutional frameworks governing human knowledge; differs because Michel Foucault analyzed social power relations, historical archives, and discursive regimes (épistémès) across sociopolitical institutions, whereas Bachelard focused on scientific problem-solving, laboratory rationalism, and the lyrical phenomenology of poetic imagination.
  • Canguilhem: Similar because both were foundational French historical epistemologists who rejected naive continuous positivism in favor of conceptual shifts and scientific obstacles; differs because Georges Canguilhem applied these principles specifically to the biomedical sciences, pathology, and vital norms, whereas Bachelard divided his work between mathematical-physical epistemology and the psychoanalysis of literary reverie.
  • Merleau-Ponty: Similar because both deployed phenomenology to analyze how human consciousness inhabits and experiences the world; differs because Maurice Merleau-Ponty anchored his phenomenology in bodily perception, motor intentionality, and corporeal intersubjectivity, whereas Bachelard explored the intimate, symbolic architecture of dwelling spaces and elemental poetic imagery.
  • Barthes: Similar because both were brilliant French semioticians and literary theorists who deciphered cultural myths and textual poetics; differs because Roland Barthes operated primarily within structuralist linguistics, cultural signs, and post-structuralist textuality, whereas Bachelard operated through an alchemical synthesis of scientific philosophy and Jungian-influenced material imagination.
  • Althusser: Similar because both emphasized the necessity of radical theoretical ruptures to overthrow ideological illusions; differs because Louis Althusser repurposed Bachelard's concept of the "epistemological break" into a dogmatic Marxist framework to divide early humanist Marx from late scientific Marx, whereas Bachelard's epistemology was anti-dogmatic, pluralist, and dialectical.
