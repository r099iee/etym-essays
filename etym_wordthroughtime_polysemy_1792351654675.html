- Late 19th Century (1897) — Coined in French as polysémie by Michel Bréal in Essai de sémantique, establishing that a single word naturally retains older senses alongside newly acquired meanings.
- Early 20th Century (1900–1930s) — Borrowed into English linguistic scholarship, where lexicographers and philologists initially treated it as a potential hazard for clear logical communication.
- Mid-20th Century Structuralism (1940s–1970s) — Systematically formalized in structural semantics by Stephen Ullmann and John Lyons, establishing the rigorous diagnostic boundary between polysemy and homonymy.
- Cognitive Linguistics Revolution (1980s–2000s) — Reinterpreted by George Lakoff and cognitive semanticists not as lexical clutter, but as primary empirical proof of human conceptual metaphor and radial category architecture.
- Modern Computational Era (2000s–Present) — Became the central technical challenge in machine translation and artificial intelligence, solved through dynamic contextual token embeddings in Transformer models.
