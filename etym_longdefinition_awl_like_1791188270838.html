Awl-like is a compound adjective meaning resembling an awl in shape, sharpness, or function — that is, like a small pointed hand tool with a spike-like blade and handle, used for piercing leather, wood, or paper. The hyphen marks it as a synthetic simile-adjective: X is awl-like if it shares the awl's salient properties (slender, tapering to a hard point, designed to puncture by pressure or twisting) without literally being an awl. In your source — using an awl-like stylus — it describes Barbier's blunt pointed punch that pricked raised dots into paper for night writing, functionally and formally akin to a cobbler's awl.

Beyond tools, the term ranges across natural history, medicine, and craft description. Botanists call certain leaves, thorns, or shells awl-like (subulate) when they taper from a broader base to a fine point; zoologists describe awl-like bills, beaks, teeth, or barnacle spicules; geologists speak of awl-like crystals or stalactites. Medical writers may describe an awl-like pain — sharp, stabbing, penetrating — or awl-like instruments (trocar, probe, awl-like drill for surgery). In each case the comparison imports hardness, narrowness, and piercing agency.

Senses and nuances:

  • Core tool simile: shaped like an awl — short handle + steel spike; e.g. an awl-like bodkin, an awl-like punch, awl-like stylus. Emphasis is morphological: pointed, rigid, hand-held.
  • Functional simile: working like an awl — piercing, perforating, pricking; e.g. awl-like incisors for boring bark, awl-like ovipositor. Emphasis is on puncturing action rather than exact silhouette.
  • Botanical / zoological technical: synonymous with subulate, awl-shaped — narrowly awl-form foliage (e.g. juniper, sedum), awl-like spines of sea urchins, awl-like claws. Here it is a standard descriptive term in keys and floras, neutral and precise.
  • Sensory-metaphorical: sharp and jabbing like an awl's prick — awl-like pain, awl-like cold, awl-like scrutiny (poetic). The bridge is tactile pain memory: everyone who has been pricked grasps instant penetration.
  • Archaeological / typological: lithic or bone artefacts classified as awl-like implements — bone points, flint borers — when use-wear suggests perforating hide or wood even if typology is uncertain.

Polysemy is compositional: awl supplies the concrete prototype (a humble but ancient tool), -like hedges identity into resemblance. The hyphen is stylistically significant: awl-like (hyphenated attributive) versus awl like (looser simile) versus awllike (rare closed form). Dictionaries list it under productive -like compounds rather than as a headword, but its meaning is fully lexicalised in craft and science prose. Register is plain-descriptive, slightly technical; it avoids committing to exact tool typology while vividly conveying pointedness. Antonyms include blunt, knob-like, paddle-like, spatulate; near-synonyms awl-shaped, subulate, bodkin-like, spike-like, needle-like (each with finer distinctions of slenderness and flexibility).
