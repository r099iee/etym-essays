Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*meu- / *mu-t-
"damp, wet, dirty; slimy liquid"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*mud- / *muddō
"wet marshland, mud, wet sediment"
↓
Middle Low German / Middle Dutch (c. 1250 CE)
moddelen / modelen
"to stir up mud, dabble in muddy water, wallow"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1580 CE)
muddle (verb)
"to make turbid with mud; to confuse or stupefy"
↓
Late Modern English (Participle / Adjective c. 1680 CE)
MUDDLED
"mentally confused, stupefied by drink, disorganized"
