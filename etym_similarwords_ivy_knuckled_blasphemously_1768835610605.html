• Vine: Similar because both are climbing plants that grow on other supports; differs because vine is a loose, general word for any climbing or trailing plant (often grape-bearing) and carries ideas of fruitfulness and trained cultivation, whereas ivy is a specific evergreen climber of walls and ruins.
  • Creeper: Similar because both spread as trailing growth over surfaces; differs because creeper is a neutral, almost mechanical horticultural term for anything that creeps along ground or wall, lacking ivy’s emblematic charge of tenacity and fidelity.
  • Climber: Similar because ivy is a climbing plant; differs because climber is a generic functional label (also used of mountaineers and ambitious people) that says nothing of clinging rootlets or evergreen leaves.
  • Moss: Similar because both are green growths that cover stone and ruins in poetic imagery; differs because moss is soft, low, humbly moist, and purely a plant of damp shadow, whereas ivy is a vigorous vertical vine that grips and climbs.
  • Kudzu: Similar because, like ivy, it is a clinging, smothering climber; differs because kudzu is a fast-growing Asian legume famous as a southern-US invader, with none of ivy’s classical or Gothic symbolic history.
  • Bindweed / morning glory: Similar because both twine upward over supports; differs because they climb by twining stems, not by adhesive rootlets, and are herbaceous and ephemeral rather than woody and evergreen.
  • Honeysuckle: Similar because it is a woody climber of walls and hedges; differs because it is fragrant, ornamental, and twining, carrying sweetness rather than melancholy.
  • Gnarled: Similar because both render rough, knobbed, bone-like surfaces (“knuckled masonry”); differs because gnarled stresses twisted, knotted wood or limb and great age, whereas knuckled stresses bony, joint-like protrusions.
  • Knotty: Similar because both name knobbed, lumpy texture; differs because knotty is commonest of wood and of problems, and lacks the skeletal, hand-like anatomy of knuckled.
  • Bony: Similar because both suggest bone-like jutting prominence; differs because bony is a general anatomical adjective, while knuckled specifically recalls the clenched joints of a fist.
  • Jointed: Similar because knuckles are joints; differs because jointed is neutral and mechanical (of bamboo, insects, limbs), without the image of a fist or of gnarled stone.
  • Rugged: Similar because both describe rough, irregular surfaces; differs because rugged implies strong, uneven natural terrain or sturdiness, whereas knuckled implies articulated, skeletal bumps.
  • Profanely: Similar because both mean irreverently toward the sacred; differs because profane stresses defiling the holy with common or coarse use, without the specific “speaking evil of” attack that blasphemously carries.
  • Sacrilegiously: Similar because both violate the sacred; differs because sacrilege is the violation or theft of holy objects or places, whereas blasphemy is evil or insulting speech about God or the sacred.
  • Implously: Similar because both deny due reverence; differs because impiety is want of piety or dutiful worship — a lack rather than an active insult — while blasphemy actively slanders.
  • Irreverently: Similar because both show lack of reverence; differs because irreverent can be merely cheeky or light-hearted toward the august, while blasphemous is stronger and specifically insults the sacred.
  • Defiantly: Similar because the tower points “blasphemously” — i.e. in open defiance — at heaven; differs because defiantly is purely about resistance to authority and carries no religious or slanderous content.
  • Swearingly / cursingly: Similar because both involve profane speech; differs because swearing and cursing are everyday, often mindless utterances, whereas blasphemy is a directed insult against the sacred itself.
