• Pillar: Similar because both are upright supports; differs because a pillar is architectural, load-bearing and often decorative, while a post is a plain timber or pole for support, marking or fencing.
  • Column: Similar because both are vertical supports; differs because a column is a classical architectural member (with base and capital), while a post is plain and utilitarian.
  • Pole: Similar because both are long round timbers set to stand; differs because a pole is usually round and purposive (flagpole, telegraph pole), while a post is often squared timber and carries the extra senses of "station" and "job".
  • Stake: Similar because both are uprights driven into the ground; differs because a stake is slender, sharpened and provisional (tent stake, execution stake), while a post is substantial and permanent.
  • Stanchion: Similar because both support or fence; differs because a stanchion is a metal upright, often part of a railing system, while a post is generic timber or metal.
  • Pylon: Similar because both are tall upright supports; differs because a pylon is monumental (electricity pylon, temple gateway), while a post is modest and everyday.
  • Upright: Similar because a post is the canonical upright; differs because "upright" is the adjective and generic noun for verticality, not the specific piece of timber.
  • Mail / Letters / Correspondence: Similar because the post is the postal system and mail is what it carries; differs because post (British) names the system and the institution, while mail emphasises the carried matter itself — though the two systems merged in practice.
  • Position / Appointment / Job: Similar because a post is a job or position; differs because "post" keeps the military image of one stationed in a place, while "job" is the modern neutral word of employment.
  • Station: Similar because a post is a station and stations are posts; differs because station is the place (railway, police, radio), while post is the duty or job tied to the place — "at one's post".
  • Message / Entry / Article: Similar because an online post is a message, entry or article; differs because these name the content of what is posted, while "post" names the act and place of putting it up.
  • Publish / Upload: Similar because to post online is to publish or upload; differs because publish is formal and broad and upload is the technical transfer, while "post" is the social verb of putting content before an audience.
