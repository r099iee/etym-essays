• Canter: Similar because both are a horse's fast forward gaits; differs because a canter is a smooth, controlled three-beat gait of moderate speed, while a gallop is the fastest four-beat gait with full airborne suspension.
  • Trot: Similar because both are quadruped gaits; differs because a trot is a steady two-beat diagonal gait without airborne suspension, while a gallop involves bounding leaps with all four feet off the ground.
  • Sprint: Similar because both denote running at maximum speed; differs because sprint applies broadly to short bursts of human or animal running without specifying gait, while gallop names a structured equine gait.
  • Stampede: Similar because both describe rapid forward movement of animals; differs because a stampede implies chaotic, panic-driven mass flight, while a gallop is controlled, rhythmic, and intentional.
  • Lope: Similar because both name a bounding, forward gait; differs because a lope is relaxed, easy, and long-stride, while a gallop is full-out, effortful speed.
  • Race: Similar because both convey moving very fast; differs because race implies competition against others or a clock, while gallop names the gait itself.
  • Career: Similar because both describe moving swiftly and at full tilt; differs because career (verb) means to rush headlong out of control, while gallop is a deliberate, rhythmic gait.
  • Charge: Similar because both mean to move forward rapidly; differs because charge implies attack or purpose (often military or aggressive), while gallop is the horse's gait.
  • Hurtle: Similar because both describe violent, rapid motion; differs because hurtle implies being flung with great force and little control, while gallop retains a rhythmic, structured motion.
  • Rush: Similar because both mean to move quickly; differs because rush is a general word for haste with no equestrian or gait meaning.
  • Run: Similar because both describe fast forward motion; differs because run is the generic word, while gallop specifies the horse's four-beat bounding gait.
  • Dash: Similar because both convey rapid, energetic movement; differs because dash suggests a short, sudden burst of speed, while a gallop is a sustained gait.
