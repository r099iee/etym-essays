• Taut: Similar because both describe tight stretching under tension; differs because taut is the crisp neutral state of ropes and sails, while strained adds effort, suffering, or damage — the tautness is costing something.
  • Tense: Similar because both name tightness in muscles, nerves, and atmospheres; differs because tense is the general condition (past tense, tense shoulders), while strained stresses the pull that caused it and hints at injury or rupture.
  • Stretched: Similar because both mean pulled out to length; differs because stretched can be easy and elastic (a cat stretching), while strained is stretched to the painful or dangerous edge.
  • Overexerted: Similar because both describe faculties pushed past comfort; differs because overexerted is clinical and total (the whole body), while strained pinpoints one part — a muscle, the ears, the voice.
  • Forced: Similar because both describe unnatural effort (forced smiles, strained smiles); differs because forced stresses compulsion from outside, while strained stresses tension felt from inside.
  • Stressed: Similar because both cover pressure on minds and materials; differs because stressed is the modern engineer's and psychologist's umbrella term, while strained is older, more physical, and keeps the rope-and-fibre image alive.
  • Sprained: Similar because both are soft-tissue injuries from sudden pulls, often confused; differs because a sprain is specifically a wrenched ligament at a joint, while a strain is an overstretched muscle or tendon — doctors keep them strictly apart.
  • Aching: Similar because strained muscles ache; differs because aching names only the pain sensation, while strained names the mechanism — the pull that produced it.
  • Frosty: Similar because both describe cold hostility between people (frosty relations, strained relations); differs because frosty is distance and chill, while strained is tension and effort — politeness stretched thin rather than frozen.
