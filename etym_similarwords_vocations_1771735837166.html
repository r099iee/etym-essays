• Calling: Similar because both mean work to which one feels summoned or inwardly suited, and both share a religious ancestry of divine summons; differs because calling is the plainer, more emotional word, while vocation is more formal and institutional, which is why one speaks of the new vocations of a profession rather than the new callings.
  • Profession: Similar because both name a career requiring training and commitment; differs because a profession is defined by formal credentials, learning, and standards (law, medicine), while a vocation adds the inner dimension of aptitude and summons — a profession can be merely a job, a vocation cannot.
  • Occupation: Similar because both name the work one does; differs because occupation is the neutral, all-purpose word for any paid or regular work, implying nothing about aptitude or dedication, while vocation insists on suitability and commitment.
  • Career: Similar because both concern one's working life; differs because career is the descriptive arc of a person's jobs and advancement, with no implication of inner summons, while a vocation is the work one is called to do.
  • Trade: Similar because both can name a line of work; differs because a trade is a skilled manual or craft occupation, usually learned by apprenticeship and defined by skill, while a vocation emphasizes the sense of calling rather than the kind of skill.
  • Employment: Similar because both can refer to work; differs because employment is a legal-economic arrangement — holding a job for wages — with no connotation of fitness or summons, while a vocation is chosen work one is suited to.
  • Pursuit: Similar because both name an activity one devotes oneself to; differs because a pursuit is any activity one engages in, professional or recreational, while a vocation is specifically the work to which one feels called.
  • Avocation: Similar because both come from the same Latin root vocare and both name activities one undertakes; differs because an avocation is a secondary occupation or hobby that "calls one away" from the main work, while the vocation is the primary calling itself.
