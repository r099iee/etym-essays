• Unchecked: Similar because both describe growth or forces escaping control; differs because unchecked merely notes the absence of restraint, while rampant adds vigorous aggressive spreading.
  • Unbridled: Similar because both picture forces running free beyond all curbs; differs because unbridled comes from horsemanship and suits passions and appetites, while rampant comes from weeds and beasts and suits invasions.
  • Rife: Similar because both mean abundantly prevalent, especially of evils; differs because rife stresses how common a thing is, while rampant stresses how aggressively it spreads.
  • Widespread: Similar because both describe thingscovering much ground; differs because widespread is neutral about energy and approval, while rampant always accuses and always moves.
  • Overgrown: Similar because rampant vegetation is the very type of the overgrown; differs because overgrown describes the neglected result, while rampant describes the violent process.
  • Luxuriant: Similar because both name vigorous abundant growth; differs because luxuriant admires the abundance, while rampant condemns it as invasion.
  • Runaway: Similar because both describe escape from control, as in runaway inflation; differs because runaway stresses flight from handlers, while rampant stresses trampling advance.
  • Raging: Similar because both intensify uncontrollable forces like epidemics and fires; differs because raging burns hot and loud, while rampant spreads wide and grasps.
  • Epidemic: Similar because both describe ills sweeping through populations; differs because epidemic is the technical term of public health, while rampant is the editorial metaphor laid over it.
