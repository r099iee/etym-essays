DERIVATION CHAIN 1 — MANIFEST
R → PIE *man- (variant *men-) 'hand, to grasp' — the ancestral root of Latin manus 'hand'.
E → Latin manus 'hand; force, power, possession' — the everyday Latin noun for the hand, source of 'manual', 'manipulate', 'manner', 'manufacture'.
D → Latin manifestus 'palpable, plain, clear, evident; literally seized by the hand, caught in the act' — compound of manus 'hand' + -festus 'struck, seized', the participial stem of fendere 'to strike, thrust, ward off' (seen in offendere, defendere), ultimately from PIE *gʷhen- 'to strike, slay'. The literal image: a thief caught red-handed, the evidence in his grasp.
C → Old French manifeste 'clear, evident, obvious' — borrowed from Latin, retaining the forensic weight.
B → Middle English manifest (c. 1400) 'clearly revealed, plain to see' — via French and directly from Latin.
A → Modern English MANIFEST 'clearly visible or obvious' — the adjective of the card, extended as verb 'to show forth' and noun 'a ship's cargo list'.
Debate note: The second element -festus is debated. Most authorities (de Vaan, Etymonline) connect it to the stem of fendere 'to strike' and hence to PIE *gʷhen- 'to strike'; others link it to PIE *gʷhedh- 'to pierce', or read the whole compound as an archaic legal formula. The core sense 'seized by the hand, caught in the act' is universally accepted.

DERIVATION CHAIN 2 — VATS
R → PIE *ped- / *pod- 'vessel, container' (Watkins) — a disputed root of obscure origin, with Greek pithos 'wine jar' offered as a cognate; some scholars instead posit PIE *pet- 'to spread, stretch', the image of a hollowed, spread-open vessel.
E → Proto-Germanic *fatą 'vessel, cask, container' — a wide family: Old Norse fat, Old High German faz, German Faß 'cask, barrel', Dutch vat, Gothic fat 'vessel'.
D → Old English fæt 'container, vessel, pot, cup' — the direct Germanic inheritance.
C → Middle English fat / vat — southern dialects voiced initial /f/ to /v/ (compare fox/vixen), giving the standard modern form 'vat'.
B → Early Modern English vat 'large tub or cask for liquids' — extended from any vessel to the industrial processing tank.
A → Modern English vat, plural VATS 'large containers for liquids' — the brewing, dyeing and tanning tanks of the card's context.
Debate note: The PIE stage is genuinely uncertain; no secure root survives, Greek pithos is suggestive but not definitively cognate, and *ped- / *pod- 'vessel' is an inference, not a documented form. The Germanic stage onward is solidly attested.
