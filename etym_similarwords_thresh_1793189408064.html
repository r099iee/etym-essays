• Thrash: Similar because it is the doublet twin of thresh, sharing its origin and its beating motion; differs because thrash took the violent senses (beatings, heavy defeats, wild movement), while thresh kept the agricultural core.
  • Winnow: Similar because it is the sister operation in grain processing, separating grain from chaff; differs because winnowing uses wind or air to blow away the light husks, while threshing uses beating to loosen the kernels first.
  • Flail: Similar because the flail is the traditional threshing tool and its swinging stroke is the verb's very image; differs because flail names the implement and any wild swinging of it, while thresh names the grain-separating purpose.
  • Beat: Similar because threshing is rhythmic striking; differs because beat is the broad general word for all striking, while thresh specifies the agricultural separation of grain.
  • Grind: Similar because it is the next step in the bread chain after threshing, processing grain toward flour; differs because grinding crushes kernels between stones, while threshing frees whole kernels from their husks.
  • Mill: Similar because milling continues the grain's journey from field to flour; differs because a mill grinds already-threshed grain, while thresh is the earlier freeing of grain from straw.
  • Husk: Similar because husking also strips a covering from an edible kernel, as with corn; differs because husking peels the outer sheath (often by hand, ear by ear), while threshing beats loose grain from dry ears in bulk.
  • Shell: Similar because shelling peas or corn also frees edible seeds from their casings; differs because shelling removes a pod or shell, while threshing separates tiny cereal grains from chaff and straw.
  • Pound: Similar because both involve heavy repeated striking; differs because pound is blunt force in general (pounding doors, pounding grain to paste), while thresh is striking with the precise aim of freeing unbroken kernels.
