• Cicero: Similar because Cicero is the Roman counterpart who perfected political oratory and was styled the Roman Demosthenes; differs because Cicero wrote and spoke in Latin during the late Roman Republic, while Demosthenes spoke in Greek in fourth-century Athens, and their rhetorical styles (Cicero's fuller, more periodic; Demosthenes' more intense and compressed) differ markedly.
  • Aeschines: Similar because Aeschines was Demosthenes' great rival in Athenian oratory and politics, and the two fought the famous case On the Crown; differs because Aeschines favoured compromise with Philip of Macedon, lost the case, and was exiled, whereas Demosthenes stands as the champion of resistance.
  • Pericles: Similar because both are the most famous statesmen-orators of Athens, masters of the political speech; differs because Pericles led Athens in its fifth-century Golden Age and shaped the democracy at its height, while Demosthenes fought a losing rearguard action for independence in the fourth century.
  • Isocrates: Similar because Isocrates was a leading fourth-century Athenian rhetorician and teacher of oratory whose written speeches circulated as models; differs because Isocrates wrote polished speeches for others and ran a school rather than speaking personally in the assembly and courts, and he was not an active politician.
  • Lysias: Similar because Lysias was an Attic orator famous for the clear, plain style of forensic speeches; differs because Lysias wrote speeches for litigants in the courts and avoided high politics, while Demosthenes delivered his own passionate, sublime political orations.
  • Philippics: Similar because the Philippics are Demosthenes' famous speeches against Philip II and the source of a literary term; differs because Philippics is the title of those orations (later borrowed by Cicero for his speeches against Antony), while Demosthenes is the orator who delivered them.
  • Orator / rhetorician: Similar because Demosthenes is the archetypal orator and rhetorician; differs because orator and rhetorician are the general names of the profession, while Demosthenes is the specific historical exemplar and standard of eloquence.
  • Statesman: Similar because Demosthenes was a statesman as well as an orator; differs because statesman stresses political leadership, policy, and administration, whereas Demosthenes is remembered above all for the overwhelming power of his spoken word.
