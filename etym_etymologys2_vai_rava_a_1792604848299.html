Note on historical debate: The etymology of Vaiśravaṇa exhibits a famous dual trajectory in Indo-Aryan linguistics. In orthodox Sanskrit grammar (codified by Pāṇini in the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* 4.1.112), the derivation is an indisputable patronymic: it is formed by applying *vṛddhi* vowel lengthening to the initial syllable of the father's name *Viśravas* (*vi-* → *vai-*) and suffixing *-aṇa* (with regular retroflexion of *-na* to *-ṇa* following the *r* in *śravas*), yielding "son or descendant of Viśravas." In Buddhist tradition, however, an influential folk-etymology arose connecting the name directly to the primary verbal root *śru* ("to hear"), interpreting *Vaiśravaṇa* as "he who is renowned for hearing" or "he who hears much." This Buddhist semantic reinterpretation directly generated the Chinese calque *Duōwéntiān* (多聞天, "Much-Hearing Heavenly King"), which was later borrowed into Japanese as *Tamonten* / *Bishamonten*.
