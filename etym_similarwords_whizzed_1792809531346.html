• Buzzed: Similar because both name a fast vibrating hiss of motion or sound; differs because buzzed stresses a lower, thicker insect-like drone (bees, saws, intercoms), while whizzed stresses a thinner, faster, higher whistle of something tearing past.
  • Hissed: Similar because both are sibilant rushing sounds made by escaping air or steam; differs because hissed is the sound alone, often hostile or sustained (snakes, cats, crowds), while whizzed fuses the sound with swift bodily motion through space.
  • Zoomed: Similar because both describe small fast things hurtling along, often with a rising engine note; differs because zoomed is the camera-age word for lenses and cars, while whizzed is older, thinner, and more whistling — arrows and rockets rather than motorways.
  • Whooshed: Similar because both imitate air displaced by sudden movement; differs because whooshed is broader, breathier, and lower (water, wind, gusts), while whizzed is narrower, keener, and faster — a whistle where whoosh is a sigh.
  • Sped: Similar because both mean travelled fast from place to place; differs because sped is neutral and dignified (speed limits, solemn departures), while whizzed is playful and noisy, carrying the sound of the going with it.
  • Rushed: Similar because both mean hurried along at pace; differs because rushed stresses urgency and pressure (deadlines, crowds), while whizzed stresses the singing speed itself — joy in velocity rather than pressure of time.
  • Zipped: Similar because both picture small things darting nippily past; differs because zipped suggests short, snappy, stop-start darts (flies, motorbikes), while whizzed suggests one sustained screaming passage.
  • Hurtled: Similar because both describe violent fast motion through the air; differs because hurtled is heavier, wilder, and often out of control (boulders, bodies), while whizzed is lighter and keener — the missile flies true and sings as it goes.
  • Streaked: Similar because both evoke a fast line drawn across sky or vision; differs because streaked stresses the visible trail left behind (light, rain, tears), while whizzed stresses the whistling passage itself, heard as much as seen.
