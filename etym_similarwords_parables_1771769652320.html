• Allegory: Similar because both are symbolic narratives that teach by likeness; differs because allegory is a sustained, systematic narrative in which characters and events stand for abstract ideas throughout a whole work (Pilgrim's Progress is an allegory) — grander and more extended — whereas a parable is a short, realistic story whose single point lies hidden in one likeness.
  • Fable: Similar because both are short stories told for a moral; differs because the fable usually features talking animals and ends in an explicit closing moral (Aesop's fables), while the parable keeps its settings realistic and human and often leaves the application to the hearer.
  • Analogy: Similar because both draw a likeness for the sake of understanding; differs because an analogy is a logical comparison of two things with no narrative at all, whereas a parable is a story — the analogy is the resemblance, the parable is the tale that embodies it.
  • Exemplum: Similar because both are illustrative teaching devices; differs because the exemplum is a single illustrative example, a staple of medieval sermons — a cited instance rather than a full story — while the parable is a complete narrative constructed on a likeness.
  • Metaphor: Similar because both work by carrying meaning from one thing to another; differs because a metaphor is a single compressed image (one word or phrase), whereas a parable is a whole story; the parable is, so to speak, an extended metaphor in narrative form.
  • Simile: Similar because both are figures of explicit comparison; differs because the simile is an explicit comparison using 'like' or 'as' and has no narrative, while the parable embeds its comparison in a story that must be told and applied.
  • Proverb: Similar because both carry moral teaching in memorable form; differs because the proverb is a short, pithy saying of general wisdom (no story), whereas the parable is a narrative; the proverb states the lesson, the parable enacts it.
  • Parabola (mathematical homonym): Similar because it shares the same Greek root meaning 'thrown beside'; differs because parabola is a mathematical term — the conic section whose curve is 'thrown beside' its diameter — a wholly technical, non-narrative sense, whereas parable is the instructive story.
  • Mashal (Hebrew term): Similar because both denote the wisdom comparison behind the biblical material; differs because mashal is the Hebrew original (proverb, saying, or parabolic story) rendered by the Greek parabolē, while parable is the Greek-derived English word that fixed the sense of 'teaching story.'
  • Anecdote: Similar because both are short narratives; differs because an anecdote is a brief, often humorous account of a real incident told for interest or illustration, without the systematic moral application, whereas a parable is an invented or stylized tale told expressly to teach a lesson.
