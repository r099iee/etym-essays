• Perhaps: Similar because it is the direct modern equivalent, hedging a claim as uncertain; differs because perhaps is the neutral everyday word, while perchance is archaic and literary, carrying a poetic or period flavour.
  • Maybe: Similar because both express possibility and soften assertion; differs because maybe is casual, conversational, and the most informal of the hedges, while perchance is formal and bookish.
  • Possibly: Similar because both mark an outcome as within the range of what may happen; differs because possibly is the plain logical term of modern prose, while perchance adds a wistful, old-tale colouring.
  • Peradventure: Similar because it is the closest twin, also from French stock and also meaning by chance or perhaps; differs because peradventure is even rarer and weightier, the sermon-word to perchance's ballad-word.
  • Mayhap: Similar because both are archaic hedges meaning maybe, beloved of historical fiction; differs because mayhap is rustic and dialectal in flavour, while perchance is courtly and literary.
  • Peradventure (as noun sense): Similar because older writers nominalize both as a perchance or a peradventure, a mere possibility; differs because the noun use of perchance is vanishingly rare beside the adverb, while peradventure nominalized more freely.
  • Conceivably: Similar because both admit a hypothesis without endorsing it; differs because conceivably appeals to what the mind can frame, while perchance appeals to what fortune may deliver.
  • God willing: Similar because folktale speakers hedge pursuits and journeys with providence as well as chance; differs because the religious hedge invokes heaven's will, while perchance invokes blind fortune.
