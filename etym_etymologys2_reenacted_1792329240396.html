Note on morphological evolution: The word reenacted is a multi-layered compound formed from the Latin verbal root agere combined with two Romance-derived prefixes and a native Germanic past-participle dental suffix (-ed, from Old English -od / -ed). While enact initially had an exclusively statutory meaning in 15th-century English parliamentary records ("to decree on the statute roll"), the parallel theatrical usage of act ("to perform a theatrical role on stage") steadily cross-pollinated enact and reenact during the 18th and 19th centuries, expanding reenacted from a purely legal term of statutory re-authorization into the primary English designation for historical and dramatic restaging.
