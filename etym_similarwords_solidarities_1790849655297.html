• Alliances: Similar because both involve groups joining together for mutual benefit; differs because alliances typically refers to formal, pragmatic, or treaty-bound political and military pacts between separate entities, whereas solidarities implies deep emotional, moral, ideological, and shared-fate cohesion.
  • Coalitions: Similar because both denote temporary or ongoing cooperative unions of distinct factions; differs because coalitions emphasizes strategic, often transactional cooperation for a specific political goal, while solidarities encompasses foundational social bonds and ethical commitments.
  • Unities: Similar because both describe states of oneness and agreement; differs because unities is an abstract, harmonious state of being one or having singleness of purpose, while solidarities highlights mutual interdependence, shared struggle, and active social responsibility.
  • Fraternities: Similar because both emphasize brotherhood, kinship, and mutual support; differs because fraternities traditionally carries masculine, club-like, or kinship-based connotations, whereas solidarities is a broader, socio-political, and structural category of collective action.
  • Fellowships: Similar because both denote friendly association and shared community; differs because fellowships highlights warm companionship, shared religious or academic pursuits, and social harmony, lacking the explicit political resistance and collective struggle of solidarities.
  • Affiliations: Similar because both describe connections between individuals and organizations; differs because affiliations refers to formal memberships, institutional ties, or official connections, whereas solidarities represents the lived, active commitment to mutual support and shared values.
  • Cohesions: Similar because both refer to forces holding a group together; differs because cohesions is a descriptive, often mechanical or physical measurement of how tightly parts stick together, whereas solidarities represents conscious, ethical, and political bonds of mutual aid.
