• Zion: Similar because it is the same name for the same sacred hill and carries every identical reference; differs because Zion is the modern standard transliteration of Hebrew Ṣiyyōn (preferred by Protestant Bibles after Geneva and by modern usage), while Sion is the older Latin/Vulgate-derived spelling used in liturgy, the KJV tradition, and Milton.
  • Jerusalem: Similar because Zion by synecdoche comes to mean the entire holy city, and the names are used interchangeably in prophecy and psalmody; differs because Jerusalem is the full city with its walls, gates, and whole population, while Sion names specifically the sacred hill and citadel at its heart.
  • Moriah: Similar because Mount Moriah is the specific hill on which the Temple was built, overlapping the Temple-Mount part of Zion's range; differs because Moriah is the site of Abraham's near-sacrifice of Isaac and of Solomon's Temple, while Sion originally named the southeastern ridge (City of David) and broadened beyond any single summit.
  • Temple Mount: Similar because the Temple Mount is the hill of the sanctuary, the center of the cultic sense of Zion; differs because the Temple Mount is a precise modern topographical and religious designation for the Haram area, while Sion is a name whose reference slid from fortress to city to people to heaven.
  • Salem: Similar because Salem is an ancient short name for Jerusalem ("king of Salem," Psalm 76:2 "in Salem is his tabernacle"); differs because Salem is a separate archaic form of the city name, while Sion names the sacred hill and its spiritual extensions.
  • Ariel: Similar because Ariel ("lion of God" / "hearth of God") is a biblical poetic epithet for Jerusalem in Isaiah 29; differs because Ariel is a metaphor of strength and the altar-hearth, while Sion is the concrete place-name that became a symbol.
  • Bethel: Similar because Bethel ("house of God") is another holy hill where God's presence dwells, a type of sanctuary; differs because Bethel is a shrine of the northern kingdom, never the seat of the Davidic dynasty or the eschatological city.
