• Side by side: Similar because both mean adjacent alignment; differs because side by side is the general phrase for mere adjacency and need not insist the row faces the same way or stays level.
  • Alongside: Similar because both mean parallel or adjacent; differs because alongside is looser about alignment and also means working together or a ship at a quay.
  • Shoulder to shoulder: Similar because both denote close, level alignment; differs because shoulder to shoulder is emphatic and usually figurative, implying unity or solidarity in struggle.
  • Neck and neck: Similar because both mean level with another; differs because neck and neck applies to racers locked at equal speed, stressing competition rather than formation.
  • Tandem: Similar because both describe arrangement on a path; differs because tandem is the precise opposite — one behind the other, not side by side.
  • In a row: Similar because both mean sequential alignment; differs because in a row is merely linear order without abreast's insistence that the fronts be level.
