• Semantic shift: Similar because it names the same diachronic drift of word meaning; differs because shift is sometimes reserved for a single word's movement, while change covers the whole phenomenon and its typology.
  • Semantic drift: Similar because both describe meanings wandering over time; differs because drift suggests slow aimless wandering, while change is the neutral cover-term for drift in every direction, fast or slow.
  • Phonetic change: Similar because it is the sister process introduced alongside semantic change in the source passage; differs because it alters the form and sound of words, while semantic change alters their meaning.
  • Grammaticalisation: Similar because it is another classic type of language change over time; differs because it turns content words into grammar (going to becoming future gonna), while semantic change merely moves meanings without changing word-class function.
  • Amelioration: Similar because it is one of the canonical patterns studied under semantic change; differs because it names only upward movement in value (nice improving), while semantic change is the whole field including falls and sideways moves.
  • Pejoration: Similar because it is the mirror-image canonical pattern within the same field; differs because it names only downward movement (vulgar sinking), while semantic change embraces every direction.
  • Metaphor: Similar because live metaphor is the great engine that produces semantic change; differs because metaphor is the momentary figure of speech, while semantic change is what remains after the metaphor dies and hardens into a dictionary sense.
  • Etymology: Similar because both study word histories and explain current forms through past ones; differs because etymology traces origins and derivation chains, while semantic change studies the meaning-leg of that journey — what happened to senses along the way.
  • Neologism: Similar because both expand what the language can say; differs because a neologism coins or borrows a new form, while semantic change recycles an old form by pouring new meaning into it.
