• quart: Similar because both are units of liquid capacity in the same system; differs because a quart is exactly one-fourth of a gallon — the relationship is exact and fixed, the gallon the larger standard.
  • pint: Similar because both are liquid measures; differs because a pint is one-eighth of a gallon (and half a quart), the smaller everyday unit for beer, milk, and cream.
  • liter / litre: Similar because both measure liquid volume; differs because the liter is the metric unit (1 liter ≈ 0.264 US gallon), based on the metre and water, while the gallon is the customary imperial-era unit defined in inches or pounds of water.
  • gill: Similar because both are traditional liquid measures; differs because a gill is a tiny unit — one-fourth of a pint, one thirty-second of a gallon — the smallest of the family.
  • barrel: Similar because both are bulk liquid measures; differs because a barrel is much larger (31.5 US gallons for the petroleum barrel) and usually names a physical cask rather than a pure unit.
  • hogshead: Similar because both are large measures for liquids; differs because a hogshead is a cask of 63–140 gallons depending on the commodity — a shipping container, not an everyday retail unit.
  • bushel: Similar because both are statutory units of capacity; differs because a bushel measures dry goods (grain, fruit) rather than liquids, and is far larger than a gallon.
  • measure: Similar because both are units of quantity; differs because measure is the general, abstract term for any unit or the act of measuring, while gallon is a specific, concrete unit.
  • fluid ounce: Similar because both are liquid-capacity units; differs because the fluid ounce is tiny (128 per US gallon) and used for medicines and small servings, at the opposite end of the scale.
  • capacity: Similar because both concern how much a container holds; differs because capacity is the abstract dimension (how much a thing can hold), while gallon is a named unit of that dimension.
