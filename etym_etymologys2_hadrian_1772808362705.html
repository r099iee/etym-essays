Linear derivation chain for HADRIAN (single root word; TOPONYMIC — non-PIE place-name, no conceptual PIE root for the personal name): 1. Hadria (also Adria) — a town on the Adriatic coast of Italy (classical sources connect both a Picene town, near modern Atri, and a Veneto town, Adria, with the sea's name). The place-name belongs to a Venetic or northern-Italic substrate layer; its ultimate meaning is opaque and cannot be securely reconstructed as a PIE lexical root. Attested in antiquity. 2. Latin Hadria / Adria — the town-name in Latin; from it the Adriatic Sea takes its name (Latin mare Hadriaticum / Adriaticum, attested; the hydronym is a SISTER geographic derivation of the same toponym, not an ancestor of the emperor's name). 3. Latin Hadrianus / Adrianus — a relational / ethnic adjective meaning "of Hadria, from Hadria" (the -anus suffix forms place-of-origin adjectives). Used as a cognomen. 4. Latin cognomen Hadrianus — borne by Publius Aelius Hadrianus, Roman emperor AD 117-138; the cognomen passes into later imperial and Christian naming. 5. English HADRIAN — the classicizing historiographical form preserving the initial h-, used by Gibbon and standard in English history. Notes and debate: - The personal name is entirely toponymic: person < adjective < town. It does not derive from any PIE verb or abstract concept; no such root should be invented. - The vernacular name "Adrian" (English, German) and Romance forms (Italian Adriano, French Hadrien/Adrien, Spanish Adrian) are adaptations of the same Latin relational adjective, usually in Christian given-name tradition; they are not separate roots. - The town-name Hadria itself has proposed associations with "water" or "darkness" in some sources, but these are not securely established; the substrate origin is the honest statement.
