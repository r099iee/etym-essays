- [17th century: the shout of approval] - 'Acclamation' enters English naming the loud, enthusiastic shout of a crowd in approval or welcome; connotation is ceremonial and political - the voice of the assembly, the senate, or the triumph.
- [18th-19th century: the formal institutional sense] - The word hardens into procedure: a motion passed 'by acclamation' is approved by unanimous voice without a vote; connotation is institutional and unanimous - consent carried by sound alone.
- [Contemporary sense] - Loud and often spontaneous public approval or welcome, typically voiced collectively; connotation is ceremonial but can turn ironic - as 'vain acclamation' emptied of meaning when the honour is poured out upon those who neither hear it nor want it.
