• Plotter: Similar because both mean one who schemes secretly; differs because a plotter may act alone and need not be sworn to others, while a conspirator acts jointly in a shared secret plan.
  • Schemer: Similar because both describe one who devises plans; differs because a schemer emphasizes self-interested cunning and may work alone, without conspiracy's joint secrecy.
  • Accomplice: Similar because both are partners in wrongdoing; differs because an accomplice (from Old French accomplir) is a legal-criminal partner in an already framed crime, not necessarily a fellow of the plan from its start.
  • Coconspirator / co-conspirator: Similar because it is the doubled form of the same word; differs because it explicitly emphasizes the jointness (con- twice) and is common in legal and journalistic English, while conspirator alone already implies the shared plan.
  • Collaborator: Similar because both mean one who works with others; differs because a collaborator (from Latin collaborare) need not be secret or illicit, while a conspirator breathes a hidden design.
  • Ally: Similar because both are partners in a cause; differs because an ally (from Latin alligare "to bind to") is open and lawful, while a conspirator is bound by secrecy and often illegality.
  • Insider: Similar because both suggest membership in a select group with special knowledge; differs because an insider need not plot anything, while a conspirator is defined by the secret plan.
  • Cabal member / cabalist: Similar because both name members of a secret group; differs because a cabal (from Hebrew qabbālāh "tradition") suggests a small faction plotting within a larger body, while conspirator is the more general and legal word.
  • Ringleader: Similar because both name central figures in a plot; differs because a ringleader is the one who leads or organizes the group, while conspirator can be any member of the shared plan.
  • Instigator: Similar because both name someone who sets a plot in motion; differs because an instigator (from Latin instigare "to goad") may merely start others and not share the full plan, while a conspirator shares it.
  • Traitor: Similar because both are political terms of dangerous plotting; differs because a traitor betrays a prior loyalty to the state or a person, while a conspirator may plot without having been bound to anything.
  • Confederate: Similar because both mean a partner in a secret or wrongful enterprise; differs because confederate (from Latin confoederare "to league together") is slightly archaic or legal and stresses the league, while conspirator stresses the shared breath of the plot.
