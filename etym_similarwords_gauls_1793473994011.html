• Celts: Similar because Gauls were an integral branch of the wider Celtic cultural and linguistic family; differs because Celts is a broader umbrella term encompassing all Celtic-speaking peoples across Europe (including the Irish, Scots, Welsh, Cornish, Bretons, and Celtiberians), whereas Gauls specifies only the Celtic inhabitants of ancient Gaul.
  • Britons: Similar because both were ancient Celtic peoples who shared close religious, linguistic, and cultural ties; differs because Britons specifically inhabited the island of Great Britain south of the Firth of Forth, whereas Gauls inhabited the continental mainland.
  • Franks: Similar because both inhabited the territory of modern France and shaped its national destiny; differs because the Franks were a confederation of Germanic tribes who invaded and conquered Roman Gaul in the fifth century CE, providing the name France, whereas the Gauls were the earlier indigenous Celtic population.
  • Gallo-Romans: Similar because both designate the inhabitants of historical Gaul; differs because Gallo-Romans refers specifically to the culturally assimilated, Latin-speaking descendants of the Gauls living under the Roman Empire, whereas Gauls emphasizes their pre-conquest, independent Celtic tribal identity.
  • Iberians: Similar because both were ancient pre-Roman peoples of Western Europe; differs because the Iberians were a non-Indo-European indigenous populace inhabiting the eastern and southern Iberian Peninsula, distinct in language and origins from the Celtic Gauls.
  • Belgae: Similar because Caesar classified the Belgae as one of the three great nations of Gaul; differs because the Belgae were a specific northern warlike tribal confederacy occupying the region between the Seine and the Rhine, often suspected of mixed Celtic-Germanic ancestry, whereas Gauls is the broader collective designation.
