- c. 1300 — the garment: skirt names the lower hanging part of a coat or dress, borrowed from Old Norse, cousin to shirt.
- 15th–16th c. — the margin: skirts spreads to edges of anything, then to outskirts, the ragged fringes of towns and woods.
- Late 16th c. — the verb is born: to skirt is to border or run along an edge, first of coasts, forests, and garments trimmed with fur.
- 17th–18th c. — the traveller's verb: armies, coaches, and walkers skirt marshes, towns, and enemies, turning the border into a route.
- 19th c. — evasion enters: speakers begin to skirt questions, laws, and difficulties, and the graceful edge-word gains a sly reputation.
- Today — edges everywhere: lakes skirted by pines in guidebooks, figures skirting scandals in headlines, statistics skirting thresholds in reports.
