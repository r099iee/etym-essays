- Ancient Greek (Homeric) "diskos" - A specific athletic implement (discus) and a general flat, round object (plate, quoit). - Connotation of athletic prowess, competition, and basic utility.
- Latin "discus" - Retained Greek meanings, serving as a bridge for later European languages. - Continued association with sport and flat objects.
- Old English "disc" (ancestor of "dish") - Broadened to include tables, then narrowed to a vessel for food. - Shift towards domestic utility and culinary context.
- Middle English "dissh" / Modern "dish" - Primarily a food container, then the food itself, later a satellite antenna. - Strong domestic and culinary associations, later technological.
- 17th Century English "disc" (re-borrowing) - A general flat, circular object, often in technical contexts. - Connotation of scientific precision, anatomical structures, astronomical bodies, and later, information technology.
- Modern English "discus" (direct re-borrowing) - Specifically the athletic implement. - Direct link to ancient Olympic tradition, sporting competition.
