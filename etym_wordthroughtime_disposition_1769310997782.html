- Arrangement and order (14th c.) - Imported from Old French as a word for the placing of things apart: the ordering of troops, the arrangement of words, parts, or goods.
- Disposal and control (15th-16th c.) - The legal and administrative sense: the disposition of an estate or a matter, 'at one's disposition' - the word governs things, not persons.
- Inclination (16th-17th c.) - Widens to a leaning toward acting a certain way, 'a disposition to procrastinate' - a tendency noted without moral charge.
- Settled character (17th c. onward) - The ordering of a person's inner qualities becomes the ruling sense: a cheerful or sour disposition is the standing arrangement of temperament, the climate rather than the weather of the mind.
- Modern usage - Formal, literary, slightly heavy; the temperament sense dominates while the literal 'arrangement' senses linger in legal and military phrase.
