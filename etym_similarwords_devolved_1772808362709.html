• Delegated: Similar because both mean handing authority to another body or person; differs because delegation is a clean, revocable act of authorization that keeps the delegator supreme, whereas devolution is a looser, more gravitational, and often permanent transfer of powers to a lower level.
  • Transferred: Similar because both mean moving something from one holder to another; differs because "transferred" is neutral and general (ownership, objects, people), while "devolved" specifically implies downward movement, legal succession, or institutional transfer of power.
  • Relinquished: Similar because both describe the Senate's giving up of power; differs because "relinquished" stresses renunciation or letting go, whereas "devolved" stresses the downward movement and the recipient's receipt of the powers.
  • Ceded: Similar because both mean formally giving up authority or territory; differs because "ceded" implies a concession under pressure or by treaty, while "devolved" implies an orderly, customary, or constitutional passing-down.
  • Conferred: Similar because both describe giving power or honor to another; differs because "conferred" implies a dignified bestowal from above by a superior who retains authority, while "devolved" implies descent to a lower level and often a loss of control by the giver.
  • Passed down: Similar because both mean transmitted to another or to successors; differs because "passed down" is informal and general (traditions, heirlooms, powers), while "devolved" is the formal legal-constitutional word for the transfer of authority.
