- [16th c. entry] - Adapted from Latin morosus ("peevish, fretful"); originally named a sour, ill-humoured personal temper, an irritability of disposition.
- [17th–19th c. broadening] - Widened from mere peevishness to a heavy, sullen, withdrawn melancholy; in novels it describes a brooding, inward gloom, a weighed-down despondency rather than an active rage.
- [Contemporary sense] - Chiefly literary and formal; "morosely" describes acting in a gloomy, ill-tempered, self-absorbed manner, keeping its heavy, sour emotional register far from casual speech.
