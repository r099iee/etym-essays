• Syntax: Similar because both represent the foundational structural levels of grammatical theory; differs because syntax governs the arrangement and hierarchical ordering of words into phrases and sentences, whereas morphology investigates the internal structure, inflection, and composition within individual words.
  • Lexicology: Similar because both deal with the vocabulary and word stocks of a language; differs because lexicology examines the semantic meaning, idiomatic usage, and historical development of individual words in the lexicon, whereas morphology isolates the formal mechanical rules governing morpheme combination.
  • Etymology: Similar because both explore word forms and derivation; differs because etymology reconstructs the historical origins, ancestral roots, and diachronic shifts of words across centuries, whereas morphology analyzes synchronic structural rules of word formation.
  • Anatomy: Similar because both examine the internal physical form and structural composition of entities; differs because anatomy is strictly biological, studying bodily dissection and physical organs, whereas morphology spans both biological outward forms and linguistic word structures.
  • Topology: Similar because both deal with form, spatial configuration, and structural relationships; differs because topology is a branch of higher mathematics concerned with the geometric properties of space preserved under continuous deformations, whereas morphology deals with discrete symbolic or physical forms.
  • Structuralism: Similar because both emphasize that individual components derive their function from their position within an organized system; differs because structuralism is a broad twentieth-century philosophical and anthropological methodology, whereas morphology is a specific subfield of empirical linguistics and natural sciences.
