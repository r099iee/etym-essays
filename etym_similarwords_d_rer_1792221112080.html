• Holbein: Similar because Hans Holbein the Younger was Dürer's fellow German Renaissance master renowned for peerless draftsmanship and humanistic portraiture; differs because Holbein achieved his greatest renown as court painter to King Henry VIII in England producing sleek oil portraits, whereas Dürer was primarily a prolific printmaker, visionary engraver, and theoretical author centered in Nuremberg.
  • Rembrandt: Similar because both were titanic Northern European masters who elevated printmaking (woodcut and copperplate engraving for Dürer; etching and drypoint for Rembrandt) to supreme artistic status and produced profound, lifelong series of self-portraits; differs because Rembrandt worked a century later in the Dutch Golden Age focusing on dramatic chiaroscuro and psychological paint impasto, whereas Dürer worked with crisp, linear Renaissance graphic precision.
  • Cranach: Similar because Lucas Cranach the Elder was Dürer's contemporary and close associate in the German Renaissance, producing portraits of Martin Luther and Protestant imagery; differs because Cranach ran an extensive commercial workshop producing courtly, mannered nudes and theological allegories, whereas Dürer pursued mathematical perspective, anatomical theory, and monumental print cycles.
  • Leonardo: Similar because Leonardo da Vinci was Dürer's Italian contemporary and intellectual counterpart, sharing an intense passion for proportional geometry, anatomy, natural science, and theoretical treatise writing; differs because Leonardo was an Italian master of oil painting, sfumato, and engineering whose writings remained private manuscripts, whereas Dürer brought Renaissance theory north of the Alps and published his treatises widely in German and Latin.
  • Michelangelo: Similar because both were towering giants of High Renaissance art with deep Christian religious devotion; differs because Michelangelo expressed heroic anatomical form through marble sculpture and monumental Sistine frescoes in Rome, whereas Dürer expressed intellectual and spiritual tension primarily through graphic black-and-white prints.
  • Bosch: Similar because Hieronymus Bosch was a Northern European contemporary who depicted apocalyptic visions and moral torment; differs because Bosch painted enigmatic, fantastical medieval allegories in early Netherlandish oil, whereas Dürer synthesized German late Gothic expressive power with Italian classical humanism and mathematical perspective.
