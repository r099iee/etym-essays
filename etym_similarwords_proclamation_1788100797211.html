• Declaration: Similar because both name formal public statements made known to a community; differs because declaration is broader — it may be personal or official, written or spoken, and need not carry an authority's weight, while a proclamation is always official, public, and authoritative.
  • Announcement: Similar because both make something known publicly; differs because announcement is neutral, small, and everyday (a train announcement, a wedding announcement), while proclamation is solemn, state-level, and ceremonial.
  • Decree: Similar because both are formal acts of an authority; differs because a decree commands and legislates — it changes what must be done — while a proclamation announces and declares what is or shall be known.
  • Edict: Similar because both are formal pronouncements by a ruler or authority; differs because an edict is a royal or papal command, colder and more legislative in tone, while a proclamation is cried out to the people and emphasises publicity more than command.
  • Pronouncement: Similar because both are formal statements; differs because a pronouncement is a considered judgement or opinion (of a court, a critic, an expert) with no fixed public rite, while a proclamation is an official announcement to the whole community.
  • Promulgation: Similar because both concern making a law or rule publicly known; differs because promulgation is a technical legal term for the official publication of a law already made, while proclamation announces decisions and facts, often with ceremony, and can precede the law.
  • Manifesto: Similar because both are public declarations; differs because a manifesto is a programmatic statement of a political or artistic movement, addressed to the public to persuade, while a proclamation is issued by an authority to make something known — command or fact, not argument.
  • Ukase: Similar because both are declarations by a sovereign authority; differs because ukase specifically names the tsarist Russian imperial decree and carries an archaic, autocratic flavour, while proclamation is the general modern word.
  • Bull: Similar because both are formal pronouncements of authority; differs because bull is strictly the papal edict (from the seal, bulla) — a closed technical term — while proclamation covers all secular and institutional public announcements.
  • Bulletin / Communiqué: Similar because both convey official information to the public; differs because a bulletin or communiqué is news-like, brief, and practical (issued to the press), while a proclamation is ceremonial, enduring, and formulaic.
  • Fiat: Similar because both are authoritative acts of will; differs because a fiat is a bare command (let it be done) without public proclamation — an order, not an announcement to the people.
  • Notice: Similar because both make something publicly known; differs because a notice is a small formal warning or information sheet (eviction notice, notice of meeting), while a proclamation is dignified, public, and large in scope.
