• Asked: Similar because both pose a question; differs because asked is the neutral, all-purpose term carrying no implication of doubt or testing, while queried suggests a question used as an instrument of verification — you query precisely what you doubt.
  • Questioned: Similar because both raise a doubt or put a question; differs because questioned tends toward challenge or cross-examination of another person's claim, while queried is more measured and formal, closer to testing and checking than to confrontation.
  • Inquired: Similar because both seek information; differs because inquired implies courtesy and genuine ignorance — filling a gap in knowledge — while queried carries a residue of suspicion or verification, probing the truth of what one doubts.
  • Interrogated: Similar because both involve formal, systematic questioning; differs because interrogated suggests official, hostile cross-examination and would be wildly out of place as a casual speech tag, while queried stays careful, probing, and controlled.
  • Demanded: Similar because both request a response; differs because demanded is peremptory, asserting authority and expecting immediate compliance, while queried is polite and open, an invitation to answer rather than a command.
  • Challenged: Similar because both contest the validity of something; differs because challenged is confrontational, a direct call to justify or fight, while queried is gentler and intellectual — doubt expressed as a question rather than an accusation.
  • Probed: Similar because both test and search for information; differs because probed suggests a deep, invasive, persistent search (physical or medical), while queried is a single, polite, pointed question.
  • Petitioned: Similar because both make a formal request; differs because petitioned is a respectful, often written request to an authority, while queried is an oral or written question testing truth — not a plea but a probe.
  • Conferred / consulted: Similar because both involve seeking information from someone; differs because conferring implies mutual discussion and shared authority, while queried is a one-way act of asking with a tinge of doubt.
