- Biblical thou-shalt-not-covet - Tenth Commandment against desiring neighbour's house and goods; grave, absolute
- Middle English coveitous - greedy, grasping vice in sermons and Chaucer; moralised, allegorised as Avarice
- Early modern covetous as idolatry - Reformers preach covetousness is idolatry; searching, Protestant
- 16th-17th c. uncovetous coined - not covetous in sermons and Authorised Version margins; pious, rare, deliberate
- Victorian Ruskinian uncovetous admiration - reverent, healthy delight in noblemen's houses without envy; aesthetic, political, paternal
- 20th c. decline - replaced by unselfish and unmaterialistic in secular prose; archaic, pulpit-bound
- Modern revival as anti-consumerism - uncovetous lifestyle for simplicity and sustainability; ethical, green, nostalgic
