• Flag: Similar because the commonest wild irises are called flags, especially the yellow flag of marshes; differs because flag is the homely English meadow word, while iris carries the learned botanical and classical flavour.
  • Fleur-de-lis: Similar because the heraldic lily-lozenge is traditionally a stylised iris bloom; differs because fleur-de-lis names the emblem of French kingship, not a living flower nodding by a river.
  • Gladdon: Similar because it is the old country name for the stinking iris of shady places; differs because gladdon is dialectal and narrow, while iris covers the whole splendid genus.
  • Pupil: Similar because pupil and iris together form the eye's light-regulating centre; differs because the pupil is the dark opening itself, while the iris is the coloured muscular ring around it.
  • Rainbow: Similar because the rainbow is the founding Greek sense behind the word; differs because rainbow names the sky phenomenon directly, while iris lends that splendour sideways to flowers, eyes, and lenses.
  • Diaphragm: Similar because a camera iris is a kind of diaphragm governing light; differs because diaphragm is the general engineering term for any such partition, while iris specifies the adjustable eye-like leaf mechanism.
  • Cornea: Similar because both are visible structures of the front of the eye; differs because the cornea is the clear window bulging over the front, while the iris is the coloured curtain seen through it.
  • Orchid: Similar because both are showy, intricate garden flowers traded and bred by enthusiasts; differs because orchids belong to a wholly different tropical family famed for bizarre symmetry, while irises are temperate sword-leaved flags.
