• Tore: Similar because both are past forms of verbs meaning 'to pull apart violently' (tear/rend); differs because 'tore' is the everyday, neutral word, while 'rent' is literary and archaic, implying greater force and finality.
  • Ripped: Similar because both mean violently torn; differs because 'ripped' is modern colloquial (and means 'muscular' in slang), whereas 'rent' is formal or poetic.
  • Split: Similar because both can mean divided apart; differs because 'split' is neutral and may be clean or deliberate, while 'rent' implies a violent tear with ragged edges.
  • Shredded: Similar because both involve tearing; differs because 'shredded' means torn into many narrow strips or fragments, while 'rent' usually denotes one or few large tears.
  • Riven: Similar because 'riven' (past participle of rive) also means 'torn or split apart'; differs because 'riven' is even more poetic and suggests splitting along natural lines or fault planes, while 'rent' is the more general violent tear.
  • Sundered: Similar because 'sundered' means forcibly separated; differs because 'sunder' stresses separation into distinct parts rather than the tearing wound, and is still more elevated in register.
  • Severed: Similar because both mean cut or divided; differs because 'sever' implies a clean cut, usually with a blade, whereas 'rent' implies tearing by force without a tool.
  • Lacerated: Similar because both mean torn, especially of flesh; differs because 'lacerated' is a clinical/medical term for jagged wounds, while 'rent' is literary and general.
  • Divided: Similar because both can describe separation of a group; differs because 'divided' is abstract and neutral, while 'rent' (metaphorically) stresses painful, violent, irreparable division.
  • Lease (payment sense): Similar because both concern the use of property for money; differs because 'lease' is the contract (or the verb of granting use), while 'rent' is the periodic payment (or the payment verb).
  • Hire (payment sense): Similar because to hire = to pay for temporary use; differs because 'hire' in British English applies to goods and vehicles while 'rent' covers property, and 'rent' is the dominant modern word for both.
  • Let (out): Similar because to let = to grant use of property for rent; differs because 'let' is landlord-side and chiefly British, while 'rent' can be used for either side.
  • Charge / Fee: Similar because both are payments; differs because a fee is for a service, while rent is specifically for the use of property or land.
  • Rental: Similar because 'rental' is the noun/adjective of renting; differs because 'rental' refers to the arrangement or the amount, while 'rent' is the payment itself.
