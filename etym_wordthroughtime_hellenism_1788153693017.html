- [Ancient Greece: hellēnismós = correct Greek usage, "speaking Greek" (opposite of barbarismós)] - [Perception: a linguistic badge of cultural membership]
- [Later antiquity (Julian the Apostate): hellēnismos = the Greek religion, paganism] - [Perception: the word becomes a religious identity under Christian empire]
- [16th–17th c. English: "Hellenism" = Greek idiom or phraseology] - [Perception: a term of grammarians and translators]
- [18th–19th c.: expands to Greek culture and the post-Alexander world] - [Perception: civilizational nickname; Gibbon, Droysen and the historians give it epochs]
- [1869: Arnold opposes Hellenism to Hebraism in Culture and Anarchy] - [Perception: the word becomes a spiritual ideal — clarity, sweetness and light against strictness of conscience]
- [Pater and the aesthetic movement] - [Perception: Hellenism = the love of intellect and imagination for their own sake]
- [Today: scholarly and critical currency] - [Perception: still learned, still resonant; no one says it without hearing Athens]
