- 17th Century (1670s) - Borrowed from French *alcôve* as a sophisticated architectural term for a vaulted bed recess in grand residential apartments, carrying connotations of aristocratic refinement and French courtly fashion.
- 18th Century - Broadened into English landscape gardening (garden alcoves, shaded gazebos) and domestic architecture, while acquiring literary and romantic overtones as a space of intimate retirement, study, or amorous intrigue (*littérature d'alcôve*).
- 19th Century (Victorian Era) - Widely democratized in domestic building to describe any recessed nook for bookshelves, breakfast tables, or bay-window retreats, while expanding geologically to natural rock indentations.
- 20th to 21st Century - Retains its classic architectural meaning of a cozy, functional recess in modern interior design and literature, consistently evoking shelter, seclusion, and intimate containment.
