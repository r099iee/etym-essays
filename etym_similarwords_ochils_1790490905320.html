• Sidlaws: Similar because they are the neighbouring lowland volcanic hills across the Tay; differs because Sidlaws run north-east of Dundee, while Ochils face Stirling and Clackmannanshire.
  • Lomonds (Lomond Hills): Similar because they are lowland Fife hills of similar height and volcanic origin; differs because Lomonds are twin cones in Fife, while Ochils are a long scarp.
  • Pentlands: Similar because they are accessible hills above Edinburgh loved by walkers; differs because Pentlands are south of the Forth, Ochils north of it.
  • Campsies (Campsie Fells): Similar because they are a south-facing scarp above Glasgow villages; differs because Campsies are basalt plateau west of Stirling, Ochils andesite east of it.
  • Grampians: Similar because they are the great Scottish mountain system behind the Ochils; differs because Grampians are high, vast, and Highland, while Ochils are low, compact, and Lowland.
  • Cheviots: Similar because they are rounded border hills of similar scale; differs because Cheviots straddle England-Scotland, while Ochils lie wholly in central Scotland.
  • braes / hills / fells: Similar because all mean upland slopes; differs because braes and hills are generic Scots-English, while Ochils is one named range.
  • Trossachs: Similar because Scott romanticised both as fairy-haunted lake-and-hill country; differs because Trossachs are wooded loch defiles west of Stirling, Ochils open moorland scarp east of it.
  • Hillfoots villages: Similar because the name evokes Menstrie, Alva, and Dollar nestling below; differs because villages are settlements, while Ochils are the hills above them.
  • Ben Cleuch: Similar because it is the highest summit of the range; differs because Ben Cleuch is one top (721 m), while Ochils are the whole twenty-five-mile crescent.
