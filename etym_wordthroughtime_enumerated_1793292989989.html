- Ancient Rome — numbering out: Latin enumerare means to count up fully and to recount in narrative, at home in accounts, rhetoric, and law.
- 17th c. — English adopts it: enumerate enters learned prose for censuses, inventories, and sermons that number their heads.
- 18th c. — constitutional career: bills of rights enumerate powers and freedoms, and the enumerated list becomes a technology of liberty and limit.
- 19th c. — science counts: naturalists enumerate species and strata, and statistics enumerates populations street by street.
- 20th c. — logic and machines: logicians enumerate premises and theorems, then programmers make enumeration a data type with every value named.
- Today — the audit word: enumerated risks, enumerated costs, enumerated rights — thorough, checkable, and, as the source sentence sighs, never quite complete.
