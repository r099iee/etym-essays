NOTE — DEBATED / NUANCED STAGES: The deeper origin of French maraud "rogue, vagabond" is UNKNOWN and genuinely debated, and no earlier stage can be securely reconstructed — nothing is guessed here. The leading hypotheses: (1) a Germanic source related to the family of English mar "to spoil, damage" (the rogue as "one who spoils or harms"), perhaps via a Middle Dutch or Middle Low German form; (2) a link to Old French maroufle "scoundrel" or dialectal marault "beggar", and beyond these to a Latin stem meaning "blemish, defect" (cf. menda, mendicus "beggar"); (3) a popular older theory that the word was born among the roving, looting soldiery of the Thirty Years' War (1618–1648), the German Marodeur — but German almost certainly borrowed from French, so this tells us about the word's social history, not its origin. The Oxford English Dictionary and the French etymological dictionaries mark the origin as unknown; only the attested French and English stages are shown above, and the uncertainty is stated explicitly rather than guessed away.
