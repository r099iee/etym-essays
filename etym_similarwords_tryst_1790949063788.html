• Rendezvous: Similar because both denote a prearranged meeting between parties at an agreed time and place; differs because rendezvous is borrowed from French military terminology and can refer to military assemblies, casual social meetups, or space dockings, whereas tryst carries an intensely romantic, clandestine, or intimate emotional charge.
  • Assignation: Similar because both refer to an appointed secret meeting, especially between lovers; differs because assignation possesses a more formal, Latinate, and sometimes slightly illicit or transactional tone, whereas tryst evokes poetic, folkloric, or romantic passion.
  • Meeting: Similar because both describe two or more people coming together; differs because meeting is a broad, neutral term encompassing routine business conferences, committee assemblies, and casual encounters with zero romantic or secret connotation.
  • Appointment: Similar because both involve scheduling a specific time and location; differs because an appointment is typically professional, formal, or medical, lacking romantic intimacy and secrecy.
  • Liaison: Similar because both frequently refer to romantic or secretive interactions; differs because liaison usually denotes an ongoing continuous sexual or diplomatic relationship rather than a single discrete physical rendezvous.
  • Date: Similar because both signify an arranged romantic encounter; differs because a date is modern, casual, public, and commonplace, whereas a tryst implies secluded privacy, urgent romance, or hidden stakes.
  • Parley: Similar because both describe planned meetings between specific parties; differs because a parley is a formal discussion between opposing or warring sides to negotiate terms, carrying political and military weight.
