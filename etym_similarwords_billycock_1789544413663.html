• Bowler: Similar because both refer to the exact same style of hard, round-crowned felt hat with a curved brim; differs because bowler was named after the London hatmakers Thomas and William Bowler and became the standard commercial name, whereas billycock is the popular colloquial/historical term.
  • Derby: Similar because both designate the round-crowned felt hat; differs because derby is the standard American term for the style, named after the 12th Earl of Derby and the Epsom Derby horse race.
  • Fedora: Similar because both are classic men's felt hats; differs because a fedora features a soft, creased crown and a wider, flexible snap brim, unlike the stiff, hard, uncreased dome of a billycock.
  • Trilby: Similar because both are traditional British felt hats; differs because a trilby has a shorter brim turned up at the back and a pinched crown, while a billycock is completely round and rigid.
  • Top Hat: Similar because both were iconic Victorian headwear; differs because a top hat is tall, cylindrical, flat-topped, and made of silk plush for formal occasions, whereas a billycock was practical everyday working/middle-class felt headwear.
  • Homburg: Similar because both are formal/semi-formal stiff felt hats; differs because a homburg features a single central gutter crease running along the crown and a bound, curled brim.
