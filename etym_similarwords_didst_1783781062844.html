• Did: Similar because it is the same past tense of do with the same full semantic range; differs because did is the modern universal past tense used with all pronouns (I did, you did, he did), while didst was restricted to the second-person singular pronoun thou.
  • Dost: Similar because dost is the fellow thou-form of the same verb do; differs because dost is the PRESENT tense ("thou dost prefer"), while didst is the PAST tense ("thou didst inspire").
  • Doth: Similar because doth is the archaic third-person singular present of do ("he doth"); differs because doth is present tense and third person, while didst is past tense and second person.
  • Doest: Similar because doest is the full uncontracted second-person present form of do; differs because doest is present tense and, in strict early modern usage, was often preferred when do was a principal (lexical) verb, whereas dost was the auxiliary form.
  • Diddest / diddest: Similar because these are the uncontracted two-syllable forms of the same thou-past of do; differs because diddest retains the fuller -est ending and extra syllable, while didst is the contracted monosyllable favored by Milton and the KJV for meter.
  • Dydest: Similar because dydest is the direct Old English ancestor of didst, the same second-person past of dōn; differs because dydest belongs to Old English (pre-1100) phonology and spelling, while didst is the Early Modern English form.
  • Wast: Similar because wast is the parallel thou-form of the past tense of be ("thou wast"), sharing the same -st ending and archaic register; differs because wast conjugates the copula be, not do.
  • Satst: Similar because satst is the parallel thou-form of the past tense of sit, sharing the contracted -st pattern and the same Miltonic register; differs because satst belongs to the verb sit, while didst belongs to do.
  • Hadst: Similar because hadst is the parallel thou-past of have ("thou hadst"); differs because hadst conjugates the verb have, and the parallel is purely morphological, not semantic.
