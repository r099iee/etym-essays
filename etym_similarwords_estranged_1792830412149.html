• Alienated: Similar because both mean made strange and cut off from former closeness; differs because alienated stresses the process of driving away (alienated workers, alienated youth), while estranged stresses the resulting state of foreignness itself.
  • Separated: Similar because both describe closeness ended by a gap; differs because separated is neutral and often physical or legal (separated by miles, legally separated), while estranged always adds emotional coldness.
  • Divided: Similar because both name unity broken into opposing parts; differs because divided stresses the split line itself (a divided family, a divided nation), while estranged stresses the lived strangeness across it.
  • Disaffected: Similar because both describe loyalty withdrawn from a cause or ruler; differs because disaffected is political and active (disaffected voters organise), while estranged is personal and sorrowful (estranged kin grieve).
  • Detached: Similar because both picture connection loosened or cut; differs because detached can be calm and chosen (a detached observer, detached retina), while estranged is always a grievous loss.
  • Disunited: Similar because both mean no longer one in purpose or feeling; differs because disunited describes the group's broken front, while estranged describes the heart's grown distance.
  • Isolated: Similar because both leave the subject cut off from others; differs because isolated stresses solitude itself (isolated cottages, isolated cases), while estranged stresses solitude from someone once close.
  • Divorced: Similar because both end an intimate union in law or fact; differs because divorced names the legal dissolution of marriage (or, metaphorically, of things that belong together), while estranged names the emotional foreignness, with or without papers.
  • Disassociated: Similar because both record a break with former company or ideas; differs because disassociated is formal and often voluntary (disassociated from the movement), while estranged mourns the break as a loss.
