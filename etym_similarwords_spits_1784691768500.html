• Skewers: Similar because both are slender pointed rods used in cooking; differs because a skewer is short and used for small pieces threaded together (kebabs), while a spit is long and carries a whole joint or carcass for slow roasting.
  • Rods: Similar because both are long straight slender bars; differs because rod is the generic engineering term for any straight bar, while spit is specifically the cooking rod and its land-form extension.
  • Broaches: Similar because both are archaic words for a pointed spit or roasting pin; differs because broach (from Latin broccus "pointed") is now chiefly the verb "to open, bring up" and is obsolete as a roasting noun, while spit remains current.
  • Rotisserie rods: Similar because both are the rods of a mechanical rotating roaster; differs because rotisserie is the modern French-borrowed term for the rotating spit apparatus, while spit is the plain English word for the rod itself.
  • Jack / roasting-jack: Similar because both belong to the roasting apparatus; differs because the jack is the mechanism that turns the spit, not the rod that holds the meat.
  • Prongs: Similar because both are pointed projections; differs because prongs are short tines of a fork or tool, while a spit is a single long rod.
  • Sandbar: Similar because both are coastal landforms of sand; differs because a sandbar is a long ridge often submerged offshore, while a spit is a narrow tongue attached to the shore and projecting into open water.
  • Promontory: Similar because both are land projecting into water; differs because a promontory is a high rocky headland, while a spit is low and built of sand or shingle.
  • Point: Similar because both name a narrow projecting feature of land or a sharp end; differs because point is the general word for any sharp tapering end, while spit is specific to the roasting rod and the low coastal tongue.
  • Cape: Similar because both are coastal projections; differs because a cape is a large headland, often named and dramatic, while a spit is a small slender accumulation of sediment.
  • Expectorates: Similar because both are verbs for ejecting saliva; differs because expectorate is the clinical, formal term, while spit is the plain everyday verb — and is the homonym of the roasting-rod noun.
  • Impalement rods: Similar because both describe a rod on which something is thrust; differs because impalement is the general word for piercing on any point, while spit is the specific cooking rod with its land-form and measure extensions.
