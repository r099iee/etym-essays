- 12th–13th c. — Recorded in Old Burmese stone pillar inscriptions at Bagan as Mranma (မြန်မာ), denoting the territory and ethnolinguistic core of the Bamar kingdom.
- 16th c. — Encountered by Portuguese merchants and navigators (Duarte Barbosa, Fernão Mendes Pinto), who transcribed the colloquial spoken form Bama as Barma or Brama.
- 17th–18th c. — Entered European trade registers and maps as Birmah or Burmah, referring to the prosperous Konbaung kingdom centered at Ava and Amarapura.
- 19th c. (1824–1886) — Formalized under British colonial administration as Burma, establishing the province of British Burma within the Indian Empire.
- Mid-20th c. (1948–1988) — Retained at independence as the Union of Burma, internationally celebrated as a founding member of the Non-Aligned Movement.
- 1989–Present — Renamed Myanmar by the ruling military junta; the name Burma became politically freighted as a symbol of democratic defiance, while persisting as a standard historical and literary term worldwide.
