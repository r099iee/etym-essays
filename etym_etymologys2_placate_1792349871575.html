Note on semantic development: Michiel de Vaan and historical Indo-Europeanists trace Latin plācāre ("to appease") and its stative counterpart placēre ("to please") back to the Proto-Indo-European root *pleh₂k- ("broad, flat"). The physical semantic bridge transitioned from "making a turbulent surface flat and smooth" (as when oil or a calm breeze smoothes troubled waters) to "smoothing ruffled tempers, calming stormy emotions". The verb was Anglicized directly from the Latin past-participle stem plācāt-, following the standard Renaissance productive pattern for borrowing Latin first-conjugation verbs into English.
