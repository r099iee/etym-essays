• Checked: Similar because both mean stopping or slowing an advance or flow; differs because check is the broad, neutral verb for halting progress (check an advance, check a habit), while stemmed stresses pressing back against a flood-like pressure — and check lacks the dam image.
  • Staunched / stanched: Similar because both mean stopping the flow of a liquid, especially blood; differs because staunch is the medical and technical word for stopping a flow at the source, while stemmed is broader, covering floods, tides, and onslaughts, not only liquids.
  • Halted: Similar because both mean bringing movement to a stop; differs because halted is an abrupt termination with no image of resistance or pressing back, while stemmed implies a sustained, effortful holding action against pressure.
  • Held (back): Similar because both mean preventing something from advancing; differs because held back is the general, colloquial phrase, while stemmed is its formal, forceful, dam-like counterpart.
  • Curbed: Similar because both mean restraining something in motion; differs because curb applies to controlling excess (curb spending, curb a horse) rather than to physically damning a flow or onslaught.
  • Arrested: Similar because both mean stopping something in its course; differs because arrest is abrupt and often legal or medical (arrested development, cardiac arrest), without the hydraulic press-back image of stemming.
  • Dammed: Similar because both carry the literal image of a barrier against a flow; differs because dammed is confined to water and to the physical barrier, while stemmed stands for the act of resistance in any fluid or figurative current.
  • Repelled: Similar because both describe holding back an attack or onslaught; differs because repelled means driving the attacker back in defeat — a stronger claim — while stemmed means only checking and holding the pressure, often barely and temporarily.
