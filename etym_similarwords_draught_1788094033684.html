• Current (of air): Similar because both mean air moving through a space. Differs because current is neutral and technical; draught implies air drawn through an opening by pressure difference, with a chill attached.
  • Breeze: Similar because both mean moving air. Differs because a breeze is outdoor and gentle; a draught is indoor, confined, and often cold or unwelcome.
  • Gust / Blast: Similar because both mean a rush of air. Differs because these are sudden and violent; a draught is steady and sneaking.
  • Breath (of air): Similar because both describe a small flow of air. Differs because breath is gentle and life-giving; draught is about movement through an opening.
  • Sip: Similar because both mean a small amount of drink. Differs because a sip is taken deliberately in small sips; a draught is one whole swallowing act, larger.
  • Swallow / Gulp: Similar because both name one act of drinking. Differs because these are the ordinary words of the act; a draught is the measured amount drawn up, with a literary or medical flavour.
  • Dose: Similar because both name a measured amount of medicine. Differs because a dose is measured by quantity; a draught is a whole drink taken at once ("a sleeping draught").
  • Draft (US): Similar because it is the same word respelled. Differs only in orthography and national convention; in the US draft covers air, documents, and beer alike.
  • Sketch / Outline / Plan: Similar because a draught is a preliminary drawing. Differs because these are the modern words for the document sense, which English now spells draft.
  • Drawing: Similar because draught is the noun of draw. Differs because drawing is the general noun of the art; draught names the specific product or the act of pulling.
  • Haul / Catch: Similar because "a draught of fishes" is a haul. Differs because haul/catch are general; the draught sense is biblical and archaic.
  • Depth (of ship): Similar because a ship's draught is its depth in water. Differs because depth is general; draught is the naval term for how deep a vessel sits.
  • Pull / Tug: Similar because draught literally means "pulling, drawing." Differs because these are the modern everyday verbs; draught as an action-noun survives only in compounds (draught animal).
  • Checkers: Similar because draughts is the British name for the same game. Differs only in regional vocabulary; the British name comes from drawing the pieces.
  • Airflow / Ventilation: Similar because both concern moving air in buildings. Differs because these are engineering terms, neutral and technical; draught is the homely word for the chill you feel.
