PIE *gʰer- "to grasp, hold, enclose" (reconstructed, c. 4500–3500 BCE)
→ PIE *gʰor-tó- "enclosed space, fenced yard" (nominalized participle)
→ Proto-Italic *hortos → Latin hortus "garden, enclosed plot" (attested 1st c. BCE)
→ Latin cohors (gen. cohortis), co- "together" + hortus: "enclosed yard; throng, retinue; military cohort" (attested from the 2nd c. BCE)
→ Vulgar Latin *curtis / *cortis "enclosed court of a lord's estate; royal residence with its household" (attested c. 6th c. CE)
→ Old French cort, cour "royal court; princely residence; the assembly gathered around a ruler" (9th–12th c.)
→ Anglo-French / Old French courtier, cortier "one who attends court" (13th–14th c.)
→ Middle English courtier "one in attendance at a royal court" (attested c. 1300)
→ English courtier → plural courtiers ◄ TARGET (present day)

DEBATE / NUANCE: The PIE root is written both *gher- and *gʰer- in the handbooks, and its exact velar character is debated; the vowel grade of the noun *gʰor-tó- varies in reconstruction. The Vulgar Latin stage is attested in several spellings (curtis, cortis, curte, cort), reflecting dialectal variation rather than error. Everything from Latin hortus onward is securely attested; only the pre-Latin reconstructions are theoretical. The same root *gʰer- "to enclose" produced garden, yard, and orchard (via Germanic *gardaz), Greek khordē "gut, string" (our chord), and Vedic gṛhá- "house" (Hindi ghar).
