- 13th to 14th Century: Physical low elevation and subordinate feudal estate - Denoted living close to the ground, small in stature, or occupying a humble servant station in the feudal hierarchy.
- 15th to 16th Century: Religious virtue of Christian humility and meekness - Thoroughly spiritualized in devotional texts (translating biblical humilis), symbolizing freedom from vanity, arrogance, and the deadly sin of pride.
- 17th to 18th Century: Social class delineation with aristocratic condescension - Used frequently by Augustan and patrician writers to designate the laboring peasantry, often contrasting "the lowly born" with high society.
- 19th Century: Romantic glorification of rural simplicity and early natural history classification - Celebrated by Romantic poets (Wordsworth, Burns) as the ideal state of pastoral authenticity, while adopted by naturalists to describe primitive organisms.
- 20th to 21st Century: Anthropological description, self-deprecating humor, and culinary praise - Applied with affectionate respect to unheralded workers, simple staple ingredients, or foundational biological creatures.
