Notes: (1) The formation of Old English ǣfre is an acknowledged puzzle: its first element is generally connected with ā "ever", from Germanic *aiwi-, but the second element (-fre, -re) has no generally accepted explanation — the Oxford English Dictionary records the origin as uncertain. (2) The relation between the *mē- base of "more" and the *meǵh₂- "great" family (Greek mégas, Latin magnus) is debated; modern etymologists generally keep them separate. (3) Do not confuse the fused adverb "evermore" with the two-word "ever more" meaning "increasingly".
