Note on historical nuance: Suffix -age traces back to Latin -āticum, a productive suffix attached to nominal and verbal stems to form neuter collective nouns denoting an aggregate state, function, or relational condition (as in voyage, heritage, assemblage). In 18th-century French, entourage was initially restricted to physical borders, gardens, and architectural environs. Its transfer to human companions occurred during the Napoleonic era and the Bourbon Restoration, when the formal apparatus of royal courts was revived with an emphasis on protective and ceremonial social circles.
