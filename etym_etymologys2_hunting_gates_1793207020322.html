Element 1 — hunting:
Old English (9th c.)
huntian
‘to chase game’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
hunten
‘to hunt, pursue’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
hunting
‘the chase, the sport’

Element 2 — gate:
Old English (9th c.)
geat
‘gate, door, opening’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
gate, yat
‘barred entrance’
↓
Modern English (plural)
hunting-gates
‘field gates of the hunt’

Note: modern gate also absorbed Old Norse gata (‘road, way’), surviving in street names like Coppergate; the barrier sense used here descends from Old English geat. The deeper root of Old English huntian is debated — often linked with the family of hentan (‘to seize’) — and is left unforced here.
