Earliest root: PIE *dewk- "to pull, draw, lead" → Proto-Germanic *teuhan "to pull, draw, lead" (strong verb) → *tugōną (o-grade weak formation) "to draw, tear, drag" → Old English tōgian "to drag, pull, haul" → Middle English tuggen "to pull hard, strain in pulling" (c. 1200) → Modern English tug (v.) "to pull with effort, especially repeatedly" → TUGGING (present participle) Cognate lineage note: the same PIE root *dewk- gave Latin dūcere "to lead, draw" (whence English duke, duct, educate); within Germanic, Old English tēon "to draw, pull, lead" is the strong-verb cognate, and Middle English toggen / touen produced modern English tow. Modern offshoots of tug include tugboat (1817), tug-of-war, and probably toggle (1769, a frequentative of tug).
