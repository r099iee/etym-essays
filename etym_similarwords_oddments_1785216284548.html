• odds and ends: Similar because both name miscellaneous leftover articles; differs because odds and ends is the plainer, conversational, fixed phrase, looser in register, without the bookish, slightly formal flavour of oddments.
  • remnants: Similar because both are what is left behind; differs because remnants stresses pieces left after a larger whole has been consumed, especially cloth cut from a bolt, rather than a jumble of stray objects.
  • scraps: Similar because both are small leftover bits; differs because scraps stresses smallness and inferiority, often of food or paper, with a dismissive tone, while oddments is gentler and more whimsical.
  • miscellany: Similar because both are a collection of diverse items; differs because miscellany names a deliberate, curated anthology, not the accidental pile of leftovers that oddments denotes.
  • bits and pieces / bits and bobs: Similar because both are colloquial names for miscellaneous small items; differs because they are more informal and looser, while oddments is old-fashioned, bookish, and preserves the idea of the unmatched 'odd' item.
  • leftovers: Similar because both are things remaining after use; differs because leftovers usually refers to uneaten food, while oddments is general and implies oddity and lack of value rather than food.
  • odd lot: Similar because both name a mixed batch of surplus goods; differs because odd lot is a trade term for a job lot sold as it stands, without oddments' everyday, gentle sense of stray personal objects.
  • jumble: Similar because both suggest a disorderly heap of small things; differs because jumble emphasizes the disorder of the heap itself, while oddments emphasizes the oddity and surplus character of the items.
