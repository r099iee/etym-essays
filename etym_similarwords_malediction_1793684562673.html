• Curse: Similar because both denote an utterance invoking misfortune, harm, or supernatural evil upon a target; differs because curse is the common, Germanic native term of universal register across casual speech and folklore, whereas malediction is formal, Latinate, and emphasizes an elaborate, solemn, or liturgical structure.
  • Imprecation: Similar because both involve the verbal calling down of calamity or divine retribution upon an adversary; differs because imprecation stresses the passionate prayer or urgent petition to transcendent or demonic powers to execute vengeance, whereas malediction encompasses both solemn prayers for harm and secular vitriolic slander.
  • Anathema: Similar because both signify a solemn verbal condemnation resulting in spiritual ruin; differs because anathema is strictly ecclesiastical and institutional, denoting an official decree of excommunication and canonical damnation by church council or ecclesiastical authority, whereas malediction can be uttered spontaneously by any individual.
  • Execration: Similar because both express the fervent wish for another's ruin or destruction; differs because execration conveys an intense visceral revulsion, disgust, and loathing that accompanies the utterance, focusing on the speaker's emotional abhorrence.
  • Denunciation: Similar because both represent the public verbal condemnation of an individual or action; differs because denunciation is predominantly civic, ethical, or legal, openly accusing someone of wrongdoing before an assembly without invoking supernatural catastrophe or occult ruin.
  • Vituperation: Similar because both describe sustained, hostile verbal condemnation; differs because vituperation concentrates on violent scolding, caustic invective, and continuous abusive reproach in interpersonal debate, lacking the solemn supernatural gravity of a ritual curse.
  • Invective: Similar because both constitute scathing verbal assaults; differs because invective emphasizes sharp rhetorical artistry, polemical skill, and satirical hostility in writing or oratory, whereas malediction focuses on the explicit desire for the target's destruction or doom.
  • Damnation: Similar because both involve cosmic spiritual punishment; differs because damnation is the actual judicial decree or theological state of eternal suffering in hell pronounced by God, whereas malediction is the human or verbal utterance wishing such a fate.
  • Fulmination: Similar because both convey explosive verbal hostility; differs because fulmination metaphorically evokes thunder and lightning, describing a loud, sudden outburst of moral outrage or authoritarian decree, rather than a binding invocation of misfortune.
  • Bane: Similar because both are closely associated with fatal ruin, affliction, and destruction; differs because bane is a substantive agent, cause, or source of ruin or poison (e.g. "the bane of my existence"), whereas malediction is strictly a spoken utterance or verbal formula.
  • Abuse: Similar because both describe harsh, insulting, and derogatory speech directed against a person; differs because abuse is a general, everyday term lacking any literary, liturgical, or supernatural resonance.
  • Reviling: Similar because both involve speaking ill of someone with venomous hostility; differs because reviling emphasizes the contemptuous, mocking, and insulting nature of the verbal assault rather than the invocation of cosmic calamity.
