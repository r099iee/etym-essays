CHAIN 1 — the element "swine" ("pig"): PIE *sū- "pig, sow" (reconstructed; one of the most stable animal-roots of the family) → Proto-Germanic *swīną "pig, swine" (reconstructed; a *-n- stem formation on the pig-root) → Old English swīn "pig, swine" (attested 8th c.) → Middle English swine (attested 12th–13th c.) → Modern English swine "pig" (kept in formal, biblical, and compound use; the everyday word pig is a separate Old English formation) → Element swīn- in the Old English compound place-name swīn-burna "swine brook" → (compound stage below) CHAIN 2 — the element "burne" ("stream"): PIE *bʰreu- "to boil, bubble, well up, gush" (reconstructed; the water-spring as the bubbling-up source — cf. Greek phréar "well, spring"; the reconstruction is traditional, its details debated) → Proto-Germanic *brunnōn / *burnōn "spring, well, source, stream" (reconstructed) → Old English burna / burne "brook, stream, spring" (attested 8th c.) → Modern English dialect burn "a stream, brook" (chiefly Scottish and Northern English) → Element burna / burne in the compound place-name swīn-burna "swine brook" → (compound stage below) COMPOUND — swīn-burna > Swinburne: Old English toponym *swīn-burna "the brook where swine drank or were pastured" → attested place-name (West and East Swinburne, Northumberland) → Middle English place-name and surname Swinburne (recorded in the spellings Swineburn, Swinburn, Swynburn, 13th–15th c.) → Anglo-Scottish hereditary surname Swinburne (spelling fixed by the early modern period) → Algernon Charles SWINBURNE (the poet, 1837–1909) — and the modern critical usage "a Swinburnian ambiguity" (the sense in this card)
