• swelling: Similar because both mean enlarging beyond the normal level of a surface; differs because swelling suggests enlargement caused from within — fluid, inflammation, pressure — with medical overtones, whereas bulging suggests outward pressure of a flexible container's contents against its walls, more visual and informal.
  • protruding: Similar because both mean standing out from a surface; differs because protruding is neutral and technical (a protruding rock) with no sense of undesirable distension or rounded convexity.
  • protuberant: Similar because both describe rounded outward projection; differs because protuberant is the learned, clinical adjective (protuberant eyes), cooler in tone than bulging's grotesque, visceral charge.
  • distended: Similar because both mean stretched outward by internal pressure; differs because distended emphasizes the taut stretching of a hollow organ or vessel (a distended stomach), while bulging emphasizes the rounded curve of the surface itself.
  • convex: Similar because both denote an outward-curving shape; differs because convex is a purely geometric, value-free term, while bulging implies a boundary pressed outward by contents.
  • bloated: Similar because both suggest unnatural swelling; differs because bloated carries overtones of sickness, waterlogging, or excess (a bloated body, a bloated budget), where bulging is more neutral and visual.
  • puffy: Similar because both describe soft, rounded swelling; differs because puffy suggests flabbiness and softness (puffy eyes after weeping), lacking bulging's firm, outward-pressing tautness.
