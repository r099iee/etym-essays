- Old English — the hedgerow plum: slā names the blackthorn’s fruit, already sour, already thorny, gathered from wild hedges.
- Middle Ages — medicine and ink: sloes flavour verjuice, dye cloth, and physic the belly, valued precisely for the astringency that punishes the raw eater.
- Early Modern — gin’s arrival: distillation gives the sour fruit its destiny, steeped with sugar into the ruby cordial that warms winter parlours.
- 18th–19th c. — the country bottle: sloe gin becomes the squire’s and keeper’s drink, hedgerow harvest turned Christmas ritual.
- 20th c. — song and colour: sloe-eyed beauties fill lyrics and novels, borrowing the fruit’s bloom-dark blue for romance.
- Today — foraging revived: sloes headline autumn gathering, gin-making, and hedgerow conservation, wild food as heritage and pleasure.
