• stopped: Similar because it means ceased action; differs because stopped is neutral and general, desisted is formal and implies deliberate refraining from.
  • ceased: Similar because it means came to an end; differs because ceased can be intransitive or transitive and is slightly less tied to from + gerund.
  • quit: Similar because it means gave up an activity; differs because quit is informal and often implies leaving a job or habit permanently.
  • abandoned: Similar because it means gave up; differs because abandoned implies desertion or leaving behind, often with loss, while desisted implies standing off.
  • discontinued: Similar because it means ended a practice or product; differs because discontinued is commercial-administrative and transitive.
  • refrained: Similar because it means held back from; differs because refrained is prospective (did not start), desisted is interruptive (stopped after starting).
  • abstained: Similar because it means voluntarily held off; differs because abstain typically concerns voting, food or pleasures, not ongoing nuisances.
  • relented: Similar because it means softened and stopped pressing; differs because relenting is emotional yielding, desisting is behavioural stopping.
  • halted: Similar because it means brought to a stop; differs because halted is often transitive and abrupt, desisted is intransitive and deliberate.
  • suspended: Similar because it means temporarily stopped; differs because suspension implies possible resumption by authority.
  • forbore: Similar because it means held back; differs because forbore is archaic-literary and prospective.
  • knocked off: Similar because informal for stopped; differs because it is slang, abrupt and familiar versus formal desisted.
