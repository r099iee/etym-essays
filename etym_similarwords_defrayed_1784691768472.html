• Pay: Similar because the core act is the same — giving money to settle a cost; differs because pay is the neutral general verb for all payment, while defray is formal, contractual, and implies discharging a stated expense in full.
  • Settle: Similar because both mean to clear an amount owing and leave nothing outstanding; differs because settle belongs to debts and disputes of all kinds, while defray is specifically about paying an expense or cost.
  • Disburse: Similar because both mean to pay out money; differs because disburse typically means paying out from an official fund or budget to many recipients, while defray assumes responsibility for a specific identified cost.
  • Reimburse: Similar because both involve meeting a cost; differs because reimburse repays someone who has already spent their own money, while defray pays the expense outright, in advance, or on another's behalf.
  • Cover: Similar because both mean to provide money sufficient for a cost; differs because cover is informal and implies mere sufficiency ("to cover the cost"), while defray is contractual and implies a formal, fully discharged obligation.
  • Fund: Similar because both mean to supply money for something; differs because fund commonly refers to financing a project, purchase, or venture, while defray is about meeting a specific expense or charge.
  • Finance: Similar because both involve providing money for an undertaking; differs because finance suggests arranging loans or capital for large projects, while defray is the more everyday act of paying an expense.
  • Foot (the bill): Similar because both mean to accept and pay a cost; differs because foot the bill is colloquial and implies paying reluctantly or as the last resort, while defray is neutral-formal and contractual.
  • Bear: Similar because both can mean to take on and carry the cost of something ("to bear the expenses"); differs because bear stresses endurance and responsibility, while defray stresses the actual payment and settlement of the sum.
  • Discharge: Similar because both mean to clear an obligation in full; differs because discharge is the legal and military word for clearing a debt or duty entirely, while defray is narrower, referring specifically to expenses and costs.
  • Stump up: Similar because both mean to hand over money to meet a cost; differs because stump up is heavily colloquial British slang for grudgingly paying up, the polar opposite of defray's formal, willing, contractual register.
