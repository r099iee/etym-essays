• commandments vs. commands: Similar because both order obedience; differs because commands are immediate specific orders, while commandments are enduring moral precepts with biblical weight.
  • precepts: Similar because they are authoritative rules of conduct; differs because precepts are formal and didactic without necessary biblical echo, while commandments invoke Sinai.
  • injunctions: Similar because they authoritatively order or forbid; differs because injunctions are often legal and singular, whereas commandments come as a memorized moral set.
  • edicts: Similar because they are authoritative decrees; differs because edicts issue from a ruler or government, while commandments claim higher moral or divine sanction.
  • decrees: Similar because they bind by authority; differs because decrees are official legal or governmental acts, less personal and less memorized than commandments.
  • dictates: Similar because they command absolutely; differs because dictates often come from conscience, reason, or authority in the abstract, not from tablets or catechisms.
  • rules: Similar because they regulate conduct; differs because rules are generic and often procedural, while commandments moralize and monumentalize.
  • tenets: Similar because they are held principles of a group; differs because tenets are believed doctrines, whereas commandments are obeyed orders.
  • maxims: Similar because they are short memorable moral sayings; differs because maxims advise prudence, while commandments demand obedience.
  • ordinances: Similar because they are authoritative laws; differs because ordinances are civic and codified, lacking the religious memorization-chant of commandments.
