- 14th–16th c. (Middle to Early Modern English) - Compound formed in agricultural and equestrian trade to denote harness straps securing draft carts and plows.
- 17th–18th c. (Veterinary & Domestic Medicine) - Expanded into human medicine to describe the flannel or linen binder wrapped around an infant's abdomen to protect the umbilical navel.
- 19th c. (Victorian Equine Prose & Driving) - Widely used in coaching literature, stable manuals, and literary realism (Tolstoy, Hardy) to depict the daily mechanics of horse management.
- 20th–21st c. (Modern Publishing & Specialty Gear) - Adopted into printing and publishing as the English translation for the Japanese *obi* (the decorative paper promotional band wrapped around a book cover), while continuing in saddlery and veterinary care.
