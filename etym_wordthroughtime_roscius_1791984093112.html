- 80 BCE — The name enters the record as that of an acquitted defendant; for contemporaries it is simply an Umbrian clan name, notable only because the trial was political.
- 1st c. BCE — Cicero publishes the speech, and the actor Quintus Roscius Gallus makes the same nomen famous on the stage; the two bearers are already confused by later readers.
- 16th–17th c. — English schoolboys meet the name in Cicero's speech; “Roscius” begins to be used allusively for a great actor in the theatre and in commendatory verse.
- 18th c. — “The English Roscius” becomes a stock compliment for David Garrick, fixing the byword in theatrical journalism.
- 19th–20th c. — The byword fades; the name settles into classical scholarship and legal history, where “the Roscius case” means the trial of 80 BCE.
- 21st c. — Bare “Roscius” is chiefly a citation form in studies of Cicero, the proscriptions and Roman criminal procedure; the actor sense survives only in antiquarian writing.
