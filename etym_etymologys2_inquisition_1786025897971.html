Latin quaerere

"to seek, look for, ask, demand"
(attested classical; origin unknown — no secure
Indo-European ancestor has been established)

↓

Latin inquirere

"to search into, examine, investigate"
(in- "into" + quaerere "to seek"; classical)

↓

Latin inquisitio (inquisitiōnis)

"a searching into; judicial investigation,
legal inquiry" (from the participle inquisitus
of inquirere; classical)

↓

Old French inquisition

"investigation, inquest"
(12th–13th c.)

↓

Middle English inquisicioun

"judicial inquiry, official investigation,
ecclesiastical tribunal" (attested 14th c.)

↓

INQUISITION

"severe interrogation; official investigation;
(capitalized) the Church tribunals against heresy"

Note: no reliable Indo-European root for Latin quaerere has been established; proposals connecting it with roots for "acquire" or "seek" are speculative. The recorded history therefore begins with Latin, not with PIE.
