Chain for the compound and its descendants
Latin (2nd c. BCE)
perīre
"to go through, pass away,
be destroyed"
(per + īre; Plautus: perii! "I am done for!")
↓
Old French (10th c.)
perir
"to die, be destroyed"
(present stem periss-,
from inchoative *perīscere)
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
perishen
"to die, be destroyed"
↓
English (17th c.)
perishable
"liable to decay or die"
↓
English (c. 1814)
IMPERISHABLE
"not liable to perish;
everlasting"
(in- "not" + perishable)

Note: The negation prefix in- descends from PIE *ne "not"; the adjective perishable itself (formed in English from perish + -able, cf. Old French perissable) is first recorded in the 17th century, and imperishable follows in the early 19th century.
