- 1400s–1500s: [Military Disorder] - Emerged during the Hundred Years' War specifically for soldiers wandering away from ranks to forage or drop behind.
- 1500s–1700s: [General Travel & Flocks] - Broadened to encompass pilgrims, pack animals, and wandering travelers falling behind their party on rough roads.
- 1700s–1900s: [Landscape & Aesthetics] - Adopted in Romantic literature for winding woodland paths, untamed creeping vines, and messy locks of hair.
- 1900s–Present: [Urban Sprawl & Temporal Flow] - Applied to sporadic arrivals of latecomers and low-density ribbons of unplanned suburban development.
