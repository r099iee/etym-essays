Note on historical debate: Traditional Sanskrit grammarians and Purāṇic etymologists (most notably in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa and Bhāgavata Purāṇa) derive Mithilā from the verbal root math- / manth- ("to churn, produce by friction"), linking it to the founding king Mithi, who was born from the churning of the lifeless body of King Nimi. However, modern comparative philologists and linguists also point to the Vedic adverbial root mith- / mithas ("mutually, together, paired, in reciprocal contact", from PIE *mey-), suggesting the city's name originally designated an auspicious "place of confluence, meeting, or paired kingdoms" along the fertile river systems of the Sadānīrā and Gaṅgā basins.
