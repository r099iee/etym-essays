- 16th–17th Century: Spanish (*carapacho*) and Portuguese (*carapaça*) explorers document the colossal shells of American and West Indian sea turtles and tortoises.
- 18th Century: French naturalists during the Enlightenment (Buffon, Cuvier) adopt *carapace* into formal zoological taxonomy for chelonian and crustacean dorsal shields.
- 19th Century: Borrowed into English natural science (1830s, popularized by Darwin in *Voyage of the Beagle*), establishing precise anatomical usage.
- 20th–21st Century: Extensively adopted into psychoanalytic, literary, and sociotechnical prose to describe rigid emotional defenses, calcified institutions, and biomimetic shells.
