- Early 13th c.: Formed around the charismatic preaching, radical poverty, and ecological sermons of St. Francis of Assisi in Umbria, Italy.
- 14th c. to 16th c.: Developed into a major institutional and scholastic tradition within the Catholic Church, shaping European theology, mysticism, and Renaissance painting.
- 17th c. to 19th c.: Anglicized and discussed in historical, ecclesiastical, and literary contexts to describe mendicant spirituality and Franciscan philosophy.
- 20th c.: Reinterpreted by Christian existentialists, environmental ethicists (St. Francis named patron saint of ecologists in 1979), and post-structuralist theorists (Roland Barthes) as a metaphor for radical openness, non-violence, and textual hospitality.
