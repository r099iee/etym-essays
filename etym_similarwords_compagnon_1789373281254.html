• Genette: Similar because Gérard Genette was a towering French structuralist and narratologist whose foundational analyses of Proust's narrative temporality in Figures III (*Narrative Discourse*) transformed literary studies; differs because Genette focused on hyper-formalized architectural taxonomy (focalization, analepsis, prolepsis, voice) divorced from authorial biography, whereas Compagnon historicizes Proust within cultural, philological, and ideological shifts between centuries.
  • Barthes: Similar because Roland Barthes was Compagnon's doctoral mentor at the École des Hautes Études en Sciences Sociales and shared a deep passion for Proust, Baudelaire, and the semiotics of style; differs because Barthes operated through intuitive, avant-garde post-structuralist aphorisms, the "death of the author," and radical semiotic experiments, whereas Compagnon adopted a more skeptical, measured, and archival historical scholarship that interrogated theory's dogmatism.
  • Poulet: Similar because Georges Poulet was a celebrated Geneva School thematic critic who analyzed the phenomenological experience of human time, space, and memory across French literature, including Proust; differs because Poulet focused purely on the inner consciousness and psychological cogito of authors without reference to external social contexts, whereas Compagnon rigorously grounds textual aesthetics in philological, political, and institutional histories.
  • Starobinski: Similar because Jean Starobinski was an esteemed Swiss literary critic, intellectual historian, and physician who brought extraordinary breadth to the history of ideas, bodily melancholy, and critical transparency in French letters; differs because Starobinski centered on the medical-philosophical intersections of the Enlightenment and Rousseauian interiority, whereas Compagnon's primary arenas are nineteenth- and twentieth-century French literary modernism, Proust, and the epistemology of critical theory.
  • Tadié: Similar because Jean-Yves Tadié is another titan of Proustian scholarship, director of the landmark 1987 Pléiade edition of À la recherche du temps perdu, and Proust's foremost modern biographer; differs because Tadié focuses on comprehensive biographical reconstruction, manuscript genesis (genetic criticism), and stylistic poetics, whereas Compagnon focuses on theoretical tension, intellectual history, and the philosophical dialectic between realism and allegory.
  • Blanchot: Similar because Maurice Blanchot wrote profoundly on the paradoxes of literary language, silence, and the eternal return in Proust and modern European literature; differs because Blanchot's writing was darkly philosophical, hermetic, and post-Heideggerian, viewing literature as an impossible encounter with the Void, whereas Compagnon writes with classical clarity, academic wit, archival precision, and common-sense engagement.
