• Compel: Similar because both mean forcing someone to act; differs because compel stresses external necessity or authority (law, threat, circumstance) that leaves little choice, while impel emphasizes an inner drive coming from the person's own feelings.
  • Urge: Similar because both press someone to act; differs because urge is milder and usually interpersonal persuasion addressed to another person, lacking the sense of sustained inner propulsion of impel.
  • Drive: Similar because both push toward action; differs because drive is broader and more colloquial, and can be external (fear drives him), where impel keeps a formal, inward nuance.
  • Force: Similar because both apply pressure to make action happen; differs because force implies overriding resistance by power or violence, while impel can be an inner motive that does not overpower the will.
  • Coerce: Similar because both cause action against resistance; differs because coerce is specifically about threats and the exercise of power, and is far more negative than impel's moral-psychological tone.
  • Prompt: Similar because both cause something to happen; differs because a prompt is a light cue or trigger, not a sustained push of felt necessity, and it lacks impel's force.
  • Push: Similar because both imply propulsion; differs because push is concrete, physical and casual, lacking the Latinate, literary weight of impel in its moral sense.
  • Propel: Similar because both mean driving forward; differs because propel is chiefly physical and spatial (rockets, boats, mechanisms), where impel covers motives and duties.
