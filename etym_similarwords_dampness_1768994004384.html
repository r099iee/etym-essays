• Moisture: Similar because both denote slight wetness; differs because moisture is the broad neutral scientific term, often desirable, while dampness is the perceptible chill of absorbed water, felt rather than seen and usually unwelcome.
  • Wetness: Similar because both name the presence of liquid; differs because wetness is the state of being covered or saturated, more extreme and usually visible, while dampness is subtle and pervasive.
  • Humidity: Similar because both concern water in the air; differs because humidity is the quantitative meteorological measure of water vapour, while dampness is the sensory experience of it clinging to surfaces and skin.
  • Dankness: Similar because both describe a damp, uncomfortable atmosphere; differs because dankness adds a gloomy, unhealthy, gothic colour — the dampness of a dungeon — while dampness itself is cooler and more neutral.
  • Clamminess: Similar because both describe unpleasant wetness; differs because clamminess is specifically the cold, sticky feel on the skin, while dampness is the general absorbed wetness of air and surfaces.
  • Mugginess: Similar because both describe wetness in the air; differs because mugginess is the warm, oppressive, sticky humidity of hot weather, while dampness is cool, heavy, and seeping.
  • Sogginess: Similar because both describe absorption of water; differs because sogginess implies saturation, soddenness of a substance, while dampness is slight, surface, and atmospheric.
  • Condensation: Similar because both name water collecting in air or on surfaces; differs because condensation is the scientific process of vapour turning to liquid, while dampness is the resulting state, felt and unquantified.
  • Moistness: Similar because both are slight wetness; differs because moistness is gentle, pleasant, and often desirable (a moist cake), while dampness is chillier and faintly unpleasant.
  • Dewiness: Similar because both describe light wetness; differs because dewiness is fresh, delicate, and appealing, associated with morning dew, while dampness is heavy, stagnant, and pervasive.
  • Damp: Similar because both denote the same wetness; differs because damp is the adjective or simple noun, while dampness is the abstract noun naming the state or condition.
  • Wateriness: Similar because both describe wetness; differs because wateriness implies being thin, dilute, or full of visible water, while dampness is the subtle absorbed wetness of air and surface.
