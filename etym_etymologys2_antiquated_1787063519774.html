Note: The formation of Latin antiquus from ante is standard, but scholars debate whether the suffix is a re-bracketing of an earlier anticus (“at the front”) with -quus, and whether the sense “ancient” developed from “that which lies before (in time).” These nuances do not affect the basic chain. Note also that English antique entered via Old French in the 16th c. from the same Latin base, while antiquated was built in English.
