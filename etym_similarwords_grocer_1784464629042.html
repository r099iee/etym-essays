• Shopkeeper: Similar because both are people who sell goods at retail; differs because shopkeeper is the near-general term for any shop owner, while grocer is specifically tied to food and household provisions with a bulk-trade history.
  • Merchant: Similar because both are sellers of goods; differs because a merchant is broader and may be grand, wholesale, or international, while a grocer is the homely local seller of provisions.
  • Tradesman: Similar because both name a commercial person; differs because tradesman covers many crafts and manual trades, not specifically food and household provisions.
  • Greengrocer: Similar because both are food retailers sharing the -grocer element; differs because a greengrocer sells fresh fruit and vegetables, while a grocer deals in the wider range of dry and tinned provisions.
  • Purveyor: Similar because both can denote a supplier of provisions; differs because purveyor is grander, more formal, and often institutional, while grocer is ordinary and domestic.
  • Provisioner: Similar because both supply food and stores; differs because provisioner emphasizes the wholesale or logistical supply of stores (ships, armies, expeditions), while grocer is the retail shopkeeper of the high street.
  • Dealer: Similar because both are traders in goods; differs because dealer is generic and can apply to any commodity, while grocer specifically sells food and household goods at the shop counter.
  • Retailer: Similar because both sell goods to the end customer; differs because retailer is an abstract, modern business term for any kind of shop, while grocer carries the warm, old-fashioned, food-specific image.
