• Onöndowáʼga꞉: Similar because both refer to the identical indigenous nation of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy; differs because Onöndowáʼga꞉ ("People of the Great Hill") is the sovereign indigenous autonym in the Seneca language, whereas Seneca is an exonym derived from Algonquian and anglicized through colonial contact.
  • Haudenosaunee: Similar because both designate the same cultural and political community; differs because Haudenosaunee ("People of the Longhouse") is the confederate name for the entire Six Nations League, of which the Seneca nation constitutes the westernmost member.
  • Iroquois: Similar because both refer to this indigenous confederacy; differs because Iroquois is a broader French-mediated colonial exonym encompassing all constituent nations (Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca, Tuscarora), whereas Seneca specifies one sovereign nation.
  • Mohawk: Similar because both are foundational nations of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy; differs because the Mohawk (*Kanienʼkehá꞉ka*) are the "Keepers of the Eastern Door" situated along the Hudson and Mohawk rivers, whereas the Seneca are the "Keepers of the Western Door."
  • Cayuga: Similar because both are sister nations within the western branch of the League; differs because the Cayuga (*Gayogo̱hó꞉nǫʼ*, "People of the Great Swamp") are the Seneca's immediate eastern neighbors, historically designated as the "Younger Brothers" in Grand Council protocol.
  • Onondaga: Similar because both are constituent Iroquois nations; differs because the Onondaga (*Onöñda’gega’*, "People of the Hills") are the "Keepers of the Council Fire" and the central capital nation of the Confederacy.
  • Oneida: Similar because both belong to the Six Nations; differs because the Oneida (*Onyota'a:ka*, "People of the Standing Stone") sit as "Younger Brothers" in the eastern quadrant, possessing their own distinct territory and clan lines.
  • Tuscarora: Similar because both are Iroquoian-speaking nations of the League; differs because the Tuscarora migrated northward from North Carolina after colonial wars in the early eighteenth century, being formally admitted as the Sixth Nation under the sponsorship of the Oneida.
