Note on historical debate & gender morphology: In traditional French grammar, protégé is the masculine past participle and noun, while protégée is the feminine form. In 18th- and 19th-century English borrowing, careful writers strictly maintained the distinction (e.g. referring to a female pupil as a protégée). In contemporary English, however, descriptive usage exhibits a strong tendency to use protégé as a gender-neutral noun, although style guides (such as the Chicago Manual of Style and Oxford) still acknowledge the feminine spelling when honoring French orthographic origins.
