- Early 1400s to squirt as to eject in a jet - practical, bodily perception; of water, blood, and humours spurting from narrow openings
- 1500s–1600s noun for jet and syringe - technical-domestic perception; squirts for perfumes, medicines, and garden watering; also sea-squirt for the ascidian
- 1800s schoolboy and naval slang for a stripling - patronising perception; a little squirt is a junior midshipman, clerk, or schoolboy who presumes above his size
- Early 1900s Wodehousian little squirt - comic-indulgent perception; Angela the little squirt is offensive yet unserious, an upper-class tease rather than a curse
- Today dual everyday and teasing use - neutral-technical for liquid, affectionately dismissive for people; little squirt survives as mild, almost fond insult
