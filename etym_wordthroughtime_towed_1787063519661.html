- Old English togian "to drag, pull, haul" - a plain, rough verb of effort, used of dragging heavy loads on land.
- By the age of sail - "to tow" becomes a nautical verb: pulling a vessel by a rope from another boat; towing is essential for entering harbours and river mouths against wind and current.
- 18th–19th c. - the canal age: towpaths and towboats; towing barges by horse or steamer becomes a vast commercial operation, and "towage" enters law and commerce.
- 20th c. - the motor age: tow trucks and towing cars, caravans and trailers; aero-towing of gliders.
- Modern - "towed" is the neutral past form of the standard verb, at home on water, road, and air alike; the older "drag/pull" senses have been absorbed by "haul" and "tug".
