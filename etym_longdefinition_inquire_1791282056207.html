inquire — and its common Commonwealth spelling enquire — means to seek information by asking, to make a formal request for knowledge, or to investigate something systematically. At its core it describes a directed act of questioning: one party lacks knowledge and addresses another party, a record, or a phenomenon in order to close that gap. The word carries a distinctly formal, polite, and often procedural register, which separates it from casual ask. One asks a friend the time; one inquires about a job vacancy, a patient's condition, train times, or the cause of an accident. In institutional contexts it implies method, documentation, and seriousness of purpose rather than idle curiosity.

In contemporary usage the verb operates transitively and intransitively, often with prepositions that encode its frame. Inquire about something means to request facts concerning it ("She inquired about the scholarship deadline"). Inquire after someone means to ask about their health or welfare ("He inquired after your mother"), a courteous, slightly traditional formula. Inquire into means to investigate formally and at length, typically by a committee, journalist, court, or scholar ("The panel will inquire into the safety failures"). Inquire whether / if / when / why introduces an indirect question ("Might I inquire whether seats are still available?"). This last pattern explains the fragmentary literary context "Might I inquire —", a polite interruption formula in dialogue.

British usage traditionally reserves a spelling distinction: enquire for general asking and inquire for formal investigation, though the distinction is collapsing and American English uses inquire for both. Reflexive and passive extensions exist: "to inquire of someone" (to direct a question to them), "to inquire within" (a notice inviting interested persons to ask inside a shop or office). As a noun family, inquiry (or enquiry) denotes both the act and the institutional process — from a brief customer enquiry to a full public inquiry with witnesses, evidence, and published findings.

  • To ask politely / request information: the primary modern sense — to pose a question in order to learn something. Example: "Visitors should inquire at the front desk." The semantic bridge from Latin quaerere "to seek" is direct: questioning is verbal seeking. This sense dominates everyday formal interaction, customer service, hospitality, and correspondence ("Thank you for inquiring").
  • To investigate formally / conduct an inquiry: to examine systematically, often with authority. Example: "The commission inquired into allegations of corruption." This developed because repeated questioning becomes methodical investigation; institutions that ask questions under oath are said to inquire. It underlies inquiry as a legal-administrative proceeding in the UK, Canada, and Australia.
  • To inquire after (someone's health / welfare): a socially coded courtesy sense. Example: "She inquired after his father." Historically this reflects the etiquette of calling cards and servants asking after the household; the preposition after preserves an older sense of seeking someone out.
  • Scientific / scholarly inquiry: in philosophy and education, inquiry denotes disciplined, open-ended investigation — "inquiry-based learning," "scientific inquiry," "the spirit of inquiry." Here the word sheds its interrogative-to-a-person frame and means pursuit of truth through evidence and reasoning, echoing the Latin sense of seeking generally, not merely asking.
  • Commercial / routine enquiry: in business, an enquiry is a routine request for a catalogue, quotation, availability, or booking. "We received 200 enquiries about the flat." This jargon sense is deliberately soft and non-committal: it signals interest without commitment, a crucial pragmatic nuance in sales pipelines.
  • Reflexive / self-inquiry (to inquire within / of oneself): a rarer, literary-philosophical extension meaning introspection — to question one's own motives or conscience. "He inquired within himself whether he had acted justly." The bridge is metaphorical: the self is treated as a witness to be examined.

Tone and register deserve emphasis for learners. Inquire is formal, respectful, and distancing; used among friends it can sound stiff or ironic. "Might I inquire—" in fiction typically marks deference, hesitation, or a character steeling themselves to ask something delicate, exactly as the truncated context suggests. In customer-facing language it softens intrusion: "May I inquire what brings you here?" feels less abrupt than "What do you want?" Mastery therefore means knowing not only what it denotes but when its politeness is appropriate and when plain ask is more natural.
