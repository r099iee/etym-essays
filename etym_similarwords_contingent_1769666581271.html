• Conditional: Similar because both mean dependent on specified terms; differs because conditional stresses agreed human stipulations ("conditional on their consent"), while contingent stresses chance or unforeseen circumstances.
  • Dependent: Similar because both mean relying on another factor; differs because dependent is far broader (economic, grammatical, social reliance) and neutral, while contingent specifically implies uncertainty.
  • Accidental: Similar because both imply not necessary; differs because accidental means happening by chance or mishap, while contingent describes the state of depending on an uncertain outcome.
  • Fortuitous: Similar because both involve chance; differs because fortuitous describes lucky happenstance, while contingent describes conditionality, not luck.
  • Possible: Similar because both concern what may occur; differs because possible is a simple modal ("could happen"), while contingent specifies dependence on another uncertain factor.
  • Uncertain: Similar because both imply unknown outcome; differs because uncertain describes ignorance of the future, while contingent analyzes the future’s dependence on conditions.
  • Incidental: Similar because both mean secondary or happening by the way; differs because incidental means accompanying something as a minor side-effect, while contingent means conditionally dependent.
  • Provisional: Similar because both imply not final; differs because provisional means temporary until conditions are met, while contingent emphasizes that occurrence hangs on chance events.
  • Delegation (noun): Similar because both name a group acting for a larger body; differs because delegation is officially appointed with authority to represent, while contingent is any subgroup contributed or present, not necessarily empowered to speak.
  • Detachment (noun): Similar because both name a group separated from a main force; differs because detachment is specifically a military unit sent on separate duty, while contingent is a contributed share of a combined force.
  • Group (noun): Similar because both name a collection of people; differs because group is a neutral general word with no connotation of forming part of a larger assembly.
  • Corps (noun): Similar because both name organized bodies; differs because corps is a specific military or organizational structure with a defined composition.
  • Cohort (noun): Similar because both name a distinct sub-group; differs because cohort in modern use means a demographic or study group sharing a characteristic, not a contributed unit.
  • Share / Quota: Similar because the noun sense derives from a contributed share; differs because share and quota name the proportion, while contingent names the body of people constituting that share.
