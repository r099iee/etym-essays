Noun sense — the horse:
Middle English (c. 1400)
nagge
‘small horse, pony’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
nag
‘inferior horse; worn-out hack’
↓
Verb sense — to pester:
Scandinavian dialects (medieval to early modern)
nagga, gnaga forms
‘to gnaw, grumble, pester’
↓
English and Scots (early 1800s)
nag
‘to find fault continually’
↓
Modern English (current)
nag
‘sorry horse; to pester’

Note: both halves of this chain are debated: the noun's exact donor language is uncertain, and whether the verb descends from the horse-word or from an independent Scandinavian gnaw-word is disputed, so the two senses are shown side by side without asserting a single shared root.
