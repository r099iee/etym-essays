↓
Middle English (12th-15th c.)
stingen
"to pierce, prick; to sting (of bees, nettles)"
↓
Modern English (present)
sting
"to wound with a sharp-pointed organ; to burn sharply"
↓
Modern English (-ing suffix)
STINGING
"causing a sharp, burning, pricking pain"

Note: outside Germanic the verb has no secure cognates; proposed links to Greek stizō ("to prick, tattoo", PIE *stig-) or Latin īnstīgāre ("to goad") are debated but unproven, so the PIE stage is flagged as a reconstruction. The modern -ing form conflates the Old English present participle (-ende) and gerund (-ung/-ing).
