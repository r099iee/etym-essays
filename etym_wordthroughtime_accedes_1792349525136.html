- [Late 14th–15th c.] - borrowed from Latin accēdere into Middle English, originally signifying physical movement: to approach, draw near, or come to a person or place - [perception: understood as a literal physical spatial verb of approach or arrival]
- [15th–16th c.] - expanded to describe intellectual and moral agreement: to assent, give adherence to an opinion, or yield to a request or argument - [perception: adopted into formal deliberative and scholarly registers as a dignified verb of intellectual consensus or reluctant agreement]
- [16th–17th c.] - specialized in constitutional and monarchical law to describe the formal assumption of sovereign authority, as in 'accede to the throne' or 'accede to high office' - [perception: invested with royal majesty and formal constitutional gravity]
- [18th–19th c.] - established in international diplomacy and statecraft for joining a treaty, alliance, or multilateral covenant already concluded by other states - [perception: regarded as an essential diplomatic term denoting formal, binding international accession]
- [20th–21st c.] - physical senses become entirely obsolete; predominant in modern prose to denote formal yielding to demands, joining international agreements, or succeeding to sovereign titles - [perception: perceived as an elevated, formal verb conveying solemn agreement, yielding under pressure, or formal assumption of authority]
