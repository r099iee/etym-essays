Fijians denotes the people of Fiji — and, crucially, two overlapping groups that colonial and postcolonial usage has struggled to disentangle: (1) Indigenous Fijians / iTaukei (Melanesian-Pacific Islanders speaking Fijian dialects, the archipelago's first settlers from ~1500 BCE, custodians of vanua land and chiefly hierarchy), and (2) Fiji nationals / Fiji Islanders broadly, including the large Indo-Fijian community descended from indentured labourers brought by Britain (1879-1916) plus Rotumans, Chinese, Europeans, and others. In your passage — The Fijians complained that the flesh of whites was too salty — the term reflects 19th-century European travel writing about Indigenous Fijian warriors in the era before (and during) Christianisation, when ritual cannibalism (bokola, eating of enemies) was reported by missionaries and traders.

Geography and culture anchor the word: Fiji comprises 300+ islands (Viti Levu, Vanua Levu, Taveuni, Kadavu) at the Melanesia-Polynesia crossroads, independent since 1970 (republic since 1987), population ~900,000, capital Suva, languages English, Fijian (Bauan), and Fiji Hindi. Traditional Fijian society is organised by vanua (land-people-chiefdom), yavusa/mataqali clans, hereditary chiefs (turaga, ratu/adi), kava (yaqona) ceremony, firewalking (Sawau), tapa (masi) cloth, and Methodist hymnody. Coloniallabel Fijian thus carries legal weight: the 2013 Constitution declares all citizens Fijians, while iTaukei reserves Indigenous status, land rights, and Great Council of Chiefs membership — a contested distinction after four coups (1987, 2000, 2006).

Polysemy and usage layers:

  • Ethnic sense (narrow): Indigenous Austronesian people of Fiji — a Fijian village, Fijian warriors, Fijian tapa. Emphasis is ancestry, language, custom. Opposite: Indo-Fijian (South Asian descent), Rotuman, expatriate.
  • National-civic sense (broad): any citizen of Fiji regardless of ethnicity — Fijians voted, the Fijian rugby team (Flying Fijians). Post-2013 official usage pushes this inclusive sense; everyday speech still often says Fijian for iTaukei and Indian for Indo-Fijians.
  • Linguistic sense: Fijian (language) = Austronesian, Central Pacific, Bauan-based standard with many dialects; Fijian Hindi / Fiji Baat is distinct. To speak Fijian usually means the Indigenous language.
  • Adjectival sense: of Fiji — Fijian beaches, Fijian coups, Fijian kava bowl. Covers geography, politics, cuisine (kokoda, lovo).
  • Historical-exotic sense (as in your source): 19th-century the Fijians as warrior-cannibal stereotype in Frazer, Williams, and missionary memoirs — tattooed, club-wielding, cannibal forks (icula), chiefly ovens. Modern scholarship reads these accounts critically as partly eyewitness, partly colonial sensationalism justifying annexation (1874) and evangelisation.

Grammatically Fijians is the regular plural of Fijian (a Fijian / two Fijians); attributive Fijian is invariable. Collocations: Indigenous Fijians, Indo-Fijians, Flying Fijians (rugby), Fijian Pijin, Fijian Holdings. Register is neutral contemporary when qualified (Indigenous vs Indo-Fijian vs Fijian national); unqualified historical Fijians in cannibalism passages must be glossed with postcolonial care. The demonym bridges Austronesian voyaging heritage, plantation diaspora, and Pacific modernity in one short -ian word.
