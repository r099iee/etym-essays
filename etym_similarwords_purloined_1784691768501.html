• steal: Similar because both denote taking another's property without permission, and purloined is simply the literary form of "stolen"; differs because steal is the neutral, universal, everyday verb usable for any scale or style of theft, while purloined is elevated, literary, and implies secret, artful removal rather than open or violent taking.
  • pilfered: Similar because both mean taken furtively and without permission; differs because pilfered suggests small, repeated, petty thefts (shop-pilfering, nicking trifles), whereas purloined suggests a single artful removal of something of value, carried out with cunning.
  • filched: Similar because both name furtive stealing; differs because filched is sly, quick, and colloquial (something snatched in a moment), while purloined is formal and theatrical, connoting craft and deliberation rather than speed.
  • pinched: Similar because both denote theft in contexts of trickery or sleight of hand; differs because pinched is British slang, casual and mundane, while purloined belongs to formal or mock-heroic prose.
  • nicked: Similar because both mean taken illegally and often secretly; differs because nicked is informal slang (chiefly British, also "arrested"), entirely colloquial, while purloined is literary and dignified.
  • appropriated: Similar because both can denote taking something not one's own for one's own use; differs because appropriated is bureaucratic, legal, and often euphemistic (money "appropriated" from a fund), without the overt criminal or stealthy flavour of purloined.
  • misappropriated: Similar because both denote wrongful taking of another's property; differs because misappropriated is a technical legal term for diverting funds or assets (often by one trusted with them), while purloined stresses the physical, secret removal of a thing.
  • lifted: Similar because both can mean stolen furtively (as in shoplifting); differs because lifted is informal and applies especially to shoplifting, while purloined is literary and suggests artful, gentlemanly theft.
  • swiped: Similar because both denote taking something without permission; differs because swiped is casual American slang implying a quick grab, whereas purloined is elevated and implies stealthy craftsmanship.
  • embezzled: Similar because both denote the wrongful taking of property entrusted to one; differs because embezzled is a precise legal term for fraud by a fiduciary (cashier, treasurer), while purloined is the general literary word for secret theft.
