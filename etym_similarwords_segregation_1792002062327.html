• Separation: Similar because both refer to the state or act of setting things or people apart; differs because separation is a neutral, universal term applying to any physical, emotional, or conceptual division, whereas segregation carries intense institutional, legal, systemic, or scientific connotations of enforced boundary-drawing.
  • Isolation: Similar because both involve detaching an entity from its surrounding environment; differs because isolation focuses on keeping an individual or group entirely alone, quarantined, or cut off from all outside contact, whereas segregation divides an entire society or material into separate internal categories that coexist within the same broader system.
  • Apartheid: Similar because both describe systematic racial separation enforced by the state apparatus; differs because Apartheid (literally "apartness" in Afrikaans) was a specific, totalizing legal and constitutional regime implemented in South Africa between 1948 and 1994 that stripped non-white populations of citizenship rights, whereas segregation is a broader, universal phenomenon that occurs in social, racial, sexual, and physical contexts worldwide.
  • Partition: Similar because both involve dividing entities into distinct segments; differs because partition typically refers to the geopolitical division of a territory or nation into distinct independent sovereign states (such as the 1947 Partition of India), whereas segregation maintains divided groups within a single civic or physical framework.
  • Discrimination: Similar because both are central mechanisms of social injustice and inequality; differs because discrimination refers to the prejudicial treatment, actions, or denial of rights directed against individuals based on their identity, whereas segregation is the spatial and institutional structure of physical separation that often enables such discrimination.
  • Exclusion: Similar because both prevent certain groups from entering specific spaces or privileges; differs because exclusion denotes completely keeping someone out of an institution or organization, whereas segregation accommodates them in separate, typically inferior, parallel institutions.
  • Differentiation: Similar because both involve distinguishing between different categories or elements; differs because differentiation is an analytical, biological, or marketing process of recognizing distinct functions or specialized traits without the coercive or spatial separation inherent in segregation.
  • Compartmentalization: Similar because both involve creating distinct, separated boundaries between components; differs because compartmentalization is primarily applied to psychological defense mechanisms, computer software architecture, or administrative operations rather than social groups or physical mixtures.
