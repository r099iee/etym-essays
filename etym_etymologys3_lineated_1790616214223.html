The evolution of lineated begins not with geometry but with flax, and it shows how a humble plant fibre lent its name to abstract order. Reconstructed PIE *lino- denotes flax and the linen thread spun from it, a word that travelled with Neolithic agriculture and Bronze Age textile trade rather than with regular sound laws alone. Ancient Greek linon meant both the plant and the thread, cord, net, and sail-cloth made from it, while Latin linum preserved the same agricultural sense. From linum Latin derived linea, originally with understood femina, meaning a thing made of linen, a thread, string, plumb-line, or fishing line. Roman builders stretched a linen cord dusted with chalk to mark straight courses, sailors used a linea for sounding, and tailors for measuring, so that the concrete thread became the standard for straightness itself.

From that builder's cord classical Latin extended linea by metaphor to any narrow stroke, incision, or boundary, and then to the abstract lines of mathematics, genealogy, and writing. Authors speak of a linea of descent, a linea of argument, and lines of script resting as if on an invisible cord. Phonologically the path into English is conservative learned transmission. Old English line, borrowed early from Latin linea via Germanic trade, already meant cord, row, and verse line by around 900, as in lines of genealogy and lines of poetry. Middle English enriched it through Old French ligne for cord, lineage, and verse, reinforcing spelling with i. Early Modern English line exploded polysemously into fishing line, battle line, equator, railway line, and verse line, all united by thread-straightness.

  • Lineate formation 1600s: learned verb lineate, to mark with lines, formed on Latin lineatus, past participle of lineare, to furnish with lines, first recorded in botanical and anatomical descriptions of leaves and shells, with lineated as its participial adjective for finely streaked surfaces.
  • Natural-history codification 1700s-1800s: Linnaean Latin lineatus becomes standard epithet for striped species, Englished as lineated woodpecker, lineated parakeet, lineated beetle, fixing the striped more firmly than the ruled-paper sense.
  • Textual and prosodic extension 1900s: bibliography, linguistics, and creative writing adopt to lineate for dividing prose into intonation lines, and lineated for texts displayed as successive breath units, turning a botanical term into a theory of voice notation central to the source passage.

Socio-culturally lineated therefore straddles two prestige domains that rarely meet, museum taxonomy and poetics. In natural history it signals precise visual diagnosis, a bird is lineated not merely streaky but diagnostically streaked in the manner that defines its species. In poetics it signals a critique of print, that consecutive lineated notation left to right is not transparent speech but a technology that suppresses pitch, timing, and gesture. The computer screen, the author argues, frees the counterculture suppressed by that lineation, allowing branching, stacking, and spacing that linen-thread straightness had forbidden. The humble flax thread thus ends as a metaphor for literacy itself, a straight cord that both guides and binds the voice.
