Cannes is a proper noun denoting a resort city on the French Riviera (Côte d'Azur) in the Alpes-Maritimes department, Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur, situated on the crescent of the Gulf of Napoule backed by wooded hills. Population about 75,000, agglomeration larger. In the source sentence Bertie Wooster traces a whole chain of comic disasters — meeting Madeline Bassett, buying a white mess jacket, Angela meeting her shark, Aunt Dahlia playing baccarat — to a single fateful visit to Cannes, the interwar playground of the English upper classes.

Geography and history:

  • Ancient port: probably a small Ligurian harbour, then Roman outpost on Le Suquet hill (Roman tombs). Name recorded as Canua (10th c.) and De Portu Canue (1030). Le Suquet's 11th-century tower overlooked reedy swamps; the Lérins Islands provided defence and monastic lordship.
  • Fishing village to resort: for 800 years a Provençal fishing and monastic town. In 1834 Lord Chancellor Brougham, detained by cholera quarantine en route to Italy, wintered there, built a villa and publicised its mild climate. Rail (1863), English, Russian and Parisian winterers, grand hotels (Carlton 1911, Martinez 1929), the Croisette promenade and palm-planting created the modern luxury fabric.
  • Festival city: the Cannes International Film Festival (Festival de Cannes, founded 1946) with its Palme d'Or is, with Venice and Berlin, one of the Big Three. To say "a Cannes premiere" or "Cannes prizewinner" invokes global art-cinema prestige. Cannes Lions (advertising), MIPIM and MIPCOM extend the brand to creativity and property.

Secondary and metonymic senses today:

  • Metonym for glamour / deal-making: "Cannes buzz, Cannes deal". The bridge is association: red carpet, paparazzi, yacht parties stand for film commerce itself. Register is journalistic, admiring or sardonic.
  • Metonym for jury taste: "very Cannes" means austere, auteurist, subtitled. The bridge is festival programming history — neorealism, New Wave, world cinema. Antonym is "very Hollywood".
  • Tourist shorthand: "doing Cannes" = Croisette stroll, palace beach, Suquet old town, Lérins boat trip, canne de Provence reeds in gardens. Demonym is Cannois(e). Pronunciation in English varies /kæn/ ~ /kɑːn/; French [kan]; Occitan Canas.
  • Wodehousean Cannes: in Jeeves stories Cannes is where aunts gamble, nephews buy absurd dinner jackets and girls meet sharks (spongers, fortune-hunters). The bridge is historical: in the 1920s–30s the Riviera season mixed aristocrats, parvenus and croupiers, perfect farce fuel. White mess jacket — tropical formal wear worn defiantly on the Riviera — signals Bertie's sartorial floater.

Do not confuse with Baccarat the Lorraine crystal town, with Canas (Occitan form) or with Cannes Lions (the award). Connotation is sunny-luxurious, slightly frivolous and high-stakes — exactly why Bertie blames everything on having gone there.
