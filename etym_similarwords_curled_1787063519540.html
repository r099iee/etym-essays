• Coiled: Similar because both involve winding into a circular, spiral, or helical form; differs because coiled specifically implies tight, orderly, concentric, or spring-like loops (like a rope, spring, or venomous snake prepared to strike), whereas curled encompasses gentler, softer, or irregular curvatures and comfortable resting postures.
  • Twisted: Similar because both involve non-linear deformation; differs because twisted involves rotational torsion around an axis, often resulting in strain, deformation, distortion, or entanglement, while curled is a smooth, continuous bend or roll.
  • Bent: Similar because both denote a departure from a straight line; differs because bent typically implies an angular change of direction or structural flexure under external load, whereas curled denotes a sweeping, circular, or multi-revolution spiral.
  • Spiraled: Similar because both describe helical or vortex geometric pathways; differs because spiraled emphasizes progressive three-dimensional movement along an axis (spiraling upward or downward), while curled often describes static, resting, or planar curved configurations.
  • Wreathed: Similar because both can describe smoke or floral loops; differs because wreathed emphasizes encircling, crowning, or enveloping an object like a garland, whereas curled describes the intrinsic shape of the entity itself.
  • Rolled: Similar because both describe wrapping something upon itself; differs because rolled implies turning over and over or compacting into a cylinder, whereas curled emphasizes the curve, wave, or ringlet structure.
