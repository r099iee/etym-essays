- 16th to 17th Century (Middle & Early Modern Scots) - *Colley* / *coally* appears in Scottish records and pastoral descriptions designating the shepherd's dog, likely derived from *coal* in reference to the black-faced Highland sheep (*colleys*) it tended.
- 18th Century (Scottish Enlightenment & Robert Burns) - Immortalized in Scottish poetry, notably by Robert Burns in *The Twa Dogs* (1786), where the ploughman's wise and honest collie *Luath* serves as a moral contrast to the idle aristocrat's hound.
- 19th Century (Victorian Era) - Popularized worldwide after Queen Victoria acquired collies at Balmoral Castle in the 1860s; the hyphenated compound *collie-dog* entered wide English literature (including Oscar Wilde's *The Remarkable Rocket* in 1888), bifurcating into working stock dogs and aristocratic show-ring breeds.
- 20th Century (Cinema & Canine Cognition) - Immortalized globally as the archetype of unyielding fidelity through Eric Knight's *Lassie Come-Home* (1940); studied extensively in evolutionary psychology as the apex of working canine intelligence.
- 21st Century - Celebrated internationally as premier competitors in agility, flyball, and obedience trials, while remaining an indispensable working asset on sheep farms worldwide.
