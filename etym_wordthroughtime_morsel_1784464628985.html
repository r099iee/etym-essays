13th-Century French Import - Morsel enters Middle English from Old French morsel, a diminutive of "bite" (from Latin mordere, "to bite"), literally "a little bite" — the portion small enough for one mouthful.
Medieval-to-Early-Modern Table Use - Through the 14th-18th centuries morsel is the ordinary word for a small piece or mouthful of food, often carrying a hint of choiceness — a carefully portioned, delicate bite rather than a scrap.
Figurative Extension - From food the word extends to small pieces of anything: a morsel of news, of comfort, of information — the "bite-sized" sense applied to knowledge and gossip.
Contemporary Sense - Today morsel keeps its slightly literary, portion-conscious tone for a small choice bite of food, while its figurative use lingers in phrases like "not a morsel left" and "a choice morsel."
