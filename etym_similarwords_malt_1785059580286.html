• Barley: Similar because both refer to the cereal crop at the heart of brewing; differs because barley is the raw, dormant grain as it grows in the field, while malt is barley that has been steeped, germinated, and kilned — a processed ingredient, not a crop.
  • Grain: Similar because both denote a cereal product in the brewing pipeline; differs because grain is the generic term for any cereal, of which malt is only a specialized, deliberately transformed sub-class.
  • Wort: Similar because both are intermediate products in the same brewing chain; differs because wort is the sweet, sugar-rich liquid drained from the mash, while malt is the solid grain that yields it.
  • Mash: Similar because both belong to the malting/brewing process; differs because mash is the working mixture of crushed malt and hot water in the tun, while malt is the dry, prepared grain before it is crushed and mashed.
  • Maltose: Similar because both derive from the same root idea of malting; differs because maltose is a specific chemical sugar produced during germination, while malt is the whole processed grain.
  • Germinated seed: Similar because both involve a seed beginning to sprout; differs because a germinated seed is any sprouting plant embryo, while malt is grain whose germination was deliberately started and then arrested by kilning for brewing.
  • Hop: Similar because both are the two signature brewing ingredients alongside water and yeast; differs because hops provide bitterness, aroma, and preservation from the flower, while malt provides the fermentable sugars, body, and color from the grain.
  • Grist: Similar because both refer to crushed brewing grain; differs because grist is the milled, crushed malt ready for mashing, while malt is the unmilled prepared grain that becomes grist.
