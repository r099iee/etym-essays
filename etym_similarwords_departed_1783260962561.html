• deceased: Similar because both are formal, respectful adjectives and nouns for those who have died, used in obituaries and legal language; differs because deceased is a dry, legal-administrative term (the deceased in a will, the deceased in a police report), while departed is warmer and more explicitly euphemistic, framing death as a journey.
  • dead: Similar because both denote those who have died; differs because dead is blunt, neutral, and total, carrying no comfort and no euphemism, while departed softens the fact by reframing death as a going-away.
  • late: Similar because both are formal adjectives for a recently dead person ("the late Mr. Smith"); differs because late is used only attributively before a name or title and refers mainly to recent death, while departed works alone and covers the whole class of the dead in religious and literary use.
  • defunct: Similar because both can denote something that has ceased to exist or be active; differs because defunct is today chiefly technical and jocular (a defunct company, a defunct theory) and is never a tender word for people, while departed is solemn and personal.
  • passed away / passed on: Similar because both are the modern conversational euphemisms for dying, framing death as a leaving; differs because passed away is the ordinary contemporary phrase of condolence, while departed is the more literary, liturgical, and collective term (the departed) with a longer pedigree.
  • gone: Similar because both describe those who have left, and "the gone" can euphemistically mean the dead; differs because gone is informal and ambiguous (gone shopping, gone away), while departed is formal and unambiguous in its funerary sense.
  • lost: Similar because both can euphemistically name the dead ("our lost loved ones"); differs because lost emphasizes bereavement from the survivors' point of view, while departed emphasizes the deceased's own action of going away.
  • at rest / resting: Similar because both are gentle euphemisms that frame death as peaceful stillness; differs because at rest implies completion and stillness after struggle, while departed frames death as continued motion, a journey onward.
  • left: Similar because both are the past tenses of verbs of going away, and left can share departed's spatial sense; differs because left is the ordinary everyday word with no funerary sense at all, while departed carries the full formal and euphemistic weight.
  • quit / withdrew: Similar because both are past tenses of verbs meaning "went away from", overlapping the spatial sense of departed; differs because quit and withdrew imply abandoning a place or position by decision, while departed implies a simple going-off, and neither has departed's special sense of the dead.
