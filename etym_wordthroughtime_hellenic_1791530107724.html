- 8th century BCE - Hellēnes names a Thessalian tribe in Homer; the word is local and ethnic, not yet a name for all Greeks.
- 5th century BCE - after the Persian Wars, Hellēnes becomes a pan-Greek self-designation opposed to barbaroi; Hellenic acquires the sense of a shared civilisation.
- 4th century BCE - Isocrates and Alexander extend the idea of Greek culture beyond the city-state; "Hellenic" begins to mean a cultural inheritance that can be spread.
- 1st century BCE-2nd century CE - Roman writers use Hellenicus for Greek art, letters and philosophy; the adjective becomes a compliment in the Roman elite's education.
- 19th century - Philhellenism and the Greek War of Independence make "Hellenic" a Romantic and political ideal; the new state takes the name Hellas, and "Hellenic" enters English institutional usage.
- 20th century - "Hellenic" is fixed in the official name of the Greek state and in the classification of the Greek language branch; "Hellenistic" is carefully distinguished in scholarship.
- 21st century - the word serves museums, universities, diaspora organisations, the Orthodox Church and modern polytheist revival groups, often as a prestige alternative to "Greek".
