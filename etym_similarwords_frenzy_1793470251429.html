• Delirium: Similar because both denote an extreme state of mental incoherence and wild excitement; differs because delirium retains a strong clinical connection to fever, medical hallucination, or chemical intoxication, characterized by mental wandering and disorientation, whereas frenzy is typically more kinetic, purposeful in its violence, and explosive in passion.
  • Hysteria: Similar because both involve the collapse of emotional control and the spread of irrational behavior; differs because hysteria emphasizes unmanageable fear, weeping, or psychological panic often accompanied by psychosomatic symptoms, whereas frenzy suggests violent, active thrashing, aggressive mania, or manic fury.
  • Mania: Similar because both describe an episode of wild, hyperactive behavioral intensity; differs because mania can be an enduring psychiatric phase of bipolar disorder or an obsessive long-term craze (like Beatlemania), whereas frenzy is an acute, tempestuous, and typically shorter paroxysm of wild agitation.
  • Fury: Similar because both convey intense, destructive emotional energy; differs because fury is strictly rooted in extreme anger, vengeance, or violent wrath, whereas frenzy can be triggered by joy, creative inspiration, panic, greed, or divine ecstasy as well as rage.
  • Paroxysm: Similar because both signify a sudden, violent burst of intense emotion or physical convulsion; differs because a paroxysm is an abstract temporal descriptor denoting a sharp, periodic spasm or fit, whereas frenzy describes the comprehensive mental state of wild chaos itself.
  • Turmoil: Similar because both describe an agitated, disorderly state; differs because turmoil refers to widespread confusion, commotion, or political instability in the surrounding environment, whereas frenzy describes the feverish, internal psychological fire driving human actors.
  • Craze: Similar because both describe widespread collective excitement over a novelty; differs because a craze is a relatively harmless, frivolous social fad or temporary trend, lacking the dangerous, violent, and intoxicating magnitude of a true frenzy.
  • Ferment: Similar because both describe an agitated, bubbling social or political condition; differs because ferment suggests a gradual, simmering chemical brewing of unrest beneath the surface, whereas frenzy is the boiling, violent eruption when all control has snapped.
  • Agitation: Similar because both involve nervous, disturbed mental states; differs because agitation can be mild, quiet, or merely restless, whereas frenzy represents the absolute zenith of uncontrolled physical and emotional turbulence.
  • Pandemonium: Similar because both evoke chaotic wildness; differs because pandemonium refers to the external scene of deafening uproar and utter lawlessness (literally "all the demons"), whereas frenzy is the mental madness fueling the individuals within that scene.
