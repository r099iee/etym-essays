• herds: Similar because both are large groups of animals; differs because herd is reserved for cattle, deer, elephants, and whales — never sheep, goats, or birds — and lacks the pastoral-religious metaphor entirely.
  • droves: Similar because it denotes moving groups of animals or crowds of people; differs because it implies being driven along a road (of cattle) and is colloquial when applied to people (“droves of tourists”), whereas flock implies voluntary cohesion.
  • packs: Similar because it is a collective of social animals; differs because it is the word for predatory hunting groups (wolves, dogs, hounds) or for cards and brand names — bonding by predation, not by grazing or flight.
  • swarms: Similar because it is a dense moving mass of creatures; differs because it belongs to insects (bees, locusts) and, of people, implies disorder or overwhelming number — while a flock is orderly and cohesive.
  • shoals / schools: Similar because they are collectives of animals; differs because they are strictly aquatic (fish and marine mammals) and are never used of birds or sheep.
  • gaggles / skeins: Similar because they are collectives of geese (gaggle on the ground, skein in flight); differs because they are species-specific, specialist birding terms, while flock is the general word.
  • murmurations: Similar because it is the spectacular collective flight of starlings; differs because it names one specific phenomenon and one species group, while flocks covers every gregarious bird.
  • congregations: Similar because both denote people assembled for worship; differs because congregation is formal, structured, and architectural (“the congregation of St Mary's”), stripped of the shepherd metaphor that gives flock its pastoral warmth.
  • crowds: Similar because both are large bodies of people; differs because a crowd is an undifferentiated, leaderless mass, while a flock implies one kind of creature and usually a shepherd or guide.
  • hosts: Similar because it denotes a great multitude; differs because it is archaic, biblical, or poetic register and implies numbers so vast they are uncountable, not a organised herding group.
  • troops: Similar because it is a band of people or primates (baboons, monkeys); differs because it carries military or primate-specific force and organisation — no pastoral tenderness.
  • colonies: Similar because it is a structured group of animals; differs because it implies sedentary organisation (ants, seabird cities, coral) rather than cohesive movement, and is a technical term of social biology.
