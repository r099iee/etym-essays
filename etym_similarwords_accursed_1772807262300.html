• Cursed: Similar because both mean under a curse; differs because cursed is the plain base form, while accursed is heavier and more literary or biblical, with the Old English intensive prefix a- still faintly felt.
  • Damned: Similar because both condemn to ruin or divine judgment; differs because damned is strongly religious or a colloquial swear-word intensifier ('that damned dog'), while accursed keeps an archaic dignity.
  • Doomed: Similar because both foretell catastrophe; differs because doomed points to inevitable ruin ahead, whereas accursed brands the thing itself as cursed in the present.
  • Ill-fated: Similar because both mark a bad destiny; differs because ill-fated stresses fate and bad fortune without the active curse image or the anger accursed carries.
  • Hapless: Similar because both describe misfortune; differs because hapless simply means unlucky, without any implication of a curse or of moral blame.
  • Execrable: Similar because both mean detestable; differs because execrable refers to hateful quality (an execrable taste), not necessarily to luck or to an active malediction.
  • Wretched: Similar because both express strong dislike or misery; differs because wretched emphasises misery and contempt rather than the supernatural curse accursed evokes.
  • Unlucky: Similar because both concern bad fortune; differs because unlucky is neutral, secular, and weak, entirely lacking the melodramatic, doom-laden charge of accursed.
