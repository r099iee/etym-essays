- 1620s - [Zoological & Physical Resemblance] - Coined in Early Modern English initially referring literally to someone possessing a head shaped like a pig or exhibiting swine-like bluntness.
- 1700s - [Moral & Behavioral Satire] - Broadened across 18th-century English satires and comedies to describe foolishly stubborn, thick-skulled boors and country squires who refused urban manners.
- 1800s - [Victorian Characterization] - Widely established in 19th-century literature (Dickens, Thackeray) as a vivid character flaw denoting irrational obstinacy and closed-minded pomposity.
- 1900s–Present - [Colloquial Pejorative & Cognitive Label] - Modern conversational and psychological usage denoting stubborn entrenchment, cognitive bias, and ego-driven refusal to compromise.
