- Middle English 'A loud outcry' — a great shout or prolonged wailing of a crowd; used for public complaint, lamentation, and collective demand, already a vocal, human, and somewhat unruly sound.
- 16th–17th c. 'Loud and repeated noise' — broadens to any loud, sustained, confused sound, including animals and the elements, while retaining its central colour of urgent, insistent human voices.
- 18th–19th c. 'Vehement public demand' — 'to clamour for' becomes the standard phrase for noisy, insistent petitioning; the word gains political and journalistic weight as the sound of popular grievance and protest.
- Contemporary 'Chaotic outcry' — describes a din of overlapping, demanding voices and a loud public demand for action; slightly formal and literary in register, now outranked in news headlines by outcry and uproar.
