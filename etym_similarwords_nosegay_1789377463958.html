• Bouquet: Similar because both refer to an arranged bunch of cut flowers; differs because a bouquet is a broad, general term encompassing arrangements of any size, shape, or purpose (often large, loose, or presented as a gift), whereas a nosegay is specifically small, compact, hand-held, and traditionally assembled for its sweet fragrance.
  • Posy: Similar because both denote small, charming, hand-held bunches of sweet-scented flowers; differs because posy (originally a variant of poesy, referencing the inscribed motto or verse accompanying a floral gift) emphasizes sentimental affection and courtly rhyming charm, whereas nosegay emphasizes olfactory pleasure and scent.
  • Tussie-mussie: Similar because both are historical synonyms for a compact, circular bunch of sweet herbs and flowers; differs because tussie-mussie is a quaint medieval and Victorian folk term specifically associated with intricate silver posy-holders and coded floriography, whereas nosegay is the more formal and enduring literary term.
  • Boutonnière: Similar because both are small floral arrangements worn on personal attire; differs because a boutonnière consists of a single flower blossom or tiny spray pinned exclusively into the lapel buttonhole of a man's jacket, whereas a nosegay is a complete hand-held bouquet held or carried by women.
  • Corsage: Similar because both are miniature decorative floral clusters worn on the body; differs because a corsage is traditionally pinned to a woman's dress bodice or strapped around her wrist with an elastic band at formal proms and weddings, whereas a nosegay is carried in the hand by its stems.
  • Pomander: Similar because both were carried historically in the hand or worn on a chain to repel foul stenches and plague miasmas; differs because a pomander is a perforated spherical ball of metal or an orange studded with dried cloves and ambergris paste, containing no living, fresh-cut flowers.
  • Garland: Similar because both are decorative floral assemblies celebrating beauty and scent; differs because a garland is a long, woven, flexible wreath, festoon, or chain of leaves and flowers worn upon the head or hung around architectural columns, entirely distinct from a compact hand-held nosegay.
