• Notable: Similar because both denote prominent persons of high standing or renown; differs because a notable (often plural: *notables*) refers broadly to any eminent, distinguished, or well-known citizen (such as local business leaders or scholars) without necessarily holding an official government or court office, whereas a dignitary strictly holds formal official, ecclesiastical, or diplomatic rank.
  • Magnate: Similar because both refer to powerful, highly influential individuals; differs because a magnate is a powerful tycoon whose influence derives from immense private wealth and industrial dominance (e.g., "shipping magnate", "oil magnate"), whereas a dignitary's authority derives from institutional, court, or state office.
  • Luminary: Similar because both describe highly esteemed individuals in public life; differs because a luminary is a person who inspires or influences others through intellectual, literary, scientific, or artistic brilliance (an intellectual "light"), whereas a dignitary is defined by ceremonial position and official title.
  • Personage: Similar because both describe individuals of high rank or distinction; differs because personage is an elevated literary term emphasizing an individual's imposing personal presence, celebrity, or historical stature (e.g., "an illustrious personage"), whereas dignitary focuses on official institutional status.
  • Grandee: Similar because both refer to exalted aristocrats or high officials; differs because a grandee historically denoted a nobleman of the highest rank in Spain and Portugal (and figuratively any pompous elite), whereas dignitary applies universally across church, state, civic, and diplomatic hierarchies.
  • Potentate: Similar because both describe holders of high sovereign status; differs because a potentate emphasizes autocratic, sovereign, or autocratic power (a ruler with absolute dominion, like an emperor or monarch), whereas a dignitary may be a ceremonial official, judge, or prelate serving under a sovereign.
  • Official: Similar because both hold public appointments; differs because official is a broad, neutral, utilitarian noun covering any government or corporate employee (from a tax official to a customs clerk), whereas dignitary specifically denotes the exalted upper echelon of ceremonial rank and prestige.
