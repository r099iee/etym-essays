• salvific: Similar because both directly describe the power or intention to bring about spiritual salvation or deliverance; differs because salvific is heavily entrenched in formal Christian dogma and systematic ecclesiastical theology (e.g. "salvific grace"), whereas soterial (along with soteriological) more readily lends itself to philosophical, existential, and literary critical analyses of secularized redemption.
  • redemptive: Similar because both describe the act or quality of delivering someone from sin, suffering, disaster, or worthlessness; differs because redemptive etymologically implies buying back, paying a ransom, or compensating for an offense through sacrifice, whereas soterial centers broadly upon total deliverance, preservation, and the ontological state of salvation.
  • restorative: Similar because both involve renewing a fallen or impaired condition; differs because restorative emphasizes returning something to its original health, vigor, or functional baseline, whereas soterial signifies ultimate spiritual rescue from eternal ruin, mortal damnation, or absolute temporal oblivion.
  • emancipatory: Similar because both denote release from oppressive, confining forces; differs because emancipatory focuses primarily on socio-political liberation, civil rights, and unchaining from legal or physical servitude, whereas soterial pertains to cosmic, spiritual, or existential deliverance.
  • mediatorial: Similar because both appear within theological discussions of how human salvation is accomplished; differs because mediatorial denotes specifically the intermediary function of an intercessor or go-between who reconciles estranged parties (e.g. Christ as mediator between God and humanity), whereas soterial characterizes the saving nature, purpose, or outcome of the action itself.
  • expiatory: Similar because both belong to the theological framework of overcoming guilt and moral failure; differs because expiatory specifically characterizes ritual cleansing, atonement, or propitiation that extinguishes guilt through sacrifice, whereas soterial describes the resulting comprehensive condition of safety and deliverance.
  • purgative: Similar because both relate to religious paths toward spiritual perfection; differs because purgative refers to the painful, purifying stage of cleansing away spiritual impurities, whereas soterial focuses on the ultimate triumph of rescue and divine shelter.
