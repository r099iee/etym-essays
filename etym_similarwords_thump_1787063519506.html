• Thud: Similar because both describe a dull, heavy, non-resonant sound caused by the impact of a solid, blunt object; differs because *thud* denotes a softer, more deadened, flat, or cushioned impact with minimal rebound (such as a body falling onto damp turf), whereas *thump* carries greater vibrational force, percussive energy, and active kinetic drive.
  • Bang: Similar because both denote sudden, loud impact noises; differs because *bang* is sharp, explosive, and acoustic with prominent high-frequency treble energy (such as a slammed door, firecracker, or gunshot), whereas *thump* is deep, low-frequency, and muffled.
  • Pound: Similar because both involve forceful, rhythmic striking against a surface or the pulsating beat of the heart; differs because *pound* implies continuous, sustained, and pulverizing repetitive heavy blows over time, whereas *thump* can refer to a single discrete blunt blow or dull strike.
  • Whack: Similar because both represent physical strikes delivered against a target; differs because *whack* conveys a sharp, crisp, slicing impact or slap with a broad flat implement or stick, whereas *thump* denotes a heavy, blunt, cushioned impact from a fist, heavy mass, or boot.
  • Knock: Similar because both refer to striking a hard surface to produce an auditory signal; differs because *knock* is deliberate, structured, and typically sharp and hollow (as at a door), whereas *thump* is heavier, less defined, and carries a duller, deeper acoustic signature.
  • Clout: Similar because both refer to a heavy blow delivered with the hand; differs because *clout* often denotes a stinging slap or cuff to the head, and in modern usage has shifted heavily toward political or social influence and leverage.
  • Beat: Similar because both describe striking an object or the pulsating rhythm of the heart; differs because *beat* is a general, overarching term for repetitive cadence or rhythmic contact without the specific deep, dull timbre intrinsic to *thump*.
