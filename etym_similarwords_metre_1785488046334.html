• Rhythm: Similar because [both name the patterned movement of sound in language and music]; differs because [rhythm is the broader, older, natural felt pulse present even in prose and everyday speech, while metre is a deliberate, rule-governed, countable subset of rhythm].
  • Cadence: Similar because [both describe the rise and fall of the voice in speech or verse]; differs because [cadence is the voice’s own ungoverned music, especially in prose, with no fixed foot, whereas metre imposes a fixed, measured pattern of feet].
  • Prosody: Similar because [both are concerned with the sound-structure of verse]; differs because [prosody is not a pattern but the whole theory and study of versification — the discipline that analyses metre, rhyme, and sound].
  • Scansion: Similar because [both work with the feet of a poetic line]; differs because [scansion is the analytical act of marking and naming the feet, while metre is the patterned system itself that scansion analyses].
  • Beat: Similar because [both involve the pulse that structures time in music and verse]; differs because [a beat is the individual, repeated pulse, whereas metre is the larger scheme that groups beats into regular patterns].
  • Measure: Similar because [both can mean a rhythmic unit or a system of counting]; differs because [measure in music denotes a single bar of fixed length and in verse can name a specific metre, while metre is the general organising scheme of a whole line or poem].
  • Tempo: Similar because [both govern the pacing of music]; differs because [tempo is the speed at which music moves, while metre is the grouping of beats into patterns, independent of speed].
  • Pulse: Similar because [both describe an underlying regular movement]; differs because [pulse is the raw, felt beat that runs beneath all rhythm, whereas metre is the formal, nameable system built on top of it].
  • Time (musical): Similar because [both name the rhythmic organisation of music]; differs because [time is the everyday term for metre in musical notation, while metre is the more technical and prosodic word].
