• Sensitivity: Similar because both name the capacity to be affected by stimuli or impressions; differs because sensitivity can imply emotional rawness, irritability, or physical delicacy, whereas susceptibility here is trained, educable receptivity to impression.
  • Receptivity: Similar because both denote openness to impressions and influence; differs because receptivity is more neutral and passively accepting, while susceptibility carries the richer sense of being seized or taken by an influence.
  • Vulnerability: Similar because both denote openness to being affected from outside; differs because vulnerability stresses exposure to harm or attack, while Pater's susceptibility is exposure to impression and pleasure, not injury.
  • Impressionability: Similar because both mean readiness to be shaped by impressions; differs because impressionability can sound passive, youthful, or credulous, while Paterian susceptibility is the goal of mature aesthetic education.
  • Responsiveness: Similar because both involve being affected by an outside stimulus; differs because responsiveness emphasizes the active reaction or answer produced, whereas susceptibility emphasizes the capacity to take something in.
  • Suggestibility: Similar because both describe openness to influence; differs because suggestibility is narrowly clinical and implies compliance with hypnotic or social suggestion, usually in a weakness frame.
  • Openness: Similar because both denote receptivity to experience; differs because openness is a broad, positive personality trait, while susceptibility adds the specific sense of being capable of being seized by an influence.
  • Liability: Similar because both can denote exposure to being affected (liability to disease); differs because liability is legal-abstract and also implies responsibility or financial obligation, lacking the aesthetic positive charge.
  • Predisposition: Similar because both denote a tendency to be affected; differs because predisposition implies an inborn or constitutional leaning, while susceptibility can be cultivated and varies in degree.
  • Proneness: Similar because both denote a tendency to be affected; differs because proneness is everyday and implies habitual inclination, without the refined, Latinate openness that susceptibility commands.
