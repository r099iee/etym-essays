- Late 19th Century - Formed in late Victorian and Edwardian English as a compound referring to the full contents of a traveler's steamer trunk or wardrobe chest.
- Early 20th Century (1920s–1940s) - Transferred seamlessly to the automotive era with the invention of the automobile trunk (luggage compartment), becoming a standard measure of family road trip gear and bulk shopping.
- Mid to Late 20th Century - Flourished as a vibrant American and British colloquial idiom for winning, buying, or receiving a lavish abundance of tangible prizes or goods.
- 21st Century - Continues in automotive, retail, and descriptive figurative prose representing high-capacity personal transport and generosity.
