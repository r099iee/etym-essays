• Molon: Similar because it is the same man, the standard English and Latin form of the name; differs only as a spelling convention — Molon is the usual scholarly form, while Molo is an older English variant that omits the final consonant.
  • Apollonius: Similar because it is his praenomen-like given name, the element that identifies him formally as Apollonius Molon; differs because Apollonius was one of the commonest Greek names and denotes dozens of other writers, so it identifies him only in combination.
  • rhetorician: Similar because it names his profession and is the apposition used in the source; differs because it is a common noun applicable to any teacher or theorist of rhetoric, from Gorgias to a modern professor of composition.
  • rhetor: Similar because it is the Greek term for a public speaker and teacher of speaking; differs in being the older, more technical designation, and in carrying the Greek citizen-orator's political role rather than the classroom teacher's.
  • sophist: Similar because Greek teachers of rhetoric in the Roman period were often called sophists, and Molon belonged to that professional world; differs because sophist carries a polemical edge, implying showy display and indifference to truth, which Cicero never applies to Molon.
  • orator: Similar because Molon trained orators and was himself a speaker; differs because an orator is the practitioner of public speech, while Molon's fame rested on teaching and criticism rather than on his own delivered speeches.
  • grammarian: Similar because both were Greek scholars who taught language and texts to Roman students; differs because a grammarian taught reading, metre and textual criticism, whereas a rhetorician taught argument, arrangement and delivery.
  • declaimer: Similar because Molon's method centred on declamation; differs because declaimer names the performer or the exercise, not the master who corrected the performance.
  • Rhodes: Similar because it is inseparable from his identity — he is Molon of Rhodes, and the island was his school; differs because it is a place, not a person, and the name survives in modern geography.
