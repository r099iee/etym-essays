• rightly: similar as the everyday adverb for "with good reason"; differs because rightly is moral-cognitive and informal, whereas justly adds the full colouring of legal equity and fairness.
  • fairly: similar for impartial, even-handed dealing; differs because fairly is much broader (it also means "moderately, quite") and weaker on deservedness.
  • equitably: similar in law and political theory; differs as a technical term about distribution and balance, not general fairness of judgement.
  • deservedly: similar for a merited outcome; differs by restricting attention to what a person deserves, in praise or blame, without the wider legal-moral reach.
  • lawfully: similar in the legal sense; differs by appealing to legality alone, dropping the inner moral claim of justice.
  • legitimately: similar for acting on proper grounds or rights; differs by institutional sanction (what the rules allow) rather than inner fairness.
  • correctly: similar for "in the right way"; differs by truth and accuracy rather than justice.
  • properly: similar for conformity to standard; differs by propriety and convention rather than equity.
  • appropriately: similar for fittingness to the occasion; differs by fitness of match, not moral rightness.
  • accurately: similar only for the archaic "precisely" sense; differs by measured fidelity to fact.
  • duly: similar for "as is due"; differs by formal procedural colouring and rhythm of timeliness.
