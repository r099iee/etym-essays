--- Etymology of roving ---
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*raubōną
"to rob, plunder, strip"
↓
Middle Dutch / Old French (c. 13th c.)
roven / rover
"to wander as a pirate, roam"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.)
roven
"to wander, roam; shoot at rovers"
↓
Modern English (c. 16th c.)
roving
"wandering, mobile, roving"

--- Etymology of disk ---
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*dikei̯n- / *deiḱ-
"to throw, show, point out"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE)
δίσκος (dískos)
"quoit, circular throwing-plate"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
discus
"discus, circular plate, dish"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1650s)
disk / disc
"flat circular plate, circular surface"
↓
Gestalt Psychology (1954)
roving disk
"mobile circular visual stimulus"
"used to test perceptual forces"

Note: Roving disk is a compound technical construct popularized by Rudolf Arnheim in 'Art and Visual Perception' (1954). 'Roving' traces through archery and maritime pirate wandering ('rovers'), while 'disk' descends from Greek dískos ('throwing-plate', from dikeîn 'to cast, throw').
