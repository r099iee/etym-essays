- 17th c. 'To draw out or bring forth something hidden' — borrowed from Latin elicere; used of drawing hidden truths into view, chiefly through reasoning and inquiry, with a formal, intellectual register.
- 18th–19th c. 'To draw out information or a response through tactful questioning' — becomes the refined word for coaxing facts from reluctant or guarded speakers in polite and diplomatic contexts, implying subtlety rather than force.
- Contemporary 'To evoke a response, reaction, or reflex' — broadened beyond speech to any elicited reaction (applause, answers, physiological reflexes) in psychology and everyday usage; still implies skill and indirection in bringing the latent into the open.
