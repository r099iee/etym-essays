• Dairy: Similar because both refer to milk-producing domestic livestock and products; differs because dairy is the standard, modern, comprehensive commercial adjective encompassing milk production, processing facilities, and products (e.g., dairy cattle, dairy farm, dairy aisle), whereas milch is an archaic, traditional agricultural adjective applied specifically to the living lactating animal itself.
  • Lactating: Similar because both denote an animal actively secreting milk; differs because lactating is a precise physiological, biological, and medical term describing the hormonal state of mammary glands across all mammals, whereas milch is a functional agricultural and socioeconomic term for domestic livestock kept for human milk harvest.
  • Cash Cow: Similar because both denote a dependable, effortless source of continuous wealth and revenue; differs because cash cow is a modern twentieth-century business and marketing idiom (popularized by portfolio management), whereas milch cow is its historic seventeenth-century agrarian predecessor.
  • Productive: Similar because both describe animals or assets that yield valuable output; differs because productive is an abstract, general descriptor applying to any fruitful economic activity, whereas milch specifically denotes the biological output of milk.
  • Fertile: Similar because both describe reproductive vigor in animals; differs because fertile denotes the biological ability to conceive and produce offspring, whereas milch denotes the subsequent ability to yield milk after giving birth.
  • Wet Nurse: Similar because both denote a female providing milk to nourish others; differs because a wet nurse refers exclusively to a human woman hired to breastfeed another woman's infant, whereas milch applies to domestic animals and economic metaphors.
