• Impudence: Similar because both denote disrespectful boldness or audacity; differs because impudence stresses shameless boldness and audacity in speech, while impertinence stresses conduct that steps outside one's proper business or sphere.
  • Insolence: Similar because both express disrespect toward another; differs because insolence implies contemptuous, haughty disrespect toward someone in authority, while impertinence need not involve authority at all — it is about prying and meddling where one has no rightful place.
  • Effrontery: Similar because both denote brazen disregard for good manners; differs because effrontery emphasizes audacious cheek and shamelessness, while impertinence retains the sense of meddling, of conduct that does not pertain to one's own business.
  • Rudeness: Similar because both denote discourteous behavior; differs because rudeness is the general, everyday term without the specific idea of trespass or not-pertaining, which impertinence uniquely carries from its root sense of "not pertinent."
  • Cheek / nerve / sauce: Similar because all name audacious disrespect; differs because cheek, nerve, and sauce are informal, colloquial British terms for bold insolence, while impertinence is formal, literary, and carries the fuller moral idea of intrusion beyond one's sphere.
  • Presumption: Similar because both denote the overstepping of proper bounds; differs because presumption is the general term for taking more liberty than is warranted, while impertinence specifically denotes prying or meddling in what is not one's own business.
  • Meddling / prying: Similar because both name interference in what is not one's concern; differs because meddling and prying name the actions themselves (and can be neutral descriptions), while impertinence is the censuring abstract noun that judges such intrusion improper and disrespectful.
  • Irrelevance (the historical sense): Similar because both mean "not pertaining to the matter at hand"; differs because irrelevance is the neutral logical term for what is beside the point, while impertinence in its modern sense has shifted to social rudeness and intrusion, retaining "not pertaining" only as a buried root.
  • Intrusion: Similar because both name an improper entry into what is private or another's; differs because intrusion is broader (an intruder enters physically), while impertinence is the moral-social judgment that the entry or question does not belong to the interloper's business.
  • Discourtesy: Similar because both denote want of courtesy; differs because discourtesy is a mild, general failure of politeness, while impertinence is a sharper, more specific overstep — presumptuous meddling rather than mere lack of polish.
