PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed)
*(s)bʰel- / *(s)bʰl̥-tó-
"to swell, burst forth, split open"
↓
Sanskrit (c. 500 BCE)
sphuṭati / sphāṭayati
"to burst open, split, expand"
↓
Prakrit (c. 200 CE)
phaṭṭa-
"broken, burst, split"
↓
Hindustani (Hindi/Urdu) (16th–19th c.)
phaṭ / phaṭnā
"sound of bursting; to split, burst"
↓
Anglo-Indian English (1870s)
phut
"a dull pop; sudden failure"
↓
Modern English (c. 1888–present)
phut
"to fail, collapse (in 'go phut')"
Note: While the direct borrowing into English came from Hindustani phaṭ / phaṭnā during the British Raj, the root in Indo-Aryan is both etymological (descending from Sanskrit sphuṭ) and strongly onomatopoeic, imitating the natural sound of a sudden burst or muffled rupture. Western Indo-Europeanists connect the Sanskrit root to PIE *(s)bʰel- (swelling, bursting), though expressive sound symbolism heavily reinforced the form at every historical stage.
