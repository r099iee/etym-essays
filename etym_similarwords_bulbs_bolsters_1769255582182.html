• Corm: Similar because it is a swollen underground storage organ; differs because a corm is a solid stem base without layered fleshy scales (crocus, gladiolus), whereas a true bulb is sheathed in scale-leaves (onion, tulip).
  • Tuber: Similar because it is a fleshy storage organ; differs because a tuber is a starch-filled swollen root or stem dotted with "eyes" (potato, dahlia), not a layered bud-sheath like a bulb.
  • Rhizome: Similar because it is an underground plant-storage organ; differs because a rhizome is a creeping horizontal stem (ginger, iris) that grows sideways, while a bulb is a compact bud-like structure growing from a single base.
  • Pillow: Similar because it is a soft support for the head; differs because a pillow is the individual head-support, while a bolster is long, firm, and runs the full width of the bed, usually under the pillows.
  • Cushion: Similar because it is a soft pad of support; differs because a cushion is a seat-pad rather than a head-support, and it lacks the bolster's figurative verb — only a bolster can "bolster" confidence.
  • Support: Similar because both name what holds something up from beneath; differs because support is the general word for any physical or moral upholding, while a bolster is a specific long firm cushion and its verb keeps that propping-pillow image.
  • Prop: Similar because both describe holding something up; differs because a prop is a rigid structural brace (and the verb carries a hint of artificiality), whereas a bolster is a soft, continuous support beneath.
  • Reinforce: Similar because both verbs mean to strengthen; differs because reinforcing adds strength or material to an existing structure (reinforced concrete), while bolstering supports from beneath, lifting what might otherwise sag or fall.
  • Buttress: Similar because both mean to support something from outside; differs because a buttress is a massive architectural support built against a wall, while a bolster is a soft underlying support and its verb is gentler and more homely.
  • Shore up: Similar because both mean to prop something failing; differs because shoring up suggests emergency bracing against collapse (shores against a leaning wall), while bolstering suggests steady, cushioning support from below.
  • Underpin: Similar because both describe supporting from beneath; differs because underpinning is technical and suggests laying a foundation under a structure, while bolstering is softer and often moral or psychological (bolstering confidence).
  • Strengthen: Similar because both verbs mean to make stronger; differs because strengthening can act in any direction and at any point, while bolstering specifically supports from underneath, like the pillow the word names.
