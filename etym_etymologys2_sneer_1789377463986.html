Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–1st c. CE)
*snerwaną / *snurraną
"to twist, wrinkle the nose, snort, contract"
Note: Expressive onomatopoeic root imitating nasal sound and facial contraction; cognate with Middle High German snarren ('to grumble').
↓
Middle Low German / Middle Dutch (c. 13th–15th c.)
snerren / snirren
"to snort, utter a sharp nasal sound, chuckle mockingly"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1580–1600)
sneere / sneer (verb)
"to snort derisively, smile contemptuously with curled lip"
Note: Naturalized into English maritime and urban speech from North Sea trading contacts with Low German merchants.
↓
Early Modern English (Nominalization) (c. 1650–1680)
sneer (noun)
"a contemptuous facial expression or mocking, derisive remark"
↓
Modern English (present)
SNEER
