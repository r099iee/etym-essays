• Bunk: Similar because both are narrow, simple beds; differs because a bunk is built into a wall, a ship's cabin, or a railway carriage — permanent but cramped — while a cot stands free and can be folded and carried.
  • Berth: Similar because both are simple sleeping places; differs because a berth is fixed to a ship or train, with institutional or travelling overtones, whereas a cot is a portable, free-standing frame.
  • Pallet: Similar because both are humble beds; differs because a pallet is a makeshift bed of straw or blankets laid on the floor, suggesting destitution, while a cot is a real piece of furniture with a frame.
  • Bed: Similar because both are furniture to sleep on; differs because "bed" promises comfort, privacy, and a settled household, while a cot promises only support — and even that support is lent.
  • Crib: Similar because both are small enclosed beds for an infant (the British "cot" is a crib); differs because "crib" is the American word for the baby's bed and carries no sense of portability, while a cot remains a frame-bed that can be folded and moved.
  • Cradle: Similar because both are infant beds; differs because a cradle rocks on curved rockers, while a cot is stationary, and "cradle" also names the earliest stage of life in metaphor.
  • Charpoy: Similar because both are light string-woven frame-beds of the Indian subcontinent; differs because the charpoy is the native term for the very object from which the English cot was borrowed, culturally specific, while "cot" has generalised to any light portable bed.
  • Camp bed: Similar because both are portable folding beds; differs because a camp bed is specifically the military/outdoor folding bed, while a cot covers hospital, baby, and household senses as well.
  • Hammock: Similar because both are light, easily moved sleeping places; differs because a hammock swings, suspended from two points, while a cot stands on its own frame and does not rock.
  • Truckle bed: Similar because both are low, simple beds; differs because a truckle bed is a low bed on castors kept under a larger bed — a servant's or child's pull-out — while a cot is free-standing and portable anywhere.
