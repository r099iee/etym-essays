PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*plāk-
"to be flat, calm, smooth, soothing"
↓
Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
placere
"to be pleasing, agreeable, calm"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
displicere
"to displease, be disagreeable"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
desplaisir (infinitive used as noun)
"displeasure, sorrow, affliction"
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
displesir / displeasour
"offense, grievance, displeasure"
↓
English (modern)
displeasure
"feeling of annoyance, dissatisfaction, or disapproval"

Note: Middle English reshaped Old French infinitive noun desplaisir by suffix substitution with -ure (modeled on pleasure, which similarly reshaped Anglo-French plaisir on the analogy of Latinate abstract nouns in -ura like mesure and creature).
