• Cribs: Similar because both are infant beds; differs because a crib is a fixed-sided bed that does not rock, while a cradle is classically mounted on rockers for gentle motion.
  • Bassinets: Similar because both are small infant beds; differs because a bassinet is lighter, open, and often portable, while a cradle suggests the rocking frame.
  • Cots: Similar because both are small beds for children; differs because a cot (British English) is a simple barred bed that does not rock, while a cradle rocks.
  • Rockers: Similar because both involve rocking motion on curved rails; differs because a rocking chair rocks an adult seated in it, while a cradle is a bed that rocks an infant lying in it.
  • Hammocks: Similar because both are places where an infant may be gently rocked; differs because a hammock suspends from two points and swings, while a cradle rests on rockers or a supporting frame.
  • Beds: Similar because both are places to sleep; differs because a bed is the general term for any sleeping surface, while a cradle is specifically a small, rocking infant bed.
  • Moses baskets: Similar because both are portable newborn beds that can be rocked; differs because a Moses basket is a woven, handle-less carrier rocked by hand, while a cradle is a frame bed on rockers.
  • Nurseries: Similar because both relate to infancy and early care; differs because a nursery is the room or facility where infants are cared for, while a cradle is the specific rocking bed within it.
  • Supports / Frames: Similar because both name structures that hold something; differs because support and frame are the general technical terms for any holder, while cradle specifies a holder shaped to rock or protect something fragile.
  • Cradling (verb sense): Similar because the verb 'to cradle' shares the noun's protective-holding image; differs because the verb is an action (holding gently), while the noun names the bed or frame itself.
