- Vedic Era (c. 1500–600 BCE) — Elemental Forest & Sacred Shelter: The independent roots *vana* (sacred wood, abundance, desire) and *okas* (dwelling, habitual abode, house) existed as fundamental Vedic religious terms, with *okas* designating the home where gods are invited to dwell and feast upon Soma offerings.
- Epic Sanskrit Era (c. 500 BCE–200 CE) — Forest Monkeys & Wilderness Hermits: Compounded into *vanaukas* in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata, serving as a dual-edged kenning applied alternately to the vast monkey armies of Kiṣkindhā and to the saintly ascetics who established hermitage communities in the Daṇḍaka wilderness.
- Classical Kāvya & Smṛti Era (c. 300–1000 CE) — Poetic Contrast Between Court & Nature: Utilized by courtly dramatists (such as Kālidāsa in Abhijñānaśākuntalam) and legal commentators to symbolize the pastoral, untamed realm of nature in stark aesthetic contrast to the pampered, intrigue-filled royal city (*nagara*).
- Medieval Scholasticism & Bhakti (c. 1000–1600 CE) — Allegory of Spiritual Renunciation: Deployed in devotional commentaries on the Rāmāyaṇa to celebrate the humble forest creatures whose unpretentious wilderness devotion surpassed the pride of worldly kings and ritualistic priests.
- Modern Indological & Ecological Studies (19th–21st c.) — Environmental & Mythological Category: Analyzed by modern scholars examining traditional Indian concepts of wilderness (*araṇya* / *vana*) versus settlement (*kṣetra*), demonstrating how ancient epithets conceptualized fauna and ascetics as co-dwellers of sacred forest spaces.
