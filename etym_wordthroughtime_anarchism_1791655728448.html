- 17th c. (English Civil War) — Pejorative polemical epithet hurled by Royalists and Parliamentarians alike to condemn political insubordination, religious leveling, and the terrifying breakdown of public order.
- Late 18th c. (French Revolution) — Revolutionary insult wielded by Jacobins and Girondins to denounce the radical sans-culottes and Enragés who rejected centralized state dictates.
- Mid-19th c. (Proudhon) — Boldly reclaimed as an affirmative political doctrine and philosophical science in 1840 ("Anarchy is Order without power"), initiating the modern movement.
- Late 19th c. — Polarized in the global public imagination between high-minded utopian intellectualism (Kropotkin, Reclus) and sensationalist terror following "propaganda of the deed" bombings and political assassinations.
- Early 20th c. — Embodied in massive militant labor movements (the Spanish CNT-FAI and Ukrainian Black Army) before being ruthlessly suppressed by totalitarian fascism and Soviet Bolshevism.
- Mid-to-Late 20th c. — Re-emerged as a foundational pillar of countercultural rebellion, anti-war mobilization, situationism, radical environmentalism, and libertarian socialist critique (Noam Chomsky).
- 21st c. — Reconceptualized around peer-to-peer digital networks, open-source governance, platform cooperativism, horizontalist social movements (Occupy), and decentralized cryptographic systems.
