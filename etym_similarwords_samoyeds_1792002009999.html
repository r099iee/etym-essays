• Nenets: Similar because the Nenets are the largest and most prominent Indigenous ethnic group historically subsumed under the exonym Samoyeds; differs because Nenets (meaning "human beings" in their own language) is the accurate contemporary autonym officially adopted to replace the archaic, historically ambiguous exonym.
  • Enets: Similar because they represent one of the four surviving Indigenous peoples belonging to the Samoyedic language group; differs because the Enets constitute a distinct, highly endangered community native to the lower Yenisey River with their own unique dialect and social history.
  • Nganasans: Similar because they are an Indigenous Uralic-speaking Arctic people traditionally classified as Samoyeds; differs because the Nganasans lived as high-latitude wild-reindeer hunters on the remote Taymyr Peninsula and preserved unique shamanic institutions distinct from the reindeer pastoralism of the western tundra.
  • Selkups: Similar because their tongue belongs to the Samoyedic branch of Uralic; differs because the Selkups inhabited the southern boreal taiga rather than open tundra, traditionally relying on riverine fishing, canoeing, and forest trapping rather than large-scale tundra reindeer pastoralism.
  • Sámi: Similar because both are Indigenous northern European peoples speaking Uralic languages who historically practiced reindeer husbandry; differs because the Sámi inhabit Sápmi (northern Scandinavia and the Kola Peninsula) and belong to the Finno-Ugric branch, whereas the Samoyeds are native to northeastern Europe and Siberia and belong to the Samoyedic branch.
  • Chukchi: Similar because both are Siberian Indigenous peoples renowned for polar survival and reindeer herding; differs because the Chukchi inhabit far northeastern Siberia near the Bering Strait and speak a Chukotko-Kamchatkan tongue unrelated to Uralic.
  • Tungus: Similar because both were historical umbrella exonyms used by Tsarist administrators to categorize Siberian foraging peoples; differs because Tungus (now the Evenks and Evens) belong to the Tungusic language family native to eastern Siberia and the Amur basin.
  • Inuit: Similar because both are circumpolar Indigenous peoples adapted to sub-zero Arctic ecosystems; differs because the Inuit inhabit North America and Greenland and speak Eskimo-Aleut languages, possessing an exclusively maritime seal- and whale-hunting heritage distinct from the terrestrial reindeer pastoralism of the Eurasian Samoyeds.
