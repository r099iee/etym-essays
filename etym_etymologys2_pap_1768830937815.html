Derivation chain for PAP (single root word): Stage 1 — (No PIE root: the word is a nursery formation of imitative, reduplicative origin.) Latin pappa "soft food, pap, baby food" — a reduplicated child's word of the pa-pa type, attested in Classical Latin (e.g. in Martial), meaning the soft food a baby eats. Stage 2 — Medieval / learned Latin pappa "pap, soft infant food," continued in the vocabulary of medicine and of infant care across Europe. Stage 3 — Middle English pap "soft semi-liquid food for infants, invalids, or the toothless" (attested from the 14th–15th century), borrowed from Latin pappa. Stage 4 — Modern English pap "soft food for infants; bland or trivial ideas" (the figurative "intellectually soft" sense develops from the 19th century) — TARGET. Note on the debate: a second, formally distinct English word pap meaning "nipple, teat" (plural paps = breasts) also exists. It is probably of separate origin — possibly Scandinavian or another independent nursery word — and is NOT derived from the "food" pap, although the two are homophones and have been historically confused and entangled. The adjective pappy ("soft, pulpy, feebly sentimental") derives from the food-word pap + -y.
