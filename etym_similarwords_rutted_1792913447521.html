• Grooved: Similar because both describe surfaces cut with channels; differs because grooved is general and often deliberate (grooved records, grooved timber), while rutted means worn by wheels through use.
  • Furrowed: Similar because both picture parallel depressive lines in earth; differs because furrowed belongs to ploughs and worried brows, deliberately cut, while rutted belongs to roads, accidentally worn.
  • Potholed: Similar because both describe broken worn road surfaces; differs because potholed means pitted with round holes from frost and traffic, while rutted means lined with lengthwise wheel-channels.
  • Rugged: Similar because both describe rough going underfoot or wheel; differs because rugged means naturally rocky and uneven, while rutted means patterned by repeated wheels.
  • Uneven: Similar because both warn of bumpy passage; differs because uneven is the bland general term for any irregularity, while rutted names the precise twin-groove cause.
  • Muddy: Similar because rutted lanes are often muddy lanes; differs because muddy names wet softness underfoot, while rutted names the wheel-shaped memory pressed into it.
  • Worn: Similar because both record long use; differs because worn covers every kind of erosion from coats to patience, while rutted specifies wheel-wear on tracks.
  • Beaten: Similar because a beaten track and a rutted lane are both much-travelled; differs because beaten stresses flattening by feet into a path, while rutted stresses twin channels cut by wheels.
  • Track-marked: Similar because both show where wheels have passed; differs because track-marked is a neutral descriptive compound, while rutted is the single traditional word novelists reach for.
