- 9th to 15th Century - Civic Toponym: The Flemish city name *Machlina* / *Mechelen* developed as a major trading hub and ecclesiastical center in the Low Countries.
- 16th to 17th Century - Birth of the Lace Industry: Master lacemakers in Mechelen perfected continuous bobbin lace techniques; English merchants and royalty began importing Flemish lace.
- 18th Century - The Golden Age of Mechlin Lace: Reached supreme international prominence as the favorite lace of European royalty, aristocracy, and dandies; revered for cravats, ruffles, and sleeve cuffs due to its fine hexagonal ground (*fond de glace*) and shiny cordonnet outline.
- 19th Century - Mechanization and Victorian Heirlooms: Faced competition from machine-made Nottingham laces; preserved as an exquisite collectible antique textile and bridal luxury.
- Contemporary Usage - Retains prestigious proper noun status in art history, costume history, and period literature as the supreme symbol of eighteenth-century aristocratic sartorial elegance and Flemish craftsmanship.
