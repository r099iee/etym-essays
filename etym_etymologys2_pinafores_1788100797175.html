Chain 1 — PIN
↓
PIE (reconstructed, 4th–3rd mill. BCE)
*pet-
"to fly, spread"
↓
Latin (classical)
penna
"feather, wing"
from *pet-nā, "that which flies"
↓
Latin (classical)
pinna
"feather, wing, fin, point, pinnacle"
↓
Old English (loanword, c. 9th c.)
pinn
"peg, bolt, pin, nail"
semantic bridge: a feather quill is a slender pointed shaft
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
pin
"pin, brooch-pin, peg"
↓
Modern English (16th c. onward)
pin
"thin pointed fastening wire"

Chain 2 — AFORE
↓
PIE (reconstructed, 4th–3rd mill. BCE)
*per-
"forward, through, in front"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*furai / *fora
"before, in front"
↓
Old English (c. 8th c.)
fore / foran
"before, in front (of)"
phrase on foran, "in front"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
afore
"before, in front of"
a- (on) + fore
↓
Modern English (16th c. onward)
afore
"before; in front"
survives in dialect, nautical and legal use

Chain 3 — the compound
↓
English (1780s, first recorded c. 1782)
pin-afore
"an apron pinned before (on the front)"
↓
English (19th c.)
pinafore
"child's apron; sleeveless protective dress"
↓
English plural (this card)
pinafores

Note: the Latin ancestor of pin is genuinely debated only at the intermediate stage — Old English pinn is a secure loan from Latin pinna, and pinna derives from penna, "feather, wing", itself from PIE *pet- "to fly", via the pointed-quill bridge; alternative proposals linking pinna to a separate Italic word for "point" have not displaced the traditional account. The first recorded date of pinafore varies across dictionaries within the 1780s.
