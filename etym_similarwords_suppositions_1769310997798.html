• Assumptions: Similar because both are beliefs accepted without proof; differs because assumptions are usually taken for granted unreflectively and feel more settled, while suppositions are consciously set down for the sake of argument.
  • Hypotheses: Similar because both are proposed explanations awaiting confirmation; differs because hypotheses are formal, testable propositions in science and logic, while suppositions are informal and untested.
  • Theories: Similar because both are explanatory frameworks; differs because theories are systematic and (ideally) evidence-backed, while suppositions are single provisional guesses.
  • Presumptions: Similar because both are beliefs formed without full certainty; differs because presumptions lean on probability, precedent, or legal standing, while suppositions rest merely on conjecture.
  • Conjectures: Similar because both are conclusions on incomplete evidence; differs because conjectures are assembled from scattered reasoning, while suppositions are laid down as foundations for further thought.
  • Speculations: Similar because both are unverified thoughts; differs because speculations wander freely over possibilities (and financial risk), while suppositions are anchored as premises.
  • Surmises: Similar because both are guesses without firm proof; differs because surmises are lighter, more intuitive hunches, while suppositions are more reasoned and structural.
  • Premises: Similar because both are starting points for reasoning; differs because premises in logic are the stated grounds of an argument, while suppositions are provisional ideas that may never be granted.
  • Postulates: Similar because both are propositions assumed at the outset; differs because postulates are formal starting assumptions in mathematics and logic, while suppositions are everyday guesses.
  • Guesses: Similar because both are answers without proof; differs because guesses are casual and everyday, while suppositions are formal and reasoned-sounding.
  • Presuppositions: Similar because both underlie reasoning; differs because presuppositions are buried, unexamined background beliefs, while suppositions are explicit and foregrounded.
  • Inferences: Similar because both are conclusions; differs because inferences are drawn from evidence by reasoning, while suppositions outrun the evidence.
