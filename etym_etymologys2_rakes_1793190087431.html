Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE)
*rakō-
‘rake, implement for gathering’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
raca
‘rake, hay rake’
↓
Middle English (13th–14th c.)
rake
‘toothed tool for hay and soil’
↓
Modern English (plural)
rakes
‘toothed gathering tools’

Note: cognates such as Old Norse reka (‘spade’) and Middle Dutch rake confirm the Germanic root, but no deeper PIE ancestor is securely established; older guesses linking it to PIE *reg- (‘to move straight’) are not accepted in current scholarship, so the chain honestly begins at Proto-Germanic.
