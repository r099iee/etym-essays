- [Classical Greek Antiquity (5th–4th c. BCE)] — *genealogía* emerged as a compound of *geneá* ("race, family, generation") and *lógos* ("discourse, computation"), used by Hecataeus, Herodotus, and Plato to record the heroic descent of aristocratic civic families from Olympian gods and founding heroes — [perception: revered as a foundational civic and sacred validation of divine kingship, heroic virtue, and aristocratic entitlement]
- [Hellenistic & Biblical Era (3rd c. BCE–1st c. CE)] — widely deployed in the Septuagint and the opening chapters of the Gospels of Matthew and Luke to establish the strict linear patrilineal succession from Abraham and King David to Jesus of Nazareth — [perception: regarded as sacred scriptural testimony confirming covenantal prophecy, messianic legitimacy, and providential history]
- [Medieval Feudalism & Chivalric Heraldry (12th–15th c.)] — passed through Late Latin *genealogia* into Old French *genealogie* and Anglo-Norman legal codices, charting noble pedigrees, heraldic quarterings, and dynastic succession to feudal fiefdoms, titles, and royal thrones — [perception: celebrated as an indispensable legal and chivalric instrument of dynastic inheritance, land ownership, and aristocratic blood purity]
- [Late Middle English to Early Modern Antiquarianism (16th–17th c.)] — anglicized into *genealogie* / *genealogy*, expanding from royal chronicles into municipal burgher rolls, parish baptismal registers, and college heraldry during the Tudor and Stuart heraldic visitations — [perception: respected as a prestigious mark of family honor, ancestral antiquity, and upward social mobility for the rising gentry]
- [19th Century (Romantic Archivalism & Nietzschean Critique)] — professionalized as a systematic archival historical discipline on one side, while revolutionized philosophically by Friedrich Nietzsche (*Zur Genealogie der Moral*, 1887) into a diagnostic critique that exposes the base, contingent psychological origins of revered moral and religious concepts — [perception: transformed from an aristocratic praise of pristine origins into a radical philosophical weapon that destabilizes metaphysical claims to absolute truth]
- [20th Century (Foucauldian Critical Theory & Evolutionary Genetics)] — adapted by Michel Foucault as a critical historiographical method tracking discursive formations and institutional power networks, while simultaneously revolutionized in molecular biology and evolutionary cladistics as phylogenetic genealogical mapping — [perception: embraced across both postmodern critical philosophy and quantitative biological and genomic sciences]
- [21st Century (Direct-to-Consumer Genomics & Mass Digital Archives)] — popularized globally through consumer DNA testing kits (23andMe, AncestryDNA) and digitized genealogical repositories, intertwining personal ethnic identity, medical hereditary risk, and forensic cold-case solving — [perception: transformed into a universally accessible, democratic everyday pursuit of personal roots, genetic belonging, and self-discovery]
