• invention: Similar because it is the direct English descendant of inventio and names the same canon in English-language rhetoric; differs because modern invention usually means creating something new or devising a device, whereas the Latin term means finding the arguments already available in a case.
  • discovery: Similar because both centre on finding something that exists rather than making it; differs because discovery is general and often accidental, while inventio is a deliberate, methodical search conducted through topics and stasis theory for the purpose of persuasion.
  • devising: Similar because it suggests working out a plan or scheme; differs because devising stresses constructive ingenuity and has no fixed place in an art, whereas inventio is a disciplined canon with procedures and a forensic aim.
  • conception: Similar because both involve forming an idea in the mind; differs because conception is a mental act with no necessary audience or argument, while inventio is audience-directed and constrained by the case.
  • contrivance: Similar because it can denote a cleverly arranged device or plan; differs because contrivance often implies artificiality or scheming, whereas inventio is a neutral technical term for finding proofs.
  • fabrication: Similar because both can describe the production of a case or story; differs because fabrication means invention in the sense of falsehood, the opposite of Cicero's requirement that the material be true or truth-like.
  • innovation: Similar because it is a near-synonym of modern "invention"; differs because innovation stresses novelty introduced into practice and belongs to business and technology, not to the recovery of arguments.
  • creativity: Similar because it names the capacity that modern readers associate with invention; differs because it is a psychological and aesthetic concept, unknown to classical rhetoric and irrelevant to the technical canon.
  • excogitation: Similar because Cicero's own definition uses excogitatio, "a thinking out," and the English word preserves that sense; differs because excogitation is archaic and purely mental, without the rhetorical machinery of topics, stasis and proof.
  • originality: Similar because it is a quality prized in modern invention; differs because it is a judgement of novelty and individuality, whereas inventio is a procedure and can be entirely traditional.
