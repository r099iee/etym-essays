• Alliteration: Similar because both are phonetic literary devices involving the repetition of speech sounds in nearby words; differs because alliteration strictly involves the repetition of initial consonant sounds (e.g., "peter piper picked"), whereas assonance involves the repetition of internal vowel sounds (e.g., "hear the lark bark").
  • Consonance: Similar because both are forms of partial, internal rhyme; differs because consonance is the repetition of identical consonant sounds (especially at the ends or middles of words, as in "stroke of luck"), while assonance is strictly confined to vowel sounds.
  • Rhyme: Similar because both create acoustic correspondence between words; differs because full rhyme requires the matching of both the vowel and all subsequent consonants (e.g., c-at / h-at), whereas assonance requires only the vowel to match while the ending consonants differ (e.g., h-at / m-an).
  • Euphony: Similar because both involve pleasing, harmonious sound combinations in language; differs because euphony is a broad aesthetic judgment describing any smooth, melodious sound pattern, whereas assonance is a specific technical device based on vowel repetition.
  • Onomatopoeia: Similar because both exploit the acoustic and sensory properties of words; differs because onomatopoeia refers to words that phonetically imitate the actual physical sounds they describe (e.g., buzz, hiss, sizzle), while assonance is purely a structural repetition of vowel timbres.
  • Resonance: Similar because both denote an acoustic richness and lingering vibration; differs because resonance is a general acoustic or metaphorical property of deep, lingering sound and emotional impact, whereas assonance is a precise linguistic and prosodic pattern.
