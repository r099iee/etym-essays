- Late 14th to 15th Century - Enters Middle English from Old/Middle French *parisien*, denoting simply an inhabitant, native, or civic jurisdiction of Paris, often appearing in royal chronicles and commercial treaties.
- 16th to 17th Century - Gains intellectual and religious authority across Europe as the University of Paris and the French court centralize political and literary power; the Parisian dialect of Île-de-France becomes the definitive benchmark of grammatical elegance and courtly French.
- 18th Century (The Enlightenment) - Evolves into an emblem of cosmopolitan wit, salon conversation, philosophical daring, and radical political ferment; across Europe, speaking with Parisian finesse was the hallmark of the enlightened aristocrat, before 1789 brought the fierce image of the revolutionary Parisian working class (*sans-culottes*).
- 19th Century (Belle Époque & Haussmannization) - Reimagined globally as the universal archetype of style, haute couture, literary modernism (Baudelaire's *flâneur*), café society, and bohemian artistry, cemented by the Universal Expositions and the romanticization of the Seine.
- 20th to 21st Century - Universalized as a premier international luxury and aesthetic concept ("Parisian chic", "Parisian lifestyle"), denoting understated elegance, effortless style, and intellectual romanticism, while retaining in domestic French usage a playful tension between Parisian fast-paced self-absorption and provincial authenticity.
