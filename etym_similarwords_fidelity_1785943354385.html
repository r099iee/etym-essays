• LOYALTY: Similar because both name steadfast commitment to a person, group, or cause and are often used interchangeably; differs because loyalty is a broader emotional allegiance (often to a group, institution, or country and frequently conceived as earned or natural), while fidelity stresses the keeping of a specific trust, vow, or standard and carries a more formal, ethical gravity.
  • FAITHFULNESS: Similar because it is the near-exact synonym of fidelity in the moral sense, sharing the same Latin root of "faith, trust"; differs because faithfulness is the everyday English noun emphasizing reliability in relationships and duties, while fidelity additionally carries the technical meanings (audio, data, translation) and the ceremonial, vow-bound tone.
  • ALLEGIANCE: Similar because both denote loyalty to a ruler, state, or party; differs because allegiance is specifically the political or military duty owed to a sovereign or government (from Old French ligeance, the bond of the liege lord), while fidelity covers personal, marital, religious, and technical devotion far beyond the political sphere.
  • DEVOTION: Similar because both express deep, committed attachment; differs because devotion adds warmth, love, and religious fervour and may be one-directional, whereas fidelity is a cooler, contractual keeping of a promise that implies an obligation both parties recognize.
  • CONSTANCY: Similar because both name the quality of remaining true over time; differs because constancy emphasizes unchanging steadiness of mind or affection in the face of temptation, while fidelity emphasizes the fulfilment of an explicit bond or trust.
  • FEALTY: Similar because both derive from the medieval oath of loyalty and share the notion of sworn allegiance; differs because fealty is the archaic, feudal term for the vassal's bond to a lord, strictly political and historical, while fidelity is the living, everyday word covering every kind of faithfulness.
  • ADHERENCE: Similar because both express staying true to a belief, cause, or standard; differs because adherence is usually about following rules, doctrines, or treatments (adherence to a diet, to a party line) and is impersonal, while fidelity implies a personal or moral bond and a standard of honour.
  • DEDICATION: Similar because both describe committed service to a cause or task; differs because dedication stresses energetic self-giving and enthusiasm for an endeavour, whereas fidelity stresses the keeping of trust and the avoidance of betrayal.
  • COMMITMENT: Similar because both name an engagement to a person or cause; differs because commitment is the act or state of being pledged, often to a plan or relationship, and is less exalted, while fidelity is the sustained virtue of honouring that pledge.
  • RELIABILITY / DEPENDABILITY: Similar because both suggest that one can be trusted to do what is expected; differs because these are practical, everyday terms about performance and predictability (a reliable car, a dependable worker), lacking the moral, vow-bound depth and the sense of possible betrayal that fidelity carries.
  • ACCURACY / EXACTNESS: Similar because in the technical register fidelity means exact correspondence to an original, and "accuracy" is its everyday near-synonym; differs because accuracy is a neutral measurement of correctness, while fidelity in this sense implies a "faithful" reproduction — a moral metaphor of truthfulness applied to machines, texts, and data.
  • PRECISION: Similar because both can describe exactness in technical reproduction; differs because precision stresses fineness of measurement or detail (a precise instrument, precise language), while fidelity stresses conformity to a source — fidelity asks "true to what?", precision asks "how fine?".
  • VERISIMILITUDE: Similar because both concern the degree to which a copy resembles a reality; differs because verisimilitude (from Latin veri similis "like the truth") is a literary and rhetorical term for the appearance of truth, while fidelity is the general term for actual, measured correspondence to an original.
  • INTEGRITY: Similar because both name moral soundness and incorruptibility; differs because integrity is internal wholeness and honesty of character that a person holds regardless of others, while fidelity is outward loyalty to a specific person, cause, or vow.
  • HONESTY: Similar because both oppose deception and suggest trustworthiness; differs because honesty concerns truthfulness in speech and conduct, while fidelity concerns keeping commitments and refraining from betrayal — one can be honest yet unfaithful.
