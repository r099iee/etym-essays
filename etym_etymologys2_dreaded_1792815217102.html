Old English (before 12th c., prefixed forms)
ondrǣdan (also ādrǣdan)
‘to fear, dread’
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
dreden (also draden)
‘to fear greatly, shrink from’
↓
Middle English (14th c., participle)
dred (past participle of dreden)
‘feared, held in awe’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
dread (verb and noun)
‘to fear in advance’
‘great apprehension, awe’
↓
Modern English (18th c. onward)
dreaded
‘feared beforehand, held in awe’

Note: no secure Proto-Germanic or PIE ancestor for this family is established; handbook sources treat the pre–Old English history of dread as obscure and possibly of expressive or imitative formation, so the chain begins at the earliest firmly attested stage rather than inventing a deeper root.
