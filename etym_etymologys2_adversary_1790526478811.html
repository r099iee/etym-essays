Etymological Note: The morphological formation of adversary combines the Latin spatial prefix ad- ("to, toward") with the verbal root vertere ("to turn"), producing the participle adversus ("turned against, opposed"). The addition of the substantival suffix -ārius created adversārius, literally "one who stands turned against another." In Jerome's Latin Vulgate translation of the Bible, adversārius was systematically employed to translate the Hebrew word Śāṭān (שָׂטָן, "accuser, opponent"), cementing the capitalized theological sense of the Adversary throughout Western European religious literature.
