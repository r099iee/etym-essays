Note on historical derivation: Sanskrit Jāhnavī is a regular secondary taddhita patronymic formation derived from the proper noun Jahnu (the sage who adopted the river). The derivation follows Pāṇinian morphological rules: the base stem jahnu undergoes vṛddhi strengthening of the initial vowel (a → ā) with the addition of the patronymic suffix -a- (Pāṇini's aṇ), yielding masculine jāhnava ("son of Jahnu"), followed by the feminine suffix -ī (ṅīp) replacing the vocalic end to yield jāhnavī ("daughter of Jahnu"). The root of Jahnu itself is conventionally traced by Indian grammarians to the verbal root hā ("to leave, abandon, renounce", 3rd class jahāti), from PIE *gʰeh₁- ("to leave behind"), characterizing the sage as one who has renounced worldly clinging.
