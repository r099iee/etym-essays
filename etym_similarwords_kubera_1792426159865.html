• Plutus: Similar because both are classical deities personifying material wealth and agricultural abundance; differs because Plutus is a Greek deity (son of Demeter) famously depicted as blind so that he distributes riches indiscriminately without regard to virtue, whereas Kubera is a cosmic guardian (*Lokapāla*) whose wealth in epic lore is held under righteous custodianship (*dharma*).
  • Mammon: Similar because both are mythological or religious figures identified with gold, riches, and treasure; differs fundamentally because Mammon in New Testament Christian theology represents an evil, corrupting demon of greed and covetousness that lures souls away from God, whereas Kubera is a venerable divinity associated with righteous fortune, charity, and divine order.
  • Croesus: Similar because both are culturally renowned archetypes of astronomical, staggering wealth; differs because Croesus was an actual historical 6th-century BCE king of Lydia whose legendary wealth ended in tragic hubris and military downfall, whereas Kubera is an immortal divinity and cosmic regent.
  • Midas: Similar because both are legendary figures linked with vast hoards of gold; differs because Midas in Greek myth embodies the catastrophic curse of unchecked avarice (everything he touched turned to stone-cold gold, starving him), whereas Kubera personifies divine abundance and benevolent philanthropy.
  • Pluto (Hades): Similar because both are chthonic deities ruling subterranean mineral wealth, precious gems, and underground treasures; differs because Pluto is primarily the grim ruler of the Underworld and the souls of the dead, whereas Kubera is a celestial regent presiding over joyful, lush Himalayan paradises (*Alakā*).
  • Vaiśravaṇa: Similar because both designate the identical deity (patronymic meaning "son of Viśravas"); differs because Vaiśravaṇa is the formal Sanskrit and pan-Asian Buddhist name used when highlighting his role as one of the Four Heavenly Kings (*Caturmahārāja*) defending the northern direction, whereas Kubera is the standard Hindu epic and Puranic personal name.
  • Jambhara: Similar because both represent the master of wealth and lord of yakṣas; differs because Jambhara is the specific Tantric Buddhist emanation of Kubera, visualized holding a mongoose (*nakula*) spitting jewels and invoked in Vajrayāna wealth rituals.
  • Lakshmi: Similar because both are primary Hindu deities of prosperity and good fortune; differs because Lakshmi is the supreme divine Mother Goddess (consort of Viṣṇu) embodying auspiciousness, spiritual grace, and fortune itself, whereas Kubera is the celestial treasurer and custodian who manages the physical distribution of wealth.
  • Crassus: Similar because both serve as proverbial standards of immense fortune; differs because Marcus Licinius Crassus was a historical Roman triumvir whose vast fortune was amassed through cynical real-estate speculation and political corruption, meeting a violent death at Carrhae.
  • Vessavaṇa: Similar because both represent the same northern guardian deity; differs because Vessavaṇa is the Pali canonical form revered in Theravada Buddhist scriptures across Sri Lanka, Myanmar, and Thailand.
