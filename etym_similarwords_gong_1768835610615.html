• Bell: Similar in being a struck metal instrument that sounds a summons; differs because a bell is a cup-shaped resonator tuned to a definite pitch and rung with a clapper, whereas a gong is a flat bronze plate that yields a deep, shimmering tone of indefinite pitch.
  • Cymbal: Similar in being a struck metal disc; differs because a cymbal is a small shallow disc beaten in pairs for a bright clash, while a gong is struck alone for a deep, sustained, long-decaying tone.
  • Tam-tam: Similar in being a large flat bronze gong; differs because tam-tam is the orchestra's technical name for the untuned flat gong, while gong proper can also denote the bossed, tuned gamelan instrument.
  • Drum: Similar in being a percussion instrument beaten for rhythm or signal; differs because a drum has a membrane stretched over a shell and gives a pitched, rhythmic thump, whereas the gong is a solid bronze plate that rings one long sonorous blow.
  • Gavel: Similar in being a struck object that silences and commands attention; differs because a gavel is a small wooden hammer tapped on a block to bring order, a dry judicial click rather than the gong's deep ceremonial resonance.
  • Chime: Similar in being a struck sound used as a signal; differs because chimes are tuned tubes or bells that ring a series of notes, while a gong delivers a single indefinite, absorbing stroke.
