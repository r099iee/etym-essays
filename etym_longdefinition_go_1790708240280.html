Primary Definition & Core Concept
Go is a primary Germanic irregular intransitive and transitive verb denoting movement, travel, or departure from one place to another, away from the speaker or current vantage point. At its fundamental physical level, it signifies self-propelled motion, passage, or transit across space. Because of its core status in human language, go has expanded into one of the most prolific and polysemous verbs in the English language, expressing progression, functioning, extension, transformation, state transition, elapsed time, and modal futurity.

Polysemy, Contextual Nuances & Idiomatic Senses

  • Spatial Extent, Reach & Limitation (Contextual Sense): In phrases detailing degree or scope (as in the prompt text: "as far as house-education and a country free school generally go"), go means to extend, reach, suffice, or be applicable up to a particular limit or boundary. It denotes the functional capability, standard extent, or efficacy of a resource or educational standard.
  • State Change & Copular Transition: Go functions as a linking verb indicating a transition into a negative, deteriorated, or altered state of being, as in to go blind, to go mad, to go bankrupt, or milk going sour. This contrasts with become or turn by typically framing the change as an uncontrolled or undesirable drift.
  • Operation, Functionality & Mechanism: In mechanical, electrical, and systemic contexts, to go means to operate, run, tick, or function properly (e.g., "the clock won't go", "make the engine go"). Conversely, when something ceases functioning or fails structurally, it 'gives way' or 'goes' (e.g., "the bridge's main support went").
  • Grammaticalized Future Auxiliary (The 'Going-To' Future): In modern English grammar, the progressive form going to (often contracted in spoken speech to gonna) has undergone complete grammaticalization into a periphrastic future modal marker expressing intention, premeditation, or immediate certainty (e.g., "it is going to rain").
  • Discourse, Quotation & Sound Mimicry (Colloquial): In informal modern register, go functions as a quotative verb meaning 'to say, utter, or exclaim' (e.g., "and then she goes, 'What are you doing?'") as well as to produce a characteristic sound (e.g., "the gun goes bang", "cows go moo").
  • Customary Phraseology & Idioms: The verb underpins countless core idioms, including as the saying goes (customary phrasing), go without saying (self-evident), make a go of it (achieve success), go under (perish/collapse), and from the word go (from the very beginning).

Semantic Reasoning & Historical Bridges
The immense polysemy of go stems from the cognitive metaphor LIFE AND TIME ARE MOVEMENT ALONG A PATH. Physical spatial relocation (walking away from a point) naturally mapped onto temporal progression (time goes by), functional operation (a machine moving through its cycles), reach of standards (as far as things go), and eventual state transformation (moving from a healthy state to a deteriorated one). Its grammatical development in English is uniquely characterized by profound morphological suppletion, having adopted its past tense went from the unrelated Middle English verb wenden ('to turn, wend').
