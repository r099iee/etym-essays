Notes: (1) The Latin verb fundō shows the old nasal present of the root (*ǵʰu-n-d-); its derivatives reach modern English through both the "pour" sense (fuse, fusion, confuse, refund, profuse) and the "cast metal" sense. (2) The English noun was spelled foundery/foundrie in the 17th century; the spelling foundry became standard as the "place" sense took over around 1700. (3) False friend: "to founder" (of a ship or plan, to sink or fail) is NOT from fundere — it is from Old French fondrer "to submerge", from Latin fundus "bottom".
