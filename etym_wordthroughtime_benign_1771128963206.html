- 14th-15th century - Enters English from Latin "benignus" (well-born, kind) meaning gracious and kindly, chiefly of rulers or deities; a courtly virtue of favorable disposition.
- 16th-18th century - Extends to circumstances, climates, and remedies: auspicious, favorable, mild rather than harsh.
- 19th-century medicine - Adopted into pathology to mark non-invasive, non-recurring growths against "malignant" ones; becomes the dominant technical sense of "not harmful."
- Contemporary - Split register: technical (of tumors, structurally harmless) and general (gentle, kindly), though the everyday sense now often carries a faintly clinical or condescending tone of mere harmlessness.
