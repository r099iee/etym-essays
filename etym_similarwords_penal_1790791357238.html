• Punitive: Similar because both relate directly to the infliction of punishment; differs because punitive focuses on the act, intent, or severity of punishing (e.g., punitive damages, punitive taxes), whereas penal relates more broadly to the formal, statutory system, institutions, and legal codes of criminal justice (e.g., penal code, penal institution).
  • Correctional: Similar because both refer to prison systems and criminal justice; differs because correctional is a modern, rehabilitative euphemism emphasizing the reform, education, and moral improvement of offenders rather than their retributive punishment.
  • Retributive: Similar because both concern punishment for wrongdoing; differs because retributive specifically emphasizes the moral philosophy of 'an eye for an eye' (inflicting equivalent suffering on the offender to balance the scales of justice), whereas penal encompasses deterrence, incapacitation, and institutional management.
  • Disciplinary: Similar because both involve enforcing rules through penalties; differs because disciplinary applies primarily to non-criminal, administrative, military, or school settings (e.g., disciplinary hearing, disciplinary action) rather than formal state criminal law.
  • Castigatory: Similar because both involve punishment; differs because castigatory emphasizes severe verbal reprimand, censure, or chastisement rather than formal statutory imprisonment or legal codes.
