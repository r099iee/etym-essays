• Crowd: Similar because both denote a large group of people; differs because a crowd is a neutral gathering, while a horde is vast, disordered, and menacing.
  • Throng: Similar because both denote a dense press of people; differs because a throng is merely dense and jostling, without the wild, uncivilized menace of a horde.
  • Mob: Similar because both denote a disorderly, potentially violent crowd; differs because a mob has urban, European associations, while a horde suggests vast, near-barbaric numbers.
  • Swarm: Similar because both denote a mass acting as one; differs because a swarm is insect-like and moves together with a single purpose, while a horde is more human or creature-like and disorderly.
  • Host: Similar because both can denote a great number of beings; differs because a host is an archaic, orderly concept of a great army, while a horde is undisciplined and threatening.
  • Legion: Similar because both denote a large body of people; differs because a legion is a disciplined, organized military unit, while a horde is chaotic and without order.
  • Multitude: Similar because both denote a great many people; differs because a multitude is neutral and simply emphasizes number, lacking the menace and disorder essential to a horde.
  • Herd: Similar because both denote a mass of beings; differs because a herd is a group of animals, neutral in tone, while a horde is threatening and quasi-barbaric even when applied to creatures.
  • Mass: Similar because both denote a large undifferentiated body; differs because a mass is static and neutral, while a horde is on the move and hostile.
  • Press: Similar because both denote a tightly packed body of people; differs because a press is the mere physical pressure of a crowd, without the wild, menacing quality of a horde.
