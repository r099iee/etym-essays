• Channel: Similar because both are long, narrow passages that convey or guide the flow of a liquid; differs because channel is a general word for any natural or artificial watercourse (including TV channels and English Channel), while gutter specifically means a shallow trough built at a roof's eaves or a street's side for carrying off rainwater.
  • Trough: Similar because both are long, open, shallow containers or channels; differs because a trough is typically a feeding or watering container for animals (or a low point between waves), while a gutter is specifically a drainage channel for water.
  • Drain: Similar because both carry off excess water; differs because a drain is usually an underground pipe or a covered opening into the sewage system, while a gutter is an open, visible channel above ground.
  • Ditch: Similar because both are channels for water along a roadside; differs because a ditch is a wide, dug trench, often with earthworks, and often larger and more rural, while a gutter is a narrow built channel along streets and rooftops.
  • Trench: Similar because both are long, narrow excavated channels; differs because a trench is dug in the earth for drainage, pipes, or warfare, much larger and usually unlined, while a gutter is a shallow manufactured trough.
  • Culvert: Similar because both convey water under or along a road; differs because a culvert is a covered pipe or tunnel carrying a stream under a road or railway, while a gutter is an open channel at the surface.
  • Conduit: Similar because both are passages that carry fluid; differs because a conduit is an enclosed pipe or tube (also figurative: a channel of transmission), while a gutter is an open trough and purely about liquid runoff.
  • Runnel: Similar because both are small channels for water; differs because a runnel is a small stream or rivulet, often natural, while a gutter is an artificial trough for rainwater.
  • Groove: Similar because both are long, narrow channels or depressions; differs because a groove is a thin cut or furrow in a surface (as in a record), not designed to carry water away from buildings or streets, while a gutter is a functional drainage trough.
  • Furrow: Similar because both are long narrow depressions; differs because a furrow is a line cut by a plow in a field (or a wrinkle), while a gutter is a man-made water channel for drainage.
  • Flume: Similar because both are channels that convey water; differs because a flume is an artificial channel carrying a controlled flow of water for power or transport (logging flume, amusement-park water slide), while a gutter is a small shallow trough for rainwater runoff.
  • Sewer: Similar because both carry away waste and water from where people live; differs because a sewer is an enclosed underground pipe system for sewage and storm water, while a gutter is the open surface channel that feeds into drains and sewers.
