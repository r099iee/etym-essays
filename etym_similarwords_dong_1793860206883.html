• Chime: Similar because both describe the musical ringing sound of a bell or metal instrument; differs because chime denotes a light, melodious, tuned, or harmonious sequence of bells, whereas dong evokes a single, heavy, deep, and reverberating resonant boom.
  • Peal: Similar because both relate to the sound of bells ringing out loudly; differs because a peal denotes a joyous, complex ringing pattern or sustained volley of church bells (or peals of laughter/thunder), whereas dong is the specific percussion sound of a singular stroke.
  • Toll: Similar because both describe the slow, resonant striking of a large bell; differs because toll carries a somber, mournful, and solemn ceremonial connotation (as for funerals or disasters), whereas dong is purely acoustic and echoic, readily used in cheerful nursery rhymes and ballad refrains.
  • Clang: Similar because both are loud metallic percussion sounds; differs because clang represents a harsh, sharp, dissonant metallic impact without musical warmth, whereas dong has a rich, deep, low-frequency reverberation that lingers.
  • Ring: Similar because both describe the acoustic vibration of struck metal; differs because ring is a broad, universal verb and noun encompassing bells of all sizes and telephone chimes, whereas dong specifically indicates low pitch and heavy resonance.
  • Ding-dong: Similar because both represent the sound of bells; differs because ding-dong is a rhythmic reduplicative pair alternating between high and low pitch, whereas dong isolated represents the lower, heavier bass beat.
