Note on historical morphology: The noun estrangement is formed by the suffixation of Old French -ment (from Latin -mentum, an instrumental noun suffix derived from PIE *-mn̥-to-) to the verbal base estrangier. In Vulgar Latin, the Classical Latin adjective extrāneus (source of Modern English strange via Old French estrange) was denominalized into the verb *extrāneāre. Initial Latin consonant clusters with s- (here ex- [eks] before dental/alveolar) routinely triggered prosthetic e- in Old French, while the cluster -n- followed by the glide -i- palatalized into the affricate [dʒ] represented orthographically as -g-.
