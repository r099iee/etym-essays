- Middle English 14th century - 'clambren/climberen', close kin of 'climb' and probably of 'claw'; meant to climb with effort, scrambling up using hands and feet, and could also be said of plants creeping upward.
- 16th-18th century - the settled form 'clamber' became the everyday word for hauling oneself up rocks, fences, banks and rigging; always awkward, unskilled, improvised movement, never a graceful ascent.
- 19th-20th century - extended figuratively: to clamber up a career, a social ladder or an idea, retaining its slow, ungainly, frictional tone of hard-won progress.
- Modern - a common, slightly informal verb; its heavy clomping rhythm (clam-ber) still echoes the physical effort it names, and it typically carries comic-affectionate colouring rather than neutral description.
