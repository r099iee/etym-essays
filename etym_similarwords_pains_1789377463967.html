• Trouble: Similar because both refer to inconvenience, effort, or disturbance undertaken to achieve a goal; differs because trouble emphasizes personal inconvenience, disruption, or burden placed upon someone, whereas taking pains emphasizes conscientious, meticulous care, precision, and active dedication to excellence.
  • Exertion: Similar because both involve the expenditure of vigorous effort and energy; differs because exertion denotes the vigorous physical or mental force applied to an immediate task (such as muscular strain), whereas pains denotes sustained, careful, detail-oriented thoroughness over time.
  • Diligence: Similar because both characterize persistent, attentive application to duty; differs because diligence is an enduring character trait or moral virtue of steady industriousness, whereas pains is the actual painstaking trouble, detail-checking, and labor applied in a specific instance.
  • Meticulousness: Similar because both highlight extreme care regarding minor details; differs because meticulousness can sometimes carry a negative connotation of obsessive, fussy over-attention to trivialities, whereas taking pains always conveys laudable, earnest devotion to producing an excellent outcome.
  • Labor: Similar because both involve arduous physical or mental toil; differs because labor refers broadly to the ongoing work or socioeconomic production itself, whereas taking pains refers specifically to the extra degree of care, precision, and trouble invested into that labor.
  • Travail: Similar because both historically link painful suffering to productive output (especially in childbirth and arduous work); differs because travail belongs to an elevated, literary register emphasizing agonizing, exhausting struggle against overwhelming adversity, whereas taking pains is commonly used for domestic, scholarly, and artistic diligence.
