• Cargo space / cargo bay: Similar because both name the same interior stowage volume of a vessel or aircraft; differs because they are modern descriptive phrases, technical and uncoloured, whereas ‘hold’ is the traditional nautical word with four centuries of usage behind it.
  • Storeroom: Similar because both are places where goods are kept; differs because a storeroom is land-based, built for shelving and access, and is not part of a vessel’s architecture.
  • Bilge: Similar because both are the lowest interior of a ship; differs because the bilge is the very bottom curve of the hull where water collects, not the cargo volume.
  • Cabin: Similar because both are interior compartments of a ship; differs because a cabin is fitted out for people — dry, lit, furnished — whereas the hold is the dark, unlined space for freight.
  • Grip: Similar because, as a noun from the verb, a ‘hold’ is a seizure with the hand; differs because ‘grip’ is purely physical and momentary, while a ‘hold’ can be figurative and lasting (a hold over someone).
  • Grasp: Similar because both mean to seize and keep; differs because ‘grasp’ also means mental comprehension, a sense the noun ‘hold’ does not share.
  • Clutch: Similar because both are firm seizures; differs because ‘clutch’ is tighter, more urgent, and more emotional — the clutch of fear — than a steady ‘hold’.
  • Stronghold: Similar because both are places that are ‘held’; differs because a stronghold is a fortress built on land, formed from the verb-derived noun with ‘strong’.
  • Foothold / handhold: Similar because both are small literal ‘holds’ for the limbs; differs because they are specific climbing terms — concrete, tiny, physical — and nothing to do with ships.
  • Storage: Similar because both concern the keeping of goods; differs because ‘storage’ is abstract and general, covering warehouses and digital memory alike, with no image of a ship’s interior.
