• Practical: Similar because both deal with real situations and workable solutions; differs because practical is the broad everyday word, while pragmatic implies a more deliberate philosophy of judging by results.
  • Realistic: Similar because both face facts accurately; differs because realistic stresses seeing things as they are, while pragmatic focuses on choosing effective action among constraints.
  • Utilitarian: Similar because both value usefulness and consequences; differs because utilitarian invokes a specific ethical theory (the greatest good for the greatest number), which pragmatic does not entail.
  • Expedient: Similar because both do what works; differs because expedient often hints at moral compromise — doing what is convenient rather than right — while pragmatic can be ethically neutral or even positive.
  • Sensible: Similar because both are grounded and reasonable; differs because sensible stresses good judgment and moderation, while pragmatic stresses effectiveness in action.
  • Down-to-earth: Similar because both avoid abstraction; differs because down-to-earth is informal and stresses plainness, while pragmatic is neutral in register and can be intellectual.
  • Hard-headed: Similar because both refuse sentimentality; differs because hard-headed stresses toughness and unsentimental calculation, which pragmatic need not emphasize.
  • Businesslike: Similar because both are efficient and matter-of-fact; differs because businesslike implies brisk efficiency in tasks, while pragmatic implies a whole way of deciding.
  • Matter-of-fact: Similar because both stick to the factual and concrete; differs because matter-of-fact names a flat, unemotional manner, not a philosophy of consequences.
  • No-nonsense: Similar because both cut through theory to action; differs because no-nonsense is informal and stresses briskness, while pragmatic is the broader, more philosophical term.
  • Unsentimental: Similar because both resist romantic or ideological appeal; differs because unsentimental is a narrower emotional description, not a method of decision-making.
  • Doctrinaire (antonymic foil): Similar because both are adjectives used of political and intellectual temperament; differs because doctrinaire clings to doctrine regardless of outcomes — the exact opposite of the pragmatic stance.
