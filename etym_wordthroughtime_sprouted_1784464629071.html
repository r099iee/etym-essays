“Sprout” entered English meaning to put forth shoots — new growth breaking from seed, root, or stem, the very act of a plant beginning to grow.
“Sprout” through the centuries widened from plants to anything that appears suddenly and in abundance — buildings sprout, rumors sprout, stories sprout up all over — the image of rapid germination applied everywhere.
“Sprout” in the 19th century also named the edible young shoot itself — bean sprouts, Brussels sprouts — the noun for the growth the verb describes.
“Sprouted” today is the past of that sudden springing growth: weeds sprouted overnight, tales sprouted up wherever the storyteller went — small vigorous beginnings multiplying at once.
