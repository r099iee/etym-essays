• Pounding: Similar because both describe repeated heavy striking; differs because pounding is heavier, more regular and rhythmic, like a pestle or a drummer's fist, while thumping is duller, clumsier and less rhythmically precise.
  • Beating: Similar because both are general words for repeated striking; differs because beating is neutral and extremely broad, while thumping insists on the weight and dullness of the blows.
  • Hammering: Similar because both denote insistent, repeated striking; differs because hammering is sharp, mechanical, tool-like and directed, whereas thumping is blunt, unwieldy and ponderous.
  • Battering: Similar because both involve heavy, repeated impacts; differs because battering implies savage, damaging force and a victim being worn down, while thumping is heavy but less overtly destructive and more comic.
  • Banging: Similar because both describe loud striking; differs because banging is louder, clattering, hollow and often door- or table-based, while thumping is lower, duller and weightier.
  • Smacking: Similar because both name blows; differs because smacking is the sharp slap of an open hand with a bright crack, while thumping is a heavy, dull, closed impact.
  • Whacking: Similar because both are sound-symbolic words for striking; differs because whacking is the crisp, ringing crack of a stick, brighter and sharper in pitch, while thumping is the low, dull, massive impact — the exact acoustic contrast in Tolkien's sentence.
  • Knocking: Similar because both involve percussive contact; differs because knocking is the sharp, repeated rap of a hard object, usually on a door, lighter and more regular, while thumping is heavier and blunter.
  • Drumming: Similar because both suggest repeated, rhythmic impacts; differs because drumming is deliberately rhythmic and often finger- or tool-based, while thumping is heavy, clumsy and only incidentally rhythmical.
  • Throbbing: Similar because both, of the heart or pulse, describe violent, insistent beating; differs because throbbing is a continuous painful ache or pulse, while thumping is the heavy, sudden slam of the heart.
  • Pulsing: Similar because both can describe heart or blood beating strongly; differs because pulsing is regular and gentle, while thumping is hard and violent.
  • Clubbing: Similar because both describe blunt, heavy impacts; differs because clubbing specifically names the instrument (a club) and implies deliberate attack, while thumping is the dull impact itself.
