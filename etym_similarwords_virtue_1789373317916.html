• Morality: Similar because both concern ethical conduct, goodness, and distinguishing right from wrong; differs because morality refers to the broader, often external system of rules, codes, cultural customs, and social standards of right behavior, whereas virtue refers to the internalized character disposition, personal excellence, and moral habitus of an individual.
  • Integrity: Similar because both signify high moral uprightness and adherence to ethical standards; differs because integrity emphasizes wholeness, consistency, honesty, and incorruptibility under pressure (acting consistently in private and public), whereas virtue encompasses specific excellences such as compassion, bravery, charity, and wisdom.
  • Righteousness: Similar because both denote moral goodness and ethical rectitude; differs because righteousness carries strong biblical, theological, and juridical overtones of divine justification, holy obedience, or standing blameless before God's law, often with a potential connotation of sanctimony or self-righteousness, whereas virtue is grounded in classical philosophy and human character.
  • Merit: Similar because both designate commendable qualities, assets, or advantageous features of a person or thing; differs because merit focuses on deserved reward, practical worth, measurable performance, or credit earned through effort, whereas virtue emphasizes ethical nobility, intrinsic goodness, or inherent active power.
  • Efficacy: Similar because both historically refer to the active capacity of a remedy, substance, or action to produce a desired effect; differs because efficacy is purely functional, chemical, or operational without any moral or spiritual dimension, whereas virtue retains a legacy of living vital potency and moral excellence.
  • Goodness: Similar because both denote positive ethical qualities and benevolence; differs because goodness is a warm, instinctive, and general quality of kindness and unpretentious decency, whereas virtue denotes a cultivated, disciplined, and habituated moral strength achieved through practice and wisdom.
