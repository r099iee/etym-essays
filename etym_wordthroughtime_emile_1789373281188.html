- [Roman Antiquity] - Borne as the patrician gentilicium Aemilius (from aemulus 'rival, zealous, striving') by the illustrious Gens Aemilia (e.g. Aemilius Paullus) - [connotation: patrician nobility, military triumph, civic authority]
- [Enlightenment (1762)] - Immortalized across Europe by Jean-Jacques Rousseau's foundational pedagogical treatise Émile, ou De l'éducation - [connotation: natural innocence, progressive education, uncorrupted virtue]
- [19th c. – Naturalism & Belle Époque] - Embodied by Émile Zola, pioneering titan of literary Naturalism (Les Rougon-Macquart) and fearless civic martyr of the Dreyfus Affair with his open letter 'J'Accuse...!' - [connotation: uncompromising social realism, investigative courage, intellectual defiance of state tyranny]
- [20th–21st c. – Modernity] - Maintained as a classic, dignified personal name throughout the Francophone world and globally, celebrated in sociology (Émile Durkheim) and philosophy - [connotation: intellectual rigor, humanitarian justice, timeless cultural prestige]
