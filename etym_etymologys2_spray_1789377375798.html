PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*sper- / *spereg-
"to scatter, strew, burst, sprout"
Note: Ancestral root of both the botanical lineage ('spreading shoot') and the liquid lineage ('scattered droplets').
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*sprakô / *sprawją
"slender twig, shoot, brushwood; that which sprouts out"
↓
Old English (c. 8th–10th c.)
*spræc / *spræġ
"twig, small branch, brushwood"
Note: Survived in toponymy and regional dialects before Middle English recorded attestations.
↓
Middle English (c. 1250–1300)
spray / sprai
"slender shoot, sprig, leafy branchlet"
Note: In the 16th century, this botanical noun phonetically merged with Middle Dutch 'spraeyen' ('to sprinkle/spray water').
↓
Modern English (present)
SPRAY
