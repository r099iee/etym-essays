PRUDENCE is built from TWO PIE roots that converge in Latin; each chain is given in isolation.

CHAIN 1 — root *per- “forward, in front of, through, first”:
1. Proto-Indo-European *per- “forward, before, in front of, through” (reconstructed; one of the most productive roots of the family).
2. → Latin prō “before, forward, on behalf of” (preposition and prefix; from *per-).
3. → Latin prōvidēre “to look forward, to foresee” (prō- + vidēre “to see”; classical Latin).
4. → Latin prōvidēns (present participle) “foreseeing.”
5. → syncope (loss of the medial syllable): prōvidēns → prūdēns (classical Latin) “foreseeing, wise, skilled.”
6. → Latin prūdentia (abstract noun) “foresight, practical wisdom, prudence” (classical; the standard Roman translation of Greek phronēsis).
7. → Old French prudence.
8. → English prudence, attested 14th c. — OUR CARD: PRUDENCE.

CHAIN 2 — root *weid- “to see, to know”:
1. Proto-Indo-European *weid- “to see, to know” (reconstructed; the root of Sanskrit veda, Greek oîda “I know,” i.e. “I have seen”).
2. → Latin vidēre “to see” (perfect vīdī; from *weid-).
3. → Latin prōvidēre “to foresee” (with prō-, converging with Chain 1).

Debate / nuance: the syncope prōvidēns → prūdēns is the standard, well-attested development (the same syncope appears in other Latin words such as calidus → caldus); it is not in dispute. The two chains are separate: *per- and *weid- are distinct roots that meet only inside the Latin compound prōvidēre.
