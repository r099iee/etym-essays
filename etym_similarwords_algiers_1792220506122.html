• Algeria: Similar because both share the exact same Arabic root al-Jazā'ir; differs because Algeria denotes the sovereign North African nation—the largest country in Africa by geographic area—whereas Algiers refers specifically to its capital city, historic port, and urban metropolitan core.
  • Alger: Similar because both designate the identical Mediterranean capital city; differs because Alger is the official French and contemporary international Romance spelling, whereas Algiers is the traditional English name featuring an Anglicized excrescent terminal -s.
  • Casbah: Similar because both are intimately connected with the historic identity of the city; differs because the Casbah designates specifically the ancient walled Islamic citadel, stepped alleys, and Moorish palaces rising above the harbor, whereas Algiers encompasses the entire modern urban metropolis.
  • Tunis: Similar because both are prominent North African Mediterranean capitals with profound Ottoman corsair and French colonial legacies; differs because Tunis lies farther east near the ruins of ancient Carthage and features distinct Hafsid and Tunisian architectural traditions, whereas Algiers was the premier naval regency of the central Maghreb.
  • Tripoli: Similar because both operated as semi-autonomous Ottoman Barbary regencies renowned for privateering across the Mediterranean; differs because Tripoli is the capital of modern Libya, whereas Algiers developed into the political and administrative hub of French North Africa.
  • Oran: Similar because both are major coastal port cities in Algeria with extensive French colonial architecture; differs because Oran is situated in western Algeria with heavy Spanish historical influence, whereas Algiers is the historic political seat of power and cultural capital.
