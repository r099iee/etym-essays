• Bluster: Similar because both involve loud, aggressive, and boastful posturing without real substance; differs because bluster emphasizes noisy, turbulent talk, verbal bullying, or empty threats like a howling gale, whereas bravado specifically feigns heroic courage or daring in the face of peril.
  • Swagger: Similar because both denote an ostentatious display of confidence; differs because swagger is primarily behavioral, physical, and postural (an arrogant physical strut or demeanor), whereas bravado is a psychological performance of bravery intended to intimidate or compensate for fear.
  • Vainglory: Similar because both involve excessive boasting; differs because vainglory is an ancient moral and theological sin rooted in hollow personal vanity, narcissism, and unearned pride in one's achievements, whereas bravado is specifically a martial or heroic pretense of courage.
  • Gasconade: Similar because both denote extravagant boastfulness; differs because gasconade refers specifically to humorous, hyperbolic brags regarding one's personal prowess and lineage, historically attributed to the natives of Gascony in southwest France (like D'Artagnan).
  • Rodomontade: Similar because both represent boastful bluster; differs because rodomontade derives from King Rodomonte in Ariosto's Orlando Furioso, carrying a literary, inflated, bombastic tone of heroic ranting that borders on comic theatricality.
  • Fanfaronade: Similar because both denote swaggering ostentation; differs because fanfaronade (from Spanish fanfarrón) evokes musical fanfare and flamboyant, noisy showmanship designed to dazzle crowds, rather than just masking fear.
  • Audacity: Similar because both involve taking daring risks; differs because audacity represents real, daring boldness (which may be insolent, heroic, or shocking), whereas bravado is largely an exaggerated or fake outward facade.
  • Temerity: Similar because both describe reckless disregard for danger; differs because temerity is the foolish, rash quality of underestimating real danger (foolhardiness), whereas bravado is the outward display of not caring about danger.
  • Ostentation: Similar because both involve showy displays; differs because ostentation focuses on the vulgar, pretentious display of wealth, luxury, learning, or status, whereas bravado is strictly an exhibition of boldness or courage.
