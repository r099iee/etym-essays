Latin (c. 1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE)
animalia mixta
"mixed animals; blended livestock"
↓
Medieval Latin (c. 12th c.)
mixta / mesta
"association of livestock owners,
annual gathering of mixed flocks"
↓
Old Spanish (c. 13th c.)
mesta
"council of ranchers,
guild of migratory stock owners"
↓
Spanish (c. 15th c.)
mesteño
"belonging to the Mesta, stray animal,
unclaimed livestock without owner"
↓
Mexican Spanish (c. 18th c.)
mesteño / mestengo
"wild feral horse or stray cattle"
↓
American English (c. 1808)
mustang
"feral horse of the western plains"

Note: English 'mustang' is an Anglicization of Mexican Spanish 'mesteño', likely blended with or influenced by 'mostrenco' (stray, unowned animal, from 'mostrar', to exhibit publicly at fairs). A dialectal variant 'mestengo' mediated the nasalized '-ang' ending.
