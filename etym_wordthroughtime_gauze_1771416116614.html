- [16th-century luxury import] - Enters English as the name of a sheer, transparent silk from the East, a costly dress and veil fabric of the elite.
- [17th-18th century diversification] - The fabric becomes commoner - curtain gauze, millinery veils - while its first figurative use appears: anything thin enough to see through dimly.
- [19th-century medical turn] - Gauze becomes standard surgical dressing material, open-woven and sterile; the concrete sense shifts from fashion to medicine.
- [Contemporary register] - A split usage: clinical (medical gauze) and atmospheric (a gauze of fog or time), both keeping the core idea of a translucent filtering membrane.
