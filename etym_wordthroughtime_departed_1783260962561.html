- Middle English root - departed is the past participle of depart (from Old French departir, to divide, part), whose literal sense of gone away was always primary.
- 16th-century euphemistic extension - from the sixteenth century the participle was pressed into reverential service as a term for the dead, the departed framing death as a journey of departure and softening the blunt fact of death.
- Contemporary sense - departed remains the most solemn and literary of the words for the dead, at home in obituaries and formal prose, where its gentleness - refusing to say dead outright - is precisely its point.
