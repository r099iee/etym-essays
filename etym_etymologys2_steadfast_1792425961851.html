Note on Germanic cognates and morphological structure: The compound steadfast is a native Germanic inherited syntagm. The first element, stead (Old English stede), descends from PIE *steh₂-tis ("a standing"), producing Old High German stat (modern German Stadt "city" and Stätte "place"), Old Saxon stedi, Old Norse staðr, and Gothic staþs. The second element, fast (Old English fæst), descends from Proto-Germanic *fastuz ("firm, solid, bound"), producing Old Frisian fest, Old Saxon fast, Old High German festi (modern German fest), and Old Norse fastr. Parallel compounds emerged across northern Europe: Old Norse possessed staðfastr ("steadfast, firm, obstinate"), Middle Low German stēdevast, and Middle Dutch stede-vast. Originally designating physical immobility in defense—refusing to yield ground—the compound underwent an early metaphoric shift in West Germanic into spiritual and ethical loyalty.
