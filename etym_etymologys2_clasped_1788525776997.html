PIE (reconstructed)
*gel- / *glebʰ-
"to form into a ball, mass, clutch, stick together"
(ancestral Indo-European base of Germanic words for clamping, cleaving, and clutching)
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*klamp- / *klap-
"to pinch together, fasten, hold firmly"
↓
Old English (pre-10th c.; related formation)
clyppan
"to embrace, hug, hold in one's arms"
(source of modern English 'clip'; closely related in root semantics to the clasp-group)
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
clapsen / claspen
"to fasten with a buckle or brooch; to embrace, hold tightly"
(showing phonetic metathesis of -ps- to -sp-)
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
clasp
"to fasten securely; to grasp firmly with the hands"
↓
Modern English (participial & past tense form)
clasped
"firmly held in an embrace or grasp; mechanically fastened"

Note: The transition from Middle English clapsen to modern clasp represents a classic example of liquid/sibilant metathesis (reversal of adjacent consonant sounds), identical to the sound shifts seen in Old English æxe becoming modern ask and wæps becoming wasp.
