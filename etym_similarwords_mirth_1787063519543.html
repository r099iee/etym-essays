• Glee: Similar because both express visible, high-spirited joy and delight; differs because glee often carries a childish, triumphant, or sometimes malicious nuance (gloating with glee at a rival's failure), whereas mirth is broader, festive, wholesome, and communal.
  • Merriment: Similar because both denote lively, laughter-filled festive cheer; differs because merriment emphasizes playful, boisterous activity and partying (eating, drinking, dancing), while mirth can also encompass witty appreciation, aesthetic delight, and emotional lightheartedness.
  • Jollity: Similar because both capture convivial good humor and high spirits; differs because jollity implies hearty, robust, and often noisy good-fellowship (associated with stout feasts and good cheer), whereas mirth possesses greater poetic depth and literary refinement.
  • Hilarity: Similar because both involve intense amusement and laughter; differs because hilarity denotes noisy, uproarious, and often chaotic or uncontrolled laughter, while mirth encompasses warm, harmonious, and graceful joy.
  • Joy: Similar because both refer to positive emotional states; differs because joy is a profound, deep, often spiritual or serene emotion that can exist in complete silence, whereas mirth is buoyant, social, and openly expressed through laughter and festivity.
  • Cheer: Similar because both describe pleasant, uplifting spirits; differs because cheer focuses on optimism, encouragement, and hospitable welcome (bringing cheer to the sick), whereas mirth is the active spark of laughter and fun.
