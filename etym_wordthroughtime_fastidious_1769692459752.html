- 15th–16th c. 'Disdainful, haughty, proud' — applied to someone who looks down on others, carrying strong social reproach for arrogant contempt.
- 17th–18th c. 'Squeamish, easily disgusted' — the emphasis moves from arrogance toward a delicate, oversensitive temper readily repelled by anything coarse or vulgar.
- 19th c. 'Hard to please, over-particular' — in Regency society a mark of refined taste, but still implying an exacting, irritable niceness that makes others uncomfortable.
- Contemporary 'Extremely attentive to accuracy and detail' — now largely positive and professional, describing meticulous standards (e.g., a fastidious editor), with the old disdainful edge softened or lost.
