- Latin — the lot decides: sors means a lot, share, or lot-cast — destiny portioned out, and by extension a kind or category.
- Old French — sorting out: assorter means to match, mate, and allot things into kinds — pairing like with like in wardrobe and warehouse.
- 17th c. — English adopts it: to assort is to distribute into sorts, and assortment names both the sorting and its varied result.
- 18th–19th c. — commerce claims it: shopkeepers, confectioners, and catalogues make assorted goods the language of choice and plenty.
- 20th c. — the chocolate-box century: assorted biscuits and assorted chocolates carry the word into nearly every household.
- Today — variety as virtue: from streaming catalogues to holiday piles, an assortment promises that there is something for everyone in the heap.
