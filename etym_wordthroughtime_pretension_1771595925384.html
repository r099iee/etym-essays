- [16th century neutral claim] - [Enters meaning a claim or assertion of a right, quality, or standing; connotation at first neutral — the act of stretching a claim out in front of oneself]
- [17th-18th century the unearned claim] - [Drifts pejorative: a claim regarded as unearned or doubtful — the assertion of a standing not actually possessed]
- [19th century social and literary] - [Ruskin uses it of would-be critics who stake claims to artistic authority their ignorance exposes as hollow; pretension becomes the empty gesture held up to be seen — assertion beyond warrant]
- [20th-21st century plural ambitions] - [The plural 'pretensions' softens toward mere ambitious aspirations, while the singular keeps its accusatory force: a claim of merit or knowledge that does not match reality]
