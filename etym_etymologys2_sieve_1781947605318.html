SIEVE is a native English word inherited from Proto-Germanic, with a secure Indo-European root. Linear chain: 1. PIE *seib- / *seip- "to pour out, strain, sift, drip" (a root denoting the separation of liquid or fine matter; the exact vocalic grade and the aspiration of the final consonant are debated in detail, but the root is securely attested by its descendants). 2. Proto-Germanic *sibą "sieve" (a noun formed on the strain/sift root; reconstructed from the attested Germanic set, which also includes the verb-forming derivative seen in Old English siftan "to sift"). 3. Old English sife "sieve" (attested in Old English glosses from the 8th–9th centuries; the neuter noun of the same stock as siftan "to sift"). 4. Middle English sive, syve, seve "sieve" (13th–15th centuries; the spelling settles toward the modern form). 5. Modern English SIEVE "sieve" — a mesh utensil for straining or sifting; pronounced /sɪv/ (the written -ie- reflects a former long vowel, later shortened). Related facts: the sibling verb sift (Old English siftan) comes from the same root, as do German Sieb "sieve" and Dutch zeef "sieve". The secure cognates are the Germanic set; no non-Germanic stage needs to be invented, and the chain PIE → Proto-Germanic → Old English → Middle English → SIEVE is fully attested.
