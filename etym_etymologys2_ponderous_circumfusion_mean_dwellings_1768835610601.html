Root 3 — MEAN:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*mey-no-
"to think, intend"
(uncertain)
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*mainō
"an intention, complaint"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*mainijaną
"to intend, say"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
mænan
"to mean, intend, say"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
menen
"to mean, intend"
↓
English (14th c.)
mean
"to intend, signify"

Debate: the PIE root *mey-no- is uncertain. English has three distinct words spelled mean: "to intend" (this chain, < OE mænan), "middle" (< Latin medianus), and "low, poor, shabby" (< OE gemanne, literally "common", from mann "person") — the sense "poor or shabby" in "mean dwellings" belongs to the third, a separate word.

Root 4 — DWELLINGS:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*dʰwel-
"to dull, confuse"
(uncertain)
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*dwaljaną
"to delay, dwell"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
dwellan
"to hinder, delay; linger"
↓
English (13th c.)
dwell
"to remain, reside"
↓
English (14th c.)
dwelling
"a place of residence"
↓
dwellings
"places of residence"

Debate: the PIE root *dʰwel- "to dull, confuse" is only tentatively linked to PGmc *dwaljaną; Old English dwellan originally meant "to lead astray, hinder", shifting to "to linger" and hence "to reside".
