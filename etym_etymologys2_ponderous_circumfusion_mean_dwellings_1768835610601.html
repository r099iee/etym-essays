CHAIN 1 — PONDEROUS PIE *(s)pen- 'to draw out, stretch, spin' (reconstructed, c. 4000–3000 BCE) → Latin pendere 'to hang; to weigh out' (a balance hangs a weight against a counterweight; attested Classical Latin) → Latin pondus 'weight' (from pendere) → Latin ponderōsus 'weighty, heavy' (from pondus; Classical Latin) → Old French pondereus / ponderos 'heavy, weighty' (Medieval period) → Middle English ponderous 'heavy, weighty' (15th c.) → English ponderous 'heavy, slow, laborious' (target root word) CHAIN 2 — CIRCUMFUSION PIE *gheu- 'to pour' (reconstructed; also spelled *gheu-d-; attested by Greek khéō 'I pour', Sanskrit juhóti 'pours a libation', Old English ġēotan 'to pour') → Latin fundere 'to pour' (1st c. BCE, Classical Latin) → Latin circumfundere 'to pour around' (circum 'around' + fundere; attested in Pliny the Elder, 1st c. CE) → Latin circumfūsiō, circumfūsiōnis 'a pouring around; the act of being surrounded' (Late Latin) → English circumfusion (16th–17th c., a learned coinage; target root word) CHAIN 3 — MEAN (the sense 'poor, shabby, inferior') PIE *mei- 'to change, exchange' (reconstructed, c. 4000–3000 BCE; source also of Latin mūtāre 'to change', Greek ameíbein 'to exchange') → *moinos 'exchanged, shared' (zero-grade formation of *mei-) → Proto-Germanic *gamainiz 'shared, common' (with collective prefix *ga-; source of Gothic gamains 'common, defiled') → Old English gemǣne 'common, shared by all' (attested before the Norman Conquest, 8th c. onwards) → Middle English mene 'common, mean, inferior' (13th–14th c.) → English mean 'poor, shabby, of low quality or rank' (target root word) NOTE ON HOMONYMS (MEAN): The root word 'mean' in this note is a homonym with three entirely separate etymologies. (1) mean 'to intend, to signify' is a DIFFERENT word, from Old English mǣnan 'to say, complain, intend', descended from PIE *men- 'to think'; it is unrelated to the 'common' sense. (2) mean 'average, middle' is a THIRD word, from Old French meien < Latin medianus 'middle' < Latin medius 'middle', from PIE *medhyo-; again unrelated. Only the 'common/inferior' sense descends from *mei- and gemǣne as chained above. The modern American sense 'cruel, unkind' (19th c.) is a further development of the 'low, unprincipled' sense. CHAIN 4 — DWELLINGS PIE *dheu- 'to be cloudy, smoky, obscure' (reconstructed), extended form *dhwel- 'to become dim, be confused, lead astray' → Proto-Germanic *dwaljan 'to lead astray, deceive, delay' (zero-grade causative) → Old English dwellan 'to hinder, mislead, cause to linger' (attested before 1000 CE); also the noun dwola 'error, heresy' → Middle English dwellen 'to tarry, linger, remain' (12th–13th c.); sense shift from 'to be delayed' to 'to stay' → Middle English dwellen 'to reside, make one's home' (14th c.) → English dwell 'to live, reside' + suffix -ing → English dwelling 'a place of residence' (target root word; the noun formed from the gerund)
