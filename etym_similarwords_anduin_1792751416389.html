• Baranduin (Brandywine): Similar because both are major Sindarin-named rivers in western/central Middle-earth associated with Hobbit history and bearing the hydronymic root duin; differs because the Baranduin ("Golden-brown river") is the western river defining the boundary of the Shire, whereas Anduin is the colossal continental spine flowing across Wilderland and Gondor.
  • Sirion: Similar because both are legendary great continental rivers in Tolkien's legendarium; differs because Sirion was the sacred river of Beleriand in the First Age, originating from Eithel Sirion and guarded by Ulmo, which sank beneath the sea during the War of Wrath, whereas Anduin is the primary artery of the Second and Third Ages in Middle-earth proper.
  • Celduin (River Running): Similar because both are significant eastward-flowing rivers of Wilderland; differs because the Celduin flows from the Lonely Mountain (Erebor) south-east into the Sea of Rhûn, whereas Anduin flows north-to-south over thousands of miles into the ocean.
  • Danube: Similar because both function as epic, continental river highways in geographic and mythic reality; differs because the Danube is an actual European river flowing through Central and Eastern Europe to the Black Sea, which served as Tolkien's real-world geopolitical and geographic analogue for the vastness of the Anduin.
  • Rhine: Similar because both represent contested martial frontiers separating civilized kingdoms from wild forests and threatening barbarian or imperial forces; differs because the Rhine is a real historical borderland of Roman Gaul and Germania, whereas Anduin is an integral element of Tolkien's mythopoeic geography.
  • Entwash (Onodló): Similar because both are prominent waterways in Wilderland; differs because the Entwash is a major western tributary rising in Fangorn Forest that empties into the Anduin through vast marshlands, rather than the primary continental trunk itself.
  • waterway: Similar because both denote navigable aquatic corridors for transport and settlement; differs because waterway is a generic, modern infrastructural term, lacking the epic mythological majesty and cultural significance of Anduin.
  • artery: Similar because both are used metaphorically to describe a central geographic channel sustaining populations and commerce; differs because artery is an anatomical or abstract transport metaphor, whereas Anduin is a living, enchanted hydrographic entity endowed with ancient Elvish naming.
  • frontier: Similar because both serve as demarcation lines between territorial powers; differs because a frontier can be an arbitrary political boundary or mountain range, whereas Anduin is a physical natural body of water that happens to define the boundaries of Gondor, Rohan, and Wilderland.
  • flumen: Similar because both represent flowing river bodies; differs because flumen is a classical Latin geographical noun for a large river, whereas Anduin is a specific proper hydronym laden with Sindarin philology.
