• Grill / grille: Similar because both are barred structures from the same Latin family (cratis, craticula); differs because ‘grill’ means the rack for cooking over fire or a car’s front bars, and ‘grille’ is the French-borrowed ornamental screen, while ‘grate’ is specifically the hearth frame for fuel.
  • Grid: Similar because both are arrangements of parallel or crossing bars; differs because ‘grid’ is abstract and geometric — any lattice of lines, from graph paper to power networks — with no hearth meaning.
  • Lattice: Similar because both are crossed-bar frameworks; differs because a lattice is usually ornamental or structural woodwork of crossed laths (trellis, window lattice), not a fire frame.
  • Gridiron: Similar because both are barred frames over a fire; differs because a gridiron is a portable cooking rack for food laid directly over coals, and by slang extension a football field.
  • Rack: Similar because both are frames of bars for holding things; differs because a rack serves storage or display (drying rack, luggage rack) and is not associated with fire.
  • Screen: Similar because both are metal hearth fittings; differs because a fire screen stands in front of the fire to catch sparks and heat, while the grate holds the fuel itself.
  • Fire basket: Similar because both hold burning fuel above the hearth floor; differs because a fire basket is the movable, basket-like portable form of the grate, without fixed built-in bars.
  • Trellis: Similar because both are lattices of bars; differs because a trellis is a wooden garden support for climbing plants — decorative, botanical, never fired.
