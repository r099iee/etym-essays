[Root 1: GUN (Middle English gonne / Old Norse Gunnhildr)]
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*gʷʰen-
"to strike, slay, kill"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*gunþiz
"battle, fight, combat"
↓
Old Norse (c. 8th–11th c.)
gunnr / Gunnhildr
"war, battle" / female name ("war-battle")
↓
Anglo-Latin / Middle English (c. 1330–1380)
Domina Gunilda / gonne, gunne
"siege engine named Lady Gunilda; cannon, firearm"
Note: The word gun originated from the personal name Gunhilda, applied to a large ballista at Windsor Castle (recorded in 1330 as 'una magna balista de cornu quae vocatur Domina Gunilda'), transferred to early gunpowder artillery.
