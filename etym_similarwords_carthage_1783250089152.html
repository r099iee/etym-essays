• Emporium: Similar because it also denotes a great trading mart at the centre of Mediterranean exchange; differs because it isolates the commerce while Carthage names a whole sovereign city-state with an empire of its own.
  • Metropolis: Similar because it names a great ancient city of consequence; differs because a metropolis is a mother-city, whereas Carthage was itself a colony of Tyre — the relation is genealogical, not equivalent.
  • Tyre: Similar because it was the Phoenician mother-city that founded Carthage and shared its mercantile culture; differs because Tyre stayed on the Levantine coast and declined in power while the daughter city outgrew her.
  • Sidon: Similar because it was the other great Phoenician port of the same civilisation; differs because it was not Carthage's coloniser and never built a western empire to rival the African colony.
  • Punic: Similar because it denotes the same Carthaginian civilisation and language; differs because Punic is the adjective/noun for the people, tongue, and wars, not the name of the city itself.
  • Byblos: Similar because it was an ancient Phoenician trading city of the same world; differs because Byblos was an early Lebanese port tied to the papyrus trade and never fought Rome.
  • Babylon: Similar because it is another famous ancient city whose fall became a byword for ruin; differs because Babylon's destruction is chiefly biblical and legendary, while Carthage's was a specific, documented act of Roman annihilation.
  • City-state: Similar because Carthage was a sovereign city-state; differs because this is a generic political category that carries none of the name's historical, commercial, or tragic freight.
