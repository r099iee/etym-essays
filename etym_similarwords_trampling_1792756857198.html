• treading: Similar because both involve placing the feet upon a surface and stepping over it; differs because treading can be gentle, deliberate, rhythmic, or neutral (e.g., treading water, treading softly), whereas trampling always conveys heavy, destructive, crushing, or aggressive force.
  • stomping: Similar because both denote placing the feet down with deliberate, noisy, heavy downward impacts; differs because stomping emphasizes the acoustic thump and emotional frustration of the stomper, whereas trampling emphasizes the physical destruction and flattening inflicted upon the object beneath.
  • crushing: Similar because both result in the severe flattening, shattering, or deformation of an underlying object; differs because crushing can be executed by any heavy weight, hydraulic press, or falling boulder, whereas trampling specifically requires the repeated stepping of biological feet or hooves.
  • stamping: Similar because both describe pounding the feet downwards against the ground; differs because stamping is often stationary (stamping one's feet to keep warm, stamping an official seal), whereas trampling involves locomotion, traversing territory, and pulverizing terrain underfoot.
  • overriding: Similar because in abstract or figurative contexts, both describe sweeping aside rules, objections, or concerns; differs because overriding operates through administrative, legal, or mechanical superiority, whereas trampling conveys brutal, visceral, disrespectful violence (as in trampling upon someone's dignity).
  • infringing: Similar because both denote encroaching upon or violating legal rights or established boundaries; differs because infringing is a precise, dry legalistic term for breach of copyright or territory, whereas trampling evokes emotional outrage and callous contempt for the victim.
  • violating: Similar because both describe transgressing sacred boundaries, agreements, or liberties; differs because violating is broad and moral/legal, whereas trampling serves as an evocative somatic metaphor of physically treading rights into the dirt.
  • flouting: Similar because both show contemptuous disregard for laws, conventions, or feelings; differs because flouting emphasizes open mockery, scornful defiance, and verbal flaunting, whereas trampling focuses on active, crushing subjugation.
  • pulverizing: Similar because both reduce solid physical structures to debris; differs because pulverizing grinds matter into fine dust or powder through extreme mechanical force, whereas trampling flattens, bruises, and tears surfaces underfoot.
  • scuffing: Similar because both involve dragging or bringing shoes into contact with ground surfaces; differs because scuffing merely abrades or marks the surface lightly with minimal downward weight, whereas trampling exerts total crushing mass.
