• worsens: Similar because both denote causing a situation, condition, or state to decline in quality or increase in harm; differs because worsens is plain native Germanic, applicable universally to any deterioration, whereas aggravates is Latinate, carries higher formal weight, and emphasizes the active addition of a burdensome complicating factor.
  • exacerbates: Similar because both signify intensifying the severity, bitterness, or violence of an existing malady or problem; differs because exacerbates (from Latin acerbus 'harsh, bitter') focuses on sharpening the acuteness, pain, or virulent hostility of a crisis, whereas aggravates (from Latin gravis 'heavy') emphasizes increasing its weight, magnitude, or cumulative burden.
  • compounds: Similar because both involve making an already difficult situation more severe; differs because compounds emphasizes the mathematical or chemical combination of multiple distinct problems together into a complex, multiplied whole, whereas aggravates denotes intensifying the single underlying problem.
  • inflames: Similar because both describe the intensification of a crisis, injury, or emotional state; differs because inflames employs the metaphor of fire and heat, suggesting sudden passion, violent anger, or burning physiological swelling, whereas aggravates emphasizes heavy, enduring burden or progressive degradation.
  • intensifies: Similar because both convey an increase in degree or strength; differs because intensifies is semantically neutral and can apply to positive states (such as intensifying joy or light), while aggravates applies almost exclusively to negative conditions, injuries, crimes, or annoyances.
  • exasperates: Similar because both are commonly used to describe driving someone to frustration or anger; differs because exasperates is strictly psychological and personal (one cannot exasperate a disease or legal crime), targeting human patience to the point of utter loss of composure, whereas aggravates applies to both situations and people.
  • irritates: Similar because both denote provoking annoyance or causing physical sensitivity; differs because irritates primarily suggests friction, petty nuisance, or localized surface inflammation (such as skin itching), whereas aggravates suggests deeper structural worsening or prolonged psychological vexation.
  • provokes: Similar because both involve actions that trigger negative reactions; differs because provokes emphasizes inciting or challenging someone to produce an active retaliatory response, whereas aggravates emphasizes worsening an already active state of difficulty or distress.
