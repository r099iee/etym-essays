PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*klap-
"to clap, bang" (echoic)

↓
Old English (before 1100)
clæppan
"to clap, throb, beat"

↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
clappen
"to clap, strike, knock"

↓
English (13th c.)
clap
"to strike together loudly"

↓
English (present)
clapped
"struck or brought suddenly
into forceful contact"

Note: in the idiom "clapped eyes on", the verb means "caught sight of suddenly" (18th c.). The root *klap- is echoic (onomatopoeic), not a standard reconstructed PIE root; Middle English clappen also meant "to chatter".
