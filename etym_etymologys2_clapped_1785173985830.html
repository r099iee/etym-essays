Chain for clapped:

  • Proto-Germanic *klappon / *klap- "to clap, strike, make a sharp noise" (reconstructed; imitative/sound-symbolic — no secure PIE antecedent).
  • Old English clappian "to clap, throb, make a clapping noise" (attested c. 1000; also claeppan).
  • Middle English clappen "to clap, strike, make a noise" (attested 13th–15th centuries; also the noun clap).
  • Modern English clap (16th century onward, with the applause, sudden-placement, and clap-eyes-on senses) → regular weak past/past participle clapped.

Cognates: Old Norse klappa "to pat, stroke, clap," Middle Dutch clappen, Old High German klaphon — the family is West and North Germanic, and the imitative kl- onset places the group outside any securely reconstructable PIE etymon; a deeper Indo-European attachment is debated and is generally treated as secondary.
