- [Late Middle English legal-administrative sense] - 'Pursuance' enters English as a formal noun meaning the act of pursuing a case, suit, or claim; connotation is courtly and procedural, with no emotional charge.
- [17th-19th century: the fixed formula 'in pursuance of'] - The word crystallises into the standing phrase 'in pursuance of,' used in legal deeds, wills, and official correspondence; connotation is dutiful compliance, bureaucratic rectitude, and the orderly execution of an adopted rule.
- [Modern usage] - 'Pursuance' survives almost exclusively in the frozen formula 'in pursuance of,' now felt as stiff, legalistic, and archaic; it lives on in statutes and formal documents, while ordinary speech prefers 'in accordance with' and 'pursuant to.'
