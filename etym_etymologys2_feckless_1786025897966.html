CHAIN FOR "feck":
*dʰeh₁- (PIE, reconstructed, c. 4500 BCE)
"to put, set, place, do"
(one of the great verbal roots; cf. Latin facere)

▼

facere (Latin)
"to make, do"
(from *dʰeh₁- via Proto-Italic)

▼

efficere (Latin, 1st c. BCE)
ex- "out, thoroughly" + facere
"to work out, bring about, accomplish"

▼

effectus (Latin, 1st c. BCE)
"accomplishment, result, effect"
(past participle of efficere used as a noun)

▼

effect (Anglo-Norman / Middle English, 14th c.)
"result; power, efficacy, substance"

▼

feck (Early Scots, 15th c.)
"force, vigour, value, substance; the greater part"
(aphetic: the unstressed initial e- was dropped)

CHAIN FOR "-less":
*lausaz (Proto-Germanic, reconstructed)
"loose, free from"

▼

-lēas (Old English privative suffix)
"without, lacking"
(descendant of the adjective lēas "loose, devoid of")

COMBINED CHAIN:
feckless (Scots, 16th c.)
feck + -less
"without vigour, weak, worthless, useless"

▼

feckless (English, entered via Scottish literature, 18th-19th c.)
"ineffective, irresponsible, lacking drive"

▼

FECKLESS (modern English)
"lacking initiative, strength of character,
effectiveness, or sense of responsibility"
