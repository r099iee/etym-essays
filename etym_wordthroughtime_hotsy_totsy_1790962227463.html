- 1920s - Coined and popularized by American cartoonist Billy DeBeck in the smash-hit comic strip Barney Google.
- Late 1920s - Embraced as prime Roaring Twenties jazz-age slang by flappers, bootleggers, and collegiate youth.
- 1930s - Cross-Atlantic transmission into British comedic literature (P.G. Wodehouse) and Hollywood screwball comedies.
- 1940s–1960s - Developed a sarcastic secondary nuance in urban American dialogue, meaning haughty, fancy, or snobbish.
- Late 20th–21st c. - Settled into an iconic retro-vintage colloquialism celebrating vintage American and British comedy.
