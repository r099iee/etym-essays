- 15th–18th century: Ottoman Turkish balkan names a mountain range (the Haemus/Stara Planina); European travellers borrow it as a local place-name.
- 1808: the German geographer August Zeune coins “Balkan Peninsula,” extending the mountain name to the whole landmass.
- 19th century: “the Balkans” becomes a geopolitical region, coloured in Western writing by Orientalist images of violence and backwardness.
- Early 20th century: the Balkan Wars and the First World War fix the “powder keg” image; balkanization is coined as a term of reproach.
- Cold War: the region is split by the Iron Curtain, and the word often means the communist southeast.
- 1990s: the Yugoslav wars revive the darkest connotations; “Balkan” becomes a byword for ethnic conflict.
- 21st century: “Western Balkans” is a technocratic EU term, while “Southeastern Europe” is preferred by many; cultural uses (“Balkan music”) reclaim the word positively.
