Proto-Semitic (c. 3000 BCE)
*g-z-r
"to cut off, sever, separate"
↓
Classical Arabic (c. 6th c. CE)
جَزِيرَة (jazīra)
"island; land cut off by water"
[Formed on the fa'īlah verbal noun pattern from root j-z-r]
↓
Classical Arabic (c. 7th c. CE)
الجَزَائِر (al-Jazā'ir)
"the islands (broken plural of jazīra)"
[Definite article al- + broken plural fa'ā'il pattern]
↓
Medieval Maghrebi Arabic (c. 944 CE)
جَزَائِر بَنِي مَزْغَنَّة (Jazā'ir Banī Mazghanna)
"the islands of the Beni Mazghanna tribe"
[Named after the 4 offshore rocky islets sheltering the harbor]
↓
Catalan / Medieval Aragonese (c. 13th–14th c.)
Alguere / Alguer
"Mediterranean port city of Algiers"
[Romance nautical transliteration: Arabic [dʒ] rendered as voiced velar [ɡ]]
↓
Middle French (c. 16th c.)
Alger
"North African capital and corsair port"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1580s)
Argier / Algiers
"Barbary corsair port; capital of Algeria"
[Anglicized with locative/plural suffix -s, as in Marseilles and Lyons]
↓
Modern English (present)
Algiers
"capital of Algeria; subject of Delacroix's masterpiece"
