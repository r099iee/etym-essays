- 14th c. borrowing as verb bateren - to beat down walls and enemies; violent, military, frequentative of beat
- 16th-17th c. participial adjective battered - dented fortifications, bruised soldiers, storm-hit ships; connotation of honourable endurance
- 18th-19th c. domestication - battered hats, furniture, travellers; from battlefield to poverty, travel, and comic character; affectionate as well as pitying
- 1960s-1970s medical-legal turn - battered child / battered woman syndromes; connotation becomes traumatic, clinical, activist; marks pattern of abuse
- Contemporary range - from battered Mars bar (culinary pun) to battered economy; dominant perception remains visibly worn by repeated hard knocks, often with respect for survival
