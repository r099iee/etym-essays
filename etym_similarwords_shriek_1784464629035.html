• Scream: Similar because both name a loud, involuntary cry of terror or pain; differs because scream is the broader term and may be longer or lower in pitch, while shriek is specifically high, thin, and piercing.
  • Screech: Similar because both are harsh, high-pitched sounds; differs because screech usually suggests metal, machinery, or birds, while shriek centers on the human voice.
  • Squeal: Similar because both are high-pitched, sharp cries; differs because squeal is often excited, childish, or animal-like and lighter than the terror of a shriek.
  • Squawk: Similar because both are harsh, shrill cries; differs because squawk suggests birds or coarse mechanical noise, short and flat, not a rising human cry.
  • Yell: Similar because both are loud vocal outcries; differs because yell is voluntary, communicative, and usually lower, while a shriek is involuntary and piercing.
  • Shout: Similar because both are loud vocalizations; differs because shout is deliberate and carries words or address, with none of the reflex panic of a shriek.
  • Howl: Similar because both can be cries of pain or alarm; differs because howl is low, prolonged, and often animal or wind-like, the opposite acoustic profile of a shriek.
  • Wail: Similar because both express distress; differs because wail is mournful, drawn-out, and plaintive rather than sharp and sudden.
  • Yelp: Similar because both are short, sharp cries; differs because yelp is shorter and smaller, often a sudden pain cry of animal or child, less piercing and less sustained.
  • Cry: Similar because both are vocal outcries; differs because cry is a neutral, broad umbrella term with none of the high, piercing quality that defines a shriek.
  • Holler: Similar because both are loud calls; differs because holler is colloquial, deliberate, and meant to carry distance, not a panicked reflex.
