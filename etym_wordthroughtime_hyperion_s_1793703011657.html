- Ancient Greece (Mythological Deity) - Revered as a primordial Titan god of light, father of the sun, moon, and dawn. Connotation: Fundamental cosmic power, source of celestial light.
- Classical Literature (Epithet & Archetype) - Sometimes used as an epithet for Helios (the sun god), but increasingly as a distinct figure representing divine beauty, strength, and an ideal. Connotation: Elevated, majestic, a standard of excellence.
- Renaissance & Shakespeare (Literary Allusion) - Becomes a powerful literary allusion, particularly in *Hamlet*, symbolizing the pinnacle of masculine perfection and virtue. Connotation: Supreme ideal, god-like beauty, moral excellence, a benchmark for comparison.
- 19th Century (Romantic Poetry & Astronomy) - Explored in epic poetry (e.g., Keats) for his tragic grandeur; name given to a moon of Saturn. Connotation: Poetic, celestial, grand, enduring.
- Modern Usage (Literary & Symbolic) - Primarily a literary or symbolic reference, often in fantasy/sci-fi, or for names evoking power, light, and elevated status. Connotation: Archetypal greatness, luminous, powerful, extraordinary.
