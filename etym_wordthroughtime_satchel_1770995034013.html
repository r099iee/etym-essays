- Middle English 'A small bag or case' — a soft container carried by travellers, students, couriers, and peddlers for books, papers, and personal goods; a plain, practical word with no special prestige.
- 18th–19th c. 'The school bag' — the satchel becomes the emblematic bag of the schoolchild, gaining associations with education, diligence, and the bookish routines of youth, especially in England.
- 20th c. 'Everyday carrier of personal effects' — still the school satchel of children and the shoulder bag of commuters and postmen; it keeps a modest, domestic, dependable register.
- Contemporary 'Fashion piece and intimate archive' — the word now also names smart leather shoulder bags in fashion; figuratively it connotes a portable archive of personal belongings carried close against the body, intimate and individual.
