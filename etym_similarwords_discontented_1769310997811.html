• Dissatisfied: Similar because both name a lack of satisfaction; differs because dissatisfied is a neutral, focused displeasure with a specific result, while discontented is a chronic, restless mood about one's general circumstances.
  • Disgruntled: Similar because both describe displeasure with one's situation; differs because disgruntled implies sulky, grumbling, bad-tempered annoyance, while discontented can be a quieter but more pervasive unhappiness.
  • Restless: Similar because both involve an inability to settle; differs because restless emphasizes physical and mental agitation and fidgeting, while discontented centres on dissatisfaction with one's lot.
  • Uneasy: Similar because both describe a lack of ease; differs because uneasy is anxiety or discomfort without a definite object, while discontented specifically targets one's circumstances.
  • Unhappy: Similar because both denote absence of happiness; differs because unhappy is the broad, general word, while discontented adds the specific sense of dissatisfaction with one's situation or condition.
  • Malcontent: Similar because both describe a person ill-pleased with the existing order; differs because malcontent is stronger and more political — one actively hostile or rebellious toward authority.
  • Displeased: Similar because both mean not pleased; differs because displeased is a milder, more momentary reaction to a specific act, while discontented is a settled state.
  • Unfulfilled: Similar because both imply a lack of satisfaction; differs because unfulfilled emphasizes unfulfilled desires or potential and can be applied to needs, promises, or ambitions, not just to mood.
  • Resentful: Similar because both can accompany dissatisfaction; differs because resentful adds active bitterness and grievance against a person or cause, while discontented need not be directed at anyone.
  • Disaffected: Similar because both describe alienation from a situation or authority; differs because disaffected is chiefly used of populations, workers, or groups estranged from an institution or regime — a political rather than personal mood.
  • Fed up: Similar because both describe being tired of one's situation; differs because fed up is informal, conversational, and directed at a specific exasperating situation, while discontented is formal, chronic, and general.
  • Restive: Similar because both imply restlessness under constraint; differs because restive (of a horse, then of people) means impatient under control or discipline, while discontented is a general unhappiness with circumstances.
