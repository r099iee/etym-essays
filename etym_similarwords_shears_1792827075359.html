• Scissors: Similar because both are pivoted double-bladed cutting tools; differs because scissors are light and small for paper, hair, and thread, while shears are large and heavy for hedges, cloth, and fleece.
  • Clippers: Similar because both trim growth with steel; differs because clippers are small, often sprung or electric (nail clippers, hair clippers), while shears are long-bladed manual levers.
  • Secateurs: Similar because both prune the garden with one hand; differs because secateurs are single-handed pruners for stems, while shears are two-handed for hedges and topiary.
  • Sheep shears: Similar because they are the pastoral tribe of the same tool; differs as species versus genus, the sheep shears being the spring-loaded single-piece blades of the shearing shed.
  • Pinking shears: Similar because both are tailor's heavy scissors; differs because pinking shears cut a zigzag edge against fraying, while plain shears cut straight.
  • Snips: Similar because both cut sheet metal and stout stuff; differs because snips are compact tinner's nippers, while shears range up to bench-mounted levers.
  • Loppers: Similar because both cut woody garden growth with long handles; differs because loppers have hooked bypass jaws for branches, while shears have straight blades for hedges.
  • Tongs: Similar because both are paired hinged tools always plural in form; differs because tongs grip and lift (sugar tongs, fire tongs), while shears meet edge to edge and sever.
  • Pruners: Similar because both dress plants with blades; differs because pruners is the general American word for hand clippers, while shears stresses the large two-handed tool.
