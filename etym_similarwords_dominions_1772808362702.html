• Territory: Similar because both name stretches of land under control; differs because territory is neutral geography, while dominion adds the note of lordship, ownership, and right.
  • Realm: Similar because both name a ruler's domain; differs because realm traditionally implies a monarchy and often a single people, whereas dominions can be composite, multi-ethnic holdings.
  • Kingdom: Similar because both are political units under a ruler; differs because kingdom denotes a specific form of state (a king's realm) and usually a defined nation, while dominions is looser and plural-friendly.
  • Empire: Similar because both can denote a sovereign's whole domain; differs because empire can mean the political system or supreme rule itself, while dominions stresses the owned territories as such.
  • Provinces: Similar because both are parts of a larger state; differs because provinces are the defined administrative units inside a system (e.g., Roman provincia), while dominions is the higher, looser term for all that a ruler holds.
  • Domain: Similar because both derive from the same Latin "house / lord" family and mean a sphere of control; differs because domain is now abstract and metaphorical (an area of knowledge, an internet name), while dominions stays concrete and territorial.
  • Demesne: Similar because both descend from the lordship family and mean land under a lord; differs because demesne is the feudal technical term for land kept for the lord's own use, not a whole territory.
  • Colonies: Similar because both are territories controlled from outside; differs because colonies are settlements of citizens or dependents planted abroad — only one subset of what a ruler dominates.
  • Possessions: Similar because both are things owned by a state; differs because possessions is blunter and includes goods as well as lands, while dominions specifies territory under rule.
  • Protectorate: Similar because both are lands under external control; differs because a protectorate retains nominal internal autonomy under protection, whereas a dominion is directly ruled territory.
