Note on historical debate: Germanic philologists (including Guus Kroonen, Vladimir Orel, and Hjalmar Falk) debate the ultimate Proto-Indo-European root of Proto-Germanic *þrahilaz. The traditional and prevailing etymology connects it to the verbal base *þreg- ("to run"), preserved in Gothic þragjan ("to run, hasten"), indicating that a thrall was originally a "runner, swift messenger, errand-drudge." An alternative hypothesis seeks an origin in PIE *terh₁- ("to overcome, subdue, conquer"), meaning "the conquered captive." Both views acknowledge that Old Norse þræll entered Anglo-Saxon England via Viking incursions during the Danelaw period, replacing native Old English þēow.
