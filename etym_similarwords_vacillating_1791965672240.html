• indecisive: The closest everyday equivalent, sharing the core sense of an inability to settle a course; it is milder and more neutral, naming a failure of decision rather than a repeated, morally culpable swaying, and it can describe a single occasion where vacillating implies a habit.
  • irresolute: Nearly interchangeable in formal prose, both meaning "without fixed purpose"; irresolute is the more static word — a settled want of resolution — while vacillating keeps the image of movement back and forth and therefore implies repeated changes of front.
  • wavering: Its closest synonym and the one that preserves the physical metaphor; wavering is gentler and often sympathetic (a wavering supporter may be won over), whereas vacillating is a standing accusation of weakness.
  • hesitant: Shares the sense of not advancing decisively, but hesitant describes a momentary pause or a cautious temperament and carries no moral condemnation; vacillating describes a pattern of reversals and always condemns.
  • fluctuating: Used of quantities, prices, moods and fortunes that rise and fall; it is quantitative and often impersonal, whereas vacillating is about intention and choice, and is applied to agents rather than to numbers.
  • oscillating: Implies regular, measurable alternation between two states, as of a pendulum or a current; it is technical and morally neutral, while vacillating implies irregular, human wavering that ought to have been avoided.
  • dithering: Colloquial British English for fussy, ineffective indecision; it is comic and dismissive rather than grave, and suggests busy helplessness, whereas vacillating is grander and suggests principled cowardice.
  • fickle: Describes changeability in loyalty or affection, especially in love; fickle looks at the object of devotion, vacillating at the decision itself, and fickle lacks the connotation of tormented inability to choose.
  • inconstant: An archaic-literary synonym for changeable in feeling; it is gentler and more elegiac than vacillating, which carries public and political weight.
  • spineless: The companion word in the source passage, and the bluntest of the group; it condemns lack of courage without reference to decisions at all, whereas vacillating specifically names the oscillation between courses.
