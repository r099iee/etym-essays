- 14th century (dasen) — stupefaction from weariness, drink, or shock; a mental fog rather than a physical injury.
- 16th century — "dazed" crystallises as a past-participial adjective for bewilderment and confusion.
- 18th–19th centuries — extends to physical stunning (a blow, a fall, a head wound) and to overwhelming sights and sounds.
- 20th century — medical and popular sense: concussion, shock, recovery from anaesthesia; "dazed and confused" becomes a set phrase and a rock/film title.
- Present day — covers sensory overload, trauma, grief, and information fatigue; milder and more transient than "traumatised" or "stupefied".
