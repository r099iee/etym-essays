• Bildungsroman: Similar because *Jean Santeuil* belongs structurally to the genre of a youth's psychological, social, and aesthetic coming-of-age; differs because a Bildungsroman is an overarching literary genre encompassing works across all traditions (e.g., Goethe’s *Wilhelm Meister*), whereas Santeuil designates a specific fictional protagonist and unfinished proto-modernist text.
  • Roman-à-clef: Similar because both involve fictional narratives populated by characters that thinly veil real historical personalities (e.g., Jean Santeuil’s aristocratic mentors and Émile Zola); differs because a roman-à-clef is a broad genre classification often written for satirical or gossipy unmasking, whereas Santeuil was an intensely serious, unpublished aesthetic workshop exploring the soul of the artist.
  • Autobiographical novel: Similar because both blur the boundary between the author's real life events and artistic fabrication; differs because autobiographical novel is a generic category, whereas Santeuil specifically represents the preliminary, fragmented crucible from which Proust forged his monumental first-person masterpiece.
  • Draft / Preparatory study: Similar because both refer to preliminary, unpolished creative writings preceding a finalized masterwork; differs because a draft is any generic working manuscript, whereas *Jean Santeuil* is an extensive, standalone literary artifact of over a thousand pages possessing its own poignant lyricism and historical value.
  • Alter ego: Similar because Jean Santeuil serves as a direct fictional surrogate and psychological mirror for the young Marcel Proust; differs because alter ego is a general psychological and narrative term for a double or second self, whereas Santeuil is a fully individualized, named literary character embedded in late nineteenth-century France.
