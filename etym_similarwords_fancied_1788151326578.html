• Imagined: Similar because both denote what exists in the mind; differs because "imagined" is the plain neutral word for any mental image, while fancied adds an archaic-poetic or whimsical colouring.
  • Supposed: Similar because both mean held to be so; differs because "supposed" implies assumption on some evidence, however thin, while fancied implies invention by the imagination.
  • Envisioned / pictured: Similar because both mean formed an image of; differs because these are modern, deliberate acts of mental projection, while fancied describes a spontaneously produced or idealized image.
  • Imaginary / illusory: Similar because both describe the unreal; differs because "imaginary" is categorical (not real), while fancied may be real but exaggerated in the mind.
  • Idealized: Similar because both describe improvement by the imagination; differs because "idealized" implies perfection-making, while fancied may be fanciful without being perfect.
  • Whimsical / capricious: Similar because both derive from fancy's playfulness; differ because they describe the agent's character (full of whims), while fancied describes the object of the fancy.
  • Liked / wanted / desired: Similar because British "fancied" is the participle of wanting; differ because these are neutral desire words, while fancied retains the flavour of a whim of the heart.
  • Favoured: Similar because both mean expected or preferred to win; differs because "favoured" is the general term (favoured by odds and opinion), "fancied" the British racing idiom built on the public's fancy.
  • Dreamt-of: Similar because both denote desire-tinged imagining; differs because "dreamt-of" implies yearning for the future, while fancied may be instant and arbitrary.
