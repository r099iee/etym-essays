- 6th c. BCE – 5th c. CE — Settled by Phoenicians as Ikosim and expanded by Romans as the colony Icosium, an important maritime trading post along the Mauretanian coast.
- Mid-10th c. (c. 944 CE) — Refounded by Bologhine ibn Ziri as Jazā'ir Banī Mazghanna ("The Islands of the Sons of Mazghanna"), describing the four rocky islets offshore; shortened in Arabic to al-Jazā'ir.
- 16th c. — Captured by privateer brothers Aruj and Khair ad-Din Barbarossa; incorporated into the Ottoman Empire as the fortified capital of the Regency of Algiers, the premier corsair stronghold of the Barbary Coast.
- 1830 — Conquered by French naval forces under Charles X, inaugurating 132 years of French colonial administration; visited in 1832 by Eugène Delacroix, inspiring his 1834 Louvre masterpiece The Women of Algiers.
- 1954–1962 — The primary urban battleground of the Algerian War of Independence (the Battle of Algiers, 1956–1957), leading to the proclamation of Algerian sovereignty in 1962.
- Late 20th–21st c. — A thriving Mediterranean metropolis and the diplomatic, financial, and cultural capital of the Democratic and Popular Republic of Algeria.
