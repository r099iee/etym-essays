Note on antonymic pair with expedite: Latin impedīre forms a perfect morphological pair with expedīre. While impedīre meant "to put the feet into shackles" (in- + pēs), expedīre literally meant "to take the feet out of shackles, disentangle the feet" (ex- + pēs). From expedīre English inherited expedite, expedition, and expeditious. Consequently, an impediment is literally a trap that binds your feet, while an expedition was originally an unencumbered march where the feet were entirely free of baggage.
