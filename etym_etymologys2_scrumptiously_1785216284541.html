SCRUMPTIOUSLY = scrumptious + adverbial suffix -ly. Chain:
1. PIE *em- "to take, to distribute" (reconstructed) — c. 4000 BCE; cf. Greek némein "to distribute".
2. Latin emere "to take, to buy" — attested from the 6th–5th centuries BCE.
3. Latin sumere "to take up, to undertake, to spend" — from sub- "under" + emere, with assimilation *subm- → summ-; Classical Latin (1st century BCE).
4. Latin sumptus — past participle of sumere, used also as a noun meaning "expense, cost, outlay"; 1st century BCE.
5. Latin sumptuōsus "costly, expensive, lavish" (from sumptus + -ōsus "full of") — Classical Latin, 1st century BCE–2nd century CE.
6. Old French / Middle French somptueux "sumptuous, lavish, magnificent" — 13th–14th centuries.
7. Middle English sumptuous "lavish, costly, magnificent" — late 15th century (Caxton, c. 1474).
8. scrumptious "delightful, delicious" — c. 1830–1850, a jocular English alteration of sumptuous (see debate note).
9. scrumptious + -ly → scrumptiously "in a delicious, delightful manner" — mid-19th century (first attested 1857).
10. scrumptiously — Modern English.
Note on debate: the formation of scrumptious is not fully explained. The standard view is a playful corruption of sumptuous (with the -mp- eased, an expressive r- inserted, and the vowel shortened); some dialectal variants (scrumptious, skrumptious) and a possible link to 19th-century slang scrum "food" keep the precise path uncertain. The OED describes it as "of uncertain origin; probably an alteration of sumptuous".
