- Archaic & Republican Rome (6th–2nd c. BCE): Etruscan & Italic Artisan Deity - Honored as Menrva, patroness of weaving, guilds, craftsmanship, and healing, celebrated at the spring Quinquatrus.
- Classical & Imperial Rome (1st c. BCE–4th c. CE): Capitoline State Goddess - Fully syncretized with Athena, enthroned on the Capitoline Triad as the supreme emblem of tactical defense, state wisdom, and imperial victory.
- Renaissance & Enlightenment (14th–18th c.): Humanist Allegory of Reason - Revered in European painting, literature, and emblem books as the personification of liberal arts, scientific knowledge, and enlightened governance.
- 19th Century: Hegelian & Academic Emblem - Forever associated with Hegel's twilight owl of historical retrospection; adopted as the heraldic insignia of universities, academies, and libraries worldwide.
- 20th–21st Century: Modern Scientific & Strategic Icon - Invoked in institutional statecraft (DoD Minerva Initiative), scientific foundations (Max Planck Society), and popular archetypes of wise guardianship (McGonagall).
