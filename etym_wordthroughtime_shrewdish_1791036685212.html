- Wicked-cursed sense (13th-14th c.) - shrewe as evil person, shrewed as accursed, depraved; wholly negative, moral-religious condemnation
- Scolding sense (15th-16th c.) - shrew as nagging woman, shrewd as shrew-like, sharp-tongued; gendered insult
- Cunning-artful sense (16th-17th c.) - a shrewd fellow is dangerously clever; fear and admiration mix; Shakespeare uses shrewd for both painful and astute
- Astute-judicious sense (18th-19th c.) - shrewd businessman as praise; evil bleached out, worldly wisdom celebrated in commercial society
- Hedged shrewdish (19th-20th c.) - comic writers add -ish to soften to somewhat clever; affectionate understatement for reluctant schemers
