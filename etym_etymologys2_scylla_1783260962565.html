Ultimate etymology debated: Greek Skýlla (Σκύλλα) is traditionally connected with the Greek verb skyllō "to tear, rend, flay, harry," giving "the Renderer," but many modern scholars (e.g., Beekes) regard the name as of possible Pre-Greek origin, since skyllō itself has no secure PIE etymology. The chain is therefore not traceable to a certain Indo-European root; earlier stages are unknown. 2. Ancient Greek skyllō (σκύλλω) "to tear, rend, flay, harry, distress" (attested from the 5th c. BCE; the connection with the monster's name is ancient and traditional). 3. Ancient Greek Skýlla (Σκύλλα) "Scylla, the Rending sea-monster of the Strait of Messina" (attested in Homer, Odyssey Book 12, 8th c. BCE). 4. Latin Scylla — the Latinized form (Greek upsilon rendered as Latin y), attested in Roman literature from the 3rd century BCE onward (e.g., in the Latin epic and rhetorical tradition; also used of the rock). 5. English SCYLLA — borrowed via Latin into English classical allusion and literature from the 16th century onward (e.g., in translations of Homer and in Gibbon's Decline and Fall). ◄ TARGET Debated/nuanced note: whether Skýlla derives from skyllō ("the Renderer") or is a Pre-Greek name is debated; the folk-etymology is ancient and satisfying (and matches the monster's behaviour), but the linguistic evidence is inconclusive.
