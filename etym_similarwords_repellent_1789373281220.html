• Repugnant: Similar because both describe that which arouses severe disgust, aversion, and psychological rejection; differs because repugnant carries a heavier moral, legal, or intellectual connotation (frequently describing principles inherently contradictory to justice, decency, or reason), whereas repellent emphasizes the immediate, visceral sensation of recoiling or being physically driven away.
  • Offensive: Similar because both characterize behavior, statements, or odors that cause intense displeasure and disapproval; differs because offensive implies an active assault upon sensibilities, taste, or manners that causes hurt feelings or indignant outrage, whereas repellent evokes a deep, cold revulsion that makes one wish to flee contact altogether.
  • Abhorrent: Similar because both express extreme detestation and recoil; differs because abhorrent carries profound ethical, religious, or righteous indignation regarding abominations and heinous crimes, whereas repellent can apply across aesthetic, physical, social, and material registers.
  • Loathsome: Similar because both convey intense physical or moral sickening disgust; differs because loathsome emphasizes foulness, wretchedness, or nauseating physical decay and disease, whereas repellent often connotes a cold, forbidding, or aggressively disagreeable nature that actively drives people back.
  • Distasteful: Similar because both denote something unappealing or unpleasant to contemplate; differs because distasteful is a far milder, polite term signifying an uncomfortable clash with decorum, manners, or taste, whereas repellent denotes an acute, forceful, and unyielding revulsion.
  • Revolting: Similar because both evoke physical or emotional nausea and refusal; differs because revolting focuses on the stomach-turning, gut-level shock of gross physical impropriety or grotesque deformity, whereas repellent highlights the defensive barrier that forces observers away.
  • Deterrent: Similar because both designate agents or factors that keep unwanted entities away; differs because deterrent functions primarily in strategic, criminological, and behavioral contexts (preventing action through fear of consequences), whereas repellent is a physical substance, surface property, or an inherent visceral quality of unpleasantness.
