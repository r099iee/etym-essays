• Interlace: Similar because both mean to cross threads or elements over and under each other; differs because interlace is more technical and physical, describing the crossing itself, while weave implies the full craft of making a fabric or whole.
  • Interweave: Similar because it is nearly the same word, adding the 'between' nuance; differs because interweave stresses the mixing of distinct strands into one compound (facts interwoven with fiction), while weave alone stresses the act of construction.
  • Braid: Similar because both join strands; differs because braiding plaits three or more strands diagonally over each other (hair, rope) and produces a raised cord, whereas weaving crosses warp and weft at right angles to produce flat cloth.
  • Plait: Similar because both interwork strands; differs because plait is essentially braid under another name (chiefly British), a narrow banding, not the loom's broad fabric.
  • Knit: Similar because both make fabric from yarn; differs because knitting builds loops with needles into stretchy cloth, while weaving interlocks straight crossing threads on a loom to make firm cloth.
  • Crochet: Similar because both are yarn crafts; differs because crochet is a hooked-loop technique, single-thread, producing openwork — a hobby craft, not the civilisation-scale industry of weaving.
  • Entwine / Twine: Similar because both mean to twist together; differs because entwining/twining winds strands around each other spirally (vines round a pole), with no warp-and-weft structure and no flat product.
  • Spin: Similar because both belong to textile-making and both mean 'to produce a story' figuratively; differs because spinning twists a single thread from raw fibre, while weaving crosses threads — hence 'spin a yarn' (one continuous thread) vs 'weave a tale' (many threads ordered).
  • Fabricate / Construct: Similar because both mean to build something complex; differs because fabricate now suggests building from parts (often with a hint of inventing falsely), while weave keeps the image of interlacing elements into a seamless whole.
  • Zigzag / Slalom / Dodge (motion sense): Similar because all describe changing direction while moving; differs because zigzag is angular and regular, slalom is race-gated and sport-specific, dodge implies avoiding a specific threat — while weave suggests smooth, continuous side-to-side streaming under way.
