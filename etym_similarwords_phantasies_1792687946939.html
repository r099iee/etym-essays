• fantasies: Similar because both represent the exact same lexical word denoting imaginary concepts, reveries, or fictional creations; differs because the spelling fantasies is the standard modern unmarked variant used for everyday daydreams and the commercial genre of speculative fiction, whereas phantasies is a deliberate archaizing, poetic, or psychoanalytic spelling specifically evoking Greek philosophical lineage, unconscious psychic structures, or high-literary Romantic imagination.
  • fancies: Similar because both derive historically from the same Greek root (phantasía) and denote products of the imaginative faculty; differs because fancies has undergone semantic narrowing to denote lighter, more transient whims, superficial predilections, or capricious notions, lacking the visionary depth, structural complexity, or psychological weight of phantasies.
  • daydreams: Similar because both describe self-generated internal mental visions detached from immediate sensory reality; differs because daydreams are specifically idle, waking reveries often characterized by passive wish-fulfillment, whereas phantasies can encompass profound creative architectures, dark unconscious psychic projections, or artistic mythmaking.
  • delusions: Similar because both describe beliefs or perceptions lacking objective physical existence; differs because delusions are pathological, fixed false beliefs stubbornly maintained despite contrary evidence, whereas phantasies are recognized as imaginative constructs (or poetic/creative fabrications) and do not inherently involve psychotic breaks with reality.
  • chimeras: Similar because both refer to fanciful mental fabrications; differs because a chimera specifically denotes a grotesque, monstrous hybrid or an absurd, unattainable impossibility, evoking an illusion that can never be realized in the physical world.
  • hallucinations: Similar because both involve the perception of things not physically present; differs because hallucinations are involuntary sensory perceptions occurring in the absence of external stimuli (frequently auditory or visual pathology), whereas phantasies are products of the mind's ideational or imaginative faculties, typically experienced without full sensory deception.
  • illusions: Similar because both involve misperceptions of reality; differs because an illusion is a distorted perception of a real, existing sensory stimulus, whereas a phantasy is an internally generated creation of the imaginative faculty without requiring an external trigger.
  • visions: Similar because both designate vivid mental or transcendent imagery; differs because visions often imply prophetic, divine, or supernatural revelation with an aura of revelatory truth or spiritual gravity, whereas phantasies highlight the whimsical, subjective, or psychodynamic creation of the mind.
