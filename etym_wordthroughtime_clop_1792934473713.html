- Imitative roots — the stable's oldest music: hoofbeats were rendered clap, clatter, and clip for centuries before the hollow note got its own spelling.
- c. 1920s — clop enters the dictionaries: noun and verb together, the echoic word finally standardised in print.
- Mid-20th c. — the sound of period drama: films, radio plays, and children's books make clip-clop and clop the universal hoof-effect.
- Tolkien's use — menace by rhythm: the slow clop of ponies' hoofs in the night becomes one of fantasy's most imitated listening scenes.
- Today — the countryman's word: bridle paths, riding schools, and wedding carriages keep it literal, while writers borrow it for any hollow measured beat in the dark.
