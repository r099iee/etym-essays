Ancient Greek (c. 5th–4th c. BCE)
kṓnōps (κώνωψ)
"mosquito, gnat"
Note: Expressive Mediterranean root referring to long-snouted stinging insects, related to kōnos ('cone, point').

Ancient Greek (c. 3rd–2nd c. BCE)
kōnōpeîon (κωνωπεῖον)
"couch or bed with mosquito netting; Egyptian netting tent"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE)
cōnōpēum / conopeum
"mosquito net, fine-netted bed curtain, canopy"
Note: Roman satirists (Horace, Juvenal) initially mocked Roman officers for sleeping under unmanly Egyptian conopea.
↓
Medieval Latin (c. 6th–12th c.)
canopium / canopeum
"cloth covering over a throne, bed, or altar"
↓
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 13th–14th c.)

↓
Middle English (c. late 14th c.)
