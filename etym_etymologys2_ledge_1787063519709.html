NOTE — DEBATED / NUANCED STAGES: The origin of ledge is UNCERTAIN and is honestly marked as such by the Oxford English Dictionary ("origin obscure") and other authorities. The chain above presents the most commonly cited hypothesis — that ledge descends from the English verb-family "to lay" (Old English lecgan, Middle English leggen), via a nominal form legge meaning "that which is laid (out), a bar or rail" — but this link is conjectural, not proven. Objections: the earliest attested legge (14th c.) means "a bar, rod, rail", a meaning not obviously derived from "to lay"; and no Old English noun *lecge is securely attested. Alternative hypotheses connect ledge (a) to the root of edge (Old English ecg), the ledge being the sharp edge of rock; (b) to Scandinavian or Low German forms such as Dutch legge "a lying-place, a ledge"; or (c) to a conflation of several of these. No single ancestor is secure, and no earlier stage beyond the 14th-century legge can be reconstructed with certainty — the earlier part of the chain is therefore presented as the probable lay-family derivation, with the caveat that it is the leading hypothesis rather than a verified fact.
