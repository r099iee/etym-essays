PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*slīdʰ- / *sley-
"to slip, slide, glide smoothly"
Note: The root that also produced English slide and sled; extended into Proto-Germanic nominal derivatives.
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*slidō / *slidjōn
"a sledge, sliding vehicle, slider"
↓
Middle Dutch / Middle Low German (c. 12th–14th c.)
sledde / slede
"drag, sledge, vehicle without wheels"
Note: Borrowed into Middle English through extensive North Sea wool trade and Hanseatic maritime shipping contacts.
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.)
slegge / sleg / sledge
"a carriage without wheels running on slides; heavy dragging sled"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
sledge
"vehicle on runners for traveling over snow or ice; drag"
