• truly: Similar because both denote in accordance with truth and fact; differs because truly is the standard everyday Germanic adverb, whereas verily is a Romance-Germanic hybrid now perceived as archaic, biblical, or literary.
  • indeed: Similar because both serve as emphatic affirmations of reality ("in deed / in truth"); differs because indeed functions frequently as a discourse connective or concession marker ("it is, indeed, raining"), whereas verily is a direct assertion of factual truth.
  • assuredly: Similar because both express total certainty without doubt; differs because assuredly emphasizes subjective confidence and guaranteed security of outcome rather than ancient biblical affirmation.
  • certainly: Similar because both affirm absolute reality and absence of doubt; differs because certainly is common, modern, and pragmatic, whereas verily carries sacred, solemn historical weight.
  • undeniably: Similar because both indicate that a fact cannot be disputed; differs because undeniably focuses on the impossibility of rational contradiction or argument.
  • in truth: Similar because both mean in accordance with reality; differs because in truth is a prepositional phrase used for straightforward clarification, lacking the compact archaic elegance of verily.
  • indisputably: Similar because both assert an unassailable truth; differs because indisputably is an academic, forensic Latinate adverb emphasizing incontrovertible evidence.
  • veritably: Similar because both share the Latin root verus; differs because veritably is used almost exclusively as an intensifier alongside metaphors (e.g., "a veritably exploding volcano"), meaning "literally" or "virtually," whereas verily means "truly/certainly."
  • unfeignedly: Similar because both denote speaking without deceit or pretense; differs because unfeignedly specifically stresses emotional genuineness and sincerity of heart.
  • genuinely: Similar because both denote authenticity; differs because genuinely focuses on real, uncounterfeited origin or feeling rather than solemn affirmation.
  • positively: Similar because both express absolute certainty; differs because positively is an emphatic modern intensifier (e.g., "positively glowing") without sacred or historical connotations.
  • indubitably: Similar because both express freedom from doubt; differs because indubitably carries a formal, intellectual, and slightly pedantic tone ("beyond doubt") rather than prophetic or solemn gravity.
