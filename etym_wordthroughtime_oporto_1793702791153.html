- 17th-19th Century (English Usage) - 'Oporto' became the standard English name for the city, strongly associated with the burgeoning Port wine trade. It carried connotations of a vital commercial hub, a source of luxury goods, and a key partner in Anglo-Portuguese relations.
- Late 19th-Mid 20th Century - The name 'Oporto' remained prevalent in English, particularly in commercial and historical contexts related to wine. Its perception was largely stable, signifying a historic European port city.
- Late 20th Century-Present - With increased globalization and a push for linguistic accuracy, the native 'Porto' has gained significant traction and is now widely preferred in English-speaking contexts, especially in tourism and general media. 'Oporto' is increasingly seen as an archaic or historical term, though still recognized, particularly by those familiar with the traditional wine trade. The shift reflects a broader trend towards using native place names.
