hastened (verb, past tense and past participle of hasten) — Moved with speed, urgency, or swift dispatch; quickened one's pace toward a destination; or caused an event, process, decision, or condition to occur sooner or proceed more rapidly than it otherwise would have. The verb combines the concept of physical velocity with an underlying psychological motive of purposeful promptness, sudden concern, or external pressure.

1. Primary Intransitive Sense — Rapid Physical Movement and Urgent Dispatch. In its primary physical and narrative use, hastened describes a person, animal, or group moving rapidly, hurrying, or traveling with accelerated steps toward a specific objective. Unlike mindless rushing or frantic scurrying, hastening typically carries a dignified, deliberate, or emotionally charged urgency. In literary prose and narrative storytelling, a character who hastened across a courtyard, down a corridor, or along a country road acts under the spur of immediate duty, sudden revelation, compassion, or alarm (as in the reading context, where the Giant "hastened across the grass, and came near to the child" upon seeing the miraculous arrival of the beloved boy in the garden).

2. Transitive Causal Sense — Accelerating Processes and Precipitously Advancing Events. In its transitive capacity, hastened means to accelerate, expedite, precipitate, or bring about the premature occurrence of an outcome:

  • Medical and Biological Acceleration: Severe illness, grief, or physical trauma may be said to have hastened a patient's demise or bodily decline ("the bitter winter cold hastened his death").
  • Political, Economic, and Historical Catalysts: An economic crisis, military defeat, or diplomatic scandal often hastened the collapse of a government, the signing of a treaty, or the downfall of an empire ("the sudden bank failure hastened the economic recession").
  • Project and Production Expediting: In organizational and industrial contexts, managers hastened the completion of construction works or product rollouts to meet critical deadlines.

3. Discourse and Conversational Idiom — "Hastened to Add / Clarify". In formal rhetoric, journalism, and diplomatic discourse, the fixed formula "hastened to add" (or "hastened to assure / point out") is widely employed when a speaker immediately offers a qualification, clarification, or soothing remark to prevent a misunderstanding or soften an unintended offense ("The prime minister criticized the proposal, but hastened to add that negotiations would continue").

4. Polysemy, Literary Nuance, and Emotional Tone. Across classic and modern literature, hastened bridges several distinct emotional and dramatic registers:

  • Eager Devotion and Joyful Reunion: Used when lovers, messengers, or devoted friends rush forward to embrace or deliver joyful news ("she hastened to welcome her returning brother").
  • Guilty or Fearful Retreat: Used when fleeing danger, embarrassment, or threatening weather ("frightened by the thunder, the travelers hastened toward shelter").
  • Dramatic Inevitability: In tragic drama, the fatal actions of a protagonist frequently hastened their own tragic catastrophe.

5. Register and Stylistic Standing. Hastened represents a formal, literary, and elevated verb compared to everyday conversational alternatives such as hurried, rushed, ran, or sped up. It imparts narrative dignity and measured seriousness to prose, making it an enduring staple of classic English storytelling, historical biography, and high-register journalism.
