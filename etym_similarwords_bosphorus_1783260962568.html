• Dardanelles: Similar because the Dardanelles (the ancient Hellespont) is the other great Turkish strait, part of the same maritime system and subject to the same international regime (Montreux); differs because the Dardanelles connects the Sea of Marmara to the Aegean Sea, while the Bosphorus connects the Sea of Marmara to the Black Sea.
  • Hellespont: Similar because it is the classical name of the strait at Gallipoli, the second member of the same waterway complex; differs because the Hellespont is the older, mythologically loaded name of the Dardanelles, while Bosphorus is the name of the narrower, more celebrated strait at Istanbul.
  • Golden Horn: Similar because the Golden Horn belongs to Istanbul's maritime geography and is intimately linked with the Bosphorus; differs because the Golden Horn is a sheltered inlet of the Bosphorus forming the historic inner harbour of the city, not the main strait connecting two seas.
  • Sea of Marmara: Similar because it is the sea into which the Bosphorus empties and a necessary link in the Black Sea–Mediterranean route; differs because the Sea of Marmara is a broad inland sea, whereas the Bosphorus is a narrow, heavily constricted channel.
  • Strait / channel / passage: Similar because the Bosphorus is precisely a strait, a narrow body of water connecting two larger ones; differs because strait, channel, and passage are generic hydrographic terms, while Bosphorus is the specific proper name with its own mythic and imperial associations.
  • Byzantium / Constantinople / Istanbul: Similar because the Bosphorus is the waterway on which these successive cities were built and from which they drew their greatness; differs because these are names of the city itself, while the Bosphorus names the strait that gives the city its command of the sea routes.
  • Cimmerian Bosporus (Kerch Strait): Similar because the ancient Greeks used the very same word for another strait, and modern scholarship distinguishes the two Bospori; differs because the Cimmerian Bosporus is the Kerch Strait linking the Black Sea with the Sea of Azov, far to the north of Istanbul.
  • Boğaziçi / Boğaz: Similar because these are the modern Turkish names for the Bosphorus; differs because they are Turkish topographic terms meaning "within the strait" and "strait/throat," respectively, whereas Bosphorus is the classical Greek-derived international English name.
