- 16th Century - Borrowed from Italian *antico* to denote grotesque classical Roman decorative motifs excavated in the ruins of Nero's Golden House.
- Late 16th – Early 17th Century - Extended to theatrical performers, mummers, and court jesters who dressed in bizarre, fantastical, or grotesque costumes to dance and perform ludicrous postures ("playing the antic").
- 17th–18th Century - Shifted from the person/performer to the eccentric gestures, grimaces, and bodily contortions themselves.
- 19th Century - Pluralized into a general term for silly, playful, or wildly eccentric deeds, pranks, and theatrical capers.
- 20th–21st Century - Standard modern usage describing childish, attention-seeking, eccentric, or disruptive public behavior (e.g. "celebrity antics", "political antics").
