- [Ancient kingdom] - The current English form derives from Greek Makedonia; in antiquity it named the homeland of Philip II and Alexander the Great, whose conquests began the Hellenistic age - a name of great military and cultural consequence.
- [Roman province] - After 148 BC the name became a Roman province, its perception shifting to a settled administrative region of the eastern empire rather than an independent power.
- [Ottoman and modern region] - Through the Ottoman centuries and the Balkan wars, 'Macedonia' came to denote a contested ethnic and geographical region claimed by several nations, charged with nationalist emotion.
- [Contemporary sense] - Since the 2019 Prespa Agreement the name officially applies to the Republic of North Macedonia, so that in modern English it carries careful diplomatic nuances - the region versus the state.
