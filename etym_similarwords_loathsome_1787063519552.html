• Abhorrent: Similar because both denote something that elicits profound revulsion; differs because abhorrent emphasizes deep moral or intellectual incompatibility and rejection (e.g., 'violence is abhorrent to their philosophy'), whereas loathsome frequently involves visceral, physical, nauseating disgust.
  • Repulsive: Similar because both describe that which repels the senses or mind; differs because repulsive focuses on driving something away (active repulsion/rejection), whereas loathsome emphasizes the sickening nature of the object itself.
  • Detestable: Similar because both signify intense hatred; differs because detestable emphasizes that the subject deserves active condemnation and hatred, while loathsome emphasizes the sickening, foul quality that induces nausea or horror.
  • Repugnant: Similar because both express strong aversion; differs because repugnant often relates to logical contradiction, offensive principles, or moral unpalatability in legal/philosophical discourse, whereas loathsome carries heavy sensory and emotional disgust.
  • Abominable: Similar because both describe shocking and vile things; differs because abominable connotes a violation of divine, sacred, or basic human laws that brings down a curse or severe moral outrage (derived from Latin *abominari*).
  • Vile: Similar because both denote extreme foulness; differs because vile is shorter, more punchy, and often implies low moral worth, base degradation, or wretched quality.
  • Hideous: Similar because both describe horrifying things; differs because hideous focuses specifically on shocking, grotesque physical ugliness or horrifying visual appearance.
  • Noxious: Similar because both describe harmful, offensive things; differs because noxious emphasizes poisonous, physically toxic, or destructive qualities (e.g., 'noxious fumes'), whereas loathsome describes sensory and moral revulsion.
