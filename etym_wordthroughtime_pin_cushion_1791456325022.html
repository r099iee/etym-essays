- Early modern period — the compound names a practical sewing accessory; pins are valuable, individually made, and carefully kept, so the cushion is a tool of thrift as much as of convenience.
- 19th c. — decorative and novelty pin-cushions flourish in Victorian domestic culture; the object becomes an emblem of feminine needlework and household order.
- Early 20th c. — “human pin-cushion” appears as a jocular image for someone repeatedly injected or pricked, extending the word from object to person.
- Mid 20th c. — pincushion distortion becomes standard terminology in optics and photography, naming the inward bowing of straight lines at the edges of an image.
- Late 20th–21st c. — the literal object becomes largely decorative or nostalgic, while the technical and figurative senses keep the word alive in medicine, photography, and casual speech.
