- Archaic Greece (c. 8th–6th c. BCE) - Mythological princess of Mycenae in epic traditions (*Laodice* in Homer, *Elektra* in Stesichoros).
- Classical Athens (5th c. BCE) - Canonized as the quintessential tragic heroine of mourning and filial vengeance in plays by Aeschylus, Sophocles, and Euripides.
- Hellenistic & Roman Era (3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE) - Emblem of unrelenting grief and unyielding devotion to retributive justice.
- Early 20th Century (1913) - Adopted by psychoanalyst Carl Jung to designate the "Electra complex" (a daughter's psychosexual rivalry with her mother).
- 20th–21st Century - Cultural archetype of trauma, filial rebellion, and psychological intensity across literature, opera (Richard Strauss), and cinema.
