- Ancient and medieval Esterèu - a local rocky height, spring and road marker in the Provençal hinterland - the name is practical and obscure, known only to travellers and villagers
- Fourteenth to seventeenth centuries - charters record Estelell, Sterellum, Estereu and Estreli - the name slowly attaches itself to the whole wooded massif
- Eighteenth century - cartographers extend the label from a single hill or spring to the entire range - “Estérel” becomes a map-name rather than a local microtoponym
- Nineteenth century - Romantic travellers and painters discover the red cliffs and the Corniche d'Or - the word acquires scenic and aristocratic-tourist glamour
- Twentieth century - Cannes, Saint-Raphaël and the Allied landings put the massif on the cultural map - the name suggests Riviera leisure, film and wartime memory
- Twenty-first century - national forest, nature reserve, Estérel Côte d'Azur branding and hiking trails - the word now signals protected Mediterranean landscape and regional tourism
