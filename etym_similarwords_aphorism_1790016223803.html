• maxim: Similar because it is a short statement of general truth or rule of conduct; differs because maxim is more didactic and prescriptive (a rule to follow), while aphorism is more observational and literary.
  • adage: Similar because it is a traditional pithy saying; differs because adage stresses age and long-established use, while aphorism stresses originality and individual authorship.
  • proverb: Similar because it expresses folk wisdom concisely; differs because proverb is anonymous, communal, and homely, while aphorism is attributed, polished, and often philosophical.
  • saying: Similar because it is a familiar short expression of wisdom; differs because saying is a loose umbrella term, while aphorism is a specific artful genre.
  • epigram: Similar because it is brief, witty, and pointed; differs because epigram aims chiefly at wit or satire and need not be true, while aphorism aims at truth.
  • apophthegm / apothegm: Similar because it is a memorable saying of a great person; differs because apophthegm stresses the speaker's authority and anecdotal occasion, while aphorism stresses the sentence's universal form.
  • dictum: Similar because it is an authoritative pronouncement; differs because dictum stresses authoritative utterance, while aphorism stresses concision and polish.
  • precept: Similar because it conveys a principle for conduct; differs because precept is explicitly instructional and moral, while aphorism can be descriptive or paradoxical.
  • gnome: Similar because in its literary sense it means a pithy maxim; differs because gnome is rare, archaic, and tied to ancient Greek moral verse, while aphorism is current.
  • saw: Similar because it means an old moral saying; differs because saw is often dismissive (an old saw = a cliché), while aphorism retains prestige.
  • motto: Similar because it is a short guiding sentence; differs because motto is adopted by a person or institution as a principle, while aphorism is offered to all as an observation.
