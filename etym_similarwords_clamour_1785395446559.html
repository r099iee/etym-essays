• Din: Similar because both are loud noises; differs because din can be any prolonged loud sound, from machinery, traffic, or weather, with no implication of voices or purpose, while a clamour is specifically collective vocal noise made by many throats at once.
  • Racket: Similar because both name loud, disturbing noise; differs because a racket is irregular and clattering, often of things knocking together, and disapprovingly informal, while a clamour is the coordinated shouting of many voices.
  • Uproar: Similar because both describe noisy commotion; differs because uproar emphasizes disorder and chaos, the breaking of quiet, while clamour emphasizes volume, coordination, and a collective demand — a clamour is purposeful where an uproar is chaotic.
  • Noise: Similar because both are sound; differs because noise is any sound at all, broad and neutral, while a clamour specifically means loud, raised voices united in protest, demand, or outcry.
  • Hubbub: Similar because both name the confused sound of a crowd; differs because hubbub is the indistinct, buzzing chatter of a busy gathering, without the urgent, demanding force or the volume of a clamour.
  • Cacophony: Similar because both name harsh, jarring sound; differs because cacophony is a clashing discord of many sounds — musical or mechanical — while a clamour is human (or animal) voices crying out together with purpose.
  • Outcry: Similar because both name a public raising of voices; differs because outcry emphasizes protest and public indignation, often a reaction to an event, while clamour can be a mere loud noise as well as a demand, and carries more sheer volume.
  • Babel: Similar because both evoke the confused noise of many voices; differs because Babel is specifically a din of mutually unintelligible speech — a failure of understanding — while a clamour is united, a single demand made by many throats.
  • Turmoil: Similar because both imply disturbance and agitation; differs because turmoil is a state of confusion and upheaval, not a sound at all, while a clamour is specifically an audible outcry.
  • Vociferation: Similar because both mean loud, insistent shouting or crying out; differs because vociferation is a formal, technical word for noisy utterance, while clamour is the common literary noun for the collective cry itself.
