PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed)

*ḱerh₂- / *ḱer-

"horn, head, topmost projecting point"

(ancient root for horns and projecting promontories; source of Latin cornu, Greek keras, and English horn)

↓

Proto-Celtic (reconstructed)

*karno- / *kornu-

"horn, horn-like headland, promontory"

↓

Common Brittonic / Celtic (c. 1st–4th c. CE; Roman-era tribal name)

Cornovii / *Kernow

"the people of the horn; dwellers of the horn-shaped peninsula"

(referring to the tapering southwestern horn of Great Britain)

↓

Old English / Anglo-Saxon (8th–10th c.)

Cornwealas (noun) / Cornwylisc / Cornwielisc (adjective)

"the Horn-Celts; of the Welsh/Celts of the Horn"

(compound of the Celtic tribal name Corn- + Proto-Germanic *walhaz / Old English wealas 'foreigners, Celts, Britons' + adjectival suffix -isc)

↓

Middle English (12th–14th c.)

Cornyssh / Cornisshe / Corniss

"belonging to Cornwall, its people, or language"

↓

Early Modern to Modern English (16th c. – present)

CORNISH

"pertaining to Cornwall, its Celtic people, language, or folklore (e.g. Cornish ogre)"
