Single linear derivation chain for hutch: 1. ORIGIN UNKNOWN / DEBATED — there is no securely established PIE root or earlier stage for this word. Two hypotheses are current: (a) a Gaulish / Celtic substrate word for a chest or woven basket, surviving into Gallo-Roman speech; (b) the Frankish / West Germanic root *hutta- "shelter, covering, shed" (the source of German Hütte and English hut), with the "box for storing" sense developed from "covered shelter." Neither is proven; OED marks the origin as uncertain, and no attested pre-Latin form survives. This stage must therefore be recorded honestly as unknown. 2. Medieval Latin hutica (also hucha, hucca) — "a chest, coffer, storage box" (recorded in early medieval documents, from the 9th c. onward). The non-classical Latin word for the wooden storage chest of the medieval household, attested in monastic inventories and administrative records across Gaul and Western Europe. 3. Old French huche — "a chest, trunk, bin, kneading-trough" (attested from the 10th–12th c.). Gallo-Romance carried the Latin chest-word into the daily vocabulary of the medieval kitchen and pantry. 4. Middle English hucche / huche — "a chest, bin, storage box" (attested from the 13th c., after the Norman Conquest). The Old French word crosses the Channel with the Anglo-Norman dialect. 5. Modern English hutch — "a storage chest; a wooden animal cage (especially for rabbits); a display cabinet; (mining) an ore tub or wagon" (attested from the 14th c. onward) — the final node, the note's exact word form, highlighted above. NOTE ON DEBATE: the old assignment of the word to PIE *kew- "to bend, curve" (via a claimed connection to "hut") is speculative and not accepted by OED; the chain above gives the attested record from Medieval Latin onward and states explicitly that the earlier stage is unknown.
