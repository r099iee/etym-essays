BOUTS is the plural of bout, denoting in its most familiar modern sense a short, sharp period or fit of something, especially illness, weather, or activity: a bout of flu, a bout of coughing, early bouts of arthritis, a bout of depression, a bout of drinking. The noun profiles boundedness and recurrence: each bout has onset, peak, and remission, and bouts imply more will come. Register is everyday-medical and journalistic, sympathetic without being technical, suitable for school reports and biographies where precise diagnosis matters less than disrupted life.

In the immediate context, BOUTS frames chronic disease as episodic assault: early ______ of the arthritis that crippled him in later life. The young Baker already suffers intermittent arthritic attacks, forerunners of the ankylosing or rheumatoid condition that will stiffen him. The plural matters: not one accident but a pattern, arthritis announcing itself in instalments. Paired with glandular fever and ill health, bouts completes a triptych of bodily vulnerability explaining the unusual extra school year.

  • Primary sense: fit or spell of illness or trouble. A bout of malaria, of morning sickness, of hay fever. Semantic bridge from circuit or turn: an illness goes its round or course through the body and returns, like a wrestler circling back. The bridge preserves roundness and return while adding suffering.
  • Secondary sense: contest, match, or round of fighting and sport. A boxing bout, a wrestling bout, a bout of fisticuffs. Here bout keeps its oldest athletic sense of a turn at combat, a going-and-returning of blows. Bridge to illness is metaphorical combat: to have a bout of flu is to go rounds with flu, the patient as boxer against disease.
  • Tertiary senses: spell of activity, labour, or indulgence. A bout of hard work, of house-painting, a drinking bout, a bout of nostalgia. Dialectally a bout can mean a turn at ploughing (a bout-row) or a circuit of the field. Bridge: from physical turn around a field to temporal turn at any task; what transfers is bounded effort with start and finish, whether ploughing, painting, or pub-crawling.

Connotation is transient-but-recurrent: a bout is never trivial while it lasts, yet implies recovery between rounds. To speak of early bouts dignifies schoolboy pains as genuine premonitions rather than growing pains, inviting retrospective pity for a body already betraying its owner.

Sporting usage keeps precise etiquette: boxers weigh in for a bout, programmes list bouts in order, and commentators score each bout round by round, while medical usage keeps parallel precision in notes recording frequency, duration, and triggers of bouts. Both domains respect the same temporal shape — a bounded clash followed by interval — which is why the same noun serves ringside and bedside without strain, and why early bouts of arthritis reads instantly as both diagnosis and contest.
