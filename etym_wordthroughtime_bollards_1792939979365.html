- Medieval — boles and trunks: bole, the tree-trunk, gives the language a word for thick stumpy timber standing fast.
- 16th–18th c. — shipboard bitts: bollard appears for stout deck posts, tree-trunks brought aboard as rope-holders.
- 1840s — ashore on quays: expanding docks need named mooring furniture; bollard fixes on the iron mushroom-heads along coping stones.
- Victorian — whitewash and gaslight: painted bollards with lamps, exactly the source scene, mark safe edges on night quays.
- 20th c. — streets adopt them: traffic engineers plant bollards to save pavements and pedestrians from the motor car.
- Today — security furniture: crash-rated steel bollards guard bridges, stadia, and waterfronts, old rope-holders turned ram-stoppers.
