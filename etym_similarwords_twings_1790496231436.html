• twigs: Similar because it is the standard modern word for the same slender shoots; differs because twigs is universal and neutral, while twings is a rare dialectal spelling of it.
  • switches: Similar because they are supple rods used for whipping or sweeping; differs because switch stresses punishment use, while twings stresses broom material.
  • withes / withies: Similar because they are pliant willow or hazel shoots for binding; differs because withes are specifically for tying and weaving, while twings are for brooms.
  • osiers: Similar because they are thin willow shoots; differs because osiers belong to basketry vocabulary, while twings belong to broom-making.
  • sprays: Similar because they are slender leafy shoots; differs because spray is botanical and decorative, while twings is rustic and functional.
  • besom (material sense): Similar because a besom is made of such twigs; differs because besom names the finished broom, while twings names its components.
  • faggots (sticks): Similar because they are bundles of sticks; differs because faggots are for fuel, while twings are for sweeping (and in Ruskin, for whipping).
  • birch (rod): Similar because birch rods were the classic whipping implement; differs because birch names the tree and the punishment, while twings names the individual shoots.
  • sprouts / shoots: Similar because they are young plant growths; differs because sprouts are general botany, while twings are cut, dried, and bound artefacts.
  • kindling: Similar because it is also small sticks in bundles; differs because kindling is for lighting fires, while twings are for sweeping.
