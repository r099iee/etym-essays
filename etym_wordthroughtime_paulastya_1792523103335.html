- [Vedic & Brāhmaṇa Era, c. 1500–800 BCE] — The primordial sage Pulastya emerges in the Ṛgveda and Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa as one of the seven cosmic mind-born seers (Saptarshis) and Prajāpatis of Brahmā — [perception: venerable, sacred progenitor of celestial and earthly wisdom]
- [Epic Era, c. 500 BCE–200 CE: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa] — Formulated through Pāṇinian patronymic rules into Paulastya to designate Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, and their clan; functions dramatically to emphasize Rāvaṇa's high brahminical sage ancestry juxtaposed against his rakshasa arrogance and spiritual degeneration — [perception: tragic, awe-inspiring titan endowed with Vedic mastery whose hubris brings divine retribution]
- [Classical Kavya Era, c. 4th–8th c. CE] — Adopted as a high-register poetic patronymic by master dramatists and poets such as Kālidāsa (in Raghuvaṃśa), Bhavabhūti (in Mahāvīracarita), and Bhaṭṭi (in Bhaṭṭikāvya), where "the slaying of Paulastya" (Paulastya-vadha) serves as an elevated alternative title for the Rāmāyaṇa epic — [perception: grand literary and metrical moniker denoting the supreme archetypal antagonist]
- [Medieval Purāṇic Era, c. 8th–15th c. CE] — Codified in the Viṣṇu, Bhāgavata, and Padma Purāṇas within extensive mythological genealogies classifying the Paulastya race, contrasting the virtuous wealth-god Kubera (also a Paulastya) and pious Vibhīṣaṇa against Rāvaṇa's destructive clan — [perception: complex mythological lineage encompassing both celestial virtue and demonic catastrophe]
- [Modern Indological & Contemporary Cultural Era, 19th–21st c.] — Studied in global Indology and comparative mythology as a quintessential example of Sanskrit patronymic morphology (*vṛddhi* ablaut) and celebrated in living traditional theatre (Rāmlīlā, Kathakali, Yakshagana) — [perception: enduring scholarly and cultural emblem of majestic tragic complexity in Indian literary heritage]
