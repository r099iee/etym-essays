- 14th-16th c. shog/jog - a push, shake, and trudge; expressive and Northern
- 14th c. trotten - horse gait borrowed from French; brisk and equestrian
- Early 19th c. jog-trot compound - slowest trot and dull round; sporting and moral
- 1820s Ruskin childhood pace - hired pair at walking trot; observant and commercial
- Victorian figurative staple - jog-trot minds, parishes, and prose; humdrum and reliable
- 1960s jogging revival - foot jog-trot as health exercise; popular and medical
- Modern routine metaphor - out of the jog-trot, stuck in a jog-trot; psychological and managerial
