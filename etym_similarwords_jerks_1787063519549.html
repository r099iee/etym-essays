• Twitches: Similar because both describe sudden, involuntary, sharp muscular movements; differs because a *twitch* is minor, small, localized, and subtle (such as a fluttering eyelid or minor nerve reflex), whereas a *jerk* involves a larger, violent, abrupt displacement of an entire limb or body.
  • Spasms: Similar because both represent involuntary muscular contractions; differs because a *spasm* is a sustained, often painful sustained contraction or physiological cramp, whereas a *jerk* is an instantaneous, sharp kinetic snap.
  • Jolts: Similar because both describe sudden, jarring mechanical shocks; differs because a *jolt* emphasizes the external shock or impact delivered to an object from the outside (like a car hitting a pothole), whereas a *jerk* can be an internally generated sudden pull, snap, or convulsion.
  • Tics: Similar because both involve rapid, recurrent motor actions; differs because a *tic* is a chronic, repetitive, stereotyped behavioral or neurodevelopmental motor pattern (e.g. Tourette syndrome), whereas a *jerk* is an isolated, erratic physical movement.
  • Convulsions: Similar because both describe violent, uncontrolled bodily thrashing; differs because *convulsions* denote whole-body, rhythmic, continuous paroxysms of contractions over an extended duration, whereas *jerks* can be discrete, individual sharp snaps.
  • Shudders: Similar because both are sudden bodily reactions; differs because a *shudder* is a trembling vibration caused by cold, horror, or disgust, lacking the sharp angular violence of a *jerk*.
  • Tugs: Similar because both involve applying tension or pulling force; differs because a *tug* is a deliberate, steady pull of modest force (as in tugging a rope), whereas a *jerk* is an abrupt, instantaneous snap of tension.
