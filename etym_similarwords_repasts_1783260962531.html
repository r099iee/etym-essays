• Meal: Similar because both denote an occasion of eating; differs because meal is the plain, everyday, colourless word, while a repast carries studied, literary formality.
  • Feast: Similar because both are meals; differs because feast implies lavish, celebratory, abundant eating, while a repast may be the humblest of meals.
  • Banquet: Similar because both are formal meals; differs because banquet means a large public or ceremonial dinner, whereas a repast need be nothing grand.
  • Refection: Similar because both are formal words for a meal; differs because refection is rarer and more archaic (a light restorative meal, from Latin reficere), while repast still has a usable, elegant life.
  • Fare: Similar because both relate to food served; differs because fare denotes the food itself ("country fare"), close to provisions, not the occasion of eating.
  • Victuals: Similar because both concern food; differs because victuals stresses plain, practical foodstuffs with an earthy, rustic flavour, far from repast's decorum.
  • Collation: Similar because both name an occasion of eating; differs because collation is specifically a light meal between regular meals, originally a monastic term, narrower in scope than repast.
  • Spread: Similar because both can mean a meal laid out; differs because spread is informal and implies abundance spread on a table, not the formal or literary tone of repast.
  • Sustenance: Similar because both concern feeding; differs because sustenance is what nourishes — food and drink in general — abstract and neutral, not a single occasion of eating.
  • Nourishment: Similar because both relate to food; differs because nourishment is the abstract nutritional value or act of feeding, not an occasion of taking a meal.
  • Board: Similar because both can mean regular meals (room and board); differs because board is institutional and transactional, the provision of meals as a service.
  • Dinner: Similar because both are meals; differs because dinner is the specific, concrete main meal of the day, modern and everyday, without repast's elevation.
  • Feeding: Similar because both relate to taking food; differs because feeding is plain and even animal, while repast is dignified and human, the act of eating as ritual.
