• Spoon: Similar because both are handheld utensils used to handle food and liquid at a table; differs because a spoon is small and shallow, carried to the mouth, whereas a ladle is large and deep-bowled, used only to transfer liquid from a big vessel to plates.
  • Scoop: Similar because both have a cup-shaped bowl on a handle for lifting material; differs because a scoop has a rigid, often flat-bottomed bowl built for dry solids such as flour, grain, or ice cream, while a ladle's rounded, cupped bowl is built for fluids.
  • Dipper: Similar because both draw liquid out of a large container; differs because a dipper is a smaller, simpler cup on a handle for casual or rustic use (a barrel, a tankard), whereas a ladle is the larger, more formal serving spoon of a table setting.
  • Skimmer: Similar because both are long-handled implements used at the pot; differs because a skimmer is perforated and lifts solids out of liquid, while a ladle is unperforated and holds the liquid itself.
  • Serving spoon: Similar because both transfer food from a communal dish to a plate; differs because a serving spoon is flat and shallow, better for solid food and salads, while a ladle is deep and cupped, built specifically for liquids.
  • Punch bowl accessory / toddy lifter: Similar because both serve liquid from a large communal vessel; differs because these are specialised bar or punch-service utensils, whereas the ladle is the general, everyday word for the long-handled deep-bowled server.
