• extrasensory perception (ESP): Similar because clairvoyance is one of the primary operational forms of ESP; differs because ESP is a broad, clinical umbrella term coined in the 1930s that encompasses clairvoyance, telepathy, precognition, and retrocognition collectively, whereas clairvoyance refers specifically to anomalous visual or spatial perception of objective facts and objects.
  • telepathy: Similar because both are anomalous psychic phenomena investigated in parapsychology; differs fundamentally because telepathy is strictly mind-to-mind communication (reading thoughts, feelings, or mental images of another conscious person), whereas clairvoyance is the direct perception of physical objects, locations, or states of affairs without any mental intermediary.
  • precognition: Similar because both involve knowing things inaccessible to ordinary sensory observation; differs because precognition is strictly temporal (perceiving future events before they occur), whereas clairvoyance primarily describes spatial perception of contemporary, currently existing hidden objects or distant scenes (though it can overlap in prophecy).
  • second sight: Similar because both denote an innate supernatural faculty of perceiving distant or supernatural occurrences; differs because second sight (*taibhsearachd*) is specifically an ancient Scottish and Celtic folkloric tradition, predominantly involuntary and characterized by grim, terrifying visions of impending deaths and funeral shrouds, whereas clairvoyance is a formal, international parapsychological and literary term.
  • remote viewing: Similar because both describe the ability to perceive and sketch distant, hidden geographical locations; differs because remote viewing is a strict, protocol-driven intelligence and laboratory methodology developed in the 1970s (Project Stargate at SRI), designed to minimize occult interpretation through double-blind coordinate systems, whereas clairvoyance carries historic spiritual, mesmeric, and intuitive nuances.
  • intuition: Similar because both allow an individual to grasp truths without conscious linear reasoning; differs because intuition is a universally recognized, natural psychological process based on subconscious pattern recognition, instinct, and experience, whereas clairvoyance claims actual perception of objective data beyond physical sensory reach.
  • prescience: Similar because both describe exceptional foresight regarding unfolding developments; differs because prescience is a secular intellectual and literary term denoting keen predictive wisdom and acute foresight, without requiring any occult, extrasensory, or paranormal mechanisms.
  • discernment: Similar because historically clairvoyance meant clear-sighted judgment; differs because discernment refers strictly to moral, intellectual, and aesthetic discrimination—the ability to distinguish between truth and falsehood, good and evil, or quality and mediocrity.
  • divination: Similar because both seek to discover hidden or future information; differs because divination typically relies upon formal external occult rituals, mechanical apparatuses, and symbolic systems (such as tarot cards, runes, astrology, or scrying), whereas clairvoyance is conceived as an internal, perceptual sensory faculty.
  • scrying: Similar because both involve visual perception of hidden realms; differs because scrying specifically requires staring into a reflective, translucent, or dark surface (such as a crystal ball, mirror, or pool of ink) to induce visions, serving as a mechanical catalyst for clairvoyance.
  • sagacity: Similar because both reflect profound insight; differs because sagacity is the grounded, worldly wisdom and shrewd practical judgment acquired through long life experience, contemplation, and philosophical maturity.
