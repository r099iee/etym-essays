• Curtly: Similar because both describe brief, abrupt speech; differs because curtly emphasises extreme brevity and the economy of words themselves (a curt reply is a short reply), while brusquely combines short words with a rough, offhand physical manner — one can be brusque with many words, but curt always means few.
  • Abruptly: Similar because both describe sudden, unceremonious behaviour; differs because abruptly is the neutral word for anything that ends or happens suddenly, with no implication of rudeness, while brusquely adds the prickly, discourteous edge of rough impatience.
  • Bluntly: Similar because both describe direct, unvarnished speech; differs because bluntly focuses on frank, honest truth-telling without softening (the blunt person says exactly what they think), while brusquely focuses on the rough, abrupt manner rather than the honesty of the content.
  • Gruffly: Similar because both describe rough, abrupt speech; differs because gruffly specifically involves a deep, hoarse, throaty voice and often a gruff exterior that hides kindness, while brusquely describes the manner and tone of social interaction, not the physical voice quality.
  • Tersely: Similar because both describe short, unfriendly speech; differs because tersely is neutral or even approving of efficient brevity in writing and speech, while brusquely is mildly disapproving of the roughness of the manner.
  • Sharply: Similar because both describe cutting, abrupt delivery; differs because sharply emphasises the piercing, stinging quality of the words or tone and can be approving of precision, while brusquely emphasises blunt, offhand roughness.
  • Snappishly: Similar because both describe irritable, sharp speech; differs because snappishly implies active bad temper and a biting retort, while brusquely implies mere impatience or prickly briskness without open anger.
  • Testily: Similar because both describe impatient, sharp responses; differs because testily reveals irritation or peevishness provoked by the situation, while brusquely may be a permanent, impersonal habit of abruptness.
  • Shortly: Similar because both describe brief, abrupt answers; differs because shortly also means "soon" and as a manner-word is weaker and less condemning than brusquely, which names a distinctly rough and offhand treatment.
  • Peremptorily: Similar because both describe commanding, abrupt speech that admits no reply; differs because peremptorily stresses the finality and authority of the command, while brusquely stresses the rough, discourteous manner of delivery.
  • Rudely: Similar because both describe discourteous treatment; differs because rudely is broader and more condemnatory, covering any kind of impoliteness or offence, while brusquely names the specific, milder failing of abruptness and lack of ceremony.
  • Dismissively: Similar because both describe offhand, unceremonious treatment; differs because dismissively implies actively waving someone or something off as unworthy, while brusquely implies rough haste and impatience without necessarily judging the other person worthless.
  • Offhandedly: Similar because both describe casual, unceremonious behaviour; differs because offhandedly can be relaxed and unstudied without any rudeness, while brusquely carries the prickly sharpness of impatience.
  • Unceremoniously: Similar because both describe behaviour stripped of ceremony and politeness; differs because unceremoniously is a neutral description of the absence of formalities, while brusquely adds the active edge of rough, sharp abruptness.
