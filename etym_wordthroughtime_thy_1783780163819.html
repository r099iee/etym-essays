Old & Middle English Ordinary Use - Thy is the everyday possessive for a single addressee, the normal "your" of the language, used before consonants in both mundane and sacred speech.
Early Modern Sacred Intimacy - As you-forms take over the polite register, thy becomes the pronoun of devotion and intimacy — the voice of prayer ("thy kingdom come") and of address to God, kin, or beloved, marked by reverence.
18th-19th Century Archaism - Driven from ordinary speech, thy survives in the King James Bible, hymns, and poetic diction, where it instantly lifts a sentence into scripture or verse and acquires a deliberately solemn ring.
Contemporary Sense - Today thy is archaic and ceremonial, used in fixed quotations, prayer, poetry, and deliberate period flavour, while your is the unmarked everyday possessive.
