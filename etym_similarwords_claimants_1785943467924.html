• applicants: Similar because both are people who seek something from an authority; differs because applicants request a position, benefit, or grant at the discretion of the grantor, while claimants assert an existing right they maintain is owed.
  • petitioners: Similar because both formally approach an authority for a benefit or remedy; differs because petitioners humbly request action or relief, whereas claimants demand what they argue is already due to them.
  • plaintiffs: Similar because both are parties who initiate legal action; differs because plaintiff is the technical term for the suing party in a civil court (especially in American usage), while claimant is broader and covers insurance, administrative, and dynastic assertions outside the courtroom.
  • pretenders: Similar because both assert a right to a throne or title; differs because pretender implies the claim is disputed, unrecognized, or invalid, while claimant is neutral about the validity of the assertion.
  • litigants: Similar because both are parties involved in legal proceedings; differs because litigant covers every party to a lawsuit, including defendants, while claimant names only the party asserting the right or demand.
  • supplicants: Similar because both seek something from a higher power or authority; differs because supplicants plead humbly and submissively, while claimants press a right with confidence and even aggression.
  • aspirants: Similar because both seek an office, title, or position; differs because aspirants merely desire or strive toward a goal without asserting an entitlement, while claimants assert a specific right or title.
  • heirs: Similar because both can claim an inheritance or succession; differs because an heir is a person legally entitled by descent or will, while a claimant may have a disputed, contested, or merely asserted entitlement.
  • demandants: Similar because both assert a legal demand for something; differs because demandant is a rare, technical historical term in property law, while claimant is the modern standard term across all areas of claims.
  • contenders: Similar because both compete for a title, office, or prize; differs because contenders merely compete in a contest, while claimants assert a formal right or entitlement to what is contested.
