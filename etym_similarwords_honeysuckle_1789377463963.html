• Woodbine: Similar because both refer historically and poetically to the fragrant climbing honeysuckle (specifically Lonicera periclymenum); differs because woodbine is an archaic, folk-botanical name describing its habit of winding around trees and binding wood, whereas honeysuckle is the standard modern common name emphasizing its sweet nectar.
  • Clematis: Similar because both are woody climbing vines widely cultivated to adorn garden walls, trellises, and porch arbors; differs because clematis belongs to the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae), producing broad, flat, showy petals without sweet-scented tubular nectar tubes, whereas honeysuckle belongs to Caprifoliaceae and features two-lipped tubular blossoms.
  • Jasmine: Similar because both are climbing vines famous for producing extraordinarily sweet, intoxicating evening fragrances; differs because jasmine belongs to the olive family (Oleaceae) with star-shaped white or yellow petals, whereas honeysuckle has distinct elongated, nectar-filled tubular florets.
  • Ivy: Similar because both are vigorous evergreen or semi-evergreen climbers that attach themselves to porches, trellises, and brickwork; differs because ivy climbs by adventitious clinging rootlets rather than twining stems, has leathery foliage without sweet nectar, and lacks the showy, fragrant blossoms of honeysuckle.
  • Wisteria: Similar because both are vigorous woody climbing vines trained over garden arbors; differs because wisteria belongs to the pea family (Fabaceae) and bears massive, pendulous racemes of violet-blue pea-like flowers in early spring, whereas honeysuckle blooms with tubular nectar flowers throughout the summer.
  • Bindweed: Similar because both are twining climbing plants found in English hedgerows; differs because bindweed (in the convolvulus family) has trumpet-shaped morning glory flowers and is widely despised as a pernicious, deep-rooted agricultural weed, whereas honeysuckle is a treasured woody ornamental.
