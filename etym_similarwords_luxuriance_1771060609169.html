• abundance: Similar because both mean "great plenty"; differs because "abundance" is purely quantitative and neutral (an abundance of gravel), while "luxuriance" implies rich, thriving, sensuous growth or elaboration.
  • profusion: Similar because both mean "great quantity poured forth"; differs because "profusion" stresses sheer amount and even wastefulness, while "luxuriance" stresses vigorous, healthy, organic fullness.
  • lushness: Similar because both mean "rich, juicy, thriving growth"; differs because "lushness" is a modern, sensory word for green watery health, while "luxuriance" carries a longer history of excess and opulence.
  • richness: Similar because both mean "abundant and splendid"; differs because "richness" is generic (can be financial, flavoured, or textural), while "luxuriance" is specifically about unrestrained, thriving growth.
  • opulence: Similar because both mean "wealthy, abundant display"; differs because "opulence" belongs to money, jewels, and ornament, while "luxuriance" applies to plants, hair, and organic vitality.
  • rankness: Similar because both mean "overgrown, wild, coarse abundance of vegetation"; differs because "rankness" carries negative or weedy overtones, while "luxuriance" is admiring and sensuous.
  • exuberance: Similar because both mean "overflowing vitality"; differs because "exuberance" is chiefly about spirits and energy (and is positive), while "luxuriance" is about growth and elaboration of form.
  • excess: Similar because both mean "beyond what is needed"; differs because "excess" is abstract, neutral-to-negative and quantitative, while "luxuriance" is concrete, admiring, and describes flourishing fullness.
  • fullness: Similar because both mean "completeness of quantity or development"; differs because "fullness" is plain and general, while "luxuriance" evokes rich, tangled, vital overgrowth.
  • verdure: Similar because both describe lush green vegetation; differs because "verdure" is specifically the greenness of growing plants, while "luxuriance" extends to abundance of any growth, style, or vitality.
  • plethora: Similar because both mean "superabundance"; differs because "plethora" is clinical and mildly pejorative (too much of a bad thing), while "luxuriance" is admiring and sensuous.
