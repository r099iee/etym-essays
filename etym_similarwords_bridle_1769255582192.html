• halter: Similar because both are headgear placed on a horse, and both can be verbs meaning to restrain a horse. Differs because a halter (headcollar) has no bit and is used only for leading or tying, never steering; a bridle has bit and reins and is the instrument of control and direction.
  • harness: Similar because both are horse equipment, and both are verbs meaning to control or put to use. Differs because a harness is the whole system of straps that attaches the horse to a cart or plow (and is used of any gear or equipment), while a bridle is specifically the head-and-mouth control gear.
  • rein: Similar because both are part of the horse-control system, and both are used figuratively for restraint ("the reins of power," "to rein in"). Differs because the rein is the long strap held by the rider, part of the bridle; to rein in is to slow or restrain, while to bridle is to put on the headgear or, as a verb, to show offended pride.
  • bit: Similar because both are part of the horse's mouth control, and both are figurative for control and severity ("the bit between the teeth"). Differs because the bit is the metal mouthpiece itself — a part of the bridle — while bridle is the whole headgear, and as a verb bridle means restraint rather than the sharpness of a bit.
  • curb: Similar because both are restraint devices (a curb is also a chain under a horse's chin, part of a bridle) and both verbs mean "to restrain, check, hold in check." Differs because curb (the verb) is general and modern — curbing costs, curbing enthusiasm — and its noun is a roadside edge or a limit, while bridle is more specifically equestrian and, as a verb, can also mean "to show offence."
  • headstall: Similar because both are the head parts of horse equipment. Differs because the headstall is only the leather cap over the head (without bit and reins), a component of the bridle, not the whole apparatus.
  • hackamore: Similar because both are headgear used to control a horse. Differs because a hackamore (or bosal) is a bitless bridle that works by pressure on the nose; a standard bridle works through the bit in the mouth.
  • control: Similar because both, as verbs, mean to govern, direct, or hold in check (to bridle / to control one's temper). Differs because control is the broad, neutral modern verb for any direction or command, while bridle is a vivid, equestrian metaphor restricted to strong restraint.
  • restrain: Similar because both mean to hold back or check (bridle your anger = restrain your anger). Differs because restrain is general (physical or legal), while bridle is a specific, slightly old-fashioned figure of putting a bit on unruly passion.
  • check: Similar because both mean to halt or moderate a force or impulse ("to check one's tongue," "to bridle one's tongue"). Differs because check also means to inspect or verify (its dominant modern use), while bridle keeps its horse-and-restraint identity.
  • tame: Similar because both imply bringing wild or unruly force under discipline. Differs because tame means to domesticate an animal or make something docile, while bridle means to impose momentary restraint on what may remain wild.
  • govern: Similar because both mean to exercise controlling authority (governing / bridling the passions). Differs because govern is formal, political, and systematic, while bridle is a concrete, physical metaphor of pulling on reins.
  • stifle: Similar because both can mean to suppress an impulse (stifle a yawn, bridle an impulse). Differs because stifle implies suffocating or killing something, while bridle implies managing and directing it — control without destruction.
  • muzzle: Similar because both are restraint devices on an animal's head, and both are verbs meaning to silence or restrict. Differs because a muzzle covers the mouth to prevent biting or noise, while a bridle works through the mouth to direct; muzzling is silencing, bridling is steering.
  • checkrein: Similar because both are equestrian control gear, and both involve the horse's head. Differs because a checkrein is a specific strap that holds the horse's head up or prevents it from lowering the head — a specialized component — while a bridle is the general headgear with bit and reins.
