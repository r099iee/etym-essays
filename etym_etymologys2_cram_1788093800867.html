Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 100 CE)
*krammōną
"to press, squeeze, stuff"
(no secure PIE root; see note)
↓
Old English (8th–10th c.)
crammian
"to cram, stuff, fill full"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
crammen
"to cram, pack tight, thrust in"
↓
Modern English (16th c.)
cram
"to stuff; to feed greedily"
↓
Modern English (c. 1800)
cram
"to study hastily for an examination"
↓
English (noun, Tolkien) (1937)
cram
"a dense compressed travel-biscuit"

Note: the ultimate Indo-European ancestry of the Germanic root is unknown — no PIE stage is securely recoverable, so none is invented. Dialectal "cram" ("a crumb") may be the same word acting as a noun; Tolkien's food-name is a 20th-century literary reuse of the native verb, not a continuous inheritance.
