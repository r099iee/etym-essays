• Accident: Similar because both denote an unintended, usually harmful event; differs because accident is a far more general and neutral word for anything unplanned, including neutral or even positive chance (e.g. “a happy accident”), whereas mishap strongly implies an unfortunate, usually small, occurrence.
  • Misfortune: Similar because both are unlucky events; differs because misfortune is broader and heavier, covering lasting adversity and abstract strokes of bad luck, while mishap is lighter, fleeting, and often trivial.
  • Misadventure: Similar because both carry the “mis-” of badness plus an event; differs because misadventure implies a venture or undertaking that goes wrong, is more serious and formal, and is also a legal term for accidental death.
  • Blunder: Similar because both are things that go wrong; differs because a blunder is a gross, clumsy error of judgement and squarely implies fault, whereas a mishap is an unlucky accident that need not involve human error.
  • Slip-up: Similar because both are small mishaps; differs because slip-up is informal and specifically a human lapse or oversight, whereas mishap can be impersonal and purely accidental.
  • Contretemps: Similar because both are minor awkward incidents; differs because contretemps is French-derived, suggests an embarrassing social clash or awkward moment, and is far more formal and witty.
  • Setback: Similar because both are adverse occurrences; differs because a setback is a check or reverse in one’s progress or plans, implying delay and consequence rather than the sudden, self-contained unlucky event of a mishap.
  • Calamity: Similar because both are harmful events; differs because calamity is a major catastrophe causing widespread suffering, far graver and more public than a mishap.
  • Disaster: Similar because both are things that go wrong; differs because disaster is a sudden great failure or catastrophe, large-scale and ruinous, whereas a mishap is small and private.
  • Casualty: Similar because both can name an unfortunate occurrence; differs because casualty implies injury or loss of life and is formal, medical, or military, carrying none of mishap’s lightness.
