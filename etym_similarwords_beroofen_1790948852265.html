• Touch wood: Similar because both are common apotropaic charms uttered to prevent good fortune from turning into bad luck; differs because touch wood is the standard British English idiom accompanied by the physical act of tapping timber, whereas beroofen (or oom beroofen) is an eccentric, Germanic/Yiddish-derived colloquial exclamation popularized in comedic literature.
  • Knock on wood: Similar because both serve to ward off the evil eye or superstitious jinxes; differs because knock on wood is the standard North American equivalent phrase, whereas beroofen is a vintage European linguistic loanword.
  • Absit omen: Similar because both are verbal formulas expressing the hope that an utterance will not bring ill fortune; differs because absit omen is formal, scholarly Latin ("may the omen be absent") used in elevated literary and academic discourse, whereas oom beroofen is a humorous, informal vernacular interjection.
  • God forbid: Similar because both express a strong desire to avert an impending or mentioned misfortune; differs because God forbid is a serious or dramatic religious invocation, whereas beroofen is a lighthearted, superstitious folk charm against jinxing positive circumstances.
  • Unberufen: Similar because unberufen is the exact standard German and Yiddish source word from which beroofen / oom beroofen was corrupted; differs because unberufen is the authentic German/Yiddish spelling, whereas beroofen is the Anglicized, phonetic rendering found in P.G. Wodehouse's comedic fiction.
  • Heaven forfend: Similar because both express a desire for protection against misfortune; differs because heaven forfend is an archaic, theatrical English expression of pious dread, whereas beroofen functions specifically after boasting or praising someone.
  • Apotropaic: Similar because both relate to warding off evil; differs because apotropaic is the formal anthropological, archaeological, and linguistic adjective describing the category of rituals and words, whereas beroofen is the actual spoken charm itself.
  • Jinx-averting: Similar because both describe preventing bad luck; differs because jinx-averting is a descriptive modern compound adjective, whereas beroofen is an idiomatic spoken exclamation.
