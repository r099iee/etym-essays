• Saraca asoca: Similar because both refer to the identical botanical tree; differs because Saraca asoca is the formal Latin binomial taxonomic designation used in modern botanical science, whereas aśoka is the cultural, philosophical, and Sanskrit vernacular name.
  • Śāla: Similar because both are sacred indigenous trees deeply venerated in ancient Indian epics and Buddhist history; differs because the Śāla (Shorea robusta) is a massive timber hardwood tree associated with the Buddha's birth and passing (parinirvāṇa), lacking the fiery scarlet blossoms and feminine romantic dohada tradition of the aśoka.
  • Kadamba: Similar because both are revered flowering trees celebrated in classical Sanskrit poetry; differs because the Kadamba (Neolamarckia cadamba) produces spherical golden-yellow blossom balls associated with the monsoon season and Lord Kṛṣṇa's pastoral dances, whereas aśoka blossoms in early spring with scarlet clusters.
  • Palāśa: Similar because both trees produce dazzling fiery red-orange spring blossoms that transform the forest canopy; differs because the Palāśa (Butea monosperma or "Flame of the Forest") has trifoliate leaves and claw-shaped flowers used in Vedic fire rituals, whereas the aśoka has drooping lanceolate pinnate leaves and dense floral clusters.
  • Bakula: Similar because both are prominent flowering trees in classical Sanskrit garden poetry (kāvya); differs because the Bakula (Mimusops elengi) produces small, highly fragrant cream-colored flowers that, according to the dohada tradition, blossom when sprinkled with wine from the mouth of a young woman, rather than the foot-kick of the aśoka.
  • Tamāla: Similar because both are sacred evergreen forest trees of epic groves; differs because the Tamāla (Garcinia xanthochymus) is celebrated for its very dark bark and lustrous leaves, frequently compared to the dark complexion of Viṣṇu, rather than brilliant crimson flowers.
  • Vītaśoka: Similar because both are Sanskrit compound adjectives meaning "freed from sorrow"; differs because Vītaśoka is strictly an abstract adjectival descriptor (literally "having sorrow departed"), lacking any botanical, pharmaceutical, or imperial identity.
  • Niḥśoka: Similar because both are negative compounds signifying grieflessness; differs because Niḥśoka is a purely grammatical Sanskrit adjective, devoid of the mythic, literary, and state-symbolic history of aśoka.
  • Akrodha: Similar because both are ethical terms formed with the privative prefix a-; differs because Akrodha denotes freedom from anger and wrath, whereas aśoka denotes freedom from burning grief and lamentation.
  • Nirvāṇa: Similar because both denote the transcendent cessation of suffering; differs because Nirvāṇa is a comprehensive soteriological term meaning the total extinguishing of desire, ignorance, and rebirth, whereas aśoka is specifically the affective state of grieflessness, alongside its physical tree identity.
  • Mokṣa: Similar because both denote spiritual freedom; differs because Mokṣa is the formal philosophical release of the individual soul from the cosmic wheel of saṃsāra, whereas aśoka functions across botany, history, poetics, and spiritual states.
  • Jonesia asoca: Similar because both refer to the exact same botanical flowering tree; differs because Jonesia asoca is an obsolete 18th-century botanical synonym coined by Sir William Jones, whereas aśoka is the ancient, living Sanskrit name.
