• sensations: Similar because both denote mental states initiated by physical stimuli acting upon sensory receptors; differs because sensations are the raw, unanalyzed, primitive neurobiological data (such as an isolated flash of light, temperature sensation, or pure auditory pitch) before cognitive interpretation, whereas percepts are the integrated, structured, and meaningful perceptual objects produced after cognitive processing.
  • concepts: Similar because both are cognitive mental representations used by the mind to navigate reality; differs because concepts are abstract, universal, and non-sensory mental categories (such as 'justice', 'triangularity', or 'mammal') formed through reflection and logic, whereas percepts are concrete, sensory-bound mental images tied directly to current or immediate sensory encounters.
  • impressions: Similar because both refer to cognitive effects produced by external stimuli; differs because impressions is a broader, more subjective term spanning vague feelings, emotional reactions, and intuitive judgments (as in 'first impressions'), whereas percepts is a rigorous technical term in epistemology and cognitive psychology for a specific sensory construct.
  • perceptions: Similar because both refer to the apprehension of reality through the senses; differs because perception primarily designates the continuous act, psychological process, or cognitive faculty of perceiving, whereas percepts refers specifically to the discrete structural products or psychological objects generated by that process.
  • gestalts: Similar because both describe organized perceptual forms that cannot be reduced to the sum of their individual sensory components; differs because gestalts specifically emphasizes the holistic, structural configuration and emergent relational rules of organization, whereas percepts is the standard phenomenological noun for any unified sensory object.
  • representations: Similar because both are internal cognitive models standing for external states of affairs; differs because representations is an overarching computational and semiotic umbrella encompassing symbolic language, algebraic formulas, memories, and abstract models, whereas percepts are strictly sensory-derived phenomenological objects.
  • images: Similar because both evoke phenomenal sensory qualities in awareness; differs because images frequently implies a quasi-visual internal picture generated by memory or imagination in the absence of external stimuli (mental imagery), whereas percepts strictly require the presence of real-time sensory stimulus reception.
  • stimuli: Similar because both are fundamental components of the perceptual loop; differs because stimuli are physical events or energy patterns in the environment (e.g. photons, sound waves, pressure waves) that activate sensory receptor cells, whereas percepts are internal psychological creations synthesized by the nervous system.
  • constructs: Similar because both acknowledge the active, synthetic role of the human brain in framing reality; differs because constructs typically refers to complex, higher-order theoretical or psychological frameworks (such as 'personality', 'intelligence', or social models), whereas percepts are low-to-mid-level sensory configurations.
  • qualia: Similar because both involve conscious sensory experience; differs because qualia refers to the subjective, qualitative 'what-it-is-like' feeling of an experience (the raw redness of red or the painfulness of pain), whereas percepts refers to the bounded, structured object or spatial configuration perceived in consciousness.
