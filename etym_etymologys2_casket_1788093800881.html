Latin (2nd–1st c. BCE)
capere
“to seize, take, hold, contain”

Latin (1st c. BCE)
capsa
“box, chest — esp. for books,
scrolls; case, container”
(derived from capere
“that which holds”)
↓
Old French (12th c.)
casse
“box, chest”
↓
French (14th c.)
cassette
“little box”
(diminutive of casse, in -ette)
↓
Middle English (mid-15th c.)
casket
“small box for jewels or valuables”
↓
Modern English (19th c. – present)
casket
“small chest; (chiefly US) a coffin”

Note: The -k- spelling is peculiar — probably reflecting the hard k sound of the northern French forms, so the exact continental source of the English word is not fully settled. The “coffin” sense is a mid-19th-century American euphemism of the funeral trade.
