• Vehicle: Similar because both carry or convey things; differs because vehicle is more concrete and technical — literal transport or a figurative medium — and lacks conveyance's formal legal-historical weight.
  • Transport: Similar because both involve moving people or goods; differs because transport names the act or system of movement rather than a person-as-instrument or a legal deed.
  • Carriage: Similar because both can be a means of transport; differs because carriage is a specific wheeled vehicle, more concrete than the abstract or metaphorical conveyance.
  • Carrier: Similar because both carry something; differs because carrier is logistical and can mean a bearer of disease, messages, or cargo, and rarely designates a person-as-instrument in elevated prose.
  • Medium: Similar because both can be a channel for transmitting; differs because medium stresses the material or channel of communication (paint, language, air) without the motion-of-carrying image.
  • Channel: Similar because both transmit something; differs because channel emphasizes a route or conduit for information or signals, not the carrying of people, goods, or property.
  • Conduit: Similar because both pass something along; differs because conduit is a physical pipe or an abstract channel, usually one-way and passive, whereas conveyance implies deliberate carrying.
  • Transmission: Similar because both involve passing something along; differs because transmission stresses the act of sending or passing (signals, disease, power) rather than the means or the legal transfer.
  • Transfer: Similar because both involve moving from one to another; differs because transfer is broad and neutral — people, money, data, energy — without conveyance's sense of an instrument or vehicle, and in law is only loosely synonymous with the deed.
  • Delivery: Similar because both involve conveying something to a destination; differs because delivery emphasizes the handover at the end of the journey and the goods themselves, not the means of carrying.
  • Instrument: Similar because both can be a means by which something is done; differs because instrument is generic for any tool or agent, lacking the motion-along-a-way image of conveyance.
  • Ferry: Similar because both are a means of conveying across water; differs because ferry is a specific vessel and service on a route, much narrower than the general conveyance.
  • Wagon: Similar because both are vehicles that carry goods; differs because wagon is a concrete four-wheeled vehicle, with none of the abstract, legal, or metaphorical extension of conveyance.
  • Communication: Similar because both involve conveying information; differs because communication is the modern umbrella term for information exchange, while conveyance adds the imagery and mechanics of carrying or the legal transfer of property.
