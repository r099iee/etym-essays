• Awry: Similar because both describe situations or arrangements that have departed from the proper order; differs because awry has a stronger visual and physical connotation of being crooked, twisted, or violently derailed, whereas amiss is predominantly intuitive, circumstantial, or moral.
  • Astray: Similar because both stem from ideas of wandering off the correct path; differs because astray emphasizes spatial, physical, or spiritual roaming away from a group or moral truth, while amiss emphasizes defectiveness or impropriety within a situation.
  • Wrong: Similar because both denote error or imperfection; differs because wrong is far more broad, blunt, and absolute, encompassing moral evil, factual falsehood, and total functional failure.
  • Faulty: Similar because both describe defects; differs because faulty is an attributive and predicative adjective pointing directly to structural, mechanical, or logical flaws (e.g., a faulty wire), whereas amiss is strictly predicative and refers to general states of affairs.
  • Askew: Similar because both imply deviation from standard alignment; differs because askew is almost entirely physical and spatial (tilted to one side, crooked).
  • Improper: Similar because both denote deviation from correctness or decency; differs because improper emphasizes social rule-breaking, lack of etiquette, or regulatory illegitimacy.
  • Defective: Similar because both signify that something is lacking or broken; differs because defective is a technical and clinical diagnosis applied to manufactured objects or physiological organs.
  • Flawed: Similar because both indicate imperfection; differs because flawed focuses on an inherent blemish or weakness within an otherwise whole object or character.
