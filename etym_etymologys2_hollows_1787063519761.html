Proto-Indo-European (c. 4500–2500 BCE; reconstructed)
*kel-
"to cover, conceal, save"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE; reconstructed)
*hul- / *hulōn
"hollow, cave; a covered place"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
holh
"cave, den, hollow place, cavity"
↓
Middle English (13th–14th c.)
holowe / holow
"cavity, depression in the ground"
↓
Modern English (present)
hollows
Note on the debate: The PIE gloss "to cover, conceal" is the standard account for the Germanic *hul- family (cf. hole, hell, hall, and Latin-derived cell), the reasoning being that a hollow is a covered or enclosed space; however, some scholars argue for a separate PIE *kel- variant meaning "to bend, curve" (leading to concavity), which would make the "hollow" idea purely about curvature. Both roots are reconstructed, not directly attested.
