- 1920s — Parisian avant-garde & Purist manifesto: Charles-Édouard Jeanneret adopts the pseudonym Le Corbusier in Paris, co-founding the journal L'Esprit Nouveau, publishing Vers une architecture (1923), and shocking academic Beaux-Arts circles with radical Purist villas and the dictum "a house is a machine for living in."
- 1930s–1940s — CIAM leadership & urban polemics: leads the International Congresses of Modern Architecture (CIAM), drafting the Athens Charter; perceived as an uncompromising visionary and radical polemicist whose utopian high-rise city plans spark fiery debate.
- 1950s — Monumental triumph & sculptural Brutalism: stuns the world by pivoting from pristine white functionalism to expressive, raw concrete (béton brut) in the Unité d'Habitation at Marseille and the sacred curves of Notre-Dame du Haut at Ronchamp; commissioned by Prime Minister Nehru to design the new capital of Chandigarh in India.
- 1960s–1980s — Academic canonization vs Postmodern backlash: canonized worldwide as the father of modernism, while facing sharp critiques from urban reformers (Jane Jacobs, Lewis Mumford) who condemn Corbusian urban clearance and car-dominated towers for destroying organic street life.
- 1990s–21st c. — UNESCO World Heritage & enduring historical synthesis: seventeen of his masterworks inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage sites in 2016; "Corbusian" remains a foundational aesthetic term denoting heroic modernist space, pilotis, and sculptural reinforced concrete.
