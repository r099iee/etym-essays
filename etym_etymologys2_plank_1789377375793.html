PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*pleh₂- / *plaḱ-
"flat, broad, spread out"
↓
Proto-Germanic / Proto-Italic Substrate (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*plankō / *plankaz
"broad flat piece of wood, flat board"
Note: Nasalized extension of the PIE root *plaḱ- ('flat'); cognate with Greek pláx ('flat surface, tablet').
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
planca
"broad board, slab of timber, thick plank"
↓
Old Northern French / Norman-Picard (c. 11th–12th c.)
planke / planche
"wooden beam, board, footbridge made of timber"
Note: The Northern Norman-Picard dialect retained the unpalatalized velar stop /k/ (planke), whereas Central Old French palatalized it to /ʃ/ (planche).
↓
Middle English (c. 1250–1300)
planke / plaunke
"thick board, timber walkway, bridge of wood"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
planke / plank
"heavy structural timber, ship's decking"
↓
Modern English (present)
PLANK
