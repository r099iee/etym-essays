Old Norse (early Scandinavian record)
tǫturr
"a torn piece, rag"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
tater
"a rag, torn scrap of cloth"
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
tattered, tatter'd
"torn into tatters, ragged"
↓
Modern English (participial adjective and verb form)
TATTERED
"ragged, hanging in shreds"

Note: The further origin beyond Old Norse is unknown (OED: tǫturr is a word of unknown further origin); the shape of the word (t-…-t-) suggests an expressive or imitative formation. The double-tt spelling stabilises in the 16th century, and a denominative verb tatter "to tear into rags" is recorded from the 16th–17th c., so the participle is both verbal and adjectival.
