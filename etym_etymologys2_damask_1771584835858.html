[Root core] Semitic (possibly pre-Semitic) place-name *Dmsq — the ancient name of the city of Damascus. NO PIE ANCESTRY: this is a toponym, not a conceptual root, and no Indo-European tree can be drawn for it. [Stage 2] Egyptian texts (15th c. BC) t-ms-ḳw "Damascus" — among the oldest records of the name [Stage 3] Akkadian Dimašqa "Damascus" — attested in the Amarna letters (14th c. BC) [Stage 4] Hebrew דַּמֶּשֶׂק (Damméseq) "Damascus" — biblical spelling (Gen. 14:15; 2 Sam. 8:5); Arabic دِمَشْق (Dimashq) — the city's name to this day [Stage 5] Medieval Latin damascus "cloth of Damascus" (12th c.) — the city's name pressed into service as a textile name; the adjective damascenus "of Damascus" had long labelled eastern goods [Stage 6] Old French damas "damask fabric" (13th–14th c.) [Stage 7] English damask (16th c.) "a rich figured silk or linen fabric with a woven pattern" — this card's word; also the adjective "made of or patterned like such fabric" Derivatives along the same chain: damask rose (16th c., via Latin rosa damascena), damson "plum of Damascus" (via Latin damascenum prūnum), damascene/damaskeen "to decorate metal with watered patterns" (17th c., from the metalwork of Damascus), Damascus steel. Note on the debate: The oldest stratum of the city-name may be pre-Semitic; its meaning is unknown and possibly lost before the Semitic languages fixed it. What is not debated is the metonymic transfer: the cloth, rose, plum, and steel all take the city's name because Damascus was the great entrepôt through which these eastern goods reached the Mediterranean.
