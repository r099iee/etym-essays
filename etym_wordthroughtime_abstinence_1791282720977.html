- Religious ascetic discipline — medieval and early modern perception as holy fasting and chastity, admired as proof of spiritual mastery and monastic virtue.
- Civic and moral virtue — 17th–18th century broadening to general temperance and respectability; abstinent person seen as sober, reliable and self-governed.
- Temperance-movement pledge — 19th century politicisation as total abstinence from alcohol; connotation of earnest reformism, chapel respectability, sometimes killjoy puritanism.
- Clinical and public-health term — 20th century medicalisation as abstinence-based recovery and abstinence-only education; perceived as technical treatment goal, sometimes contested as moralistic.
- Modern ironic and general use — contemporary sense of principled non-indulgence in anything (drink, sex, social media), plus Wodehousian mock-heroic use where trivial ailments are comically abstained from.
