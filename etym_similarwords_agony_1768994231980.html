• Pain: Similar because both name physical or mental discomfort; differs because pain is the neutral, clinical fact of discomfort at any intensity, while agony is the extreme, struggling end of pain — a crisis of endurance rather than a sensation.
  • Suffering: Similar because both name undergoing something adverse; differs because suffering is broad, passive endurance of anything unpleasant over time, while agony is a concentrated crisis of pain at the limit of what can be borne.
  • Distress: Similar because both name states of acute discomfort; differs because distress is general, often momentary, and lower in intensity — alarm or strain — while agony is the maximum register of physical or mental pain.
  • Torment: Similar because both name intense suffering; differs because torment implies a tormentor, suffering deliberately inflicted from outside, while agony need not involve any external agent — it can be purely bodily or inward.
  • Anguish: Similar because both name intense mental distress; differs because anguish, from a root meaning "narrowness," is the choking, constricted grief of the mind, while agony retains the physical struggle — the body wrestling at its limit.
  • Misery: Similar because both name unhappy states; differs because misery is prolonged, dull, and passive wretchedness, while agony is sharp, acute, and intense — a crisis rather than a condition.
  • Throes: Similar because both name violent, struggling pain; differs because throes (especially "death throes," "throes of childbirth") name the specific paroxysms of a struggle, while agony is the more general term for the extreme suffering itself.
  • Torture / excruciation: Similar because both name extreme inflicted pain; differs because torture names deliberate infliction by an agent, while agony names the suffered state regardless of source, and excruciating is an adjective rather than a noun of the same family.
  • Woe: Similar because both name deep distress; differs because woe is archaic, literary, and usually mental calamity associated with lamentation, while agony is the physical-plus-mental crisis of endurance.
  • Despair: Similar because both can name the depths of emotional suffering; differs because despair is the loss of hope — a mental condition without the physical register — while agony is pain pushed to the limit of endurance, with or without hopelessness.
