Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*kwankwijanan
"to extinguish, put out"
↓
Old English (attested pre-1100)
cwencan
"to extinguish (fire, light);
to quench (thirst)"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
quenchen
"to put out, extinguish"
↓
English (16th c.)
quench
"to extinguish; to satisfy (thirst)"
↓
English (late 16th c.)
unquenchable
"that cannot be quenched"
↓
English (modern)
UNQUENCHABLE
"impossible to extinguish
or to satisfy"

Note: The deeper origin of cwencan is obscure: the OED marks the ultimate etymology uncertain, and the suggested PIE link (a root meaning "to strike") is speculative. The word is Germanic-only, a causative built beside Old English cwincan "to go out, be extinguished". The affixes are separate elements: un- (Old English un-, from PIE *n- "not") and -able (from Latin -abilis via Old French), combined in England in the late 16th century.
