• Heart tree: Similar because both refer to the sacred white tree worshipped in Westeros; differs because heart tree specifically denotes the single carved weirwood standing at the center of a godswood, while weirwood names the entire species.
  • Yggdrasil: Similar because both are mythical world trees connecting realms and housing divine wisdom; differs because Yggdrasil is the cosmic ash tree of Norse mythology spanning nine worlds, whereas weirwood is a terrestrial species of red-leafed, white-barked trees.
  • Nemeton: Similar because both represent sacred groves where ancient rituals and nature worship take place; differs because nemeton is a historical Celtic sacred space, not a species of tree.
  • Sentinel tree: Similar because both act as silent guardians watching over a landscape; differs because sentinel is a general metaphor, while weirwood literally has carved eyes that see across time.
  • World tree: Similar because both embody the archetype of a sacred central tree of life and memory; differs because world tree is a global mythological motif, while weirwood is a specific literary creation.
  • Totem pole: Similar because both are carved trees holding spiritual and ancestral meaning for a community; differs because totem pole is a historical carved pole of Northwest Coast peoples, while weirwood is a living, sentient tree of fantasy.
  • Tree of the Dead / Gallows tree: Similar because both connect trees to death, sacrifice, and memory; differs because the gallows is punitive, while the weirwood is sacred and redemptive.
