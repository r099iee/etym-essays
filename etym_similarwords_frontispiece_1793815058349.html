• Title page: Similar because both appear at the very beginning of a printed book; differs because the title page is a typographic leaf containing essential bibliographical text (title, subtitle, author name, publisher, and publication year), whereas the frontispiece is an artistic or pictorial illustration positioned on the verso facing the title page.
  • Facade: Similar because both denote the prominent exterior front of an architectural structure; differs because facade (from French façade, from Italian facciata) is a universal architectural term for any exterior building face or elevation, whereas an architectural frontispiece specifically refers to an ornamental, sculpted entrance portal, central bay, or pedimented feature framing the doorway.
  • Pediment: Similar because both refer to decorative architectural compositions crowning an entrance; differs because a pediment is a specific triangular or segmental classical gable above an entablature, while an architectural frontispiece encompasses the entire decorative framing portal, columns, entablature, and niches combined.
  • Cover art: Similar because both provide an introductory visual presentation for a published book; differs because cover art is printed on the exterior binding, dust jacket, or paperback cover for retail marketing and shelf display, whereas the frontispiece is a formal interior plate bound inside the preliminary leaves.
  • Vignette: Similar because both are artistic book illustrations; differs because a vignette is a small illustration with soft, unframed edges that fade into the surrounding page and can be integrated anywhere within text, whereas a frontispiece is typically an imposing, full-page framed plate.
  • Colophon: Similar because both are distinctive structural elements of classical book design; differs because the colophon is placed at the very end of the volume recording printing, typeface, and press details, representing the exact positional and functional opposite of the opening frontispiece.
  • Headpiece: Similar because both are opening ornamental features; differs because a headpiece is a decorative banner or woodcut placed at the head of a chapter above the text block, whereas a frontispiece is a standalone full-page plate preceding the main text entirely.
  • Poutraiture: Similar because author portraits frequently serve as frontispieces; differs because portraiture refers to the artistic genre of depicting a human face, while frontispiece defines the specific structural position within a book, whether the artwork is a portrait, an allegorical landscape, or an architectural arch.
