Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE)
αὔρα (aúra)
"breeze, breath of air"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
aura
"air, breeze, breath"
↓
Vulgar Latin (reconstructed)
exaurāre
"to raise into the air"
ex- "out, up" + aura "air"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
essorer
"to fly up, soar, rise on the air"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
soren
"to fly aloft, hover at great height"
↓
Modern English (from c. 1500)
soar
"to rise or fly high; to increase steeply"
↓
Past-tense inflection (regular -ed)
soared

Note on nuance: the Vulgar Latin exaurāre is a reconstruction (it is inferred from the Old French outcome, not directly attested in texts); the directly attested links are Greek aúra, Latin aura, Old French essorer, and the Middle English verb soren. English borrowed the verb from Old French; the modern noun soar is a later back-formation from the verb.
