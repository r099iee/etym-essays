• reparations: Similar because they mean payments for wrongs; differs because reparations stress moral repair after war or injustice, indemnities stress legal cover for loss.
  • compensations: Similar because they mean making up for loss; differs because compensation is general everyday term, indemnities formal legal-diplomatic.
  • restitutions: Similar because they mean restoring what was taken; differs because restitution implies returning specific thing, indemnity paying equivalent value.
  • damages: Similar because they mean money for injury; differs because damages are court-awarded for tort, indemnities often contractual or treaty-based.
  • reimbursements: Similar because they mean repaying expense; differs because reimbursement repays exact outlay, indemnity covers broader loss including future risk.
  • recompenses: Similar because they mean reward for loss or service; differs because recompense is literary and can be non-monetary, indemnity almost always monetary-legal.
  • remunerations: Similar because they involve payment; differs because remuneration is pay for work, indemnity pay for loss.
  • tributes: Similar because they are payments to a stronger power; differs because tribute is ancient submission, indemnity modern legalized exaction.
  • guarantees: Similar because indemnity can mean protection; differs because guarantee promises performance, indemnity promises cover for loss.
  • assurances: Similar because they promise security; differs because assurance is promise of confidence, indemnity legally enforceable cover.
  • settlements: Similar because they resolve claims by payment; differs because settlement ends dispute by agreement, indemnity may be imposed.
  • redress: Similar because it means remedy for wrong; differs because redress is broad corrective action, indemnity specific monetary form.
