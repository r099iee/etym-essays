• Trawl (Trawl Net): Similar because it is a large fishing net dragged along the seabed; differs because trawl is the modern commercial maritime term for motorized vessel nets, whereas drag-net historically includes smaller river seines pulled by hand from riverbanks.
  • Seine (Seine Net): Similar because it hangs vertically in water with floats and weights; differs because a true seine primarily encircles fish near the surface or mid-water rather than continuously scraping and dragging along the bottom substrate.
  • Cordon: Similar because it traps targets within a designated area; differs because a cordon is a defensive or preventive perimeter of officers stationed at fixed points, whereas a drag-net actively sweeps through the interior of the zone.
  • Manhunt: Similar because it is an organized search for a fleeing fugitive; differs because a manhunt emphasizes the relentless pursuit and tracking of a specific human target, while a drag-net emphasizes the sweeping mechanism across an area.
  • Sweep: Similar because it involves clearing or combing an entire sector; differs because sweep is a broad military or investigative verb/noun that can describe rapid passes without the metaphorical meshed net.
  • Round-up: Similar because it gathers scattered individuals into custody; differs because round-up draws on cattle-herding imagery and usually applies to arresting known lists of suspects simultaneously rather than searching for hidden ones.
  • Comb-out: Similar because it involves an exhaustive search through an area or group; differs because comb-out implies fine-toothed elimination of redundancies or specific individuals from an existing organization.
  • Canvass: Similar because it involves systematically working through an entire neighborhood; differs because canvassing relies on door-to-door questioning and interviews rather than physical interception.
  • Dredge: Similar because it scrapes riverbeds and sea bottoms; differs because a dredge is a rigid iron frame or mechanical scoop designed primarily to harvest oysters, scallops, or deepen waterways rather than catch moving fish.
  • Encirclement: Similar because it prevents the escape of targets; differs because encirclement is a formal military encirclement of an armed force rather than a police or search operation.
  • Net (Fisher's Net): Similar because it shares the physical mesh fabric; differs because net is the universal parent category covering cast-nets, gill-nets, landing nets, and spider webs without the towing motion.
  • Surveillance Net: Similar because it captures data across a broad population; differs because surveillance net focuses purely on electronic monitoring and eavesdropping rather than physical capture.
