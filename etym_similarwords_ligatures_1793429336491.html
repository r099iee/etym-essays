• Bonds: Similar because both refer to things that tie, bind, or confine; differs because bonds is a broad, often figurative or emotive term (bonds of friendship, chains of bondage), whereas ligatures is technical, physical, and surgical.
  • Ties: Similar because both denote cords or connections that hold things together; differs because ties is simple everyday Germanic vocabulary, whereas ligatures is formal, Latinate, and specifically associated with anatomy, surgery, or typography.
  • Cords: Similar because both describe slender, flexible strands used for tying; differs because cords refers strictly to the raw physical rope-like material, while ligatures refers to the functional instrument of binding or its specialized medical/typographic role.
  • Sutures: Similar because both are surgical threads used on the human body; differs because a suture is a stitch used to sew tissues or wound edges together, whereas a ligature is specifically a thread tied around a vessel or pedicle to strangulate and shut off flow.
  • Restraints: Similar because both restrict physical movement; differs because restraints emphasizes the legal, psychiatric, or physical act of preventing motion (like handcuffs or straitjackets), whereas ligatures specifies the slender cords or binding threads themselves.
  • Fetters: Similar because both hold a person captive; differs because fetters are heavy metallic shackles or chains placed specifically upon the feet or ankles, whereas ligatures are delicate threads or cords binding any part of the body.
  • Ligaments: Similar because both share the Latin root ligare (‘to bind’); differs because a ligament is an anatomical band of tough, fibrous connective tissue joining bone to bone within a biological joint, whereas a ligature is an external cord or surgical thread.
  • Bandages: Similar because both wrap around the body; differs because bandages are broad strips of fabric used to dress wounds, absorb fluids, or support limbs, while ligatures are narrow, tight cords applied to constrict or tie down.
