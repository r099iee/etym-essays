- Roman & Anglo-Saxon Britain (1st–10th c.) — Originated as the Brittonic Celtic river name *Ligore (River Soar) compounded with Old English ceaster (Roman fortress) to form Ligoraceaster, recorded in 917 CE in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle.
- Middle Ages (11th–15th c.) — Recorded in the Domesday Book (1086) as Ledecestre and in Middle English charters as Leycestre; medial unstressed syllable syncopation compressed the spoken pronunciation from three syllables to two (/ˈlɛstər/).
- Early Modern era (16th–18th c.) — Phonetic spelling Lester emerged widely in parish registers, tax rolls, and colonial emigrant manifests as an English habitational surname for families originating from Leicestershire.
- 19th c. — Rose to widespread popularity in the United States and Britain as an independent masculine given name; achieved intellectual renown through Lester Frank Ward (1841–1913), the pioneer of American sociology.
- 20th c. — Cemented in global popular culture through jazz innovator Lester Young ("Prez") and prominent literary and dramatic figures (e.g., in American drama and television).
- Contemporary era — Recognized worldwide as an established Anglo-American surname, a classic given name, and a widespread toponym across North America.
