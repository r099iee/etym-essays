- 17th century - "pony" entered English from Lowland Scots "powney" (1650s), adopted from a French diminutive for a little foal; in Scotland it named the hardy, sure-footed hill ponies bred for work on poor pasture.
- 18th century - the word was firmly English, defined by height: a small horse of a small breed, the sturdy pack-animal of moors and mines rather than a charger of romance.
- 19th century to modern - the Pony Express gave "pony" a romantic American lease of life, and today it still means the small, serviceable, endearingly workaday horse — the mount of children and of practical, unheroic travel.
