• Strainer: Similar because it is a mesh utensil that separates solids from liquids; differs because a strainer is chiefly the coarse kitchen tool that holds back solids while liquid passes, while a sieve also sifts dry powders and riddles soil.
  • Colander: Similar because it is a perforated vessel for draining; differs because a colander is a large bowl-shaped utensil with holes for rinsing and draining, without a fine mesh or dry-sifting use.
  • Sifter: Similar because it separates fine from coarse; differs because a sifter is specialised for dry powders such as flour and sugar, often with a crank, while a sieve handles both wet straining and dry sifting.
  • Riddle: Similar because it is a coarse sieve used in farm and garden; differs because riddle is the rural dialectal word for a coarse screen for soil or grain, and its "puzzling question" sense is a separate homonym.
  • Screen: Similar because it separates by particle size; differs because a screen is a larger industrial or window mesh for grading aggregates or filtering air, not a handheld kitchen utensil.
  • Filter: Similar because it separates components of a mixture; differs because a filter removes matter from a fluid through a porous medium, while a sieve separates purely by particle size through holes.
  • Mesh: Similar because it is the open netting of which a sieve is made; differs because mesh is the material itself, not the framed utensil that is wielded.
  • Tamis: Similar because it is a fine cooking strainer; differs because a tamis is the specialised drum-shaped cloth or hair sieve of the professional kitchen, a sub-type of sieve rather than the general word.
