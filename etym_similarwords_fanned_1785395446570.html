• Blown: Similar because air is set in motion over something by an external force; differs because blown describes mere air movement with no shape, instrument, or purpose, whereas fanned implies a deliberate, rhythmic, fan-shaped sweep.
  • Spread: Similar because both name the opening out of something wide; differs because spread is static, general, and toneless, while fanned insists on the semicircular, radiating silhouette of an opened fan.
  • Unfurled: Similar because both name opening out into a wider form; differs because unfurled implies something rolled up being unrolled and suits sails, flags, and banners, whereas fanned opens from a fixed point into a half-circle and works on feathers and fire alike.
  • Splayed: Similar because both describe something forced open or spread apart; differs because splayed is an awkward, uncontrolled spreading of limbs or digits, while fanned is deliberate, decorative, and instrumental.
  • Winnowed: Similar because both can name tossing so the wind separates material; differs because winnowed is the literal agricultural action of separating chaff from grain, while fanned extends that action to fire, cooling, display, and abstraction.
  • Stirred: Similar because both name the rousing of fire or feeling; differs because stirred lacks the fan's shape and the directed blast of air, reading as vaguer and more generic.
  • Wafted: Similar because both involve air acting on a light object; differs because wafted is passive and directionless (carried gently on a current), while fanned is active, applied, and purposeful.
  • Ventilated: Similar because both move air through or over a space; differs because ventilated is technical and systemic (buildings, lungs, machinery), while fanned is personal, manual, and often visual.
