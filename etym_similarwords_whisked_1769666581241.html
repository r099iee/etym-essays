• Swept: Similar because both mean to move or remove with a quick, broad, brushing motion (swept the crumbs away / whisked the crumbs away); differs because swept is slower and more continuous (a broom's stroke), while whisked is quicker, lighter, and more flick-like.
  • Whipped: Similar because in cooking both mean beaten rapidly to incorporate air, and both can mean moved with sudden force; differs because whipped implies force and can mean "struck with a whip," while whisked is lighter and never violent.
  • Flicked: Similar because both mean a quick, light, jerky movement (flicked the crumb away); differs because a flick is a single sharp snap of wrist/finger, while whisking is a broader sweeping motion, and flicked has no cooking sense.
  • Beat: Similar because in cookery both mean mix vigorously (beat eggs / whisk eggs); differs because beat is a general, forceful mixing term with no speed/removal sense, while whisked implies the rapid circular tool-motion and also the "moved away quickly" sense.
  • Snatched: Similar because both can mean taken away suddenly; differs because snatched implies grabbing with the hand and often stealth or force, while whisked implies smooth, light, efficient speed.
  • Spirited away / swept away: Similar because all describe rapid, effortless removal; differs because "spirited away" is a fixed magical idiom, while whisked is an ordinary verb usable of any quick removal.
  • Darted: Similar because both describe sudden rapid movement; differs because darted is intransitive and describes the mover itself (the fish darted), while whisked usually describes moving or being moved (often transitive: whisked something/someone).
  • Flitted: Similar because both mean light, quick movement; differs because flitted suggests brief, restless hovering (a bird flitting), while whisked suggests a single fast sweeping motion or removal.
