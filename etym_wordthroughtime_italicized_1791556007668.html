- 16th c. — Italian origins and Renaissance introduction: Emerged from the Italian printing revolution initiated by Aldus Manutius in Venice (1501), originally called cancelleresca or the 'Aldine letter', soon referred to in English simply as Italick letter or type, referring directly to its Italian provenance.
- 17th–18th c. — Auxiliary typographic integration and verb formation: Shifted from an independent text font (used for whole books) to an auxiliary companion font alongside Roman type for citations, foreign phrases, and verse; the verb italicize was coined in the mid-18th century to describe the act of setting words in italic type.
- 19th c. — Victorian stylistic expansion and critical pushback: Became the preeminent device in Victorian fiction and essays for rhetorical stress and emotional vocalization, prompting sharp critiques from stylistic purists who cautioned against excessive, melodramatic italicization.
- 20th c. — Institutional codification: Institutionalized by academic style bodies (MLA, Chicago, APA) and Linnaean biological nomenclature as an absolute orthographic requirement for titles of works, foreign loanwords, and taxonomic binomina.
- 21st c. — Digital ubiquity and cognitive metaphor: Universalized across computing via HTML, Markdown, and text editors; expanded colloquially to metaphorically describe any experience, remark, or moment that is vivid, stressed, or underscored by historical circumstance.
