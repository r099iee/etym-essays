• Unworked: Similar because both mean “not worked, not fashioned” (un- + worked). Differs because “unworked” is the everyday, fully modern synonym; “unwrought” is the older, more formal and technical form.
  • Raw: Similar because both denote material in its natural, unprocessed state. Differs because “raw” is broad (raw flesh, raw data, raw talent) and informal, while “unwrought” is restricted to craftable materials and carries a formal register.
  • Crude: Similar because both describe matter not yet refined or finished. Differs because “crude” adds the idea of coarseness and imperfection; “unwrought” is neutral, implying only that manufacture has not occurred.
  • Unfinished: Similar because both mean “not brought to completion.” Differs because “unfinished” implies a process begun but stopped short; “unwrought” implies material never taken in hand at all.
  • Unprocessed: Similar because both label a material untouched by manufacture. Differs because “unprocessed” is the modern industrial term (food, data, minerals); “unwrought” belongs to traditional crafts, mining and precious metals.
  • Unrefined: Similar because both mean “not purified or worked up.” Differs because “unrefined” stresses impurity and lack of purification; “unwrought” stresses lack of shaping and manufacture.
  • Unfashioned: Similar because both mean “not shaped by skill” (fashion = make). Differs because “unfashioned” is rarer and more literary, while “unwrought” is the standard technical and trade-register term.
  • Undressed: Similar because both describe material not prepared for use (undressed stone, undressed hides). Differs because “undressed” applies chiefly to stone, timber, hides and cloth with a more practical ring.
  • In the rough / rough: Similar because both denote the state before working (a diamond in the rough). Differs because the phrase is idiomatic and often metaphorical, while “unwrought” names the state precisely in metal and trade usage.
