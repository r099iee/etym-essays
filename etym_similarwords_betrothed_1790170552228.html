• Fiancé: Similar because both name a man engaged to marry; differs because fiancé is French, everyday and gendered male, while betrothed is native, formal, and gender-neutral.
  • Fiancée: Similar because both name a woman engaged to marry; differs because fiancée marks female gender with -ée, while betrothed leaves gender to context.
  • Intended: Similar because both mean the person one intends to marry; differs because intended is euphemistic and coy, while betrothed is solemn and contractual.
  • Promised: Similar as past-participle for pledged in marriage (biblical "promised wife"); differs because promised is broader and needs a complement, while betrothed is self-sufficient.
  • Engaged: Similar because both describe pre-marital commitment; differs because engaged is the normal modern term, while betrothed is loftier and more archaic.
  • Espoused: Similar because both involve a marriage pledge; differs because espoused often means already married or wedded to a cause, while betrothed is strictly pre-wedding.
  • Affianced: Similar because both mean pledged in marriage; differs because affianced is even rarer and more literary, from French fiance, while betrothed is the core Germanic term.
  • Plighted: Similar in the phrase "plighted lover" for one who has plighted troth; differs because plighted is poetic and fragmentary, while betrothed is the full status noun.
  • Future spouse: Similar as a plain descriptive equivalent; differs because it is bureaucratic and neutral, while betrothed carries romantic and religious colour.
  • Lover: Similar because betrothed persons are lovers; differs because lover implies romance or sexuality without commitment, while betrothed implies commitment with or without passion.
