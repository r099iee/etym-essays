• Svarga: Similar because both refer to the heavenly celestial realm governed by Indra; differs because Svarga is the overarching metaphysical realm or dimension (the upper heavenly world), whereas Amarāvatī is the specific fortified capital city and urban jewel located within that realm.
  • Devaloka: Similar because both denote the divine dwelling place of the Hindu gods; differs because Devaloka is a broad theological classification encompassing any realm inhabited by deities, whereas Amarāvatī is a specific named city characterized by legendary royal architecture and sovereign court life.
  • Olympus: Similar because both represent the glorious summit or seat of power of a king of the gods (Indra / Zeus) accompanied by a celestial pantheon; differs because Olympus is conceived in Greek mythology as a real physical mountain whose peak touches the heavens, whereas Amarāvatī is a luminous, fully built mythological metropolis.
  • Valhalla: Similar because both are heavenly abodes where fallen heroes and deities dwell in celebration; differs because Valhalla in Norse myth is specifically a martial feasting hall presided over by Odin where warriors prepare for Ragnarok, whereas Amarāvatī is an opulent city of aesthetic delights, music, and divine governance.
  • Vaikuṇṭha: Similar because both are sacred spiritual abodes in Hindu cosmography; differs because Amarāvatī is a temporal heaven of sensory enjoyment governed by Indra subject to cyclic karmic expiration, whereas Vaikuṇṭha is the eternal, transcendent, non-material supreme abode of Lord Viṣṇu from which liberated souls never fall.
  • Goloka: Similar because both are divine realms of eternal bliss; differs because Goloka is the highest devotional realm dedicated exclusively to Kṛṣṇa and Rādhā in Gaudiya Vaishnavism, whereas Amarāvatī is the Vedic/Purāṇic administrative capital of cosmic gods.
  • Śambbala: Similar because both are legendary sacred cities associated with esoteric Indian spirituality; differs because Śambbala (Shambhala) is a hidden earthly kingdom where pure wisdom is preserved for the future Kalki / Kālacakra victory, whereas Amarāvatī is Indra's celestial capital in the sky.
  • Alakā: Similar because both are wondrous, gem-encrusted celestial cities in Hindu mythology; differs because Alakā is the northern city of wealth ruled by Kubera, king of the Yakṣas, on Mount Kailāśa, whereas Amarāvatī is the royal seat of Indra and the devas.
