- 17th c. 'Vexation and anxiety' — borrowed from French as a fashionable term for melancholy, ill humour, or gnawing distress, partly blended with the other French chagrin (a rough leather that chafes), giving the word its prickly, irritating flavour.
- 18th–19th c. 'Distress at disappointment or failure' — settles into the sense of acute vexation caused by frustration or humiliation, a polite, literary word for the smart of wounded pride.
- 20th–21st c. 'Embarrassment at one's own failure' — the dominant modern sense is mortified embarrassment after a public or private misstep; still formal and slightly bookish, it now centres on chafing self-consciousness rather than general melancholy.
