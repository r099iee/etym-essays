PIE *key- (also *kie-) "to set in motion, move to and fro" (reconstructed; debated — see note) → Latin cieō, ciēre "to set in motion, rouse, summon" (attested from the 2nd century BCE) → Latin citāre (frequentative of ciēre) "to put into quick motion, summon, urge" (Classical Latin, 1st century BCE) → Latin incitāre (in- "into" + citāre) "to spur on, urge forward, incite" (Classical Latin) → Old French inciter "to spur on, egg on, provoke" (12th century) → Middle English inciten "to move, stir up" (c. 1440) → Modern English incite (verb) + -ment (suffix of action/result) → incitement (noun; act-sense 1590s, thing-sense c. 1600) Note on debate: the PIE reconstruction *key- rests chiefly on Latin cieō and Greek κινέω (kineō) "to move"; some scholars doubt the Greek connection, and the root has no secure Indo-Iranian or Germanic reflexes. The noun incitement itself is an English formation on the verb incite, not a direct Latin loan. Zero guesswork: the intermediate forms cited are the attested record; nothing is invented beyond the reconstructed PIE node, which is flagged as such.
