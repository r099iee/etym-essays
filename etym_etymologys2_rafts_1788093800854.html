Note: No generally accepted PIE root for Germanic *raftaz has been established — older proposals connecting it with a PIE base meaning “to break off” are speculative — so the chain begins at Proto-Germanic, per the zero-guesswork rule. The native English cognate is Old English ræfter “rafter” (roof beam), which shows the root was also at home in Anglo-Saxon; the Middle English raft is commonly attributed to Old Norse raptr, borrowed in the Danelaw. The colloquial sense “a great number” (1837, American English) is of debated origin: some connect it with this word (a raft as a heap of logs), others with raff “heap, large amount” (as in riffraff); the connection to the platform sense, though plausible, is not proven.
