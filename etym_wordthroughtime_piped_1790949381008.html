- Antiquity to Old English - Late Latin pīpāre (echoic for chirping/peeping) enters Old English as pīpian (to play a musical pipe or tube).
- Middle English - Widely attested in literature (Chaucer's Canterbury Tales) describing pipers playing reed pipes and bagpipes at festivals and pilgrimages.
- 16th to 17th Century - English naval traditions establish piping the side or piping down using the boatswain's call.
- 19th Century - Industrial Revolution adopts piped to describe municipal gas, clean water, and steam infrastructure routed through iron pipelines.
- Mid-20th to 21st Century - Computer scientists adopt piping in Unix operating systems, cementing its role in modern digital data streams alongside culinary and musical senses.
