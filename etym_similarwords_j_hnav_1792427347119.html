• Gaṅgā: Similar because both refer to the exact same sacred river of northern India; differs because Gaṅgā is the primary, universal name denoting her primordial descent from heaven through Śiva’s locks, whereas Jāhnavī is a specific patronymic epithet highlighting her earthly rebirth as the adopted daughter of Sage Jahnu.
  • Bhāgīrathī: Similar because both are patronymic titles commemorating mortals whose spiritual actions shaped the river's earthly course; differs because Bhāgīrathī honors King Bhagīratha whose intense austerities brought the river down from heaven (and designates the upper Himalayan headstream above Devprayag), whereas Jāhnavī honors Sage Jahnu who swallowed and liberated her upon the plains.
  • Mandākinī: Similar because both are divine names belonging to the sacred Ganges network; differs because Mandākinī refers specifically to the celestial river flowing through the heavenly realm of Svarga (and geophysically to the glacial tributary flowing past Kedarnath into the Alakanandā), whereas Jāhnavī designates the fully manifest river of the mortal plains.
  • Tripathagā: Similar because both are elevated poetic names for the Ganges in classical Sanskrit; differs because Tripathagā ("she who travels the three paths") is a cosmological epithet celebrating her simultaneous presence in heaven, earth, and the netherworld, whereas Jāhnavī is an intimate mythological patronymic grounded in human ascetic history.
  • Alakanandā: Similar because both denote sacred waters of the holy Ganges system; differs because Alakanandā is a specific major Himalayan headstream originating at the Satopanth Glacier that merges with the Bhāgīrathī at Devprayag to form the Ganges proper, whereas Jāhnavī encompasses the entire consecrated person of the holy river.
  • Surasarit: Similar because both designate the Ganges as a divine waterway; differs because Surasarit ("river of the gods") emphasizes her supernatural heavenly origin among the immortals, whereas Jāhnavī emphasizes her earthly adoption and filial relationship to a terrestrial sage.
