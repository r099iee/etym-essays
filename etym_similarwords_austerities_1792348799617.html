• penances: Similar because both involve self-inflicted hardships or disciplined suffering; differs because penances are specifically performed as acts of atonement, remorse, or sacramental restitution for committed sins or moral transgressions, whereas austerities are often proactive spiritual disciplines aimed at cosmic power, self-mastery, or divine boons without necessarily presupposing prior guilt.
  • mortifications: Similar because both describe deliberate physical suffering to suppress bodily desires; differs because mortifications emphasizes the literal or metaphorical "deadening" (Latin mortificare) of the flesh, pride, and carnal appetites, often involving visceral bodily discomfort (e.g., hairshirts, fasting).
  • asceticism: Similar because both denote the lifestyle of strict self-abnegation and abstention from pleasure; differs because asceticism is the overarching philosophical, ethical, or religious system, whereas austerities refers to the concrete, empirical practices, exercises, and physical rigors themselves.
  • privations: Similar because both signify the lack of comforts and severe physical hardship; differs because privations are typically involuntary conditions imposed by poverty, warfare, famine, or disaster, whereas austerities in an ascetic context are chosen voluntarily for a higher spiritual or philosophical purpose.
  • hardships: Similar because both entail severe physical difficulty and struggle; differs because hardships are generic life trials or adversity without any inherent spiritual, philosophical, or disciplinary framework.
  • severities: Similar because both denote extreme strictness and unyielding harshness; differs because severities often describe cruel external treatment, harsh weather, or rigid punishment imposed by an outside authority on others, whereas religious austerities are self-imposed disciplines.
  • abnegations: Similar because both describe the voluntary surrender of pleasures and personal desires; differs because abnegation emphasizes the psychological act of renouncing rights, ego, or self-interest (self-abnegation), rather than bodily physical rigors.
  • rigors: Similar because both denote strict, demanding, and uncompromising conditions; differs because rigors refers to the challenging demands of a harsh climate, intensive training regimen, or intellectual pursuit rather than sacred spiritual mortification.
  • chastisements: Similar because both involve disciplinary correction through pain; differs because chastisement implies punitive discipline inflicted by a parent, judge, or deity to correct wrongdoing, rather than personal ascetic exercises.
  • fasts: Similar because fasting is one of the primary forms of ascetic self-denial; differs because fasts refers specifically to the abstention from food and drink, representing only one component of a broader program of physical and spiritual austerities.
