• Mime: Similar because both involve conveying meaning and emotion through silent physical gesture rather than vocal speech; differs because mime is often a minimalist, stylized, and abstract performance art focused on illusions of physical resistance, whereas pantomime can refer specifically to the noisy, musical, comedic British theatrical extravaganza or everyday expressive gesturing.
  • Dumb-show: Similar because both denote a dramatic performance conducted entirely without spoken dialogue; differs because dumb-show is an archaic, formal theatrical device—such as the explanatory silent vignettes staged within Elizabethan plays like Hamlet—whereas pantomime encompasses broad contemporary entertainment, spontaneous body language, and cultural metaphor.
  • Charade: Similar because both involve using silent physical gestures to convey an unspoken message or identity; differs because charade is primarily a parlor guessing game or, metaphorically, a deceitful pretense or fraudulent facade intended to mislead, whereas pantomime is theatrical or genuinely communicative.
  • Farce: Similar because both denote theatrical performances characterized by ridiculous situations, energetic slapstick, and broad caricature; differs because farce typically relies heavily on rapid-fire spoken dialogue, mistaken identities, and slamming doors, while pantomime centers on visual dumb-show or British holiday conventions.
  • Tableau vivant: Similar because both are visual, non-verbal theatrical displays using the human body; differs because a tableau vivant consists of performers holding completely motionless, frozen poses to replicate famous paintings or historical scenes, whereas pantomime is characterized by continuous kinetic movement and dramatic action.
  • Gesticulation: Similar because both involve dramatic and noticeable bodily and hand movements; differs because gesticulation refers to isolated, often involuntary or agitated hand and arm motions accompanying speech, whereas pantomime is a deliberate, structured system of silent representation.
  • Burlesque: Similar because both feature broad comedy, parody, and theatrical exaggeration; differs because burlesque historically relies on provocative satire, grotesque imitation of serious literary works, and bawdy musical revue, whereas pantomime maintains a family-oriented holiday structure or purely silent physical storytelling.
