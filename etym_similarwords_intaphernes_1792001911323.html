• Otanes: Similar because both were members of the elite Seven Persian conspirators who assassinated Gaumāta in 522 BCE; differs because Otanes famously advocated for democratic/republican government during the constitutional debate and withdrew from royal competition, whereas Intaphernes sought to maintain aggressive aristocratic dominance and was executed for treason.
  • Gobryas: Similar because both were noble conspirators who physically grappled with the Magian usurper in the palace bedchamber; differs because Gobryas became Darius's lifelong father-in-law and trusted marshal, whereas Intaphernes fell victim to royal execution soon after Darius's coronation.
  • Megabyzus: Similar because both were founding Achaemenid generals among the Seven; differs because Megabyzus lived to conquer Thrace and establish a durable dynastic lineage, whereas Intaphernes' entire line was nearly extinguished by his fatal hubris.
  • Antigone: Similar because both figures are inextricably linked in classical thought through the identical moral defense of prioritizing a brother over husband or child; differs because Antigone is a mythological Greek heroine acting out of religious devotion to burial rites, whereas Intaphernes is the historical Persian husband whose fate triggered his wife's ethical choice.
  • Satrap: Similar because both denote high-ranking Persian nobility wielding imperial authority; differs because a satrap was a provincial administrative governor ruling a designated territory, whereas Intaphernes was a member of the supreme Seven grandees residing at the imperial court with direct personal privileges.
  • Regicide: Similar because both relate to the violent killing of a reigning monarch; differs because regicide is an abstract noun for the act of killing a king, while Intaphernes was the individual aristocratic assassin who helped slay the usurper-monarch.
