NOTE ON ORIGIN: the verb moor has NO securely reconstructible PIE ancestry; its documented life is West Germanic, born in the maritime vocabulary of the North Sea. The chain below records the attested stages and flags the speculative base explicitly rather than asserting it.\n\n(SPECULATIVE ULTIMATE BASE — flagged, not asserted) PIE *mer- — "to bind, tie, fasten" (marked UNCERTAIN by OED and Etymonline; a common Germanic *mair-ja- "to make fast" is sometimes derived from it, but the chain is not solid).\n\nSTAGE 2 — Proto-Germanic *mairjaną / *mōrijaną (reconstructed) — "to fasten, tie, moor (a vessel)"; the shape assumed behind the attested Low German and Dutch verbs.\n\nSTAGE 3 — Middle Low German mōren (14th-15th c., nautical usage) — "to moor (a ship)"; with the contemporary North Sea cognate Middle Dutch māren, "to fasten, tie" (later Dutch meren, with meertouw "mooring rope" and meerpaal "mooring post"). German itself uses ankeren "to anchor" rather than this verb.\n\nSTAGE 4 — Middle English moren / mouren (c. 1500, nautical) — "to moor"; a maritime borrowing, probably travelling with the Hanseatic and North Sea trade into English ports.\n\nSTAGE 5 — English moor (v.) (16th c.) — "to secure a vessel by cables, chains, or ropes to a fixed point"; also, from the 16th c. onward, the figurative sense "to fix, fasten, anchor (a thing, a person, an idea)."\n\nSTAGE 6 — English MOORED — the regular weak past-tense / past-participle form (moor + -ed), the inflection on this card.\n\nHOMONYMS KEPT STRICTLY SEPARATE: moor (n.), "a tract of open, waterlogged wasteland," is from Old English mōr (cf. Dutch moer, marsh); Moor (n.), "a member of the Muslim peoples of North Africa," is from Latin Maurus; neither shares a root with the nautical verb.\n\nNOTE ON THE DEBATE: because the earliest attestations are English (c. 1500) and the continental forms are known only from a later period of Hanseatic records, the direction of borrowing is inferred rather than directly attested; the ultimate link to PIE *mer- is plausible but unproven. Both uncertainties are stated here rather than papered over.
