*kop-
"to strike, beat"
↓
Greek (Classical)
κόπτειν
kóptein
"to cut, strike, knock"
↓
Greek (c. 4th c. BCE)
προκόπτειν
prokóptein
"to advance, make progress"
↓
Greek (c. 6th c. CE)
Προκόπιος
Prokópios
"personal name, 'the advancing one'"
↓
Latin (6th c. CE)
Procopius
"Latinized form of the Greek name"
↓
English (16th c.)
PROCOPIUS
"late antique Byzantine historian"

Note: PIE *kop- for κόπτειν is uncertain;
Beekes allows a Pre-Greek origin. The
derivation of the name itself is secure.
