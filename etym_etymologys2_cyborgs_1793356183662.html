Root 1: cyber- (from cybernetics / Greek kybernētēs)
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500 BCE)
*kwebh- / *kub- (?)
"to turn, bend, steer; obscure substrate root"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE)
κυβερνάω (kybernáō)
"to steer, pilot a ship, guide, direct"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 5th c. BCE)
κυβερνήτης (kybernḗtēs)
"steersman, helmsman, sailing pilot, guide"
↓
French (1834)
cybernétique
"the political science of civil government (Ampère)"
↓
Modern English (1948)
cybernetics
"study of control and communication in animals and machines (Wiener)"

Root 2: -org (from organism / Greek organon)
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500 BCE)
*werǵ-
"to work, do, act, bring about"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE)
ἔργον (érgon)
"work, deed, action, physical labor"
↓

ὄργανον (órganon)

Synthesis & Modern Plural:
American English (September 1960)
cyborg
"cybernetic organism; homeostatically augmented being (Clynes & Kline)"
↓
Modern English (current)
cyborgs
"entities with technologically augmented biological/cognitive systems"

Note on etymology and multiple roots: cyborg is a modern twentieth-century portmanteau blending two distinct ancient linguistic heritages: the nautical steering vocabulary of Ancient Greece (kybernáō, kybernḗtēs), which gave rise to Norbert Wiener's 1948 mathematical discipline of cybernetics, and the Indo-European work root *werǵ-, which evolved through Greek órganon into biological organism. The final plural suffix -s descends directly from Proto-Germanic *-ōs / Old English -as.
