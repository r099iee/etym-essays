- 17th Century (1680s): Fencing mechanics and swordsmanship terminology - Understood as the rigid, unyielding lower half of a rapier blade where parrying leverage is greatest, contrasted with the foible.
- 18th Century: Chivalric metaphor applied to human character - Transitioned through courtly dialogue and moral essays into a celebrated descriptor of an individual's personal strength, genius, or primary virtue.
- 19th Century: Victorian literary ubiquity and musical convergence - Frequently paired with "foible" in Victorian novels, while simultaneously blending phonetically in the public ear with Italian musical "forte" (loudly).
- 20th to 21st Century: Everyday idiom and modern pronunciation debate - Thoroughly entrenched in colloquial and professional English for core competence, accompanied by widespread acceptance of two-syllable pronunciation.
