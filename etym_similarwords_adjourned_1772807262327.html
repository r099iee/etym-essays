• Recess: Similar because both name a break in proceedings; differs because recess usually means a scheduled temporary pause within a continuing sitting, whereas adjourn formally suspends the sitting to another day or place.
  • Postpone: Similar because both put something off; differs because postpone delays a matter to a later date without the ritual suspension of a body in session, while adjourn is the procedural act of a formal body.
  • Suspend: Similar because both halt something temporarily; differs because suspend ranges into disciplinary or constitutional interruption and need not imply reconvening by design, while adjourn implies resumption.
  • Defer: Similar because both delay; differs because defer is lighter and more general (defer a decision, defer payment) and lacks the institutional calendar-setting of adjourn.
  • Prorogue: Similar because both end a parliamentary sitting; differs because prorogue is the technical term for ending a session of parliament until the next session, not a day-to-day or case-by-case break.
  • Retire: Similar because both describe withdrawal from a session; differs because retire focuses on persons withdrawing to deliberate (the jury retires), while adjourn puts the formal business itself to another day.
  • Table (AmE): Similar because both set a matter aside in a meeting; differs because tabling (in US parliamentary usage) sets a motion aside, often indefinitely, while adjourn ends the sitting itself with intent to resume.
  • Put off: Similar because both defer; differs because put off is informal and general, without the institutional formality of adjourn.
  • Break up: Similar because both end a gathering; differs because break up is informal and implies complete dispersal, while adjourn implies formal suspension with resumption intended.
