• Monarchist: Similar because both denote a supporter of monarchy as a form of government; differs because monarchist is the modern, neutral term of political science, without emotional loyalty to a particular dynasty, while royalist is the older, more romantic and partisan word for devotion to a king or royal house.
  • Loyalist: Similar because both denote fidelity to an established authority or cause; differs because loyalist can apply to any established authority or cause (as to the British Crown in the American Revolution) and does not require a monarch, while royalist is specifically devoted to the king or the royal cause.
  • Cavalier: Similar because both name a supporter of King Charles I in the English Civil War; differs because Cavalier is the specific, colourful historical name for a royalist partisan, with overtones of gallantry and swagger, while royalist is the general and wider term.
  • Tory: Similar because both can denote a supporter of the crown and of traditional authority; differs because Tory is a specific party label, originating as a nickname for the late-17th-century court party and evolving into a political party name, not a general synonym for royalist.
  • Legitimist: Similar because both denote supporters of a specific royal dynasty; differs because legitimist is the technical word for those who support a deposed dynasty as the rightful (legitimate) sovereign, especially the French Bourbons, while royalist is the broader term for any supporter of monarchy.
  • Jacobite: Similar because both denote a supporter of the exiled king; differs because Jacobite is the precise historical label for the supporters of the deposed Stuart dynasty after 1688, a special case of royalism, not its general name.
  • King's man: Similar because both denote a devoted supporter of the king; differs because "king's man" is a plain, informal historical phrase for a loyal servant or follower of the monarch, while royalist is the formal political label.
  • Partisan: Similar because both denote a committed supporter of a cause; differs because a partisan may support any cause or faction, while a royalist is specifically committed to the monarchy and the king.
  • Regal: Similar because both can suggest kingly dignity; differs because regal is a pure temperament adjective denoting stately, majestic bearing, with no political commitment at all, while royalist carries the energy of allegiance to a crown.
  • Aristocratic: Similar because both can denote lofty, privileged bearing; differs because aristocratic refers to the nobility and its manners, while royalist refers to support of the monarchy and can be used of a proud temperament without any noble title.
  • Royal: Similar because both derive from the same root and relate to the king; differs because royal is the neutral adjective "of the king," while royalist is the partisan noun and adjective for one who supports the crown.
  • Royalist sympathizer / royalism: Similar because both share the same political commitment; differs because a sympathizer is one who privately favours the royal cause without acting, while royalist proper names the committed supporter, and royalism is the doctrine itself.
