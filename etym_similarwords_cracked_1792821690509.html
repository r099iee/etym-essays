• Broken: Similar because both describe things damaged and no longer whole; differs because broken is the general term and often means fallen apart entirely, while cracked stresses a line of damage in something still holding together.
  • Fractured: Similar because both mean split along a line of weakness; differs because fractured is clinical and formal (fractured ribs, fractured parties), while cracked is homely and audible, with the snap still in it.
  • Split: Similar because both describe division along a seam or grain; differs because split often means divided fully in two, while cracked means marred by lines but undivided.
  • Chipped: Similar because both name small damage that leaves the object usable; differs because a chip is a missing flake at an edge, while a crack is a line running through the body.
  • Shattered: Similar because both follow a sharp blow; differs because shattered means burst into fragments beyond repair, while cracked stops one step short of catastrophe.
  • Eccentric: Similar because in the source sentence both describe Bilbo's oddness; differs because eccentric is polite and almost admiring (charmingly off-centre), while cracked is blunter village slang with a wink of madness.
  • Dotty: Similar because both are affectionate British words for mild madness; differs because dotty suggests vague forgetful old age, while cracked suggests a once-sound mind with a definite fracture in it.
  • Unhinged: Similar because both describe minds come loose; differs because unhinged is modern, harsh, and total (a door off its frame), while cracked is older, gentler, and partial.
  • Crackpot: Similar because both come from the cracked-vessel metaphor for folly; differs because crackpot is a noun naming the person with contempt, while cracked is an adjective that can still be fond.
