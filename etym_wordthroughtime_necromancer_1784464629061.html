“Necromancer” entered English from medieval magic-language for one who practises divination by the dead — a conjurer who claims to consult corpses and spirits for knowledge.
“Necromancer” through the 16th–18th centuries was the technical word for the blackest kind of sorcery — divination and command over the dead — spoken of with horror and Church condemnation.
“Necromancer” by the 19th century loosened into a popular word for any dark evil wizard — the horror of death-magic blurring into generic villainous sorcery in fiction.
“Necromancer” today, in fantasy, is a title of dread — a dark sorcerer bound up with death, shadows, and forbidden lore, a word that freezes a company at the very mention of his dungeons.
