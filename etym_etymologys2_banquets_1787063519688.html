Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE) *bankiz "bench; raised platform" (origin uncertain) ↓ Frankish (6th–9th c.) *bank "bench" ↓ Old Italian (13th c.) banco "bench, table; money dealer's counter" ↓ Italian (14th c.) banchetto "little bench; a light meal taken on a bench between meals" ↓ Middle French (15th c.) banquet "a feast, a grand dinner" ↓ English (15th c.) banket / banquet "a sumptuous feast" ↓ English (Modern) BANQUETS "large formal feasts, ceremonial dinners" Note on the semantic bridge: the leap from "little bench" (banchetto) to "feast" happened because diners in medieval Italy often ate light refreshments seated on benches between the main courses of a meal; the word then climbed from "snack on a bench" to "grand feast".
