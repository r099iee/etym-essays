Old English (9th c., attested brēr)
brēr, brær
‘thorny shrub, bramble’
↓
Middle English (13th–14th c.)
brere, brer
‘briar, thorn bush’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
brier, briar
‘prickly shrub; thorn’
↓
Modern English (current)
briar
‘thorny shrub; thicket; pipe wood’

Note: Old English brēr has no accepted PIE parent and no certain cognates outside English; handbooks treat it as a native word of unknown origin, possibly substrate. The pipe sense comes by convergence with French bruyère, from Latin brūcaria, heath, whose root is likewise obscure, so the chain honestly stops at Old English rather than inventing a root.
