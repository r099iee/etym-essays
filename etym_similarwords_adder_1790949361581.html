• Viper: Similar because both refer to venomous snakes of the family Viperidae; differs because viper is the broader, Latinate zoological and international term encompassing all pit vipers and true vipers, whereas adder is the native Germanic English name specifically identified with Vipera berus.
  • Serpent: Similar because both denote legless squamate reptiles; differs because serpent is a grand, archaic, mythological, or theological term carrying monumental biblical and cosmic weight, while adder is a specific physical creature.
  • Snake: Similar because an adder is a snake; differs because snake is the overarching generic common noun for all members of the suborder Serpentes, venomous and non-venomous alike.
  • Asp: Similar because both are small, historically famous venomous snakes; differs because asp refers historically to Egyptian cobras (Naja haje) or the European horned viper (Vipera aspis), famously associated with the suicide of Cleopatra.
  • Cobra: Similar because both are venomous serpents; differs because a cobra belongs to the family Elapidae, possesses a hood that expands during threat displays, and delivers deadly neurotoxic venom.
  • Copperhead: Similar because both are venomous vipers with camouflaged patterns; differs because the copperhead is an American pit viper (Agkistrodon contortrix) with distinctive hourglass markings and heat-sensing facial pits.
  • Rattlesnake: Similar because both are venomous vipers; differs because a rattlesnake is an American pit viper distinguished by a specialized keratin rattle at the end of its tail.
