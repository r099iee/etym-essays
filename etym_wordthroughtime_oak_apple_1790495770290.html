- Old English ac + æppel - oak-tree plus any round fruit; plain and rural
- 14th c. okappel - herbal name for round oak-galls; medicinal and confused with ink-galls
- 1651 Boscobel oak - Charles hides in an oak; tree becomes relic
- 1660-1859 Oak Apple Day - 29 May thanksgiving, oak worn, gall gilded; loyalist and compulsory
- Ruskin boyhood gilded oak-apple - pious Tory button-hole on the 29th; earnest and sacramental
- 1859 repeal to modern - official day abolished, few parishes keep it; nostalgic and local
- Modern ecological oak-apple - cynipid generations and parasitoid webs; scientific and dissected
