• Bulge: Similar because both come from the same swelling-curve root and can mean a barrel’s belly; differs because bulge now means any outward swelling (a bulging bag), while bilge is fixed nautically to the hull’s bottom.
  • Hull / hold: Similar because both are parts of a ship’s body; differs because hull is the whole shell and hold the cargo space, while bilge is specifically the lowest inner sump.
  • Keel: Similar because both sit at the bottom of the boat; differs because keel is the structural backbone timber or fin, while bilge is the hollow curve alongside it.
  • Bilge water / slop: Similar because both name foul stagnant liquid; differs because bilge water is specifically the hull sump’s mix of sea, oil, and dirt, while slop is general waste water.
  • Nonsense / rubbish: Similar in the slang sense (worthless talk); differs because nonsense is neutral and general, while bilge is coarse, naval, and contemptuous.
  • Balderdash / claptrap / rot: Similar because all mean pretentious or stupid talk; differs because balderdash is old-fashioned comic, claptrap theatrical, rot blunt British, while bilge keeps a salty sea flavour.
  • Drivel / twaddle: Similar because both mean silly chatter; differs because drivel stresses drooling foolishness, while bilge stresses foul uselessness.
  • Swill / sludge: Similar because both evoke disgusting liquid waste; differs because swill is pig feed or drink waste, sludge industrial mud, while bilge is ship waste — the metaphor behind the insult.
