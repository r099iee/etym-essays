- Roman Law (Classical Era) — Originated as Latin praescriptiō, a legal exception written at the head of a legal plea or establishing ownership through long, continuous possession.
- 18th c. — Formalized in English common law (Sir William Blackstone) as prescriptive right, legal title founded on custom beyond "time out of mind."
- Late 18th–19th c. — Applied metaphorically by schoolmasters and lexicographers (Lowth, Murray) to denote authoritarian rules governing grammatical correctness and pronunciation.
- Early 20th c. — Codified by Ferdinand de Saussure and Leonard Bloomfield in modern structural linguistics as the critical counter-concept to scientific descriptive linguistics.
- Late 20th–21st c. — Expanded into data science, artificial intelligence, and operational management to denote "prescriptive analytics" (algorithms that prescribe actionable decisions).
