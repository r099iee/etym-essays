• trudged: Similar because it means walking slowly and heavily with effort; differs because trudging suggests a longer, wearier slog over distance (and can mean walking wearily away), while plodding is steadier, duller and more rhythmic — step, thud, step, thud.
  • slogged: Similar because it means working or moving with slow, heavy effort; differs because slogging is more forceful and more exhausting — it can mean hitting hard as well as labouring — while plodding is monotonous, flat and unglamorous rather than strenuous.
  • toiled: Similar because it means working hard and doggedly; differs because toiling is labouring under strain or hardship (physical or mental), while plodding can describe merely dull, steady progress with no great exertion.
  • lumbered: Similar because it describes heavy, clumsy movement; differs because lumbering is the uncoordinated, rolling gait of a big body (like a bear), while plodding is deliberate and uniform, more tired than clumsy.
  • stomped: Similar because it is heavy, loud footfall; differs because stomping is forceful, angry or emphatic — you stomp to show displeasure — while plodding carries no anger, only weight and weariness.
  • tramped: Similar because it means walking heavily and steadily over ground; differs because tramping is more purposeful and more energetic — tramping boots on a long march — while plodding is slower, duller and lacking in purpose or energy.
  • clomped: Similar because it pictures heavy, noisy steps; differs because clomping emphasises the sound of heavy footwear striking the ground, while plodding emphasises the laborious, effortful quality of the walk itself.
  • waded: Similar because it means moving with effort through a resisting medium; differs because wading specifically means walking through water (or figuratively through a task) with the feet grounded, while plodding is about heavy, reluctant walking and dogged work in general.
