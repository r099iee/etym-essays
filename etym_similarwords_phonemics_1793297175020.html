• Phonetics: Similar because both study speech sounds and train the ear; differs because phonetics measures articulation and acoustics universally, while phonemics analyses which sound differences matter contrastively inside one language.
  • Phonology: Similar because both analyse patterned sound systems rather than raw noise; differs because phonology is the broad modern cover term including generative theory, while phonemics names the classical structuralist inventory-and-distribution phase.
  • Morphophonemics: Similar because both track sound alternations tied to meaning; differs because morphophonemics studies changes at morpheme boundaries like plural voicing, while phonemics maps the underlying phoneme stock itself.
  • Allophony: Similar because allophones are the working material of phonemic proof; differs because allophony names predictable variants of one unit, while phonemics is the whole method that sorts variants into phonemes.
  • Minimal-pair analysis: Similar because minimal pairs are the chief diagnostic of phoneme status; differs because minimal-pair analysis is one technique, while phonemics is the full framework of distribution, features, and system.
  • Distinctive-feature theory: Similar because both decompose sounds into contrastive properties; differs because distinctive-feature theory analyses binaries like voiced or nasal, while phonemics organises whole inventories and their patterning.
  • Structural linguistics: Similar because phonemics was its showpiece achievement after Saussure; differs because structural linguistics spans grammar, semantics, and poetics, while phonemics is the sound-module crown jewel.
  • Sociophonetics: Similar because both connect fine sound detail to language identity; differs because sociophonetics correlates pronunciation with class, region, and style, while phonemics maps the abstract contrast system beneath variation.
