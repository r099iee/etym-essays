Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed, prehistoric)
*stebh-
post, trunk; to support firmly
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, prehistoric)
*stumpaz
stump, blunt, truncated
↓
Middle Low German (13th-14th c.)
stump
truncated part, blunt end
↓
Middle English (early 14th c.)
stumpe
truncated portion of a tree
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
stump
short projecting base, blunt end
↓
English (present)
stumps
cut tree bases; wicket posts; remnants

Note: The immediate source of the
Middle English borrowing is debated:
Middle Low German stump, Middle
Dutch stomp, or Old Norse stumpr.
All point to the same Germanic base.
