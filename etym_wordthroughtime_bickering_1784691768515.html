- Middle English sense - to bicker is to skirmish, to exchange light, sharp blows; the word enters the language as a word of combat, though of a light, desultory, running kind
- 16th-17th century shift - bicker moves from blows to words: to quarrel about trifles in rapid, snappish exchanges; the physical and verbal senses coexist as the word's sharp, flickering quality transfers from blades to tongues
- 18th-19th century - the word settles on petty, repetitive contention, and a secondary imitative sense describes quick, light repeated sounds — water rippling over stones, rain pattering, a flame flickering
- Contemporary sense - bickering is the wearying, ongoing squabble about small things that never resolves but simply goes on; the skirmish sense survives only in archaic or literary use
