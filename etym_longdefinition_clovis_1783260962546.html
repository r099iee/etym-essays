Clovis (prop. n.) — the first King of the Franks to unite all Frankish tribes under one ruler (reigned 481–511 CE) and to convert to Nicene (Catholic) Christianity, thereby founding the Merovingian dynasty and, in the traditional narrative, the French nation itself. On this card the word appears in the sentence "All the chiefs who founded Teutonic dynasties in the continental provinces of the Roman empire, Alaric, Theodoric, Clovis, Alboin, were zealous Christians": the name stands in the roll-call of the Germanic founder-kings who, unlike the still-pagan rulers of Wessex, embraced Christianity.

Sense 1 — The historical king (the primary sense). Clovis I succeeded his father Childeric as king of the Salian Franks, conquered the Gallo-Roman ruler Syagrius at Soissons (486), united the Frankish tribes, and in about 496 was baptized at Reims by Bishop Remigius into Catholic Christianity — abandoning the Arianism that most Germanic peoples (Visigoths, Ostrogoths, Vandals) professed. This conversion aligned the Franks with the papacy and the Gallo-Roman episcopate and is regarded as one of the hinge-events of European history. His dynasty, the Merovingians, ruled the Franks until the 8th century, when the Carolingians succeeded them.

Sense 2 — The onomastic sense. In historical linguistics Clovis is the early Germanic form of the French royal name Louis and the German Ludwig: all three descend from the Frankish name *Chlodowig, "famous in battle." The phonetic chain *Chlodovech → Clovis → Louis shows the same Germanic name reshaped by Romance sound change as it travelled from the 6th century to the modern French monarchy (18 kings named Louis).

Sense 3 — The archaeological sense. In 20th-century archaeology "Clovis culture" names the Paleo-American stone-tool culture of North America (distinctive fluted projectile points, c. 11,500–10,900 BCE), named after the town of Clovis, New Mexico, where the first points were found in the 1930s. Here the medieval king's name migrates, via a New Mexico town, into prehistoric archaeology — a completely modern, non-historical sense.

  • Register: a historical proper name used in historiography, French national history, onomastics, and (as "Clovis culture") archaeology.
  • Collocations: King Clovis, the baptism of Clovis, Clovis I, the Merovingians, Clovis points / Clovis culture.
  • Historical bridge for the name's meaning: "famous in battle" (Germanic *Hlūdawigaz) became, through the baptism of 496, the emblem of the convert-king and founder of France; the same name supplied both the French royal Louis and, by the accident of a New Mexico placename, the name of an American archaeological culture.
  • Distinction from Charlemagne: Clovis founded the Merovingian kingdom (6th century); Charlemagne, three centuries later, founded the Carolingian empire and is the other monumental Frankish name in French history.
  • Religious-historical note: the phrase "zealous Christians" on this card is precise: unlike the Arian Germanic kingdoms (Visigothic Spain, Ostrogothic Italy, Vandal Africa), the Franks under Clovis accepted Nicene orthodoxy directly, which is why the baptism at Reims became the foundation-myth of Catholic France and of the French claim to be the Church's eldest daughter.
