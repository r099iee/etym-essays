pādas (Sanskrit: पाद, plural in Anglicized scholarly prose: pādas, nominative singular: pādaḥ) denotes the fundamental metrical quarter, verse-line, or individual rhythmic limb of a stanza in Sanskrit, Prakrit, and Pali prosody. Arising from the anatomical word for "foot" or "leg," the term relies on an ancient organic metaphor comparing a complete four-part poetic stanza to a living quadruped beast walking steadily on four balanced limbs. Beyond prosody, the term possesses profound polysemic depth across classical Indian philosophy, ritual theology, law, mathematics, and geography.

Primary Metrical Meaning in Sanskrit Prosody (Chandaḥśāstra):

  • Structural Quarter-Line: In classical and Vedic metrics, a standard stanza (such as the śloka, anuṣṭubh, triṣṭubh, or jagatī) is traditionally conceived as having four quarters (catur-aṅga). Each individual quarter-line is a pāda. In the 32-syllable śloka, each pāda consists of precisely eight syllables. Prosodic treatises analyze stanzas by evaluating the weight (light laghu or heavy guru) of each syllable within the individual pāda.
  • Caesura and Pausal Integrity: Each pāda traditionally constitutes a discrete syntactic and acoustic breath unit. In printed editions and manuscript transmission, a caesura (pause) occurs at the end of the second pāda (marked by a single danda: ।) and a final termination at the end of the fourth pāda (marked by a double danda: ॥).

Polysemic Scope, Secondary Meanings & Conceptual Bridges:

  • Mathematical Fraction ("One-Fourth / A Quarter"): Because a quadraped stands on four legs, pāda naturally developed the arithmetic meaning of "one-fourth" (25% or 1/4th of any whole unit). In classical Indian commerce, it denoted a quarter of a coin (such as a quarter-kārṣāpaṇa); in astronomy, a quarter-quadrant of an eclipse cycle or zodiacal constellation (nakṣatra-pāda).
  • Textual and Philosophical Chapters ("Major Quartos"): Major philosophical and grammatical treatises that were conceived in four comprehensive thematic divisions used pāda as the title for their primary chapters. Most famously, Patañjali's Yogasūtras are structured into four successive pādas: the Samādhi-pāda (on contemplative absorption), Sādhana-pāda (on spiritual discipline), Vibhūti-pāda (on supranormal faculties), and Kaivalya-pāda (on final liberation). Similarly, the fundamental chapters of the Brahmasūtras and the sub-sections of Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī are divided into pādas.
  • Devotional Surrender and Sacred Anatomy ("Lotus Feet"): In Indian religious ethics and bhakti theology, the physical foot (pāda) represents the lowest bodily member yet the highest object of humble veneration. Devotees revere the guru-pāda or the divine caraṇa-padma (lotus feet). This semantic domain generated honorific titles such as Pādāḥ or Śaṅkarācārya-śrīmat-pādāḥ (literally "the revered feet of the master"), expressing absolute humility by addressing only the master's feet rather than his person.
  • Physical Base or Foot of a Mountain: In classical geography and landscape poetry, pāda denotes the base, foothill, or root of a mountain (parvata-pāda) or tree trunk, mapping the anatomical metaphor onto the natural earth.
