• Pterosaur: Similar because both refer to flying reptiles of the Mesozoic Era; differs because pterosaur is the scientifically accurate taxonomic order (Pterosauria) encompassing all flying archosaurs, whereas pterodactyl is either a single specific genus (Pterodactylus) or a colloquial, scientifically obsolete umbrella term.
  • Pteranodon: Similar because both are famous prehistoric winged creatures; differs because Pteranodon is a distinct, much larger, toothless Late Cretaceous genus famous for its large cranial crest, while true Pterodactylus was a smaller, toothed Late Jurassic animal.
  • Dinosaur: Similar because both are Mesozoic archosaurs popularly imagined sharing the same prehistoric landscapes; differs because dinosaurs form a separate clade characterized by an erect, upright limb posture, whereas pterosaurs are flying reptiles and not dinosaurs.
  • Archaeopteryx: Similar because both were winged Mesozoic vertebrates illuminating the history of flight; differs because Archaeopteryx is a feathered avialan dinosaur representing the evolutionary transition from theropods to true birds, while pterodactyl had skin membranes (patagia) and reptilian pycnofibers without true feathers.
  • Wyvern: Similar because both are depicted as winged creatures with two legs and reptilian anatomy; differs because a wyvern is a mythical heraldic beast, whereas a pterodactyl is an empirically documented fossil animal.
  • Fossil: Similar because both evoke deep geological time and ancient remains; differs because fossil is an all-inclusive geological category of petrified organisms, whereas pterodactyl refers specifically to a flying reptile.
  • Dragon: Similar because both are formidable winged reptilian forms in cultural imagination; differs because a dragon is a mythological, fire-breathing creature of folklore, whereas a pterodactyl was an organic, biological organism governed by aerodynamics.
