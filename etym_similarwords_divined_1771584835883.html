• Guessed: Similar because both mean formed an opinion without certainty; differs because guessed is the humble, ordinary word, possibly a wild shot in the dark with no prestige, whereas divined implies a penetrating, almost supernatural insight.
  • Inferred: Similar because both mean reached a conclusion; differs because inferred claims the conclusion follows from evidence by logical steps, whereas divined makes no such claim—it leaps past evidence by intuition.
  • Surmised: Similar because both mean conjectured on slight evidence; differs because surmise keeps a cool, tentative, provisional tone, while divined carries a confident, quasi-oracular flash of certainty.
  • Deduced: Similar because both mean worked something out; differs because deduced claims logical necessity, drawn step by step from premises, whereas divined is a sudden apprehension that skips the reasoning entirely.
  • Intuited: Similar because both mean knew without explicit reasoning; differs because intuited is personal and psychological—an inner feeling—while divined adds a penetrating, almost oracular, supra-personal character.
  • Sensed: Similar because both mean became aware of something without being told; differs because sensed is faint and passive, a mere impression, while divined is an active, sudden, certain apprehension.
  • Detected: Similar because both mean discovered something hidden; differs because detection implies the observation of evidence and clues, whereas divining surpasses the available evidence.
  • Perceived: Similar because both mean became aware of something; differs because perceived is the neutral, general word for awareness, while divined emphasises the sudden, penetrating, revelatory quality of the insight.
  • Foretold / Prophesied: Similar because both involve foreseeing the future; differs because foretold and prophesied predict what is to come, whereas divined in its modern sense reads present hidden meaning (while in its oldest sense it too meant to foresee by supernatural agency).
  • Anticipated: Similar because both involve knowing something in advance; differs because anticipation is reasoned expectation of what may come, while divining is a sudden certainty about what is.
  • Suspected: Similar because both mean formed an opinion without proof; differs because suspicion is negative, doubtful, and uncertain, while divining is positive, confident insight.
