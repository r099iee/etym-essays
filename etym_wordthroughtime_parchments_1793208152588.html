- 3rd–2nd c. BCE — the Pergamene invention: rival kings quarrel over papyrus, and prepared skins become the luxury book material of the Hellenistic world.
- Roman and early medieval — the codex triumphs: skins folded into books outlast rolls, and parchment carries Scripture, law, and classics through the Dark Ages.
- High medieval — the deed-chest fills: conveyancing grows complex, and parchments come to mean title-documents as much as blank skins.
- 15th–19th c. — paper besieges it: print eats the book trade, then bureaucracy eats the office, yet law clings to skins for solemn instruments.
- 19th c. radicalism — the satirical turn: reformers brandish musty parchments as the landlord armoury, as in the source sentence.
- Today — heritage and craft: vellum statutes until 2016, Torah scrolls, calligraphy, and diplomas keep the skin alive while baking parchment borrows only the name.
