• Self-titled: Similar because both describe something named after its creator or subject; differs because self-titled is colloquial and used chiefly of records and films, while eponymous is the formal term that can also describe the name-giver himself.
  • Namesake: Similar because both involve sharing a name; differs because a namesake is a person or thing named AFTER another, whereas eponymous describes the giver of the name — the two stand on opposite sides of the naming relation.
  • Onomastic: Similar because both relate to names; differs because onomastic pertains to the scholarly study of names in general, while eponymous specifically describes the act of giving one's name to something.
  • Eponym (noun): Similar because both share the same root and refer to the name-giving relation; differs because eponym is the noun naming the person/thing after which something is named, while eponymous is the adjective describing that relation.
  • Titular: Similar because both can describe the title character of a work; differs because titular means "having the title" or "in title only" (a titular role), while eponymous specifies that the name itself comes from the person.
  • Self-named: Similar because both describe something bearing its own name; differs because self-named is informal and stresses the bearer, whereas eponymous is formal and stresses the donor of the name.
  • Patronymic: Similar because both concern names derived from a person; differs because patronymic specifically means "derived from one's father's name" (Johnson, MacDonald), while eponymous covers names given to places, eras, and works, not just surnames.
  • Denominative: Similar because both describe deriving from a name; differs because denominative is grammatical jargon for verbs formed from nouns (e.g. "to lynch"), whereas eponymous is the general adjective of name-giving.
  • Homonymous: Similar because both are -onymous adjectives built on Greek ónyma; differs because homonymous means "having the same name or sound" (two different words spelled alike), a totally different relation from eponymous name-giving.
