PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*dek-
"to take, accept; perceive"
↓
Latin (2nd c. BCE)
docēre
"to teach, show, instruct"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
doctor
"teacher, instructor"
↓
Latin (late)
doctōrem (acc.)
"teacher; learned man"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
docteur
"doctor, learned man"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
doctour / doctor
"learned teacher;
later, physician"
↓
English (18th c.)
doctor (v.)
"to treat medically;
to patch up, falsify"
↓
English (18th-19th c.)
doctored
"treated medically; altered,
tampered with, adulterated"

Note: The link between Latin docēre and PIE *dek- (“to take, accept”) is traditional but debated; some scholars read it as “to take into the mind”, hence “to perceive, to teach”.
