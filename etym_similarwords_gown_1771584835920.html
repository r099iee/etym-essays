• Robe: Similar because both are long, loose outer garments covering the body; differs because a robe tends to be more formal or ceremonial — judicial, monastic, or academic — and in Western dress is chiefly male, while a gown has extended to women’s evening wear and to utilitarian hospital wear, and tends to be more full-length and enveloping.
  • Dress: Similar because both are one-piece garments worn by women; differs because dress is the modern, everyday, neutral word for any one-piece outer garment, while a gown is specifically the dress in its most formal, floor-length, ceremonial form — an evening gown or wedding gown is the dress’s grand cousin.
  • Frock: Similar because both are loose outer garments; differs because frock is now dated — a girl’s dress, a smock, or a clergyman’s garment — and carries a plainer, older, more modest air, while gown covers both casual domestic wear and the highest formal attire.
  • Habit: Similar because both are full garments worn for a purpose; differs because a habit is a fixed, institutional garment — the habit of a nun or monk, or a riding habit — defined by rule and uniform, while a gown is a freer, less prescribed garment of comfort or ceremony.
  • Tunic: Similar because both are loose outer garments; differs because a tunic is characteristically short, ending at hip or thigh, while a gown is long, full-length, and enveloping; the gown also carries ceremonial and formal weight the tunic lacks.
  • Caftan / Kaftan: Similar because both are long, loose, full-length garments; differs because a caftan is an Eastern robe, traditionally of silk or brocade, worn by men or women, while gown is the general Western term spanning everything from hospital wear to the bridal dress.
  • Mantle: Similar because both are outer garments; differs because a mantle is a sleeveless cloak or shawl worn over the shoulders, while a gown is a sleeved, full-body garment; mantle is also archaic or poetic beside gown’s living, everyday currency.
  • Cloak: Similar because both are outer garments; differs because a cloak is a sleeveless cape fastened at the neck and worn over other clothes, while a gown is a full, sleeved garment worn directly over the underclothes or the body.
  • Smock: Similar because both are loose, enveloping garments; differs because a smock is a plain protective outer garment (an artist’s or labourer’s smock) or an old undergarment, and is coarse and utilitarian, while gown may be either utilitarian (hospital gown) or supremely ceremonial (wedding gown).
  • Peignoir / Négligée: Similar because both are loose, flowing women’s domestic garments; differs because these are specifically feminine boudoir garments of lace and silk, while gown is the general term and also covers formal, professional, and medical uses.
