• Proverb: Similar because both are traditional sayings that state a general truth; differs because a proverb is anonymous, folk, and broadly circulated, while a maxim may be attributed to a wise source and is more sententious and prescriptive.
  • Aphorism: Similar because both are terse statements; differs because an aphorism is a witty observation that need not prescribe conduct, whereas a maxim is a rule to live by.
  • Axiom: Similar because both state an accepted truth; differs because an axiom is a self-evident starting point in logic or mathematics, not a practical moral saying.
  • Precept: Similar because both are rules for action; differs because a precept is an explicit directive of conduct, while a maxim is a condensed principle often quoted as wisdom.
  • Adage: Similar because both are short traditional sayings; differs because an adage emphasizes age and popular currency, while a maxim leans toward learned or attributed authority.
  • Dictum: Similar because both are authoritative pronouncements; differs because a dictum is a formal, often judicial or authoritative saying, not necessarily a general rule of conduct.
  • Motto: Similar because both can be short guiding phrases; differs because a motto is a word or phrase adopted as a badge of intention (family, school, creed), not a general truth claim.
  • Saw: Similar because both are traditional proverbial sayings; differs because 'saw' is informal, often homely, and usually anonymous, without a maxim's sententious authority.
  • Epigram: Similar because both are brief pointed sayings; differs because an epigram is prized for wit and polish, often satirical, and need not express a rule of conduct.
  • Apothegm (apophthegm): Similar because both are terse instructive sayings; differs because an apothegm is typically a short saying attributed to a famous person ('a pithy saying of the wise'), more anecdotal than a maxim's abstract rule.
  • Truism: Similar because both state something accepted as true; differs because a truism is a self-evident, often empty statement, whereas a maxim is practical and prescriptive.
  • Principle / rule: Similar because both are general statements guiding conduct; differs because these are neutral, often abstract and longer than a maxim's pithy, quotable form.
