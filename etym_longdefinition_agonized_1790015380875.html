agonized means suffering, expressing, or characterised by intense anguish of body or mind — wrung with extreme pain, terror, pity, or remorse, as a face, cry, or conscience can be. As past tense and participial adjective of agonize, it covers both undergoing and displaying torment: an agonized scream, agonized eyes, the wounded man was agonized, the committee was agonized by indecision. In your sentence — the black slime-things seemed only agonized — the sense is uncannily restrained: men are killed outright, but the alien slime-creatures, presumably more vulnerable or more sensitive, are merely (only) thrown into paroxysms of pain without dying. Only agonized implies disappointment or dread — weapons that kill men merely torture slime, which endures, writhes, and perhaps learns.

The bridge from athletic contest to anguish is Greek ethics turned Christian martyrdom. Greek agon meant gathering, contest, struggle for a prize; agonia meant the mental strain of contending; Christian writers seized agonia for Christ's soul-struggle in Gethsemane, then for martyrs' throes, then for any soul-wrestling with death, sin, or decision. English agonized therefore joins exertion and suffering: to be agonized is to strive painfully, whether against wounds, grief, or a choice that tears. An agonized cry strives to escape; an agonized mind strives to decide.

  • Physical torment core: wracked with bodily pain. Agonized groan, agonized writhing, agonized face, agonized breathing. Means pain so extreme it distorts expression and voice. Medical and battle narratives use it when pained is too weak and excruciating too clinical: agonized keeps the sufferer human and audible. Your slime-things writhing fit here — bodies in spasm.
  • Mental-emotional anguish (commonest literary): tormented by grief, guilt, fear, pity. Agonized mother, agonized confession, agonized suspense, agonized silence. Means inner struggle made visible: clenched hands, broken voice, staring eyes. Collocates with look, glance, voice, cry, expression, wait. Stronger than distressed (upset) and anguished (near-synonym) by adding active wrestling — agony as verb, not state.
  • Agonized deliberation — to agonize over: American-English extension where the struggle is decisional. She agonized over the offer, committee agonized for weeks, agonized reappraisal. Here agonized means anxiously hesitant, painfully thorough, self-laceratingly careful. Not quite suffering, but suffering-to-choose. Business and advice columns normalise it: don't agonize, just decide treats torment as inefficiency.
  • Theological-literary shade: echo of agony in the garden. Agonized prayer, agonized conscience, agonized Christ. Means soul-wrestling with God, death, or sin unto sweat and blood. Hymns, sermons, and Dostoevskian novels preserve it; implies moral stakes, not mere nerves. To be agonized in this sense is to be Gethsemane-like.
  • Grammar — agonized vs agonizing: agonized describes the sufferer or the expression (agonized victim, agonized cry = cry of one agonized); agonizing describes the cause or the lingering quality (agonizing pain, agonizing wait, agonizing decision = pain that agonizes). Agonized is participial-passive (having been agonized, having agonized); agonizing is active-present. Your seemed only agonized is predicative adjective after seemed, stressing state, not action — they were in agony, not performing it.

Form is regular: agonize / agonizes / agonized / agonizing / agonizingly (adverb), noun agony, agonizing. Transitive agonize (to torment: agonized by doubts) is older; intransitive agonize (to struggle: agonizing over choice) and reflexive anguish are modern. Register is high-emotional literary; everyday speech says in pain, upset, stressed, reserving agonized for witnessed extremity or deliberate gravity. Antonyms are serene, calm, composed, relieved, painless, untroubled — what agony interrupts.

In the slime-scene only agonized does moral work. It measures weapons, aliens, and men at once: what annihilates humanity merely pains the inhuman, suggesting either superior endurance or inferior dignity — slime cannot even achieve death, only endless writhing. Agony without death is hell's definition, and the adverb only makes hell sound like failure.
