• slightly: Similar because both describe a small amount or degree; differs because slightly is a broad, neutral descriptor applicable to physical movement, weight, or emotion, whereas marginally carries a stronger analytical connotation of being on the outer threshold of significance or measurement.
  • barely: Similar because both suggest that an outcome scarcely occurred; differs because barely emphasizes extreme closeness to failure or non-occurrence with dramatic tension, whereas marginally provides a measured, objective assessment of a small difference.
  • minimally: Similar because both indicate the smallest possible degree; differs because minimally relates directly to a minimum threshold or baseline requirement, whereas marginally relates to an incremental change at the perimeter or boundary.
  • nominally: Similar because both express that something is modest in effect; differs because nominally implies "in name only" or as a mere formality without real substance, while marginally denotes a genuine, measurable change, albeit a tiny one.
  • fractionally: Similar because both denote an exceedingly small increment; differs because fractionally evokes mathematical precision and sub-unit portions, whereas marginally conveys peripheral status or boundary analysis.
  • peripherally: Similar because both originate from the concept of outer edges; differs because peripherally remains predominantly spatial or structural (dealing with side issues rather than the core), while marginally is far more commonly applied as a quantitative adverb of degree.
  • incrementally: Similar because both can describe tiny shifts; differs because incrementally emphasizes a step-by-step sequential progression over time, whereas marginally describes the static size or relative insignificance of a particular difference.
  • negligibly: Similar because both indicate that an effect is small; differs because negligibly explicitly asserts that the effect is so trivial it can be completely discounted or ignored, whereas marginally acknowledges that the effect exists and may be detected, even if modest.
