PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*gel- / *glem-
"to form into a ball, compact mass"
↓
Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
glomus (gen. glomeris)
"ball of yarn, clew, spherical mass"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
conglomerare
"to wind together into a ball, gather up"
↓
Latin (participle) (1st c. CE)
conglomeratus
"gathered into a ball, heaped together"
↓
English (16th–17th c.)
conglomerate (adj./v.)
"clustered into a rounded mass"
↓
English (geological & corporate) (19th–20th c.)
conglomerate
"sedimentary rock; multi-industry corporate giant"

Note: Latin conglomerare combines the associative prefix com- ("together") with glomus ("a clew or ball of wool or thread"). Related Latin formations include glomulus (diminutive, whence English glomerulus in renal anatomy) and globus (whence English globe). The financial sense arose in the 1950s as a specialized extension of the physical sense of gathering disparate materials into a single mass.
