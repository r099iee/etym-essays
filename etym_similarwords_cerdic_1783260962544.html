• Cynric: Similar because both are early kings of Wessex named in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle as father and son; differs because Cynric is presented as Cerdic's son and successor in the West Saxon line, not the founder.
  • Hengist: Similar because both are legendary Germanic chieftain-founders of Anglo-Saxon kingdoms in Britain; differs because Hengist (with his brother Horsa) traditionally founded the Kingdom of Kent in the 5th century, while Cerdic founded Wessex.
  • Horsa: Similar because both belong to the legendary migration-age founders of Anglo-Saxon kingdoms; differs because Horsa is Hengist's brother in the Kentish foundation legend, unconnected to the Wessex line of Cerdic.
  • Ida: Similar because both are named on this card as Anglo-Saxon founder-kings who brought pagan custom to Britain; differs because Ida is the traditional founder of the kingdom of Bernicia (Northumbria), while Cerdic founded Wessex.
  • Alfred: Similar because both are Wessex kings of the royal line traditionally traced to Cerdic; differs because Alfred the Great (849–899) is a fully historic, documented king of all the Anglo-Saxons, four centuries after the semi-legendary founder.
  • Egbert: Similar because both are Wessex kings in the Cerdicing line; differs because Egbert (reigned 802–839) is the historic king often regarded as the first king of England, while Cerdic belongs to the legendary foundation period.
  • Caradog / Caratacus: Similar because both are the Celtic names from which Cerdic is usually derived, both meaning "beloved"; differs because Caradog/Caratacus is the Brittonic historical name (borne by a 1st-century British chieftain), while Cerdic is its supposed Old English royal descendant.
  • Woden: Similar because both anchor Anglo-Saxon royal genealogy — Wessex kings traced descent through Cerdic back to the god Woden; differs because Woden is the pagan chief god himself, while Cerdic is the human founder-king of the West Saxon house.
