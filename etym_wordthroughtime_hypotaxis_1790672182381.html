- Under-arrangement in Greek - hypotaxis as military subordination and grammatical dependence; ranking as order
- Philological antithesis to parataxis - 19th-century German classical scholarship pairs hypotaxe/parataxe to narrate progress from Homeric chaining to Attic period
- Exalted as reason and civilisation - Victorian rhetoric teaches hypotactic period as logical, manly, civilised mind; schoolroom prestige
- Attacked as fog and power - Orwell, plain-English and modernist reformers indict hypotaxis as bureaucratic evasion and mandarin hierarchy
- Measured by science today - psycholinguistics counts subordination as maturity and load; stylistics treats hypotaxis/parataxis as neutral alternating resources, now mourned as lost vocabulary
