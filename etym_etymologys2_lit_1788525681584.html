"light in weight, agile, swift, not heavy"

Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*līhtaz
"light, easy, unburdened"

Proto-Germanic (causative verb)
*līhtijaną
"to make light, disburden, dismount"
↓
Old English (c. 9th c.)
līhtan (past tense līhte)
"to dismount from horseback, alight, descend"
↓
Middle English (12th–14th c.; Chaucer)
lighten (past tense lighte / lit)
"to alight, descend from flight, settle on a perch"
↓
Modern English (past tense / participle)
LIT
"alighted, descended onto a perch; (homograph) illuminated, kindled"

Note on Homographic Streams: Sense 1 (the motion/perch sense in context) descends from PIE **h₁lengʷʰ-* ("light in weight") via Proto-Germanic **līhtijaną* ("to relieve of weight, dismount"). Sense 2 (illumination/ignition) derives from a completely separate PIE root **lewk-* ("to shine, be bright") → Proto-Germanic **leuhtijaną* → Old English *līehtan* / *lȳhtan* ("to shine, ignite") → Middle English *lighten* (past: *lighted* / *lit*). Both roots converged into identical phonetic forms in Middle English.
