Note on phonology and morphology: Latin accēdere exhibits regular regressive consonant assimilation: the voiced dental stop *d of the directional prepositional prefix ad- assimilated completely to the following voiceless velar stop *c [k], producing the geminate cluster acc- [kk]. The simplex verb cēdere ("to go, withdraw, yield") forms one of the most productive morphological families in Latin and English, giving rise through different directional prefixes to precede (go before), succeed (go under/follow), recede (go back), concede (yield entirely), intercede (go between), and exceed (go beyond). In each case, the underlying kinetic motion of "going" or "yielding" was metaphorically transferred to interpersonal, legal, and intellectual relationships.
