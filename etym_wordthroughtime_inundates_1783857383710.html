17th-Century English Borrowing - Inundates enters English in the 1620s from Latin inundare ("to overflow, flood"), first in literal prose about rivers and seas covering land with water, a formal, Latinate word.
Literal-Plus-Figurative Range - Through the 18th-19th centuries the verb extends naturally from flooding to overwhelming: one is inundated by work, letters, information, or demands, so the water image becomes a standing metaphor for quantity.
20th-21st Century Information Age - With mass media and email, inundates thrives in its figurative sense — swamped by messages, requests, data — while keeping its literal sense in hydrology and news; the flood imagery remains vivid.
Contemporary Sense - Today inundates is the standard formal verb for overwhelming with an unstoppable volume, equally at home describing rivers, workloads, and streams of information.
