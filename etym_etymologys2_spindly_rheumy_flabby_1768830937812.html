Root 3 — FLABBY:

Modern English (14th c.)
flap
"to swing or hang loosely"
↓
Modern English (19th c.)
flab
"loose, flabby flesh"
(an alteration of flap)
↓
Modern English (17th c.)
flabby
"soft, loose, lacking firmness"

Debate: flab is chiefly attested only from the 19th c. and is likely a back-formation from flabby, which itself is flap + -y (17th c.); the attestation dates are tangled and the whole group is of uncertain, possibly echoic, origin.
