• Adaptation: Similar in being a biological feature whose function is explained by selection; differs because an adaptation was built by selection for its present job, whereas an exaptation was co-opted into a new role after the fact.
  • Preadaptation: Similar in naming a trait that comes to serve a function other than that for which it arose; differs because preadaptation wrongly implies the trait was "waiting" for its future use, a teleology that exaptation explicitly rejects.
  • Spandrel: Similar in describing a feature not designed for its present function; differs because a spandrel is a non-adaptive structural byproduct by origin, which becomes an exaptation only when later co-opted for a use.
  • Byproduct: Similar in naming something that arises as a secondary consequence; differs because byproduct describes origin alone, whereas exaptation names the subsequent reuse of that feature.
  • Side effect: Similar in denoting a consequence that accompanies a primary effect; differs because side effect is a loose everyday term with no claim about function, while exaptation names a functional re-deployment.
  • Co-option: Similar in naming the taking over of something for a new role; differs because co-option is a general word used of genes, traits, and even people, while exaptation is the technical biological term for the trait that results.
  • Repurposing / reuse: Similar in describing the application of an existing thing to a new function; differs because repurposing is everyday and agentive (someone does it), whereas exaptation is non-teleological, describing what selection does without intent.
  • Vestigial structure: Similar as a biological leftover whose current role does not match its origin; differs because a vestigial structure has lost its function and is typically degenerating, whereas an exaptation has gained a new one and flourishes.
