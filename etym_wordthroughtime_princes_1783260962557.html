- 13th-century entry - prince entered English from Old French, from Latin princeps (first-taker, first), and originally named any sovereign ruler, potentate, or chief, including monarchs of whole kingdoms.
- Early modern narrowing - the word gradually narrowed to male members of a royal family or rulers of small principalities, while surviving in older and literary English as a general term for any ruler, so that a king could still be called a prince.
- Modern sense - prince now usually denotes a royal son or heir, or the ruler of a principality, and flourishes in fixed phrases (crown prince, Prince of Wales, merchant prince, prince of darkness) that preserve its older breadth.
