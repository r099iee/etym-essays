• rake: Similar because both denote a dissolute man of pleasure who flouts moral restraints. Differs because rake (from rakehell) emphasises restless, extravagant dissipation and often carries a faint, forgiving charm, while libertine is graver and stresses the philosophical corruption of freedom into license.
  • roué: Similar because both name a licentious, debauched man. Differs because roué (French, "broken on the wheel") implies a hardened, cynical sensualist past redemption, while libertine foregrounds the deliberate rejection of moral law and the misuse of liberty.
  • debauchee: Similar because both denote a person given to sensual excess and moral corruption. Differs because debauchee stresses corruption through indulgence itself (debauchery), while libertine stresses the intellectual and moral stance of freedom turned into license.
  • hedonist: Similar because both centre a life devoted to pleasure. Differs because hedonist is a neutral philosophical label (the pursuit of pleasure as the good) that need not be immoral or destructive, while libertine is condemnatory and implies flouting moral and social law.
  • profligate: Similar because both describe reckless extravagance and dissipation. Differs because profligate emphasises wastefulness and squandering (of money and virtue) rather than the freedom-fuelled defiance at the heart of libertine.
  • reprobate: Similar because both are morally condemned persons. Differs because reprobate carries theological weight — one cast off by God, morally lost — while libertine is social and philosophical, about the abuse of freedom rather than divine rejection.
  • wanton: Similar because both denote unrestrained, licentious behaviour. Differs because wanton is an adjective stressing capricious, ungoverned excess (often of cruelty or lust), while libertine is a noun naming the person and the philosophy behind the excess.
  • sensualist: Similar because both describe a person devoted to the gratifications of the senses. Differs because sensualist is a neutral descriptive label for the pursuit of sensory pleasure, while libertine implies the deliberate transgression of moral codes.
  • freethinker: Similar because the word's own history links them — a 17th-century libertine was a freethinker. Differs because freethinker today is positive or neutral (independence of religious thought), while libertine has become wholly condemnatory and moral.
  • scoundrel: Similar because both name a morally bad person. Differs because scoundrel is a general word for a dishonest, base person without the aristocratic, pleasure-seeking, freedom-misused colouring of libertine.
