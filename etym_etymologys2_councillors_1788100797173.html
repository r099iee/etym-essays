Note — two Latin words converge here. The word itself descends from consulere → consilium → consiliarius, the “advice, counsel” line. The modern spelling, however, follows council, which is a DIFFERENT Latin word: concilium, “assembly, meeting”, from com- “together” + calare “to call”. The 16th-century spelling “councillor” fused the two historically distinct words orthographically. The base of consulere is debated: the Romans themselves connected it to salire “to leap” (“those who leap together”); modern scholarship treats the base as uncertain, with a tentative link to PIE *sel- “to take”. The chain above uses only attested forms and marks the uncertain stages as such.
