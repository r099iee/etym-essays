- 16th-century coinage - English mints 'heraldry' from the officer-title 'herald,' naming the whole systematic science of armorial bearings; heavily institutional, a legal-record discipline administered by the College of Arms and its Tudor visitations.
- 17th-18th century - The word matures as the codified grammar of rank: blazon fixes an exact descriptive language, and heraldry reads as the written record of lineage, marriage alliances, and honours, inseparable from genealogy.
- 19th century - Romantic-medievalist revival floods the word with chivalric glamour; heraldry is re-read as the poetry of noble lineage and national pageantry, not merely legal record.
- Contemporary - Ceremonial and institutional life (royal, civic, and military arms) keeps the word alive, but its practical, legal force has receded; it now also drifts metaphorically into 'the heraldry of a brand or cause.'
