• libertarianism: Similar because both prioritize individual liberty and express deep skepticism toward state coercion; differs because modern American-style libertarianism defends private capitalist property rights and often accepts a minimal state (minarchism), whereas classical anarchism historically rejects capitalism and the state entirely.
  • syndicalism: Similar because both reject parliamentary politics and favor direct working-class management of society; differs because syndicalism focuses strictly on industrial trade unions as the vehicle of revolution, whereas anarchism encompasses broader cultural, philosophical, and ecological dimensions beyond the workplace.
  • mutualism: Similar because it is an explicit branch of anarchism advocating non-coercive social arrangements; differs because mutualism specifically promotes a reciprocal market economy based on worker-owned cooperatives, cost-price exchange, and interest-free mutual credit, whereas other anarchist currents (like anarcho-communism) reject market mechanisms completely.
  • nihilism: Similar because both movements were prominent in nineteenth-century Russian revolutionary politics and rejected established moral, religious, and monarchical authority; differs because nihilism is primarily an epistemological and skeptical rejection of all traditional values and institutions without necessarily proposing a positive vision of cooperative social organization.
  • antiauthoritarianism: Similar because both oppose arbitrary command, top-down hierarchies, and authoritarian control; differs because antiauthoritarianism is a general ethical disposition or critical stance that can exist within democratic or reformist frameworks, whereas anarchism is an uncompromising total rejection of the state itself.
  • collectivism: Similar because both advocate social ownership of productive resources and opposition to private capitalist monopolies; differs because collectivism can be organized hierarchically by a centralized state (as in state socialism), whereas anarchist collectivism insists on horizontal, decentralized federation without a governing elite.
  • anarcho-capitalism: Similar because both profess a desire to eliminate the sovereign state apparatus; differs because anarcho-capitalism seeks to replace public governance with unregulated private corporate property and market contracts, a model rejected by historical anarchists who view private capital accumulation as intrinsically coercive and hierarchical.
  • autarchism: Similar because both emphasize self-rule and independence from political coercion; differs because autarchism is primarily an individualist doctrine of personal self-mastery and internal moral sovereignty, lacking anarchism's rich tradition of collective social action and communal solidarity.
  • antinomianism: Similar because both reject externally imposed legal codes and statutory commands; differs because antinomianism is historically a theological concept asserting that religious believers are freed from moral or statutory laws by divine grace, whereas anarchism is a secular sociopolitical philosophy rooted in human ethics and cooperation.
  • direct democracy: Similar because both champion face-to-face popular deliberation and the elimination of detached political oligarchies; differs because direct democracy still accepts majority rule and binding civic enforcement over the minority, whereas anarchism emphasizes voluntary association, free federation, and consensus to prevent majority tyranny.
  • council communism: Similar because both advocate for workers' direct control of society through autonomous neighborhood and factory councils rather than vanguard political parties; differs because council communism remains rooted in Marxist historical materialism and retains a transitional concept of working-class governance.
  • voluntaryism: Similar because both hold that all human interactions and social relations should be strictly voluntary; differs because voluntaryism typically focuses on private property rights and non-aggression principles derived from classical liberal natural law, whereas traditional anarchism emphasizes mutual aid, communal resources, and anti-hierarchical ethics.
