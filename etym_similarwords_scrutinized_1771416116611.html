• Examined: Similar because both describe careful, methodical looking-over; differs because examined is the neutral professional term, while scrutinized adds the sense of hunting for concealed detail with a critical, evaluative eye.
  • Inspected: Similar because both describe systematic, official checking; differs because inspected is bureaucratic and impersonal — the word of auditors and building codes — while scrutinized is intimate and evaluative.
  • Studied: Similar because both describe prolonged, absorbed attention; differs because studied can be appreciative (to study a face) and lacks the critical edge of looking for what may be wrong or hidden.
  • Surveyed: Similar because both describe looking over something; differs because surveyed is broad and panoramic, taking in a whole scene quickly and shallowly, while scrutinized implies minute, prolonged digging for detail.
  • Scanned: Similar because both describe a visual search; differs because scanned is quick and cursory, barely touching the surface, while scrutinized means exhaustive, careful, and searching.
  • Perused: Similar because both describe careful attention; differs because perused is restricted to documents and books and is rather formal, while scrutinized applies to objects, faces, and scenes as well.
  • Analyzed: Similar because both describe close investigation; differs because analyzed emphasizes intellectual breaking-apart of a problem, while scrutinized is the visual, searching examination of a particular object or person.
  • Probed: Similar because both describe testing something closely; differs because probed suggests physically or figuratively poking and testing with specific questions, while scrutinized is a comprehensive visual examination.
  • Investigated: Similar because both describe systematic examination for facts; differs because investigated is the formal, often official process of gathering evidence in an inquiry, while scrutinized is the intense inspection itself.
  • Pored over: Similar because both describe bent-over, absorbed close reading; differs because pored over is restricted to texts and implies long, patient reading, while scrutinized covers any object of intense inspection.
