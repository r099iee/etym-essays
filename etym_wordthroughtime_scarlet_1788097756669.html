- Medieval luxury cloth (11th-13th c.) - "scarlet" names the costliest dyed woollen cloth of Europe, almost a brand of the elite; perception: wealth, rank, forbidden display.
- Colour-name transfer (13th-15th c.) - the word slides from the fabric to its famous kermes red; perception: the hue itself becomes a status marker.
- Court and church signalling (14th-17th c.) - cardinals, kings, judges and town dignitaries wear scarlet; perception: authority, ceremony, pomp.
- Sin and shame (17th-19th c.) - the scarlet woman (Revelation 17) and Hawthorne's scarlet letter (1850) recast the colour; perception: adultery, scandal, public disgrace.
- Sickroom terror (17th-19th c.) - scarlet fever makes the word a household fear of epidemics; perception: contagion, quarantine, childhood danger.
- Modern vibrancy (20th-21st c.) - scarlet returns as glamour: lipstick shade, team colour, given name, Scarlet Witch; perception: energy, passion, style - the old prestige rebranded.
