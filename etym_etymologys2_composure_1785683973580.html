PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*seh₁-
"to let go, release"
↓
Latin (Old Latin, c. 3rd c. BCE)
sinō
"to let, allow; to put, place"
↓
Latin (3rd–2nd c. BCE)
pōnō
"to put, place, set; to arrange"
from po- (PIE *apo "away") + sinō
↓
Latin (2nd c. BCE)
componō
"to put together, assemble,
arrange, compose"
com- "together" + pōnō
↓
Old French (12th c. CE)
composer
"to put together, arrange, compose"
↓
English (15th c. CE)
compose
"to put together, form by combining;
to settle, calm"
↓
English (c. 1600)
composure
"calmness, self-control;
settled state of mind"
note: an English coinage, compose + -ure, modelled on Latin compositūra "arrangement"; the calmness sense (17th c.) grew from "composed" = self-possessed. The PIE link for sinō (*seh₁- "to let go") is de Vaan's tentative reconstruction and remains debated.
