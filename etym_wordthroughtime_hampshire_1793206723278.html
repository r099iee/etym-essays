- Roman Britain — Venta Belgarum: the market town that will become Winchester anchors the district, walled, minted, and road-crossed.
- 6th–7th c. — Hamtun grows: a Saxon settlement by the Itchen, a home-farm writ large, gives its name to the surrounding district of Wessex.
- 10th c. — the shire is born: West Saxon kings organise Hamtunscir with its reeve and moot, and Winchester becomes the capital of a kingdom, not merely a county.
- 1086 — Domesday writes Hantescire: Normans count its hides, mills, and forests, fixing the county on parchment with Southampton rising as a port.
- Tudor–Stuart centuries — sea power: Portsmouth's dockyard and the New Forest's oaks make Hampshire the county that builds and berths the Navy.
- 1830 — Cobbett rides it: Rural Rides turns the north-west corner round Andover into the moral centre of England, corn against funds, labourer against placeman.
- Victorian age — Austen's county and the watering-places: Chawton pilgrims, Bournemouth villas, and the cricket club carry the name worldwide.
- Today — downland, forest, and harbours: a ceremonial county of national parks and naval bases, still spelling itself Hants on the county-cricket cap.
