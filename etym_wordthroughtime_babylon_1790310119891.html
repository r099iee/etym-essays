- Middle English (c. 1250–1500) - Understood primarily through theological texts and the Vulgate as the legendary site of the Tower of Babel, the Babylonian exile, and the apocalyptic symbol of spiritual blindness and heresy.
- Renaissance & Reformation (c. 1500–1650) - Fiercely deployed as a polemical epithet; Protestant reformers (such as Martin Luther in On the Babylonian Captivity of the Church) labeled Papal Rome as the modern "Babylon."
- Enlightenment & Victorian Era (c. 1700–1900) - Expanded in romantic literature and secular history into the supreme symbol of ancient oriental luxury, monumental architectural splendor, and inevitable imperial decay.
- 20th Century (1930s–1980s) - Transformed by the Rastafari movement and global counterculture into an anti-imperialist political concept representing the capitalist establishment, police oppression, and systemic corruption.
- Contemporary Era (1990s–Present) - Continues in literature, film, and social critique as shorthand for intoxicating, hedonistic urban centers, commercial hubris, and the fragile illusions of material wealth.
