• Dissonance: Similar because both involve harmonic tension and sounds that clash according to traditional rules; differs because dissonance refers to specific harsh or unresolved harmonic intervals within any musical system (including tonal music), whereas atonality refers to the total, systemic abolition of a central key or harmonic hierarchy altogether.
  • Polytonality: Similar because both represent radical departures from standard single-key composition; differs because polytonality involves the simultaneous sounding of two or more distinct, identifiable keys (such as Stravinsky's *Petrushka* chord), whereas atonality rejects the presence of any recognizable key centers whatsoever.
  • Serialism: Similar because both are foundational modern compositional techniques associated with 20th-century avant-garde music; differs because serialism (twelve-tone technique) is a specific, highly mathematical method of ordering pitches into fixed tone rows, whereas atonality is the broader acoustic condition of lacking a tonal center (which can be either freely composed or strictly serialist).
  • Pantonality: Similar because pantonality was Arnold Schoenberg’s preferred alternative term for his music; differs because pantonality implies the inclusion and synthesis of all tones/keys rather than a purely negative absence ("without tone"), whereas atonality became the standard historical musicological label.
  • Disharmony: Similar because both describe the absence of pleasant concord; differs because disharmony is a broad, often derogatory term for general acoustic discord or social conflict, whereas atonality is a precise, sophisticated musicological and theoretical framework.
  • Cacophony: Similar because untrained listeners may perceive both as harsh and jarring; differs because cacophony denotes pure, chaotic, unorganized harsh noise, whereas atonality exhibits meticulous, complex internal logic and architectural coherence.
  • Acentrism: Similar because both denote the absence of a central organizing anchor; differs because acentrism is a general philosophical or astronomical term, whereas atonality specifically originates in music theory before being applied metaphorically to aesthetics.
  • Microtonality: Similar because both challenge Western diatonic scales; differs because microtonality uses intervals smaller than a semitone (quarter tones), whereas atonality primarily utilizes the standard twelve equal-tempered semitones while stripping them of hierarchical key relationships.
