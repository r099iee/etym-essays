• crowning: Similar because it describes the placing of a crown; differs because crowning is the plain, functional word, used loosely of anything that tops or completes something, while coronation names the full institutional ceremony of the state.
  • enthronement: Similar because it is a monarchical ceremony; differs because enthronement focuses on the specific act of seating the monarch on the throne — one moment within the larger ritual, centred on the chair rather than the head.
  • investiture: Similar because it is a formal installation into a high office; differs because investiture deliberately avoids the monarchical crown, applying to knights, bishops, and presidents — anyone installed into an office, honour, or order.
  • inauguration: Similar because it is a formal ceremony that opens a new status; differs because inauguration is secular and civic — the swearing-in of an official — deliberately republican and uncrowned, while a coronation is the crowning of a sovereign.
  • consecration: Similar because it is a solemn religious ceremony of investiture; differs because consecration shifts the focus to the sacred blessing of a person or thing and is used of bishops, churches, and objects, not specifically of crowning a monarch.
  • installation: Similar because it is a formal ceremony of induction; differs because installation is generic — used of professors, rectors, and clergy — with no crown and no monarchical weight.
  • anointing: Similar because it is a royal rite of investiture; differs because anointing names the chrism rite that precedes the crowning, the oiling of the head, not the placing of the crown itself.
  • accession: Similar because it belongs to the beginning of a reign; differs because accession is the legal act of succeeding to the throne — a matter of inheritance and law — which precedes the public ceremony of the coronation.
  • acclamation: Similar because it is a formal public act acknowledging a ruler; differs because acclamation is the shouted approval of the people (as in an elected kingship), not the ritual crowning itself.
  • swearing-in: Similar because it is a formal ceremony that inaugurates an office; differs because a swearing-in is the modern, secular, republican equivalent (a president, a judge) with oath and no crown.
