- 14th century: "rustelen" = to make the soft dry sound of leaves, paper, silk, wings - a purely echoic word of the natural world.
- 16th-17th centuries: extends to brisk, busy human movement - "to rustle about" - the sound of activity becomes the activity.
- 18th-19th centuries, American English: "to rustle up" = to gather or prepare quickly (food, supplies) - energetic improvisation.
- c. 1880s American West: "to rustle cattle" = to steal livestock; the "rustler" becomes the romanticised outlaw of the range - the jump from busy gathering to quiet theft.
- 20th century: the noun "rustle" is the everyday word for the sound of leaves, grass, paper, and silk; "cattle rustling" is both a genre staple and a still-prosecuted crime.
- 21st century: the senses coexist — one soft, one bold; the echoic core still sounds in every use.
