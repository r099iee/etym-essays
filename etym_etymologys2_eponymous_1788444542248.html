Note on usage evolution: In strict classical Greek and 19th-century English historiography, eponymous applied exclusively to the person who *bestowed* the name upon the tribe or city. In mid-20th-century media and arts criticism, the semantic direction experienced a symmetrical extension (bidirectional usage), allowing the *work, album, or entity itself* to be described as eponymous (e.g. 'the band's eponymous album'). Lexicographers now recognize both active and receptive senses.
