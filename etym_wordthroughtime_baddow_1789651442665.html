- Pre-English river-name Beadwan - a small Chelmer tributary name of uncertain, possibly Celtic meaning (birch stream or goddess-name); purely hydronymic, no village yet.
- 1086 Domesday vill Baduven - Norman scribes fix the settlement on the Beadwan as a taxable manor; agricultural, feudal, ecclesiastical life begins to gather around the name.
- 1172-1400 Great versus Little - St Mary's baptisms, parish division and Middle English spelling settling to Baddow; the river above Chelmsford increasingly called Chelmer while villages fossilise the old name.
- 1500s-1900s agricultural parishes - quiet Essex farming villages with church, hall, commons and woods; the name is purely local-administrative, unknown beyond the county.
- 1960s-present beautiful commuter village - unlocked doors until the 1970s gives way to London overspill desirability; Little Baddow becomes shorthand for surviving rural trust inside the commuter belt, while Great Baddow suburbanises — affectionate, elegiac, heritage-loaded perception.
