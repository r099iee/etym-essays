• Vanished: Similar because both denote that which has disappeared completely and left no visible trace behind; differs because vanished is a versatile English Germanic adjective used across informal, spatial, and supernatural contexts, whereas disparue carries distinct French literary prestige, feminine grammatical marking, and specific Proustian tragic resonance.
  • Fugitive: Similar because both describe someone who has slipped away and cannot be located, directly competing in Proust's title history (La Fugitive vs Albertine disparue); differs because fugitive emphasizes active flight, deliberate evasion of capture, and transgression of legal or emotional custody, whereas disparue emphasizes the resulting void, absence, and potential demise of the departed.
  • Departed: Similar because both function as elegant, comforting euphemisms for someone who has died; differs because departed emphasizes a dignified spiritual journey to an afterlife or another realm, whereas disparue emphasizes the sharp, agonizing vacuum and optical invisibility left behind in the physical world.
  • Missing: Similar because both identify individuals whose current whereabouts cannot be confirmed; differs because missing is a practical, administrative, and neutral designation (e.g., a missing person report) that remains agnostic about survival, whereas disparue in legal and literary registers heavily implies mortal peril, death, or permanent irrevocable loss.
  • Absent: Similar because both denote non-presence in a specific location; differs because absent is a temporary, reversible state that implies the person remains safely in existence elsewhere, whereas disparue indicates that the person has disappeared beyond reach, often permanently.
  • Extinct: Similar because both signify complete disappearance, as in a vanished biological line (espèce disparue); differs because extinct is an objective, technical biological and geological term for the irreversible termination of a species or volcano, whereas disparue encompasses intimate human bereavement, romantic longing, and individual death.
