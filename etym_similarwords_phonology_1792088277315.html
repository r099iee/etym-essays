• Phonetics: Similar because both investigate the sounds of human speech; differs because phonetics is an empirical physical science studying the physiological articulation, acoustic waveform properties, and auditory perception of raw vocal sounds across all humans, whereas phonology investigates the abstract, cognitive, grammatical rule systems and functional contrasts that govern sounds within specific languages.
  • Phonotactics: Similar because both analyze the legal structuring of speech sounds; differs because phonotactics is a specific sub-branch within phonology dedicated strictly to the constraints governing allowed sound sequences and syllable positions, whereas phonology encompasses the entire broader discipline including feature theory, stress, and alternations.
  • Morphophonology: Similar because both analyze phonological processes; differs because morphophonology focuses exclusively on the intersection where morphological boundaries trigger phonological alternations (e.g., how the plural morpheme alternates between /-s/, /-z/, and /-ɪz/ in English).
  • Morphology: Similar because both are core structural modules of grammatical theory; differs because morphology investigates the internal structure of words and the combination of meaningful units (morphemes), whereas phonology operates on meaningless sound segments and syllable structures.
  • Orthography: Similar because both represent the forms of words; differs because orthography designates the artificial, written spelling system and script conventions of a language, whereas phonology resides in the spoken, mental linguistic competence of native speakers.
  • Prosody: Similar because both deal with sound patterning in spoken speech; differs because prosody specifically highlights the expressive musical features of speech—rhythm, meter, stress, and intonation contours—whereas phonology encompasses both segmental phonemes and suprasegmentals.
