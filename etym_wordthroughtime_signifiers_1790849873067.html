- Late 14th c. to 16th c.: Emerged in Middle and Early Modern English as a general agent noun denoting one who makes known, signifies, indicates, or foretells (e.g., an omen or a messenger).
- 17th c. to 19th c.: Used occasionally in scholastic philosophy and logic to denote an external indicator, symptom, or distinguishing mark.
- 1916 (Posthumous publication of Saussure's *Cours*): Transformed into a foundational technical term of structural linguistics (*signifiant*), defining the acoustic image or physical mark within the sign.
- 1950s to 1970s: Re-theorized in psychoanalysis by Jacques Lacan and in post-structuralism/deconstruction by Jacques Derrida and Roland Barthes as autonomous, floating elements of desire and textual play.
- Late 20th c. to Present: Broadly disseminated across media studies, cultural theory, marketing, and general educated parlance to denote status markers, aesthetic cues, and symbolic indicators.
