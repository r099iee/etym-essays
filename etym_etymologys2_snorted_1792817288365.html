Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, imitative, c. 500 BCE)
*snurt-
“to snore, breathe harshly”
“through the nose”
↓
Old Norse (13th c.)
snorta
“to snore, snort”
↓
Middle English (c. 1350)
snorten
“to snore loudly”
“breathe harshly”
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
snort
“noisy nasal blast”
“of beast or man”
↓
Modern English (current)
snorted
“past of snort”
“derisive nasal blast”

Note: the word is classed as imitative in origin, so no secure PIE root stands behind it; handbooks sometimes gesture toward an extended nasal form related to snore, but the deeper pre-Germanic connection is debated and the imitative account is the standard one.
