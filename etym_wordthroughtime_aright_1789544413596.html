- Old English (c. 9th – 11th Century): On the right path, straightly, directly, properly - Originated as the prepositional phrase on riht, describing physical alignment, moral righteousness, and correct conduct under divine law.
- Middle English (c. 12th – 15th Century): Formed single compound adverb ariht - Widely used in Chaucer and biblical translations to mean correctly, justly, or accurately in speech, thought, and deed.
- Early Modern & Victorian English: Standard literary adverb of memory, perception, and judgment - Extensively employed in fixed collocations ("if I remember aright," "judge aright," "hear aright") across British literature and detective fiction.
- 20th – 21st Century: Highly literary, poetic, and archaic marker - Primarily preserved in formulaic idioms of recollection and formal philosophical or ecclesiastical prose.
