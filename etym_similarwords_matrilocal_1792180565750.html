• Uxorilocal: Similar because both denote post-marital residence with or near the bride's kin; differs because uxorilocal (from Latin uxor, "wife") broadly describes settling near the wife's home regardless of lineage structure, whereas matrilocal specifically denotes living with or under the household of the wife's mother and maternal clan.
  • Matrilineal: Similar because both center on maternal kinship ties; differs because matrilineal pertains strictly to the descent, clan affiliation, and inheritance traced through the female line, whereas matrilocal governs physical geographic residence after marriage.
  • Matrifocal: Similar because both focus on maternal primacy within the family; differs because matrifocal describes a family structure or domestic unit where the mother is the central structural figure, emotional anchor, and primary economic head, regardless of geographic residence rules or clan lineage laws.
  • Patrilocal: Similar because both are formal anthropological residence rules governing post-marital domestic establishment; differs because patrilocal is the direct structural opposite, mandating that the bride leave her natal family to reside with the groom's father and paternal clan.
  • Virilocal: Similar because both designate spouse-relocation patterns; differs because virilocal (from Latin vir, "man") is the opposite category describing residence with the husband's kin, contrasting with female-centered matrilocality.
  • Neolocal: Similar because both describe post-marital residence choices; differs because neolocal residence requires the newlyweds to establish an independent household completely separate from both the husband's and the wife's kin, as is standard in modern Western industrialized societies.
  • Bilocal: Similar because both involve residence with parental kin; differs because bilocal (or ambilocal) residence grants the couple an active cultural choice or seasonal alternation between living with the husband's kin or the wife's kin.
  • Avunculocal: Similar because both occur in matrilineal societies; differs because avunculocal residence requires the married couple to live with or near the husband's maternal uncle (the mother's brother), reinforcing male lineage authority over maternal property rather than maternal co-residence.
  • Duolocal: Similar because both are traditional residence arrangements; differs because in duolocal residence, the husband and wife never cohabit, each continuing to live permanently with their own respective natal families after marriage.
