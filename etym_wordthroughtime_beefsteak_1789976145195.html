- Early 1700s club dish - hearty masculine festivity, Beef-Steak Clubs celebrate English liberty and appetite
- Victorian staple - emblem of prosperity and manliness, chophouse pride and domestic roast-rival
- Early 1900s menu generic - any substantial beef steak, plus tomato and fungus transfers by red fleshy likeness
- Mid-1900s American banquet - all-you-can-eat beefsteak dinners as political fundraisers
- Contemporary usage - slightly old-fashioned alone, alive in compounds (beefsteak tomato) and simile (like a raw beefsteak)
