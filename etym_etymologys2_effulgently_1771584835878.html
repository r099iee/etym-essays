EFFULGENTLY — single Latin-derived chain, fully attested from Latin onward. • PIE *bʰel- "to shine, flash, burn" (reconstructed, c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE; among the most productive of all PIE roots of light: Greek phlégein "to blaze," phálos "shining white," Old English bealca/belcan? no — Old English bærnan "to burn," beorht "bright" (from *bʰer-? no), Old English bǣl "fire," Sanskrit bhāti "shines," Lithuanian balti "to grow white"; the root appears with various suffixes, including *bʰleg- "to burn, blaze" which underlies Latin flagrāre "to blaze" and Greek phlégein). → PIE *bʰleg- (extended form of *bʰel-, "to burn, blaze, flash," the form that yields the Latin "ful-" of fulgēre) — the exact relationship of Latin fulgēre to *bʰel- via *bʰl̥g- is standard (Latin fulgur "lightning," fulgere "to flash" are connected to the Greek phlégein family; the reconstruction is accepted but involves the well-known laryngeal/sonorant detail of *bʰel-g-). → Latin fulgēre "to shine, flash, lighten" (attested from the 2nd c. BCE, in Ennius and Plautus; an everyday poetic verb for lightning, gleaming metal, and flashing eyes; its noun fulgur "lightning" and adjective fulgidus are attested). → Latin effulgēre "to shine forth, flash out, beam out" (compound of ex- "out" + fulgēre, attested in Classical Latin prose and poetry, 1st c. BCE, e.g., in the poetic diction of the Augustan age). → Latin effulgēns (present participle) "shining forth, beaming, effulgent" (attested). → English effulgent "shining brilliantly, radiant, resplendent" (borrowed in the 18th century, c. 1730–40, from Latin effulgentem; used by poets and rhetoricians). → English effulgence "brilliant radiance, a pouring forth of light" (borrowed 17th–18th century). → English effulgently "in a brilliantly shining manner" (adverb formed in English from effulgent + -ly, used from the 18th–19th century onward in elevated prose and poetry). → Modern English EFFULGENTLY. Note: the only reconstructed stages are the PIE root *bʰel- and its extended form *bʰleg-/*bʰl̥g-; from Latin fulgēre onward every stage is attested. The derivation of fulgēre from *bʰel-g- is standard but involves reconstruction of the internal suffix, and older handbooks sometimes reconstructed a separate *bhleg-; the majority view is that fulg- continues *bʰl̥g- from *bʰel-g- (see de Vaan, s.v. fulgēre).
