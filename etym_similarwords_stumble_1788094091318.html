• Trip: Similar because both mean to catch the foot and lose balance; differs because trip is a single quick catch (and can be deliberately caused by another person, "she tripped him"), while stumble is a clumsier, more drawn-out loss of footing with far broader figurative uses.
  • Stagger: Similar because both are unsteady walks; differs because stagger is a sustained reeling (from blows, drink, or weakness) that continues in motion, while stumble is a momentary loss of footing within otherwise possible movement.
  • Totter: Similar because both describe precarious upright motion; differs because totter emphasizes fragile teetering as if about to collapse (of frailty, old age, a tower), with no specific foot-catch involved.
  • Falter: Similar because both imply a loss of steadiness, physical or figurative; differs because falter stresses hesitation and loss of momentum (of voice, heart, resolve) without the literal tripping image.
  • Lurch: Similar because both are abrupt unsteady movements; differs because lurch is a sudden sideways or forward jerk (of a person, ship, or vehicle) with no implication of catching the foot.
  • Flounder: Similar because both are clumsy, struggling motion; differs because flounder suggests prolonged thrashing confusion, often in water or mud or an argument — a whole desperate struggle rather than a single misstep.
  • Blunder: Similar because both can mean "to err clumsily"; differs because blunder is a foolish, conspicuously inept mistake (a howler), while stumble-as-error is lighter, accidental, and free of stupidity.
  • Err: Similar because both mean to go wrong; differs because err is formal and purely abstract (moral or logical deviation) with no bodily metaphor at all.
  • Tumble: Similar because both are falls or near-falls; differs because tumble means actually falling and rolling over — the outcome a stumble may lead to, not the loss of balance itself.
  • Slip: Similar because both are losses of footing; differs because slip is a loss of traction (ice, a wet floor) with the feet sliding out from under, while stumble is catching the foot on an obstacle.
  • Misstep: Similar because both name a wrong step, literal or figurative; differs because misstep is a single contained placement error (or a single social or strategic error), much narrower than stumble's full range.
  • Wobble / Teeter: Similar because both describe unsteady balance; differs because they denote oscillation more or less in place, while stumble implies motion with a near-fall, and they lack stumble's moral and serendipity senses.
  • Reel: Similar because both are staggering movements; differs because reel implies dizziness and swaying (from a blow, intoxication, or shock) with no tripping involved.
  • Stammer / Stutter: Similar because both relate to troubled speech, as in "stumble over one's words"; differs because they name speech disorders of blocked or repeated sounds, while stumble-for-speech is a momentary, recoverable trip of the tongue.
  • Hesitate: Similar because both imply a pause or loss of smoothness; differs because hesitate is purely mental delay with no physical image and none of stumble's accidental-discovery sense.
