• Legion: Similar because both name large disciplined bodies of soldiers; differs because a legion is a Roman administrative unit (~5,000 men of all arms), while a phalanx is specifically the dense shoulder-to-shoulder fighting formation.
  • Battalion: Similar because both are massed bodies of troops acting as one; differs because a battalion is a modern organisational unit (~500–1,000 soldiers), while a phalanx describes the formation's shape and density, ancient or metaphorical.
  • Column: Similar because both are ordered military formations on the move; differs because a column is long and narrow (marching order), while a phalanx is broad and deep (battle order).
  • Rank and file: Similar because both evoke ordinary soldiers standing in ordered lines; differs because rank and file stresses the common members versus leaders, while phalanx stresses the united front itself.
  • Shield wall: Similar because both are locked-shield infantry walls; differs because the shield wall is the Germanic/Viking/English formation (Hastings, 1066), while the phalanx is the Greek pike-block — cousins across a millennium.
  • Array: Similar because both describe an ordered arrangement (of troops, or things); differs because array is general and static (battle array, data array), while phalanx is dense, deep, and formidable.
  • Host: Similar because both can mean a great body of armed people; differs because host is a vast, often biblical multitude, while phalanx is a tight, disciplined block.
  • Flock: Similar because Tolkien's phalanx of swans is literally a flock; differs because flock is loose and natural, while phalanx imposes military order and menace on the birds.
  • Bloc: Similar because both name united fronts acting together (‘a voting bloc’); differs because bloc is political and modern, while phalanx carries ancient martial weight.
