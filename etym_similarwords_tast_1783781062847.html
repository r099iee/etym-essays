• Flavour: Similar because both name the sensory quality perceived when food or drink is in the mouth; differs because flavour is the objective property of the food itself (its overall chemical–sensory profile, taste plus smell), while taste is the human act, sense, or judgement.
  • Savour: Similar because both denote the appreciation of flavour; differs because savour implies lingering, unhurried, delighted enjoyment of a taste or experience, whereas taste is the neutral act or faculty of perception.
  • Palate: Similar because both relate to the sense of taste; differs because palate literally names the roof of the mouth and metaphorically one's acquired standard of judgement ("a refined palate"), while taste is the gustatory sense or aesthetic faculty itself.
  • Discernment: Similar because both can mean refined judgement of quality; differs because discernment stresses acute intellectual or perceptual discrimination, while taste stresses a culturally formed sense of what is pleasing or proper.
  • Judgement: Similar because both name the capacity to evaluate; differs because judgement is general and intellectual, applicable to any decision, while taste is specifically aesthetic or preferential.
  • Preference: Similar because both can name personal inclination ("not to my taste"); differs because preference is an explicit ranking of alternatives, while taste is the underlying disposition that produces preferences.
  • Sample / sampling: Similar because both involve taking a small amount to test; differs because sampling is the deliberate procedure of testing a portion, while taste is both the act and the resulting sensation.
  • Experience: Similar because "a taste of X" means a brief experience of X; differs because experience is the general, neutral term for anything undergone, while taste implies a short, often tantalising sample.
  • Relish: Similar because both denote enjoyment of flavour; differs because relish emphasises hearty, appreciative pleasure, while taste may be purely sensory and value-free.
  • Testing / trial: Similar because to taste can be to test or try; differs because testing is systematic or scientific evaluation, while tasting is sensory, informal, and gustatory.
  • Zest / tang: Similar because both name a sharp, piquant flavour; differs because zest and tang describe a particular lively quality of the flavour itself, not the sense or act of perceiving it.
