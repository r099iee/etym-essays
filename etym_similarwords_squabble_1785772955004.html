• Bicker: Similar because both name minor, often irritating verbal conflict; differs because bicker stresses ongoing, back-and-forth nagging friction, while a squabble is typically framed as a discrete noisy episode (though repeated squabbles amount to bickering).
  • Quarrel: Similar because both name verbal or interpersonal disagreement; differs because quarrel is the general cover term and can be grave, justified, or long-running without ridicule, while squabble always adds pettiness and undignified noise.
  • Spat: Similar because both are brief, minor falling-outs; differs because spat often involves intimates and need not be loud or public, while squabble emphasizes noisy, slightly ridiculous wrangling.
  • Row (esp. BrE): Similar because both are loud disputes; differs because a row can involve real grievance and hurt feelings, while calling it a squabble deliberately shrinks those stakes.
  • Tiff: Similar because both are light disagreements; differs because tiff is softer and often private or romantic, while squabble is harsher and more openly contemptuous of the participants' maturity.
  • Altercation: Similar because both name a verbal conflict; differs because altercation is more formal and neutral and can be serious, while squabble is informal and belittling.
  • Feud: Contrast term — similar in being a sustained conflict; differs because a feud is bitter, long-lasting, and often lethal, the opposite pole from a squabble's triviality.
  • Debate / Argument: Similar as forms of spoken dispute; differs by implying reasoned exchange of views, while squabble names noisy, petty wrangling without reasoned exchange.
