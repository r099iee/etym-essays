PIE *gʰrebʰ- "to scratch, dig, carve" — PROPOSED root for OE grāf; per the OED the origin of Old English grāf is UNCERTAIN (reconstructed; no written records) → Proto-Germanic *grabaną "to dig, carve, bury" — reconstructed; reflected in Old English grafan, Old High German graban, Gothic graban, Old Norse grafa → Old English grafan "to dig, carve, engrave" — attested c. 8th–11th c. (source of English grave v. "to engrave") → Old English grāf (n.) "grove, thicket" — attested c. 8th–11th c.; of uncertain origin, possibly "a dug/hollowed place" or "a place of branches" → Middle English grove — attested 12th–15th c. → English GROVE (noun, "small wood, group of trees") — current Note on debate: The OED states that Old English grāf "grove" has no certain cognates beyond English (only a possible Old Frisian grēf) and that its origin is unknown. The chain above is therefore best understood as a hypothesis, not an established lineage. The link to PIE *gʰrebʰ- "to scratch, dig" goes through the Germanic "dig" family (grave v., engrave, and the homograph grāf "furrow, ditch" → groove "a channel cut in a surface"); a rival theory connects grāf with a base meaning "branch, sprout" (compare Latin germen "shoot, branch"), making a grove literally "a place of branches." Either way the word is native Germanic, not a Latin or Romance loan.
