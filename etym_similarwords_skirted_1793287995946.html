• Bordered: Similar because both describe lying along the edge of an area; differs because bordered is the neutral general term for any shared boundary, while skirted pictures a fringe or hem running lightly round the outside.
  • Fringed: Similar because both evoke a decorative rim of trees, light, or cloth; differs because fringed stresses the tasselled ornamental edge itself, while skirted stresses the enclosing relationship of edge to centre.
  • Flanked: Similar because both place features along the sides of something; differs because flanked is military and symmetrical (flanked by guards, by towers), while skirted is softer and often partial or irregular.
  • Edged: Similar because both name the boundary line of a thing; differs because edged is sharp and precise (edged with steel, edged in black), while skirted is broad and flowing, a margin with width.
  • Rimmed: Similar because both circle an opening or hollow; differs because rimmed suits cups, craters, and spectacles with crisp circular lips, while skirted suits vales and towns with spreading irregular borders.
  • Bounded: Similar because both mark the limits of an area; differs because bounded is abstract and mathematical (bounded by rivers, by conditions), while skirted is visual and concrete, something you can walk beside.
  • Bypassed: Similar because both avoid going through the middle; differs because bypassed is modern and infrastructural (bypassed by a motorway), while skirted keeps the traveller close to the edge, still in sight of it.
  • Circled: Similar because both move round an outside; differs because circled implies a full loop returning to the start, while skirted implies passing by along one side on the way elsewhere.
  • Evaded: Similar because in the figurative sense both dodge the point; differs because evaded stresses clever escape from duty or capture, while skirted stresses staying near the edge without entering, whether from caution or courtesy.
