Chain for EXUDED
1. Proto-Indo-European *sweid- (also written *swid-, *swoid-), 'to sweat, to ooze, to drip' — reconstructed; c. 4500–3000 BCE (one of the rare roots whose meaning survived almost unchanged in three great branches; the *w produced different initial clusters in each daughter)
2. Latin sūdāre, 'to sweat' — 3rd c. BCE (from *sweid-ā-; Latin lost the *w after s before a following vowel, so *swoid- became sūd-; the source of sudor, 'sweat,' sudation, sudarium, sūdātōrium)
3. Latin exsūdāre (also written exūdāre), 'to sweat out, to ooze forth' — 1st c. BCE (the prefix ex-, 'out,' + sūdāre)
4. Latin exsūdātus, 'sweated out, oozed' — past participle — 1st c. CE
5. Old French exsuder / exuder, 'to exude, to sweat out' — 12th c. CE (borrowed from Latin)
6. English exude, 'to ooze out, to sweat out' — 1570s CE (borrowed from Latin via French)
7. Modern English exuded, past tense of exude — present day

Note: The cognate branches are direct witnesses to the root's meaning: Vedic Sanskrit svēdá (स्वेद), 'sweat,' giving Hindi pasīnā (पसीना); Avestan xvaēδa-; Ancient Greek hidrṓs (ἱδρώς), 'sweat,' giving English hidrosis, hyperhidrosis, anhidrosis. These are siblings, not ancestors, of exude.
