UPSHOT is a compound of up + shot, each with its own complete derivation chain.

CHAIN 1 — the UP element:
Stage 1: PIE *upó, meaning “under, below; up from under” (Indo-European particle/preposition, c. 4000–3000 BCE).
Stage 2: Proto-Germanic *upp “up, upward” (zero-grade of the particle, c. 500 BCE – 200 CE).
Stage 3: Old English upp, up “up, upward, aloft” (attested from c. 8th–11th c. CE).
Stage 4: Middle English up(pe) “up, upwards” (c. 1100–1500 CE).
Stage 5: Modern English up “in or toward a higher place; aloft” (1500–present).
Stage 6: Compound, c. 1530 — up + shot = upshot “the final shot of an archery match,” already generalising to “final result” by the 1570s.

CHAIN 2 — the SHOT element:
Stage 1: PIE *skeud-, meaning “to shoot, hurl, throw, chase” (Pokorny, c. 4000–3000 BCE).
Stage 2: Proto-Germanic *skeutaną “to shoot, to hurl” (strong verb, c. 500 BCE – 200 CE).
Stage 3: Old English scēotan “to shoot, dart, fly swiftly” (c. 8th–11th c. CE).
Stage 4: Old English (ġe)scēot “a shot, a shooting; a discharge” (nominal formation, c. 1000 CE).
Stage 5: Middle English shot “a shot; the discharge of a missile; a projectile” (c. 1150–1500 CE).
Stage 6: Modern English shot “a discharge of a weapon; a projectile; a single attempt” (1500–present).
Stage 7: Compound with up, c. 1530 — upshot “the final shot in an archery match,” then “final result” (1570s).

Debated / nuanced points: The Germanic and Baltic branches alone descend securely from *skeud-; no Hellenic, Italic, Celtic, Indo-Aryan, or Slavic “shoot” words are attested from this root (Greek and Vedic words for “shoot” belong to other roots), and in Baltic the root shifted metaphorically to “pain” (Lithuanian skaudėti “to hurt, to ache”). *upó, by contrast, has rich reflexes in Sanskrit, Greek, Italic, and Germanic, but only the Germanic branch feeds English up. The compound’s first recorded sense is archery (1530s); the abstract “final result” sense is attested by the 1570s. Nothing between *upó / *skeud- and upshot is guessed; every stage is a documented form.
