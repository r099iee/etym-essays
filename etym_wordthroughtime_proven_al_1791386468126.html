- Roman Provincia - a proud administrative label for the first conquered province of Gaul, not yet a regional identity - Provençal begins as “of the province”
- Medieval Proensa - the county and marquisate of Provence, and the prestigious literary language of the troubadours - the word carries courtly and artistic glamour across Europe
- Sixteenth to eighteenth centuries - French centralisation reduces Provençal to a regional vernacular - the label acquires a rustic, local, slightly inferior connotation
- Nineteenth-century Félibrige revival - Mistral and Roumanille turn “Provençal” into a banner of literary pride - the word becomes romantic, nostalgic and politically charged
- Twentieth century - schooling and mass media stigmatise the language as a patois - “Provençal” can sound old-fashioned or merely touristic
- Contemporary usage - heritage language, bilingual schools, festivals and regional branding - the word now signals authenticity, sunshine and cultural resistance
