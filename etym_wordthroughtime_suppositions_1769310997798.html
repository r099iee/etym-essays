- 15th c. (Middle English): from Old French "supposition", from Latin "suppositio" ("a placing under") — a technical term of scholastic logic for positing a hypothesis beneath an argument.
- 16th–17th c.: widens from formal logic to general educated speech — an assumption taken as the basis of reasoning; begins to sound provisional and untested.
- 18th–19th c.: common in prose for a tentatively "set-down" idea used to explain things, often marked as ingenious but unproven (Austen's "ingenious suppositions").
- Contemporary: "supposition" now reads as formal and bookish, almost forensic; everyday speech prefers "assumption", "theory", or "guess", giving it a scholarly, cautious flavour.
