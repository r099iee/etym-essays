Note: In 1920, Swiss architect Charles-Édouard Jeanneret adopted the artistic pseudonym Le Corbusier in Paris for his polemical essays in the avant-garde journal L'Esprit Nouveau. The name was adapted from his maternal grandmother's family surname, Lecorbésier (a regional Franco-Swiss variant of Old French corbesier / corveisier, "shoemaker or cordwainer", derived from cordoan "leather of Córdoba", cognate with English cordwainer). Jeanneret was also delighted by its humorous phonetic similarity to French corbeau ("raven", from Latin corvus), frequently drawing self-portraits with an avian profile, sharp nose, and round spectacles.
