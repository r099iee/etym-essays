• Swoosh: Similar because both are imitative sounds of air in motion; differs because swoosh is a broader, deeper, longer and fuller rush of air, while swish is thinner, faster, and more sibilant.
  • Whish: Similar because it is swish's nearest kin, a sw- rustling sound of movement; differs because whish is a lighter, more abrupt rustle, without swish's sense of a definite sweeping stroke.
  • Swash: Similar in the sw- onset and in being imitative; differs because swash is reserved for liquid splashing or surging against a surface, while swish is the sound of a body cutting through air.
  • Hiss: Similar because both are sibilant noises; differs because a hiss is a prolonged sound made by a steady breath (as of a snake or steam), with no motion, while swish is produced by rapid movement through the air.
  • Whistle: Similar because both are sounds made by air; differs because a whistle is a musical, pitched tone, while swish is an unpitched friction sound of a moving body.
  • Smack: Similar because both belong to the whip's sound-pair "swish, smack"; differs because smack is the sharp, flat sound of impact, while swish is the preceding sound of the stroke in flight.
  • Whoosh: Similar because it is an emphatic variant of swish; differs because whoosh is louder and more drawn-out, often of a large body of air, while swish is a quick, thin hiss.
  • Rustle: Similar because both are soft sounds of movement; differs because a rustle is a dry, crackling sound of leaves or paper, while swish is a smooth, sibilant sweep.
