- 1st c. BCE-1st c. CE Classical Latin sublimis - signified physical loftiness, soaring in the air, and grand rhetorical style; architectural and poetic
- 1200s-1400s Medieval alchemy & scholasticism - designated the chemical and mystical purification of matter by vaporizing solids into spirits; alchemical and transformative
- 1674-1750s Neoclassical revival of Longinus - transformed into a literary ideal of language that enraptures and overwhelms the audience (Boileau, Milton); rhetorical and dramatic
- 1750s-1850s Burke & Kantian Romantic philosophy - established as a central aesthetic category of awe, terror, infinite nature, and divine sovereignty (Wordsworth, Turner, Tolstoy); philosophical and awe-inspiring
- 1900s-present Modern polysemy - dual tracks of rigorous chemical sublimation (solid to gas) alongside universal praise for exquisite sensory perfection and ironic wit ("from the sublime to the ridiculous"); scientific and versatile
