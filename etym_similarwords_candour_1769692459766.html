• Frankness: Similar because both express the truth plainly; differs because frankness is bold, unvarnished, sometimes blunt delivery of facts, while candour in its classic sense was a warm, benevolent fairness of judgement that avoided harsh confrontation.
  • Openness: Similar because both suggest transparent, unreserved expression; differs because openness emphasizes receptiveness and accessibility to others, while candour emphasizes honest, fearless expression of one's own view.
  • Honesty: Similar because both concern truthfulness; differs because honesty is the broad virtue of not deceiving in word or deed, while candour is the specific, active willingness to speak one's mind openly.
  • Directness: Similar because both mean speaking without evasion; differs because directness is a neutral efficiency of communication - getting to the point - while candour carries moral overtones of honesty and fairness of heart.
  • Bluntness: Similar because both describe plain, unvarnished speech; differs because bluntness is the sharp, sometimes tactless edge of directness, whereas candour may be warm and kindly even when frank.
  • Straightforwardness: Similar because both name plainness of manner; differs because straightforwardness describes an uncomplicated, uncalculated approach, while candour specifically involves the act of telling the truth openly.
  • Sincerity: Similar because both involve genuineness; differs because sincerity is authenticity of feeling (not faking), while candour is active verbal openness regardless of warmth.
  • Truthfulness: Similar because both are about telling the truth; differs because truthfulness is the virtue of accuracy in statements, while candour adds the connotation of boldly volunteering the truth.
  • Outspokenness: Similar because both involve speaking one's mind; differs because outspokenness emphasizes fearlessness and sometimes agitation, while candour is calmer and more considered.
  • Ingenuousness: Similar because both suggest innocent openness; differs because ingenuousness implies naivety and childlike candour, while candour implies conscious virtue.
  • Probity: Similar because both are virtues of character; differs because probity is strict moral integrity in conduct, while candour is specifically openness in expression.
  • Fairness / impartiality: Similar to the 18th-century candour of judgement (freedom from bias); differs because fairness/impartiality is judicial even-handedness, while candour's classic sense was a generous inclination to think well of others.
