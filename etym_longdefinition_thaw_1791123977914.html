Thaw as a verb means to melt from a frozen solid state into liquid or softened state by warming, and figuratively to become warm, genial, friendly, or relaxed after coldness, stiffness, or hostility; as a noun it means a spell of warmer weather that melts snow and ice.

The literal meteorological sense is primary: ice thaws, snow thaws, frozen pipes thaw, the ground thaws. It denotes the phase change driven by temperature rising above 0°C, often gradual and welcome after frost. It is thawing means the frost is loosening; a thaw set in means winter is breaking. Dickens's Scrooge didn't thaw it one degree at Christmas uses transitive thaw — to cause to thaw, to warm — negated to stress absolute, season-proof cold: he iced his office in summer heat and refused to warm it even slightly in winter festival.

  • Intransitive melting: the snow thawed by noon, the windshield is thawing. Subject is the frozen thing itself. No agent implied beyond weather. Synonymous with melt but specifically connotes winter ice/snow rather than chocolate or metal melting.
  • Transitive warming/defrosting: to thaw meat, to thaw pipes, to thaw the office. Subject is person or warmth causing melting. Everyday domestic use centres on food safety: thaw in fridge, do not refreeze after thawing. Technical synonym defrost is interchangeable here, though defrost can also mean de-ice a freezer.
  • Figurative softening of persons and relations: to thaw toward guests, to thaw out at a party, a frosty manner thawed. Coldness maps to reserve, hostility, fear; warmth maps to openness, kindness, humour. A person who thaws becomes talkative, smiling, generous. Nations thaw when diplomacy warms: a thaw in relations, the Khrushchev Thaw.
  • Noun — a thaw: a January thaw, spring thaw, rapid thaw caused floods. Countable weather event, relief after freeze but also danger (floods, slush, burst pipes). Contrasts with freeze, frost, cold snap.
  • Phrasal extensions: thaw out emphasizes gradual return to normal temperature or sociability (thaw out the car, thaw out after skiing, thaw out enough to chat). Thaw toward marks direction of new friendliness.

Connotation is almost always positive except when denied. Thawing promises relief, spring, hospitality, peace. To refuse to thaw, like Scrooge, is to refuse humanity. To thaw too fast can flood — hence rapid thaw warnings — but the dominant feeling is welcome loosening after constriction.

Grammar: regular verb thaw/thaws/thawed/thawing; both transitive and intransitive (causative alternation); noun countable. Collocations: thaw slowly, thaw overnight, thaw completely, thaw relations, hopes thawed, smile thawed his reserve. Register is neutral everyday English, equally at home in weather forecasts, cookbooks, and diplomacy.

Domain notes: meteorology distinguishes thaw (air temperature above freezing melting cover) from melt (any solid→liquid); food science distinguishes thawing (controlled) from spoiling; politics uses Thaw capitalized for post-Stalin liberalization; literature uses unthawed hearts as miser trope whose redemption is a thaw.
