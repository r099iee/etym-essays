PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*per- / *pro-
"forward, in front, through, toward oneself"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*pro-pʷo- / *pro-pwo-
"near, close by, one's own"
↓
Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
proprius
"one's own, personal, special, proper"
↓
Latin (abstract noun) (c. 1st c. BCE)
proprietas
"ownership, private property, essential quality"
↓
Late Latin (agent noun) (c. 4th c. CE)
proprietarius
"an owner of property, lawful titleholder"
↓
Old / Middle French (c. 13th–14th c.)
propriétaire
"an owner, landlord, holder of an estate"
↓
Middle / Early Modern English (c. 15th–17th c.)
proprietary / proprietor
"owner, titleholder, business possessor"
↓
English (plural) (modern)
proprietors
"owners, holders of legal title or business"

Note: Middle English originally borrowed the word from Anglo-Norman and Old French in the form proprietary (from Latin proprietarius). During the seventeenth century, under the stylistic impulse to remodel agent nouns directly on Classical Latin nominatives, the form was re-formed with the agent suffix -or as proprietor (plural proprietors), while proprietary was preserved as an adjective and in commercial patent designations.
