• Staring: Similar because both mean looking fixedly at an object for a prolonged period; differs because staring is a neutral, general term that can be thoughtful, blank, rude, or intense, whereas goggling specifically emphasizes wide, bulging, protuberant eyeballs and an expression of foolish amazement, bewilderment, or shock.
  • Gawking: Similar because both denote an unrefined, foolish display of gaping curiosity; differs because gawking emphasizes open-mouthed, clumsy, or socially awkward gawking behavior as a whole, while goggling focuses directly on the physical appearance and rolling movement of the protruding eyes.
  • Glaring: Similar because both involve a fixed, unblinking ocular gaze; differs fundamentally because glaring conveys intense anger, hostility, and fierce aggression, whereas goggling conveys astonishment, fear, curiosity, or bizarre bug-eyed staring.
  • Ogles (Ogling): Similar because both involve looking intently at someone; differs because ogling implies an overtly flirtatious, suggestive, or lecherous gaze, whereas goggling is free of sexual desire and instead reflects sheer visual stupefaction or grotesque curiosity.
  • Gaping: Similar because both describe an involuntary visual reaction to astonishment; differs because gaping primarily describes the wide, slack opening of the mouth, while goggling focuses on the bulging prominence of the eyes.
  • Peering: Similar because both describe looking with concentrated effort; differs because peering suggests squinting or looking closely through darkness, fog, or narrow openings to see clearly, whereas goggling involves wide-open, protruding ocular fixation.
