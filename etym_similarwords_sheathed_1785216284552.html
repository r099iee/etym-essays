• encase: Similar because both enclose something in a protective covering; differs because encase implies a full outer case with no special link to blades or close-fitted weapon covers, while sheathed specifically names the fitted covering of a weapon (and, by extension, of any tight-fitting cover).
  • envelop: Similar because both surround something in a covering; differs because envelop suggests loose wrapping or surrounding on all sides, with no implication of a tight, protective, close-fitting fit.
  • cover: Similar because both hide something away or place something over it; differs because cover is far broader and looser — covering a book, a face, a distance — with no sense of a fitted casing or of concealing a drawn blade.
  • enfold / wrap: Similar because both enclose something in a covering; differ because they imply flexible materials folded about an object (a blanket, paper), where sheathed implies a hard, fitted, purpose-built casing.
  • scabbard (noun): Similar because the scabbard is the sword's sheath; differs because scabbard is the specific term for the rigid, often ornate sword-case, while sheath (and the verb sheathe) is the general word for any blade-cover or close-fitting casing.
  • house (as verb): Similar because both protect something by containing it; differs because house is for buildings, people, or goods, with no sense of a close, fitted cover for a blade.
  • conceal: Similar because both can mean hiding something away; differs because conceal stresses keeping something secret or out of sight, while sheathed specifies the means — a fitted protective covering — and often a blade specifically.
  • draw / unsheathe (opposites): The direct antonyms, both meaning to take the blade out of its covering and restore its menace; a sheathed weapon is dormant, a drawn one is active and threatening.
