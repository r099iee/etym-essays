CHAIN 1 — ROOT WORD "TOOL": PIE root *deu- — "to do, perform, prepare, accomplish"; a verbal root of making ready and working at (chiefly Germanic; distinct from the PIE "do" root *dʰeh₁- of English do, deed, Latin facere) ↓ Proto-Germanic *tawjaną — "to prepare, make, do, work at" (cf. Gothic taujan "to do, make," attested in Wulfila's Gothic Bible, 4th c. CE; Old Norse tœja "to prepare, help") ↓ Old English tawian — "to prepare, to dress (especially leather)" (attested c. 8th–11th c.; survives in the dialect verb taw "to make leather pliable by soaking and rubbing") ↓ Old English tōl — "an implement, instrument," literally "that which is used in preparing/working" (nominalized from the same base; the Old Norse sister form is tól) ↓ Middle English tool / toole — "an instrument of any craft" (c. 1200; generalized from the craftsman's sense to any implement of work) ↓ Modern English tool (noun) — "an instrument for working"; verb to tool (to work with a tool), to tool around (slang, to drive, 20th c.); compounds toolbox, toolkit, machine-tool ↓ English compound toolshed — "a shed for storing tools" (19th c.; with the noun shed, see CHAIN 2) — THE CARD WORD NOTE: *deu- is distinct from the great PIE "do" root *dʰeh₁- (English do, deed; Latin facere); tool is a specifically Germanic formation, with no direct Romance cognate. CHAIN 2 — ROOT WORD "SHED": PIE root *skot- — "darkness, shade" (secured by Greek σκότος skótos "darkness," Old Irish scáth "shadow, protection," and the Germanic family) ↓ Proto-Germanic *skadwaz — "shadow, shade, darkness" (cf. Old Saxon skado, Old High German scato → German Schatten; Old Norse skuggi "shadow," an irregular sister form) ↓ Old English sceadu — "shade, shadow, darkness," also "protection, covering, shelter" (attested c. 8th–11th c.) ↓ Middle English schade / shadde — "shade, a sheltering cover, a shadow" (c. 12th–15th c.) ↓ English shed (noun) — an ELLIPTICAL use of "shade": "a place of shade" → "a light shelter" (c. 1480; a slight building for shelter or storage) ↓ English compound toolshed — "a shed for storing tools" (19th c.; with the noun tool, see CHAIN 1) — THE CARD WORD NOTE: this noun shed (from shade) is a different word from the verb shed "to cast off" (Old English sceadan "to separate," of the root that also gave scissors and schism). No debated stage exists in the shade lineage; the ellipsis from "shade" to "shelter" is securely attested.
