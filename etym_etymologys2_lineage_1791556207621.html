lignage

↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
linage / lynage

↓
Modern English (15th c.–present)
lineage
"lineal descent from an ancestor; pedigree"

Note: Derived from Old French lignage, formed from ligne ('line', from Latin linea) + noun suffix -age (denoting collection or status). The spelling was reformed in Early Modern English to lineage to visibly reflect the base word line. The semantic leap originated with the carpenter's linen measuring line (plumb line) serving as a metaphor for a direct, straight generational cord.
