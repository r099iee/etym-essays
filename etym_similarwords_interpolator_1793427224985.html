• Forger: Similar because both produce fraudulent, deceptive written material; differs because a forger typically fabricates an entire fake document, signature, or work from scratch, whereas an interpolator inserts spurious passages into an otherwise genuine text.
  • Tamperer: Similar because both illicitly alter existing material; differs because tamperer is a broad, non-textual term that applies to physical machinery, ballot boxes, or witnesses, whereas interpolator has a precise historical home in textual corruption and data calculation.
  • Corrupter: Similar because both degrade the original purity of a work; differs because a corrupter can alter through deletion, mutilation, moral decay, or ideological perversion, whereas an interpolator specifically corrupts by insertion and addition.
  • Falsifier: Similar because both introduce untrue or deceptive elements; differs because falsifier emphasizes the deceitful, untrue intent of altering accounts or records, whereas interpolator describes the specific mechanical act of inserting foreign elements into an authentic sequence.
  • Editor: Similar because both insert, alter, or emend words in a text; differs because an editor works legitimately to refine, correct, or prepare an author's text for publication, whereas an interpolator (in philology) acts without authority, often introducing alien or anachronistic intrusions.
  • Interloper: Similar because both represent an unwanted intrusion into a protected space; differs because an interloper is an intruder into a physical place, social circle, or commercial monopoly, whereas an interpolator intrudes into a text, conversation, or data stream.
  • Fabricator: Similar because both invent spurious material; differs because a fabricator invents whole tales, lies, or myths from whole cloth, whereas an interpolator weaves invented matter between authentic threads.
  • Intrusive emender: Similar because both alter texts under the guise of improvement; differs because an emender seeks (even if misguidedly) to restore original readings from corruption, whereas an interpolator introduces alien matter not part of the original archetype.
  • Polynomial estimator (in technical contexts): Similar because both compute intermediate mathematical values; differs because estimator is an abstract statistical term for finding approximate parameters, whereas mathematical interpolator specifically fits a curve passing strictly through every known discrete point.
  • Upsampler: Similar in digital signal processing because both increase data density between sample points; differs because upsampler refers to the clock rate expansion and filter hardware, while interpolator describes the mathematical algorithm calculating the inserted values.
