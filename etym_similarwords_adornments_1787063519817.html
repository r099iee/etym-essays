• Ornaments: Similar because both mean decorative objects added for beauty; differs because ornament is the everyday near-synonym and can also mean a person who adds distinction ("an ornament to his profession"), while adornment more strictly means the added decoration and the act of adding it.
  • Decorations: Similar because both name things that beautify; differs because decorations is broader (banners, lights, bunting) and often temporary or festive, whereas adornments are usually permanent, refined, and personal or architectural.
  • Embellishments: Similar because both mean added beautifying details; differs because embellishments often means details added to stories, music, or writing, while adornments are more often physical objects; embellish also allows the sense of exaggeration.
  • Trimmings: Similar because both mean added marginal decorations; differs because trimmings are the fringes, borders, and edgings on cloth or the garnish on food — lighter, more superficial, less precious.
  • Finery: Similar because both describe beautiful dress and jewels; differs because finery is a collective noun for showy best clothes and is inseparable from attire, while adornments may decorate buildings and objects too.
  • Trappings: Similar because both mean outward decorative additions; differs because trappings are the functional accoutrements of office or rank (the trappings of power), often suggesting superficial display, whereas adornments are simply things that beautify.
  • Accoutrements: Similar because both mean accessories added to a whole; differs because accoutrements are functional gear or equipment (often military), not necessarily decorative at all.
  • Frippery: Similar because both mean showy decoration; differs because frippery is contemptuous — trivial, fussy ornament — while adornments are neutral or admiring.
  • Garnishes: Similar because both mean things added to decorate; differs because garnish is confined to food and drink presentation (the legal sense of garnishment is unrelated).
