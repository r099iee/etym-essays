- 17th–18th c. 'Exotic luxury cotton from the East' — named after Mosul in present-day Iraq and first imported as a precious, almost magical fabric; the finest Indian muslins from Dacca were famous as 'woven air' and sold at princely prices.
- 19th c. 'Democratized everyday cotton' — industrial manufacture makes muslin cheap and widely available; it becomes the humble, workaday cloth of ordinary clothing, undergarments, and household use, shedding its aura of Eastern luxury.
- 20th–21st c. 'Practical, utilitarian textile' — a neutral, matte cotton weave used for dressmakers' practice garments, curtains, medical gauze substitutes, and theatrical backdrops; the register is plain and functional rather than sumptuous.
