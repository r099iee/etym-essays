• Bound: Similar because both report that ropes or bonds hold someone fast; differs because bound is neutral and generic, stating only that bonds exist, with no sense of how or where the limbs are fixed.
  • Shackled: Similar because both immobilise a captive; differs because shackled implies metal fetters or chains, usually about the ankles, with a penal or maritime overtone rather than the tight pinning of the arms.
  • Manacled: Similar because both restrain a prisoner; differs because manacled refers specifically to handcuffs or metal rings on the wrists and belongs to the cold, official language of arrest.
  • Fettered: Similar because both fasten the limbs against motion; differs because fettered names chains or shackles on the feet, restricting walking rather than the arms and wings of pinioned.
  • Tethered: Similar because both fasten so that one cannot move freely; differs because tethered suggests a slack, animal-like connection to a fixed point — a beast at pasture — not the complete immobilisation of the limbs.
  • Chained: Similar because both secure a captive physically; differs because chained stresses the material means (a metal chain) and can be loose, while pinioned stresses tight binding of the arms themselves.
  • Tied: Similar because both describe fastening by cord or rope; differs because tied is the everyday, non-technical word with no specific limb, whereas pinioned names the arms-or-wings binding.
  • Handcuffed: Similar because both involve restraint of the hands or arms; differs because handcuffed is specifically police procedure with metal cuffs at the wrists, without the wing-clipping cruelty of pinioned.
  • Restrained: Similar because both prevent free movement; differs because restrained is a broad, neutral term covering any limitation, while pinioned is precise about the limbs and the degree of immobilisation.
  • Secured: Similar because both make escape impossible; differs because secured is a general term for making safe (a secured door, a secured border), without the bodily specificity of pinioned.
  • Lashed: Similar because both involve fastening by cord; differs because lashed stresses the tight wrapping of rope (as to a mast) and often doubles as the verb of whipping, whereas pinioned carries the wing-metaphor.
  • Trussed: Similar because both bind limbs tightly to the body; differs because trussed belongs to fowls being prepared for the oven — deliberately comic or brutal — while pinioned keeps the falconer's dignity.
  • Hobbled: Similar because both limit a captive's movement; differs because hobbled means tying the feet loosely so that short steps are possible (a horse is hobbled), the opposite of the total immobilisation of pinioned.
  • Immobilised: Similar because both render motion impossible; differs because immobilised is a clinical, mechanical term that can describe a car or a leg in a cast, lacking the human-and-bird cruelty of pinioned.
