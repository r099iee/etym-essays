• Case: Similar because both denote a protective container or receptacle; differs because case is an ordinary, generic term for any box, luggage, or sheath, whereas quiver is specialized exclusively for holding arrows, darts, or crossbow bolts.
  • Sheath: Similar because both are close-fitting protective coverings for bladed or pointed weapons; differs because a sheath (or scabbard) encloses the blade of a single sword, dagger, or knife, whereas a quiver holds a collective bundle of loose projectile arrows.
  • Arsenal: Similar because both represent a collection or repository of ready weapons; differs because an arsenal is a massive military storehouse, armoury, or comprehensive stockpile of diverse arms, whereas a quiver is a small, portable personal container carried on an archer's body.
  • Scabbard: Similar because both protect edged weaponry from the elements; differs because a scabbard is a rigid holster tailored specifically to house the steel blade of a sword, while a quiver is an open-topped vessel designed for rapid manual arrow extraction.
  • Holster: Similar because both are body-worn carriers for portable missile weapons; differs because a holster is designed to hold a handgun or firearm securely at the hip or shoulder, whereas a quiver holds arrows for a bow.
  • Tremor: Similar in the homophonic sense of a rapid shaking movement; differs because tremor is a heavier, more seismic, or pathological shaking (as in an earthquake or Parkinsonian tremor), whereas a quiver is a light, delicate, high-frequency flutter.
  • Shudder: Similar in the homophonic sense of an involuntary bodily vibration; differs because a shudder is a convulsive, full-body spasm typically caused by intense cold, horror, or profound revulsion, whereas a quiver is subtle, localized, and often associated with anticipation or delicate emotion.
  • Shiver: Similar in the homophonic sense of trembling; differs because a shiver is primarily a thermoregulatory physical reaction to low temperature, while a quiver frequently involves delicate psychological vibration, fluttering lips, or vibrating strings.
