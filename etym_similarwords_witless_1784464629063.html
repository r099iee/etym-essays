• Stupid: Similar because both mean lacking intelligence; differs because stupid is the plain, informal default for slow thinking, while witless is stronger and more archaic, implying a mind emptied rather than merely slow.
  • Foolish: Similar because both mean lacking sense or good judgment; differs because foolish criticises judgment in a specific matter, while witless describes the state of the mind itself.
  • Senseless: Similar because both mean without sense, foolish or meaningless; differs because senseless can also mean unconscious (knocked senseless), a physical sense witless lacks.
  • Brainless: Similar because both mean lacking intelligence; differs because brainless is informal, dismissive, and modern, without witless's archaic, serious gravity.
  • Empty-headed: Similar because both describe a vacant mind; differs because empty-headed is colloquial and comic, while witless can be tragic and clinical.
  • Mindless: Similar because both mean without mind or thought; differs because mindless usually describes automatic or unthinking activity, while witless describes a person's state.
  • Dazed: Similar because both can describe a stupefied, unresponsive state; differs because dazed is purely the temporary stunned state with no judgment of intelligence, while witless also covers folly.
  • Stupefied: Similar because both describe someone robbed of the power to think; differs because stupefied emphasises the external cause (shock, drink), while witless emphasises the absent faculty.
  • Bewildered: Similar because both can describe mental confusion; differs because bewildered is confusion in a specific, recoverable situation, while witless implies the mind lacking its power altogether.
  • Daft: Similar because both mean foolish or silly; differs because daft is British, dialectal-informal, and lighter, with none of witless's seriousness.
  • Unwise: Similar because both involve a lack of good sense; differs because unwise judges a choice as imprudent, while witless condemns the mind's state.
  • Humourless: Similar because both can be heard (via wit = humour) as lacking cleverness or fun; differs because humourless names the lack of humour specifically, while witless names the lack of intellect.
