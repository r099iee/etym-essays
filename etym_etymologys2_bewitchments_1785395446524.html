Linear derivation chain for BEWITCHMENTS (root: witch): 1. PIE *weyk- 'to set apart, consecrate' (contested reconstruction; the root behind both 'holy' and 'magic' words in some branches) — DEBATED, see note below. 2. Proto-Germanic *wikkjaz 'necromancer, one who works magic' (reconstructed; cf. Old Frisian wicka, Middle Low German wicken). 3. Old English wicca (m.) 'male wizard, sorcerer' / wicce (f.) 'female witch' (attested from the 9th c. in Anglo-Saxon laws and homilies). 4. Middle English wicche 'witch, wizard' (used of both genders, from the 12th c.). 5. Modern English witch 'one who works magic'. 6. bewitch v. — be- + witch, mid-16th c.; the intensive prefix be- from Proto-Germanic *bi, ultimately PIE *h₁embʰi 'around, about' (cf. beset, bedazzle). 7. bewitchment n. — bewitch + -ment, 17th c.; the suffix -ment from Latin -mentum via Old French. 8. BEWITCHMENTS (plural count form, denoting a body or repertoire of such acts). Debate note: the link of wicca to PIE *weyk- 'consecrate' (cf. Gothic weihs 'holy', Old High German wīhen 'to consecrate', Latin victima 'sacrificial victim') is attractive but contested — the OED marks the origin of wicca as uncertain, and the connection is a reconstruction, not an attested fact. The homophonous roots *weyk- 'to bend, winnow' (Latin vices, English vetch) and *weyk- 'to overcome' (Latin vincere) are different roots and are deliberately not merged into this chain. No Indo-Aryan cognates are attested; the family is western Indo-European only.
