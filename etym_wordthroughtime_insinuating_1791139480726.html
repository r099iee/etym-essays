- 1520s borrowing - to insinuate: to creep or push in physically, to worm into favour; neutral-technical and courtly
- 1600s rhetorical sense - to introduce an idea gently when audience hostile; praised as tactful exordium
- 1700s hinting sense dominant - to insinuate a charge means to imply something bad without proof; growing distrust, sly and unmanly
- 1800s adjectival personality - insinuating fellow, smile, voice as stock villain trait; Uriah Heep figure
- Contemporary dual use - artfully worming into confidence versus slyly implying discredit; mostly pejorative, except neutral medical/literary creeping
