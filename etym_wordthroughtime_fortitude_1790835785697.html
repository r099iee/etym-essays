- c. 1300s-1400s (Middle English): Physical bodily strength, martial prowess, and theological cardinal virtue - Highlighted in chivalric romances and devotional texts.
- c. 1500s-1700s (Renaissance & Enlightenment): Philosophical ideal of noble, patient endurance of affliction, tragedy, and political tyranny - Central in Stoic revivals and Addisonian essays.
- c. 1800s-1900s: Moral steadfastness, quiet dignity in grief, and steadfast endurance during wartime or systemic adversity.
- c. 2000s-Present: Elevated, formal term for profound emotional and moral resilience in the face of chronic illness, personal disaster, or existential trials.
