PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*wih₁-ró- / *wiro-
"man, free man; hero, strong one"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*wiros
"man, adult male"
↓
Old Latin (c. 5th–3rd c. BCE)
vir
"man, adult male citizen, hero, warrior"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
virtūs (stem virtūt-)
"manliness, masculine strength, martial courage, valor, prowess; excellence, moral worth"
Note: Formed from vir ('man') + abstract quality suffix -tūs (cognate with English -hood/-th). Used by Cicero to translate Greek aretē ('excellence').
↓
Late Latin / Ecclesiastical Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
virtūs
"moral righteousness; divine power, miracle, miraculous efficacy"
↓
Old French (c. 11th–12th c.)
vertu / virtu
"strength, power, miracle, moral excellence, healing property"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200–1250)
vertu / virtue
"divine power, magical efficacy, moral excellence, valor"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
virtue
"moral goodness, righteous conduct; efficacy, beneficial quality, chastity"
Note: Spelling remodeled from Middle English vertu to virtue under the influence of Classical Latin virtus.
↓
Modern English (present)
VIRTUE
