CHAIN 2 OF 2 — the element pot:

Note: the earliest Germanic form may

↓

*puttaz
"pot, vessel"
↓

"or metal"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
pot
"cooking vessel; pot;"
"drinking vessel"
↓
Modern English (19th c.)
chimney-pot
"compound: the pot-shaped"
"pipe of a chimney"
↓
Modern English (present)
CHIMNEY-POT
"terracotta or metal flue"
"terminal on a chimney top"

Debate note (pot): the OED classes Old English pott as of uncertain origin; the commonest scholarly bridge is post-classical Latin pottus “drinking vessel”, itself built on potāre “to drink”, making the Germanic word an early loan — but the direction of the borrowing (Latin into Germanic, or the reverse) is still debated, and an independent echoic origin cannot be excluded.
