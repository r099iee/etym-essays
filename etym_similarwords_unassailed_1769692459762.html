• Unaffected: Similar because both describe remaining untouched by outside influence; differs because unaffected is neutral and may simply mean natural or sincere, whereas unassailed emphasizes having escaped an actual attack.
  • Untouched: Similar because both denote an absence of contact; differs because untouched is broader (untouched food, untouched by pity) and literal, while unassailed is more deliberate and bellicose, implying a hostile force was expected but never arrived.
  • Unimpeached: Similar because both mean standing unchallenged; differs because unimpeached is anchored in law, morality, and official accusation (from impeach), whereas unassailed covers attack from any quarter.
  • Unchallenged: Similar because both mean not called into question; differs because unchallenged often implies a challenge was declined or absent, while unassailed implies no hostile attack was ever mounted.
  • Unscathed: Similar because both imply emerging unharmed; differs because unscathed presupposes that an assault actually occurred and the subject survived it uninjured, while unassailed means the attack never even reached the subject.
  • Invulnerable: Similar because both describe safety from harm; differs because invulnerable implies an innate or permanent capacity to resist (cannot be wounded), while unassailed merely records that no attack has yet landed.
  • Impervious: Similar because both describe impenetrability; differs because impervious emphasizes an inherent resistance to entry (impervious to criticism), while unassailed emphasizes the mere fact of not having been attacked.
  • Undamaged: Similar because both mean free from harm; differs because undamaged is a general physical or statistical term with no implication of siege, while unassailed carries the military-metaphorical frame of an attack that never came.
  • Unshaken: Similar because both mean not destabilized; differs because unshaken implies that a force (shock, doubt) was applied and the subject held firm, while unassailed means no force was applied at all.
  • Unimpugned: Similar because both mean not called into question; differs because impugn is a narrower formal verb of dispute (to challenge as false) and unimpugned stays within that realm, while unassailed includes physical and metaphorical attack.
