Proto-Algonquian (c. 1000 BCE)
*šekâkwa
"skunk; literally 'one who squirts or urinates'"
↓
Massachusett / Abenaki (early 17th c.)
squncke / seganku
"emitting or spraying animal"
↓
Early American English (c. 1634)
squncke
"the foul-spraying American mammal"
↓
Modern English (18th c.)
skunk
"mephitid mammal; despicable person; to shut out"

Note: Derived from Proto-Algonquian *šek- ('to urinate') combined with an animal noun suffix *-a·kw-. First attested in English by William Wood in New England's Prospect (1634) as squncke. The city name Chicago derives from the related Miami-Illinois cognate shikaakwa ('place of the striped skunk / wild leek').
