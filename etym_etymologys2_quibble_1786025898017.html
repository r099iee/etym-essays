Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)

↓
Early Modern English (1620s)
quib
"a petty evasion, pun, or academic quip"
↓
Early Modern English (1670s–present)
quibble
"to evade truth by trivial distinctions"

Note: Etymologists broadly trace quibble to a diminutive/frequentative elaboration of the earlier 17th-century slang noun quib (a pun or evasion). Quib was an academic and legal abbreviation of Latin quibus, parodying the endless formulaic Latin phrases in scholastic pleadings.
