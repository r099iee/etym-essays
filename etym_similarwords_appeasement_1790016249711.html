• conciliation: Similar because it means bringing into harmony and ending hostility; differs because conciliation is more mutual and dignified, while appeasement often implies one-sided yielding.
  • pacification: Similar because it means bringing peace and calming conflict; differs because pacification can imply imposed order by force, while appeasement implies peace by concession.
  • placation: Similar because it means calming anger by pleasing; differs because placation is usually interpersonal and immediate, while appeasement is often political and strategic.
  • propitiation: Similar because it means winning back favour, especially divine; differs because propitiation is religious and sacrificial, while appeasement is secular and diplomatic.
  • mollification: Similar because it means softening anger; differs because mollification stresses softening of feeling, while appeasement stresses satisfaction of demands.
  • assuagement: Similar because it means easing pain or anxiety; differs because assuagement is about soothing suffering, while appeasement is about satisfying a claimant.
  • accommodation: Similar because it means adjusting to meet demands; differs because accommodation can be neutral or positive compromise, while appeasement often carries weakness.
  • concession: Similar because it means yielding something to avoid conflict; differs because concession is the thing yielded, while appeasement is the policy or act of yielding.
  • compromise: Similar because it involves mutual giving to settle dispute; differs because compromise implies both sides yield, while appeasement implies only one side yields to threat.
  • peacemaking: Similar because it aims at peace; differs because peacemaking is honourable mediation, while political appeasement is now dishonourable surrender.
  • satisfaction: Similar because in older senses it means giving what justice demands; differs because satisfaction is now neutral fulfilment, while appeasement retains strategic calculation.
