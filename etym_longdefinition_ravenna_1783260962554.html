Ravenna (proper noun) — A historic city in Emilia-Romagna, north-eastern Italy, standing a few kilometres from the Adriatic coast on a low, marshy plain between the Ronco and Montone rivers. From 402 to the mid-sixth century it was the capital of the Western Roman Empire, then of the Ostrogothic kingdom of Theodoric (493–526), and from 584 to 751 the seat of the Byzantine Exarchate of Ravenna — the last outpost of Roman administration in the West — before its archbishopric became one of the most powerful sees in medieval Italy.

In the source context. Gibbon lists Ravenna among the great continental courts — Paris, Toledo, Arles, and Ravenna — where "German princes" reigned while listening reverently to bishops and debating the Nicene theology, in pointed contrast to the still-pagan rulers of Wessex and Mercia. Ravenna here is an emblem of the fusion of Germanic kingship with Roman and Christian civilization: Theodoric, an Arian Ostrogoth, ruled there in nominal subordination to the eastern emperor and built the great churches whose mosaics still survive.

History. The site was an early settlement of the Umbrian peoples, later drawn into Etruscan and Roman orbit, and was chosen by the Emperor Honorius in 402 as the western capital because its lagoons and marshes made it almost unassailable. It remained the capital under the last western emperors and, after Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus (476), under the Ostrogoths. Belisarius captured it for Justinian in 540; the exarchs who followed governed Italy's surviving Byzantine fragments from Ravenna until the Lombards extinguished the exarchate in 751. Thereafter the city slowly declined, though its churches — San Vitale, Sant'Apollinare Nuovo, the Arian Baptistery, the Mausoleum of Galla Placidia — preserved the most spectacular mosaic cycles of the late antique world. Dante died and was buried at Ravenna in 1321.

Polysemy and later senses.
  • The city itself: the primary sense — a living Italian city, provincial capital, and UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 1996) famous for its mosaics.
  • Metonym for Byzantine mosaic art: in art history, "Ravenna" functions as shorthand for the finest early Christian and Byzantine mosaics of the 5th–6th centuries, and for the imperial patronage that produced them.
  • The Exarchate of Ravenna: in Byzantine and medieval history, the name designates the imperial administrative province that governed Byzantine Italy from 584 to 751.
  • Ravenna (Ohio): an American city in Ohio, named by Connecticut settlers in the 19th century; the toponym has spread as a place-name in the New World.
  • Proper-name extensions: churches, dioceses, and institutions worldwide bear the name (for example the archdiocese of Ravenna-Cervia), and the name recurs in literature as a type of the late antique imperial city.

Semantic bridge. Ravenna is a pre-Roman toponym of uncertain meaning (probably Etruscan or a local Italic/substrate word); its later history — imperial capital, mosaic capital, exarchal seat — is what loaded the mere place-name with its immense historical and artistic resonance in English and every other language.
