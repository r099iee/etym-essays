- Mid-17th Century (c. 1640s) - Introduced into English theological, philosophical, and legal treatises from Late Latin *connātus*; used by Cambridge Platonists to describe innate virtues and ideas coeval with human birth.
- Late 17th Century (c. 1672–1704) - Centralized in natural philosophy by Sir Isaac Newton in his groundbreaking communications and *Opticks*; used to establish that colors are inherent, immutable properties of light rays rather than optical illusions; commanded immense scientific authority.
- 18th to 19th Century - Adopted into taxonomic biology following Linnaean botanical methods; strictly specialized in plant morphology to designate opposite leaves or petals united at the base.
- Early 20th Century - Acquired a major geological definition when hydrologists and petroleum geologists coined the term 'connate water' to identify fossil pore fluids preserved since the original sedimentation era.
- Present Day - Retained as a refined, precise technical and philosophical descriptor in botany, geology, and epistemology, denoting primordial inseparability from inception.
