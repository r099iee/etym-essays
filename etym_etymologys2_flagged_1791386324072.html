Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed)
*plāk- / *plek-
"to strike, beat; flap loosely"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*flak-
"to flap loosely, flutter in wind"
↓
Old Norse / Early Scandinavian (c. 9th–11th c.)
flakka
"to flutter, flap about, rove"
↓
Middle English / Early Scots (c. 14th–15th c.)
flakken / flaggen
"to flap, flutter, hang loosely"
↓
Early Modern English (1540s)
flag
"to hang down limp, droop loosely"
↓
Early Modern English (1570s)
flag
"to lose vigour, grow faint, decline"
↓
Modern English (past tense / participle)
flagged
"drooped, lost vigour, languished"
"(distinct homograph) marked, alerted"
Note: Modern English *flagged* conflates three historically distinct homographic roots: (1) the verb *flag* "to droop" (derived from the Germanic flapping root *flak- above); (2) the verb *flag* "to signal/mark" (derived from the noun *flag* "banner", from Scandinavian/Low German *flag* "fluttering cloth"); and (3) the verb *flag* "to pave" (derived from Old Norse *flaga* "stone slab"). In the source sentence, sense (1) is active.
