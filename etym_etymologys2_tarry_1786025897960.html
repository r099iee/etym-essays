TARRY from Anglo-Norman tarier "to delay, to wait; to vex", ultimately from Latin tardus "slow".

Latin tardus
"slow, sluggish, tardy" - adjective (2nd-1st c. BCE; of disputed origin, possibly non-Indo-European)

▼

Late Latin tardare
"to delay, to make slow, to retard" - denominative verb from tardus (Imperial Latin)

▼

Anglo-Norman tarier
"to delay, to wait; also to vex, to provoke" (12th-13th c.)

▼

Middle English tarien
"to delay, to wait, to linger; also to vex, to weary" (14th c., from Anglo-Norman)

▼

English tarry
"to delay, to linger, to stay or wait" (modern form, with the vexation sense dead by the 17th century)

▼

TARRY
"to delay or be tardy in acting or doing; to linger in expectation" (the base verb on this card)

Debated points: The chain from Latin tardare through Anglo-Norman tarier is the standard account (OED, EtymologieNederlandseWoonordenboek), but scholars note that (1) tardus itself has no secure Indo-European etymology and may be a substrate word; and (2) the Anglo-Norman verb's "vex, irritate" sense may reflect conflation with a separate Gallo-Romance verb, so the exact medieval history is debated. No reliable PIE root is reconstructable for the chain.
