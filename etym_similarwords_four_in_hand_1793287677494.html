• Pair: Similar because both name a driven team of matched horses; differs because a pair is two horses abreast for lighter carriages, while a four-in-hand is two pairs together, twice the power and far more than twice the skill.
  • Tandem: Similar because both are multi-horse hitches driven by one whip; differs because a tandem sets horses one behind the other in single file, while a four-in-hand sets them two-and-two, leaders before wheelers.
  • Unicorn: Similar because both are sporting three-horse-plus hitches of the coaching age; differs because a unicorn teams three horses — a pair with a single leader ahead — while a four-in-hand is the full quartet.
  • Six-in-hand: Similar because both name multi-horse teams in one grip; differs because six-in-hand is the rarer show hitch of six horses, heavier wagons and state occasions, while four-in-hand was the standard sporting and mail-coach team.
  • Drag: Similar because the private drag was the classic four-in-hand carriage; differs because drag names the vehicle — the open sporting coach — while four-in-hand names the team and the driving of it.
  • Coachman: Similar because coachmen and amateurs both drove four-in-hand teams; differs because coachman names the professional servant on the box, while Cobbett's driver of four-in-hand is the gentleman amateur driving for glory.
  • Windsor knot: Similar because both are named necktie knots with club histories; differs because the Windsor is the broad triangular formal knot, while the four-in-hand is the long narrow everyday knot inherited from the drivers.
