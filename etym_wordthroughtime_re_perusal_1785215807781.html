- 15th–17th century (peruse) - “Peruse” means to go through a text thoroughly and completely — to use or wear through it; perusal is careful, complete inspection.
- 18th–19th century - Perusal is the standard bookish word for attentive reading, often of legal and literary documents; the hyphenated compound “re-perusal” stresses the deliberate second, point-by-point pass — Mill's re-perusal of the Iliad and the Odyssey in the original Greek.
- 20th century (drift of peruse) - In ordinary usage “peruse” weakens toward “read casually, skim”, so the root loses intensity; “re-perusal” itself becomes rare, kept in scholarly prose.
- Contemporary - Reads as formal and slightly old-fashioned; it survives where a writer wants the precise sense of a repeated, thorough reading, guarding against the casual drift of “peruse”.
