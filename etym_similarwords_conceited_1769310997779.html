• Vain: Similar because both mean having too high an opinion of oneself; differs because vain emphasises pride in appearance or in the admiration of others ("vain about her looks"), while conceited emphasises a general, inner self-satisfaction with one's own worth.
  • Arrogant: Similar because both denote excessive self-importance; differs because arrogant implies claiming unearned rights and superiority and behaving overbearingly toward others, while conceited is more about smug self-delight than about lording it over people.
  • Egotistical: Similar because both centre on the self; differs because egotistical means habitually talking about and focusing on oneself, while conceited means holding a flattering opinion of oneself.
  • Narcissistic: Similar because both involve self-admiration; differs because narcissistic names a pathological, clinical self-worship and preoccupation with one's own image, while conceited is a mild everyday censure.
  • Proud: Similar because both involve a sense of one's own worth; differs because proud can be neutral or even positive (justified pride, dignity), while conceited always names an exaggerated, disapproved self-regard.
  • Smug: Similar because both denote complacent self-satisfaction; differs because smug stresses a quiet, self-righteous, holier-than-thou satisfaction, while conceited stresses an inflated opinion of one's talents or worth.
  • Haughty: Similar because both imply thinking very well of oneself; differs because haughty stresses disdainful superiority and contempt for those below, while conceited stresses self-satisfaction rather than contempt.
  • Supercilious: Similar because both denote an air of superiority; differs because supercilious describes an overbearing, eyebrow-raising manner, while conceited describes the underlying self-opinion that manner expresses.
  • Self-important: Similar because both mean having an exaggerated sense of one's own importance; differs because self-important emphasises a pompous sense of one's role in the world, while conceited emphasises personal vanity of talent and worth.
  • Pretentious: Similar because both can denote an inflated self-image; differs because pretentious means claiming greater importance or refinement than one actually has (a display aimed at others), while conceited is the inner conviction itself.
  • Pompous: Similar because both imply self-importance; differs because pompous stresses inflated, ceremonious solemnity of manner and speech, while conceited stresses personal self-admiration.
  • Big-headed: Similar because both colloquially mean having too high an opinion of oneself; differs because big-headed is informal slang, while conceited is standard English with a literary pedigree.
