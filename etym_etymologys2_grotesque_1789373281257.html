Ancient Greek (c. 5th–4th c. BCE)
krýptē (from krýptein)
"hidden place, vault, subterranean vault, crypt"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
crypta
"underground passage, vault, cavern"
↓
Vulgar Latin / Early Italian (c. 5th–10th c.)
*grupta → grotta
"cave, subterranean hollow, grotto"
Note: Metathesis of [r] and initial velar softening shifted crypta to grotta.
↓
Renaissance Italian (c. 1480–1500)
pittura grottesca / grottesco
"painting found in subterranean grottoes; fanciful antique mural"
Note: Coined upon the excavation of the subterranean rooms of Nero's Domus Aurea in Rome.
↓
Middle French (c. 1530–1560)
crotesque → grotesque
"bizarre decorative painting; comically distorted figure"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1600–1640)
grotesque / grotesco
"fantastically distorted, wildly incongruous, bizarre"
↓
Modern English (present)
GROTESQUE
