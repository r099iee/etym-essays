• Brazen: Similar because both denote shameless audacity that flouts decency in public view; differs because brazen (literally "made of brass") images a hard, metallic, clanging defiance, whereas barefaced images a naked, unblushing face — openness rather than hardness is the core image.
  • Flagrant: Similar because both describe wrongdoing that is glaringly, conspicuously obvious; differs because flagrant (from Latin flagrare "to burn") images the wrong as blazing and impossible to miss, while barefaced focuses on the wrongdoer's refusal to hide or blush.
  • Audacious: Similar because both imply bold, daring behaviour that oversteps norms; differs because audacious can be positive or heroic (a bold venture) and often lacks moral condemnation, whereas barefaced is essentially always censorious.
  • Unblushing: Similar because both mean showing no shame or embarrassment; differs because unblushing is a rarer, more analytic word built on the physiology of shame, while barefaced is the idiomatic, everyday term and carries a stronger tone of moral outrage.
  • Undisguised: Similar because both mean not concealed or disguised; differs because undisguised is neutral and applies to any open thing (undisguised contempt), whereas barefaced specifically condemns shamelessness and normally applies to lies or wrongs.
  • Effrontery (noun family): Similar because both denote shameless audacity; differs because effrontery (from French, "without forehead/blush") is a noun for the quality of insolent nerve, while barefaced is the adjective that flags an act as wearing that nerve openly.
  • Impudent: Similar because both describe disrespectful, shameless boldness; differs because impudent is broader (rude, cheeky, insolent toward authority) and need not involve lying or concealment, whereas barefaced centres on the openness of the shameless act.
  • Open: Similar because both can mean "not concealed"; differs because open is a vast, neutral word without moral colour, while barefaced always carries strong censorious force and the image of the face.
