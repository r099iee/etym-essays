• Coercion: Similar because both involve compelling someone to act through force or intimidation; differs because coercion is a broad sociological and psychological term covering any application of leverage or force, whereas duress is a specific legal and forensic term denoting severe, unlawful constraints that negate free will or legal consent.
  • Compulsion: Similar because both describe an overwhelming pressure to act; differs because compulsion often arises from internal psychological drives, irresistible impulses, or lawful statutory requirements, whereas duress strictly implies external, wrongful, and threatening pressure from another human actor.
  • Constraint: Similar because both refer to limitations placed on a person's liberty or decision-making; differs because constraint is often neutral, structural, or practical (e.g., budget constraints or time constraints), whereas duress carries an overtly coercive, menacing, and unlawful character.
  • Intimidation: Similar because both utilize fear to influence someone's behavior; differs because intimidation focuses primarily on the psychological state of fear induced by menacing gestures or words, while duress encompasses the totality of the coercive condition, including actual confinement, physical violence, or legal extortion.
  • Extortion: Similar because both involve using wrongful pressure to extract compliance; differs because extortion is a specific criminal act focused on extracting money, property, or services through threats, while duress is the condition of being subjected to such coercion and serves as an affirmative legal defense.
  • Pressure: Similar because both denote forces influencing someone toward a certain decision; differs because pressure is a mild, ubiquitous everyday term (such as peer pressure or sales pressure), whereas duress implies an extreme, illegitimate deprivation of choice.
