- [13th century: to offer forward] - 'Proffer' enters English from Old French, meaning to hold out for acceptance; connotation is formal and courtly - an offering made with deliberate courtesy.
- [15th-19th century: the formal and legal register] - The word settles into ceremonial and legal English - 'proffered evidence', a proffered hand or gift; connotation is solemn and weighty, the vocabulary of courts and etiquette, where even a servant's gesture is choreographed.
- [Contemporary sense] - Rare in everyday speech, where 'offer' has displaced it; 'proffered' now reads as consciously formal and slightly old-fashioned - the ceremonious gesture of holding something out in the open hand for acceptance, unsolicited, anticipatory, and courteous.
