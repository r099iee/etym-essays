Note: In Northern Italian (Venetian), intervocalic Latin [k] followed by a front vowel underwent lenition and voicing, yielding Venetian doxe (pronounced with a voiced sibilant /z/ or affricate /dz/). This contrasts sharply with Tuscan (Standard Italian), where Latin duce(m) retained the unvoiced affricate, giving duce (/ˈdutʃe/). Italian adopted the Venetian spelling doge (/ˈdɔdʒe/) to refer specifically to the Venetian and Genoese magistrates, from which English borrowed the word.
