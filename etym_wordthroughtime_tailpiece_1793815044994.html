- 1720s–1790s (Golden Age of Typography & Printmaking) - Coined in early Georgian England to describe the small decorative woodcuts, copperplate engravings, and vignettes used by printers to fill the unsightly blank void at the bottom of a page where a chapter ended; reached artistic zenith in the naturalistic miniature engravings of Thomas Bewick.
- 1750s–1820s (Classical Instrument Making & Luthiery) - Adopted into English musical organology to denote the triangular wooden bracket (traditionally carved of ebony or boxwood) anchored to the lower bout of a violin, viola, or cello that secures the strings.
- 1840s–1910s (Industrial Architecture & Plumbing) - Extended into practical engineering trades, specifically describing short structural tail-beams in timber roof-framing and the vertical drainage pipe descending from a sink basin into the trap.
- Modern Era - Retained as a refined term in bibliophilic book arts, musicology, and classical luthiery, as well as an elegant literary metaphor for a retrospective postscript or visual coda capping an intellectual work.
