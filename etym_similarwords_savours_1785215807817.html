• Smacks (of): Similar because both mean to be tinged with or suggest a quality; differs because smacks of is slightly more colloquial and stresses a discernible, often negative resemblance, while savours of is the more refined and literary form, with a sensory metaphor of taste rather than sound.
  • Suggests: Similar because both point toward or imply a quality; differs because suggests is neutral and merely hints at a possible quality, without implying that the quality is distasteful or detectable as a taint — savours of carries moral aftertaste.
  • Hints at: Similar because both indicate a quality without stating it outright; differs because hints at is light, indirect, and neutral, while savours of detects the quality as a perceptible flavor, usually a discreditable one.
  • Resembles: Similar because both indicate a likeness; differs because resembles states a likeness of form or appearance, while savours of expresses a perceivable flavour or moral aftertaste that one detects by tasting.
  • Reeks (of): Similar because both convey a detectable, usually negative quality; differs because reeks of intensifies the negative force to an offensive, overwhelming extreme, while savours of is delicate and understated.
  • Betrays: Similar because both mean that a quality is revealed in a thing; differs because betrays suggests an unconscious or involuntary revelation (often of guilt), while savours of suggests a quality one can taste in the thing itself.
  • Is tinged with: Similar because both mean lightly coloured or affected by a quality; differs because tinged with is visual and neutral, while savours of is gustatory and usually carries censure.
  • Tastes of / tastes like: Similar because both use the literal sense of taste; differs because tastes of/like is purely sensory about food, while savours of is the figurative, moralized extension applied to situations and conduct.
  • Relish / enjoy (transitive savour): Similar because both mean to take pleasure in something; differs because savour in this positive transitive sense means to enjoy fully and deliberately, lingering over the pleasure, while the intransitive savours of is a censure of detected quality — the two senses are near opposites in tone.
