• Plato: Similar because both are foundational Greek philosophers whose schools shaped Western thought; differs because Plato wrote dialogues and located truth in transcendent Forms, while Aristotle wrote systematic treatises, grounded inquiry in observation, and criticised the theory of Forms.
  • Socrates: Similar as the origin of the Greek philosophical tradition that Aristotle inherited; differs because Socrates wrote nothing and pursued ethics through conversation, whereas Aristotle produced an encyclopaedic written corpus covering logic, nature, politics and rhetoric.
  • Theophrastus: Similar as Aristotle’s pupil, colleague and successor at the Lyceum, and as a student of rhetoric and biology; differs because Theophrastus is known chiefly for botany and for the Characters, and his rhetorical work survives only in fragments.
  • Cicero: Similar as a central authority on rhetoric in the same tradition; differs because Cicero was a Roman orator-statesman writing literary dialogues, while Aristotle was a Greek philosopher analysing persuasion as a branch of knowledge.
  • Quintilian: Similar as the other ancient authority named beside him in the source; differs because Quintilian’s Institutio is a practical curriculum for training a speaker, whereas Aristotle’s Rhetoric is a theoretical account of what persuasion is.
  • Isocrates: Similar as a Greek educator for whom rhetoric was the core of civic life; differs because Isocrates taught by example and left speeches rather than a philosophical system, and he distrusted the abstract theorising Aristotle pursued.
  • Alexander the Great: Similar only through the famous connection of tutor and pupil; differs entirely in vocation — Alexander conquered, Aristotle studied and taught, and the tradition remembers them as the mind and the sword of the same Macedonian moment.
  • Thomas Aquinas: Similar as the thinker who made Aristotle the foundation of a great intellectual system; differs because Aquinas was a Christian theologian who harmonised Aristotle with revelation, while Aristotle himself was a pagan philosopher with no such project.
