• dislike: Similar because it names a negative feeling towards something; differs in being the plain, general term, with none of distaste’s suggestion of fastidiousness or offended taste.
  • aversion: Similar as a settled turning away; differs in being stronger and more deep-rooted, often instinctive rather than a matter of judgement.
  • antipathy: Similar in describing a persistent dislike; differs in being more impersonal and often reciprocal, suggesting a natural incompatibility between parties.
  • disgust: Similar as a negative reaction; differs in being far more physical and intense, provoked by the vile or contaminating rather than the merely unappealing.
  • repugnance: Similar in meaning strong distaste; differs in being more formal and more strongly bodily, close to revulsion.
  • disapproval: Similar as a negative verdict; differs in being explicitly moral and often public, whereas distaste is aesthetic and usually private.
  • disfavour: Similar as a withholding of approval; differs in implying a judgement from a position of authority, not a personal reaction of taste.
  • disinclination: Similar as a reluctance to engage; differs in emphasising unwillingness rather than dislike of the object itself.
  • odium: Similar as strong dislike; differs in being public, lasting hatred directed at a person or group.
  • revulsion: Similar in the image of recoil; differs in intensity and in its physical, almost nauseated force, which distaste never reaches.
