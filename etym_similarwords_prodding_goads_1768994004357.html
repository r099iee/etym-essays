• poking: Similar to prodding in a thrust with a pointed thing; differs because poking is a single, casual, often aimless thrust, while prodding is repeated, pointed, and businesslike.
  • jabbing: Similar in a sharp pointed thrust; differs because jabbing is sudden and hostile, while prodding is the routine, insistent nudge of the drover rather than the fury of the fight.
  • nudging: Similar in urging a reluctant creature or person forward; differs because nudging is gentle and rounded, usually an elbow, with no point at all, while prodding uses a point.
  • pricking: Similar in using a point against a surface; differs because pricking uses a fine point and barely breaks the skin, while prodding is a blunter, more insistent jab.
  • spurring: Similar in urging a beast forward; differs because spurring applies the horseman's wheeled heel-spur, pressing from the boot, while prodding is the repeated jab of a hand-held stick.
  • urging: Similar in driving a person or animal to action; differs because urging is verbal and encouraging, while prodding is physical and insistent.
  • prompting: Similar in stimulating a person to act; differs because prompting is gentle and suggestive (a prompt from a script), while prodding is more insistent and physical.
  • provoking: Similar in stimulating a reaction; differs because provoking tends to incite anger or a strong response, while prodding merely nags a reluctant thing into motion.
  • needling: Similar in pestering with small, irritating pressure; differs because needling is verbal teasing meant to annoy, while prodding is physical or practical urging.
  • badgering: Similar in pestering insistently; differs because badgering is loud, repeated verbal harassment, while prodding is a quieter, more pointed insistence.
  • nagging: Similar in persistently pressing someone; differs because nagging is complaining and verbal, while prodding is action-oriented and often physical.
  • spurs: Similar to goads in being instruments that urge a beast forward; differs because spurs are worn on the heel and press rather than stab, while a goad is an iron-tipped stick wielded in the hand.
  • whips: Similar in driving animals by punishment; differs because whips strike with a flexible lash and punish from outside, while a goad is a pointed stick that presses or stabs the hide to elicit speed.
  • prods: Similar as pointed instruments for urging beasts; differs because prods are blunter and often electric (cattle prods), while goads are the traditional iron-tipped sticks.
  • cattle prods: Similar as modern electric goads; differs because they operate by shock rather than by the ancient pointed stick, and lack the goad's long historical and figurative freight.
  • pricks: Similar in being fine pointed instruments; differs because pricks are lighter and sharper, while a goad is a heavier, sturdier driving stick.
  • gadfly: Similar in stinging and urging cattle or people; differs because the gadfly is a biting fly (17th-c. coinage from gad + fly) that irritates from outside the hand, while a goad is the human instrument.
  • stimulus: Similar in being anything that provokes action; differs because stimulus is abstract and neutral (a stimulus to growth), while a goad is pointed, insistent, and often painful.
