• decayed: Similar because both name the breakdown and decomposition of matter; differs because decay is broader, can be fast or slow, and covers many kinds of ruin, while moulder specifically suggests slow crumbling to dust.
  • rotted: Similar because both describe organic decomposition; differs because rot is driven by bacteria and fungi, can be quick and wet, and is a blunter word, while moulder suggests slow, dry disintegration.
  • crumbled: Similar because both describe breaking into small pieces or dust; differs because crumble is generic and can be quick, deliberate, or even mechanical, while moulder implies decay over time.
  • disintegrated: Similar because both mean falling apart into pieces; differs because disintegration is neutral and may be sudden or violent, while moulder is organic, slow, and laden with decay.
  • decomposed: Similar because both name the breaking down of matter; differs because decompose is the scientific, neutral term for chemical breakdown, while moulder is the poetic word for gradual ruin.
  • perished: Similar because both can describe things wasting away; differs because perish usually means death or total destruction and is more final, while moulder names slow decay rather than death.
  • withered: Similar because both describe drying and shrivelling away; differs because wither applies chiefly to living plants and tissue shrivelling from drought, while moulder applies to crumbling non-living matter.
  • rusted / corroded: Similar because both describe material breakdown over time; differs because rust and corrode are specific to metal and chemical processes, while moulder is reserved for organic matter turning to dust and earth.
