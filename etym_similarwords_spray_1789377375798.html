• Sprig: Similar because both denote a small, slender shoot or leafy stem of a plant; differs because a sprig is typically a short, detached piece gathered for culinary seasoning or decoration (such as a sprig of rosemary or mint), whereas a botanical spray is longer, more branched, and often remains gracefully attached to the living branch.
  • Twig: Similar because both represent minor woody subdivisions of a tree limb; differs because a twig is a plain, small woody division often bare of blossoms, whereas a spray evokes delicate, flowering, or leafy elegance with an aesthetic, arching form.
  • Bough: Similar because both are physical divisions of a tree; differs because a bough is a major, heavy, load-bearing structural limb of a tree, whereas a spray is a tiny, fragile, terminal shoot at the outer periphery of the foliage.
  • Mist: Similar because both refer to clouds of airborne water droplets; differs because mist is a gentle, atmospheric suspension of microscopic condensation suspended in the air like thin fog, whereas spray consists of larger, faster-moving droplets forcibly propelled by pressure, wind shear, or waves.
  • Aerosol: Similar because both describe minute liquid or solid particles dispersed in a gas; differs because aerosol is a formal physical and technical classification describing colloidal suspensions or pressurized canisters, whereas spray is the common, dynamic sensory term for liquid propelled outward in droplets.
  • Spume: Similar because both denote froth or airborne water driven from turbulent seas; differs because spume specifically emphasizes the bubbly, foamy mass floating on water or whipped into the gale, whereas spray encompasses any airborne liquid droplets regardless of foaminess.
