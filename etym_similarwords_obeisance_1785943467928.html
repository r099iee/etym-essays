• Deference: Similar because [both denote respectful yielding to a superior's standing, judgment, or wishes]; differs because [deference is chiefly an internal mental attitude and can be shown without any outward act, whereas obeisance requires a visible physical gesture such as a bow or curtsy].
  • Homage: Similar because [both express formal honour and respect toward a ruler or revered figure]; differs because [homage emphasises public allegiance and often involves oaths, pledges, or feudal bonds, while obeisance names the immediate bodily gesture that displays honour].
  • Reverence: Similar because [both denote deep respect and veneration, especially toward the sacred]; differs because [reverence is an inward feeling of awe that may never be performed, whereas obeisance insists on an outward act of bowing or kneeling].
  • Submission: Similar because [both involve yielding to a superior authority]; differs because [submission is the general act or state of yielding power to another and may be forced or unwilling, while obeisance is a formal, dignified, often voluntary gesture of respect].
  • Genuflection: Similar because [both are physical gestures of deference, usually in religious or courtly contexts]; differs because [genuflection is strictly the bending of one knee, typically in Christian worship, whereas obeisance covers bows, curtsies, and prostrations as well].
  • Prostration: Similar because [both are gestures of extreme reverence and submission]; differs because [prostration means lying flat, face down, and is reserved for the highest superiors or the divine, while obeisance spans the whole scale from a slight nod to full prostration].
  • Salutation: Similar because [both are bodily greetings or acknowledgements of another person]; differs because [a salutation is an equal or neutral greeting (a bow of hello, a wave, a handshake), whereas obeisance is an asymmetrical gesture of submission to a greater power].
  • Bow: Similar because [the bow is the most common form of obeisance]; differs because [bow names only the specific act of bending the upper body, while obeisance includes curtsies, genuflections, kneelings, and prostrations and also the respectful attitude they express].
  • Curtsy: Similar because [the curtsy is a formal gesture of respect performed before royalty or a superior]; differs because [curtsy is limited to the lowering of the body by a woman as a specific courtly act, while obeisance is the broader, gender-neutral category].
  • Obedience: Similar because [both are descended from the same root and both involve submission to authority]; differs because [obedience is the abstract, ongoing compliance with commands and rules, while obeisance is the ceremonial, momentary bodily demonstration of that submission].
  • Allegiance: Similar because [both express loyalty and submission to a sovereign or cause]; differs because [allegiance is a political or legal bond of loyalty and duty, typically pledged and enduring, while obeisance is the immediate physical gesture of respect].
  • Veneration: Similar because [both involve honour paid to the sacred or the august]; differs because [veneration is the reverential honour itself, often interior and sustained, whereas obeisance is the outward ceremonial act that pays it].
  • Kowtow: Similar because [the kowtow is a prostration of deep respect before a superior, especially in East Asian ritual]; differs because [kowtow is a culture-specific act of touching the ground with the forehead and is now often used critically for servility, whereas obeisance is a general English term for any formal bow of respect].
  • Servility: Similar because [both can describe an exaggerated display of submission]; differs because [servility is a demeaning, cringing excess of obedience that invites contempt, whereas obeisance is a dignified, conventional gesture owed to legitimate authority].
  • Supplication: Similar because [both involve lowering the body before a greater power]; differs because [supplication is an act of entreaty, begging for a favour or mercy, whereas obeisance is an act of honour and acknowledgement, not necessarily a request].
