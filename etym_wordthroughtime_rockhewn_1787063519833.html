- Old English stan (rock) and heawan (hew) — the components existed separately, with hewan meaning "to cut with a sharp instrument."
- Middle English — "hewen" is used of cutting wood and stone; rock is "the hard stone of the earth."
- 18th–19th century — archaeology popularises the description of "rock-hewn" tombs and temples, especially after the discovery and exploration of rock-cut cities like Petra (1812) and the wonders of Egypt and India.
- 19th century — "rock-hewn" and "rock-cut" become standard terms in antiquarian and travel literature.
- 20th century — Tolkien compresses it to rockhewn in The Hobbit, giving the compound a distinctly dwarven, monumental flavour that has entered modern fantasy vocabulary.
