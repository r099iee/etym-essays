Proto-Indo-European *leǵ- “to gather, collect” → Greek légein (λέγειν) “to gather, pick out, count, say, speak” → léxis (λέξις) “a speaking; a word, phrase, diction” → lexikós (λεξικός) “of or for words” → lexikón (λεξικόν) “word-book, lexicon” (the neuter of the adjective used as a noun — a book that “gathers” words) → lexikología (λεξικολογία) “discourse about words” (lexikón + -logía “study, discourse”, built on lógos “word, speech, reason”, itself from the same PIE root *leǵ-) → Late Latin lexicologia (rare, learned) → English lexicology (late 17th century; recorded in the 1690s by the OED) “the knowledge of words” → modern sense: the branch of linguistics that studies a language’s vocabulary as a system → LEXICOLOGY (the exact form of this card). Sister branch: the same PIE root through Latin legō, legere “to gather, choose, read” gave lēx “law” (what is gathered and laid down), lectiō “a reading” (source of lecture, lesson) and collecta (source of collect).
