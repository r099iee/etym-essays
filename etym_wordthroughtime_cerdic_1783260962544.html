- Anglo-Saxon chronicle tradition (9th century) - Cerdic first appears in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle as the founder-king of Wessex, and for centuries he was accepted without question as the ancestor of the West Saxon royal line and therefore of the English monarchy itself.
- 18th-19th-century historiography - Macaulay and his contemporaries used the name as a synecdoche for the whole pagan Anglo-Saxon conquest, a heathen warlord who brought the superstitions of the Elbe to Britain, so the name came to carry a quiet verdict of cultural backwardness.
- Modern scholarship - historians now treat Cerdic as at best semi-legendary, noting that the name looks Brythonic and may conceal a British warlord, so his standing has shifted from founding ancestor to a symbol of how legend is woven into national history.
