CHAIN 1 — BANNER:

*PIE *bhendh-* —
to bind
(reconstructed; the traditional root of band and, indirectly, of banner — but the link is uncertain)

↓

Proto-Germanic *bandwa- / *bandwō —
sign, ensign, banner
(reconstructed; a derivative of *bindan- “to bind”)

↓

Late Latin bandum —
banner, standard
(6th–7th centuries; a borrowing from Germanic, ultimately the same root)

↓

Old French baniere —
banner, standard
(from late Latin bandum)

↓

Middle English banere —
banner, standard
(13th century; borrowed from Old French)

↓

English banner —
a flag or standard
(first element of the compound)

↓

banner —
the first element of the compound

CHAIN 2 — MEN:

*PIE *man-* —
man, human being
(reconstructed; traditionally linked to *men- “to think”, i.e. “the thinking being” — debated)

↓

Proto-Germanic *mann- —
human being, person

↓

Old English mann / mon —
person, human being, man

↓

Middle English man —
person, man

↓

English man —
an adult male human being; a person

↓

men —
plural form; the second element of the compound

COMBINATION:

banner + men → English bannermen —
armed followers, retainers, or vassals who follow a lord’s banner; loyal supporters
(transparent modern compound; institutionalized as a collective in modern epic-fantasy fiction)

↓

BANNERMEN —
the present word

Notes: One related historical word bannerman (“a standard-bearer”) is attested; the plural bannermen as “sworn vassal houses” is chiefly a modern and fantasy coinage, though built entirely of ancient parts. The PIE root behind banner is uncertain — some scholars prefer simply a Germanic root without a clear Indo-European antecedent; the link to *bhendh- “to bind” is traditional but not certain.
