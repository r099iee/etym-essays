• Accumulate: Similar because both verbs mean to increase in quantity or gather over a period of time; differs because accumulate (Latin accumulare, 'to heap up') focuses on the physical or quantitative pile-up of items, goods, or debts through active collection or passive gathering, whereas accrue emphasizes an organic, legally sanctioned, or natural entitlement that flows to someone as an incremental yield or periodic addition.
  • Amass: Similar because both involve building up a substantial volume or store of valuable assets over time; differs because amass (from Latin massa, 'lump, mass') implies conscious, aggressive, and deliberate human striving to hoard a vast personal fortune or collection (e.g., amassing a billion-dollar empire), whereas accrue occurs steadily, periodically, and often automatically through the mere passage of time or operation of law.
  • Inure: Similar because both verbs describe legal or moral benefits, habits, or rights taking effect or becoming operative for a beneficiary over time; differs because inure (originally meaning 'to habituate' or 'put into use') is primarily used in legal contracts to specify that an agreement or advantage takes legal effect for the benefit of an individual (e.g., 'to inure to the benefit of heirs'), whereas accrue focuses on the progressive accumulation and vesting of the entitlement itself.
  • Devolve: Similar because both describe rights, duties, or property transferring to an individual; differs because devolve (Latin devolvere, 'to roll down') signifies the downward transfer or delegation of power, responsibility, or an estate from a higher authority or deceased ancestor to a subordinate or successor, whereas accrue denotes lateral or organic accretion and incremental growth.
  • Augment: Similar because both describe an increase in size, amount, or strength; differs because augment (Latin augmentare) is a transitive verb denoting an intentional, active addition made to enlarge an existing structure or salary, whereas accrue is primarily an intransitive verb denoting steady periodic growth that arises naturally or contractually (usually paired with to).
  • Aggregate: Similar because both involve the combination of individual units into a larger collective total; differs because aggregate emphasizes the mathematical sum or collective gathering of disparate, scattered parts into a unified mass or total figure, whereas accrue tracks the ongoing temporal process of continuous accumulation.
  • Culminate: Similar because both involve a process unfolding over time toward a substantial result; differs because culminate refers strictly to reaching the highest, final, or climactic point of a process (Latin culmen, 'peak'), whereas accrue refers to the ongoing accumulation of increments along the way.
