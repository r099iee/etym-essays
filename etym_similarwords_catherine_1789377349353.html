• Pinwheel: Similar because both denote rotating circular devices that spin rapidly around a central axis, often in pyrotechnics or children's toys; differs because pinwheel is an informal, generic American term describing paper or plastic wind-driven toys or simple spinning fireworks, whereas Catherine wheel carries deep historical, eponymous weight derived from the martyrdom of Saint Catherine of Alexandria.
  • Rose window: Similar because both designate circular architectural stained-glass windows featuring radial stone tracery; differs because rose window is the comprehensive umbrella term spanning elaborate, multi-foiled Gothic designs (including rayonnant and flamboyant styles resembling rose petals), whereas Catherine wheel window specifically denotes designs whose tracery strictly mimics wheel spokes and rims.
  • Cartwheel: Similar because both describe circular rotational movement around a central axis; differs because cartwheel is the universal international athletic term for lateral hand-to-foot tumbling or the wooden wheel of a wagon, whereas Catherine wheel in gymnastics is a predominantly British colloquial idiom with primary pyrotechnic associations.
  • Girandole: Similar because both are ornamental rotating or radial pyrotechnic devices designed to spray sparks; differs because a girandole historically designates a revolving cluster of sky rockets or an ornate, branched candelabrum, whereas the Catherine wheel rotates continuously on a single stationary axle.
  • Gyroscope: Similar because both involve rapid rotation about a fixed central pivot; differs because a gyroscope is a scientific, mechanical, and navigational instrument utilizing the conservation of angular momentum for orientation, completely devoid of pyrotechnic, artistic, or hagiographic dimensions.
  • Tourbillion: Similar because both denote specialized pyrotechnic devices that spin furiously while discharging brilliant fire; differs because a tourbillion (or aerial spinner) is an unguided, free-flying firework that lifts into the air on its own reactive thrust, whereas the Catherine wheel remains securely anchored to a stationary pivot pin.
