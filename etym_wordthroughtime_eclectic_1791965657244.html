- 1680s — A learned label for philosophers who selected doctrines from several schools, especially the Eclectic sect of Potamo and the Roman eclectics.
- 18th c. — Extended to any system or person that borrows from various sources; neutral in philosophy, mildly suspect in polemic.
- 19th c. — Art and architecture: Eclecticism names the deliberate mixing of historical styles, and the word also labels Eclectic medicine and McGuffey’s Readers.
- 20th c. — Interior design and music: “eclectic taste” and “eclectic mix” become terms of praise, implying cosmopolitan curation.
- 21st c. — Widely used in lifestyle and marketing; still pejorative when coherence is absent, as in “a shallow eclectic.”
