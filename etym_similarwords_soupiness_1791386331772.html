• Mawkishness: Similar because both condemn sickening, exaggerated, and insincere sentimentality; differs because mawkishness carries a slightly archaic, nauseating moral edge (historically rooted in maggots and sour taste), whereas soupiness evokes comic, warm, formless mush in the tradition of British humor.
  • Sentimentality: Similar because both denote an indulgence in tender or romantic emotion; differs because sentimentality is a broad, neutral-to-formal philosophical and psychological concept that can be examined seriously, whereas soupiness is an inherently humorous, deflating, and informal critique.
  • Mushiness: Similar because both use physical culinary food textures (soup vs. mush) to mock emotional softness and romantic gush; differs because mushiness emphasizes a soft, pulpy, squashed solid state (like mashed vegetables), whereas soupiness emphasizes fluid opacity, murk, and atmospheric or acoustic muddiness.
  • Sloppiness: Similar because both describe messy, formless physical or behavioral states; differs because sloppiness implies carelessness, lack of discipline, or untidy execution (sloppy work, sloppy clothes), whereas soupiness specifically targets romantic overabundance, viscosity, or atmospheric fog.
  • Slushiness: Similar because both metaphorically equate semi-liquid winter melt with cheap romantic melodrama; differs because slushiness specifically evokes half-melted, dirty snow and freezing puddles, whereas soupiness evokes a warm, comforting broth turned ludicrously cloying.
  • Cloyingness: Similar because both characterize an overwhelming, suffocating excess of emotional sweetness; differs because cloyingness focuses directly on sensory revulsion caused by excessive sugar or rich fragrance that satiates to the point of disgust, lacking the fluid, murky physical associations of soupiness.
  • Bathos: Similar because both describe a ridiculous, failed attempt at deep emotion that ends in absurdity; differs because bathos is a classical literary term denoting an abrupt descent from the sublime to the ridiculous, whereas soupiness is a sustained condition of gooey romantic over-indulgence.
  • Treacliness: Similar because both describe heavy, sticky, sweet emotion in speech or art; differs because treacliness evokes thick golden syrup or molasses that drips slowly and heavily, whereas soupiness suggests a lighter, warmer, more fluid slurry.
  • Turbidity: Similar because both can describe cloudy, opaque liquids or atmospheres; differs because turbidity is a rigorous scientific and limnological measurement of suspended particles in water, entirely devoid of emotional, comedic, or acoustic connotations.
  • Viscosity: Similar because both describe physical resistance to flow in liquids; differs because viscosity is an objective rheological property measured in poise or pascals, whereas soupiness is an informal, qualitative sensory impression.
