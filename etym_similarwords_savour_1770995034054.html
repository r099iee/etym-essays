• relish: Similar because both mean enjoying something with great pleasure; differs because relish is more energetic and zestful, often with eager anticipation, whereas savour is slower, quieter, and more reflective.
  • enjoy: Similar because both mean deriving pleasure from something; differs because enjoy is a broad, general state of satisfaction requiring no deliberate focus or slowness, whereas savour demands lingering attention.
  • appreciate: Similar because both mean valuing an experience deeply; differs because appreciate is cognitive, recognising worth and quality, whereas savour is prolonged, deliberate sensory or emotional pleasure.
  • taste: Similar because both involve the sense of flavour; differs because taste is the neutral physiological act, whereas savour is deliberate, appreciative tasting.
  • flavour: Similar because both name a quality perceived by taste or smell; differs because flavour is a property of the thing itself, whereas savour is the experienced, characteristic quality including its appreciation.
  • zest: Similar because both name piquancy of flavour or interest; differs because zest is a lively, sharp tang, whereas savour is the fuller, lingering characteristic quality.
  • tang: Similar because both name a distinctive taste; differs because tang is sharp, pungent, and specific, whereas savour is the general characteristic quality of a thing.
  • cherish: Similar because both involve deep, careful enjoyment; differs because cherish is protective, long-term, and usually of people or valued things, whereas savour is about relishing the present moment of experience.
  • bask in: Similar because both mean delighting in something at length; differs because bask in suggests passively soaking up warmth or approval, whereas savour implies active, focused tasting of the moment.
  • luxuriate in: Similar because both mean indulging in pleasure slowly; differs because luxuriate in stresses self-indulgent comfort and ease, whereas savour stresses appreciative attention.
  • delight in: Similar because both mean taking great pleasure in something; differs because delight in is lively, expressive, and joyful, whereas savour is quiet, reflective, and sensory.
  • smack of: Similar because both mean "to have a trace or quality of"; differs because smack of is informal and usually negative, whereas savour of is literary and can be neutral or positive.
