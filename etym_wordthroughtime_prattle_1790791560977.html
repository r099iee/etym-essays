- 15th Century: Emerged as a frequentative verb describing the repetitive, charming vocalizations and babbling of infants and young children.
- 16th–17th Century: Expanded into Elizabethan drama to depict both innocent, unguarded speech and foolish, trivial courtly gossip.
- 18th–19th Century: Solidified in social satire and Victorian novels as a critique of frivolous salon chitchat, trivial tea-table banter, and empty small talk.
- 20th–21st Century: Maintained its everyday meaning while acquiring a specialized literary critical sense denoting automated, superficial textual static.
