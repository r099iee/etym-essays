- Old English (7th-11th c.) - "clāþ" = "cloth" and "clothing"; verb "clāþian" = "to clothe, cover" — the garment-making sense
- Middle English (12th-14th c.) - "clothen" as the ordinary verb; the strong participle "cladd" emerges alongside "clothed", reinforced by the Old Norse past participle "klæddr"
- 14th-16th c. - "clad" becomes the preferred past participle in verse and high prose for full, distinctive dress — knights, kings, saints in glory
- 17th-19th c. - the split hardens: "clothed" for ordinary dressing, "clad" for the poetic and the complete ("clad in snow", "clad in armour")
- 19th-20th c. - the industrial extension: "ironclad" warships, "cladding" on buildings, "steel-clad" structures — covering in materials, not garments
- 21st c. - technical neutrality (building cladding, optical-fibre cladding) alongside a retained literary glamour for dress; "ironclad" lives on figuratively for unbreakable promises
