• Serrated: Similar because it denotes a border or outline featuring a continuous series of tooth-like notches or projections; differs because serrated specifically implies small, sharp, uniform, saw-like teeth oriented in a single direction, whereas indented encompasses broader, deeper, and more irregular recesses, bays, or margins.
  • Notched: Similar because it describes an edge interrupted by angular V-shaped cuts or hollows; differs because notched usually implies individual, distinct, or mechanically carved single incisions rather than a continuous, sweeping natural coastline or systematic typographical margin.
  • Recessed: Similar because it indicates an element set back from a primary plane or baseline; differs because recessed describes a smooth, uniform planar setback (such as an architectural alcove or recessed lighting) without the jagged, undulating, or tooth-like perimeter inherent to indented.
  • Crenellated: Similar because it consists of alternating high projections and low indentations along a border; differs because crenellated strictly denotes square-cut architectural battlements or fortifications designed for military defense, characterized by rigid geometric symmetry.
  • Embayed: Similar because it characterizes coastlines broken into inward curves; differs because embayed is exclusively oceanographic and geographical, describing expansive bays enclosed between headlands rather than general margins, legal parchments, or mechanical notches.
  • Sinuous: Similar because it describes a meandering, non-linear boundary; differs because sinuous emphasizes smooth, sweeping, curvilinear, snake-like bends rather than angular, tooth-like, or recessed notches.
  • Scalloped: Similar because it refers to a patterned, decorative, or physical border; differs because scalloped consists of repeating convex, semi-circular curves resembling the rim of a scallop seashell, rather than angular or deep structural recesses.
  • Jagged: Similar because it denotes an irregular, uneven, and broken outline; differs because jagged carries a strong connotation of sharp, raw, chaotic, and dangerous fracture, whereas indented suggests either natural geographic harbor formation or orderly, deliberate craftsmanship and typography.
