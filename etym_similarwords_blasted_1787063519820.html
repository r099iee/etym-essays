• withered: Similar because both describe a blighted, dried-up condition; differs because wither applies to living plants shrivelling slowly, while blast is a sudden destructive blow of wind, fire, or force.
  • scorched: Similar because both describe damage by searing heat; differs because scorch is purely heat-based burning of surfaces, while blast includes wind, explosion, and cursing.
  • blighted: Similar because both describe devastation, especially of plants or landscapes; differs because blight is caused by disease or insects and is slow and creeping, while blast is sudden and violent.
  • ravaged / devastated: Similar because both describe ruin and destruction; differs because ravage and devastate imply large-scale destruction over an area, while blast can be a single strike or a withering gust.
  • ruined: Similar because both describe destruction; differs because ruin is general and can be gradual or moral, while blast specifies the manner: explosion, gust, blight, or curse.
  • demolished: Similar because both mean destroyed by force; differs because demolish is deliberate and structural (buildings), while blast is explosive and also blights and withers.
  • cursed / damned: Similar because both are oaths and imprecations; differs because cursed and damned are stronger swear-words, while blasted is a milder, old-fashioned intensifier.
  • pulverized: Similar because both can mean reduced to powder; differs because pulverize is mechanical grinding, while blast is explosive or atmospheric force.
