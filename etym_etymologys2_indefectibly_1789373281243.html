PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*n̥- ("un-, not") + *de- ("away, down from") + *dʰeh₁- ("to put, place, set, make, do")
"not doing away; incapable of failing, falling short, or being undone"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1500–1000 BCE)
*in- + *dē- + *fakjō
"not departing, not failing, not falling short"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
deficere (past participle dēfectus)
"to fall short, withdraw, desert, fail, run out"
↓
Late Latin / Ecclesiastical Latin (c. 4th–5th c. CE)
indēfectibilis → indēfectibiliter
"unfailing, incapable of decay or failure; indefectibly"
↓
Early Modern English (c. early 17th c., c. 1620–1640)
indefectible + -ly
"in an indefectible manner; unfailingly, without possibility of decay"
↓
