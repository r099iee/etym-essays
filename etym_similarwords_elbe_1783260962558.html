• Rhine: Similar because both are great German rivers that served as boundaries of the Roman and Carolingian worlds and carry deep historical symbolism; differs because the Rhine was the actual, fortified Roman frontier and the axis of western Germany, while the Elbe lay beyond the empire as the symbol of the unconquered East.
  • Oder: Similar because both are eastern German rivers, politically charged as frontiers between German and Slavic Europe; differs because the Oder formed the post-1945 Polish-German border, while the Elbe runs entirely through Germany and the Czech Republic.
  • Danube: Similar because both are great Central European rivers and classical frontier lines of the Roman Empire; differs because the Danube was a southern imperial frontier with its own provinces and cities, while the Elbe was a northern goal that Rome never held.
  • Vistula: Similar because both are major Central European rivers lying beyond the old Roman frontier; differs because the Vistula drains Poland and the Baltic lands and never functioned as a Roman boundary, while the Elbe was the emblem of the frontier itself.
  • Weser: Similar because both are North Sea German rivers and sites of Drusus's campaigns; differs because the Weser is shorter and entirely German, while the Elbe spans the Czech-German border and is historically grander.
  • Saale: Similar because both are central German rivers east of the Rhine; differs because the Saale is a tributary-scale river of Thuringia, while the Elbe is a major international waterway.
  • Moldau / Vltava: Similar because both are Bohemian rivers feeding the same Czech-German river system; differs because the Vltava is a tributary that joins the Elbe near Mělník and carries no frontier symbolism of its own.
  • Spree: Similar because both flow through eastern Germany into the Elbe system; differs because the Spree is a tributary (via the Havel) that carries Berlin, while the Elbe is the trunk river.
  • Albis: Similar because Albis is simply the classical Latin name of the same river; differs because Albis is the Roman-period form used in Latin sources, while Elbe is the modern English and German name.
  • Labe: Similar because Labe is the Czech name of the very same river; differs because Labe is the Slavic-adapted form used in Czech, while Elbe is the Germanic-adapted form used in German and English.
