Taped is the past tense and past participle of tape, meaning fastened with adhesive tape, bound with fabric strip, or recorded on magnetic or digital tape. In the phrase taped out, it means arranged, settled, or fixed so that nothing remains loose, as in everything is taped out for the event.

The two great modern senses share one image: a thin strip that holds things in place. Adhesive tape holds parcels, bandages, and wires in place. Recording tape holds sounds and pictures in place for replay. To have plans taped out merges both: arrangements are sealed and preserved.

As an adjective, taped describes the resulting state: taped ankle, taped interview, taped confession. As a verb form, it describes the action: she taped the poster, they taped the show.

  • Adhesive sense — stuck or sealed with tape: taped box, taped window, taped ankle. Means joined or protected by sticky strip. The reasoning is direct from textile binding to modern pressure-sensitive tape. Includes medical strapping, parcel sealing, and makeshift repair.
  • Recording sense — captured on tape: taped programme, taped evidence, taped call. Means sound or video preserved on magnetic tape and by extension any recording. Developed in the 1930s-50s with reel-to-reel and cassettes, then generalised to digital: taped retains prestige of live capture even when no tape exists.
  • Figurative settled sense — fixed, arranged, sorted: to have it taped, everything taped out. Means plans are complete and secure, nothing left to chance. The bridge is a sealed parcel that will not open plus a finished recording that will not change. Common in business and crime slang: the deal is taped up.
  • Measuring and marking senses: taped off, taped outline. Means cordoned with barrier tape or marked with measuring tape. Police tape off scenes; builders tape lines. From tailors tapeline to hazard tape.
  • Sport and medical jargon: taped knee, taped wrist. Means stabilised with athletic tape to prevent injury. Distinct from bandaged because tape is rigid support, not absorbent cover.

In this context everything is taped out means everything is arranged and under control, the opposite of a mess. Register is everyday standard, slightly informal in figurative use. Collocations include taped up, taped together, taped off, taped interview, red-taped. Antonyms include unsealed, live, unplanned, loose.

For learners, taped out in this card is the settled sense, not recording: everything is taped out equals everything is sorted and sealed. Listen for particles — taped up for parcels, taped off for cordons, taped live for broadcasts. The strip image unites them all, and recognising which strip is meant makes the past form instantly clear.

Whether adhesive, magnetic, or figurative, taped always promises that what was loose will now hold fast and stay put for inspection, replay, or use.

That holding power, whether by glue or by recording, is the whole word in miniature.
