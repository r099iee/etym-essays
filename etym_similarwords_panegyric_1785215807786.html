• eulogy: Similar because both are formal speeches of high praise; differs because a eulogy is specifically a funeral or memorial speech, whereas a panegyric may praise the living, an institution, or a cause and derives from festival rather than funeral oratory.
  • encomium: Similar because both denote a formal composition of high praise; differs because an encomium (from Greek enkṓmion, a victory-song) is typically a literary tribute to a particular person's virtues, while a panegyric stresses the public, ceremonial, full-dress speech before an assembly.
  • laudation: Similar because both name the act or composition of praising; differs because laudation is a plainer, more general Latin-derived word that lacks the formal genre-character and festival grandeur of panegyric.
  • tribute: Similar because both denote an expression of respect and praise; differs because tribute is far broader and warmer, usable of a small gesture of gratitude (a tribute to her courage), whereas panegyric is reserved for the grand, set-piece performance.
  • testimonial: Similar because both are public acts honouring someone; differs because a testimonial is a practical honouring (as of a retiring official) or a statement vouching for character, lacking the rhetorical artistry and festival grandeur of the panegyric.
  • homage: Similar because both express formal honour to a person; differs because homage is a gesture, ceremony, or acknowledgement of deference (originally a feudal act of loyalty), not a composed speech of praise.
  • flattery: Similar because both involve speaking well of someone; differs because flattery is insincere, self-serving praise designed to please or manipulate, while a panegyric is presumptively sincere, formal, and public.
  • compliment: Similar because both are words of approval; differs because a compliment is a small, private courtesy, while a panegyric is a grand, public, elaborate composition.
  • praise: Similar because both denote favourable estimation; differs because praise is the everyday general word, while panegyric names a specific formal genre of full-dress public celebration.
  • accolade: Similar because both denote a mark of honour and recognition; differs because an accolade is typically a brief award or acknowledgement, not an extended formal speech.
  • paean: Similar because both are elevated forms of tribute; differs because a paean is specifically a song of joy, triumph, or thanksgiving (from the hymn to Apollo), while a panegyric is an oration or composition of praise.
  • commendation: Similar because both are formal expressions of approval; differs because commendation is usually brief and official (letters of commendation, commendation in a review) and lacks the lavish rhetorical scale of the panegyric.
