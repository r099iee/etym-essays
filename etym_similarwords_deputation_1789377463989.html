• Delegation: Similar because both denote a designated group of representatives commissioned to act on behalf of a larger body; differs because delegation is the broader, modern, and universal term for any official contingent (such as a diplomatic delegation to the UN), whereas deputation often carries a more formal, historical, or specifically petitioning character (a group appearing before an authority to deliver a request or address).
  • Embassy: Similar because both represent a diplomatic mission acting for a sovereign power; differs because an embassy is a permanent diplomatic mission headed by an ambassador with continuous diplomatic immunity and an official physical residence in a foreign capital, whereas a deputation is a temporary, specific task-oriented group.
  • Legation: Similar because both involve sending representatives on an official mission; differs because a legation was historically a permanent diplomatic mission of slightly lower rank than an embassy (headed by a minister rather than an ambassador), whereas a deputation disbands immediately after completing its petition or meeting.
  • Contingent: Similar because both describe a representative group drawn from a larger collective; differs because a contingent emphasizes a group sent as part of a larger gathering, military force, or convention (e.g., "the Canadian contingent at the Olympics"), without necessarily having a specific diplomatic petition to deliver.
  • Committee: Similar because both are small groups appointed by a larger assembly to handle specific matters; differs because a committee is typically an internal deliberative body tasked with studying problems, drafting legislation, or executing ongoing administration, whereas a deputation is an external representative group sent outward to communicate with another authority.
  • Envoy: Similar because both represent a messenger or representative acting for an authority; differs because an envoy is usually an individual diplomat or special emissary, whereas a deputation is explicitly a collective group of persons.
  • Commission: Similar because both are bodies appointed with official authority; differs because a commission is a formal governmental or regulatory panel granted judicial, investigative, or executive powers to enforce laws or examine public scandals, whereas a deputation is a petitioning representative assembly.
