Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*spey- or *spī-
"sharp, pointed, thorn, prickle"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
spīna
"thorn, prickle, spine, backbone"
↓
Old Galician-Portuguese / Old Spanish (c. 12th–14th c.)
espinha / espino
"thorn, hawthorn bush"
↓
Iberian toponymic formation with suffix -osa (c. 13th–15th c.)
Espinosa / Spinoza
"literally 'thorny place' or 'place full of hawthorns'"
Note: Sephardic Jewish surname borne by Marrano exiles from Portugal/Spain; borne by Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677).
