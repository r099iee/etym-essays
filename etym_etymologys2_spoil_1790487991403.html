Latin (c. 200 BCE–100 CE)
spolium
"hide stripped from animal,"
"arms stripped from foe"
↓
Latin (Classical)
spoliare
"to strip of clothing,"
"to rob, plunder"
↓
Old French (11th–13th c.)
espoillier
"to strip, plunder,"
"to despoil"
↓
Middle English (early 14th c.)
spoilen
"to strip enemy,"
"to plunder"
↓
Modern English (present)
spoil
"plunder; to ruin,"
"to overindulge"

Note: deeper origin of Latin spolium beyond stripping is debated. Often linked to PIE *(s)pel- "to split, tear off" with cognates in Greek spolas and English spill, but de Vaan marks it uncertain. The Latin to French to English chain itself is secure.
