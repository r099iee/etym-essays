• Thickets: Similar because both mean dense masses of shrubs and undergrowth; differs because thicket is the plain general word, while brakes leans older and wilder, with bracken and briar in its roots.
  • Coppices: Similar because both are growths of small woodland trees like hazel; differs because a coppice is managed and cut on rotation, while brakes are unmanaged tangle.
  • Brushwood: Similar because both name low scrubby growth; differs because brushwood stresses cut or gathered sticks, while brakes stresses the living tangled patch.
  • Undergrowth: Similar because both are the low layer beneath taller trees; differs because undergrowth needs a wood above it, while brakes can stand alone on open slopes.
  • Brambles: Similar because brambles are the classic brake-content, thorny and tangling; differs because brambles names the plants (blackberry canes), while brakes names the whole patch they fill.
  • Bracken: Similar because bracken-brakes are the original brake, fern seas turning copper; differs because bracken is the single fern species, while brakes is any thicket it or others compose.
  • Coverts: Similar because both shelter game and foxes; differs because covert is the hunter’s functional term for shelter, while brakes describes the vegetation itself.
  • Cane-brakes: Similar because both are dense waterside tangles; differs because cane-brakes are the tall American river-cane thickets, while Tolkien’s brakes are English hazel scrub.
  • Spinneys: Similar because both are small woods or clumps loved by game; differs because a spinney is a planted or preserved little wood, while brakes are wilder and lower.
