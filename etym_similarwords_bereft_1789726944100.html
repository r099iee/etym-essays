• Bereaved: Similar because it denotes suffering loss by death; differs because bereaved is the standard funeral epithet for mourners, while bereft is wider and can mean lacking anything.
  • Deprived: Similar because it means stripped of something needed; differs because deprived is bureaucratic and often implies injustice or poverty, while bereft implies desolate feeling.
  • Robbed: Similar because it preserves the original theft frame; differs because robbed names a criminal act with a thief, while bereft names the resulting emptiness.
  • Stripped: Similar because it means laid bare of covering or possession; differs because stripped is physical and often violent, while bereft is emotional and elegiac.
  • Destitute: Similar because it means utterly lacking resources; differs because destitute stresses poverty and helplessness, while bereft stresses sorrowful absence.
  • Forlorn: Similar because it conveys lonely abandonment; differs because forlorn stresses pitiable appearance and hopelessness, while bereft stresses the fact of having been deprived.
  • Forsaken: Similar because it means abandoned; differs because forsaken implies deliberate desertion by someone, while bereft can come from fate or timetable.
  • Devoid: Similar because it means entirely lacking; differs because devoid is cold and absolute, while bereft retains warmth and pity.
  • Wanting: Similar because it means lacking; differs because wanting is flat and formal, while bereft adds desolation.
  • Desolate: Similar because it describes emptied, joyless states; differs because desolate can describe landscapes themselves, while bereft centres on a sufferer missing something.
