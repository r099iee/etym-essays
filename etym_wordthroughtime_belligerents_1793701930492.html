- Late 16th Century (Adjective) - "Waging war; hostile, warlike." - Initially used to describe a person or nation actively engaged in or inclined towards war. Connotation: descriptive of a state or characteristic.
- Mid-18th Century (Noun) - "A nation or person engaged in war." - The adjectival form began to be substantivized, particularly in the context of international law, to refer to the actual parties involved in a conflict. Connotation: formal, legal, defining a specific status in international relations.
- 19th-20th Century (Expanded Noun Usage) - Application to non-state actors, insurgents, and broader conflicts. - The term's scope expanded beyond sovereign states to include organized rebel groups, reflecting the changing nature of warfare. Connotation: still formal, but with increasing complexity in legal recognition.
- Late 20th Century - Present (General & Metaphorical) - Any party in a dispute or confrontation; aggressive individual/group. - While retaining its legal meaning, the term also gained broader, less formal usage to describe any actively hostile or confrontational party, even outside armed conflict. Connotation: can be formal or informal, often implying active, sometimes aggressive, participation in a dispute.
