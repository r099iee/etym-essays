- 1300s–1500s: [Aristocratic Venery] - Introduced through Anglo-Norman hunting culture specifically for the deep, musical cry of scent hounds tracking game.
- 1500s–1700s: [Literary & Celestial Idiom] - Adopted in Elizabethan drama to describe wolves howling at the moon and packs of hounds sounding like tuned bells.
- 1700s–1900s: [Mob Bloodlust] - The idiom "baying for blood" solidified in historical writing to depict furious crowds, lynch mobs, and vengeful political factions.
- 1900s–Present: [Media & Public Frenzy] - Pervasive in journalism to describe aggressive press scrums, partisan mobs, and digital outrage swarms pursuing public figures.
