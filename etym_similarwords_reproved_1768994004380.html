• Rebuke: Similar because both are authoritative verbal criticism of a person’s conduct; differs because rebuke is sharper and often public or stern, while reprove is mild, gentle, and aimed at amendment rather than humiliation.
  • Reprimand: Similar because both are formal censure of misconduct; differs because reprimand is an official, institutional act (military, disciplinary), while reprove is personal, domestic, or pastoral, softer and more corrective.
  • Censure: Similar because both express strong disapproval; differs because censure is a formal judgment, often of a body or publication, and impersonal, while reprove is direct, personal, and intended to mend.
  • Admonish: Similar because both warn and correct gently; differs because admonish emphasises warning against future error, while reprove emphasises correcting present fault.
  • Chide: Similar because both are mild scolding; differs because chide is often fretful or nagging, while reprove is calm, firm, and dignified.
  • Scold: Similar because both reprimand someone; differs because scold is emotional, sometimes shrill and continuous, while reprove is measured and temperate.
  • Chastise: Similar because both punish or correct; differs because chastise can include physical or severe punishment, while reprove is purely verbal and gentle.
  • Castigate: Similar because both criticise severely; differs because castigate is harsh, severe, even abusive, while reprove is mild and corrective.
  • Upbraid: Similar because both reproach someone for a fault; differs because upbraid implies bitter, angry accusation, while reprove stays calm and constructive.
  • Reproach: Similar because both express blame; differs because reproach emphasises disappointment and the shaming of the offender, while reprove aims at correction and improvement.
  • Berate: Similar because both scold at length; differs because berate is prolonged, angry, and harsh, the opposite of reprove’s quiet firmness.
  • Lambaste: Similar because both criticise strongly; differs because lambaste is violent, public attack in strong language, wholly unlike reprove’s temperate correction.
  • Correct: Similar because both aim to right a fault; differs because correct is neutral and can be non-verbal (correcting an error), while reprove is verbal censure of a person.
  • Criticise: Similar because both find fault; differs because criticise is general and can be neutral or professional, while reprove is personal moral correction with authority.
  • Dress down: Similar because both reprimand someone; differs because dress down is informal, blunt, and severe, while reprove is formal, gentle, and literary.
  • Call out: Similar because both publicly correct someone; differs because call out is modern, confrontational, and public, while reprove is old-fashioned, quiet, and private.
  • Take to task: Similar because both hold someone to account; differs because take to task is emphatic and often angry, while reprove is mild and improvement-seeking.
