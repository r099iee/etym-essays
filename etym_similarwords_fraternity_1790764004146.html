• Brotherhood: Similar because both signify the bond of solidarity and kinship among individuals; differs because 'brotherhood' is an Anglo-Saxon, emotionally resonant, and visceral term, whereas 'fraternity' is a Latinate, formal term that also specifically designates collegiate Greek-letter organizations and professional bodies.
  • Fellowship: Similar because both describe mutual association, warmth, and shared purpose; differs because 'fellowship' emphasizes collaborative companionship, egalitarian partnership, and academic financial grants, lacking the gendered biological root of fraternity.
  • Confraternity: Similar because both refer to voluntary fraternal societies; differs because 'confraternity' specifically denotes a Roman Catholic lay religious organization established for charitable, devotional, or penitential purposes.
  • Guild: Similar because both unite members of a shared profession or craft; differs because a 'guild' is historically an economic and trade monopoly regulating quality, apprenticeship, and prices in medieval towns.
  • Sorority: Similar because both are collegiate Greek-letter student organizations fostering communal bonds; differs because a 'sorority' (from Latin *soror*, sister) is exclusively a female sisterhood, whereas 'fraternity' historically denoted male brotherhoods (though some professional fraternities are co-educational).
  • Camaraderie: Similar because both evoke warmth and good fellowship among peers; differs because 'camaraderie' describes an informal emotional atmosphere of mutual trust and good humor, rather than an organized formal association.
