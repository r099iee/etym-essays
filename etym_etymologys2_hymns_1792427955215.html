Note on historical etymology and academic debate: The deeper Proto-Indo-European ancestry of Greek hýmnos remains one of the classic debates of Indo-European historical linguistics. Robert Beekes (EDG) and Pierre Chantraine support the traditional thesis that hýmnos derives from PIE *sh₂ew- ("to bind, weave together"), viewing a hymn as an intricate "textual weaving" or "fabric of metrical song" (directly matching the ancient Indo-European poetic metaphor seen in Greek hyphainein aoedēn "to weave a song" and Sanskrit sūktam / tantum tanoti "weaving the thread of sacrifice"). Conversely, other historical linguists (including Manfred Mayrhofer and Johann Schindler) propose a connection with the PIE verbal root *seh₂i- / *sh₂- ("to sing, chant"), cognate with Hittite išḫamai- ("he sings") and Vedic Sanskrit sā́man- ("sacred chanted melody of the Sāmaveda"). Both etymological paths illuminate the archaic reality of sacred poetic recitation: whether conceived as an intricately woven verbal tapestry or a chanted mystical resonance, the word has maintained its sacral musical essence for three millennia.
