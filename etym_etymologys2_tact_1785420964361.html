TACT: single root word. PIE *tag- "to touch, handle, take hold of" (well-attested root; the base of Latin tangere and Greek tattein) → Greek tattein / taktos "to arrange, order" (sister branch; attested from classical Greek; source of English tactics via taktikos) → Proto-Italic *tag- (inherited from PIE) → Latin tangere "to touch" (nasal-infix present of *tag-, attested from the 2nd c. BCE) → Latin tactus, past participle of tangere, "touched" (attested 1st c. BCE) → Latin tactus, noun, "the sense of touch, handling" (formed from the participle stem tact-; attested) → Latin taxare "to appraise, assess" (frequentative branch of tangere; source of English tax — a related card) → French tact "sense of touch" (inherited from Latin tactus) → figurative French tact "mental delicacy, delicate discrimination" (18th c., in the culture of politeness) → English tact (mid-18th c., c. 1740s) "delicate social sensitivity, adroitness in dealing with others" → TACT (exact form of this card) Note on nuance: the shift from "sense of touch" to "delicate social sensitivity" happened inside French in the 18th century and was carried into English at the moment of borrowing; no further change is needed. The chain is linear and attested from Latin onward; PIE *tag- is reconstructed but is one of the most secure touch-roots of the family.
