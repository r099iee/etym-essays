- A native Middle English verb trillen means to turn, spin, whirl — a rolling motion, from an ancient root of turning and boring.
- In the 17th century English music borrows Italian trillo and trillare for the quavering ornament of the voice and instruments, and the two strands fuse.
- Through the 18th–19th centuries trill is the standard word for bird-song warbling and for the rolled r of speech.
- In the 19th–20th centuries it generalises to any fast, trembling, bright sound — laughter, insects, telephones.
- Today trill is at once a precise musical term, a phonetic label, and a vivid everyday verb for a bird's quick bright burst of song.
