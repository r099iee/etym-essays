- 1982 coinage (Gould & Vrba) - Deliberately coined to displace “preadaptation”, stripping the false suggestion that a trait was designed in advance for its future role; a scholarly, reformist term born in paleobiology.
- 1980s–1990s (evolutionary biology) - Becomes a banner of the anti-adaptationist turn: exaptation sits beside “spandrel”, insisting evolution recycles parts without foresight; the connotation shifts from neutral coinage to a loaded critique of adaptationist orthodoxy.
- 21st century (evolutionary psychology, linguistics, tech) - Broadens into a general metaphor for repurposing: brain machinery “hijacked” for art and story, old systems retasked for new functions; it loses its technical edge and becomes a fashionable interdisciplinary word.
