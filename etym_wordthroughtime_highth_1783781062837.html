Old English Abstract Noun - Highth descends from the Old English abstract noun hēahþu, "height, summit," built from high with an abstract suffix, meaning vertical extent or the topmost point.
Middle English Spelling Tangle - Middle English scribes spell the word dozens of ways (heihte, heghte, heighte, hyght), and the form splits between analogical highth (built directly beside high) and the eventually standard height.
Early Modern Spelling Contest - In the 17th century highth and height compete in print; Milton uses highth in Paradise Lost for the sublime summit of his argument, giving the form an archaic, dignified character even as height wins the standard-language contest.
Contemporary Sense - Modern English uses height exclusively for both measurement and pinnacle; highth survives only in archaic reproduction and echoes of early modern poetry.
