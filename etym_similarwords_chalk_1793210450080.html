• Limestone: Similar because chalk is a variety of limestone, built of the same calcium carbonate; differs because limestone is the broad family — hard marbles, oolites, grey carboniferous — while chalk is only its softest, whitest, youngest member.
  • Calcite: Similar because both are crystalline calcium carbonate, the chemist's name for the chalk mineral; differs because calcite is the pure mineral of veins and spars, while chalk is the earthy oceanic rock of cliffs and classrooms.
  • Gypsum: Similar because modern blackboard chalk is very often pressed gypsum, and both are soft white writing stones; differs because gypsum is calcium sulphate — plaster of Paris in waiting — while true chalk is calcium carbonate from Cretaceous seas.
  • Marl: Similar because marl is another soft lime-rich earth spread on fields to sweeten them; differs because marl is a crumbly clay-limestone mixture of the lowlands, while chalk is the pure white highland rock.
  • Plaster: Similar because plaster of Paris coats walls in smooth white lime just as chalk whitens a board; differs because plaster sets hard through chemistry, while chalk merely crumbles — one builds, the other marks.
  • Crayon: Similar because both are sticks for drawing and marking; differs because a crayon is wax or oil pigment for colour and permanence, while chalk is dry dust for boards, cloth, and skin — temporary by design.
  • Pastel: Similar because pastels are chalk-like sticks of pigment for artists; differs because pastel names the coloured medium and its soft pictures, while chalk is white and workaday — classrooms and cliff faces, not galleries.
  • Whiting: Similar because whiting is powdered purified chalk itself, for paint, putty, and polish; differs because whiting is the processed powder of the trade, while chalk is the rock in the hill and the stick in the hand.
