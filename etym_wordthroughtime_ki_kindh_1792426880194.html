- [Epic Antiquity: c. 500 BCE–200 CE] — Immortalized in the *Kiṣkindhākāṇḍa* of the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* as the formidable cavern-capital of Vālin and Sugrīva, nestled among the rocky peaks of the southern wilderness — [perception: awe-inspiring forest kingdom representing nature's covenant and alliance with righteous dharma]
- [Classical Sanskrit Kāvya Era: c. 4th–10th c. CE] — Celebrated in Kālidāsa's poetry (*Raghuvaṃśa* Canto 13) and classical Sanskrit drama as the breathtaking Deccan landscape viewed from the flying Puṣpaka Vimāna, characterized by roaring waterfalls, granite crags, and primal loyalty — [perception: sublime romantic landscape of rugged granite hills, roaring rivers, and heroic monkey warriors]
- [Medieval Vijayanagara Imperial Epoch: c. 14th–16th c. CE] — The emperors of Vijayanagara chose Hampi as their imperial capital precisely because it was the sacred soil of Kiṣkindhā, identifying their defense of Hindu culture against northern sultanates with the martial heroism of Sugrīva and Hanumān — [perception: sacred heroic homeland providing divine royal legitimacy, martial courage, and imperial protection]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era: 19th–21st c.] — Universally recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site and vibrant pilgrimage destination at Hampi, Karnataka, evoking both ancient epic wonder and geological marvel — [perception: living sacred pilgrimage landscape and permanent geographic bridge connecting ancient myth to modern history]
