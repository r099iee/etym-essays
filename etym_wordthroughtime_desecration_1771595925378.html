- [16th century religious crime] - [Enters English as the direct antonym of consecration — the violent violation of what has been made holy; connotation wholly sacred, the language of churches, altars, and sanctified persons]
- [17th-18th century legal-theological] - [Used in religious and legal discourse of defiling holy places and things; connotation grave — an offence against God and against the reverence of the community]
- [19th century moral extension] - [Ruskin presses the altar-word into secular ethics: the 'desecration of the highest powers of the human mind'; misuse of man's best faculties is felt as an unhallowing — connotation solemn, prophetic, judicial]
- [20th-21st century figurative breadth] - [Applied to graves, monuments, memory, and ideals — any contemptuous violation of what a community holds sacred; still intense and rhetorical, but no longer requires a consecrated object]
