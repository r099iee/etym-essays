- Old English þæc: any roof or covering — the word is generic; þeccan means simply 'to cover'.
- Middle English: thatch narrows to roofs of straw and reeds — the default technology becomes the default meaning.
- 16th–17th c. – fire legislation pushes thatch out of the cities after the Great Fire of London (1666); it becomes the countryside's roofing.
- 18th–19th c. – the thatched cottage is romanticised as the rural idyll; 'thatch' is transferred to a thick head of hair (19th c. colloquial).
- 20th c. – thatching becomes a protected heritage craft; thatched = picturesque, chocolate-box, insurable at a premium.
- 21st c. – 'thatched' still roofs the world's countryside (and burns in fantasy literature), while the hair joke keeps it in everyday speech.
