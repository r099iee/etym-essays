- Early 11th century - the shire of Hertford: an administrative district organised around the ford and burh at Hertford, recorded in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle.
- Medieval period - a county of market towns, monasteries, and manors, dominated by St Albans Abbey and the traffic of the Great North Road.
- 16th–18th centuries - a prosperous agricultural and coaching county close to London, associated with gentry estates, malt, grain, and the road to the north.
- 19th century - the railway transforms the county into a commuter hinterland; towns expand and London money reshapes the countryside.
- 20th century - garden cities, aviation, film studios, and postwar suburbanisation give Hertfordshire a modern identity as a mixed commuter and technology county.
- 21st century - “Hertfordshire” signals leafy affluence, London proximity, and a strong local heritage brand; “Herts” is the everyday shorthand.
