The root word loft descends from a Germanic element-word with no secure Indo-European ancestor; the attested record therefore begins at the Proto-Germanic stage, and honesty requires stating that the PIE stage is unknown rather than inventing it.

Stage 1 — Proto-Germanic *luftuz, “air, sky, upper region” (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE). No secure PIE cognates exist outside Germanic; a speculative link to PIE *leup-, “to peel, strip” (the sky as what lies “stripped bare” above the world) has been proposed but is not generally accepted.
Stage 2 — Gothic luftus, “air” (attested in Wulfila's Bible, 4th century CE): the East Germanic branch preserves the element-word in its pure atmospheric sense.
Stage 3 — Old Norse lopt, “air, sky; also upper room, attic” (attested c. 9th–13th century): the North Germanic branch performs the metonymic shift from the element “air” to the enclosed air-chamber under a roof.
Stage 4 — Middle English loft (also louft, lofte), “upper room, attic, gallery” (borrowed from Old Norse lopt, 13th century).
Stage 5 — Modern English loft (noun: attic, gallery, hayloft, organ loft; verb “to strike or throw high”; derivative lofty, 14th century).

Cognate branches (not the direct line, recorded for completeness): Old English lyft, “air, sky, heaven”, which survived in northern dialect as lift, “the sky”; Old High German luft → German Luft, “air”; Old Dutch lucht → Dutch lucht, “air, sky”; Old Norse á lopt, “in the air” → English aloft (13th century); Old Norse lypta, “to raise” → English lift (verb).

Note on debate/nuance: Because *luftuz has no secure cognates outside Germanic, its PIE ancestry is genuinely unknown. The proposed link to *leup- (“to peel, strip”) rests on the notion that the sky is what lies “stripped bare” above the world; the evidence is thin, and standard etymological dictionaries (OED, Etymonline) either begin the story at Proto-Germanic or flag the *leup- connection as speculative. The word is a rare case where the derivation chain is short and begins late — the honest answer to “what is the PIE root?” is “none that can be established”.
