- Old English - drencan means to make drink, to submerge, to drown; the verb is the causer, forcing water in upon its subject.
- Middle English - the sense settles on soaking: to saturate thoroughly, rain driven into a helpless body to the skin.
- 17th-19th centuries - drenched is the standard word for being soaked through by rain or sea, and extends to 'drenched in sweat or blood'.
- Contemporary - drenched describes the victim of wetness — sudden, violent, complete — where soaked describes a sponge.
