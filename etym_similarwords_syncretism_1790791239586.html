• Amalgamation: Similar because both involve the union or blending of distinct elements into a whole; differs because amalgamation often refers to a physical, structural, or corporate merging without necessarily involving the reconciliation of conflicting philosophies or belief systems.
  • Eclecticism: Similar because both draw components from diverse and heterogeneous sources; differs because eclecticism selects the best elements from various systems to construct a personalized or practical viewpoint without necessarily synthesizing them into an overarching, unified philosophical framework.
  • Hybridization: Similar because both result in a novel mixed entity formed from divergent parent traditions; differs because hybridization emphasizes biological or postcolonial cross-breeding and structural fusion, whereas syncretism explicitly centers on religious, theological, and ideological reconciliation.
  • Assimilation: Similar because both concern cultural interaction and transformation; differs because assimilation requires a subordinate group to absorb and conform to the dominant culture's norms, whereas syncretism entails a two-way structural fusion creating a mutually altered system.
  • Synthesis: Similar because both represent the combination of opposing or disparate ideas into a new coherent whole; differs because synthesis is a formal dialectical or intellectual process (thesis-antithesis-synthesis), whereas syncretism often occurs through organic, historical, and grassroots cultural evolution.
