- Roman foundation tradition - Romulus, the legendary founder and first king of Rome (753 BC), organized its tribes and Senate and was carried up to heaven in a storm, apotheosized as the god Quirinus, so his name stood from the start for the city's myth of self-foundation.
- Ancient and medieval usage - in Roman literature and later antiquarian tradition he was the type of the heroic founder whose existence was nevertheless questioned, the standard against which all other national origin-figures were judged.
- Late-antique revival - the name was revived in late antiquity (Romulus Augustulus, the boy-emperor deposed in 476), so the heroic founder and the pathetic last emperor became a favourite contrast of historical writing.
- Contemporary sense - Romulus denotes the legendary founder of Rome, inseparable from his twin Remus, and in Macaulay's catalogue he is the type-case of the mythical person whose very existence may be doubted.
