• Hammering: Similar because it names the physical beating of metal into shape, the central act of forging; differs because it describes only the percussive motion, not the heating, shaping, or creative result, and it lacks forging's figurative and criminal senses.
  • Shaping: Similar because forging is above all shaping matter to a purpose; differs because shaping is far wider (wood, clay, policy, opinion) and carries none of forging's heat, force, or deception senses.
  • Smithing: Similar because it is the craft term for the smith's trade of which forging is the core act; differs because smithing names the whole craft (including fitting, riveting, tempering, quench-hardening) and is never used of documents or fraud.
  • Moulding (molding): Similar because both mean forming material into a shape; differs because moulding implies softer, cooler material shaped by a mould or by hand, not hot metal beaten under blows, and it has no counterfeiting sense.
  • Fashioning: Similar because both mean deliberate construction, physical or figurative; differs because fashioning is gentler and more elegant, suggesting skill and taste rather than heat and force.
  • Crafting: Similar because both denote skilful making; differs because crafting is modern, artisanal, and positive (handmade goods), and never means counterfeiting.
  • Creating / producing / manufacturing: Similar because all mean bringing something into existence, as forging does in its extended sense; differ in scale and character - creating is broad and neutral-to-lofty, producing is output-driven, manufacturing is industrial and impersonal - and none implies the smith's fire or fraud.
  • Building / constructing: Similar because both describe assembling something substantial; differs because they suggest adding parts together, not reshaping a single lump under pressure, and they lack the craft and deceit senses.
  • Fabricating: Similar because its primary sense "to make, construct" overlaps forging, and its secondary sense "to invent falsely" parallels forging's counterfeit sense; differs because fabricating is often used of excuses and data, and lacks the metallic, heated image entirely.
  • Counterfeiting: Similar because it is the precise crime-name for forging documents, money, and art; differs because counterfeiting is exclusively the wrongful imitation, while forging still carries its honest craft sense.
  • Faking: Similar because both mean producing something false; differs because faking is informal and broad (faking an accent, faking a smile), and never suggests the skill of the artisan.
  • Falsifying: Similar because both can mean altering or producing a false document; differs because falsifying emphasises distortion of records and evidence rather than the crafted imitation.
  • Inventing / devising: Similar because Middle English forgen once meant "to invent, devise (a tale)" - the very bridge to counterfeiting; differs because inventing today means original creation and never has the criminal or metallic sense.
  • Stamping / pressing: Similar because both are industrial processes of shaping metal by force; differs because they name machine-specific techniques (dies, presses) without the smith's fire, and with no figurative use.
