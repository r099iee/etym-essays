• EUPHEMISM: Similar because both are deliberate substitution strategies for a sensitive word and are frequently discussed as a matched pair (the euphemism–dysphemism axis); differs because a euphemism distances and softens, substituting a gentle or pleasant term ("passed away"), while a dysphemism is its mirror-image, substituting something harsh, crude, or degrading to provoke or demean.
  • PEJORATIVE: Similar because both carry negative evaluation and can name a hostile word; differs because a pejorative is any negatively-charged word, affix, or sense that needs no neutral counterpart to exist, while a dysphemism specifically names a degrading substitution chosen over a neutral term — the substitution and the deliberate choice are essential to dysphemism, not to pejorative.
  • VULGARISM: Similar because both occupy a crude, low register; differs because vulgarism refers to coarse or obscene language generally, without any requirement of substitution or deliberate strategy, while a dysphemism is a targeted replacement designed to demean, shock, or deflate.
  • INVECTIVE: Similar because both can be abusive and hostile; differs because invective is sustained vituperation or insulting language directed at a person, whereas a dysphemism may target a concept, a process, or the dead and is defined by its function as a substitution, not by personal attack.
  • ABUSE: Similar because both involve harsh, offensive words; differs because abuse is the act or practice of insulting and mistreating someone, a broader behavioural term, while a dysphemism is a specific linguistic unit used for a communicative end — and a dysphemism can be comic or affectionate where abuse cannot.
  • INSULT: Similar because both are offensive; differs because an insult is an act or remark intended to hurt a person's dignity, while a dysphemism may be aimed at an object, a dead body, or a process, and its defining feature is replacing a neutral term rather than wounding a specific person.
  • PROFANITY: Similar because both breach polite speech; differs because profanity is irreverent, blasphemous, or obscene language itself, without the requirement of substitution, while a dysphemism is the deliberate deployment of such language as a stand-in for a standard term.
  • TABOO WORD / SWEAR WORD: Similar because both draw their force from social prohibition; differs because a taboo word is merely a banned or shocking item of vocabulary, while a dysphemism is the strategic use of such items in place of a neutral term — the strategy, not the word, is what makes it a dysphemism.
  • SLANG: Similar because both can be blunt, informal, and vivid; differs because slang is informal nonstandard vocabulary shared by a group, often affectionate and neutral in force, while a dysphemism is defined by its harsh, degrading function and its status as a deliberate substitution.
  • MOCKERY: Similar because both can express derision and contempt; differs because mockery is an act or attitude of ridiculing someone, while a dysphemism is a linguistic substitution that may serve mockery, defiance, or the puncturing of solemnity — mockery is a motive, dysphemism a mechanism.
  • DERISION: Similar because both belittle and degrade; differs because derision is contemptuous laughter or ridicule, a behaviour, whereas a dysphemism is a word or phrase whose harshness performs the degradation; derision can be expressed without any dysphemism at all.
  • CACOPHEMISM: Similar because both are rare technical names for the harsh opposite of euphemism; differs because cacophemism (from Greek kakos "bad" + phēmē) is a very rare scholarly variant for dysphemism, while dysphemism is the standard, established term in linguistics and rhetoric.
