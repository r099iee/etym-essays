• Girls: Similar because both name young females; differs because girl is age-general and modern-neutral, covering children and adolescents, while maiden is archaic-elevated and insists on unmarried young womanhood.
  • Young women: Similar because both denote female adults; differs because young woman is plain, modern description, while maiden carries literary virginity, innocence, and pathos.
  • Damsels: Similar because both are young women of romance; differs because a damsel is courtly, often in distress and waiting to be rescued, while a maiden is the broader literary young-unmarried woman.
  • Lasses: Similar because both are familiar names for young women; differs because lass is regional and dialectal (chiefly northern English and Scots), while maiden is formal and archaic-elevated.
  • Virgins: Similar because both denote unmarried, sexually chaste women; differs because virgin states sexual status explicitly and clinically, while maiden implies it more gently and romantically.
  • Maids: Similar because both are short forms of the same word-family for young women; differs because maid has specialized toward "female servant," while maiden keeps the literary young-woman sense.
  • Brides: Similar because both concern marriageable women; differs because a bride is specifically a woman on her wedding day, already married in the moment of the ceremony, while a maiden is pre-marital.
  • Nymphs: Similar because both are young female figures of legend; differs because a nymph is a mythological nature-spirit, not a human girl, and lacks the moral colour of maidenhood.
  • Spinsters: Similar because both denote unmarried women; differs because a spinster is an older unmarried woman with a modern social charge, while a maiden is young and literary.
