LINEAR CHAIN FOR "reeds" (plural of "reed", "tall hollow-stemmed wetland grass")

NOTE ON ORIGIN: The deeper Indo-European ancestry of "reed" is uncertain and debated; no secure PIE root is universally accepted. The attested Germanic record is solid, but the step before Proto-Germanic is speculative. The chain below gives the attested record and flags the uncertain stage explicitly.

Stage 1 — uncertain deeper origin: Various proposals link Proto-Germanic *hreudą to a root meaning "to clear (land)" (the reed as the plant of cleared, open marshy ground) or to an Indo-European root for "shake, sway, tremble" (the plant's trembling motion in water), but neither connection is securely established; OED marks the further etymology as uncertain. This stage is therefore stated as UNKNOWN/DEBATED rather than invented.

Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic: *hraudą / *hraudaz (c. 500 BCE – 1st millennium CE) — "reed, marsh grass". The reconstructed Germanic noun for the plant. Cognates include Old Saxon hreid, Old High German hriot (later German Ried "marsh, marshy ground, reed"), and Old Norse hreyrr.

Stage 3 — Old English: hrēod (before 12th century, attested in Old English) — "marsh grass, reed". The English descendant of the Germanic noun, still with the initial hr- cluster.

Stage 4 — Middle English: rede, reade (12th–15th centuries) — "reed". The initial h is lost in the course of Middle English (a regular sound change in which word-initial hr-, hl-, hn- simplified to r-, l-, n-).

Stage 5 — English: reed (16th century onward) — "the tall hollow-stemmed wetland grass; also a musical pipe, a writing pen, a strip of cane used to sound a wind instrument".

Stage 6 — English: reeds (plural) — the ordinary plural, referring to a stand or clump of such plants. REEDS ◄ TARGET

NOTE ON DEBATE / NUANCE: The most uncertain point is the pre-Germanic stage. Pokorny's proposed root *krei-/*krey- with a sense related to shaking or to filtering (cf. a riddle or sieve made from reeds) has been suggested, and some connect the family to the concept of "cleared land" (German Ried "marshland"), but none of these links is secure enough to present as fact. The Germanic stage onward — *hraudą → hrēod → rede → reed — is well attested and uncontroversial.
