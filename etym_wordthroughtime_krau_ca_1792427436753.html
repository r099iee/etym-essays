- [c. 1200–800 BCE: Vedic Samhitās & Brāhmaṇas] — Attested in late Vedic texts (Yajurveda, Atharvaveda) as *kruñca* / *krauñca*, naming a wetland crane identified by its piercing vocalizations — [perception: wild wading bird observed in sacrificial wetland rituals and seasonal migrations]
- [c. 500 BCE–200 CE: Epic Formulation in the Rāmāyaṇa & Mahābhārata] — Elevated to supreme literary significance as the catalyst of Sanskrit poetry (*krauñca-vadha*), while also naming a major continental division (*Krauñca Dvīpa*), a pierced Himalayan mountain, and a strategic crane military formation (*krauñca-vyūha*) — [perception: sacred icon of marital devotion, tragic pathos (*karuṇā*), and epic literary genesis]
- [c. 200–1200 CE: Classical Sanskrit Kāvya & Aesthetic Treatises] — Universally cited by Sanskrit dramatists and literary theorists (such as Ānandavardhana in *Dhvanyāloka* and Abhinavagupta in *Locana*) as the supreme exemplar of how personal grief (*śoka*) sublimes into aesthetic artistic rasa (*śloka*) — [perception: theoretical archetype for all aesthetic poetic creation]
- [Modern Era: Ornithology & Environmental Heritage] — Identified definitively with the Sarus crane (*Antigone antigone*) and revered in modern South Asian cultural conservation as a living symbol of India's classical literary heritage — [perception: cherished national emblem of avian fidelity and living relic of ancient epic landscapes]
