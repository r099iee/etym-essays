- Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE) - alludere meant "to play with, jest, joke with, or mock playfully."
- Renaissance Humanism (c. 1530–1600) - Borrowed into English to mean "to play upon words, make a pun, or make a sportive comparison."
- 17th Century (c. 1620–1680) - Shifted decisively from playful joking to rhetorical indirect reference and literary intertextuality.
- Victorian Era (c. 1837–1901) - Standardized in formal British and American literature (Conan Doyle, Macaulay) as the quintessential intellectual term for covert mention.
- Contemporary Era - Maintained universally across academic literary criticism, political commentary, and formal journalism.
