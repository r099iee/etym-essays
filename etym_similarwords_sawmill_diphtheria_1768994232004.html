• Lumber mill: Similar in being a factory where logs become boards; differs because "lumber mill" emphasizes the product (lumber), while "sawmill" emphasizes the process — the sawing of the tree.
  • Timber mill: Similar in being a sawing factory for logs; differs because "timber mill" stresses the structural material (timber), while "sawmill" names the machine and the act of sawing itself.
  • Lumberyard: Similar in belonging to the same timber world; differs because a "lumberyard" is an open storage ground for logs and boards with no sawing machinery at all, while the sawmill is the powered place of production.
  • Sawpit: Similar in being a place where logs are sawn into boards; differs because a "sawpit" is the older pre-industrial arrangement of two men sawing by hand, one above and one below in a pit, where the sawmill is the machine-driven factory.
  • Mill: Similar in being a powered factory; differs because "mill" is the generic word for any processing plant (gristmill, cotton mill, steel mill), while "sawmill" specifies the sawing of timber.
  • Gristmill: Similar in being a powered mill of the same family; differs because a "gristmill" grinds grain to flour, while a "sawmill" saws logs to boards — different material, different machinery, same mill-concept.
  • Planing mill: Similar in being a timber-processing factory; differs because a "planing mill" smooths and finishes already-sawn boards, while the "sawmill" does the primary cutting of the log.
  • Croup: Similar in being a childhood throat disease with a harsh, obstructing cough; differs because "croup" is a syndrome of airway inflammation (often viral), while "diphtheria" is the specific bacterial infection with the leathery membrane.
  • Laryngitis: Similar in inflaming the throat; differs because "laryngitis" is a mild inflammation of the voice box, while "diphtheria" is a severe, contagious bacterial disease with membrane and systemic toxin.
  • Tonsillitis: Similar in a sore, inflamed throat; differs because "tonsillitis" is an infection of the tonsils alone, usually milder, while "diphtheria" forms a grey membrane that can block breathing and carries a heart-and-nerve toxin.
  • Scarlet fever: Similar in being a feared, contagious childhood disease of the era; differs because scarlet fever is caused by streptococcal toxin with a rash, while diphtheria is the bacterial throat disease with the membrane.
  • Plague: Similar in being a historical killer disease with pesthouses; differs because "plague" is the bubonic disease of rats and fleas, while "diphtheria" is the throat infection spread by droplets among children.
  • Quinsy: Similar in being a severe throat abscess; differs because "quinsy" is a localized pus collection beside the tonsil, while "diphtheria" is the systemic contagious membrane disease.
  • Pesthouse: Similar in belonging to the world of the passage's quarantine; differs because "pesthouse" names the isolation building itself, while "diphtheria" names the disease that fills it.
