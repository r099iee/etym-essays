- Early 20th c. (c. 1900–1915) - Coined in Modern English as a compound participial adjective (out- + mode + -ed), initially applied within the sartorial and decorative arts to designate clothing, garments, and interior decor that had slipped outside the boundaries of current Parisian or high-society fashion (la mode).
- 1920s to 1940s - Broadened rapidly during the Second Industrial Revolution and the interwar era to encompass manufacturing machinery, naval equipment, military tactics, and industrial infrastructure eclipsed by electrical and assembly-line innovations.
- Mid-20th c. (1950s–1970s) - Extended into intellectual, academic, and scientific realms to characterize discarded theoretical models (such as the caloric theory, phlogiston, or Galenic humoral pathology) and superseded socioeconomic dogmas.
- Late 20th c. to Present - Pervades digital computing, corporate workflows, software architectures, and sociocultural values, frequently carrying a sharp critical edge denoting systems, policies, or dogmatic assumptions that stubbornly fail to adapt to contemporary reality.
