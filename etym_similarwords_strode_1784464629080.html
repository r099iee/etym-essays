• Walked: Similar because both are forms of moving on foot; differs because walked is the neutral, unmarked word, carrying no information about step length or confidence, whereas strode insists on long, purposeful steps.
  • Marched: Similar because both are firm, deliberate, forward-moving walks; differs because marched is rhythmically military or mechanical, in step and possibly in formation, whereas strode is the free, confident stride of an individual.
  • Stalked: Similar because both describe a controlled, deliberate gait; differs because stalked is predatory, stiff, or angrily tense (stalking prey), whereas strode is confident without menace.
  • Strided: Similar because it is the would-be regular past of the same verb; differs because it is a rare, non-standard form — the correct strong past is strode.
  • Paced: Similar because both involve regular, measured walking; differs because paced usually means walking back and forth anxiously or measuring distance, while strode is forward, purposeful travel.
  • Tramped: Similar because both are long, covering walks; differs because tramped is heavy-footed, weary, or marching over rough ground, lacking the clean confidence of strode.
  • Paraded: Similar because both can be showy, self-possessed walking; differs because paraded is ostentatious display or formal procession, whereas strode need not be theatrical at all.
  • Strutted: Similar because both suggest confident walking; differs because strutted is swaggering, puffed-up self-display, while strode is quiet, natural authority.
  • Swaggered: Similar because both imply self-assured movement; differs because swaggered is arrogant, rolling, and exhibitionist, the opposite of the controlled energy of strode.
  • Sauntered: Similar because both are walking verbs with attitude; differs because sauntered is leisurely, loose, and unhurried to the point of idleness, while strode is brisk and purposeful.
  • Strolled: Similar because both are relaxed walking verbs; differs because strolled is easy and aimless, with no long-step or decisive intent.
  • Plodded: Similar because both are sustained walking; differs because plodded is slow, heavy, and laborious, the precise opposite of the energy of strode.
  • Trudged: Similar because both describe long walking; differs because trudged is weary, reluctant, and heavy-footed.
  • Bestrode: Similar because it shares the same Germanic root-family and means to stand or sit astride; differs because bestrode is the archaic astride sense (as in Shakespeare), no longer a synonym of long-step walking.
