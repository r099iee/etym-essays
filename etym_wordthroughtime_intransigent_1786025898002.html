- 1873 - [Spanish Radical Politics] - Coined in Spanish as *los intransigentes* to label radical federalist republicans who refused any alliance or constitutional compromise with monarchists.
- 1876–1880 - [French Cultural & Avant-Garde Borrowing] - Adopted in Paris as *les intransigeants*, applied not only to radical communards but also to the Impressionist painters who defiantly refused salon conventions.
- 1879–1900 - [English Diplomatic & Political Entry] - Entered English as a foreign loanword denoting political extremists and irreconcilables who blocked legislative consensus.
- 1900s–Present - [General Formal Usage] - Broadened across English discourse to describe any rigid, unyielding negotiating posture in labor disputes, treaty negotiations, and corporate governance.
