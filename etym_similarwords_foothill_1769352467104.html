• Hillock: Similar because both are small hills; differs because a hillock is any small mound anywhere in the landscape, while a foothill is specifically the low hill standing at the base of a mountain range.
  • Knoll: Similar because both name a small, rounded elevation; differs because a knoll is a small, smooth, isolated mound with no implication of lying before mountains.
  • Hill: Similar because both are natural elevations of land; differs because hill is the general word for any rise, with no requirement of being at the base of a mountain range.
  • Piedmont: Similar because both name land at the foot of mountains; differs because piedmont is the broader geological region (from Italian piedmonte "foot of mountain"), the whole belt of foothills and slopes at a range's edge, while a foothill is one specific hill within that belt.
  • Slope: Similar because both involve rising ground; differs because a slope is an inclined surface (a gradient) rather than a discrete hill, and slopes can occur anywhere.
  • Butte: Similar because both are hills associated with mountain and mesa country; differs because a butte is an isolated, flat-topped hill with steep sides, a dry-country feature, not a low outlier of a mountain range.
  • Highlands: Similar because both name elevated land; differs because highlands are extensive regions of high country, far more massive than a foothill and usually not at the foot of a range.
  • Piedmont belt: Similar because both describe the whole series of low hills before a range; differs because the belt is a zone of many hills, whereas a single foothill is one hill within it.
