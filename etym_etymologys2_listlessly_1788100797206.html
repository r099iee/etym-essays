PIE (reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*leus-
"to want, desire, crave"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*lustuz
"desire, pleasure, appetite"
↓
Old English (8th–10th c.)
lyst
"desire, pleasure, lust"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
list / lust
"pleasure, inclination, appetite"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
listles
"without desire, heedless"
"list" + suffix "-les" = "without"
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
listless
"lacking vigour, energy, or desire"
↓
Modern English (1727 onward)
LISTLESSLY
"without energy, interest, or desire"

Note: the PIE root *leus- is a standard reconstruction (Pokorny) but its exact shape is debated. Listless is NOT related to listen: that verb descends from PIE *ḱlew- “to hear” — an etymological false friend.
