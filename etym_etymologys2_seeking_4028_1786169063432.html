PIE *seh₂g- (also written *sāg-; to track, smell out, seek out, perceive keenly) — earliest known stage
├── Proto-Germanic: *sōkijaną (to seek out, to search for; a causative or iterative formation built on the *sōk- grade)
│ ├── Old English: sēcan / sēcean (to search, seek, visit, approach; a class 1 weak verb, sēcan-sōhte-gesōht)
│ │ └── Middle English: seken / sechen
│ │ └── English: seek → seeking (present participle, gerund, and participial adjective) ◄ TARGET
│ ├── Old Norse: sœkja; Gothic: sōkjan; Dutch: zoeken; German: suchen (to seek)
├── Cognates of *seh₂g-: Latin sāgīre (to perceive keenly, track by scent) → sagāx (keen-scented, shrewd) → English sagacious, presage; Old Irish saigid (seeks, goes toward)
└── Note: Two scholarly nuances — (1) the root is written *seh₂g- in laryngeal notation and *sāg- in the older system, the same root; (2) the Greek hēgéomai (to lead, guide; the basis of English hegemony) is sometimes cited as a cognate, but the correspondence is debated, and several handbooks treat the Greek form as belonging to a different root. The Germanic chain *sōkijaną → sēcan → seken → seek is secure.
