Sanskrit (c. 1st millennium CE)
ḍoṅgara-
"hill, hilly country, rugged mound"
↓
Old / Middle Marathi (c. 13th–15th c.)
ḍoṅgara / ḍoṅgrī
"hilly; Dongri (promontory of Bombay)"
↓
Marathi / Hindi textile term (c. 16th c.)
ḍuṅgrī-kapṛā
"coarse cloth produced in Dongri"
↓
Anglo-Indian (East India Co.) (early 17th c., c. 1610s)
dungaree
"coarse blue or unbleached calico"
↓
Nautical English (late 18th–19th c.)
dungarees
"trousers of coarse Indian cloth"
↓
Modern English (20th c.–present)
dungaree
"heavy cotton fabric; (pl.) bib overalls"

Note: Named after Dongri, a rocky hill quarter and fishing settlement on the eastern side of Bombay Island (now Mumbai, Maharashtra). Weavers in this locality produced an exceptionally durable, cheap cotton textile purchased by Portuguese and British East India Company traders for sail-making, barter in the Spice Islands, and rugged clothing for maritime crews.
