- 14th century - 'rough' is an adjective meaning coarse, unpolished, and unrefined in manners
- 19th century - the adjective is substantivized: a 'rough' is a coarse or violent man, and the plural 'roughs' names rowdies, street thugs, and hired muscle
- early 20th century - slang and journalistic usage continues ('a gang of roughs', 'hired roughs'), especially in British-influenced writing
- Contemporary - dated and period-flavored; modern speech prefers 'thugs', 'goons', or 'toughs', making 'roughs' a marker of old-fashioned criminal slang
