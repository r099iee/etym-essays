Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*tewh₂- / *tu-
"to swell, be strong, powerful, whole"
↓
Proto-Hellenic (c. 2000 BCE)
*swo- / *sawo-
"sound, safe, healthy, intact"
↓
Ancient Greek (archaic/classical period)
σῶς (sôs) / σαϝός (sawós)
"safe, sound, alive, unhurt"
↓
Ancient Greek (classical period)
σωτήρ (sōtḗr)
"savior, deliverer, preserver, guardian"
(agent noun formed with suffix -τήρ /-tḗr)
↓
Ancient Greek (classical/Hellenistic)
σωτηρία (sōtēría)
"deliverance, preservation, salvation, safety"
(abstract noun formed with suffix -ία /-ía)
↓
Late Greek / Hellenistic Adjective (Hellenistic period)
σωτήριος (sōtḗrios)
"saving, delivering, bringing salvation"
↓
Late Latin / Ecclesiastical Greek Loan (patristic era)
sōtēria
"salvation, deliverance"
↓
Modern English Scholarly Formation (19th c.–present)
SOTERIAL
"relating to salvation or spiritual deliverance"
(formed on Greek sōtēria + Latin adjectival suffix -al)
