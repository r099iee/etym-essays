- Early contact label (16th-17th c.) - Neutral-seeming outsider name used by traders and explorers for distant northern peoples.
- Heroic-explorer era (19th-early 20th c.) - Romanticized and paternalistic: noble, hardy primitives; indispensable guides yet portrayed as childlike; Peary-type usage.
- Anthropological codification (late 19th-mid 20th c.) - Clinical scientific umbrella term and language-family label; taught as standard in textbooks and museums.
- Indigenous activism and rejection (1970s-1990s) - Perceived as colonial slur in Canada/Greenland; replaced by Inuit; retained institutionally in linguistics.
- Contemporary contested usage - Offensive to use for Canadian Inuit; still accepted by many Alaskan Yupik as inclusive; surviving mainly in historical quotes and frozen compounds being renamed.
