First element — chiro-, hand:
Ancient Greek (8th c. BCE, Homer)
χείρ (kheir)
‘hand, arm; power’
↓
Hellenistic Greek (3rd–1st c. BCE)
χειρο-
‘hand- (combining form)’
↓
Late Latin (borrowed) (medieval)
chiro-
‘hand- in learned terms’
↓
Modern English (17th c.)
chiro-
‘of the hand’

Second element — graphic, writing:
PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*gerbh-
‘to scratch, carve’
↓
Ancient Greek (8th c. BCE)
γράφειν (graphein)
‘to scratch, draw, write’
↓
Ancient Greek (classical)
-γραφικός (-graphikos)
‘pertaining to writing’
↓
Latin (borrowed) (1st c. BCE)
-graphicus
‘of writing, drawing’
↓
Modern English (17th c. compound)
chirographic
‘of handwriting’

Note: Greek kheir has no certain PIE etymology and Beekes treats it as likely Pre-Greek substrate vocabulary; the link of graphein to *gerbh- is the standard handbook connection but remains debated, with some scholars preferring a purely Aegean origin for the writing word.
