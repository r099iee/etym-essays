Linear derivation chain (single root word): Proto-Indo-European *per- "feather, wing" (reconstructed root, c. 4500–3000 BCE; the source of feather- and wing-words across the family) │ └─> PIE *por-nó- "feathered one" (reconstructed derivative built with the -no- suffix; the fern conceived as the feathery, winged plant) └─> Proto-Germanic *farną "fern" (reconstructed West-Germanic plant-name, c. 500 BCE–200 CE; meaning literally "the feathered plant") ├─> Old English fearn "fern" (attested c. 9th–11th c.) │ └─> Middle English fern(e), ferne "fern" (attested 12th–15th c.) │ └─> English fern "fern" (modern form, unchanged in sense) │ └─> FERN (the exact form of this card) ├─> Old Saxon farn "fern" → Middle Low German varn "fern" ├─> Middle Dutch varne / varen "fern" → Dutch varen "fern" └─> Old High German farn "fern" → Middle High German varn → German Farn "fern" Sister branches of the same root (not ancestors of English fern, but evidence of its source):
  • Sanskrit parṇá- "feather, wing, leaf" (Rigvedic; one word straddling bird and foliage) → Hindi parna "leaf".
  • Avestan parǝna- "feather".
  • Proto-Slavic *pero "feather" → Russian peró, Polish pióro, Czech péro (pen — the quill-pen being literally a feather).
  • Reduplicated Slavic and Baltic fern-names from the same root: Russian paporotnik "fern", Lithuanian papartis "fern" — a separate formation meaning "the feather-feather plant".
  • Greek pterón "wing, feather" (second element of pterodactyl, helicopter) — traditionally grouped with *per-, but many scholars derive it from *pet- "to fly" (cf. Greek pétomai "I fly"); the question is unsettled and is noted, not decided, here.
  • Latin pennapenna "feather" — possibly related via *pet-no- (again the *pet- vs *per- crux); not used in this chain.
Note on the record: the North Germanic languages do not use this root for the fern — Icelandic burkni and Norwegian bregne descend from a different base (*bräkn, related to bracken) — so the Germanic fern-name is a West Germanic formation, which is why the chain above runs only through the West Germanic branch into English. The core chain Old English fearn → Middle English fern(e) → English fern → FERN is securely attested at every stage; no stage is invented.
