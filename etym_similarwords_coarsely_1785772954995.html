• Roughly — Similar because both describe unrefined, uneven action or treatment. Differs because roughly also means "approximately" (roughly ten, roughly done) and need not imply vulgarity or texture, while coarsely specifically means "in large grains/pieces" and, socially, "without refinement" — the direct opposite of finely.
  • Crudely — Similar because both mean lacking refinement or polish. Differs because crudely stresses primitiveness, unfinished quality, or blunt offensiveness (a crudely built shelter), while coarsely stresses grain, texture, and coarseness of manner, and is neutral in cooking.
  • Harshly — Similar because both describe rough, severe, or grating qualities. Differs because harshly emphasizes severity, cruelty, or discordance (harshly punished, harsh sound), while coarsely emphasizes lack of fineness or refinement, not cruelty.
  • Vulgarly — Similar because both describe behavior, speech, or taste lacking refinement. Differs because vulgarly stresses commonness, low taste, and often obscenity, while coarsely has a neutral physical sense (coarsely ground pepper) that vulgarly entirely lacks.
  • Indelicately — Similar because both describe tactless or rough behavior. Differs because indelicately is about sensitivity and tact (an indelicate question), while coarsely is about refinement and physical texture (coarsely woven cloth).
  • Boorishly — Similar because both describe rude, unpolished social behavior. Differs because boorishly (from boor) stresses clumsy, insensitive, peasant-like manners and is purely social, while coarsely covers both texture and manners.
  • Finely — Antonym; similar only in that both are manner adverbs for degree of division. Differs absolutely in meaning: finely means "in very small pieces" or "with great refinement," while coarsely means "in large pieces" or "without refinement" — the two define the opposite ends of the scale.
  • Unrefinedly / roughly made — Similar because both indicate a lack of fine finishing. Differs because unrefinedly is awkward and rare, while coarsely is the established adverb for both grain size and manners.
