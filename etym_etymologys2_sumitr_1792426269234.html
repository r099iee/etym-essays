Note on morphology and semantics: The Sanskrit proper name Sumitrā (सुमित्रा) is a classical Bahuvrīhi compound formed from the auspicious prefix su- ("good, noble", from PIE *h₁su-) and mitra ("friend, ally", from Proto-Indo-Iranian *mitrás). The PIE base *mey- originally related to reciprocal social contracts, which crystallized in Indo-Iranian as both the deity Mitra/Mithra and the common noun for a loyal friend. With the feminine ending -ā, the name translates literally to "she who possesses good friends" or "she who is an excellent companion"—an appellation that perfectly mirrors her role in the Rāmāyaṇa as the unshakeable, peaceful friend to co-queens Kausalyā and Kaikeyī.
