- 1682 to 1762 (French Imperial Colonial Realm) - Coined by René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle, to encompass the entire monumental watershed of the Mississippi River from the Great Lakes to the Gulf of Mexico, representing French geopolitical ambition and mercantilism in North America.
- 1762 to 1803 (Imperial Chessboard & Spanish Administration) - Partitioned between Britain and Spain, then secretly retroceded to Napoleonic France, symbolizing frontier geopolitical intrigue, diplomatic rivalry, and contested continental sovereignty.
- 1803 to mid-19th century (The Louisiana Purchase & U.S. Statehood) - Shifted from an imperial territory to the historic American land deal that fueled westward expansion; upon 1812 statehood, the name narrowed to the modern state, celebrated for its unique civil law code and New Orleans cosmopolitanism.
- Late 19th to 20th century (Plantation Mythos, Industrialization, & Cultural Flowering) - Perceived through the dual lens of Post-Reconstruction racial struggles, petrochemical industrial expansion ('Cancer Alley'), and an internationally renowned explosion of jazz, Cajun/Creole culinary culture, and Mardi Gras celebrations.
- 21st century to Present (Ecological Vulnerability, Resilience, & Cultural Heritage) - Viewed globally as a vibrant, irreplaceable crucible of American music and food, yet also as ground zero for climate change, severe coastal land loss, and devastating hurricanes (e.g., Katrina, Ida).
