• Barrel: Similar because both name the bulging wooden liquid vessel; differs because barrel is the general everyday word and a unit of measure while cask stresses wine, beer, and spirit storage with craft overtones.
  • Keg: Similar because both hold beer and are tapped for serving; differs because a keg is now usually a small metal pressurised container while a cask is traditionally wooden and larger, tied to real ale.
  • Hogshead: Similar because it is literally a large cask of fixed capacity; differs because hogshead names one specific size (about 52 imperial gallons for beer) while cask covers all sizes.
  • Butt: Similar because a butt is a great wine or beer cask; differs because butt is the giant of the cellar (a butt of sherry) while cask is the neutral hypernym.
  • Tun: Similar because a tun is the largest brewing cask; differs because tun now mostly survives in brewery vessels and the word ton, while cask is the ordinary portable barrel.
  • Vat: Similar because both are large vessels for fermenting or storing drink; differs because a vat is an open or fixed tank for making, while a cask is a closed movable barrel for keeping.
  • Drum: Similar because both are cylindrical shipping containers for liquids; differs because a drum is modern steel or plastic for industry while a cask is wooden and redolent of cellar and craft.
  • Firkin: Similar because a firkin is a small beer cask; differs because firkin is one fixed small measure (nine gallons) while cask is the whole family.
  • Casket: Similar in spelling and in naming a small precious container; differs because a casket holds jewels or, in American use, a coffin — never wine — and the resemblance is a famous false-friend trap.
