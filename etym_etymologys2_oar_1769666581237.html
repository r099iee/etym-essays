CHAIN — "oar" - Proto-Indo-European *h₁erh₁- "to row, propel by oars" (reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE) - Proto-Germanic *airō "oar" (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE) - Old English ār "oar" (attested 7th c.) - Middle English ore "oar" (13th–15th c.) - Modern English oar "long-bladed pole for rowing" (16th c.–present) → oar (target root word) Debate / nuance note: Latin rēmus "oar" (giving trireme), Greek eretmós, and Sanskrit aritra- are cognates, not ancestors — the English line runs purely through Germanic. The PIE reconstruction *h₁erh₁- is standard; an older citation form *er- is used in some sources. The meaning has remained "rowing pole with a blade" at every attested stage.
