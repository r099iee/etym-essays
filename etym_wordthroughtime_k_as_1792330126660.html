- Vedic Era (c. 1500–800 BCE) — Strictly concrete botanical sense in Vedic ritual and liturgy, denoting the joint, knot, or stalk-segment between two nodes of a reed, sugar cane, or bamboo plant (attested in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa).
- Late Vedic & Sūtra Period (c. 800–500 BCE) — Transferred into sacrificial and ceremonial manuals to denote a discrete operational section or ritual codex of the Veda dedicated to a specific category of sacrificial offerings.
- Epic Sanskrit Period (c. 500 BCE–400 CE) — Formalized as the principal structural book or master canto in heroic poetry, permanently establishing the seven canonical divisions of Vālmīki’s Rāmāyaṇa (Bāla, Ayodhyā, Araṇya, Kiṣkindhā, Sundara, Yuddha, and Uttara Kāṇḍas).
- Classical Philosophical Śāstras (c. 400–1200 CE) — Codified in theological hermeneutics to designate grand epistemological divisions of the Vedic revelation, contrasting Karma-kāṇḍa (the ritual action portion) with Jñāna-kāṇḍa (the philosophical wisdom/Upanishadic portion).
- 18th–19th Century (Colonial Indology) — Introduced into Western Orientalist scholarship by British philologists (William Jones, H.T. Colebrooke, Ralph Griffith) as an indispensable technical term for analyzing Sanskrit narrative architecture without reductionist translation.
- 20th–21st Century (Modern Scholarly & Global English) — Fully naturalized in global comparative literature, Indology, and religious studies, inflected with the English plural suffix -s to classify the canonical books of Hindu epic traditions.
