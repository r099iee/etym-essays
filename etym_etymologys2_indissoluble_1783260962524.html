Derivation chain for INDISSOLUBLE (single root word): 1. Proto-Indo-European *leu- "to loosen, divide, cut apart" (Pokorny IEW 681; attested in Greek lū́ein "to loosen," Latin luere "to loose, release, pay," Germanic *leusan "to lose"), c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE. 2. Latin luere "to loose, set free, release, pay" (root lu-; the "pay" sense from "loosening" an obligation), attested 2nd–1st c. BCE. 3. Latin solvere "to loosen away, unbind, dissolve, pay" (se- "apart" + luere "to loosen"), attested 2nd c. BCE. 4. Latin dissolvere "to break apart, loosen asunder, dissolve" (dis- "apart" + solvere), attested 1st c. BCE. 5. Latin dissolūbilis "capable of being loosened apart / dissolved" (dissolvere + -bilis "able to"), late Latin. 6. Latin indissolūbilis "not to be dissolved, unbreakable" (in- "not" + dissolūbilis), late Latin / medieval Latin. 7. Middle French indissoluble "not to be dissolved" (15th c.) — the immediate source of the English word. 8. Middle English / Modern English INDISSOLUBLE "not to be dissolved; unbreakable" (attested from c. 1540). Note: The twin chains via Old French (dissoudre) and Middle French (indissoluble) both descend from dissolvere; the modern English word is a Latin-French learned loan, and its negative prefix is in-, not un-.
