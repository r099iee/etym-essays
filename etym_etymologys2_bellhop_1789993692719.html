CHAIN B — hop
Proto-Germanic (1st millennium BCE)
*huppana
"to hop, spring up,"
"leap on one motion"
↓
Old English (before 1100)
hoppian
"to hop, dance, leap"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
hoppen
"to hop, skip, run"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–present)
hop

"hurry to a task"
↓
American English (c.1897–1905)

Note: bell is ultimately imitative, so any deeper PIE ascription is expressive rather than regular; hop likewise rests on an imitative-expressive Germanic base with no secure wider cognates asserted here.
