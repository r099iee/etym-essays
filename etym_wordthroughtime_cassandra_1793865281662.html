- Archaic & Classical Greece (c. 8th–4th c. BCE) - The tragic Homeric and Aeschylean prophetess; the supreme archetype of sacred inspiration paired with agonizing social alienation, bodily violation, and divine curse.
- Hellenistic & Roman Antiquity (c. 3rd c. BCE–4th c. CE) - Celebrated in Virgil's Aeneid and Seneca's tragedies as the ultimate symbol of ignored political wisdom, futile royal lamentation, and fatalistic Trojan suffering.
- Middle Ages & Renaissance (c. 1300–1650) - Moral exemplum in Boccaccio, Chaucer, and Shakespeare; cited by civic humanists as an illustration of sovereign arrogance and the fatal danger of ignoring wise counsel.
- 19th Century (Romantic & Victorian Literature) - Began transitioning into a common-noun eponym in political essays, parliamentary debates, and editorials for any commentator predicting national decay or economic collapse.
- Mid-to-Late 20th Century (Psychology & Psychoanalysis) - Codified into clinical and analytic theory as the 'Cassandra Complex' or 'Cassandra Dilemma', describing individuals whose truthful perceptions are systematically dismissed as hysterical.
- 21st Century (Colloquial, Ecological, & Technocratic Discourse) - Ubiquitous modern epithet for climate scientists, macroeconomic forecasters, whistleblowers, and artificial intelligence safety researchers whose data-driven warnings are brushed aside by complacent bureaucracies.
