• Actor: Similar because both denote a person who portrays a character in a dramatic production; differs because actor is the universal, neutral, everyday term, whereas thespian is a formal, elevated, or classically evocative descriptor.
  • Histrion: Similar because both are literary terms for stage performers derived from ancient languages (histrion from Etruscan/Latin histrio); differs because histrion often carries a derogatory connotation of deceit, overacting, or hollow histrionics.
  • Trouper: Similar because both describe theatrical veterans; differs because trouper emphasizes durability, professional loyalty, resilience, and uncomplaining work ethic in show business ("a real trouper").
  • Tragedian: Similar because both are dramatic performers; differs because a tragedian specifically specializes in serious, tragic drama rather than comedy or general theater.
  • Player: Similar because both denote a performer on stage; differs because player is an archaic or poetic term (frequent in Shakespeare) evoking the playing of roles and wandering troupes.
