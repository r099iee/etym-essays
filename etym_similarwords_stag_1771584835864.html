• Buck: Similar because both denote the male of a deer; differs because buck is used of smaller deer and of the males of many other animals (rabbit, goat, antelope), while stag is reserved for the larger Old World deer, especially the red deer with a full head of antlers.
  • Hart: Similar because both name an adult male deer; differs because hart is the archaic and poetic word for a male red deer, now rare and literary (Old English heorot), while stag is the living, standard term.
  • Deer: Similar because both name the same animal family; differs because deer is the general term covering the whole species and both sexes, while stag specifies the adult male.
  • Doe: Similar because both are sex-specific deer words; differs because doe names the adult female deer, whereas stag names the adult male, so the two are complementary opposites.
  • Hind: Similar because both are sex-specific deer words; differs because hind names the adult female red deer (the partner of the stag in the classic British pairing "stag and hind"), while stag is the male.
  • Roebuck: Similar because both denote a male of the deer kind; differs because roebuck is the male roe deer, a small woodland species, while stag properly names the larger red deer of the open forest.
  • Elk: Similar because both are large, antlered deer; differs because elk (in British usage the European moose; in American usage the wapiti) names a distinct larger species whose male is usually called a bull, not a stag.
