• Sprees: Similar because both refer to periods of unrestrained indulgence or activity (shopping sprees, drinking sprees); differs because a spree emphasizes lively, energetic, often carefree recreation, whereas binges frequently carries a stronger connotation of excessive, compulsive, or uncontrolled consumption.
  • Carouses: Similar because both describe boisterous, drunken partying; differs because carouses is a slightly older, literary word emphasizing loud, merrymaking tavern revelry and drinking songs rather than modern compulsive habits.
  • Bouts: Similar because both denote concentrated periods of an intense activity; differs because a bout is a neutral temporal unit that can apply to boxing matches, bouts of illness, or bouts of depression, whereas binges implies excessive overconsumption.
  • Orgies: Similar because both denote extreme, uninhibited self-indulgence; differs because an orgy historically and modernly evokes wild, sexually promiscuous, or Dionysian bacchanals, carrying far heavier moral and hedonistic intensity than a typical party or food binge.
  • Jamborees: Similar because both describe lively social celebrations; differs because a jamboree is a large, cheerful, noisy gathering or official convention (such as for Boy Scouts), free from the connotations of compulsive overindulgence.
  • Benders: Similar because both describe multi-day drinking bouts; differs because a bender is informal American and British slang used almost exclusively for prolonged, destructive alcohol or drug binges that incapacitate the individual.
  • Splurges: Similar because both involve sudden, excessive expenditure or indulgence; differs because a splurge specifically highlights spending lavish amounts of money on luxury items or fine dining as a treat, rather than compulsive bingeing.
