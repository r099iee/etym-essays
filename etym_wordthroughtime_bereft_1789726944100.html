- Old English literal plundering - bereafian means to rob, strip, despoil of goods or land; perception harsh, legal-military, a crime of seizure.
- Middle English widowed and orphaned - bereft widows, bereft children; connotation of pitiable persons robbed by death rather than thieves, elegiac and Christian-charitable.
- Early modern poetic desolation - Shakespeare and the Authorised Version use bereft for swords, senses, and hopes; image of noble emptiness, from battlefield to soul.
- Victorian funeral solemnity - bereft and bereaved become the respectable vocabulary of mourning cards and novels; feeling restrained, dignified grief, stiff upper lip.
- Contemporary literary lack - bereft of ideas, bereft of signal, bereft at a bus stop; from solemn widowhood to any droll or critical emptiness, still formal, still faintly mournful, capable of tenderness and irony as when Baker finds his future wife briefly bereft.
