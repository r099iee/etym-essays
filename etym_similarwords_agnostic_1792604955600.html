• skeptic: Similar because both question dogmatic claims and demand rigorous evidence before granting assent; differs because skeptic (from Greek sképtomai, "to examine, look about") is a broad intellectual methodology of systematic doubt across all subjects, whereas agnostic focuses specifically on the inherent unknowability of transcendent, metaphysical, and theological realities.
  • freethinker: Similar because both reject dogmatic religious authority in favor of rational inquiry; differs because freethinker carries a historical legacy of active anti-clerical rebellion and sociopolitical secularism, whereas agnostic denotes a calm, strictly epistemological conclusion about the boundaries of human knowledge.
  • nonbeliever: Similar because both do not profess religious faith; differs because nonbeliever is a simple, passive negative descriptor that defines an individual solely by the absence of belief, without articulating the principled philosophical rationale that defines an agnostic.
  • doubter: Similar because both experience hesitation and uncertainty regarding religious assertions; differs because doubter is often psychological, emotional, and vacillating—suggesting someone struggling with faith—whereas agnostic represents a stable, intellectually coherent philosophical posture.
  • empiricist: Similar because both assert that human knowledge must be grounded in sensory observation and scientific verification; differs because empiricist is a formal epistemology concerning how knowledge is acquired across all sciences, while agnostic is the specific metaphysical consequence of that epistemology applied to the supernatural.
  • atheist: Similar because both reject orthodox theological dogmatism; differs because atheist (from Greek a- "without" + theos "god") actively lacks or denies belief in deities, whereas an agnostic focuses on the question of knowledge (epistemology), asserting that whether a deity exists or not is fundamentally unproven or unknowable.
  • secularist: Similar because both operate without reliance on religious dogmas; differs because secularist pertains to political, social, and educational governance—advocating the strict separation of religious institutions from civic life—while agnostic is an individual philosophical conviction.
  • positivist: Similar because both hold that only verifiable scientific propositions possess cognitive validity; differs because positivist represents the formal philosophy of Auguste Comte and the Vienna Circle, which dismissed metaphysical statements as entirely meaningless nonsense, whereas agnostic treats them as questions beyond human verification.
  • rationalist: Similar because both elevate reason as the supreme criterion of truth; differs because historical rationalists (like Descartes and Leibniz) believed that pure reason could deduce metaphysical truths and prove the existence of God from first principles, whereas agnostics reject the claim that reason alone can penetrate transcendental realms.
  • deist: Similar because both reject revealed ecclesiastical religion and miraculous dogmas; differs because deist makes the positive metaphysical claim that an impersonal supreme intelligence created the universe and its physical laws, a claim that an agnostic refuses to make without empirical proof.
  • infidel: Similar because historically used to label those who did not adhere to orthodox faith; differs because infidel is an offensive, pejorative term used by religious dogmatists to condemn someone as faithless or treacherous, whereas agnostic is an objective, self-chosen philosophical descriptor.
  • questioner: Similar because both engage in continuous critical examination; differs because questioner is an informal conversational term for an inquisitive person, lacking the rigorous philosophical framework of agnosticism.
