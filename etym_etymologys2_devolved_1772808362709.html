Chain for DEVOLVED (single root word, from Latin dēvolvere): 1. PIE *wel- / *welh₁- — reconstructed root meaning "to turn, roll, wind, revolve"; attested indirectly through a vast Indo-European family of rolling/turning words (Latin volvere, Greek eiléō "to wind," Sanskrit val-/vṛ- "to turn," Germanic *welwan "to roll"). 2. Proto-Italic *wolw-e- type — the Italic development of the PIE root toward Latin volvere (reconstructed stage; not directly attested). 3. Latin volvere — "to roll, turn, revolve, unroll"; attested throughout Latin literature (Classical period, 1st century BC–AD); source of English volume, revolution, evolve, involve, etc. 4. Latin dēvolvere — "to roll down, roll away, fall down" (dē- "down, away from" + volvere); attested in Classical Latin, literal senses of rolling/falling; already developing the transferred sense of "to fall (to someone) by succession" in legal/imperial contexts. 5. Medieval/legal Latin dēvolutus / devolvere — the legal extension is formalized: rights, duties, or powers are said to devolve on heirs, successors, or a sovereign body (attested in medieval legal Latin). 6. Middle French dévoluer / devolver — "to transfer, to pass (power, rights) to another"; borrowed into English from the 14th–15th century; English devolve recorded from c. 1400 in the sense "to transfer or pass on (authority, duties)." 7. English devolve — "to transfer (power, responsibility, authority) to a lower level or another body"; and "to pass by legal succession"; past tense/past participle devolved. 8. English DEVOLVED — the note's rootWord, the past/participial form used in Gibbon's sentence "devolved on the emperors all the executive powers of government." Debated/nuanced note: The exact reconstruction of the PIE root (*wel- with various extensions) is standard, but some handbooks prefer *welh₁- with a laryngeal, and the specific Italic development is reconstructed rather than attested. The Latin and English stages, however, are fully documented; there is no uncertainty from Latin onward.
