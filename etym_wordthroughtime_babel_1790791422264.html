- c. 10th–6th c. BCE - *Biblical Hebrew בָּבֶל (Bāḇel)*: Named the ancient city of Babylon, folk-etymologized in Genesis 11 as the site where God confounded human speech.
- 4th c. CE - *Latin Vulgate & Medieval Theology*: Institutionalized throughout Christendom as the ultimate symbol of human pride, sinful hubris, and the tragic fracturing of universal human language.
- 1380s–1600s - *Late Middle & Early Modern English*: Shifted from a proper geographical/scriptural noun into a lowercase common noun (*a babel*) denoting any noisy, confused multilingual crowd or tumult.
- 1970s - *Translation Studies (George Steiner)*: Elevated in linguistics and literary criticism by George Steiner's *After Babel* (1975) as the definitive problem of human communication and hermeneutics.
- Late 20th–21st c. - *Postmodern Philosophy & Roland Barthes*: Reinterpreted positively in semiotics and poststructuralism not as a curse, but as a blissful celebration of polyphony, linguistic diversity, and textual pleasure.
