• Striped: Similar because both mean marked with lines of colour; differs because "barred" implies thick, straight, often horizontal bands like bars or rods, while "striped" is the general word for any lines.
  • Streaked: Similar because both mean marked with lines; differs because "streaked" suggests irregular, smeared, or uneven lines, while "barred" suggests regular bands.
  • Striated: Similar because both mean marked with parallel lines; differs because "striated" is a technical word for fine grooves or lines in natural surfaces, not broad bands of colour.
  • Banded: Similar because both mean marked with bands; differs because "banded" often implies rings or encircling bands and is the broader word, while "barred" suggests straight transverse bars.
  • Excluded: Similar because both mean kept out; differs because "excluded" lacks the image of a physical barrier — it simply states that someone or something is not included, while "barred" implies a bar across the way.
  • Prohibited: Similar because both mean forbidden; differs because "prohibited" is a formal and legal word for things forbidden by rule or law, while "barred" can be physical (a barred door) or institutional.
  • Banned: Similar because both mean officially forbidden; differs because "banned" is usually about items or activities ruled out by authority, while "barred" is commonly about people kept from places or professions.
  • Blocked: Similar because both mean obstructed; differs because "blocked" is general physical obstruction, while "barred" specifically involves a bar, rail, or legal prohibition.
  • Debarred: Similar because both mean formally prevented; differs because "debarred" is a technical and legal term for exclusion from a right or profession, while "barred" is the more general everyday word.
