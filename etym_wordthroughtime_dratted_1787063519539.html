- 1600s–1700s: The blasphemous curse *God rot [it/you]!* (expressing a wish for divine putrefaction upon an enemy) circulated in rough English speech.
- late 1700s–1815: Minced oaths contracted *God rot* into *'od rot*, *od-drot*, and eventually the monosyllabic exclamation *drat!* to avoid religious profanity.
- 1820s–1890s: English speakers appended participial suffixes to form the adjective *dratted*, widely used in Victorian domestic fiction (such as Dickens) for cantankerous but non-profane venting.
- 1900s–Present: Preserved in British, rustic, and fantasy literature (especially Tolkien) as a charming, cantankerous, and mildly exasperated exclamation.
