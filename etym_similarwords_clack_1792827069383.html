• Click: Similar because both name short sharp percussive sounds; differs because a click is lighter, smaller, and often electronic (mouse clicks), while a clack is harder, louder, and more percussive.
  • Clatter: Similar because both rattle with hard repeated impacts; differs because clatter is prolonged noisy confusion (pans clattering), while a clack can be a single clean snap.
  • Clap: Similar because both burst from flat surfaces meeting; differs because a clap booms with resonance (thunderclap, applause), while a clack stays thin, dry, and metallic.
  • Snap: Similar because both crack suddenly with breaking force; differs because a snap breaks or bites shut (fingers snapping, branches snapping), while a clack meets and rebounds without breaking.
  • Clatter of tongues: Similar because gossip is the oldest sense of clack itself; differs as phrase versus word, the idiom spelling out what clack implies in one syllable.
  • Chatter: Similar because both scold or describe rapid empty talk; differs because chatter is voluble and often cheerful (monkey chatter), while clack as talk is drier and more disapproving.
  • Rattle: Similar because both shake out loose repeated noise; differs because a rattle is hollow and loose (rattling windows), while a clack is tight and hard-edged.
  • Clang: Similar because both ring from metal struck; differs because a clang resonates deep and long (prison doors), while a clack dies the instant it is born.
  • Tick: Similar because both mark time with small hard sounds; differs because a tick is the clock's discreet pulse, while a clack announces work being done.
