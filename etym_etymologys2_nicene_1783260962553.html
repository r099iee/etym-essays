CHAIN — THE VICTORY ROOT (nīkē): Stage 1 — Ancient Greek nī́kē νίκη "victory" (attested from the earliest Greek literature; ultimate origin UNCERTAIN — no securely established Indo-European etymon is generally agreed, and the word is often suspected to be a Pre-Greek substrate loan; this point must remain open). Stage 2 — Greek Nī́kaia Νίκαια "Nicaea, city of victory" (the Bithynian city founded c. 301 BC by Antigonus Monophthalmus as Antigoneia and renamed Nicaea by Lysimachus after his wife or daughter Nicaea; the name was naturally interpreted as "city of victory", from nīkē). Stage 3 — Latin Nīcaea "Nicaea" (the standard Latin form of the Greek city name; attested in Roman sources, e.g. Pliny the Elder's Natural History, 1st century AD). Stage 4 — Latin Nīcaenus "of Nicaea" (the Latin adjective, as in fides Nicaena "the Nicene faith"; a Church-Latin formation of the 4th–5th century). Stage 5 — English Nicene (adjective; adopted from Latin Nicaenus in the 16th century — "Nicene Creed" is attested in English theological and historical prose from the 1540s–1550s). DEBATE NOTE: the root of Greek nīkē is genuinely disputed. A derivation from PIE *neik- "to reach, arrive, overcome" has been proposed but is not generally accepted; the modern consensus (e.g. Beekes) treats nīkē as a likely Pre-Greek word. The Greek-to-Latin-to-English chain after Nīkaia, by contrast, is historically secure.
