• fermenting: Similar because both describe the literal bubbling transformation behind beer and the figurative internal churning of trouble; differs because fermenting is technical and biological (yeast action) and rarely used of storms or news, while brewing spans kitchen, industry and metaphor.
  • steeping: Similar because both describe leaving leaves or grains in hot water to extract flavour; differs because steeping is passive (soaking only) and confined to tea-making, while brewing implies the fuller process with heat, boiling or fermentation.
  • infusing: Similar because both mean drawing flavour into liquid; differs because infusing is the polite technical word for steeping and has none of brewing’s ominous figurative force.
  • concocting: Similar because both mean combining ingredients or schemes into something new; differs because concocting stresses deliberate mixing and often implies artificiality or deception, while brewing suggests natural, unstoppable development below the surface.
  • developing: Similar because both mean something is taking shape over time; differs because developing is neutral, slow and used of everything from films to economies, while brewing is dramatic and specifically of ominous or imminent outcomes.
  • gathering: Similar because both mean accumulating force before breaking (“storm gathering” / “storm brewing”); differs because gathering pictures visible assembly of parts, while brewing pictures invisible internal transformation.
  • looming: Similar because both mean something threatening is about to happen; differs because looming stresses the visible, menacing approach of the threat itself, while brewing stresses its hidden preparation.
  • threatening: Similar because both mean something bad promises to happen; differs because threatening is explicit about the danger, while brewing keeps the outcome uncertain — it may yet dissipate.
  • simmering: Similar because both picture heat and agitation beneath a surface (“simmering discontent” / “brewing discontent”); differs because simmering suggests contained, ongoing low heat with possible boiling over, while brewing suggests a completed preparation about to be released.
  • seething: Similar because both picture internal agitation; differs because seething describes the agitated state itself (crowds, anger), while brewing describes the process of formation.
  • hatching: Similar because both mean a plot or scheme is being secretly developed; differs because hatching is a conscious human act (hatching a plot), while brewing can be wholly impersonal (trouble brews by itself).
  • plotting / contriving: Similar because both mean secretly preparing something; differs because both require an agent and imply deliberation, while brewing is used of impersonal processes — a crisis brews, but only people plot.
  • fomenting: Similar because both mean stirring up trouble; differs because fomenting is an act done to others (fomenting rebellion) and is clinical or formal, while brewing is reflexive and atmospheric.
