*pu- (PIE, reconstructed; the link is DEBATED)
"to feel shame, be ashamed"

NOTE: this root underlies Latin pudēre "to be ashamed" and is the
traditional but contested source of repudium; de Vaan leaves
repudium of uncertain etymology; a rival guess tied it to pes,
"foot" (a kicking-away). No secure PIE ancestry is agreed.

▼

repudium (Latin, 1st c. BCE)
"divorce, a putting away"
(technical term of Roman family law)

▼

repudiāre (Latin, Classical)
"to cast off, reject, divorce"
(verb built on repudium)

▼

repudiātus (Latin past participle)
"rejected, cast off"

▼

repudiate (English, 1540s)
"to reject, disown; to divorce a spouse"
(learned borrowing directly from Latin)

▼

repudiate (English, sense expanded)
"to refuse to acknowledge a debt"
(attested in finance and politics, 1840s)

▼

REPUDIATE (modern English)
"to refuse to accept or be associated with;
to deny the truth or validity of"

NOTE ON DEBATED ORIGIN: the pre-Latin stage is not secure. The
link to *pu- via pudēre is traditional (Pokorny) but doubted by
de Vaan, who classifies repudium as of unknown etymology; the
"foot/kicking-away" theory is rejected by modern scholarship.
