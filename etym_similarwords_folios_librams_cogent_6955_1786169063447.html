• Manuscripts: Similar because both denote written works in book form; differs because manuscripts specifies hand-written documents, whereas folios specifies the large folded-sheet page format.
  • Codices: Similar because both denote bound leaf-books as opposed to scrolls; differs because codices emphasizes the physical form of early bound manuscripts, whereas folios highlights the massive folded-sheet size.
  • Grimoires: Similar because both denote books of magic and spells; differs because grimoires emphasizes dark spell formulas and conjuration, whereas librams emphasizes heavy, authoritative, bark-derived magic volumes in Vance's fiction.
  • Volumes: Similar because both denote bound books; differs because volumes is a neutral generic term, whereas folios names the largest printing format and librams names arcane books of sorcery.
  • Broadsheets: Similar because both are single large printed sheets; differs because broadsheets are unfolded single sheets used for public notices, whereas folios are sheets folded once and bound into large volumes.
  • Leaves: Similar because both descend from the idea of the written leaf or sheet; differs because leaves is a general term for the pages of a book, whereas folios is the technical book-trade name for the largest format.
  • Compelling: Similar because both describe a powerful effect that wins assent; differs because compelling may be emotional, dramatic, or physical, whereas cogent strictly applies to clear, irresistible logical reasoning.
  • Persuasive: Similar because both mean able to convince; differs because persuasive may rely on rhetoric, charm, or emotion, whereas cogent relies on airtight logic and unassailable evidence.
  • Trenchant: Similar because both describe incisive, effective argument; differs because trenchant implies a sharp cutting edge and often severity, whereas cogent implies orderly, compelling clarity.
  • Convincing: Similar because both mean producing belief or assent; differs because convincing describes the effect of success, whereas cogent describes the intrinsic logical force of the reasoning itself.
  • Rigorous: Similar because both imply intellectual soundness; differs because rigorous emphasizes strict, thorough, demanding method, whereas cogent emphasizes the compelling force of the result.
  • Sound: Similar because both describe valid, reliable reasoning; differs because sound is a technical logical term for valid-and-true arguments, whereas cogent stresses the persuasive power of the case.
  • Potent: Similar because both mean having great force; differs because potent is general (power, drugs, arguments alike), whereas cogent is confined to reasoning and evidence.
