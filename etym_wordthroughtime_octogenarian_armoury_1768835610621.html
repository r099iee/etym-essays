octogenarian:
- The Latinising coinage (17th c.) - formed on Latin octogeni, 'eighty at a time', with the -arian suffix of the age-classification series
- The actuarial register (18th - 19th c.) - a demographer's, insurer's and lawyer's word that reduces a long human life to a numbered decade
- The Victorian dignity (19th c.) - obituaries and tributes make 'the grand old octogenarian' a respectful, monumental figure
- Contemporary - formal but standard in news and official registers, it still fixes the exact decade and carries a faint eulogistic gravity

armoury:
- The house of armour (Middle English, 13th - 14th c.) - from Old French armerie, the place where armour and arms are kept
- From armour to all weapons (14th - 16th c.) - widened into the storehouse of a fortress's whole martial kit, the heart of its readiness
- The heraldic side-sense (15th - 17th c.) - 'armoury' also names the science of coats of arms, ornament and record of noble lineage
- The rusting museum (19th - 20th c.) - as professional armies displace castle stores, the armoury becomes an institutional depot, and in Gormenghast a monument of forgotten readiness
