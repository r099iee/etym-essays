- 14th Century: Middle English condempnacioun - Heavily anchored in ecclesiastical and biblical Latin; perceived primarily as the terrifying judicial verdict of God consigning the wicked to eternal hellfire, as well as formal ecclesiastical sentencing of heretics.
- 15th to 16th Century: Late Middle to Early Modern English legal expansion - Adopted into secular criminal jurisprudence and maritime law; used by Tudor state courts for sentences of capital treason and by admiralty prize courts for the legal confiscation of seized pirate and foreign merchant vessels.
- 17th to 18th Century: Enlightenment rhetoric and moral philosophy - Shifted toward secular political rhetoric and literary polemics; employed by Enlightenment thinkers to articulate comprehensive intellectual rejections of tyranny, superstition, and royal prerogative.
- 19th Century: Municipal regulation and property law - Codified in municipal civil engineering and common-law statutes of eminent domain; used to designate the administrative demolition of plague-ridden slum housing and the compulsory acquisition of land for expanding railways.
- 20th to 21st Century: Multilateral diplomacy and media scrutiny - Became standard language in United Nations resolutions, human rights monitoring, and international diplomacy for formal, collective repudiations of war crimes, terrorism, and state violence.
