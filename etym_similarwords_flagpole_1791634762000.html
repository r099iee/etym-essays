• Flagstaff: Similar because both denote a tall vertical staff designed to fly a flag; differs because flagstaff is the traditional, formal British and international naval term (dating back to the 16th century), whereas flagpole is the standard everyday North American term.
  • Mast: Similar because both are tall vertical poles designed to support rigging, sails, or flags; differs because a mast is primarily a massive nautical spar supporting a ship's sails or a high communications tower, whereas a flagpole is specifically designed for flying banners and ensigns.
  • Jackstaff: Similar because both are dedicated poles designed to hoist flags; differs because a jackstaff is a specialized naval term for the small vertical staff mounted specifically on the bow (prow) of a ship to fly the union jack when at anchor.
  • Ensign Staff: Similar because both are masts used to display national flags; differs because an ensign staff is located specifically at the stern (rear) of a naval or commercial vessel to fly the national maritime ensign.
  • Staff: Similar because both denote long, slender vertical wooden or metal rods; differs because a staff is a broader, generic term that can denote a walking stick, a military commander's baton, a musical stave, or a ceremonial wand, rather than a fixed mast for flags.
  • Pike: Similar because both are long wooden shafts; differs because a pike is an ancient infantry polearm weapon with a steel spearhead, historically used by pikemen against cavalry, although military ceremonial flags (colors) were sometimes mounted on pikestaffs.
  • Standard-pole / Standard-bearer's staff: Similar because both support symbolic flags or heraldic standards; differs because a standard-bearer's staff is a handheld, mobile pole carried on foot or horseback in parades or combat, whereas a flagpole is predominantly a stationary architectural or ground-anchored fixture.
  • Spire: Similar because both are tall, slender vertical architectural elements tapering toward the sky; differs because a spire is a permanent conical or pyramidal architectural rooftop structure (typically crowning a church or skyscraper), not a functional pole equipped with halyards for raising flags.
  • Gaff: Similar because both are spars from which flags or signals are displayed; differs because a gaff is an angled spar extending outward from a ship's mast or a shore signal station from which flags are flown when under way.
  • Bollard: Similar because both are sturdy vertical posts mounted on docks or pavement; differs because a bollard is a short, thick, heavy post used for securing ship mooring lines or blocking motor vehicle traffic, bearing no resemblance in height or function to a flagpole.
