• Reparation: Similar because both denote compensation for damage or loss; differs because reparation carries heavy legal and historical weight (war reparations, post-conflict compensation, state-level payments), while amends is personal, everyday, and moral, bound to the idiom "make amends."
  • Compensation: Similar because both mean payment or recompense for loss; differs because compensation is a neutral, commercial/legal term for money or benefit given to offset damage, without the moral charge of voluntary repentance carried by amends.
  • Atonement: Similar because both mean making up for a wrong done; differs because atonement is explicitly religious or deeply moral, involving expiation of guilt and often a spiritual dimension (atonement for sin), whereas amends is secular and concrete — an act or payment repairing interpersonal harm.
  • Redress: Similar because both denote setting a wrong right; differs because redress is formal and legal, a remedy or satisfaction sought through courts or authority, while amends is voluntary and personal, offered by the wrongdoer rather than extracted.
  • Restitution: Similar because both mean restoring what was lost or taken; differs because restitution is a legal remedy restoring the victim's property or status, technical and judicial, while amends is non-technical and can be symbolic or moral, not merely material.
  • Satisfaction: Similar because both can denote compensation for injury (the older legal sense of satisfaction); differs because satisfaction is a broad word for fulfilment of a demand or debt and today usually means contentment, while amends keeps its specialised moral-restoration sense.
  • Reparation's verb "repair": Similar because both involve fixing what is broken; differs because repair is purely physical or mechanical in the modern language, while amends is always a moral or relational restoration — you repair a watch but make amends for a wrong.
  • Expiation: Similar because both mean removing guilt or paying for fault; differs because expiation is theological and archaic, the purification of guilt through suffering or sacrifice, while amends is practical, relational, and modern.
