Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*wana-
"lacking, wanting, deficient"
↓
Proto-Germanic (derivative verb)
*wanōną
"to diminish, lessen, become less"
↓
Old English (c. 8th c.)
wanian
"to lessen, diminish, decay"
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
wanen
"to decrease, decline, fade"
↓
Modern English (current form)
wane
"to diminish gradually"
Note: Proto-Germanic *wanōną is cognate with Old Saxon wanōn, Old High German wanōn, and Old Norse vana. The base adjective *wana- ('lacking') also gave English prefix wan- (as in wanton < Old English wan-togen 'ill-trained, unruly') and is cognate with Latin vānus ('empty, void', whence English vain and vanish) and Sanskrit ūná- ('deficient, lacking').
