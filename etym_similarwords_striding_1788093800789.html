• Marching: Similar because both describe steady, purposeful on-foot movement with a marked step; differs because marching is rhythmic, often military, step-in-unison movement in formation with a cadence, while striding is individual, unforced and defined by step length rather than rhythm.
  • Stalking: Similar because both are deliberate, long-step walking with an air of purpose or menace; differs because stalking is stealthy, slow, predatory and often hostile, whereas striding is open, confident and not necessarily threatening.
  • Strutting: Similar because both convey assertive self-display in walking; differs because strutting is short-stepped, pompous and vain (a strutting peacock), while striding is long-stepped, efficient and dignified rather than theatrical.
  • Parading: Similar because both involve walking in a prominent, conspicuous manner; differs because parading implies exhibitionism or a formal procession before spectators — a performative element striding lacks.
  • Swaggering: Similar because both project confidence through posture and gait; differs because swaggering is insolent, boastful shoulder-rolling often aimed at an audience, while striding is measured and upright — confident but not arrogant.
  • Pacing: Similar because both are deliberate walking with regular steps; differs because pacing is repeated back-and-forth walking, usually anxious or impatient, within a confined space, whereas striding is linear travel across distance.
  • Trudging: Similar because both are sustained walking; differs because trudging is slow, heavy, weary walking under burden or fatigue — the exact emotional opposite of striding's energy.
  • Tramping: Similar because both are firm, heavy-footed walking over ground; differs because tramping is rough, booted, long-distance walking with ground-pounding force, while striding is smooth, light and controlled.
  • Stamping: Similar because both involve emphatic foot movement; differs because stamping is a single violent downward foot-slam (anger, cold, a command), not a traveling gait at all, whereas striding is sustained forward motion.
  • Sauntering: Similar because both are unhurried walking with an easy gait; differs because sauntering is leisurely, relaxed and aimless, whereas striding is energetic, purposeful and directed.
  • Strolling: Similar because both describe pleasurable walking at an easy pace; differs because strolling is casual and short-distanced with no destination-urgency, while striding implies determined progress.
  • Trotting: Similar because both are rapid, rhythmical stepping; differs because trotting is the shorter, quicker two-beat gait of a horse or jogging person, whereas striding is longer, slower and more measured.
