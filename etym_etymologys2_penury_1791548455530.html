Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*peh₁-
"to hurt, damage, diminish"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*pānis-
"deficiency, falling short"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
paene
"nearly, almost, scarcely"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. CE)
paenūria / pēnūria
"want, scarcity, need,"
"lack of provisions"
↓
Old French (c. 13th c.)
penurie
"indigence, dire poverty"
↓
Middle English (c. 1400)
penurie
"destitution, physical want"
↓
Modern English (c. 1600)
penury
"abject poverty, scarcity"

Note: Michiel de Vaan derives Latin paenūria / pēnūria directly from the adverb paene ('scarcely, almost'), rooted in the notion of falling short or suffering privation, while ancient folk etymology occasionally conflated it with penus ('stored food, provisions').
