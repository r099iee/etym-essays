• Bushes: Similar because a shrub is botanically a bush — low woody multi-stemmed growth; differs because bush is the everyday loose word (even for one wild specimen), while shrub is the gardener’s and botanist’s precise term.
  • Hedges: Similar because hedges are literally rows of shrubs planted as barriers; differs because a hedge is the functional living wall, while shrubs are the individual plants that may or may not be so trained.
  • Thickets: Similar because both are dense woody growth sheltering wildlife; differs because a thicket is a wild tangled mass, while shrubs names the plants themselves, garden or wild.
  • Undergrowth: Similar because shrubs often form the undergrowth layer beneath trees; differs because undergrowth is the whole low tangle including ferns and saplings, while shrubs are only the woody bushes.
  • Saplings: Similar because both are small woody plants below tree stature; differs because a sapling is a young tree that will grow a single trunk, while a shrub stays multi-stemmed for life.
  • Perennials: Similar because garden shrubs and perennials are both permanent plantings returned to yearly; differs because perennials are soft-stemmed flowers dying back each winter, while shrubs keep woody stems year-round.
  • Ornamentals: Similar because flowering shrubs in boxes are classic ornamental plantings; differs because ornamentals covers all decorative plants including flowers and grasses, while shrubs is only the woody group.
  • Scrub: Similar because wild shrubs collectively form scrub landscapes; differs because scrub stresses stunted windswept wildness, while shrubs includes pampered garden specimens.
