- [19th c. (Victorian Era)] — Thoroughly integrated into popular Victorian narrative fiction (Dickens, Austen, the Brontës) as a standard dialogue tag conveying domestic indignation, rural stubbornness, or class-conscious friction — [perception: widely recognized narrative device for depicting realistic emotional clash and conversational spark]

Modern Usage] — Maintained across contemporary English as both a dynamic dialogue marker for witty or defensive comebacks and a specialized industrial verb in oil-shale and metallurgical extraction — [perception: standard, vivid literary term conveying immediate verbal self-assertion and defensive wit]
