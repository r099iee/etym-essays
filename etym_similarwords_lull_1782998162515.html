• Soothe: Similar because both mean to make calm or ease distress; differs because soothe can relieve pain, anger, or anxiety of any kind, while lull specifically quiets into sleep or rest through gentle, rhythmic, repetitive means.
  • Calm: Similar because both reduce agitation; differs because calm is a general all-purpose word for lessening disturbance, while lull is softer, more rhythmic, and usually aims at sleep or full stillness.
  • Quiet / Quieten: Similar because both reduce sound or activity; differs because quieten merely silences, while lull implies a gentle, soothing progression toward rest, not just absence of noise.
  • Hush: Similar because both create stillness; differs because hush is abrupt and commands silence ("hush!"), while lull is prolonged and hypnotic, easing gradually into sleep.
  • Still: Similar because both bring motion or agitation to a stop; differs because still is formal and final, while lull is tender and suggests a temporary, restful calm.
  • Pacify: Similar because both calm an agitated person; differs because pacify suggests appeasing anger or rebellion, often with force or concession, while lull works by gentle soothing without confrontation.
  • Appease: Similar because both reduce agitation; differs because appease focuses on satisfying a demand or hunger, while lull focuses on quieting into rest.
  • Assuage: Similar because both relieve distress; differs because assuage relieves pain, fear, or appetite in a formal register, while lull is the intimate word of cradle and sleep.
  • Tranquillize: Similar because both produce calm; differs because tranquillize suggests medical sedation or formal pacification, while lull is natural, gentle, and non-clinical.
  • Sedate: Similar because both quieten someone into drowsiness; differs because sedate is the medical word for drugging into calm, while lull is the natural word of rocking and murmuring.
  • Rock: Similar because both soothe a child to sleep; differs because rock names the physical motion only, while lull includes sound, rhythm, and atmosphere, and can apply to storms and thoughts.
  • Cradle: Similar because both gentlest an infant into rest; differs because cradle primarily names holding or resting in the arms, while lull is the vocal/rhythmic soothing.
  • Respite (noun): Similar because both name a period of relief from strain; differs because respite is relief from labor or suffering, while a lull is a quiet interval between disturbances.
  • Pause / Interlude (noun): Similar because both name a break in activity; differs because a pause is voluntary and brief, while a lull is an involuntary, natural quieting (a lull in the wind), usually with an implicit return.
  • Calm (noun): Similar because both name stillness; differs because calm is a state of tranquility, while a lull is specifically the quiet interval after activity, with the expectation that it may not last.
  • Hiatus: Similar because both name a gap in a sequence; differs because hiatus is formal and neutral (in time or a series), while lull is atmospheric and suggests the wind or noise dropping.
