Biblical Toponym - In Genesis Eden is first a region, an eastern plain where God plants a garden; it is a geographical name of the ancient Near East with no inherent idea of paradise.
Scriptural Reinterpretation as Paradise - Through the homophonous Hebrew root meaning "delight," the garden becomes gan-ʿēden, the Garden of Delight, so Eden is fused with Paradise in the Hebrew and Christian tradition — the primal home of humanity and the emblem of lost innocence.
Classical-to-Modern Literary Symbol - From Milton's "loss of Eden" onward the word stands for the entire condition before the Fall and for any state of perfect happiness or pristine beauty, gaining an elegiac, universal register.
Contemporary Sense - Today Eden is a common metaphor for any place of pristine beauty or untroubled happiness ("a birdwatcher's Eden"), secular but still echoing the biblical idea of a perfect, lost beginning.
