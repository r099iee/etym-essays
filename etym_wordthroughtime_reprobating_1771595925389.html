- [16th century theological judgment] - [Reprobate enters as a Calvinist term — God's eternal rejection of the wicked; to reprobate is to declare unworthy and reject; connotation solemn, doctrinal]
- [17th-18th century moral censure] - [Extends to reasoned, formal disapproval — the verdict of a court, synod, or stern reviewer pronounced after weighing the evidence]
- [19th century measured condemnation] - [Ruskin's pamphlet was 'reprobating the manner and style of those critiques' — a tested, earned condemnation, judicial rather than hot; the present participle makes condemnation the document's ongoing function]
- [20th-21st century rare and formal] - [The verb is now uncommon; 'reprobate' survives mainly as a noun or adjective for a depraved person, while the participle retains its severe, verdict-like tone in elevated prose]
