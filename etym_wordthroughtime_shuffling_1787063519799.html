- 14th century (shovelen) — pushing and scraping things about; continuous motion of a repeated shove.
- 1530s — "shuffle" (the verb) names a clumsy dragging gait, and by extension the scraping walk of the aged or infirm.
- 1660s — the playing-card sense: to mix cards thoroughly; the noun "shuffle" enters gaming vocabulary.
- 17th–18th centuries — the evasive sense: "to shuffle" = to equivocate, dodge, act dishonestly; "shuffle off" (Hamlet, 1602) = to evade or get rid of.
- 19th–20th centuries — the dance "shuffle" (a sliding, syncopated step) emerges in jazz, blues, and rock; shuffling becomes musical vocabulary.
- Present day — "shuffling" spans dragging walks, card-mixing, evasive answers, the dance step, and random playback ("shuffle mode") in digital music.
