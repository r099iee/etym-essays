Imitative (echoic) cry-root in the Germanic speech area "a sharp high cry" — a sound-symbolic formation with no secure PIE reconstruction (see note) → Old Norse skrækja "to screech, cry harshly" (attested in Viking-era Norse, c. 9th–11th c.) → Middle English schriken / shriken "to utter a shrill cry" (attested from the 13th c. in Middle English religious and romance texts) → Modern English shriek (verb and noun) → shriek (noun, this card). Debated/nuanced stage: The Middle English verb is commonly held to have been borrowed from, or at least heavily shaped by, Old Norse skrækja, but some handbooks instead posit an independent native English echoic formation belonging to the same sound-symbolic family as screech, screak, and skrike, whose spellings interchanged in Middle English. Because the word is imitative, no pre-Germanic (PIE) stage can be reconstructed; it is classified as echoic rather than as a regular inherited root. The modern spelling shriek (with -ie-) stabilized only in the modern period.
