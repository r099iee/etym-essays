• Surplus: Similar because excess over need available for use; differs because neutral economic term for stock, budget or trade, while superfluity is literary and often moralizing.
  • Excess: Similar because more than necessary or allowed; differs because broader and often implies violation of limit or intemperance, less specifically overflow imagery.
  • Overflow: Similar because literal flowing over capacity; differs because concrete physical image, while superfluity is abstracted to wealth, words, staff.
  • Redundancy: Similar because unnecessary duplication; differs because technical (data, staff, words), implies can be cut without loss, while superfluity can be generous abundance.
  • Superabundance: Similar because lavish more-than-enough; differs because wholly positive abundance, while superfluity often criticizes waste.
  • Glut: Similar because over-supply depressing value; differs because market-specific (glut of oil, graduates), cruder and more pejorative.
  • Plethora: Similar because overabundance; differs because originally Greek fullness, now often medical or formal excess, slightly pompous.
  • Profusion: Similar because lavish abundance poured out; differs because usually positive lushness (profusion of flowers), not moral excess.
  • Luxury / Luxuriance: Similar because non-essential indulgence; differs because luxury is desirable comfort, superfluity judges it needless.
  • Verbosity / prolixity: Similar because superfluity of words; differs because specific to speech/writing, while superfluity covers goods and people too.
  • Extravagance: Similar because spending beyond need; differs because focuses on reckless expenditure, not static surplus stock.
  • Satiety / surfeit: Similar because over-fullness to disgust; differs because bodily excess (food, pleasure) causing nausea, narrower than superfluity.
