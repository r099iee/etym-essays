• Dislike: Similar because it is the direct modern equivalent in meaning; differs because dislike is the standard, neutral, everyday word, while mislike is archaic, poetic, and carries a subtle foreboding.
  • Disapprove: Similar because both are negative judgments; differs because disapprove implies a moral, official, or rational verdict, while mislike is an intuitive, emotional feeling of displeasure.
  • Resent: Similar because both are negative feelings; differs because resent adds indignation at unfair treatment, whereas mislike is simple, uncomplicated aversion.
  • Loathe: Similar because both express aversion; differs because loathe is extreme, passionate hatred and disgust, while mislike is mild to moderate.
  • Detest / Abhor: Similar because both mean strong dislike; differs because they intensify the emotion to near-loathing, far stronger than the quiet aversion of mislike.
  • Take against: Similar because both mean to develop an aversion to someone; differs because take against is an idiomatic, informal phrasal verb with no archaic glamour.
  • Look askance at: Similar because both imply distrustful negative feeling; differs because it describes a sidelong, suspicious glance rather than a settled state of aversion.
  • Mistrust: Similar because both can shade into suspicion; differs because mistrust is about doubted reliability, while mislike is about simple displeasure.
