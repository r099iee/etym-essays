• Thoroughly drilled: Similar because both describe training enforced by methodical repetition; differs because it stresses repetition and habit without the physical punishment of flogging.
  • Rigorously trained: Similar because both convey severe, demanding preparation; differs because it is neutral and modern, stripping away the birch-rod imagery and the ironic period humour.
  • Chastised: Similar because both retain corporal or penal force; differs because chastised emphasizes correction or punishment and lacks the educational, formative sense of mastery that well-flogged carries.
  • Scourged: Similar because both mean whipped or severely beaten; differs because scourged is a more elevated, biblical register and lacks the schoolroom-training connotation of well-flogged.
  • Whipped: Similar because both describe beating with a whip; differs because whipped is the plain neutral participle without the compound's added "thoroughly trained" accreditation.
  • Lashed: Similar because both mean beaten with a lash or whip; differs because lashed stresses the act of punishment (often naval) rather than the outcome of discipline and skill.
  • Birched: Similar because both name the schoolmaster's birch; differs because birched is narrowly the English public-school punishment, while well-flogged is the compound with the intensifying "well" and the training metaphor.
  • Beaten: Similar because both describe physical blows; differs because beaten is generic and negative, without any suggestion of discipline producing skill or scrupulousness.
  • Well-schooled: Similar because both convey thorough education; differs because well-schooled is wholly positive and painless, missing the period joke that classical excellence was purchased with stripes.
  • Well-grounded: Similar because both convey solid, thorough training; differs because well-grounded is about secure foundations and carries no corporal or disciplinary image at all.
  • Disciplined: Similar because both imply training that forms habits; differs because disciplined is neutral and modern, describing self-control and order rather than punishment received.
  • Seasoned: Similar because both imply toughening through experience; differs because seasoned suggests hardened by use and exposure, not the specific pedagogy of flogging.
  • Hardened: Similar because both imply toughness under adversity; differs because hardened suggests calloused insensitivity rather than the acquisition of scholarly precision.
  • Honed: Similar because both describe skill sharpened to precision; differs because honed is wholly positive and painless, while well-flogged keeps the brutal method in view.
  • Drilled: Similar because both describe training through enforced repetition; differs because drilled is military or classroom routine without the whip, while well-flogged keeps the corporal method and the ironical edge.
