- c. 1200 — talking machines: clack first scolds chattering tongues and rattling mills, the gossip-mill grinding beside the corn-mill.
- 14th–15th c. — the mill takes over: clack-mill names both the mill's rattle and the chatterbox, proverb joining industry and gossip in one rattle.
- 16th–17th c. — teeth and tongues: teeth clack with cold and fear, tongues clack disapproval, the body becoming its own mill of bone.
- 18th–19th c. — industry multiplies it: presses, looms, and rails clack, and the valve that cannot help its noise is christened the clack-valve.
- 20th c. — the typewriter's century: keys clack in every office and novel, fixing clack as the sound of writing itself before silence returns with the screen.
- Today — Sam's shears keep the old music: garden tools, heels, keyboards, and gossiping tongues still clack wherever hard things meet quickly.
