Single root word: arch. 1. PIE *h₂erḱʷ- 'bow, the curved weapon' (c. 4500–3500 BCE; a 'western' lexeme attested only in Italic and Germanic) → Latin arcus, arcūs 'a bow (weapon); a curve; an arch' (attested from 1st c. BCE) → Late Latin arcus 'arch of a building' (the architectural sense; late antique period, c. 4th–5th c. CE, when Roman builders generalised the bent-bow image to the voussoir structure) → Old French arc 'arch' (11th–12th c.) → Middle English arche 'arch' (13th c.) → Modern English arch /ɑːtʃ/ 'a curved structure spanning an opening.' NOTE — two guardrails. (1) The Germanic sibling of the same PIE root, *arhwō 'arrow', reached English independently as 'arrow'; the same root gave 'bow' in the West (arcus) and 'arrow' in the North (*arhwō). (2) The prefix arch- of archbishop and the adjective arch ('playfully cunning') come from Greek arkhē 'rule, chief' and are a DIFFERENT word — do not merge them with the builder's arch.
