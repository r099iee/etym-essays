• Sturdy: Similar because both praise solid, dependable strength of body or build; differs because sturdy stresses compact toughness and health, while stout adds breadth and weight, shading toward corpulence.
  • Stocky: Similar because both describe short, thick-bodied builds; differs because stocky is plainer and more physical (a stocky wrestler), while stout carries old-fashioned warmth and a hint of moral backbone.
  • Portly: Similar because both are polite words for a large, corpulent figure; differs because portly suggests dignified roundness and presence (a portly alderman), while stout suggests strength and bravery as well as size.
  • Corpulent: Similar because both denote a bulky, fleshy body; differs because corpulent is clinical and often disapproving, while stout is affectionate and approving.
  • Brawny: Similar because both suggest powerful physical mass; differs because brawny stresses visible muscle (brawny arms), while stout stresses overall thickness and solidity of frame.
  • Stalwart: Similar because both praise loyal, unyielding strength of character; differs because stalwart is almost entirely moral (a stalwart supporter), while stout keeps one foot in the physical body.
  • Valiant: Similar because both honour bravery, matching Tolkien's sense of stout; differs because valiant is purely heroic and literary, while stout is homely — bravery in a farmer with a stick rather than on a battlefield.
  • Robust: Similar because both mean strong and healthy, of people or things; differs because robust is the modern, slightly technical term (robust health, robust data), while stout is warmer, older, and more bodily.
  • Resolute: Similar because both describe firmness that will not yield; differs because resolute names determination of will alone, while stout fuses will with physique — the resolve of someone broad enough to block the door.
