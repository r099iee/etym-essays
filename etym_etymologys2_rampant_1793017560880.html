Frankish (reconstructed, c. 5th–8th c.)
*rampōn
‘to claw, clutch, contract’
↓
Old French (11th c.)
ramper
‘to creep, climb, rear’
↓
Old French (12th c., participle)
rampant
‘climbing, rearing up’
↓
Anglo-Norman and Middle English (c. 1300)
rampaunt
‘rearing beast; fierce’
↓
Modern English (current)
rampant
‘spreading unchecked’

Note: the exact Frankish form behind Old French ramper is reconstructed and its precise sense debated; some scholars derive the verb through a contracted or clawing sense, others through crawling, but the Old French stages onward are securely attested.
