• afflicted: Similar because both mean badly affected by suffering or misfortune; differs because afflicted stresses the state of suffering (physical or mental) and is often medical or formal, while stricken retains the image of a sudden blow and greater violence.
  • smitten: Similar because both are old past participles meaning struck or afflicted; differs because smitten has been captured by romantic love (“smitten with her”) and by divine punishment, while stricken stays with grief, disaster and shock.
  • hit: Similar because both mean the target of a damaging blow; differs because hit is the plain everyday word, physical and casual, while stricken is elevated, formal and implies lasting damage.
  • struck: Similar because struck is the ordinary past participle of the same verb strike; differs because struck is neutral and mechanical (struck by lightning, struck from a list), while stricken is restricted to the mournful, afflicted senses.
  • affected: Similar because both mean influenced or damaged by something; differs because affected is weak, neutral and clinical (affected by the weather), while stricken implies overwhelming, near-fatal impact.
  • overwhelmed: Similar because both mean crushed and incapacitated by a force or emotion; differs because overwhelmed stresses quantity and pressure (overwhelmed by work, by emotion), while stricken stresses the single devastating blow.
  • devastated: Similar because both mean laid waste emotionally or physically; differs because devastated implies total ruin and is also used of landscapes and buildings, while stricken is more personal and momentary (the stricken look on a face).
  • laid low: Similar because both mean brought down by illness or adversity; differs because laid low is colloquial, physical and temporary, while stricken suggests permanent scarring and has literary weight.
  • blighted: Similar because both mean ruined by a destructive force; differs because blighted pictures creeping ruin (blight, frost on crops) and hopeless decay, while stricken pictures a sudden blow.
  • assailed: Similar because both mean attacked by troubles; differs because assailed stresses ongoing onslaught from outside, while stricken describes the completed impact on the victim.
  • wounded: Similar because both mean injured, especially in battle imagery; differs because wounded is literal and physical injury, while stricken is figurative catastrophe or shock.
  • thunderstruck: Similar because both mean shock as if hit by a blow from the sky; differs because thunderstruck is confined to astonished surprise, while stricken covers grief, disaster and disease.
  • dumbstruck / dumbfounded: Similar because both mean silenced or astonished as if struck; differs because dumbstruck is astonishment only, while stricken dumb is one specialised case of a much wider affliction vocabulary.
  • shattered: Similar because both mean broken and ruined by shock; differs because shattered emphasises the destruction of the person’s composure or world, while stricken emphasises the blow itself and the passive aftermath.
