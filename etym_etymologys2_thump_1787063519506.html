Proto-Indo-European (Echoic substrate) (Pre-historic)
*tum- / *tump-
"imitative of a dull blow or swelling sound"
Note: Purely onomatopoeic; parallel to Germanic *tumb- and Scandinavian *dump-.
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*dumpōną / *tump-
"to make a dull sound, strike heavily"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th–15th c.)
thumpen / thomp
"to beat, strike with a heavy dull blow"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th c.)
thumpe
"a heavy blow; to strike with a dull sound"
↓
Modern English (c. 1700–present)
thump
"to strike with a dull sound; a heavy blow; palpitation"
