• Abated: Similar because both are forms of the same verb and both mean lessened or diminished; differs because abate is the full, productive verb (a storm abates, anger abates), while bated is the clipped form that survives only in the phrase "bated breath."
  • Restrained: Similar because both describe something held back or controlled; differs because restrained is a general adjective of control, lacking bated's hidden image of something struck or beaten down.
  • Held: Similar because both describe breath that is not freely released; differs because held (as in "held one's breath") describes a voluntary action, while bated describes a state of being beaten down, not chosen but suffered.
  • Suppressed: Similar because both mean forcibly held down or reduced; differs because suppressed is clinical and psychological, while bated is poetic and nearly idiom-bound.
  • Subdued: Similar because both mean reduced in force or intensity; differs because subdued belongs naturally to spirits, colours, and sounds, not to breath, where bated is at home.
  • Hushed: Similar because both mean quieted or lessened; differs because hushed applies to voices, rooms, and places, while bated applies specifically to the breath itself.
  • Stilled: Similar because both describe the reduction of movement or sound; differs because stilled implies cessation or motionlessness, while bated implies reduction without stopping — the breathing continues, but subdued.
  • Checked / curbed: Similar because both mean held back or restrained; differs because checked and curbed imply an external force stopping a progress, while bated names the inward state of breath held down in suspense or reverence.
