• biennial: Similar because it is the same type of Latin-derived time-span adjective ending in "-ennial," built on annus "year" with a numeral prefix; differs because it means every two years — a more frequent, less rare cycle, and in botany it is the standard term for a two-year plant, an association "triennial" does not share.
  • annual: Similar because it describes a recurring cycle of years and shares the same family formation; differs because it means every year (or lasting one year) — a routine, expected rhythm with none of the accumulated rarity and anticipation of a three-year gap.
  • triannual: Similar because it shares the prefix tri- "three" and is near-homophonous; differs in a critical way — triannual means three times within a single year, while triennial means once every three years; the two are opposites in structure and must not be confused.
  • quadrennial: Similar because it is the same formation with a different numeral, marking a periodic cycle of years; differs because it refers to a four-year interval (as with the Olympic Games), making the occasion slightly rarer still than a triennial.
  • quinquennial: Similar because it is a sibling -ennial adjective marking a multi-year cycle; differs because it names a five-year interval, longer still, and is most familiar in the United Kingdom as the name of local government elections held every five years.
  • septennial: Similar because it belongs to the same paradigm of numbered -ennial intervals; differs because it marks a seven-year cycle, a much rarer period, historically associated with long-term leases and periodic reviews.
  • perennial: Similar because it shares the "-ennial" stem ultimately from Latin annus "year"; differs because it means lasting or recurring throughout the year and year after year without any fixed interval — the celebrated botanical term for plants that live many seasons — rather than a strict every-three-years cycle.
  • three-yearly: Similar because it states the same interval in plain Germanic words; differs because it is the everyday, informal expression people actually use in speech, while "triennial" is the formal, Latinate, institutional register.
  • centennial: Similar because it is another -ennial anniversary term built on a numeral and annus; differs because it names a one-hundred-year interval, and it is used almost exclusively of centenary commemorations, whereas "triennial" names a far more frequent cycle and is used of regularly recurring events, not one-off anniversaries.
  • decennial: Similar because it is the same formation marking a periodic multi-year interval; differs because it marks a ten-year cycle, most familiar from the decennial census, a far longer and grander institutional rhythm than the triennial.
