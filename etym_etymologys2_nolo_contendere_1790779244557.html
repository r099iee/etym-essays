Chain 1: nolo
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*ne- + *welh₁-
"not" + "to choose, wish"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*ne wellō
"to not want, be unwilling"
↓
Old Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
nevolō
"I do not wish, I am unwilling"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
nōlō
"I do not wish, I refuse"

Chain 2: contendere
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*kom- + *ten-
"together" + "to stretch, strain"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*kom-tendō
"to stretch tightly, strain against"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
contendere
"to strive, fight, dispute"
↓
Late Latin / Anglo-Latin (c. 16th c.)
nōlō contendere
"I do not wish to dispute (plea)"
↓
Modern Legal & Literary Eng. (19th c.–present)
nolo contendere
"no contest plea; tacit surrender"
