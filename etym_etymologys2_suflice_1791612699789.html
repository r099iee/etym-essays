Note: Latin sufficere is formed by assimilating the prefix sub- ("under, secretly, from below") to facere ("to make, do"), with regular vowel weakening from -a- to -i- in medial open syllables (apophony). The original physical sense was "to supply or substitute from beneath, to dye from beneath, to furnish as support". In Old French, the verb developed an extended stem in -iss- (from the Latin inchoative suffix -escere), which Middle English adopted as the base of the verb (suffisen), giving modern English its final -ce spelling and pronunciation /s/. The form suflice in this note is an OCR or typographical transcription artifact where the double 'f' was misread as 'fl'.
