• Bionics: Similar because both involve the integration of biological organisms and electronic or mechanical engineering; differs because bionics typically denotes the applied scientific discipline or the artificial electromechanical replacements themselves (e.g., bionic limbs, bionic eyes), whereas cyborgs designates the whole living entities or hybrid beings possessing such enhancements.
  • Androids: Similar because both represent high-tech fusions of humanity and machinery; differs because androids are entirely synthetic, artificial humanoid robots designed to mimic human appearance and behavior from the ground up, whereas cyborgs are fundamentally grounded in a biological organism that has been technologically augmented or modified.
  • Automata: Similar because both refer to entities capable of mechanical or automated action; differs because automata are non-biological self-operating mechanical constructs (historically clockwork or hydraulic figures) lacking organic life, sentience, and biological feedback loops.
  • Hybrids: Similar because both denote composite entities produced by blending two distinct species, categories, or natures; differs because hybrids is a broad biological and conceptual umbrella that can refer to plant cross-breeding or cultural synthesis without any technological or cybernetic component.
  • Mutants: Similar because both describe beings possessing bodily and cognitive traits altered far beyond standard human norms; differs because mutants derive their alterations from genetic mutations, biological radiation, or evolutionary divergence rather than synthetic mechanical integration.
  • Transhumans: Similar because both refer to humans whose capabilities transcend traditional biological limitations through technological intervention; differs because transhumans is a philosophical and evolutionary category describing a transitional developmental stage toward post-humanity, whereas cyborgs specifically highlights the structural, homeostatic feedback loops between machine and flesh or mind.
  • Synthetics: Similar because both encompass artificial components simulating biological life; differs because synthetics denotes organisms or materials fabricated entirely from artificial compounds rather than native biological tissue augmented by technology.
  • Clones: Similar because both populate speculative inquiries into the technological alteration of human life; differs because clones are purely biological genetic duplicates created via asexual reproductive technologies, possessing no mechanical or cybernetic hardware.
