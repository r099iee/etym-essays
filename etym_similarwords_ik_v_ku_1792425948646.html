• Sūryavaṃśa / Solar Dynasty: Similar because both identify the legendary solar lineage of ancient Indian kings; differs because Sūryavaṃśa is the overarching name of the entire cosmological solar dynasty (originating from the Sun god Sūrya/Vivasvān), whereas Ikṣvāku is the specific founding human emperor who established the mortal dynasty upon earth and whose name serves as the lineage's premier patronymic.
  • Raghukula / Raghuvamsha: Similar because both denote the royal lineage of Ayodhyā celebrated in the Rāmāyaṇa; differs because Raghukula specifically refers to the later branch of the dynasty descended from King Raghu (the great-grandfather of Rāma), whereas Ikṣvāku represents the foundational, much older ancestral progenitor of the entire house from whom Raghu himself descended.
  • Okkāka: Similar because both designate the exact same historical-mythological patriarch; differs because Okkāka is the Middle Indo-Aryan (Pali) phonetic realization utilized exclusively within Theravada Buddhist canonical texts (such as the Dīgha Nikāya), whereas Ikṣvāku is the original Vedic and Classical Sanskrit form.
  • Manu Vaivasvata: Similar because both are venerated founding patriarchs of Indian kingship and solar ancestry; differs because Manu Vaivasvata is the cosmological progenitor of post-deluge humanity and the immediate father of Ikṣvāku, whereas Ikṣvāku is the first sovereign monarch who organized the kingdom of Kosala and established the capital at Ayodhyā.
  • Paurava / House of Puru: Similar because both represent the most illustrious royal lineages of ancient Indian epic literature; differs because the Pauravas belong to the rival Lunar Dynasty (Chandravaṃśa) celebrated in the Mahābhārata, whereas the House of Ikṣvāku constitutes the core of the Solar Dynasty (Sūryavaṃśa) celebrated in the Rāmāyaṇa.
  • Yādava / House of Yadu: Similar because both are venerable epic royal dynasties celebrated in the Purāṇas; differs because the Yādavas are a branch of the Lunar line famously giving rise to Lord Kṛṣṇa, whereas the House of Ikṣvāku is the solar house that gave rise to Lord Rāma.
  • Śākya clan: Similar because both are inextricably linked by bloodline in Buddhist historiography; differs because the Śākyas are the historical aristocratic warrior clan of Kapilavastu into which Gautama Buddha was born, who traced their distant royal ancestry back to Ikṣvāku (Okkāka) as their founding patriarch.
  • Andhra Ikshvakus: Similar because both share the exact dynastic patronymic; differs because the ancient Ikṣvāku is the legendary Vedic solar progenitor, whereas the Andhra Ikshvakus were an epigraphically verified historical royal dynasty of the 3rd–4th centuries CE who ruled the Krishna-Guntur valley in southern India from Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda).
  • Primogenitor / Dynastic Patriarch: Similar because both denote the foundational forefather from whom a royal clan traces its nobility and divine right; differs because primogenitor is an abstract Anglo-Latin genealogical descriptor, whereas Ikṣvāku is a specific proper noun loaded with millennia of sacred narrative and theological resonance.
  • Dharmic Sovereign: Similar because Ikṣvāku is regarded as the archetype of the righteous king; differs because dharmic sovereign is an abstract political and ethical category, whereas Ikṣvāku is the unique epic monarch who established the throne of Ayodhyā.
