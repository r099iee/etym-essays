PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*pele-
‘flat, broad, spread out’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*felthuz
‘open land, plain’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
feld
‘open country, pasture’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
feld, field
‘open land; battlefield’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c., compounded)
field work
‘labour outdoors, campaigning’
↓
Modern English (20th c., closed)
fieldwork
‘research conducted on site’

Note: the second element work continues Old English weorc from Proto-Germanic *werkan, deed or task; the closed compound fieldwork in the research sense is first firmly attested in the early 1900s, though open field work is older.
