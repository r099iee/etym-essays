• Stacks: Similar because both evoke towering piles of paper and words; differs because stacks is the physical pile itself, while reams adds the precise trade measure turned comic exaggeration.
  • Piles: Similar because reams of advice are piles of writing; differs because piles is general and neutral, while reams specifically counts paper and sighs at verbosity.
  • Volumes: Similar because both measure large amounts of text; differs because volumes are bound books speaking volumes figuratively, while reams are loose sheets of unbound excess.
  • Loads: Similar because both mean a great deal of something; differs because loads is slangy and all-purpose, while reams belongs specifically to paper and prose.
  • Heaps: Similar because both picture accumulated masses; differs because heaps is casual and shapeless, while reams keeps the ghost of an exact count (480 or 500 sheets).
  • Sheaves: Similar because both are bundles of paper (sheaves of notes, reams of advice); differs because a sheaf is a small gathered handful, while reams means hundreds of sheets.
  • Quires: Similar because a quire (24–25 sheets) is the ream’s own building block; differs because quire is the small trade unit few know, while ream is the famous bulk bundle.
  • Tomes: Similar because both suggest weighty masses of writing; differs because tomes are thick learned books, while reams are unbound floods of words, often unread.
  • Wads: Similar because both are thick gatherings of paper; differs because a wad is crumpled or cash-like, while reams are flat sheets in stately, exasperating quantity.
