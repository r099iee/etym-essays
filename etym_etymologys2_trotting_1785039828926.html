PIE root *der- (1) "to run, walk, step" (reconstructed by Watkins as the base of roots meaning "to run, walk, step"; the connection of trot to this root is debated — see note) → Proto-Germanic *tred- "to step, tread, trample" (strong verb; reconstructed; the ancestor of English tread) → Old English tredan (v.) "to tread, walk, trample" (attested) → Modern English tread / trod / trodden → Proto-Germanic derivative *trott- / *trod- "a regular stepping pace" (a secondary formation from the stepping root, denoting a brisk stepping motion) → Old High German trottōn (v.) "to tread, stamp" (attested, showing the stepping stem on the continent) → Frankish *trottōn (v.) "to go at a trot, walk briskly" (the West Germanic stem carried into Gallo-Romance in the early Middle Ages) → Old French troter (v.) "to trot, to go" (12th century, used of horses and men); Old French noun trot (n.) "a trot, the horse's gait between walk and run" (13th century) → Middle English trotten (v.) "to move at a trot" (late 14th century, borrowed from Old French); Middle English trot (n.) "the gait faster than a walk, slower than a run" (c. 1300) → Modern English trot (v. and n.); present participle trotting "moving at a pace faster than a walk with short, quick steps" → Modern English TROTTING Note on debate: the link of Germanic *trott- to PIE *der- (1) "to run, walk, step" is Watkins' reconstruction and is not universally accepted; some scholars treat the Germanic *trott-/*trod- stem as an independent expressive formation of stepping with no secure Indo-European pedigree. The chain from Old French troter to Middle English trotten is fully attested; the stages before Old French — Frankish *trottōn, Old High German trottōn, and Proto-Germanic *trott-/*trod- — are reconstructed from the attested Germanic and Romance forms. Romance languages also borrowed the Frankish stem back southward, giving Italian trottare and Spanish trotar "to trot." The noun senses (fox-trot 1872/dance 1914, the trots "diarrhoea" 1808, trot out 1838) are later English extensions of the core gait.
