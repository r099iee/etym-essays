• Dawdling: Similar because both denote taking far more time than necessary to complete an action or journey; differs because dawdling focuses specifically on sluggish, lazy time-wasting or procrastination when one ought to be working or moving with purpose, whereas loitering emphasizes lingering or remaining in a particular physical space.
  • Lingering: Similar because both involve staying in an area longer than strictly required; differs because lingering frequently carries warm, sentimental, sensory, or reluctant connotations (such as lingering over an intimate dinner, a lingering melody, or a lingering sunset), whereas loitering oscillates between pleasant leisurely drifting and suspicious municipal nuisance.
  • Sauntering: Similar because both describe slow, unhurried, leisurely walking; differs because sauntering emphasizes an elegant, carefree, dignified stroll with positive aristocratic or philosophical associations, completely devoid of the suspicious or illicit legal overtones historically attached to loitering.
  • Meandering: Similar because both imply winding, indirect progress without a straight line; differs because meandering specifically describes a curving, sinuous physical path or discursive conversation (like a twisting river), rather than lingering statically or aimlessly in one location.
  • Tarrying: Similar because both describe delaying one's departure or lingering behind; differs because tarrying is an elevated, archaic, or biblical term implying a conscious decision to pause, lodge, or remain temporarily as a guest or traveler.
  • Skulking: Similar because both describe lingering in public or semi-public spaces; differs because skulking carries overt sinister, cowardly, and deceitful connotations, implying hiding in shadows or moving stealthily with criminal, predatory, or shameful intent.
  • Prowling: Similar because both describe slow, watchful presence in an area; differs because prowling strongly evokes a wild beast hunting prey or a burglar casing a property, marked by aggressive, stealthy alertness rather than lazy idle drifting.
  • Idling: Similar because both denote spending time in non-productive relaxation; differs because idling focuses on general passivity, lack of employment, or a stationary running engine, whereas loitering specifically emphasizes physical spatial presence and hanging around in public view.
  • Lollygagging: Similar because both mean wasting time foolishly or aimlessly; differs because lollygagging is an informal, humorous American colloquialism typically scolded by parents or employers, lacking both the poetic cadence of Wordsworth and the serious statutory penalties of municipal penal codes.
  • Hovering: Similar because both involve remaining suspended or staying close to a focal location; differs because hovering implies literal physical suspension in mid-air (like an insect or helicopter) or nervous, anxious physical proximity over someone's shoulder.
