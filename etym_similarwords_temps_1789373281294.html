• Time: Similar because time is the direct English translation and universal conceptual equivalent of French temps; differs because English time has detached itself completely from atmospheric weather (which uses a separate Germanic root, weather), whereas French temps retains the ancient Roman polysemic fusion of chronological duration and meteorological condition within a single word.
  • Durée (Duration): Similar because both denote the extension of existence through time; differs because temps can denote abstract clock time, an isolated instant, or grammatical tense, whereas durée (immortalized by Henri Bergson) signifies continuous, undivided, qualitative inner psychological flow that cannot be spatialized or segmented.
  • Chronos: Similar because both represent the measurable, forward march of chronological time; differs because Chronos in Greek mythology and philosophy personifies quantitative, relentless, devoured time (the clock), whereas temps encompasses subjective memory, musical tempo, weather, and qualitative aesthetic redemption.
  • Kairos: Similar because both touch upon temporal experience and opportunity; differs because Kairos specifically denotes the opportune, decisive, or sacred moment of action, whereas temps is the general, continuous medium of duration and cyclic change.
  • Epoch: Similar because both identify extended periods of historical duration characterized by distinct developments; differs because epoch signifies a grand historical era or turning point defined by momentous events, whereas temps spans mundane daily hours, immediate weather, and musical beats.
  • Weather: Similar because temps is the standard French term for weather (le temps qu'il fait); differs because weather in English is purely atmospheric and meteorological, possessing no linguistic connection to temporal duration, clocks, or literary memory.
