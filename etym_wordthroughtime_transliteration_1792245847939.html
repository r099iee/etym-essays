- 1850s–1860s — Coined in Victorian comparative philology (Max Müller, August Schleicher) as scholars urgently required standardized Latin letterpress typography to cite Sanskrit, Avestan, and Arabic texts.
- Late 19th c. — Formalized by scholarly societies (Royal Asiatic Society, British Academy) to establish systematic romanization keys for non-European manuscripts.
- Mid-20th c. — Standardized globally by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the Library of Congress (ALA-LC) to enable international bibliographic cataloging and intelligence processing.
- Late 20th c. — Integrated into digital telecommunications and computing systems, establishing ASCII fallbacks and powering Input Method Editors (IMEs) for East Asian scripts.
- 21st c. — Crucial algorithmic task in computational linguistics, deep learning, and neural machine translation for cross-lingual named-entity recognition and automated multilingual text processing.
