Derivation chain for SLACKLY (root word one of two): Stage 1 — PIE *(s)lēg- "to be lax, loose, to hang without tension" (reconstructed; pre-4th millennium BCE) — the root of laxity and looseness. Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *slakaz "loose, lax, slack; sluggish" (reconstructed; before c. 500 CE) — the adjective of looseness. Stage 3 — Old English slæc "loose, lax, negligent, remiss; of a rope, without strain" (attested 9th–11th centuries). Stage 4 — Middle English slak "loose, lax, slack" (12th–15th centuries) — the same adjective continued. Stage 5 — English slack (adjective: not taut, loose; noun: the loose part of a line) + the adverbial suffix -ly → slackly (adverb: in a loose, limp, un-taut manner) ◄ TARGET. Note: The chain *(s)lēg- → *slakaz → slæc → slak → slack → slackly is secure. The related Latin adjective laxus ("loose"), which enters English as lax, relax, and laxative, is a collateral branch of the same PIE root, not part of the English slack lineage.
Derivation chain for GLISTENED (root word two of two): Stage 1 — PIE *ghel- "to shine, gleam; bright; yellow, green" (reconstructed; pre-4th millennium BCE) — the great Indo-European root of shining and its colours. Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *glis- "to gleam, to glitter" (reconstructed; before c. 500 CE) — a derived extension of the shining root; then *glisēn / *glisnōn "to glisten." Stage 3 — Old English glisnian "to glitter, gleam, glisten" (attested 9th–11th centuries) — the frequentative verb of gleaming. Stage 4 — Middle English glisnen / glisten "to glisten, shine" (12th–15th centuries) — the verb continued. Stage 5 — English glisten (verb: to shine with a soft, reflected, wet luster). Stage 6 — English glistened (past tense and past participle of glisten: shone with a sparkling or wet luster) ◄ TARGET. Note: The chain is secure. The same PIE root *ghel- produced Proto-Germanic *gulþą ("gold," whence English gold), Sanskrit hári ("yellow, green, golden," whence Hindi harā, "green"), and many English light-words including gleam, glow, glare, and glass — all collateral branches of the one ancient root of shining.
