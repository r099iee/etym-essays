• Jogging: Similar because both are paces between walking and running; differs because a jog is a slow, bouncing run with longer strides, while a trot keeps short, regular, four-beat steps at a walking-speed rhythm.
  • Scampering: Similar because both are quick, light movement; differs because scampering is playful or frightened, with little hops and irregular direction, while trotting is steady and rhythmic.
  • Scurrying: Similar because both are hurried short-stepped movement; differs because scurrying adds furtive, bustling haste and often many tiny feet, while trotting is an even, deliberate beat.
  • Marching: Similar because both are deliberate, cadenced gaits; differs because marching is a stiff, ordered walk with long strides and a martial rhythm, while a trot is quick and small-stepped.
  • Running: Similar because both are faster than a walk; differs because running abandons short-stepped rhythm for long airborne strides, while trotting keeps every step on the ground in a rapid, regular beat.
  • Ambling: Similar because both are steady gaits; differs because ambling is leisurely and unhurried, while trotting is brisk and purposeful.
  • Loping: Similar because both are animal gaits between walk and run; differs because a lope is long, easy, and bounding, the opposite of the trot's short, constrained, industrious beat.
  • Hurrying: Similar because both imply rapid movement; differs because hurrying is general and undirected haste, while trotting names a specific rhythm of short, quick, steady steps.
