Chain 1 — the Old French line:

Indo-European (reconstructed)
*bhreus-
"to break, crush, shatter"
↓
Gaulish (c. 1st millennium BCE)
*brūs-
"to break"
↓
Old North French (11th-13th c.)
bruisier
"to break, shatter, smash"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
bruisen
"to bruise, crush, injure"
↓
Modern English (c. 1500 onward)
bruise
"to injure by a blow"
↓
English (past participle)
bruised
"bearing contusions; hurt, damaged"

Chain 2 — the Old English line:

Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*brūsijaną
"to crush, bruise"
↓
Old English (8th-11th c.)
brȳsan
"to crush, bruise"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
brisen
"to crush, bruise"
↓
(merges with bruisen of Chain 1)
↓
English (past participle)
bruised
"bearing contusions; hurt, damaged"

Note on the debate: the exact source of Old French bruisier is disputed — Gaulish, Frankish, or expressive origin — and the two Middle English verbs brisen and bruisen probably merged in the 14th-15th centuries; no single parentage is documented without question.
