• Growling: Similar because both name a low guttural threat-sound; differs because growling is the broader genus (stomachs and engines growl too), while snarling adds bared teeth and visible ferocity.
  • Snapping: Similar because both describe an animal on the edge of biting; differs because snapping stresses the quick striking jaws, while snarling stresses the sustained warning sound before any bite.
  • Barking: Similar because both are loud canine warnings; differs because barking is sharp, explosive, and often merely alerting, while snarling is low, continuous, and unambiguously hostile.
  • Grumbling: Similar because both are low discontented utterances; differs because grumbling is muffled complaint without bared teeth, while snarling is open aggression with the teeth on show.
  • Sneering: Similar because both curl the lip in contempt; differs because a sneer is cold, silent superiority, while a snarl is hot audible menace.
  • Hissing: Similar because both are sustained hostile sounds meant to repel; differs because hissing is breathy and serpentine (cats, geese, crowds), while snarling is throaty and mammalian.
  • Muttering: Similar because both describe hostile speech kept low; differs because muttering is indistinct and often private, while snarling is projected and meant to intimidate its target.
  • Yapping: Similar because both are noisy dog-like vocal hostility; differs because yapping is shrill, rapid, and slightly ridiculous, while snarling carries genuine menace.
