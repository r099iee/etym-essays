• groped: Similar because both are blind tactile searching with the hands; differs because groping is careful, deliberate, methodical human search, while pawing is blunt, broad, and animal, with no precision at the fingertips.
  • scrabbled: Similar because both are frantic, ungainly motion of hands or forefeet; differs because scrabbling is hurried and clawing, often with nails scoring a surface, whereas pawing keeps the flat, unskilled pad of the paw.
  • clawed: Similar because both use the hand like an animal's limb; differs because clawing implies sharp nails or talons and aggression, while pawing remains blunt, broad, and harmless.
  • fumbled: Similar because both are clumsy use of the hands; differs because fumbling suggests handling objects with a specific thing in mind, whereas pawing is aimless animal groping with no particular target.
  • scraped: Similar because both involve rubbing against a surface; differs because scraping stresses friction against a hard surface rather than the motion of the hand itself.
  • touched / felt: Similar because both involve contact with the hands; differs because touching and feeling are delicate, neutral, and deliberate, while pawing is blunt, broad, and animal.
  • handled: Similar because both involve using the hands; differs because handling is competent, purposeful manipulation of objects, whereas pawing is rough, clumsy, and undirected.
  • stroked / fondled: Similar because both can describe physical contact with another being; differs because stroking is gentle and deliberate, while pawing (in its pejorative sense) is clumsy, insistent, and unwelcome.
