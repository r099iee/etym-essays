• Obvious: Similar because both describe what is plainly seen or understood without effort; differs because 'obvious' is the everyday, colourless word, while 'manifest' is formal and suggests the thing has been actively shown or seized, not merely noticed.
  • Evident: Similar because both mean supported by clear visible proof; differs because 'evident' points to what reasoning infers from signs, whereas 'manifest' points to what stares at the observer directly.
  • Apparent: Similar because both name what appears to the sight; differs because 'apparent' can be misleading ('the apparent cause'), while 'manifest' implies true, indisputable visibility.
  • Clear: Similar because both describe freedom from obscurity; differs because 'clear' is the broad, transparent everyday term applying to water, glass, skies and meanings alike, far wider than 'manifest'.
  • Plain: Similar because both mean unmistakeable and simple to see; differs because 'plain' stresses simplicity and lack of ornament, 'manifest' stresses the act of showing-forth.
  • Patent: Similar because both mean wide open to view (Latin patens, 'lying open'); differs because 'patent' is now strongly legal (letters patent, patent absurdity) and often implies something that declares itself by its own openness.
  • Conspicuous: Similar because both describe what is impossible to miss; differs because 'conspicuous' means standing out against a background, while 'manifest' means declared open to inspection.
  • Unmistakable: Similar because both exclude all possibility of misreading; differs because 'unmistakable' is emphatic in a colloquial register where 'manifest' is sober and formal.
  • Self-evident: Similar because both name truths that need no demonstration; differs because 'self-evident' is specifically the vocabulary of axioms and rights ('We hold these truths to be self-evident'), while 'manifest' is wider and more physical.
  • Palpable: Similar because both derive from the idea of seizing or touching (manifestus literally means 'seized by the hand'); differs because 'palpable' foregrounds the tactile, felt certainty where 'manifest' foregrounds sight.
  • Overt: Similar because both mean done or shown openly; differs because 'overt' opposes 'covert' (secret action), while 'manifest' opposes 'latent' (hidden potential).
  • Blatant: Similar because both describe something glaringly visible; differs because 'blatant' carries censure and aggression (a blatant lie), while 'manifest' is neutral or even dignified.
  • Glaring: Similar because both describe what strikes the eye violently; differs because 'glaring' is the image of blinding brightness and is almost always pejorative.
  • Flagrant: Similar because both mean conspicuously bad; differs because 'flagrant' is confined to offences and wrongdoing, while 'manifest' covers neutral truths and objects too.
  • Revealed: Similar because both describe what was hidden now shown (the verbal sense 'manifested'); differs because 'revealed' implies a prior concealment and an agent doing the revealing, while 'manifest' can describe what is simply there to see.
  • Exhibited / Displayed: Similar because both describe putting on show (verbal 'manifested'); differs because they suggest deliberate arrangement for viewing, while 'manifest' implies natural, unforced self-showing.
  • Tank: Similar because both name large containers for liquids; differs because a tank is typically closed, pressurised, and now strongly military or vehicular, while a vat is open-topped and process-oriented.
  • Tub: Similar because both are open vessels; differs because a tub is smaller, domestic and round, a vat is industrial and capacious.
  • Cask: Similar because both are wooden vessels for liquids; differs because a cask is a barrel-shaped, closed container with bungs, used for ageing, while a vat is open.
  • Barrel: Similar because both are bulk liquid vessels; differs because a barrel is a standardised measure of capacity with a distinct curved shape, a vat has no standard size.
  • Butt / Tun: Similar because both are old measures and large vessels for wine or ale; differs because 'butt' and 'tun' are archaic and metrological, whereas 'vat' remains living and industrial.
  • Cistern: Similar because both are large receptacles for liquid; differs because a cistern stores water from a supply, often underground, while a vat holds material in processing.
  • Reservoir: Similar because both are large liquid holders; differs because a reservoir is vast, natural or built for the storage of a water supply, far larger than any vat.
  • Copper / Kettle: Similar in brewing because both are process vessels; differs because they are heated for boiling, whereas a vat is usually an unheated fermentation or storage tank.
