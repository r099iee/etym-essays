WHIRLED
PIE (reconstructed; uncertain)
*kʷerp-
"to turn, whirl"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*hwerbaną
"to turn, whirl"
↓
Old Norse (10th–13th c.)
hvirfla
"to whirl, turn around"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
whirlen
"to whirl, turn rapidly"
↓
English (13th c.)
whirl
"to turn rapidly in a circle"
↓
English (past tense)
whirled
"moved rapidly in a circular course"

Note: the PIE root *kʷerp- "to turn" is uncertain. Middle English whorlen (frequentative of whir) is an alternative source; the origin may be partly echoic.
