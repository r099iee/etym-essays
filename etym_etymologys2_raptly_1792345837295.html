PIE (reconstructed)
*h₁rep-
"to snatch, seize, grab violently"
(ancestor of Latin rapere, Lithuanian aprėpti "to encompass")
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*rapi-
"to snatch away, seize"
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE; Plautus)
rapere
"to seize, snatch, carry off violently, abduct"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE; Cicero, Virgil)
raptus
"snatched, carried away; abducted, ravished"
(perfect passive participle of rapere)
↓
Late Latin / Ecclesiastical Latin (4th–5th c.; Vulgate, Augustine)
raptus
"caught up in spirit, carried into ecstatic trance"
(applied to mystical transportation, as St. Paul in 2 Cor. 12:2)
↓
Middle French (14th c.)
rapt
"abducted; transported in ecstasy"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1570s; Spenser, Shakespeare)
rapt
"transported with emotion, enraptured, deeply absorbed"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1620s–1630s; Henry More, Milton)
raptly
"in a spellbound, enraptured, or utterly absorbed manner"
(formed from adjective rapt + Germanic adverbial suffix -ly)
