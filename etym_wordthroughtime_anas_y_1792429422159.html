- [Late Vedic & Epic Composition, c. 800–200 BCE] — Introduced in the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* as the austere wife of sage Atri dwelling in the forest near Citrakūṭa, renowned for performing penance that created fruits and trees during a ten-year drought — [perception: revered as a majestic forest matriarch whose moral power commanded elemental nature]
- [Epic Transmission & Encounter with Sītā, c. 500 BCE–300 CE] — Celebrated across Sanskrit and regional versions of the *Rāmāyaṇa* for delivering a profound discourse on *strīdharma* (wifely duty) and gifting Sītā the miraculous *aṅgarāga* (celestial unguent) that kept her radiant and unblemished throughout exile — [perception: revered as the ultimate feminine spiritual mentor and protector of Sītā's purity]
- [Purāṇic Era, c. 300–1200 CE] — Expanded in the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*, *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*, and *Śiva Purāṇa* with the famous legend of the Trimūrti testing her chastity, where she turned Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Śiva into babies, becoming mother to Dattātreya, Durvāsas, and Soma — [perception: elevated into a cosmic figure of supreme *tapas* surpassing even the highest male deities]
- [Medieval & Bhakti Period, c. 1200–1800 CE] — Revered in Marathi, Telugu, and Hindi devotional literature (particularly in the Dattātreya sampradāya) as the divine mother (*Mātā Anasūyā*), celebrated in pilgrim temples at Chitrakoot — [perception: worshipped as the ideal divine mother whose unconditional love disarms divine wrath]
- [Modern Era, 19th–21st c.] — Maintained in contemporary Hindu culture as a revered personal name and archetype of selfless grace, examined in feminist Indology as a complex portrait of female spiritual autonomy within classical patriarchal frameworks — [perception: honored as an enduring emblem of moral integrity, maternal strength, and non-envious spiritual generosity]
