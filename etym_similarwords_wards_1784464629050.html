• Ridges: Similar because both are raised, elongated internal projections; differs because a ridge is a general physical term for any raised line or crest, while wards are specifically the lock-guarding projections that a key must be cut to match.
  • Projections: Similar because both are parts that stick out from a surface; differs because projection is the neutral engineering term for any protrusion, while wards are a particular functional kind — the guards of a lock mechanism.
  • Tumblers: Similar because both are lock parts that must align for a key to work; differs because tumblers are the modern pin-tumbler mechanism inside the lock, while wards are the older cut ridges and grooves that the key must clear.
  • Pins: Similar because both are small lock components that must be lifted by the key; differs because pins are the spring-loaded elements of modern pin-tumbler locks, while wards are fixed ridges, not moving parts.
  • Bit: Similar because both are parts of the key-lock system; differs because the bit is the blade or projection of the key that engages the mechanism, while the wards are the internal ridges inside the lock that the bit must be shaped to clear.
  • Notches: Similar because both are the cut-outs on a key that let it pass; differs because notches are the cuts themselves, while wards are the projections in the lock that those cuts are shaped to pass (the notches are the key's wards' complement).
  • Grooves: Similar because both are channels that a matching shape must fit; differs because grooves are the channels on the key, while wards are the raised projections of the lock that the grooves must clear.
  • Guards: Similar because both are protective elements that keep something out; differs because guards is the general word for anything that protects, while wards is the specific locksmithing term for the lock's internal projections that guard against wrong keys.
  • Protections: Similar because both are mechanisms that prevent access; differs because protection is abstract and general, while wards are concrete metal parts whose guarding is mechanical and specific.
  • Barriers: Similar because both block passage; differs because a barrier is any obstruction (wall, fence, gate), while wards are precise engineered projections whose purpose is selective admission of a matching key.
  • Stops: Similar because both prevent movement; differs because stops are general arresting mechanisms, while wards are the particular internal ridges of a lock that stop wrong keys.
  • Cams: Similar because both are parts of a lock mechanism that interact with the key; differs because cams are rotating elements that transfer motion, while wards are fixed ridges that only permit or block.
  • Teeth (of a key): Similar because both are the cut parts of a key that fit a mechanism; differs because teeth are the serrations on a key that engage pins, while wards are the ridges in the lock, and the two must be complementary.
  • Baffles: Similar because both are internal structures that obstruct or divert; differs because baffles are general devices (mufflers, shields) for blocking or redirecting, while wards are the lock-specific projections tied to key shape.
