- 16th to 17th Century (Elizabethan & Jacobean Domestic Architecture) - Designated functional screens of crossed wooden laths fitted into unglazed window openings and door panels to permit fresh airflow while maintaining privacy and keeping out birds and debris; associated with domestic modesty and tavern identity (the famous 'red lattice' of alehouses).
- 18th Century (Georgian Gardening & Chinoiserie) - Elevated from utilitarian carpentry to sophisticated decorative landscape design; synonymous with garden arbours, gazebos, and trellises supporting exotic climbing flora, heavily influenced by imported Chinese architectural motifs.
- 19th Century (Victorian Engineering & Cast Iron) - Transformed by the Industrial Revolution into structural metallurgy; 'latticework' came to denote wrought-iron and steel lattice girders, bridge spans, and crane booms, symbolising Victorian industrial triumph, strength-to-weight optimization, and aerodynamic efficiency.
- Early to Mid-20th Century (Scientific & Theoretical Crystallography) - Adopted into physics, chemistry, and solid-state materials science to describe the orderly, repetitive three-dimensional spatial arrangement of atoms, ions, and molecules in crystal lattices (Bravais lattices).
- Late 20th to 21st Century (Modern Metaphor & Cognitive Science) - Widely employed as an evocative metaphor in philosophy, finance (Charlie Munger's 'latticework of mental models'), neuroscience ('a latticework of synaptic connections'), and digital networking, carrying connotations of systemic resilience, mutual interdependence, and intellectual synthesis.
