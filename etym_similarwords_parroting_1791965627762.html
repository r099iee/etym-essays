• echoing: Similar because it describes the reproduction of another’s words; differs in being neutral and often unintentional, with none of parroting’s charge of mindless or servile imitation.
  • mimicking: Similar in copying another’s speech; differs in emphasising the imitation of manner or sound rather than the repetition of content, and can be playful.
  • imitating: Similar as copying; differs in being the broad, neutral term, applicable to behaviour and style, and lacking the specific accusation of empty verbal repetition.
  • repeating: Similar as saying again; differs in being entirely neutral — one can repeat a sentence with full understanding, which is exactly what parroting denies.
  • aping: Similar in implying unthinking imitation; differs in carrying a stronger note of ridiculous or servile mimicry of a person’s whole manner, not just their words.
  • copying: Similar as reproduction; differs in being about the duplication of a product or text, without the cognitive judgement that parroting implies.
  • regurgitating: Similar in reproducing material undigested; differs in being more visceral and more contemptuous, suggesting that the material has not been processed at all.
  • reciting: Similar in reproducing words from memory; differs in implying a formal, deliberate performance, often with understanding of the text’s shape.
  • chanting: Similar in repeated formulaic utterance; differs in its ritual and rhythmic dimension, and in implying group participation rather than individual unoriginality.
  • quoting: Similar in repeating another’s words; differs in being honest about the source, whereas parroting conceals or ignores the ventriloquism.
  • mouthing: Similar in producing words without meaning; differs in suggesting the words are empty or insincere rather than learned by imitation.
