• Cosimo de’ Medici: similar because he is the other great Medici patron who shaped Renaissance Florence; differs because Cosimo founded the family’s power and prefers the role of banker-eminence behind the scenes, whereas Lorenzo personifies the full princely court.
  • Pericles: similar because Pater, and tradition generally, pairs the ages of Pericles and Lorenzo as “many-sided, centralised, complete” epochs; differs because Pericles ruled a democratic Athenian empire, while Lorenzo governed a Republic through private wealth.
  • Augustus: similar because both rulers presided over a golden age of the arts and were later read as their eras’ embodiment; differs because Augustus was an emperor of a world-empire, Lorenzo a first citizen of one city-state.
  • Laurentius / Lawrence: similar because they are the Latin and English forms of the same name, sharing the claim “from Laurentum”; differs in inventory: Laurentius is the Roman saint’s form, Lawrence the English name, Lorenzo the Italian Renaissance form.
  • Maecenas: similar because both are archetypal patrons of literature and the arts; differs because Maecenas was a Roman eques who bankrolled Vergil and Horace, while Lorenzo was himself a poet, author of the Canti carnascialeschi and a serious Petrarchan lyricist.
  • Principe / Signore: similar as terms for the Renaissance ruler whose court underwrites high culture; differs in being generic titles rather than a personal name with specific historical freight.
