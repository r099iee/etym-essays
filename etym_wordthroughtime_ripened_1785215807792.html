- Old English - “Ripen” (ripian) is the agricultural verb for the ripening of crops and fruit — the natural, seasonal growth toward harvest-readiness; a word of the fields and orchards.
- 14th–16th century - Extends from fruit to people and affairs: to ripen into maturity, plans ripening into action; the organic image of coming to fullness spreads widely.
- 18th–19th century (figurative flourish) - Used of feelings and attachments: an acquaintance that “ripened into intimacy and friendship” — the metaphor claims naturalness, inevitability, and wholesomeness for what grew swiftly; Mill's “rapidly” adds an August heat to the organic image.
- 20th–21st century - Common in both literal (fruit ripening) and figurative senses (a ripened talent, conditions ripened); retains the wholesome, natural, non-forced connotation of fulfilling an inner potential.
