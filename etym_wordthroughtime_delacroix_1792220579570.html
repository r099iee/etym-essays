- 12th–17th c. — Established throughout northern and central France as a widespread locative/toponymic surname (de la croix), designating a family residing near an intersection, village cross, or calvary chapel.
- 1798–1830 — Eugène Delacroix rises to public prominence in Paris, creating a national scandal at the Paris Salons; the name becomes synonymous in the press with dangerous artistic heresy, wild chromatic chaos, and radical romantic rebellion.
- 1830–1863 — Solidified as the supreme standard of the Romantic movement; championed by Baudelaire and respected by progressive intellectuals, while remaining controversial among conservative academic *pompiers*.
- Late 19th c. — Canonized posthumously as the intellectual wellspring of the Impressionist and Post-Impressionist vanguard, with Cézanne, Van Gogh, and Redon venerating his journals and chromatic palette.
- 20th–21st c. — Universalized in international museum culture and art history as the monumental titan of European Romanticism, an emblem of French republican liberty, and an irreplaceable metonym for museum-grade masterpieces.
