• Travel: Similar because both mean to journey or go from place to place; differs because 'travel' is the modern, neutral, general verb for journeying, while 'fare' as motion is now literary/archaic and also carries the sense of condition.
  • Journey: Similar because both mean to make one's way over a distance; differs because 'journey' focuses on the whole trip or trek (and can be a noun), while 'fare' emphasizes proceeding and the outcome of the trip.
  • Proceed: Similar because both mean to go forward or continue on a course; differs because 'proceed' is more formal and neutral of direction, whereas 'fare' retains an archaic travelling flavour and a fortune-outcome sense.
  • Get on: Similar because both mean to manage, succeed, or be in a state ('how did you get on'); differs because 'get on' is informal and purely about progress/standing, while 'fare' spans both travelling and thriving.
  • Manage / get by: Similar because both mean to cope or succeed under conditions; differs because these are entirely about outcome and survival, with no trace of the original travelling sense that 'fare' preserves.
  • Succeed: Similar because both can mean to do well or turn out well; differs because 'succeed' implies achieving a goal, a narrower and more positive sense than the neutral 'fare well or badly'.
  • Go: Similar because both mean to move or proceed; differs because 'go' is the universal, colourless verb of motion, while 'fare' is more specific, dignified, and laden with the hint of fortune.
