• Celtiberians: Similar because they were ancient peoples of Iberia; differs because Celtiberians were mixed Celtic-Iberian interior groups, Iberians proper were Mediterranean-coast non-Celtic groups.
  • Lusitanians: Similar because they inhabited western Iberia; differs because Lusitanians were a distinct western tribal group (likely Indo-European), not part of Iberian culture proper.
  • Tartessians: Similar because they were a pre-Roman southwestern Iberian people; differs because Tartessians represent an earlier Atlantic-oriented kingdom (c. 9th–6th c. BCE), Iberians the later Mediterranean Iron-Age culture.
  • Celts: Similar because they were Iron-Age tribal peoples of western Europe; differs because Celts spoke Indo-European languages and occupied central/northern Iberia, Iberians spoke non-Indo-European languages on the coast.
  • Picts: Similar because they were ancient tribal peoples of the British Isles fringe cited by Durant; differs because Picts lived in northern Britain centuries later, Iberians in Spain.
  • Gauls: Similar because they were Roman-era barbarian peoples; differs because Gauls lived in France, spoke Celtic, and were conquered by Caesar, Iberians lived in Spain and were conquered in the Punic wars.
  • Britons: Similar because they were island tribal peoples facing Rome; differs because Britons were Celtic-speaking inhabitants of Britain, Iberians of Spain.
  • Phoenicians: Similar because they settled and traded in Iberia; differs because Phoenicians were eastern colonists and traders, Iberians the native population.
  • Carthaginians: Similar because they ruled parts of Iberia; differs because Carthaginians were an imperial occupying power, Iberians the subjected natives.
  • Romans: Similar because they are the successor civilisation in Iberia; differs because Romans were conquerors and administrators, Iberians the conquered tribal substrate.
  • Spaniards: Similar because they inhabit the same peninsula; differs because Spaniards are the modern nation, Iberians the ancient tribal predecessors.
  • Aquitanians: Similar because they were a neighbouring pre-Roman people; differs because Aquitanians lived north of the Pyrenees in Gaul, Iberians south of them.
