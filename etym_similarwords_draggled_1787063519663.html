• Bedraggled: Similar because it means the same wet, dirty, limp state produced by dragging or trailing; differs because it is far commoner today and is intensified by the prefix be- ('thoroughly'), so it sounds slightly stronger and more emphatic.
  • Sodden: Similar because it describes something soaked and heavy with moisture; differs because it emphasises waterlogging alone, with no idea of dragging, trailing, or soiling from contact with the ground.
  • Soaked / Drenched: Similar because both describe extreme wetness; differ because they are neutral about dirt and dishevelment — you can be drenched by clean rain yet look neat, whereas draggled always implies filth and collapse of form.
  • Waterlogged: Similar because it describes something saturated and heavy; differs because it belongs to objects and terrain (boats, wood, ground) rather than to living creatures and clothing.
  • Dishevelled: Similar because it describes hair and appearance in untidy disarray; differs because the untidiness need not come from water or dragging — it can come from wind, sleep, or neglect.
  • Soiled / Dirty / Muddy: Similar because all describe the presence of dirt; differ because they say nothing about wetness or the dragging process — draggled bundles dirt, moisture, and motion together.
  • Draggle-tailed: Similar because it shares the root and pictures a trailing, soiled hem or tail; differs because it is used of people (especially women) and of fowls as a term of pity or contempt for slatternly untidiness.
  • Limp / Flaccid: Similar because both describe a loss of stiffness and form; differ because they are neutral physical terms with no implication of wetness or dirt.
