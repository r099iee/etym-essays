- Proto-Germanic / Old English era — perceived as act of pulling/drawing or its result; tightly bound to physical haul and the verb draw/drag
- Middle English — draught/draght broadens: loads, drinks drawn, breath/wind, sketches; plural in ordinary use for multiple pulls or potions
- 16th–17th c. — Drinking “draughts” highly idiomatic in literature and daily speech; game of draughts established in English naming; air draughts part of domestic complaint
- 18th–19th c. — Technical senses flourish (nautical draught, medical draught); American spelling reform and practice push draft; checkers gains ground as AmE game name
- 20th c. — Clear BrE/AmE split in preferred spellings and game name; “draught beer” and “cold draught” remain British household phrases; literary “deep draughts” still readable internationally
- 21st c. — Polysemy intact; learners often meet draughts first as game or as spelling variant of draft; drinking sense feels elevated or British to many American readers; root link to draw still transparent on reflection
