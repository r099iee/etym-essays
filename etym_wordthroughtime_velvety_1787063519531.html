- Late Middle English (c. 1400s) - [Textile & Silk Luxury] - Emerged alongside the importation of Italian velvet, describing fine woven fabrics with dense warp-pile.
- 1500s–1600s - [Botanical & Tactile Extension] - Extended in Elizabethan naturalism and gardening to describe the downy, delicate textures of moss, peach skin, and rose petals.
- 1700s–1800s - [Synesthetic Aesthetic Senses] - Broadened across Romantic poetry and music criticism to describe deep, light-absorbing shadows, night skies, and rich vocal timbres.
- 1900s–Present - [Gastronomy, Oenology & Natural History] - Standardized as a premier descriptor in wine evaluation, fine dining, and descriptive literature for seamless softness and richness.
