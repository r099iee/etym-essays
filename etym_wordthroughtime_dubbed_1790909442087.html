- 11th c. - Emerged in Late Old English (dubbian) from Old French and Germanic roots to describe the physical ceremonial striking of a new knight with a sword.
- 14th & 15th c. - Broadened in chivalric literature from the physical blow to the general conferral of rank, dignity, and noble titles.
- 16th & 17th c. - Extended metaphorically to mean conferring any popular nickname, moniker, or honorary descriptor on a person or thing.
- 1920s & 1930s - Transferred into the burgeoning sound film and recording industries for the technical process of duplicating soundtracks or replacing voice tracks with foreign translations.
- 1970s to Present - Cemented in modern music production for reggae 'dub' remixes, while remaining universally prevalent in journalism for public naming and in international streaming media for voiceovers.
