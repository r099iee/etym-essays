- Late 14th Century (c. 1398) — Entered Middle English strictly as a medical and physiological descriptor for bodily fluids and organs pertaining to biological reproduction.
- Renaissance & Early Modern (c. 1620s–1680s) — Re-borrowed the Latin philosophical metaphor of rationes seminales, using 'seminal' to describe latent creative principles or embryonic states of ideas.
- 18th & 19th Centuries — Expanded into historiography and literary criticism to designate foundational works that generated new artistic or scientific movements.
- 20th Century — Standardized across academic disciplines as the supreme scholarly accolade for landmark, paradigm-shifting treatises, experiments, and legal decisions.
- Contemporary Era (Late 20th c.–Present) — Scrutinized by feminist sociolinguists for its gendered male reproductive etymology, encouraging stylistic use alongside neutral terms like foundational and groundbreaking.
