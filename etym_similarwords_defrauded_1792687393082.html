• cheated: Similar because both denote suffering loss through dishonesty or bad faith; differs because cheated is more informal and broad, encompassing petty card games, classroom exams, or casual betrayals, whereas defrauded possesses formal juridical weight, implying calculated artifice, breach of covenant, or systemic deception.
  • swindled: Similar because both involve the loss of money or property via calculated schemes; differs because swindled emphasizes the crafty, slick, and manipulative performance of a confidence artist, while defrauded focuses on the resulting legal injury and breach of equity.
  • duped: Similar because deception is the instrument of injury; differs because duped places primary focus on the victim's gullibility, naivety, or foolish credulity, whereas defrauded places the moral and legal opprobrium entirely upon the bad-faith perpetrator and their structured violation.
  • fleeced: Similar because both convey stripping someone of assets; differs because fleeced is a colorful pastoral metaphor emphasizing how thoroughly and ruthlessly the victim was sheared of their wealth, often through exorbitant fees or lopsided transactions rather than strictly criminal misrepresentation.
  • bamboozled: Similar because deceit is involved; differs because bamboozled highlights profound intellectual mystification, confusion, and obfuscation, lacking the solemn legal culpability and grave material consequence implicit in defrauded.
  • dispossessed: Similar because both leave the subject deprived of rightful property; differs because dispossessed typically occurs through direct force, legal eviction, conquest, or administrative expropriation, whereas defrauded requires covert deception, concealment, or bad faith.
  • robbed: Similar because both denote being deprived of rightful possessions; differs because robbed inherently connotes violent assault, coercive menace, or stealthy physical trespass, whereas defrauded relies on fraudulent representation where the victim is tricked into relinquishing the good.
  • cozened: Similar because both describe crafty deceit; differs because cozened is an archaic, literary term emphasizing smooth-tongued flattery, wheedling, and ingratiating trickery rather than statutory fraud.
