Kunstprosa (German Kunstprosa, literally art-prose) is a German literary term meaning artistic, deliberately crafted prose — prose written and judged as art, with the same conscious attention to rhythm, sound, imagery, and structure traditionally reserved for verse. In your sentence — what has always been called Kunstprosa, ‘art prose’ — it names the high tradition of wrought prose that an information society must now take seriously rather than dismiss as ornament.

In German criticism Kunstprosa contrasts with Gebrauchsprosa (utility prose: memos, reports, instructions) and with Alltagsprosa (everyday prose). Kunstprosa includes the ceremonial essay, the literary oration, the art novel, the crafted historical narrative — Goethe, Thomas Mann, Hofmannsthal, Musil — where each sentence is weighed for euphony, hypotaxis, cadence, and figurative density. English approximates it with art prose, literary prose, poetic prose, purple prose (pejorative), but none carries the institutional weight of the German category, which has textbooks, histories, and university chairs.

  • Core literary sense: rhythmically and rhetorically elaborated prose claiming aesthetic autonomy. Features: periodic sentences, isocolon, alliteration, metaphoric chains, deliberate archaism or neologism, musical phrasing. Example: Mann's Der Zauberberg, Hofmannsthal's essays, Woolf's waves in German translation theory.
  • Historical-philological sense: in medieval and humanist studies, Kunstprosa denotes the emergence of stylized Latin and vernacular prose (Ciceronian cursus, Gottfried von Strassburg, Luther's Bible) as distinct from chronicle jottings. Scholars trace clausulae, rhyme prose, and rhetorical figures.
  • Polemical / pejorative fringe: opponents use Kunstprosa to sneer at mandarins who embroider when they should inform — exactly the executive dismissal your passage quotes. Defenders reply that information without form is noise.
  • Translation and comparative sense: English critics borrowing the German word signal that English lacks an equivalent prestige category. To say Kunstprosa instead of fine writing invokes German Geistesgeschichte and claims seriousness.
  • Extended modern sense: science writing, nature writing, and creative nonfiction that aspire to art — Sebald, Handke, Le Clézio in German reception — are discussed as new Kunstprosa, bridging information and beauty. Your information-society argument belongs here.

Form: feminine noun in German die Kunstprosa, no plural in normal use, often lower-case kunstprosa in English notes. Compound stress on first element: KUNST-pro-sa. English writers italicize it as foreignism: Kunstprosa. Adjective: kunstprosaisch (rare). Do not confuse with Kunstprosaik (theory of art prose) or Prosakunst (near-synonym with reversed emphasis).

Register is academic-literary, high, specialist. You meet it in histories of style, dissertations on Thomas Mann, and — as here — in arguments that prose analysis needs a taxonomy. Collocates: deutsche Kunstprosa, Tradition der Kunstprosa, Meister der Kunstprosa, Rhythmus der Kunstprosa. Connotation among initiates is reverent; among managers, suspicious. The book rehabilitates it: if information is the stuff of the next century, Kunstprosa is not a pesky ornament but the central discipline of shaping attention.

Bridge to neighbours: Kunst art + Prosa prose (straightforward, non-straightforward speech). The paradox — art which is prosaic, direct speech made indirect and beautiful — explains endless debate about whether true Kunstprosa hides its art (classical clarity) or flaunts it (baroque abundance). Both schools claim Goethe.
