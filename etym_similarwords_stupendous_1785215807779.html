• Prodigious: Similar because both mean "wonderfully large"; differs because prodigious leans toward feats of skill or productivity and has an old-fashioned flavour, while stupendous fuses extreme magnitude with mental paralysis — awe that stuns.
  • Colossal: Similar because both denote immense scale; differs because colossal describes sheer bulk with no necessary wonderment, while stupendous insists the scale is great enough to astonish.
  • Enormous: Similar because both mean very great in size or degree; differs because enormous is a neutral measure of size with no implication of awe, while stupendous always carries the sense of overwhelming wonder.
  • Immense: Similar because both suggest unbounded magnitude; differs because immense is a calm, factual word for "without measure," while stupendous is the same magnitude plus a stunned observer.
  • Gigantic: Similar because both name enormous size; differs because gigantic is a vivid but everyday word for great size, with no learned or paralyzing awe.
  • Monumental: Similar because both suggest impressive scale; differs because monumental implies lasting historical importance and a hint of stone and memorials, while stupendous emphasizes the immediate stunning of the imagination.
  • Astonishing: Similar because both stress the observer's wonder; differs because astonishing can describe quite small things and is now overused to the point of mildness, whereas stupendous still demands overwhelming magnitude.
  • Amazing: Similar because both express wonder; differs because amazing is casual and applicable to anything pleasant or surprising, while stupendous reserves wonder for the overwhelming and the colossal.
  • Astounding: Similar because both mean "stunning the mind"; differs because astounding stresses the shock of the news or event, while stupendous stresses the sheer magnitude of what is described.
  • Staggering: Similar because both describe something that destabilizes the mind; differs because staggering emphasizes the effect on the observer (making one reel), while stupendous emphasizes the object's magnitude that causes the effect.
  • Phenomenal: Similar because both mean extraordinary; differs because phenomenal is now a common commercial intensifier for success and growth, while stupendous keeps a more literary, awe-laden tone.
  • Breathtaking: Similar because both describe something that overwhelms the senses; differs because breathtaking is used of beauty and views, while stupendous is used of magnitude, force, and scale.
  • Mind-boggling: Similar because both mean astonishing to the point of disbelief; differs because mind-boggling is informal and jocular, while stupendous is formal and reverential.
  • Marvellous: Similar because both express admiration; differs because marvellous is a general word of approval for anything wonderful, without stupendous's insistence on overwhelming scale.
  • Herculean: Similar because both describe great effort; differs because Herculean emphasizes the difficulty of a task (worthy of Hercules), while stupendous emphasizes the astonishing scale of the achievement itself.
