• Rods: Similar in being long solid cylinders; differs because a rod has no radial implication — it may lie flat or stand alone, while a spoke runs specifically from hub to rim.
  • Bars: Similar in being rigid pieces of material; differs because a bar is a horizontal or vertical member of general use, with none of the wheel-geometry that a spoke requires.
  • Struts: Similar in being structural supports; differs because struts are compressive members in any orientation, belonging to engineering generally rather than to the wheel's hub-and-rim geometry.
  • Shafts: Similar in being long projecting pieces; differs because shafts are rotating axes or arrow-like projections (a shaft of light, a mine shaft), not wheel members.
  • Rays: Similar in radiating outward from a centre; differs because rays are purely geometric lines without solidity — light radiates, but a ray cannot bear weight like a spoke.
  • Radii: Similar in naming lines from centre to circumference; differs because radii is a technical mathematical term for half the diameter, not a physical wheel part.
  • Rungs: Similar in being connecting rods between two supports; differs because rungs connect two side rails horizontally, as on a ladder, and never radiate from a hub.
  • Spindles: Similar in being thin tapered rods; differs because spindles are ornamental or axle-like rods, without the specific hub-and-rim function of a spoke.
  • Needles: Similar in being thin pointed rods; differs because needles are pointed for piercing or knitting, with no structural wheel role.
