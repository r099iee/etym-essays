[CHAIN 1 — first component: parare “to defend”] PIE *perh₂- (also *perə-) “to provide, produce, bring forth” (reconstructed; Sanskrit pṛṇāti “he grants, bestows”) → Latin parāre “to make ready, prepare, arrange” (attested c. 2nd c. BCE) → Old French parer “to prepare, adorn, ward off” (c. 10th c.) → Italian parare “to prepare; to shield, defend, ward off” (Renaissance) [CHAIN 2 — second component: petto “breast”] PIE *peg- “breast” (reconstructed chiefly on the evidence of Latin pectus; very few certain cognates) → Latin pectus “breast, chest” (genitive pectoris; attested c. 2nd c. BCE) → Italian petto “chest, breast” (regular Romance descendant of Latin pectus) [COMPOUND] Italian parapetto “a low chest-high wall protecting a defender” (parare “to defend” + petto “breast”: literally “chest-warder”; attested in military engineering, c. 16th c.) → French parapet “parapet” (borrowed from Italian, 16th c.) → English parapet “a low protective wall at the edge of a roof, bridge, or fortification” (1580s, via French) → English parapets (plural form, as on the present card) ◄ TARGET Note on the debated PIE stage: *peg- is reconstructed chiefly on the evidence of Latin pectus; certain cognates outside the Italic branch are few, so the root stage is debated and the tree must honestly stop with this single solid daughter within Italic. The other component, *perh₂-, is far better attested (Sanskrit, Greek, and Latin cognates).
