*swe- (self) + *dheh₁- (to put, set; Proto-Indo-European, reconstructed) — the source of the Indo-European family of "custom, habit, what is one's own"; the same compound gives Greek ēthos "custom, character" (whence English ethics, ethos) and Sanskrit svadhā "one's own nature, custom"
→ Latin solere (to be accustomed, to be in the habit of; from the PIE notion of "one's own way, one's wont"; classical, attested 2nd–1st c. BCE) — present participle solens "accustomed"
→ Latin insolens (unaccustomed, unusual, immoderate, arrogant, haughty; negative in- "not" + solens "accustomed"; classical, attested in Cicero, 1st c. BCE)
→ Old French insolent (arrogant, insolent; 12th–13th c.)
→ Middle English insolent (insolent, arrogant, presumptuous; first recorded 14th c.)
→ English INSOLENT (14th c. to present) ◄ TARGET

Note on debate: The derivation of Latin solere from PIE *swe-dh(e)- "one's own way/custom" is widely accepted but not beyond dispute; some scholars prefer a link to *sel- or treat solere as a semantically isolated formation. What is certain is the in- + solens morphology of insolens itself and the documented shift from "unaccustomed" to "arrogant," already complete within Latin. Greek ēthos "custom, character" is a sister formation from the same *swedh- compound, not an ancestor.
