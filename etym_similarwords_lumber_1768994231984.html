• Timber: Similar because both name wood for building; differs because timber refers to standing or felled trees and wood suited for building, retaining a natural, arboreal, even noble flavor, while lumber is its commercial end-product — already cut to planks, drained of the forest.
  • Wood: Similar because both name the material of trees; differs because wood is the raw substance itself, fully generic and warm in register, while lumber is specifically sawn, marketable boards and planks — the wood of commerce rather than the substance of nature.
  • Planks / boards: Similar because lumber consists of planks and boards; differs because plank and board name the individual pieces of sawn wood, while lumber is the collective, commercial term for the whole sawn supply.
  • Logs: Similar because both are wood destined for building; differs because a log is the raw, round trunk before sawing, while lumber is the sawn product — the difference between the forest and the sawmill.
  • Junk / clutter: Similar to the British sense of lumber as heavy, useless objects; differs because junk and clutter are the general modern words for discarded things, while lumber (BrE) is specifically the heavy, bulky, awkward odds and ends that are hard to move — the sense that links the word to clumsiness.
  • Shamble: Similar to the verb to lumber in moving clumsily and heavily; differs because shamble suggests a shuffling, broken gait, while lumbering is the large, swaying, heavy motion of a big body or machine — more massive, less broken.
  • Plod: Similar in heavy, effortful movement; differs because plod is slow, regular, dogged walking, while lumbering is the ungainly, swaying motion of bulk — less directed and more clumsy than plod.
  • Hew / saw (the verb to lumber): Similar to the American verb to lumber ("cut forest trees") in preparing wood; differs because hew and saw are the general verbs of cutting wood, while to lumber is the specific industry verb of felling and preparing timber for market.
  • Bulk / mass: Similar in the sense of lumber as heavy, bulky material; differs because bulk and mass are abstract terms for size and volume, while lumber is the concrete stuff — heavy wooden objects, sawn or accumulated.
