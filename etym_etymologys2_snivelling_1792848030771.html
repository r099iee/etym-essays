Imitative Proto-Germanic base (c. 500 BCE)
*sn-
‘nose-sound root: sniff, snort’
↓
Old English relatives (9th–11th c.)
snot, gesnot
‘nasal mucus’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
snivelen, snevelen
‘to run at the nose, sniffle’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
snivel
‘to weep with sniffling; to whine’
↓
Modern English (current)
snivelling
‘tearful sniffling; whining’

Note: snivel belongs to the imitative sn- cluster with sniff, snuff, snort, and snot. No single PIE root is securely assigned; handbooks treat the cluster as sound-symbolic Germanic formation rather than inheritance from one reconstructible ancestor, so the chain above honestly starts from the imitative base.
