- 1860s–1880s schoolboy good show / bad show - verdicts on pluck on the field; sporting, tribal, moral
- 1890s–1910s extension to general conduct - bad show to funk, to sneak, to let down; honour-code, imperial, stoical
- 1920s–1930s Wodehousian sympathy formula - Bad show this, old flesh and blood; verdict softened into condolence; comic, affectionate, adult reuse of school slang
- Post-war ironic survival - jolly good show, bad show old chap; self-consciously vintage; nostalgic and playful
- Modern British hearty idiom - good show still cheers fêtes and charity runs; bad show still gently deplores without wounding
