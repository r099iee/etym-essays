• Commotion: Similar because both name a disturbance or rush of activity; differs because commotion is a general noisy disturbance, usually longer-lasting and without the lightness, brevity, or weather-like quality of a flurry.
  • Bustle: Similar because both describe busy activity; differs because bustle suggests purposeful, sustained, organised busyness, while a flurry is brief, scattered, and chaotic.
  • Squall: Similar because both are sudden bursts of wind or weather; differs because squall is strictly meteorological (a violent gust or short storm), while flurry is most often used of human activity or light snow.
  • Gust: Similar because both are brief bursts; differs because gust is a single rush of air, while a flurry is many small things — snowflakes, footsteps, actions — at once.
  • Frenzy: Similar because both denote agitated excitement; differs because frenzy is intense, uncontrolled, and often prolonged or violent, while a flurry is brief and comparatively light.
  • Fluster: Similar because both connote agitated, scattered nervous energy; differs because fluster is a state of mental confusion in a person, whereas flurry is an outward burst of activity or weather.
  • Outburst: Similar because both are sudden, brief releases of something; differs because outburst names an emotional or verbal eruption, while flurry emphasises the multiplicity of small movements.
  • Flutter: Similar because both imply quick, light, agitated movement; differs because flutter is the tremulous motion of a single thing (wings, a heart), while flurry is a burst of many scattered things.
  • Rush: Similar because both denote sudden haste; differs because rush is a general rapid movement or hurry, without the connotation of many separate small things or the strict brevity of a flurry.
  • Stir: Similar because both denote a small commotion; differs because stir is gentler, often a quiet ripple of interest, while a flurry is more agitated and scattered.
  • Spurt: Similar because both are short, sudden bursts of activity; differs because spurt implies a single vigorous discharge or a concentrated effort, while flurry emphasises many small, scattered motions.
