- 15th c. - Borrowed from Middle Dutch / Middle Low German into late Middle English as a noun (*proppe*, a wooden support/stake for vines, mines, or buildings) and verb (*proppen*, to support with a stake).
- 16th–17th c. - Broadened from agricultural and construction carpentry (vineyard stakes and roof timbers) to describe human physical postures: leaning against an object or supporting a sick, wounded, or intoxicated companion.
- 18th–19th c. - Extensively adopted in political and economic commentary to describe fragile institutions, corrupt ministries, or failing monarchies kept artificially in power by foreign subsidies ('propped up by foreign bayonets').
- 20th–21st c. - Pervasively used across theater/film (objects used on set), everyday domestic life (pillows propping someone up in bed, doors propped open with wedges), and global finance (bailouts propping up insolvent institutions).
