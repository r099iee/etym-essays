• gospel: Similar because both directly signify the "good news" of Christian salvation and by extension any doctrine accepted as absolute truth; differs because gospel is the native Old English compound (gōd-spell) that serves as the universal vernacular and ecclesiastical standard in English, whereas evangel is a direct Greco-Latin borrowing possessing a more elevated, solemn, or self-consciously literary register.
  • kerygma: Similar because both denote the core proclamation of religious truth; differs because kerygma is a technical theological term in New Testament scholarship specifically denoting the initial public heraldic announcement of Christ's resurrection to an uninitiated audience, whereas evangel encompasses the full content of the good news, written scriptures, and broader philosophical manifestos.
  • creed: Similar because both formulate foundational spiritual or intellectual convictions; differs because a creed (from Latin credo, "I believe") is a terse, codified confession of dogmatic articles designed for collective liturgical recitation and doctrinal policing, whereas an evangel is an expansive proclamation of good tidings and liberating salvation.
  • doctrine: Similar because both refer to a coherent set of teachings advocated by an authority; differs because doctrine emphasizes systematic, institutional instruction, analytical boundaries, and intellectual orthodoxy, whereas evangel inherently carries emotional fervor, joyful promise, and existential rescue.
  • manifesto: Similar because both represent passionate, world-transforming declarations intended to win converts and alter the course of human society; differs because a manifesto is firmly secular, political, and programmatic (outlining specific revolutionary actions and legal agendas), whereas an evangel operates primarily within spiritual, existential, or cosmic dimensions of redemption.
  • revelation: Similar because both involve the disclosure of transcendent divine truth; differs because revelation epistemologically designates the supernatural unveiling of hidden divine mysteries (from Greek apokalypsis), whereas evangel designates the joyful dissemination and public gladness of the message itself.
