The name is a compound of two Greek elements, each with its own Indo-European chain, so the derivation is given for each element separately and then fused. CHAIN 1 — the "wise" element: PIE *swop- "to taste, perceive" (traditional derivation; the root also of Latin sapiō "to taste; to know", whence sapiens "wise" and sapientia "wisdom") → Ancient Greek sophós (σοφός) "wise, skilled, clever" (with the family sophía "wisdom, skill", philosophía "love of wisdom", and sophistḗs "expert; sophist") → the first element of the compound Sophoklēs. Note on the debate: Beekes treats sophós as lacking a fully secure Indo-European etymology and admits the possibility of a Pre-Greek element; the *swop- connection with Latin sapiō remains the standard scholarly account but is not beyond doubt. CHAIN 2 — the "glory" element: PIE *ḱlew- "to hear" (the root of fame-as-what-is-heard; also in Greek kléos "glory", klýō "I hear", Latin clueō "am famous", Sanskrit śrávah "glory", śru- "hear", Old Norse hljóð "sound", English loud) → Proto-Hellenic *klewos "report, fame" → Ancient Greek kléos (κλέος) "fame, glory, report" (Homeric kléos áphthiton "imperishable fame") → the second element of the compound Sophoklēs. FUSION: Ancient Greek Sophoklēs (Σοφοκλῆς) "famous for wisdom, glory of wisdom", a standard Greek compound personal name borne by the tragedian (c. 496–406 BC) → Latin Sophoclēs "Sophocles" → English Sophocles.
