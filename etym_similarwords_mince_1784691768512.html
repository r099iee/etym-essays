• Chop: Similar because both cut food into pieces; differs because chopping is coarser, quicker, and produces larger uneven pieces, while mincing reduces matter to very small, fine particles.
  • Grind: Similar because both reduce food to small pieces; differs because grinding crushes and pulverises by machine or mortar, while mincing is the knife’s repeated, finer cutting.
  • Dice: Similar because both cut food into small uniform pieces; differs because dicing produces neat little cubes, while mincing is finer and less regular, and only mince carries the figurative senses of softened speech and affected walking.
  • Hash: Similar because both chop food small; differs because hash produces a rough, irregular mass and names a specific dish, while mince is finer and (in Britain) yields a noun for the meat itself.
  • Shred: Similar because both reduce food to small fragments; differs because shredding produces ribbons or strips (cabbage, cheese), while mincing produces particles.
  • Cut up: Similar because both divide food into smaller pieces; differs because cut up is general and casual, while mince specifies cutting very fine.
  • Soft-pedal / Water down (figurative): Similar because both mean to weaken the force of what one says, as in “not to mince words”; differs because these are the abstract idioms of the meaning, while mince keeps the image of cutting one’s speech into small, evasive pieces.
  • Affected / Dainty (of a gait): Similar because both describe the “mincing” walk; differs because these are adjectives describing the effect, while mincing is the verb’s participle naming the affected small steps themselves.
