Note: The origin is genuinely debated, as both the OED and Etymonline acknowledge: (a) Old English þrēa "threat, calamity", or (b) Old Norse þrá "longing, suffering" (a Scandinavian borrowing into northern English). Because Old Norse þrá itself descends from the same Proto-Germanic stem *þrawō as Old English þrēa, the two candidates may simply be two routes of the same Germanic word; the stem's exact sense-range ("threat" vs "longing") is what diverged. The -oe- spelling of the 16th–17th centuries was shaped by association with throw — the image of the body hurling itself about in agony.
