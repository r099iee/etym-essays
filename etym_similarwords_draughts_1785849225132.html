• Gulp: Similar because a gulp is a single large swallow of liquid, exactly the "one pull" that a draught of water is; differs because "gulp" stresses the rapid, greedy swallowing motion and is everyday, while "draught" names the measured amount drawn into the mouth and is elevated and literary.
  • Swallow: Similar because both denote taking liquid (or food) down the throat; differs because "swallow" is the neutral generic verb for any swallowing and can apply to food, while "draught" is a noun for one full drawing of liquid with no everyday neutrality.
  • Sip: Similar because a sip is an amount of liquid taken in, like a draught; differs because a sip is tiny, cautious, and restrained — the opposite of the large copious draught the card's sentence describes.
  • Swig: Similar because a swig is a large informal drink, often from a bottle; differs because "swig" is colloquial and implies quick, rough drinking, while "draught" is dignified and literary.
  • Mouthful: Similar because a mouthful is the amount taken at one go, like a draught; differs because "mouthful" is the physical capacity of the mouth, neutral and concrete, while "draught" implies the deliberate act of drawing the liquid in.
  • Dose: Similar because a medicinal draught is a liquid dose swallowed whole; differs because "dose" is the clinical, measured quantity of a drug, while "draught" (in pharmacy) names the liquid medicine itself as a single swallow.
  • Draft: Similar because it is the very same word under the American spelling; differs because American English uses "draft" for air, ships, beer, conscription, money orders, and preliminary writing, while British English keeps "draught" for liquids, air, ships, and the game.
  • Checkers: Similar because both name the same board game played with counters moved diagonally on a chequered board; differs because "draughts" is the British and international name (the pieces are drawn forward), while "checkers" is the American name, from the chequered board via Old French eschec, "check".
  • Current: Similar because a draught of air is a current of air moving through an enclosed space; differs because "current" is the general term for any moving air or water, while a "draught" is specifically the inward flow drawn through a gap or window.
  • Gust: Similar because a gust is a sudden movement of air, like a draught; differs because a gust is a short violent blast, usually outdoors, while a draught is the steady gentle inward current in a room.
  • Breeze: Similar because both can name a light current of air; differs because a breeze is a mild outdoor wind, pleasant and free, while a draught is the unwanted indoor current through a crack.
  • Pull: Similar because both mean the act of drawing something toward oneself — the root meaning of draught; differs because "pull" is the plain generic verb and noun, while "draught" preserves the "drawing" idea only in its specialized senses.
