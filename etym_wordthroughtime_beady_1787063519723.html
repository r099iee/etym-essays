- Beady first appears in the 16th century in literal senses: covered with beads or like beads — embroidery, drops, globules.
- For three centuries it stays near the literal: beaded cloth, bead-shaped drops of sweat or dew, round fruit.
- In the late 19th century beady eyes is coined for small, round, glittering, watchful eyes — and at once takes on the cunning, greedy, appraising character of the sharp-eyed observer.
- Through the 20th century the collocation solidifies: beady eyes is the phrase, and the literal covered-in-beads sense fades into craft and costume vocabulary.
- Today beady almost always means small, bright, and watchful — the greedy stare of the miser or the alert gaze of a hunting thrush.
