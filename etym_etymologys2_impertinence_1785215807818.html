Linear chain for IMPERTINENCE (compound of PIE *per- "forward, through" + *ten- "to stretch"; two root chains converge in Latin and continue as one): Chain for the element per- (PIE *per-): Stage 1 — PIE: *per- ("forward, through, beyond"), an adverbial/prepositional root widely attested in the family (cf. Latin per "through," Greek peri "around, about," Sanskrit prá "forward," English for/forth/fore). Stage 2 — Latin: per ("through, by means of"), the preposition and prefix built on *per-. Chain for the element ten- (PIE *ten-): Stage 1 — PIE: *ten- ("to stretch, extend"), an extremely wide root (cf. Greek teinō "to stretch," Latin tendere "to stretch," English thin, tend, tent, tenure). Stage 2 — Proto-Italic: *ten-e- ("to stretch, to hold") → Latin tenere ("to hold, keep") and tendere ("to stretch out"). Combined chain (from the convergent Latin stage): Stage 3 — Latin: pertinere ("to stretch out to, to extend to, to reach; to belong to, to concern"), a compound of per- + tenere, literally "to stretch through to," whence "to pertain, to belong to, to be relevant to." Stage 4 — Latin: pertinens, pertinens- (present participle of pertinere: "pertaining, relevant, belonging") — "that which reaches to the matter at hand." Stage 5 — Late Latin: impertinens ("not relevant, not proper, not belonging"), formed with the negative prefix in- (im-) + pertinens. Stage 6 — Late Latin: impertinentia ("irrelevance, inappropriateness, unsuitableness"), the abstract noun built on impertinens. Stage 7 — Old French: impertinence ("irrelevance, absurd unsuitability; insolence"), borrowed into English. Stage 8 — Middle English: impertinence (borrowed in the 14th century), at first meaning "logical irrelevance, unsuitableness"; by the 17th century shifted to "rudeness, impudence, improper intrusion." Stage 9 — Modern English: impertinence ("insolence, disrespect, rudeness; the quality of not being pertinent or proper") — IMPERTINENCE. Note on collateral forms: Latin pertinere also yielded English pertain, pertinence, pertinent; the parallel Latin adjective pertinax ("holding fast") and noun pertinacia ("stubbornness") are offshoots of the same stem; Romance descendants of pertinere include Spanish pertenecer, French appartenir, and Portuguese pertencer ("to belong"). The PIE *ten- root also underlies English tend, tense, extend, tent, tenet, tenure, contain, retain — all sharing the "stretch/hold" imagery.
