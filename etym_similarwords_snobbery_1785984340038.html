• Elitism: Similar because it involves a claim to superior status and the exclusion of others; differs because elitism can denote a structural or institutional system of privilege, while snobbery is usually a personal attitude or behaviour.
  • Arrogance: Similar in projecting superiority; differs because arrogance is a general conceit and overbearing self-importance, whereas snobbery is tied to specific social, intellectual, or taste-based hierarchies.
  • Pretension / pretentiousness: Similar in putting on airs and claiming unearned status; differs because pretension is about claiming to be better or more refined than one is, while snobbery is about looking down on others as inferior.
  • Condescension: Similar in treating others as inferior; differs because condescension is the patronizing manner itself, while snobbery is the whole attitude or outlook from which that manner flows.
  • Conceit: Similar in an over-high opinion of oneself; differs because conceit is vanity about one’s own merits, whereas snobbery is comparative — it needs others to look down on.
  • Snobbishness: Similar as the near-synonym from the same root; differs only in formation — snobbishness is the state of being snobbish, while snobbery is the behaviour, practice, or attitude of a snob.
  • Exclusiveness / exclusivity: Similar in keeping others out; differs because exclusiveness describes the fact of being exclusive, while snobbery supplies the motive and the disdain.
  • Haughtiness: Similar as proud superiority of manner; differs because haughtiness is a cold, arrogant bearing of the great, while snobbery is a socially anxious contempt for those judged beneath one.
  • Disdain / contempt: Similar in a feeling of scorn; differs because these name the emotion itself and can be directed at anything, while snobbery is a sustained social attitude built on perceived superiority.
