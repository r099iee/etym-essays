• queasiness: Similar because both name a nauseated, uneasy feeling of the stomach; differs because queasiness is a passing bodily sensation while squeamishness is a stable disposition or trait.
  • nausea: Similar because both involve stomach upset and revulsion; differs because nausea is the acute physiological symptom itself, whereas squeamishness is the tendency or temperament that produces it.
  • revulsion: Similar because both denote strong disgust and turning away; differs because revulsion is a momentary, intense recoil while squeamishness is a chronic, easily triggered sensibility.
  • repugnance: Similar because both express deep aversion; differs because repugnance can be purely intellectual or moral, while squeamishness is anchored in physical, bodily disgust.
  • disgust: Similar because both involve strong aversion to something offensive; differs because disgust is the emotion itself, felt by anyone, while squeamishness is an over-ready susceptibility to that emotion.
  • fastidiousness: Similar because both denote delicacy and being easily offended; differs because fastidiousness is about exacting standards of cleanliness, order, or taste, while squeamishness centers on visceral nausea and shock.
  • delicacy: Similar because both imply refinement and sensitivity; differs because delicacy is broader (physical fragility, tact, subtlety) and is usually positive, while squeamishness carries an overtone of excessive, often mockable sensitivity.
  • prudishness / prudery: Similar because both denote being easily scandalized or over-refined in feeling; differs because prudishness is specifically moral or sexual modesty, whereas squeamishness is chiefly about disgust and nausea rather than propriety.
  • timidity: Similar because both make a person shrink back from situations; differs because timidity is fear of danger or social judgement, while squeamishness is revulsion from unpleasantness, not fear.
  • scrupulousness: Similar because both involve an exaggerated sensitivity to something; differs because scrupulousness is a conscientious moral caution about wrongdoing, while squeamishness is a bodily and emotional aversion to grossness or shock.
