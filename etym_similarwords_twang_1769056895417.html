• thud: Similar in being an onomatopoeic sound-word; differs because a thud is dull, low, and heavy — the sound of impact, a body falling — while a twang is high, thin, and vibrating, the sound of a taut string released.
  • ping: Similar in being a short, ringing, echoic sound; differs because a ping is the metallic note of a small struck object, while a twang is the string-sound proper, with a slightly longer, more nasal ring and a quality of released tension.
  • snap: Similar in being a sharp, dry sound of tension; differs because a snap is the brittle sound of something breaking — a dry twig snapping — while a released bowstring does not break: it twangs, vibrating as the tension is released.
  • crack: Similar in being a sharp, sudden, percussive sound; differs because a crack is dry, hard, and usually of breaking or impact (a whip, a bone), while a twang is the ringing vibration of a taut string, softer and more resonant.
  • clang: Similar in being a resonant, ringing sound; differs because a clang is loud and born of struck metal, while a twang is softer in volume but sharper in pitch, born of tension rather than of impact.
  • bang: Similar in being a sudden, audible, explosive sound; differs because a bang is loud, flat, and heavy — an explosion or a slammed door — while a twang is thin, high, and vibrating, the note of a released string.
  • pluck: Similar in belonging to the same string-instrument world; differs because pluck is the action of pulling a string to set it vibrating, while twang is the resulting sound — the one causes the other.
  • strum: Similar in being a guitar-and-banjo word; differs because strumming is the brushing sweep across several strings at once, while a twang is the individual sharp ringing note of a single plucked or released string.
  • vibrato: Similar in describing a vibrating, resonant musical tone; differs because vibrato is a deliberate, controlled oscillation of pitch produced by a performer, while a twang is the natural, percussive ringing of a plucked string.
  • twinge: Similar in sharing the tw- sound-pattern of tension words; differs because a twinge is a sharp physical or emotional pang, a sensation not a sound, while a twang is an audible acoustic event.
  • ring: Similar in being a clear, resonant, vibrating sound; differs because ring is the general word for any clear tone (bells, voices), while twang is the particular sharp, nasal, string-born note with a plucked onset.
  • jangle: Similar in being a discordant, ringing, metallic sound; differs because jangling is harsh, confused, and jarring, while a twang is single, precise, and resonant, even when it is sharp.
