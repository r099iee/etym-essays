• Clergy: Similar because both name ordained ministers as a body; differs because clergy is the broader, neutral collective term opposed to the laity (from Greek klēros “lot, share”), while priesthood is narrower, more honorific, and carries sacramental and priestly dignity.
  • Ministry: Similar because both name religious office and service; differs because ministry stresses service and preaching and was the word favoured by Protestant and Puritan writers, while priesthood names a sacral order with sacrificial associations.
  • Priest: Similar because it shares the same root idea of the ordained man; differs because it is the singular individual, while priesthood names the office and the whole corporate body.
  • Holy orders: Similar because both refer to the ordained state; differs because holy orders is the specific rite or sacrament that confers the office, while priesthood is the resulting state or body.
  • Presbyterate: Similar because both name the body of priests (from Greek presbýteros); differs because presbyterate is the technical, chiefly Catholic term for the college of presbyters, while priesthood is the general English word.
  • Sacerdoce / sacerdotalism: Similar because both relate to the priestly office (Latin sacerdos “priest”); differs because they are learned, technical terms for the priestly system or priestly power, not everyday words for the body of clergy.
