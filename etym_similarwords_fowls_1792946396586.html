• Poultry: Similar because both denote domestic birds kept for eggs and meat; differs because poultry is the collective commercial term of farm and shop, while fowls is countable, literary, and redolent of the yard.
  • Chickens: Similar because chickens are the archetypal fowls of every farmyard; differs because chickens names the single species precisely, while fowls can sweep in ducks, geese, and turkeys too.
  • Hens: Similar because laying hens are the working core of any flock of fowls; differs because hens are specifically the females, while fowls covers cocks, capons, and chicks as well.
  • Birds: Similar because fowls are birds, and fowl once meant exactly this; differs because birds is the modern general term for everything feathered, while fowls has narrowed to the domestic and edible.
  • Game: Similar because both describe birds valued as food and sport; differs because game means wild birds under hunting law, while farmyard fowls are owned, fed, and housed.
  • Wildfowl: Similar because the compound preserves the old broad sense of fowl as bird; differs because wildfowl is strictly undomesticated marsh and estuary birds, the opposite pole from the coop.
  • Waterfowl: Similar because ducks and geese count both as fowls and as waterfowl; differs because waterfowl is defined by swimming habitat, while fowls is defined by farmyard life and the table.
  • Bantams: Similar because bantams are miniature fowls scratching in the same yards; differs because bantams names only the tiny ornamental breeds, while fowls embraces the full-sized flock.
  • Pullets: Similar because young laying hens are the pride of any keeper of fowls; differs because pullets are specifically young females not yet in full lay, a trade term of the poultry-keeper.
