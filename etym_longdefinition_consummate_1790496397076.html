consummate in the source sentence — At last my mother, formed into a consummate housewife, was sent for to Scotland to take care of my paternal grandfather's house — is the adjective pronounced /ˈkɒnsəmət/, meaning perfect, fully accomplished, complete in every household art: baking, mending, ordering, nursing, accounting, managing servants and cellars. Ruskin stresses formation (formed into): his mother was not born a housewife but disciplined into one by evangelical schooling, Croydon training, and conscientious practice until no seam, sauce, or account showed a flaw. The praise is absolute and slightly formidable — a consummate housewife leaves no room for disorder or for ease.

More generally consummate as an adjective always marks the top of a scale: a consummate artist, craftsman, liar, villain, politician, or lover is one in whom the relevant quality is total, finished, without gap. As a verb pronounced /ˈkɒnsəmeɪt/, to consummate means to complete, to bring to fulfilment — to consummate a marriage (make it complete by first intercourse), to consummate a deal, an alliance, a sacrifice. Adjective and verb are one word split by stress and vowel: the finished state and the finishing act. The noun consummation names both the completion and, by biblical echo, the end of the world (the consummation of all things).

  • Core adjectival sense (source sense): supremely skilled, perfect of its kind: having every requirement of excellence; e.g. a consummate housewife, consummate craftsmanship, a consummate performance of Lear, she is a consummate professional. Implies long training fully internalised: nothing laboured, nothing missing. Often collocates with roles — host, teacher, storyteller, connoisseur — where finish can be judged.
  • Moral-neutral intensifier, good or bad: complete to the utmost degree: a consummate villain, consummate folly, consummate boredom, a consummate liar. Here consummate merely perfects whatever it touches: blackguards can be as consummate as saints. The bridge is completeness without moral vote — the sum reached, whether of virtue or vice.
  • Verb sense (1): to complete, perfect, bring to fulfilment: to consummate a project, to consummate an agreement, to consummate a reform, their ambition was consummated by promotion. Formal and final: consummating is more solemn than finishing, implying ratification, sealing, fulfilment of promise. Business and diplomacy love it for exactly that gravity.
  • Verb sense (2), legal-matrimonial: to make a marriage complete by sexual union: a consummated marriage, non-consummation as grounds for annulment. Canon and civil law long held that consent makes a marriage, consummation seals it; failure to consummate could void it. The euphemism is ancient, technical, and still pleaded. Its gravity explains why the verb retains solemnity even in commerce.
  • Verb sense (3), sacrificial-religious: to perfect by offering or doom: older theology speaks of Christ consummating sacrifice, martyrs consummating testimony by death. The bridge is sum-plus-offering: what is heaped to the top is poured out. Now rare except in liturgy and literary prose.
  • Noun consummation: completion, climax, fulfilment: the consummation of their hopes, a consummation devoutly to be wished (Hamlet's phrase for death as fulfilment), the consummation of the ages. Shakespeare's borrowing fixed the word's high style: consummation is never trivial finishing but crowned ending.
  • Pronunciation and spelling trap: adjective and noun consummate /-mət/ with short, reduced vowel versus verb consummate /-meɪt/ with full diphthong — the same pattern as separate, estimate, intimate. Mis-stressing marks the speaker instantly. Spelling doubles the m (con- + summa) where consume (eat up, from a different root) has one: consummate completes, consume devours.

Register is consistently high-formal. consummate belongs to references, reviews, judgments, and obituaries rather than to chat: one writes consummate skill, one says really brilliant. Applied to women of Ruskin's generation — consummate housewife, consummate needlewoman, consummate hostess — it honours domestic mastery as seriously as connoisseurship, while quietly confessing that such mastery was compulsory: girls were formed into it as boys were formed into Latin. The irony available to modern readers was already available to Ruskin's aunt, who refused formation.

Every sense obeys the buried metaphor of summing: heap grain until the measure overflows, add figures until the total is reached, train a girl until no art is wanting. The consummate thing is the heaped measure — pressed down, shaken together, running over. Whether housewife, villainy, marriage, or symphony, nothing can be added because everything due is already in.
