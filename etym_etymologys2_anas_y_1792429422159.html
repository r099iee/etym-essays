Note on morphology and philological analysis: The formation of Anasūyā is a classical Sanskrit privative bahuvrīhi compound. The base noun asūyā ("grumbling, detraction, envy") is an abstract feminine substantive derived from the denominative verbal root asūy- ("to be indignant, grumble, find fault"), which comparative linguists (such as Manfred Mayrhofer) connect to archaic Indo-Iranian roots expressive of vocal resentment or bitterness. Before the vowel a-, the Proto-Indo-European privative syllabic nasal *n̥- takes its vocalized sandhi form an-, yielding An-asūyā, literally "one who possesses no jealousy or bitterness." In Sanskrit philosophical texts (such as the Bhagavad Gītā), the masculine dative form anasūyave ("to the ungrudging one") highlights freedom from envy as the premier ethical virtue.
