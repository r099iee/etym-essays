Single root word: dais. 1. PIE *deyḱ- 'to point out, show, indicate' (c. 4500–3500 BCE; zero grade *dik-) → Ancient Greek δικεῖν dikeîn 'to throw, hurl' (Homeric, 8th c. BCE; to throw is to aim at a point you have shown) → Greek δίσκος dískos 'discus, quoit' (lit. 'that which is thrown'; attested 5th c. BCE) → Latin discus 'a quoit; a round plate or dish' (borrowed 1st c. BCE) → Medieval Latin discus 'table-board, table' (c. 10th–12th c. CE; the round plate becomes the board on which plates stand) → Old French deis 'high table; raised platform' (12th c.) → Anglo-Norman deis → Middle English deis 'high table; platform of honour' (13th c.) → Modern English dais /deɪs/ 'a low platform for seats of honour.' NOTE — each transfer is a metonymy: thrown disc → plate (by shape), plate → table (by containment), table → platform (by contiguity). The French sibling drifted independently: modern French 'dais' means the canopy over a throne or altar, not the platform.
