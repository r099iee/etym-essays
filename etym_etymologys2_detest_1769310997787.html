PIE *tréyes / zero-grade *tri- “three” (c. 4000–3500 BCE; Sanskrit tráyas, Greek treîs, Latin trēs, Old English þrīe) → Latin testis “witness, one who stands by as a third party” (3rd c. BCE; conventionally analysed as *tris-sto-, “one who stands as the third” at a transaction) → Latin testārī “to bear witness, give evidence” (1st c. BCE; denominative verb from testis) → Latin dētestārī “to denounce, to curse by calling the gods or the law to witness” (1st c. BCE; dē- “down from, against” + testārī; attested in Cicero) → Old French detester “to detest, abhor” (12th c.) → Middle English / English detest (1530; borrowed from Middle French) → detest ◄ TARGET.

Note on the debated stage: the derivation of Latin testis from *tris-sto- (“third-stander”) is the standard reconstruction but is debated; some scholars posit a different second element. The core sense “witness” is, however, secure.
