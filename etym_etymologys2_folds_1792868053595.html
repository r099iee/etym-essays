Verb strand — to fold, the crease:
PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*pel-
‘to fold’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*falþaną
‘to fold, bend’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
fealdan, faldan
‘to fold, wrap up’
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
folden
‘to fold; enclose’
↓
Modern English (plural noun)
folds
‘creases; hollows’

Noun strand — fold, the pen:
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*falþaz, *faludaz
‘fold, enclosure’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
falod, fald
‘sheep-pen’
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
fold
‘pen; flock’

Note: the two strands — verb *falþaną and noun *faludaz — share the same root for bending but entered Old English as distinct words; their merger in spelling is complete, though scholars debate details of the noun's suffix, and some handbooks connect the root further to PIE *plek-, to plait, as a debated wider kinship.
