- 1200s–1400s: Agricultural reality - livestock manure, stable bedding, and barnyard waste, vital for fertilizing arable fields across medieval England.
- 1500s–1600s: Moral and religious allegory - worldly vanity, filthy lucre, and corrupt physical desires, immortalized in Bunyan's "Man with the Muck-rake."
- 1700s–1850s: Industrial prosperity and mining - associated with Northern manufacturing grit ("muck and brass") and technical excavated spoil in railway tunnels.
- 1900s–1950s: Military camaraderie and colloquial idioms - emergence of "muck in" (share hardship), "muck up" (bungle), and widespread culinary disdain for unpalatable dishes.
- 1960s–present: Everyday British vernacular and poker jargon - casual term for bad food, messy dilemmas, rainy weather, and folded poker cards.
