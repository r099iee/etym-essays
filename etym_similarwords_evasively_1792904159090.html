• Equivocally: Similar because both withhold a clear yes or no; differs because equivocally stresses double meaning, words open to two readings, while evasively stresses the dodge itself, slipping past the question.
  • Ambiguously: Similar because both leave the hearer unsure what was meant; differs because ambiguously can be accidental or poetic, while evasively is deliberately self-protective.
  • Elusively: Similar because both refuse to be pinned down; differs because elusively is graceful and often admiring, hard to catch like light, while evasively is shifty and mildly blamed.
  • Noncommittally: Similar because both avoid promising or deciding; differs because noncommittally is neutral and bureaucratic, while evasively suggests active wriggling away from pressure.
  • Guardedly: Similar because both speak with care to reveal little; differs because guardedly can be wise caution, while evasively hints the speaker owes the truth and is withholding it.
  • Obliquely: Similar because both approach the point sideways rather than head-on; differs because obliquely is a stylistic angle, often witty, while evasively is a moral angle, avoiding the blow.
  • Vaguely: Similar because both starve the question of detail; differs because vaguely is misty and may be mere ignorance, while evasively knows the detail and hides it.
  • Shiftily: Similar because both signal untrustworthy movement of eyes or words; differs because shiftily convicts the character as sly, while evasively can still pity him, as with Frodo guarding a secret.
