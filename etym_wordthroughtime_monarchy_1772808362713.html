- Classical and medieval political term - Since the 14th century monarchy has named rule by a single person, a neutral constitutional type inherited from Greek political thought and used of kings and emperors alike.
- Early Modern absolutism debates - In the 16th-18th centuries the word becomes charged by the struggle over divine right and royal power, standing for absolute rule and at times shading into the rhetoric of tyranny.
- Constitutional transformation - From the 18th-19th centuries monarchy is increasingly qualified — constitutional, hereditary, parliamentary — so that the word names a contained institution rather than unlimited personal rule.
- Modern analytical use - Today monarchy is a formal, analytical term for one-ruler government, quietly descriptive in historiography (Gibbon's 'extensive monarchy') and, in polemic, a fighting word for outdated power.
