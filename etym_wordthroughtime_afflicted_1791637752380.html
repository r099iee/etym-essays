- Late 14th to 15th Century (Middle English): Physical overthrow and humiliation - Derived from Latin affligere, connoted being physically cast down, humbled, crushed, or beaten to the earth by an adversary or divine judgment.
- 16th to 17th Century: Religious martyrdom, penitence, and sovereign trial - Central to devotional literature (Book of Common Prayer, King James Bible) as spiritual chastisement or pious endurance of bodily suffering ("an afflicted soul").
- 18th to 19th Century: Clinical medicine and socio-economic distress - Shifted toward describing populations ravaged by endemic disease, poverty, or chronic physical infirmity (the blind, the lame, the afflicted poor).
- 20th to 21st Century: Metaphorical and technical defect description - Extended beyond human beings to describe systems, institutions, theories, or perceptual figures troubled by an intrinsic flaw, bug, or imperfection.
