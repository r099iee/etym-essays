• Suitor: Similar because both denote a man actively courting a woman with romantic intentions; differs because suitor is more formal, legalistic, and goal-oriented (specifically aiming toward matrimonial proposal), whereas beau focuses on the personal romantic attachment, aesthetic charm, and social companionship of a sweetheart.
  • Dandy: Similar because both refer historically to a man obsessed with fashionable dress, refinement, and elegance; differs because dandy often carries a satirical or critical edge implying superficial affectation, effeminacy, and narcissistic vanity, whereas beau originally emphasized social success, gallantry, and pleasing manners.
  • Admirer: Similar because both describe a man who holds a person in high romantic or aesthetic regard; differs because admirer can be distant, unrequited, or anonymous (e.g., a secret admirer), whereas a beau is an acknowledged, active companion who participates in reciprocal courtship.
  • Gallant: Similar because both denote a charming, attentive, and polite gentleman who courts female favor; differs because gallant emphasizes heroic chivalry, courage, and dashing courtesy, whereas beau emphasizes sartorial elegance, romantic affection, or (in idiomatic usage) a gratifying role.
  • Fop: Similar because both historically categorized fashion-conscious men in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries; differs because fop is overtly contemptuous, describing a foolish, silly, and pretentious man whose obsession with clothes makes him a laughingstock, whereas a beau (like Beau Brummell) commanded immense respect and social authority.
  • Partner: Similar because both describe a romantic companion; differs because partner is a modern, gender-neutral, and egalitarian term signifying long-term commitment, shared domestic life, or cohabitation, whereas beau is distinctly male, nostalgic, and evocative of traditional romantic courtship.
