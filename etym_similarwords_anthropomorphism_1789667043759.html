• Personification: Similar because it gives human qualities to non-humans; differs because it is an owned rhetorical figure for abstractions, while anthropomorphism often presents human traits as literal fact.
  • Prosopopoeia: Similar because it lends voice and face to the absent or inanimate; differs because it is the formal classical term for the device, while anthropomorphism is the broader habit and doctrine.
  • Pathetic fallacy: Similar because it attributes feeling to nature; differs because Ruskin's term ties the error to the poet's mood under stress, while anthropomorphism covers any human projection.
  • Animism: Similar because it treats natural things as ensouled; differs because it is a religious ontology of spirits, while anthropomorphism need imply no spirit, only human likeness.
  • Anthropopathism: Similar because it ascribes human feelings to God; differs because it is restricted to emotions and theology, while anthropomorphism covers form, body and mind everywhere.
  • Anthropocentrism: Similar because it centres the human; differs because it is valuing humans above others, while anthropomorphism is picturing others as human.
  • Zoomorphism: Similar because it crosses the human-animal boundary in representation; differs because it gives animal form to humans or gods, the mirror image of anthropomorphism.
  • Reification: Similar because it confuses categories of being; differs because it makes abstractions into things, while anthropomorphism makes things into persons.
  • Sentimentalism: Similar because it captures the accusatory tone of the charge; differs because it is indulgent feeling in general, while anthropomorphism is specifically human-shaped feeling about non-humans.
