- 11th–12th c. - Founded in Brittany by Alain I de Rohan (c. 1084–1147), son of Viscount Eudon I of Porhoët, who built the fortress of Rohan on the rocky banks of the Oust River; held as an ancient feudal viscounty deriving from the sovereign Breton kings.
- 14th–15th c. - Rose to immense regional and national power during the Breton War of Succession, intermarrying with the sovereign ducal House of Montfort-Brittany and the French royal Capetian dynasty.
- 16th c. - Elevated to the rank of Duke-Peers (Duchy of Montbazon and Duchy of Rohan) and recognized by the French crown as princes étrangers (foreign princes), granting them precedence over ordinary French dukes and peers at the royal court.
- 17th–18th c. (Ancien Régime Zenith) - Proliferated into prestigious princely branches (Rohan-Soubise, Rohan-Guéméné, Rohan-Rochefort, Rohan-Chabot); occupied the fabulously wealthy Prince-Bishopric of Strasbourg for four consecutive generations; involved in the infamous Affair of the Diamond Necklace (Cardinal de Rohan, 1785).
- 1789–1815 (Revolutionary & Napoleonic Eras) - Stripped of their French feudal domains; established sovereign-recognized princely branches in the Austrian Empire (Princes of Rohan in Bohemia/Austria); scions featured prominently in the royalist émigré diaspora across Europe.
- 19th c.–Present - Preserved in French, Russian, and English literature and historiography (e.g., Tolstoy's War and Peace, Alexandre Dumas, Saint-Simon's memoirs) as the quintessential symbol of unyielding aristocratic pride, sovereign lineage, and high court grandeur.
