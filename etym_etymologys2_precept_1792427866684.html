Note on historical derivation: English precept derives through Old French precepte from Latin praeceptum, the substantivized neuter past participle of praecipere ("to take beforehand, anticipate, advise, admonish, instruct"). This verb is a compound of the prefix prae- ("before, in front", from PIE *per-) and the verb capere ("to take, seize", from PIE *kap-, "to grasp"). The semantic evolution proceeded from the concrete physical action of "taking beforehand" to mental anticipation, then to imparting advance advice or instructions so that another might avoid error, and finally to a codified rule of conduct.
