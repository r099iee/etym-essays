• Contempt: Similar because both express the judgment that someone or something is worthless or despicable; differs because contempt is the cold, considered intellectual verdict, while scorn adds a hot, mocking, derisive edge — it laughs at what it despises.
  • Disdain: Similar because both convey a haughty looking-down upon someone as beneath notice; differs because disdain is aristocratic and aloof — the sniff of the noble — while scorn is more active and cruel, the public mockery of the crowd.
  • Derision: Similar because both involve ridicule directed at someone; differs because derision is the act of laughing at someone itself, the mockery, while scorn names the underlying contemptuous attitude that produces the mockery.
  • Mockery: Similar because both involve treating someone as absurd or ridiculous; differs because mockery is the specific act of imitation or jeering designed to make a person look foolish, while scorn is the broader contemptuous attitude that may or may not express itself as mockery.
  • Ridicule: Similar because both expose someone to laughter and loss of face; differs because ridicule stresses the act of making someone the object of fun and is lighter, while scorn carries a moral judgment of worthlessness.
  • Disrespect: Similar because both fail to hold someone in proper esteem; differs because disrespect is the broad, mild, modern word for rudeness or slight, with none of the burning contempt and ridicule of scorn.
  • Contumely: Similar because both are insolent, contemptuous language or treatment; differs because contumely is a rare, literary, Latinate word for abusive contempt in speech, while scorn covers both attitude and act and is far more common.
  • Slight: Similar because both treat someone as of little account; differs because a slight is a small, often silent snub or omission, while scorn is overt, emphatic, and frequently vocal.
  • Despise: Similar because both regard someone as worthless and base; differs because despise is the verb for the internal feeling of low regard, while scorn adds the public, expressive mockery and can also be a noun.
  • Spurn: Similar because both reject with contemptuous refusal; differs because spurn specifically means to reject or refuse something offered (often literally to kick away), while scorn is the attitude and derision of which spurning is one possible act.
