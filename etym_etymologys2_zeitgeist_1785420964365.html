CHAIN 1 — the element "Zeit" ("time"): PIE *dā- / *dī- "to divide, cut up" (reconstructed; the notion of time as marked-off portions of the day, season, and hour — the same base is traditionally linked to Latin diēs "day"; the reconstruction is standard but the root's exact vocalism is debated) → Proto-Germanic *tīdi- / *tīdiz "time, season, hour" (a derived noun meaning "division of time"; reconstructed) → Old High German zīt "time, season, period" (attested 8th–9th c.) → German Zeit "time" (modern) — first element of the compound → (compound stage below) Note on cognates: the English words tide, "season, time" (as in Yuletide, eventide; narrowed to the sea's ebb and flow from the 16th c.), and time itself descend from the same Germanic base. CHAIN 2 — the element "Geist" ("spirit"): PIE *gʰeys- "to be frightened, startled, excited" (reconstructed; the "spirit" as the startling, awe-inspiring thing — cf. Gothic usgaisjan "to terrify"; the reconstruction is traditional, its details debated) → Proto-Germanic *gaistaz "spirit, ghost; breath; a startling being" (reconstructed) → Old High German geist "spirit, mind, intellect" (attested 8th–9th c.; the Christian and philosophical senses already present) → German Geist "spirit, mind, wit, genius" (modern) — second element of the compound Note on cognates: the English word ghost (Old English gāst "spirit, soul") kept the older, supernatural sense. COMPOUND — Zeit + Geist: German Zeitgeist "the spirit of the age" (coined by Johann Gottfried Herder, 1769, in his Kritische Wälder; later systematized by Hegel and the historical school) → Borrowed into English mid-19th century (first recorded 1848; naturalized in critical and historical writing, at first italicized) → ZEITGEIST (final node; the bold target rootWord)
