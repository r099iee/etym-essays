• stuff: Similar because both mean forcing matter into a space until it is full; differs because "stuff" is broader (cushions, turkeys, ballot boxes) and calmer, with no last-minute urgency.
  • pack: Similar because both mean filling a container; differs because "pack" implies orderly arrangement and luggage, while "cram" implies disorderly force.
  • jam: Similar because both mean forcing into a tight space; differs because "jam" stresses the stuck, blocked result (and is a food homonym).
  • wedge: Similar because both mean forcing something in; differs because "wedge" implies a single object into a narrow gap, with tool-like precision.
  • ram: Similar because both apply pressing force; differs because "ram" is violent and mechanical (ramming gates or pistons).
  • crowd: Similar because both mean filling a space with people; differs because "crowd" is gentle and neutral about pressure while "cram" is bluntly physical.
  • squeeze: Similar because both involve compression; differs because "squeeze" is more controlled and can be polite ("squeeze past").
  • gorge: Similar because both mean eating greedily to excess; differs because "gorge" is reflexive ("gorge oneself") and more visceral.
  • gobble: Similar because both describe fast, greedy eating; differs because "gobble" emphasizes speed and sound, not volume or stuffing.
  • swot: Similar because both mean studying hard (British slang); differs because "swot" implies sustained diligent revision and is also a noun for the studious person.
  • grind: Similar because both mean intensive study (American slang); differs because "grind" is long-suffering drudgery, not a last-minute burst.
  • mug up (on): Similar because both mean hasty exam preparation; differs because "mug up" is a British idiom, wider in form and rarer today.
  • hardtack / pemmican: Similar because both are real dense, long-lasting travel rations like Tolkien's cram; differs because they are real-world foods with precise recipes, not fantasy.
  • lembas / waybread: Similar because both are Tolkien's concentrated travel-foods; differs because lembas is the elves' sanctified, delicate bread — cram is its rough, comic dwarf-cousin.
