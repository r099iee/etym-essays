• Depth: Similar because it is the plain master term for how far down thought or feeling reaches; differs because depth is neutral and everyday, while profundity is formal and laudatory, reserved for depth that impresses.
  • Deepness: Similar because it literally means the quality of being deep; differs because deepness is rare and physical (deepness of water or colour), while profundity is intellectual and abstract.
  • Gravity: Similar because both confer weight and seriousness on thought or character; differs because gravity stresses solemn dignity and consequence, while profundity stresses penetration to what lies beneath.
  • Weightiness: Similar because both describe ideas that press on the mind and demand attention; differs because weightiness stresses heaviness of consequence, while profundity stresses fathomlessness of meaning.
  • Erudition: Similar because both are high praise for a furnished mind; differs because erudition means breadth of acquired learning, while profundity means depth of insight, and the two do not always coincide.
  • Insight: Similar because both name the power of seeing into things; differs because insight is a piercing particular perception, while profundity is the abiding capacity from which such perceptions flow.
  • Wisdom: Similar because both crown a lifetime of understanding; differs because wisdom stresses sound judgment in life, while profundity stresses reach of thought, which may be unworldly.
  • Abstruseness: Similar because both describe thought that is hard to fathom; differs because abstruseness is a complaint about difficulty, while profundity is usually praise for depth worth the difficulty.
  • Intensity: Similar because profound feeling and intense feeling are both deeply felt; differs because intensity stresses force and strength, while profundity stresses depth and fathomlessness.
