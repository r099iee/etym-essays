- c. 1300s-1400s (Middle English): Terrour denoted intense fright, supernatural dread, or the terrifying power of God and demons - Heavily theological and apocalyptic register.
- c. 1500s-1700s (Early Modern English): Flourished in dramatic literature (Shakespeare, Milton) and 18th-century aesthetics (Burke) as the highest emotion of the Sublime - Spelled terrour in Johnsonian standards.
- c. 1790s (French Revolution): The Reign of Terror (la Terreur) institutionalized the word as a formal instrument of political state violence and suppression.
- c. 1800s-Present: Spelling simplified to terror; spawned terrorism and terrorist in political discourse, while retaining its psychological sense of extreme panic and nightmare.
