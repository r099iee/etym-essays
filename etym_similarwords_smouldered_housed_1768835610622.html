• Burned (for smouldered): Similar in being combustion; differs because burned is the colourless generic term for any burning, while smouldered names slow, flameless, smoky burning — destruction without spectacle.
  • Blazed: Similar in being fire; differs because blazed is bright, flaring and rapid, while smouldered is choked, stifled, and kept below the level of flame.
  • Charred (for smouldered): Similar in being fire-damaged; differs because charred names the blackened result rather than the process of slow burning, whereas smouldered names the process itself.
  • Incinerated: Similar in being destroyed by fire; differs because incinerated is clinical, complete and instantaneous, while smouldered is slow, incomplete and smoky.
  • Simmered (for smouldered, figurative): Similar in being heat kept below the point of eruption; differs because simmered is a cooking metaphor for near-boiling emotion, while smouldered is a fire metaphor for heat below flame.
  • Seethed (figurative): Similar in describing suppressed agitation; differs because seethed implies boiling, restless turbulence within, while smouldered implies slow, contained, potentially explosive burning.
  • Rankled (figurative): Similar in describing inwardly burning resentment; differs because rankled specifically means to cause persistent irritation, while smouldered suggests deeper, more dangerous suppressed passion.
  • Festered (figurative): Similar in describing something decaying beneath the surface; differs because festered is a wound-image of suppuration, while smouldered is a fire-image of embers waiting to catch.
  • Stored (for housed): Similar in keeping something in a place; differs because stored is purely functional, with no suggestion of care or belonging, whereas housed gives something a permanent protective home.
  • Sheltered: Similar in giving protection; differs because sheltered emphasises protection against danger, while housed emphasises dwelling, permanence and honour — a residence, not a refuge.
  • Kept: Similar in retaining something in one's care; differs because kept is colourless and may imply mere possession, while housed implies accommodation within a protective structure.
  • Lodged: Similar in providing a place of residence; differs because lodged implies temporary or arranged quarters, while housed implies a settled, permanent home.
  • Accommodated: Similar in providing a place to stay; differs because accommodated is administrative and neutral about the quality of shelter, while housed carries warmth and belonging.
  • Harboured: Similar in giving shelter; differs because harboured suggests giving refuge to fugitives or (figuratively) nourishing feelings within, while housed is a straightforward giving of a home.
  • Contained (technical): Similar in enclosing within a structure; differs because contained is neutral about care or protection and is the cold engineering word, while housed implies a protective home.
