[Root 1: Wing]
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*peth₁-
"to fly, spread wings, rush"
↓
Proto-Hellenic (c. 2000 BCE)
*ptéron
"wing, feather"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE)
πτερόν (pteron)
"wing, feather, flight fin"

[Root 2: Finger]
Pre-Greek Substrate / Disputed Base (c. 3000–2000 BCE)
*dak- (?)
"finger, pointing digit"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE)
δάκτυλος (daktylos)
"finger, toe; date palm fruit"

[Compound & Modern Transmission]
French (Scientific Coinage) (1809)
ptéro-dactyle
"wing-fingered flying reptile"
↓
Neo-Latin (1812)
Pterodactylus
"genus of extinct winged archosaurs"
↓
English (c. 1826)
pterodactyl
"extinct flying reptile; winged pterosaur"

Note: Coined by Georges Cuvier from Greek pteron and daktylos because the flight membrane is uniquely supported by a hyper-elongated fourth digit. Greek daktylos has no secure Indo-European cognates and is categorized by Beekes as a Pre-Greek substrate loanword.
