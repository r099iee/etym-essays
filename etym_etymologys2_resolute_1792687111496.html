Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed)
*lewH-
"to loosen, untie, release, cut off"
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*luwō / *se-luwō
"to untie, release, dissolve"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
solvere
"to untie, loosen, release, dissolve, pay"
(compound of se- 'apart' + luere 'to loosen')
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
resolvere
"to unbind, loosen back, dissolve, melt, explain"
(re- 'back/again' + solvere 'to loosen')
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
resolūtus
"loosened, relaxed, dissolved; decided"
(perfect passive participle of resolvere)
↓
Middle French (14th c.)
resolut
"dissolved, analyzed; firm, determined"
↓
Late Middle English (early 15th c.)
resolut
"cleared of doubt, decided, firm"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–present)
resolute
"firm, determined, unwavering in purpose"
