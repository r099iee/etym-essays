• Gasps: Similar because both are strained-breath verbs; differs because gasps mark sudden, sharp intakes from shock or air hunger, while wheezes name the continuous or recurrent whistling of air through narrowed airways, not a single gulp.
  • Pants: Similar because both follow exertion; differs because pants are rapid shallow breaths without a required whistle, while wheezes highlight obstruction noise more than rate alone.
  • Rattles: Similar because both can signal unhealthy breathing; differs because rattles suggest coarse, often moist vibration in the throat or chest, while wheezes are typically higher, more musical, and linked to bronchoconstriction.
  • Hisses: Similar because both share air-through-a-gap acoustics and Germanic expressive flavor; differs because hisses are often sharp, deliberate, or snake- and steam-like, while wheezes specifically index labored respiration or analogous machine sounds, plus a British "scheme/gag" noun sense that hiss lacks.
  • Whistles: Similar because both describe a sound produced by air forced through a narrow opening; differs because whistles can be voluntary or external and tuneful, while a wheeze is involuntary, pathological or labored, and not shaped into a pitch.
  • Gags / schemes (informal noun parallel): Similar because in British slang wheezes are old gags, tricks, or clever plans; differs because ordinary gags are simply jokes, while wheezes in this register imply theatrical stock tricks or slightly labored cleverness, and schemes are practical plans rather than comic material.
  • Rales / crackles (clinical): Similar because both are abnormal breath sounds heard on auscultation; differs because rales/crackles are discontinuous, moist, or coarse (fluid in the lungs), while a wheeze is continuous, musical, and due to airway narrowing.
