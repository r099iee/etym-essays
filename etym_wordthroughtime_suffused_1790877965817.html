- 1580s - Renaissance medical & literary adoption: Borrowed from Latin *suffusus* to describe blood, humors, or liquids rising beneath the skin.
- 1600s–1700s - Poetic & natural descriptive expansion: Widely adopted in English poetry to describe the spreading glow of dawn, sunset, and twilight.
- 1800s - Romantic & Victorian psychological prose: Applied extensively to interior emotional states—characters suffused with shame, passion, or melancholy.
- 20th–Present - Cinematic & literary criticism: Retained as an elevated literary term for atmospheres, lighting, and visceral acting performances permeated with intense emotional currents.
