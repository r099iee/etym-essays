Note on philological debate and construction: In J.R.R. Tolkien's early linguistic writings (such as The Etymologies in The Lost Road and Other Writings, c. 1937–1938), the base ERE- is explicitly glossed as "be alone, deprived," yielding Quenya erya ("single, sole") and Eressëa ("the Lonely Isle"), and Sindarin elements meaning "lonely." While some early commentators noted a potential phonetic resonance with Sindarin eryd ("mountains", plural of orod), Tolkien's mature translation of Eriador as the "Lone-land" definitively confirms the derivation from the root of solitude (ERE-) compounded with the universal Eldarin territorial suffix -dôr (from *NDOR-, seen in Gondor "Stone-land", Mordor "Black Land", and Doriath "Land of the Fence").
