Old English (9th c.)
swaðu
‘track; mown strip, swath’
↓
Middle English (13th–14th c.)
swathe
‘broad band, strip of cloth’
↓
Middle English verb (14th c.)
swathen
‘to wrap in bands’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
swathe
‘to bind, envelop’
↓
Modern English (past participle)
swathed
‘wrapped round in folds’

Note: the deeper pre-Old English ancestry is debated; handbooks connect swaðu to Germanic words for swath and sweeping, but no certain PIE root is established, so the chain begins with the attested Old English noun.
