• Dale: Similar because both are Germanic words for a valley and are etymological siblings (Old English dell and dæl). Differs because a dale is broader, more open, and more everyday (in northern England it is the normal word for a valley), while dell is smaller, more enclosed, and now literary.
  • Valley: Similar because both name low land between hills. Differs because valley is the general, neutral, modern term of any size; dell is a small, sheltered, poetic hollow.
  • Glen: Similar because both name a secluded valley. Differs because glen is a narrow, often steep-sided Scottish valley, usually with a stream, and is longer than it is round; a dell is rounder, smaller and gentler.
  • Vale: Similar because both are poetic words for valley land. Differs because vale is broad and open (a vale of tears, a wide vale) and archaic-poetic in a grander, more solemn way; dell is small, green and intimate.
  • Hollow: Similar because both name a depression in the ground. Differs because hollow is neutral, common, and may be bare or muddy; dell implies a wooded, sheltered, pleasant small valley.
  • Dingle: Similar because both name a deep, shady, wooded hollow. Differs because dingle is rarer, more dialectal, and suggests a longer narrow cut; dell is the better-known literary word.
  • Coomb / Combe: Similar because both name a short, sheltered valley or hollow, especially in southern England. Differs because combe is a distinct regional word with its own Celtic-derived history; dell is the general literary term.
  • Dip: Similar because both name a downward slope of ground. Differs because dip is small, everyday and unromantic — a dip in the road — while dell is a named, sheltered little valley.
  • Basin: Similar because both name a bowl-shaped depression. Differs because basin is a technical geographic term (a river basin) with no pastoral colour.
  • Swale: Similar because both name low-lying ground. Differs because a swale is a moist, marshy hollow or low meadow; a dell is dry, firm and often rocky or wooded.
  • Gorge / Ravine: Similar because both name deep cuts in the land. Differs because these are narrow, steep, dramatic and often water-eroded; a dell is gentle, rounded and sheltered, not precipitous.
  • Nook: Similar because both name a small sheltered place (a figurative transfer). Differs because nook is a corner of a room or garden, usually indoor or man-made; dell is a whole small valley.
  • Retreat / Refuge (figurative): Similar because both can name a place of shelter and rest — the dell's emotional function. Differs because these are abstract and general; dell is concrete topography carrying the same comforting colour built in.
