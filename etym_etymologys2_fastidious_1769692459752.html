The target word fastidious is built on two Latin components; the full compound chain is given first, then the second element in isolation. MAIN CHAIN (fastus "haughtiness" + taedium "weariness" → fastidium → fastidiosus → fastidious): Latin fastus "haughtiness, arrogance, disdain, pride" (attested 1st c. BCE; origin debated — see note) + Latin taedium "weariness, disgust, aversion, boredom" (attested 1st c. BCE; source of English tedium, tedious) → Latin fastīdium "loathing, squeamishness, disgust, disdain; fastidiousness of taste" — compound of fastus + taedium (attested 1st c. BCE in classical authors) → Latin fastīdiōsus "full of disdain, squeamish, hard to please, over-delicate, fastidious" — adjective in -osus "full of" (attested 1st c. BCE/CE) → Old French fastidieux "disgusting, tiresome, fastidious" (12th–14th c. CE) → Middle English fastidious "disdainful, haughty" (borrowed c. 15th c. CE; first attestations c. 1440) → English fastidious "hard to please; meticulous, exacting, attentive to detail" ◄ TARGET (present-day rootWord) SECOND ELEMENT, ISOLATED (taedium "weariness"): PIE *swā-/sad- family? — no: Latin taedium is of uncertain Italic origin, meaning "weariness, disgust"; it produced English tedium, tedious, tediously. Its deep pre-Italic etymology is not securely established. Debate / nuance note: The origin of Latin fastus is genuinely debated. Some scholars connect it to a PIE root *bhars- "point, prong, bristle" (comparing English bristle), on the idea that the haughty man's pride "bristles" up; others treat fastus as of unknown or specifically Italic origin. The compound fastīdium from fastus + taedium is the traditional and generally accepted account, but the "bristle" connection is a hypothesis, not a settled fact. The Romance descendants (French fastidieux "tiresome," Spanish fastidiar "to annoy," Italian fastidioso "annoying") show that the Romance languages drifted toward "tedious/annoying," while English alone preserved and developed the "squeamishly hard to please" and later "meticulous" senses.
