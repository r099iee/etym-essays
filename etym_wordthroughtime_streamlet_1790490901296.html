- Old English stream - broad river, sea current, even the sea itself; grand, powerful, neutral
- Middle English streme / stream - river, current, flow of water, tears, or people; general and strong
- Late 16th c. streamlet coined - stream + French diminutive -let; dainty, miniature, affectionate; first in pastoral and gardening prose
- 17th-18th c. Augustan and picturesque streamlet - poets and landscape writers need a pretty small-water word; ornamental, sentimental
- Romantic-Scottish streamlet (1790s-1830s) - Burns, Scott, Wordsworth haunt glen streamlets with spirits and memories; fairy, nostalgic, national
- Victorian Ruskin childhood streamlet - the believing boy's White Lady burn in Glen Farg; innocent, vivid, half-real
- Modern headwater + figurative streamlet - ecology values first-order channels; journalism writes of streamlets of blood, light, refugees, and data; scientific yet still gentle
