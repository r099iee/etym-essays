• Caused: Similar because both name the bringing about of an effect; differs because caused is abstract and neutral — a bare relation of cause and effect with no suggestion of effort or craft — whereas wrought describes a process of shaping through sustained labour.
  • Effected: Similar because both mean "brought about or accomplished"; differs because effected is formal and almost administrative, suggesting the orderly execution of a plan, while wrought implies hammering and moulding a raw material into an enduring form.
  • Fashioned: Similar because both mean "shaped or made"; differs because fashioned emphasizes design and the form given to material, while wrought adds the senses of effort, forging, and archaic gravity — and, figuratively, of working upon a person's emotions.
  • Made: Similar because both mean "produced or brought into being"; differs because made is the plain, colourless everyday word, stripped of the labour, craft, and solemnity that wrought carries.
  • Produced: Similar because both mean "brought about"; differs because produced is industrial and mechanical, implying assembly or generation on a mass scale, rather than the craft of a single hand that wrought implies.
  • Worked: Similar because both are the past of the verb "work"; differs because worked is the regular, neutral modern form that reports the fact of labour, while wrought is the archaic, poetic form that dignifies the labour as forging.
  • Built: Similar because both imply construction through effort; differs because built is modern, structural, and literal — of buildings and institutions — while wrought is archaic and craft-focused, of metals and of subtle transformations.
  • Hammered: Similar because both carry the smithy image of beating metal into shape; differs because hammered is a specific, modern, physical verb of blows, while wrought names the whole process of working and shaping, material and figurative alike.
  • Created: Similar because both mean "brought into being"; differs because created suggests making from nothing and carries a divine or artistic frame, while wrought suggests shaping an existing material with effort and craft.
  • Forged: Similar because both are the language of the smithy, meaning shaped by heat and blows; differs because forged is modern and still concrete in its metal sense, while wrought has become archaic and generalized to any deeply-effected change.
