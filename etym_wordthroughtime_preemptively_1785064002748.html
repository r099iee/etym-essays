- 19th century - 'Preempt' enters English as a land-law term: to preempt is to purchase public land in advance of or in preference to others, a right of first claim when settling new territory.
- Mid-20th century - The word goes strategic: a preemptive strike or war is launched to forestall an expected enemy attack - acting first to neutralize the threat before it arrives.
- Contemporary sense - Preemptively describes any action taken ahead of an anticipated event to forestall it, in military, medical, and everyday use; it always carries the logic of the first move, acting on an expected future before it materializes.
