• Domineeringly: Similar because both describe commanding, controlling behaviour; differs because domineeringly stresses the tyrannical crushing or overriding of others' wills, while imperiously highlights proud, royal haughtiness and the assumption of a right to command.
  • Arrogantly: Similar because both describe a superior, self-important manner; differs because arrogantly centres on self-admiration and inflated self-regard, while imperiously centres on giving orders — the imperious person commands, the arrogant person merely swaggers.
  • Haughtily: Similar because both describe a lofty, superior manner; differs because haughtily emphasises disdainful aloofness and looking down on others, while imperiously emphasises the active demand for obedience rather than mere aloofness.
  • Dictatorially: Similar because both describe the absolute giving of orders; differs because dictatorially imports the specific political machinery of autocracy and tyranny, while imperiously is personal and can be used of a bossy person with no political power at all.
  • Autocratically: Similar because both describe rule by one will; differs because autocratically names the structure of absolute single-person rule, while imperiously describes the manner of behaviour — one can be imperious in a committee without being an autocrat.
  • Overbearingly: Similar because both describe an oppressive, self-assertive manner; differs because overbearingly stresses overwhelming or bullying others with one's presence, while imperiously stresses lordly command and the expectation of obedience.
  • High-handedly: Similar because both describe arbitrary exercise of power; differs because high-handedly emphasises the arbitrary, high-handed overruling of procedure or consultation, while imperiously emphasises proud, commanding self-importance.
  • Peremptorily: Similar because both describe commanding speech that brooks no refusal; differs because peremptorily stresses the abrupt, final, non-negotiable quality of a command, while imperiously stresses the proud, haughty attitude behind it.
  • Masterfully: Similar because both describe acting with confident authority; differs because masterfully is positive and admiring — skilled, commanding competence — while imperiously is negative, describing arrogant, unjustified command.
  • Authoritatively: Similar because both describe commanding behaviour; differs because authoritatively is neutral or approving, implying genuine knowledge or rank that justifies the command, while imperiously implies authority merely assumed and usually resented.
  • Condescendingly: Similar because both describe treating others as inferior; differs because condescendingly means talking down to people as if from a height of superiority, while imperiously means actively ordering them about — condescension looks down, imperiousness commands.
  • Superciliously: Similar because both describe a contemptuous sense of superiority; differs because superciliously (from Latin "raised eyebrow") describes sneering disdain expressed in look and manner, while imperiously describes the actual giving of orders with lordly arrogance.
  • Lordly: Similar because both describe the manner of a lord or ruler; differs because lordly can be grand and majestic in a neutral or admiring way, while imperiously is pejorative, describing overbearing assumption of command.
  • Magisterially: Similar because both describe authoritative command; differs because magisterially suggests the weight and dignity of a magistrate or scholar — commanding but grave and informed — while imperiously suggests haughty personal arrogance without that dignity.
