“Pantry” entered English from medieval French for the room where bread was kept — from the Latin word for bread itself, the bread-store of a medieval household.
“Pantry” through the centuries broadened from bread to all provisions: the room where food, tableware, and dry stores were staged between the store and the table.
“Pantry” by the 18th–19th centuries was the standard small food-room of an orderly British house — and in the 20th century the word extended to the built-in kitchen cupboard.
“Pantry” today still names the food-and-ware room of a private house — the cozy, practical store that a worried host retreats to, the very heart of a well-stocked hobbit-hole.
