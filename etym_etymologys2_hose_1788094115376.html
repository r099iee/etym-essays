Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, pre-Old English)
*husǭ / *husōn-
“covering for the leg,
stocking”

↓
Old English (attested from the 8th–9th c.)
hosa
“stocking, leg covering”

↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
hose
“stocking; leg covering”

↓
Modern English (tube sense from 1807)
HOSE
“leg covering (historical);
flexible tube for liquids”

Note: No secure PIE etymon is attested for this word; the OED and Etymonline both judge the origin of *husǭ unknown (possibly a substrate word or a cover-root formation). The chain therefore begins at the earliest verifiable Germanic stage.
