Note: The ultimate etymology of Venetian *gondola* remains a subject of scholarly debate among Romance philologists. The leading hypothesis, championed by Manlio Cortelazzo, Giovanni Alessio, and Giacomo Devoto, derives the word from Byzantine Greek *kondoura* (κόνδουρα), meaning a “short-tailed boat” (from *kondos* “short” and *oura* “tail”), reflecting the light, double-ended skiffs used in the Byzantine Adriatic with which Venice maintained intense maritime trade. Rival hypotheses propose a derivation from Latin *cymbula* (little boat, diminutive of *cymba*), or connection to Latin *gongrus* (conger eel, comparing the slender, black vessel to an eel gliding through water). The first written documentation appears in a decree of Doge Vitale Falier in 1094.
