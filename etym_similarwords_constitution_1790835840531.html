• Temperament: Similar because both describe an individual's innate physical and psychological makeup; differs because temperament focuses specifically on emotional disposition, mood patterns, and psychological reactivity, whereas constitution emphasizes overall physical health, somatic stamina, and bodily robustness.
  • Physique: Similar because both refer to the human body; differs because physique refers strictly to outward muscular form, shape, and physical build, whereas constitution denotes the internal functional vitality and health resilience of the organism.
  • Disposition: Similar because both refer to inherent personal qualities; differs because disposition refers to a person's prevailing emotional tendency or attitude (e.g., cheerful, gloomy), lacking the somatic health and legal-political senses of constitution.
  • Charter: Similar because both denote foundational governance documents; differs because charter usually refers to a grant of rights or incorporation bestowed by a sovereign onto a city, company, or institution, whereas a constitution represents the supreme, organic law of the whole political state.
  • Structure: Similar because both describe how parts are organized together; differs because structure is a broad, generic architectural term for the spatial or mechanical framework of anything, lacking the biological and political specificity of constitution.
  • Composition: Similar because both denote the ingredients or parts making up a whole; differs because composition emphasizes the proportion and nature of constituent elements, often in chemical, artistic, or literary contexts.
