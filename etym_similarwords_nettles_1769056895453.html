• Thorn: Similar because it is a wild plant part that wounds the hand that touches it; differs because a thorn pierces with a hard woody spike that draws blood and breaks off in the skin, whereas a nettle has no sharp edge at all — its sting is chemical, injected irritants that raise a burning, itching rash.
  • Bramble: Similar because it is a wild thicket plant that rakes and scratches an unwary traveller; differs because brambles wound with backward-curving prickles that snag clothing and rip the skin, rather than with any stinging chemical.
  • Thistle: Similar because it is a coarse, spiny weed of rough ground; differs because the thistle's prickles are passive armour — ornamental spikes with no stinging apparatus — while the nettle actively injects an irritant.
  • Annoy: Similar because, in the verb sense, both describe a minor but genuine irritation; differs because to annoy is to cause general mild displeasure, while to nettle is to provoke or smart, keeping the sharp, stinging quality of the plant.
  • Irritate: Similar because both name the act of provoking displeasure or agitation; differs because irritate covers any kind of vexation or physical inflammation, whereas nettle specifically pictures a sharp, stinging, personal provocation.
  • Vex: Similar because both describe being ruffled or provoked; differs because vex is more literary and suggests deep, persistent annoyance, while nettle is more active and pricking — the quick smart of an insult.
  • Gall: Similar because both describe a bitter irritation of the spirit; differs because gall suggests resentment that rankles and chafes over time, while to be nettled is to feel a sharp, immediate, stinging irritation.
  • Provoke: Similar because both describe deliberately stirring another's irritation; differs because provoke is neutral and can be deliberate or accidental and can stir anger, curiosity, or laughter, while nettle specifically means to sting with irritation.
  • Peeve: Similar because both describe mild, petulant annoyance; differs because peeve is informal and suggests small, niggling grievances, while nettled carries the sharper, more vivid sting of the plant.
  • Exasperate: Similar because both describe irritating someone strongly; differs because exasperate suggests frustrating someone to the point of losing patience, whereas nettle suggests a sharper, more personal sting rather than simple frustration.
