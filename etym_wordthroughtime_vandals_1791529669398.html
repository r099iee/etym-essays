- 1st–4th century: the name of a Germanic people on the Roman frontier — a neutral ethnonym in Latin geography and history.
- 455 CE onward: the sack of Rome turns “Vandal” into a byword for barbarian sacrilege and destruction in Christian historiography.
- 1660s: lower-case vandal appears in English for a person who wantonly destroys property or art; the moral sense is now fully detached from the tribe.
- 1794: vandalism is coined by Henri Grégoire during the French Revolution, hardening the word into a legal and cultural category.
- 19th century: Romantic and nationalist historians partly rehabilitate the historical Vandals, while the common noun remains wholly pejorative.
- 20th–21st century: “vandal” means a petty property destroyer or online defacer; “Vandals” survives as a team nickname and band name, and the historical sense is confined to scholarship.
