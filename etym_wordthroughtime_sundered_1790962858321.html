- 9th–11th c. - Old English sundrian: physical dividing, separation, setting apart, or isolating from a group.
- 12th–15th c. - Middle English sundren: poetic expansion into heroic romance, detailing cleaved shields, parted armies, and severed kin.
- 16th–17th c. - Early Modern English: solemn biblical, theological, and Shakespearean usage ('What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder').
- 18th–19th c. - High Romanticism & Victorian Gothic: prime vehicle for tragic lovers, exile, fractured souls, and epic sagas.
- 20th–21st c. - Canonized in epic high fantasy (Tolkien) alongside playful mock-heroic comic irony (Wodehouse).
