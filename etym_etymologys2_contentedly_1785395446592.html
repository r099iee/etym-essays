PIE *ten- "to stretch" (root of enormous range; attested reflexes in Sanskrit tanoti "he stretches", Greek teinein "to stretch") → Latin tendere "to stretch" (1st c. BCE onwards) → Latin tenere "to hold, to keep" (derived from *ten-; the "stretch" sense shifts to "stretch out one's hand to grasp, hence hold") → Latin continere "to hold together, contain" (con- "together" + tenere "to hold"; attested 2nd–1st c. BCE) → Latin contentus, past participle of continere, "contained; held in check; satisfied" (attested 1st c. BCE) → Old French content "satisfied, pleased" (adjective; attested c. 10th c.) → Middle English content "satisfied, pleased" (adjective; c. 1400) → English contented (adjective, 16th c.; past participle of the verb content, "to satisfy", itself formed from the adjective c. 1400s) → contented + -ly (adverbial suffix from Old English -lice, Proto-Germanic *-liko, "in the manner of") → CONTENTEDLY (adverb, c. 1600 — exact form of this card) Note on the chain: the linking of Latin tenere to *ten- is standard and uncontroversial (Pokorny, de Vaan); the semantic step from "stretch" to "hold" passes through the image of the outstretched hand that grasps and keeps. All stages are attested; nothing is reconstructed after Latin.
