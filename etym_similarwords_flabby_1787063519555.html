• Limp: Similar because both describe a lack of stiffness, rigidity, or tension; differs because 'limp' denotes a total drooping, lifeless sagging under gravity (e.g., a limp handshake, a limp leaf), whereas 'flabby' denotes soft, squishy, untoned mass that retains some volume but lacks firm resistance.
  • Sagging: Similar because both involve downward drooping due to loss of elasticity; differs because 'sagging' emphasizes the gravitational sinking or downward curved shape (e.g., sagging roof, sagging skin), while 'flabby' emphasizes the spongy, soft material texture.
  • Flaccid: Similar because both denote soft, yielding, and untoned tissue; differs because 'flaccid' is formal, clinical, and physiological, often describing plant turgor loss or erectile unresponsiveness, whereas 'flabby' is descriptive, sensory, and carries everyday colloquial connotations.
  • Soft: Similar because both yield easily to physical touch; differs because 'soft' is a neutral or positive general adjective (e.g., soft pillow, soft silk), whereas 'flabby' is inherently pejorative and implies an unwholesome loss of proper firmness.
  • Spongy: Similar because both compress easily under pressure; differs because 'spongy' implies porous, elastic, spring-back absorption of moisture, whereas 'flabby' describes dead, unelastic, doughy yielding.
  • Doughy: Similar because both characterize unformed, pasty, and heavy softness; differs because 'doughy' carries connotations of unbaked bread, paleness, and sticky heaviness, while 'flabby' focuses on slack hanging flesh or deflated objects.
  • Slack: Similar because both describe a lack of tautness; differs because 'slack' applies primarily to ropes, chains, schedules, or discipline, whereas 'flabby' applies primarily to fleshy substance or bloated structure.
  • Bloated: Similar because both describe expanded, out-of-shape mass; differs because 'bloated' implies swelling, distension from gas/fluid, and over-tight expansion, while 'flabby' implies deflated, unpressurized limpness.
