Solomon in your passage — some of the Solomon Islands — denotes the South Pacific archipelago east of Papua New Guinea, comprising the independent nation of Solomon Islands (capital Honiara on Guadalcanal) and the neighbouring Papua New Guinean province of North Solomons (Bougainville). The name is a European exonym bestowed in 1568 by Spanish explorer Álvaro de Mendaña, who fancied the gold he glimpsed there must be the biblical King Solomon's Ophir gold, and the label stuck through British protectorate to independence (1978). Geographically it spans some 900 mountainous volcanic islands and atolls — Guadalcanal, Malaita, Santa Isabel, Choiseul, New Georgia, San Cristóbal — famed for coral reefs, rainforest, WWII battles (Guadalcanal 1942-43), and Melanesian cultures.

The underlying personal name Solomon — Hebrew Shelomoh — means the peaceful or peaceable one, son of David, third king of united Israel (trad. 10th c. BCE), builder of the First Temple, proverbial sage, judge, magician, and lover in biblical, Qur'anic (Sulayman), and legendary traditions. To call islands Solomon's was to project fantasies of gold, wisdom, and exotic plenty onto unknown lands, a common early-modern practice (cf. El Dorado). In your anthropological source (Frazer-style comparative account of cannibalism), Solomon Islands functions as an ethnographic locale tag: reports from missionaries and traders of ritual feasting and headhunting in Melanesia, cited alongside Congo and New Britain to evidence once-widespread anthropophagy.

Modern senses radiate from these two anchors:

  • Toponymic / geopolitical: Solomon Islands as nation-state (Commonwealth realm, ~700,000 people, English + Pijin + 70 indigenous languages), Solomon Islander, Solomon Sea, Solomon bloke (Pijin). Adjective Solomons or Solomon Islands (attributive): Solomon Islands government, Solomons dive resort. News contexts include ethnic tensions (1998-2003 RAMSI intervention), logging, climate vulnerability, and 2022 security pact debates.
  • Anthroponymic / religious: King Solomon as archetype of wisdom — a Solomon means a wise judge (the Solomon of the bench), ironic for a cuckold or polygamist (700 wives), or in occult lore Solomon the exorcist (Seal of Solomon, Key of Solomon grimoires). Solomonic means wise, judicious, or pertaining to Solomonic columns (twisted Baroque pillars from Bernini's Vatican baldacchino, supposedly modelled on Temple columns).
  • Figurative and proverbial: judgment of Solomon (a shrewd compromise that reveals truth, from the two mothers claiming one baby), Solomon's wisdom, Solomon's seal (both the hexagram talisman and the woodland plant Polygonatum with seal-like scars), Song of Solomon (biblical love poetry).
  • Marine / natural history: Solomon Islands skink (Corucia zebrata), Solomon sea eagle, Solomon Islands eclectus parrot — biogeographic epithets signalling endemism in a biodiversity hotspot.

Polysemy bridge: sacred-king → fabled gold-land → Pacific islands named for that gold → modern nation and its peoples/flora. The leap from Jerusalem sage to Melanesian atolls is purely associative-colonial, not linguistic descent, but four centuries of charts cemented it. Capitalisation distinguishes Solomon (proper) from solomon (rare fish/plant vernacular, lowercased). Pronunciation stresses first syllable; possessive Solomon's is literary, attributive Solomon is geographic. Register spans scripture, statecraft, travel, and scholarship; in anthropology passages like yours it carries late-Victorian exoticism that modern readers must handle critically.
