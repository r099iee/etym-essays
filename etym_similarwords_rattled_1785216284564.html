• Clatter: Similar because both name a rapid succession of hard impacts; differs because clatter is duller and heavier (dishes, hoofs, boards), while rattle is sharper, lighter, and often metallic.
  • Clank: Similar because both describe metallic sounds; differs because clank is a single heavy, hollow ring of chains or machinery, while rattle is a quick series of small percussions.
  • Clang: Similar because both are loud metallic noises; differs because clang is a single resonant boom of struck metal, while rattle is repetitive and brittle.
  • Clash: Similar because both name collisions of hard objects; differs because clash is one heavy impact of solid masses, while rattle is many small, rapid impacts.
  • Clink: Similar because both are light metallic sounds; differs because clink is a single clear "ting" of glass or small metal, while rattle is a rapid series.
  • Jingle: Similar because both are light metallic percussions; differs because jingle is musical, tinkling, and pleasant (coins, spurs, sleigh bells), while rattle is harsh and disorderly.
  • Jangle: Similar because both are metallic noises; differs because jangle implies a discordant, grating mixture of sounds, while rattle is a uniform rapid chatter.
  • Tinkle: Similar because both are light sounds of small hard objects; differs because tinkle is delicate, bell-like, and musical, while rattle is abrupt and percussive.
  • Chatter: Similar because both are rapid repetitive sounds; differs because chatter names the tapping of teeth or the noise of birds and machinery, often with a rhythmic or animate quality, while rattle stresses hollow, loose collisions.
  • Bang: Similar because both are loud percussive noises; differs because bang is a single sharp explosive report, while rattle is a rapid succession of small reports.
  • Knock: Similar because both are tapping impacts; differs because knock is a deliberate single rap (as on a door), while rattle is an uncontrolled rapid series.
  • Crash: Similar because both are loud collision noises; differs because crash is a single violent, smashing impact, while rattle is a fast, light, repeated clatter.
