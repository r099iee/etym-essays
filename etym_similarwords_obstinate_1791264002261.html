• Stubborn: Similar because it denotes refusal to yield or change; differs because it is the neutral, everyday term without obstinate's implication of unreasonable perversity.
  • Obdurate: Similar because it means hardened against persuasion or pity; differs because it stresses emotional hardness and moral unrepentance, often colder than obstinate.
  • Intransigent: Similar because it means refusing compromise; differs because it is formal and political, describing negotiating positions rather than personal temperament.
  • Inflexible: Similar because it means unwilling to bend or adapt; differs because it can be neutral about principles or rules, while obstinate implies blameworthy rigidity.
  • Headstrong: Similar because it means self-willed and hard to restrain; differs because it implies impulsive, youthful wilfulness rather than settled, sullen persistence.
  • Wilful: Similar because it means deliberately doing as one pleases; differs because it stresses intentional disobedience rather than mere resistance to change.
  • Pigheaded: Similar because it means stupidly stubborn; differs because it is colloquial and insulting, adding a judgement of crass stupidity that obstinate need not carry.
  • Mulish: Similar because it means stubborn like a mule; differs because it evokes sullen, uncooperative immobility, more metaphorical and vivid than obstinate.
  • Adamant: Similar because it means refusing to be persuaded; differs because it is often admiring and suggests rock-like firmness of principle rather than perverse unreason.
  • Unyielding: Similar because it means not giving way under pressure; differs because it is broader and can describe materials, positions, or people without the pejorative tone of obstinate.
  • Recalcitrant: Similar because it means resisting authority or control; differs because it stresses defiance of rules or authority, while obstinate can be purely private.
  • Pertinacious: Similar because it means holding firmly to a purpose; differs because it is formal and can be positive for perseverance, whereas obstinate is almost always critical.
  • Dogged: Similar because it means persistent and tenacious; differs because it is usually approving of determined persistence, while obstinate condemns unreasonable persistence.
