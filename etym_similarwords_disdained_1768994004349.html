• Scorned: Similar because it too regards someone or something as unworthy of consideration; differs because scorn is heated and openly voiced — mockery and derision — while disdain is cool and quietly files the matter beneath notice.
  • Despised: Similar because it looks down upon as unworthy; differs because despising is the moral heavyweight — an abiding revulsion for what is base or wicked — whereas disdain may be merely aesthetic or social.
  • Spurned: Similar because it rejects what is offered; differs because spurning is physical and active (from Old English spurnan, "to kick") — the thing is kicked away — while disdain refuses without action.
  • Slighted: Similar because it fails to honor someone or something; differs because a slight is indifference or neglect, a failure to honor without the positive judgment of unworthiness that disdain carries.
  • Contemned: Similar because it means to hold in contempt; differs because contemn is a formal, literary, Latinate verb for verbal condemnation, more declarative and less coolly dismissive than disdain.
  • Belittled: Similar because it diminishes the worth of someone or something; differs because belittling actively works to make a thing seem small, while disdain simply judges it small and walks away.
  • Disparaged: Similar because it speaks or acts so as to lower another's standing; differs because disparagement involves damaging talk and comparison (Latin dispar, "unequal"), an engaged attack rather than a refusal to engage.
  • Snubbed: Similar because it rejects or ignores someone's advance; differs because a snub is a pointed, often public slight of a person, whereas disdain can be silent, private, and directed at things or ideas as well as people.
  • Deigned (mirror image): Similar because it comes from the same Latin root (dignari, "to deem worthy"); differs because deigning stoops to consider something worthy of one's attention, while disdaining refuses to stoop — one lowers, the other refuses to lower.
  • Condescended (near-mirror): Similar because it too involves superiority; differs because condescension stoops with a patronizing graciousness that still accepts, whereas disdain accepts nothing at all.
  • Rejected: Similar because it refuses something offered; differs because rejection is neutral and factual — the offer is declined — without the judgment of unworthiness or the haughty self-elevation of disdain.
  • Ignored: Similar because it gives no attention; differs because ignoring may be accidental or strategic and carries no verdict of worth, whereas disdain is a deliberate judgment of inferiority.
