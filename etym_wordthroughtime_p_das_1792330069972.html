- Vedic Era (c. 1500–800 BCE) — Primarily designated the physical leg or foot of a quadruped animal, which naturally symbolized one-fourth of an animal sacrifice and was ritually mapped onto four-line liturgical verses.
- Early Metric Codification (c. 800–400 BCE) — Established in the Chandaḥsūtra and Prātiśākhyas as the precise technical term for a single quarter-line of a metrical stanza, cementing the quadruped metaphor in poetics.
- Classical Sāstric & Philosophical Era (c. 300 BCE–800 CE) — Extended to denote the primary fourfold chapter divisions of masterworks (such as the four pādas of Patañjali's Yogasūtras) and the quarter-divisions of astronomical arcs.
- Medieval Bhakti Movement (c. 800–1600 CE) — Transformed into the ultimate symbol of spiritual surrender through the veneration of the 'lotus feet' (pāda-padma) of deities and spiritual preceptors, entering polite language as an honorific suffix.
- Modern Philology & Indology (19th c.–Present) — Preserved in international comparative prosody as the standard term for the metrical quarters of ancient Indo-Aryan poetry.
