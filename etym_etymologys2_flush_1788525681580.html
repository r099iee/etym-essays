PIE (reconstructed root)
*bʰlew- / *dʰlew-
"to swell, flow, gush forth"
(proposed root of Germanic flow-words and Latin fluxus)
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*fleuganą / *flugiz
"to fly, take flight; sudden rush"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
fluschen / flusshen
"to fly up suddenly; to rush or plunge forth"
(imitative phonaestheme blend of fly, flash, and rush)
↓
Old French (12th–13th c.; convergent influence on gaming sense)
flux
"a flowing, running; run of cards"
(from Latin fluxus "flowing stream")
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
flushe
"to blush, glow; to cleanse with water; level, even"
↓
Modern English (standardized root)
FLUSH
"rosy glow, rush of water, planar level, card suit run"

Note: English flush is an etymological confluence of at least three distinct streams: (1) an imitative Germanic hunting term (*fleug- / *flusschen, "to fly up"), (2) an expressive sound-symbolic water/blush verb rhyming with *gush*, *rush*, and *blush*, and (3) an Anglo-French borrowing of *flux* (< Latin *fluxus*) in card games. The planar/joinery sense arose as a figurative extension of water filling a container level to its brim.
