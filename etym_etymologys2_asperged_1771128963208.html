• Proto-Indo-European *sper-g- (or *sper-) "to strew, scatter, sow" — the root of "scattering" vocabulary; cognate with Greek speírō "to sow, scatter" (Greek σπείρω).
  • Latin spargere "to scatter, sprinkle, strew, spread" (Classical Latin, 1st century BCE).
  • Latin aspergere "to sprinkle upon, scatter over" — formed with the prefix ad- ("to, toward", assimilated as as-) + spargere (attested Classical Latin).
  • Latin past participle aspersus "sprinkled"; the noun aspersio "sprinkling" gives the related English aspersion.
  • Old French / Anglo-French aspergier "to sprinkle (esp. with holy water)" (12th–14th century), inherited from Latin aspergere.
  • Middle English aspergen "to sprinkle with holy water" (borrowed c. 14th–15th century); modern English verb asperge and participle asperged.
  • Final node: ASPERGED.

Debate note: The chain from Latin onward is secure; the PIE root *sper-g- ("strew, scatter") is generally accepted and links spargere with Greek speírō "sow", but some handbooks treat the Greek–Latin correspondence as probable rather than certain. The English form asperged is a participial adjective built on the Latin-based verb; the common word sprinkle is of different (Germanic) origin.
