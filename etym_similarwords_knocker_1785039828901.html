• Hammer: Similar because both are striking tools that deliver forceful blows; differs because a hammer is defined by its head for driving nails and shaping metal, while a knocker is defined by the act of knocking — producing a sharp, repeated report as a signal or blow.
  • Mallet: Similar because both are tools for striking; differs because a mallet is a lighter, broad-headed wooden or rubber beater for gentle, broad blows, whereas a knocker is a harder, ringing striking device.
  • Gavel: Similar because both are small striking devices; differs because a gavel is a ceremonial hammer for calling order in a court or meeting, while a knocker summons entry or delivers repeated blows.
  • Club: Similar because both are blunt striking instruments; differs because a club is a weapon that crushes and batters rather than reports a sharp, repeated knock.
  • Cudgel: Similar because both are heavy striking implements; differs because a cudgel is a thick stick used to bludgeon, lacking the precise, ringing signal function of a knocker.
  • Battering ram: Similar because both are heavy instruments that strike forcefully; differs because a battering ram is a massive siege beam swung to breach walls, while a knocker is a small striking device for a sharp report or blow.
  • Doorbell: Similar because both summon entry at a door; differs because a doorbell rings or chimes electrically and is silent until pressed, while a knocker produces a percussive thud by being lifted and struck.
  • Knocker-up: Similar because both derive from the verb to knock and involve repeated rapping; differs because the knocker-up is specifically the paid waker who rapped on windows to rouse factory workers, a person rather than a device.
  • Critic / detractor: Similar because both can be called a knocker when disparaging something; differs because these words name persons who judge or belittle, while knocker in that sense is a slang noun for a fault-finder derived from the figurative verb to knock.
  • Gong: Similar because both are percussion objects used to make loud, reverberating noise; differs because a gong is a struck metal disc that hums and decays slowly, while a knocker produces a sharper, crisper, percussive report.
