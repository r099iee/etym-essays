• Frowns: Similar because both express displeasure, pain, or concentration with the face; differs because frowns names the whole facial expression—the darkening of the entire face—whereas knits isolates the brows and their drawing together, and can imply a tighter, more lasting gathering.
  • Furrows: Similar because both crease the brow; differs because furrows borrows from ploughing and describes deep, vertical creases ploughed into the forehead, while knits pictures the brows knotting toward each other in a single gathered line.
  • Wrinkles: Similar because both describe a crinkling of skin; differs because wrinkles is far more general—any crinkling of skin, cloth, nose, or age—and lacks the localising of the brows and the sense of active drawing together.
  • Gathers: Similar because both draw parts together; differs because gathers is a general word for collecting or assembling, while knits specifically images the brows being tied together like threads in a knot.
  • Contracts: Similar because both mean to draw inward and tighten; differs because contracts is physiological and general (muscles, pupils, a face), without the textile imagery or the brow-localising of knits.
  • Puckers: Similar because both gather the skin into folds; differs because a pucker is a pinched bunching of small folds (lips, cloth), whereas knitting the brows draws the two brows together into one line.
  • Scowls: Similar because both signal displeasure; differs because a scowl is a hostile, angry darkening of the whole face, while knitting the brows is subtler and can signal concentration, pain, or sorrow as well as anger.
