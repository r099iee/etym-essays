• joking: Similar because both involve making light, non-serious remarks intended to provoke amusement; differs because joking is the standard, everyday colloquial term for telling jokes or behaving playfully, whereas jesting belongs to an elevated, historical, or literary register that frequently carries a sharper edge of philosophical flippancy, irony, or mocking derision.
  • bantering: Similar because both denote witty, lighthearted verbal exchanges; differs because bantering specifically emphasizes reciprocal, good-humored social teasing and back-and-forth conversational camaraderie between peers, whereas jesting can be unilateral, performative, sarcastic, or scoffing.
  • mocking: Similar because both can treat a solemn subject with levity; differs because mocking possesses an explicitly contemptuous, cruel, or demeaning intent to hold someone or something up to ridicule, whereas jesting spans a wider spectrum from gentle playfulness to ironic skepticism.
  • facetious: Similar because both describe treating serious, weighty matters with humor; differs because facetious almost invariably carries a pejorative connotation of inappropriate or annoying flippancy when solemnity is required, whereas jesting simply describes the playful speech act or theatrical persona itself.
  • scoffing: Similar because both express skepticism through dismissive levity; differs because scoffing is an overt, abrasive display of scorn, disbelief, and disdain without necessarily exhibiting wit, whereas jesting employs verbal artifice, cleverness, or playful irony.
  • quipping: Similar because both involve delivering sharp, witty observations; differs because quipping concentrates on producing rapid, spontaneous one-line retorts, whereas jesting describes a broader behavioral mood, disposition, or sustained comic tone.
  • drolling: Similar because both imply whimsical, offbeat humor; differs because drolling suggests a dry, quaintly eccentric manner of amusement or deadpan storytelling, whereas jesting is more active, theatrical, or cynically provocative.
  • chaffing: Similar because both involve teasing and making fun; differs because chaffing is a warm, informal, and harmless variety of good-natured ribbing among close companions, lacking the potential for biting philosophical dismissal present in jesting.
