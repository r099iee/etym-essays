*dʰeh₁-

↓

↓

— CONSTITUENT ROOT 2: LANE —
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*lón-eh₂
‘narrow passageway, lane’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*lanō
‘lane, narrow path between hedges or fences’
↓
Old English (c. 8th–9th c.)
lane / lanu
‘lane, narrow way between houses or enclosures’
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
lane
‘narrow urban street, passageway’
↓
Modern English (current)
Fetter Lane
‘historic London street off Fleet Street’

Note on Toponymic Debate: Modern toponymists (including Ekwall and the English Place-Name Society) confirm that Fetter Lane originates from Middle English faitour (‘idle beggar, vagrant’), attested in 1292 as Faitereslane and 1312 as Faytoreslane. A secondary historical tradition recorded by antiquarian John Stow (1598) hypothesized a derivation from fewterers (‘keepers of greyhounds’, from Old French vautrier) or felt-makers (from feutre). Popular folk-etymology also reinterpreted the name through native Germanic fetter (Old English feoter, ‘shackle’, from PIE *ped-, ‘foot’), falsely associating the street with fetters for prisoners.
