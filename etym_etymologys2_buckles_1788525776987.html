PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed)

*bʰow-k- / *bʰew-

"to swell, puff up, blow"

(imitative root for outward inflation and swelling; source of terms for cheeks, belly, and rounded bulges)

↓

Proto-Italic (reconstructed)

*buk- / *bukka

"cheek, puffed cheek, mouth"

↓

Classical Latin (2nd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)

bucca

"cheek puffed out in blowing; mouthful"

↓

Late Latin / Imperial Latin (2nd–4th c. CE)

buccula

"little cheek; cheek-strap of a helmet; rounded boss of a shield"

(diminutive of bucca; named because the cheek-flaps protected the cheeks, and the central shield-boss puffed out like a cheek)

↓

Gallo-Romance / Old French (12th c.)

bocle / boucle

"boss of a shield; ring, metal clasp, buckle"

↓

Anglo-Norman / Middle English (c. 1300–1350)

bokel / bukel

"fastening clasp for a strap; shield boss"

↓

Early Modern English (16th–18th c.)

buckle (plural buckles)

"clasp for fastening belts, shoes, and harness; to warp or bend"

↓

Modern English (present)

BUCKLES

"clasps for joining straps/shoes; bends or collapses under compressive force"
