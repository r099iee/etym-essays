=== ROOT WORD 1: MANTEL (first element) === Stage 1 — deeper origin UNKNOWN / DEBATED: Latin mantellum / mantēlum ‘cloak, covering, veil’ (late Latin; the pre-Latin history is obscure — no reliable PIE etymon is agreed, and honest scholarship stops at Latin rather than inventing an ancestor; some scholars have floated substrate or Mediterranean sources, none decisive) Stage 2 — Old French mantel ‘cloak; mantle’ (9th–12th c.) Stage 3 — English mantle ‘loose cloak, garment’ (c. 1200, via Old French) and English mantel ‘stone beam or hood over a fireplace’ (c. 1300) — the hood that ‘cloaks’ the fire and channels smoke, by architectural metaphor from the garment Stage 4 — English mantel + piece → mantelpiece ‘the shelf-and-surround unit over a hearth’ (attested 1680s) => mantelpiece (modern English compound). DEBATE / NUANCE: The Latin word’s origin is not a textbook PIE root with a neat cognate set; trees that show *something before Latin are speculative and should be treated as unproven. === ROOT WORD 2: PIECE (second element) === Stage 1 — Gaulish *pettyā ‘piece, portion’ (Continental Celtic; cf. Welsh peth ‘thing’, Cornish pyth ‘part’) — reconstructed, the standard hypothesis for the source of the Romance word, but debated (see note) Stage 2 — Vulgar Latin *pettia ‘a piece, bit, portion’ (Romano-Gallic vocabulary; absent from classical prose, attested through late Latin glosses and Romance outcomes) Stage 3 — Old French piece ‘piece, portion, share’ (10th–12th c.) Stage 4 — Middle English pece / piece ‘a fragment, a portion’ (13th c.) Stage 5 — English piece ‘a bit, a part; a constructed article’ — second element of compounds such as chimneypiece, masterpiece, timepiece, and mantelpiece (1680s) => mantelpiece (the structural ‘piece’ built on the mantel). DEBATE / NUANCE: The Gaulish origin of *pettia is widely accepted but not certain; some derive it instead from within Latin or a shared Italo-Celtic base. Both chains converge on the single English compound mantelpiece.
