• predominance: Similar because both describe superiority in power, influence, or prevalence over others; differs because predominance emphasizes active rule, control, or governing ascendancy (from Latin dominus, lord/master), whereas preponderance emphasizes measurable weight, numerical superiority, or cumulative balance.
  • prevalence: Similar because both indicate that something exists in large numbers or is widespread; differs because prevalence describes how common, frequent, or ubiquitous something is across a population or territory (e.g. disease prevalence), whereas preponderance emphasizes that one element outnumbers or outweighs competing elements.
  • superiority: Similar because both convey being greater or higher than another entity; differs because superiority is a broad, value-laden term that often implies higher moral, technical, or qualitative excellence, whereas preponderance is strictly quantitative, gravitational, or evidential.
  • dominance: Similar because both denote one force overshadowing or having the upper hand over others; differs because dominance implies an assertive, authoritative, or coercive exercise of power that commands submission, whereas preponderance describes an objective excess of weight, numbers, or evidence.
  • majority: Similar because both denote an excess in numerical count; differs because majority is an exact political and statistical threshold (specifically greater than 50% of counted votes), whereas preponderance can refer to overwhelming relative advantage, qualitative influence, or evidential weight regardless of a binary vote.
  • hegemony: Similar because both describe a state of superior geopolitical or cultural power; differs because hegemony implies systematic, institutionalized ideological and geopolitical leadership or dominance of one state or class over others, whereas preponderance refers to the raw physical, economic, or evidentiary balance of forces.
  • ascendancy: Similar because both denote a position of dominant control or influence; differs because ascendancy highlights the upward trajectory or climbing process of gaining power, whereas preponderance describes the steady, existing state of greater mass, weight, or probability.
