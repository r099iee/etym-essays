• even-tempered: Similar because both describe an individual who is not easily upset or angered; differs because even-tempered is a common Germanic compound focused exclusively on emotional mood, whereas equable is an elevated Latinate word that also applies extensively to climate, weather, and physical motion.
  • placid: Similar because both signify calm and freedom from agitation; differs because placid often connotes a quiet, peaceful stillness bordering on passivity or mild complacency (a placid lake, a placid child), whereas equable emphasizes active internal stability and consistent uniformity of spirit.
  • imperturbable: Similar because both denote an inability to be easily rattled; differs because imperturbable emphasizes a profound, almost bulletproof resistance to surprise, panic, or disorder in moments of extreme crisis, whereas equable describes the day-to-day steady, level consistency of one's temperament.
  • serene: Similar because both convey tranquility; differs because serene evokes lofty, luminous spiritual peace or clear, cloudless skies, whereas equable focuses on the absence of variability, fluctuation, or emotional extremes.
  • composed: Similar because both describe an unruffled state of mind; differs because composed implies deliberate effort or self-discipline to hold one's feelings together in the face of shock, whereas an equable disposition is an inherent, effortless constitutional trait.
  • temperate: Similar because both describe moderation and avoidance of extremes; differs because temperate implies self-restraint and conscious moderation regarding appetites and passions (or middle-latitude weather zones), whereas equable centers strictly on uniformity and freedom from fluctuation.
  • level-headed: Similar because both describe steady emotional stability; differs because level-headed highlights sound practical judgment, common sense, and pragmatic reasoning during difficulties, whereas equable highlights emotional tone and meteorological mildness.
  • phlegmatic: Similar because both describe a disposition that remains calm under stress; differs because phlegmatic historically derives from the sluggish humour phlegm and often carries a slightly negative connotation of coldness, sluggishness, or lack of enthusiasm, whereas equable is universally positive and gracious.
  • unruffled: Similar because both indicate freedom from emotional disturbance; differs because unruffled is a descriptive visual metaphor suggesting that smooth waters or feathers have not been disturbed by a passing breeze, while equable denotes a permanent structural baseline.
  • uniform: Similar because both denote lack of variation; differs because uniform is a broad, dry mechanical term meaning having the same form or degree throughout, lacking any inherent emotional or meteorological warmth.
  • equitable: Similar because both share the Latin root aequus; differs fundamentally because equitable means just, fair, and morally balanced in law and human rights, whereas equable means constant, unvarying, and steady in temperament or climate.
