- c. 700s–1100s: Old English *fyllan / fellan* functioned as the primary causative verb of *fall*, meaning "to cause to fall, strike down in battle, overthrow, or cut down trees."
- 1200s–1450s: In Middle English, *fellen* solidified into the essential forestry and woodworking term for bringing down timber, while maintaining strong chivalric connotations for unhorsing or cleaving knights.
- 1500s–1750s: Specialised in textile trades and tailoring to describe folding down a seam flat and sewing it over (a *felled seam*), while prose writers applied it metaphorically to fatal illnesses striking down populations.
- 1800s–Present: Retains authoritative standing in forestry, boxing/combat sports, and figurative accounts of sudden collapse caused by disease, exhaustion, or grief.
