Note: Outskirts is a compound combining the ancient Germanic directional particle out with the Scandinavian loanword skirt. Old Norse skyrta is an exact etymological doublet of native Old English scyrte (which evolved into modern English shirt). While native shirt underwent Anglo-Saxon palatalization (/sk/ -> /ʃ/) and rose to denote an upper-body garment, the unpalatalized Scandinavian borrowing skirt fell downward to designate the lower hem of a robe. In late Middle English, skirt was applied metaphorically to the edges or borders of landscapes (the "skirts of a wood"). Elizabethan writers prefixed out- to denote the outermost perimeter, crystallizing the compound into its standard plural form by the seventeenth century.
