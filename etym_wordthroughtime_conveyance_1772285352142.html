- [15th century to carry along] - ['Conveyance' first names the act of carrying or transporting something or someone from place to place]
- [16th-17th century legal transfer] - [Becomes a technical legal term: the deed or act by which property is transferred from one owner to another]
- [18th-19th century means of transport] - [Widens to any vehicle or means of travel — a carriage or 'public conveyance', a daily word for getting about]
- [19th-20th century figurative medium] - [Used metaphorically for whatever carries an idea, message, or spirit to an audience — 'the artist is his conveyance, not his companion']
- [Present split register] - [The transport sense sounds slightly dated or formal ('horse-drawn conveyance'), the legal sense stays fully technical, and the metaphorical sense thrives in literary prose]
