Note: endue presents an intricate etymological convergence. Morphologically, it stems from Latin indūcere (“to lead into”) via Old French enduire. However, in Anglo-Norman and Middle English, it became thoroughly conflated with two unrelated verbs: Latin induere (“to put on, clothe”, from PIE *en-dhou-) and Anglo-Norman endoer (“to endow”, from Latin dōtāre, “to provide with a dowry”). This tri-fold semantic contamination permanently shaped its meaning into “investing or clothing the spirit with virtues or gifts.”
