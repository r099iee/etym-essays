- [Vedic Saṃhitā Period (c. 1500–1000 BCE)] — originated as a verbal participle meaning "done, prepared" and the supreme winning four-point score in ritual dice gambling (*akṣadyūta*) — [perception: celebrated winning throw of total victory and complete fortune]
- [Late Vedic & Brāhmaṇa Era (c. 1000–600 BCE)] — metaphorically extended from dice scores into cosmic time, designating the four-quarter cycle of cosmological eras in the *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* — [perception: cosmological symbol of completeness, balance, and sacrificial order]
- [Epic & Purāṇic Age (c. 500 BCE – 500 CE; Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata, Manusmṛti)] — codified as the *Kṛta Yuga* or *Satya Yuga*, the pristine Golden Age where Dharma stands on four legs without disease, war, or falsehood — [perception: universal archetype of cosmic perfection, righteous governance, and primordial truth]
- [Classical Sanskrit & Mathematical Era (c. 5th–15th c. CE)] — widely utilized in scientific treatises (Aryabhata, Bhāskara) as the chronogram for the numeral 4, while ethical texts celebrated *kṛtajña* (gratitude) as supreme virtue — [perception: scientific numerical standard and moral touchstone of character]
- [Modern Philosophical & Cultural Era (19th–21st c.)] — interpreted by modern Indian thinkers (Sri Aurobindo, Vivekananda) as a future evolutionary ideal of spiritual consciousness to be reclaimed on earth — [perception: enduring visionary symbol of collective spiritual awakening and golden age harmony]
