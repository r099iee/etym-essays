• Explorer: Similar because it names one who ventures into unknown terrain; differs because an explorer seeks knowledge, glory, or empire with no necessary mineral interest, whereas a prospector's search is specifically for ore.
  • Miner: Similar because it belongs to the mineral-wealth trade; differs because a miner works an established claim, extracting ore from a found deposit, whereas a prospector hunts for the deposit itself and precedes the miner in the sequence of wealth.
  • Surveyor: Similar because it examines the land; differs because a surveyor is the official measurer and mapper, systematic and bureaucratic, not speculative like the prospector's gamble.
  • Scout: Similar because it reconnoiters ahead in dangerous country; differs because a scout gathers intelligence for a party or army rather than hunting for minerals.
  • Adventurer: Similar because it names one who courts risk for gain in unknown places; differs because adventurer is wider — any risk-taker, and historically a shareholder in trading ventures — with no specific mineral search.
  • Forty-niner: Similar because it names a specific kind of gold-seeker of the 1849 California rush; differs because it is historically narrow — the Californian gold rusher — whereas prospector is the general trade-name.
  • Sourdough: Similar because it names the Alaskan and Klondike frontier prospector; differs because sourdough is regional slang for the veteran northerner (named for the bread starter), narrower in register and place.
  • Gold-digger: Similar because it literally names one who digs for gold; differs because modern slang has shifted gold-digger to mean a person who pursues money through romantic attachment — a figurative overturning of the literal mining sense.
  • Panner: Similar because it names one who washes gravel in a pan to find gold; differs because panning is one technique within prospecting, not the whole speculative search for deposits.
  • Seeker: Similar because it names one who searches; differs because seeker is completely general — any search for anything — with none of the mining, frontier, or forward-gaze color of prospector.
  • Claim-jumper (negative neighbor): Similar because it belongs to the same frontier economy of mineral wealth; differs because it names the thief who seizes another's staked claim — an outlaw figure contrasted with the lawful prospector.
