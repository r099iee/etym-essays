PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*kot- / *kat-
"basin, vessel, bowl, circular container"
Note: Mediterranean substrate or early Indo-European base for vessels, yielding Latin catīnus ('bowl, dish').
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
catīnus (diminutive: catillus)
"deep dish, bowl, earthenware or metal pot"
↓
Proto-Germanic (Early Latin Loanword) (c. 1st–2nd c. CE)
*katilaz
"kettle, cauldron, metal cooking vessel"
Note: Borrowed across Roman frontier trade; cognate with Old Norse ketill, Old High German kezzil, Gothic katils.
↓
Old English (c. 8th–11th c.)
cetil / cetel / cytel
"cauldron, metal cooking pot, basin for boiling"
Note: Regular i-mutation shifted a -> e/y. Influenced phonetically by Old Norse ketill with hard /k/.
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–15th c.)
ketel / kettle
"cauldron, broad deep vessel for boiling liquids"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th–18th c.)
kettle (tea-kettle)
"spouted metal vessel with handle for boiling water"
↓
