PIE *kap-

"to seize, grasp, hold" — reconstructed root;
no certain attestation; the link to Latin is debated

↓

Latin capere

"to take, seize, grasp, catch"
(attested from the 3rd c. BCE)

↓

Latin capax (gen. capacis)

"able to hold much; spacious; capable"
(adjective built on capere; 1st c. BCE)

↓

Late Latin capacitas

"capacity, ability to contain, competence"
(post-classical, 3rd–4th c. CE)

↓

Middle French capacité

"ability to hold; capability"
(borrowed into English as capacity, 15th–16th c.)

↓

English capacity

"power to hold, receive, or do; ability"
(in English from the 16th c.)

↓

English incapacitate

"to deprive of power, strength, or legal competence"
(coined 17th c. from in- "not" + capacity + -ate)

↓

INCAPACITATED

past participle and adjective — "made unable to
function; rendered helpless, disabled"

Note: the connection of Latin capere to PIE *kap- is traditional but not certain; de Vaan treats the origin of Latin capio as unclear, so the root stage is debated and should be read as a hypothesis.
