English Dialect (late 18th c.)
snob
"a shoemaker, shoemaker's apprentice"
↓
University Slang (Cambridge) (c. 1790s)
snob
"a townsperson, a non-university resident"
↓
19th Century English (c. 1840s)
snob
"one who vulgarly admires social position"
↓
Modern English (19th c.)
SNOBBERY
"snobbish conduct, attitude, or quality"

Note on etymology: The popular folk etymology claiming "snob" derives from Latin "s.nob." (sine nobilitate) is historically false; its true origin lies in 18th-century cobbler and university slang.
