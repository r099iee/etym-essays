- 1540s — the hiss itself: whizz is born as pure imitation of rushing air, the sound of arrows and switched sticks made into a verb.
- 17th c. — the battlefield sound: whizzing bullets and shot fill Civil War and later memoirs; the word becomes the standard music of narrowly missed death.
- 18th c. — toys and tops: whizzing spinning-tops, whirligigs, and fireworks carry the word into playtime; speed becomes fun, not fear.
- 19th c. — the railway age: express trains, telegrams, and machinery whizz past; the hiss of steam gives the old arrow-word a whole industrial orchestra.
- 20th c. — prodigies and pills: the whiz kid makes speed clever, while street whizz (amphetamine) makes it chemical; to whizz through exams is the decade’s great boast.
- Today — joyful velocity: rockets, messages, mopeds, and deadlines all whizz; the dragon over the hobbits’ heads remains its perfect picture — beautiful, noisy, gone in a blink.
