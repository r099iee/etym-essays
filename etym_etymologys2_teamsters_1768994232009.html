TEAMSTERS — single linear chain (the plural of teamster).
1. PIE *deuk- "to lead, draw, pull" — cf. Latin dūcere "to lead, draw, conduct" (→ duct, conduct, educate, produce, reduce, introduce, seduce, duke), Gothic tiuhan "to lead, draw", Middle Welsh dygu "to bring, lead".
2. Proto-Germanic *teuhaną "to draw, pull, lead" — cf. Old English tēon "to draw, pull, lead", Old High German ziohan (→ German ziehen "to pull"), Dutch tuigen.
3. Proto-Germanic *tauh-maz "a pulling; a team of draft animals" — a nominal formation on the same root.
4. Old English tēam "family, lineage, progeny; a set of draft animals yoked together" — the "bringing forth" and the "pulling together" fused in one word.
5. Middle English teme.
6. English team — modern form; the sporting "team" (19th c.) extends the yoking to people, whence teammate, teamwork, to team up.
7. English team + -ster (agent suffix, from Old English -estre, originally feminine — spinster, songster — generalized to masculine agents — gamester, trickster) → teamster (1777, "one who drives a team"); plural teamsters — the note's word.
Note: the name then institutionalized in the International Brotherhood of Teamsters (founded 1903), the great American freight and trucking union, which today gives the word its dominant modern sense. Greek "to lead" (ἄγω, ágō) and Sanskrit nayati come from different roots (*h₂eǵ-, *ney-) and are not cognates.
