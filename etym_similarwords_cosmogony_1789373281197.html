• cosmology: Similar because both investigate the universe; differs because cosmogony focuses strictly and specifically on the moment, origin, and evolutionary birth/creation of the cosmos, whereas cosmology is the broader philosophical or scientific study of the universe's overall structure, geometry, physical laws, and ultimate fate.
  • creationism: Similar because both address how the universe and life came into existence; differs because creationism specifically denotes religious doctrine asserting that the universe was brought forth by a literal divine creator as described in sacred scripture, whereas cosmogony encompasses any mythological, philosophical, literary, or astrophysical model of cosmic origins.
  • theogony: Similar because in ancient polytheistic civilizations the birth of the world and the birth of the gods were inextricably linked; differs because a theogony describes specifically the generation and genealogy of deities (e.g., Hesiod's Theogony), whereas a cosmogony charts the birth of the physical cosmos itself.
  • genesis: Similar because both signify foundational origin, beginning, or generation; differs because genesis can refer to the birth or inception of anything (an idea, a political movement, a disease), whereas cosmogony is restricted strictly to the creation of the cosmos or a world-scale literary system.
  • ontology: Similar because both are major philosophical inquiries into the nature of existence; differs because ontology examines the metaphysical nature of being and reality as such, rather than the chronological narrative or physical mechanism of the universe's emergence.
  • mythos: Similar because many traditional cosmogonies take the narrative form of sacred myth; differs because a mythos is an overarching cultural belief system or body of interconnected folklore, whereas a cosmogony is specifically the origin story of the universe within that system.
