- 17th century (1660s) - Borrowed into English from Greek *stochastikos* in philosophical treatises meaning "conjectural, capable of aiming or guessing"; applied to rhetorical argumentation and inductive reasoning.
- Early 20th century (1910s–1930s) - Adopted by German and Russian mathematicians (Bortkiewicz, Kolmogorov) as a formal technical term for probabilistic sequences and random variables evolving over time.
- Mid-to-late 20th century - Solidified as a cornerstone term across applied mathematics, physics, econometrics, and computational fluid dynamics to contrast against "deterministic" systems.
- 21st century - Ubiquitous across computer science and artificial intelligence (notably Stochastic Gradient Descent and LLM statistical modeling), denoting any system relying on pseudo-random sampling or statistical probability.
