FORTHRIGHTLY is a native English compound and derivative: the adverb suffix -ly attached to the compound adjective-adverb "forthright," which is itself a welded compound of "forth" + "right," each element of independent Indo-European ancestry. The two root chains are given in isolation below, then joined at the compound, followed by the derivation of the -ly suffix. CHAIN 1 — the "forth" element: 1. Proto-Indo-European *per- "forward, through, in front, before" (reconstructed; projected c. 4500–3000 BCE; the great root of "forward" — cognates: Sanskrit prá "forward, on," Greek pró "before, forward," Latin per "through," English for-, fore, far) — the root of forward motion and position. 2. Proto-Germanic *furþa- / *fer- "forward, forth" (reconstructed; from the zero-grade of *per- with the dental suffix, cf. German fort "away, forth," Gothic faúr "for, before," Old Norse fram "forward") — the Germanic adverb of forward motion. 3. Old English forþ "forth, forward, onward, away" (attested from the earliest Old English texts) — the direct ancestor of modern "forth," continuing the Germanic form. 4. English forth "forward, onward, out into view" (modern; survives in "and so forth," "back and forth," "bring forth," "set forth") — the first element of the compound. CHAIN 2 — the "right" element: 1. Proto-Indo-European *h₃reǵ- "to move in a straight line, to direct, to rule" (reconstructed; projected c. 4500–3000 BCE; the great root of "straight" and "rule" — cognates: Latin regere "to rule, to keep straight" and rēctus "straight, right," Sanskrit rāj- "king" and ṛjú- "straight," Greek oregein "to stretch out") — the root from which both "right" and "rule" descend. 2. Proto-Germanic *rehtaz "straight, right, just, correct" (reconstructed; the participle form of the root, cf. Gothic raihts, Old Norse réttr, Old Saxon reht, Old High German reht "right, just") — the Germanic adjective of straightness and correctness. 3. Old English riht "straight, direct, right, just, correct; (noun) law, right, justice" (attested from the earliest Old English texts) — the direct ancestor of modern "right." 4. English right "straight, correct, proper; a moral or legal entitlement" (modern) — the second element of the compound. THE COMPOUND AND THE SUFFIX: 5. Old English forþrihte (adverb) "straight forward, directly, immediately" (attested; the welded compound forþ + riht, already present in Old English, e.g. in prose meaning "straightway, at once") — the compound is native and ancient, not borrowed. 6. Middle English forthright (adverb and adjective) "straight forward, direct, immediate; straightforward, honest" (attested from the 14th century) — the settled compound, with the modern spelling. 7. English forthright (adjective) "direct and outspoken in manner, honest and straightforward" (modern sense, fully established from the 16th century) — the adjectival base to which the adverb suffix is added. 8. English -ly — the standard English adverb suffix, from Old English -līce, the adverbial form of the adjective līc "body, form" (thus "in the manner/body of"); suffixed to "forthright," it forms the regular adverb. 9. English FORTHRIGHTLY "directly, frankly, honestly, unevasively" (adverb; attested from the 16th century onward, and standard in modern English) ◄ TARGET — the exact form of this card. NOTE ON DEBATE AND NUANCE: (1) The compound forþrihte is attested in Old English already as a single word, so the compound formation is ancient and native, not a modern coinage; the spatial sense "straight ahead, immediately" is the earliest English sense, and the moral sense "honest, outspoken" develops later, in the 15th–16th centuries. (2) The element "right" here means "straight, direct" (the sense preserved in "right line," "right angle," "right hand" as the straight side), not primarily the moral-political "right" of entitlement, though the two senses spring from the same Indo-European root. (3) The -ly suffix is a native Germanic formative (Old English -līce), not a loan; it is related to modern German -lich and to the English adjective "like." (4) The PIE reconstruction of *per- and *h₃reǵ- is standard and secure, though the exact grades and laryngeals are scholarly reconstructions rather than attested forms.
