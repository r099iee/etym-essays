• CHIMNEY: Similar because both belong to the smoke-and-hearth apparatus and carry combustion gases upward; differs because a chimney is the whole visible masonry (or metal) structure rising above the roofline, while a flue is the hidden internal shaft inside it — the bore within the gun barrel.
  • FIREPLACE: Similar as a hearth-zone term of the same domestic scene; differs because a fireplace is the entire visible assembly where the fire burns — the inclusive, warm, social word — whereas flue names only the functional internal conduit.
  • FIRESIDE: Similar as a word of the hearth; differs because a fireside is the social and domestic zone of comfort beside the fire — not a structure at all — while a flue is a measurable engineering part.
  • SMOKE-STACK: Similar because both carry gases upward and out of a building or vessel; differs because a smoke-stack is a large, plain industrial chimney on a ship or factory, visible and industrial, whereas a flue is the internal domestic or mechanical duct.
  • DUCT: Similar because both are channels that convey air or gases; differs because a duct is the general word for any air- or fluid-conveying passage (heating ducts, ventilation ducts), while a flue is specifically the smoke-and-combustion-gas channel of a fire, furnace, or boiler.
  • VENT: Similar because both allow air or gas to pass out of an enclosed space; differs because a vent is any opening for the escape of air or heat, often a simple aperture or grill, while a flue is a constructed shaft or pipe that carries the products of combustion specifically.
  • PIPE: Similar because both are tubular conduits; differs because a pipe is the general word for any tube carrying a fluid — water, gas, oil — and is usually a distinct manufactured artefact, while a flue is the built-in passageway of a chimney or furnace and is specifically for smoke and combustion gases.
  • SHAFT: Similar because both are long, narrow vertical passages; differs because a shaft is the general architectural and mining word for any vertical or inclined passage (a lift shaft, a mine shaft, a light shaft), while a flue is the smoke-conducting shaft of a fire installation specifically.
