Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*gʷʰen-
"to strike, slay, wound in combat"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE)
*gunþiz
"battle, combat, fight"
↓
Frankish / Old High German (c. 5th–8th c.)
Gundo / Gunzo / Guntio
"warrior, fighter (personal name)"
↓
Latin Compound Formation (c. 7th–9th c.)
*Gundonis curtis
"enclosed farmstead, domain, or court of Gundo"
Note: Formed from Germanic personal name Gundo + Late Latin curtis ('enclosed farmstead, agricultural estate', from Classical Latin cohors / cohortem 'enclosure').
↓
Old French (c. 11th–13th c.)
Goncort / Goncourt
"manor or estate of Gundo; toponym in Champagne/Lorraine"
↓
Early Modern French (c. 16th–18th c.)
seigneurie de Goncourt
"feudal estate in Haute-Marne; aristocratic surname Huot de Goncourt"
Note: The Huot family acquired the seigneurie of Goncourt (near Neufchâteau) in the 18th century, adopting the aristocratic title Edmond and Jules de Goncourt.
↓
Modern French (1822–1896)
Edmond et Jules de Goncourt
"pioneering naturalist novelists, diarists, and founders of the Prix Goncourt"
↓
Modern English (present)
GONCOURT
