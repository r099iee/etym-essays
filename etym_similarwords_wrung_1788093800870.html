• Squeezed: Similar because both mean to compress so as to expel contents; differs because squeezing is general pressure, while wrung adds twisting, rotary motion and greater violence.
  • Twisted: Similar because both involve rotation of a body against itself; differs because twisting is a neutral mechanical action (a rope, a lid), while wrung always aims at expulsion or damage.
  • Pressed: Similar because both mean to apply force to extract something; differs because pressing is steadily applied perpendicular force (a press, a lemon), while wrung is manual, twisting and more forceful.
  • Compressed: Similar because both describe reduction of volume under force; differs because compressed is technical and measured (gases, springs), while wrung is domestic, manual and implies extraction.
  • Strained: Similar because both can mean to separate liquid from solid by pressure; differs because straining works by passing through a filter (a sieve, a cloth held still), while wringing twists the cloth itself.
  • Extorted: Similar because both can mean to get money or a confession by pressure; differs because extortion is the legal crime of obtaining by threats, while wring covers metaphorical pressure of any kind, not only threats.
  • Wrested: Similar because both mean to obtain something by force, with a twist; differs because wresting is pulling violently away (wrest a weapon from a hand), while wringing is squeezing something out of its holder.
  • Milked: Similar because both mean to extract money or information from a source; differs because milking is gradual, repeated and often sly (milk a subscription list), while wringing is a single hard squeeze.
  • Exacted: Similar because both describe demanding something (payment, a promise) by authority or force; differs because exaction is official, formal extortion by law or power, while wringing is personal and physical in image.
  • Forced: Similar because both describe obtaining something against resistance; differs because forcing is a general verb of compulsion, while wrung keeps the vivid image of twisting pressure.
  • Throttled / strangled: Similar because both describe killing or suppressing by squeezing the neck; differs because throttling and strangling compress the windpipe to kill or silence, while wringing the neck is a twist that breaks it — and wrung also has non-lethal senses.
  • Tormented / wracked: Similar because both describe affliction of the emotions ("wrung with grief"); differs because torment and wrack emphasize the suffering itself, while wrung emphasizes the wrenching pressure imagined twisting the heart or hands.
  • Mangled: Similar because both describe brutal deformation under force; differs because mangling is tearing and crushing into ruined pieces, an accident or a machine's work, while wringing is deliberate twisting with a purpose.
  • Clasped (of hands): Similar because both describe what anxious hands do; differs because clasping is merely holding or gripping, while wringing is the intense, twisting self-pressure of distress.
