• Phonemes: Similar because both are fundamental, minimal indivisible structural units of linguistic analysis; differs because phonemes are strictly units of sound that distinguish words (e.g., /p/ vs. /b/) but possess no inherent meaning of their own, whereas morphemes are the smallest units that carry intrinsic lexical or grammatical meaning.
  • Lexemes: Similar because both represent meaningful units in the vocabulary of a language; differs because a lexeme is an abstract headword or dictionary entry encompassing all of its inflectional forms (e.g., the lexeme RUN includes run, runs, ran, running), whereas morphemes are the concrete component parts that construct those forms.
  • Graphemes: Similar because both serve as baseline structural units in communication; differs because graphemes are the smallest functional units of a written orthographic writing system (letters, characters, punctuation), whereas morphemes are abstract units of linguistic grammar and meaning.
  • Words: Similar because both carry semantic meaning and syntactic function; differs because a word is a freestanding phonological and syntactic unit that may be composed of a single morpheme (book) or a composite chain of multiple morphemes (book-keep-er-s).
  • Syllables: Similar because both segment speech sounds into structural chunks; differs because syllables are units of phonological organization determined by vowel nuclei and acoustic sonority, bearing no inherent correlation to meaning or grammar (e.g., wa-ter has two syllables but is a single morpheme).
  • Sememes: Similar because both operate at the semantic level of language; differs because a sememe is an atom of semantic meaning itself (a conceptual feature), whereas a morpheme is the actual morphological linguistic form that encodes that meaning.
  • Allomorphs: Similar because both deal with morphological realization; differs because an allomorph is a specific contextual phonetic variant of a particular underlying morpheme (such as the three phonetic shapes of the English plural).
