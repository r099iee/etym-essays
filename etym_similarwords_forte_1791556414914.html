• Specialty: Similar because both refer to a distinct field in which one possesses superior skill; differs because specialty often denotes formal professional training, certification, or commercial focus (e.g., medical specialty), while forte emphasizes innate aptitude and effortless personal mastery.
  • Metier: Similar because both describe an activity suited to one's talents; differs because metier (borrowed from French) specifically refers to one's chosen lifelong trade, craft, profession, or authentic calling, whereas forte can apply to any isolated skill, hobby, or social grace.
  • Strong suit: Similar because both denote an area of primary competence; differs because strong suit is an informal, vivid idiom derived from card games (such as bridge or whist), carrying a more colloquial, pragmatic tone than the literary forte.
  • Calling: Similar because both highlight an area where a person naturally shines; differs because calling implies a spiritual, moral, or vocational summons to a life's purpose (often involving sacrifice), while forte simply denotes an area of sharp capability.
  • Talent: Similar because both involve exceptional ability; differs because talent is the underlying raw, innate biological capacity or gift, whereas forte is the specific practical arena or application where that strength is manifest.
  • Long suit: Similar because both signify an asset or superior feature; differs because long suit is a commercial and idiomatic card-playing metaphor emphasizing abundance of resources, whereas forte highlights mechanical leverage and structural strength.
  • Foible: Contrasting partner; differs because foible denotes a minor weakness, failing, or eccentric vulnerability of character, serving as the direct structural and historical antithesis to forte.
