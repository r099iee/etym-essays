- Old–Middle English stink as strong smell, sweet or foul - neutral sensory perception; stincan covers incense and carrion alike
- 1500s–1600s stink narrows to foul odour - disgusted perception; stinker emerges for chimneys, pipes, and privies that offend the nose
- 1800s school, sport, and stage slang - jeering perception; a stinker is a hard question, bad match, or flopped play that stinks
- Early 1900s Wodehousian moral-comic stinker - amused-indignant perception; friends, aunts, rivals, and love-affairs come stinkers when they behave rottingly
- Today broad informal pejorative - playful to scathing perception; from lucky old stinker (affectionate) to rotten stinker (venomous), plus stinker of a cold, exam, or film
