Persian / Middle Persian (c. 6th–9th c. CE)
saqirlāt / saqalāt
"rich, costly woollen or silk cloth ornamented with patterns"
Note: Cognate with Arabic siqillāt ('fine luxury fabric') and Byzantine Greek sigillāton ('cloth stamped with seal-like figures', from Latin sigillum 'seal').
↓
Medieval Latin (c. 11th–12th c.)
scarlatum / scarletum
"costly luxury broadcloth, precious textile"
↓
Old French (c. 1150–1200)
escarlate
"fine, expensive woollen cloth; bright red luxury cloth"
↓
Middle English (c. 1250–1300)
scarlat / scarlet / skerlet
"costly luxury cloth; brilliant orange-red color"
Note: By 1350, the term shifted completely from the weave of the textile to the rich red hue produced by kermes dye.
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
scarlet
"vivid fiery red hue; symbol of clerical office or sin"
