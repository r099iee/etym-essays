Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*gʷʰren- / *bʰren-
"mind, diaphragm, seat of emotions"

Ancient Greek (c. 500 BCE)
φρήν (phrēn) → φρενῖτις (phrenītis)
"mind; delirium, brain fever"
↓
Late Latin (c. 300 CE)
phreneticus
"delirious, mad, mentally afflicted"
↓
Old French (c. 1200 CE)
frenetike / frentike
"frenzied, raving, insane"
↓
Middle English (c. 1375 CE)
frentik / frantic
"wildly excited, raving mad"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550 CE)
frantickly / frantically
"in a wild, mad, frenzied manner"
↓
Modern English (Attested Adverb)
frantically
"with wild, desperate panic and haste"
