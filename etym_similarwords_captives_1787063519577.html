• Prisoners: Similar because both designate individuals held in confinement under involuntary restraint; differs because prisoner is the standard modern legal and correctional term for anyone incarcerated under state law, whereas captives retains classical, martial, hostage, or predatory connotations.
  • Hostages: Similar because both are held against their will by an opposing party; differs because hostages are explicitly seized and held as conditional leverage or bargaining chips to force a third party to meet specific demands.
  • Detainees: Similar because both refer to persons deprived of liberty; differs because detainee is a sterile, modern legal and administrative euphemism often used for individuals held without formal criminal charges in military or immigration custody.
  • Slaves: Similar because captives historically were frequently enslaved; differs because slave defines a permanent legal condition of chattel property and forced labor, whereas captive describes the immediate state of seizure and custody.
  • Inmates: Similar because both live in confinement; differs because inmate specifically refers to permanent or long-term residents of an institution such as a penitentiary, asylum, or hospital.
  • Thralls: Similar because both denote persons under total subjugation; differs because thrall is an archaic Germanic term emphasizing complete spiritual, moral, or serf-like bondage to a master or passion.
