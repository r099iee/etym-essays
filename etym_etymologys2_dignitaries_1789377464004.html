PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*dek-
"to take, accept, receive; to show respect, be fitting, acceptable"
Note: Fertile root generating Latin decēre ('to be fitting') and dignus ('worthy, fitting').
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
dignus -> dignitās
"worthy, fitting -> worthiness, civic merit, high office, honor"
↓
Late / Imperial Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
dignitārius
"person holding high official office, ecclesiastical prelate"
Note: Formed from dignitās + agent suffix -ārius. Preserved in canon law for cathedral chapter leaders.
↓
Old French (c. 13th–14th c.)
dignitaire
"holder of an ecclesiastical or royal dignity or high rank"
↓
Late Middle English (c. 1380–1420)
dignitarie
"high-ranking church official holding a benefice"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th–17th c.)
dignitary (plural: dignitaries)
"person of exalted rank or title in church, state, or court"
↓
Modern English (present)
