- Shaking with fear - Since Middle English (from French trembler, 13th century) trembling has named involuntary shaking driven by fear, cold, or excitement, a plain verb of bodily agitation.
- From fright to fine vibration - While 'trembling with fright' remains the core, the word broadens to any fine, continuous quivering — of leaves, hands, voices, or excitement — losing some of its exclusive terror in the 18th-19th centuries.
- Modern everyday use - Today trembling is a neutral, vivid word for fine shaking, at home in children's literature and ordinary narrative, while clinical registers prefer tremor or tremulousness.
