- Late Roman Empire - Constantine (c. 272-337) legalized and favoured Christianity after the Milvian Bridge, convened the Council of Nicaea in 325, and refounded Byzantium as Constantinople in 330, so his name entered history as the first Christian emperor.
- Christian tradition - in the eastern church he was honoured as the equal of the Apostles, the founder of a Christian empire, while in later Reformation and Enlightenment debate his reign was scrutinized as the moment church and state fused.
- Gibbon's and Macaulay's usage - Gibbon and Macaulay made him, with Diocletian, a double synecdoche for the splendour of the late imperial court that still exhibited itself in the Eastern provinces.
- Contemporary sense - Constantine denotes the Christianization of Rome and the foundation of Byzantium, and his family names (Constans, Constantius) plus the city-name Constantinople keep his name across the map of history.
