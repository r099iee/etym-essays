• teaching: Similar because both name instruction of learners; differs because teaching is the plain everyday act, while pedagogy adds theory, system, and professional self-consciousness.
  • instruction: Similar because both deliver knowledge and skill; differs because instruction stresses directedtelling and training, often top-down, while pedagogy includes ethos and learner agency.
  • education: Similar because both concern formation of minds; differs because education is the whole enterprise and its outcomes, while pedagogy is specifically the method of guiding it.
  • didactics: Similar because both theorise how to teach; differs because didactics is the Continental-European science of lesson design, more technical-curricular than Anglo-American pedagogy.
  • andragogy: Similar because both name the guidance of learners (-agogy); differs because andragogy is reserved for adult learners, while pedagogy prototypically concerns children and schooling.
  • tutelage: Similar because both mean guidance by a teacher; differs because tutelage stresses guardianship and personal oversight, often one-to-one.
  • coaching: Similar because both improve performance through guidance; differs because coaching targets skills and results (sport, executives), while pedagogy targets understanding and formation.
  • mentoring: Similar because both guide a junior by a senior; differs because mentoring is relational and long-term wisdom-sharing, not classroom method.
  • schooling: Similar because both institutionalise learning; differs because schooling names attendance and socialisation, which may occur with poor pedagogy.
  • training: Similar because both build competence by practice; differs because training drills repeatable skills, while pedagogy aims at judgment and comprehension.
  • upbringing: Similar because both form the young; differs because upbringing is familial-moral formation, not professional instruction.
  • edification: Similar because both improve minds morally; differs because edification is literary-formal uplift, now slightly archaic, not method.
