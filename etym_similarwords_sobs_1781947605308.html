• Tears: Similar because both accompany weeping; differs because tears names the liquid only and need not imply sound, while sobs insists on the respiratory spasm.
  • Weeping: Similar in grief and crying; differs because weeping is a broader activity — one may weep quietly without sobbing — and it names the act rather than the sound.
  • Crying: Similar in the activity of weeping; differs because crying is the most general verb, covering silent tears, loud wailing, and even the cry of a baby, whereas sobs names one specific convulsive sound.
  • Wails: Similar in loud, audible grieving; differs because wails are sustained, high-pitched outcries, often of rage or public mourning, while sobs are broken, inward, and convulsive.
  • Howls: Similar in intense, loud distress; differs because howls are long, unrestrained vocalizations, closer to animal cries, whereas sobs are short, caught, and humanly broken.
  • Whimpers: Similar in soft, distressed sounds; differs because whimpers are weak, thin, nasal cries of fear or pain, while sobs are stronger, chest-driven convulsions.
  • Sniffles: Similar in the nasal signs of crying; differs because sniffles are mild, nasal, and cold-like, while sobs engage the chest and throat convulsively.
  • Snivelling: Similar in tearful, self-pitying crying; differs because snivelling is contemptuous and implies weak, noisy self-pity, whereas sobs is neutral and compassionate.
  • Lamentation: Similar in the expression of grief; differs because lamentation is formal, often verbal or ritual mourning, while sobs need no words at all.
  • Groans: Similar in audible sounds of distress; differs because groans are low, drawn-out sounds of pain or despair, not the broken breaths of weeping.
  • Moans: Similar in sounds of suffering; differs because moans are long, soft, low sounds, often of physical pain or pleasure, unlike the sharp convulsive sobs.
  • Sighs: Similar in audible breathing; differs because sighs are long, slow, usually quiet exhalations of relief or weariness, not the gasping inspiratory sobs.
  • Gasps: Similar in sudden audible breathing; differs because gasps are sharp intakes of air from shock, exertion, or surprise, without the tears and grief of sobs.
