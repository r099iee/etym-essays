• various: Similar because both refer to a group composed of different kinds, types, or sorts; differs because various is the neutral, modern, and standard term emphasizing qualitative difference, whereas sundry carries an archaic, formal, or literary flavor and frequently implies a miscellaneous, somewhat random collection of small or incidental items.
  • diverse: Similar because both describe multiplicity of character or form; differs because diverse emphasizes distinct contrast, radical heterogeneity, and significant structural variety (e.g., culturally diverse societies or biologically diverse ecosystems), whereas sundry typically characterizes an assortment of minor, assorted articles or people grouped together without deep structural distinction.
  • miscellaneous: Similar because both denote an assortment made up of varied parts or mixed elements that lack a single unifying classification; differs because miscellaneous is functional, technical, and standard in organizational and filing contexts (such as a miscellaneous file folder), while sundry functions both as an expressive literary adjective and historically as a plural commercial noun (sundries).
  • multifarious: Similar because both denote having great diversity and variety; differs because multifarious is an elevated Latinate adjective emphasizing immense complexity, manifold operations, and labyrinthine variety of duties or facets, whereas sundry is earthy, Anglo-Saxon in origin, and focuses on distinct, separate items counted together.
  • assorted: Similar because both characterize collections composed of different varieties; differs because assorted often implies intentional selection, packaging, or culinary arrangement (such as assorted chocolates or assorted biscuits), while sundry implies an accidental, incidental, or naturally occurring mixture.
  • manifold: Similar because both denote many and varied elements; differs because manifold stresses abundance, layered multiplicity, and many interrelated aspects or manifestations (e.g., manifold blessings or manifold complications), whereas sundry emphasizes that the items are individually separate and distinct from one another.
  • heterogeneous: Similar because both describe mixtures consisting of dissimilar components; differs because heterogeneous is a formal scientific, demographic, or philosophical term denoting fundamental differences in composition, kind, or nature, whereas sundry is informal, literary, and descriptive.
  • disparate: Similar because both characterize items that are different from each other; differs because disparate emphasizes irreconcilable difference, fundamental incongruity, or inability to compare elements fairly, whereas sundry easily harmonizes disparate elements into an amicable, collective gathering.
  • several: Similar because historically several and sundry were near-identical synonyms deriving from concepts of separation; differs because modern several functions primarily as an indefinite numeral quantifier denoting a small number (three or more), whereas sundry highlights diversity and miscellaneous character rather than numerical quantity.
