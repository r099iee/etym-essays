• Saxons: Similar because both are West Germanic confederations of the early Middle Ages who gave their names to major lands (Saxony, England) and figure in Gibbon as neighbors and rivals of the Franks; differs because the Saxons are remembered chiefly as sea-borne raiders and settlers of Britain, while the Franks conquered Roman Gaul and built the imperial dynasty of Charlemagne.
  • Goths: Similar because both are great Germanic peoples who founded powerful successor kingdoms in the former Roman West (Visigothic Spain, Ostrogothic Italy); differs because the Goths were Arian Christians and were crushed or absorbed by the Byzantines and Franks, while the Franks converted to Catholic Christianity and their kingdom endured to become France.
  • Vandals: Similar because both are Germanic peoples who crossed the Roman frontiers and founded kingdoms in the West; differs because the Vandals are remembered for the sack of Rome (455) and for a kingdom in North Africa destroyed by Justinian, while the Franks are remembered as founders of a lasting empire.
  • Burgundians: Similar because both are Germanic peoples who settled in Roman Gaul and gave their name to a land (Burgundy); differs because the Burgundians were absorbed into the Frankish kingdom in the 6th century and never produced a lasting empire, while the Franks absorbed them.
  • Alemanni: Similar because both are Germanic confederations on the Roman frontier of the Rhine region, and the Alemanni also gave a name (Allemagne) to Germany in French; differs because the Alemanni never conquered Gaul and were subdued by the Franks and the Carolingians, while the Franks became the rulers of Gaul.
  • Suebi / Suevi: Similar because both are Germanic tribal groups of the migration period who crossed into the Roman world; differs because the Suebi are a looser and more shadowy grouping (later known for the Suebian kingdom in Galicia), while the Franks have a continuous political history and a named empire.
  • Lombards: Similar because both are Germanic peoples who founded kingdoms inside the former Roman world (Lombard Italy); differs because the Lombards were conquered by Charlemagne himself and their name survives only in the region Lombardy, while the Franks' name survives in both France and the adjective frank.
  • Angles: Similar because both are Germanic peoples of the migration period who gave their name to a land (England), and the Angles are paired with the Franks in Gibbon's insular geography; differs because the Angles crossed to Britain and became Anglo-Saxons, while the Franks remained on the Continent and became the French.
  • Jutes: Similar because both are Germanic peoples who participated in the settlement of Britain in the 5th century; differs because the Jutes were a small group from Jutland, historically minor, while the Franks were a major power who conquered Gaul.
  • Latins / Franj: Similar because both name the Western European Christians in the medieval Islamic and Byzantine world, and in that register "Franks" (faranji) is used interchangeably with "Latins"; differs because Latins stresses the Roman Catholic liturgical language and Roman inheritance, while Franks is the specific Germanic ethnonym generalized to all Westerners.
  • Germans: Similar because both label the Germanic peoples and, in some modern usage, "the Franks" are subsumed under "the Germans"; differs because Germans is a modern national and linguistic term for the inhabitants of Germany and German-speaking Europe, while Franks names a specific historical confederation whose legacy is France as much as Germany.
