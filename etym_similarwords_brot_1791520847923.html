• Bread: the exact English cognate, from the same Proto-Germanic noun; similar because it denotes the same baked staple and shares the biblical and economic idioms (“daily bread”, “breadwinner”); differs because English bread has largely lost the sense of “livelihood” except in compounds, while German Brot still forms Brotberuf and Brotgeber productively.
  • Laib: a whole loaf, especially of bread; similar because it names the same baked object and is often used as Brotlaib; differs because it is a measure-and-shape word, not the substance, and is archaic outside the compound.
  • Brotlaib: a whole loaf of bread; similar because it is the explicit compound of Brot with the loaf-word and is the ordinary term in baking and in fairy tales; differs because it counts individual loaves and cannot mean bread as a mass or as a livelihood.
  • Brötchen: a bread roll; similar because it is the diminutive of Brot and the standard morning bakery item; differs because it denotes a small individual roll, and its regional synonyms (Semmel, Schrippe, Weck) map German dialect geography.
  • Semmel: a white bread roll, especially in southern Germany and Austria; similar because it is a small bread and often translates Brötchen; differs because it is regionally marked and traditionally made from wheat flour, with a crisp crust.
  • Schrippe: a bread roll in Berlin and eastern Germany; similar because it is the local equivalent of Brötchen; differs because it is dialectally specific and carries a strong regional identity.
  • Toast: sliced bread browned by heat, or the appliance that browns it; similar because it is bread prepared for eating and appears at breakfast; differs because it names a preparation and a method, is a loanword from English, and has no livelihood or religious senses.
  • Gebäck: baked goods collectively, especially pastries and biscuits; similar because it is the general category of oven-baked flour products that includes bread; differs because it usually excludes ordinary bread and implies sweet or small baked items.
  • Fladen: a flat cake or flatbread; similar because it is an ancient form of bread and appears in Fladenbrot; differs because it denotes a flat, unleavened or lightly leavened shape, not bread in general.
  • Lebensunterhalt: livelihood, means of subsistence; similar because it translates the economic sense of Brot in sein Brot verdienen; differs because it is a formal compound of Leben and Unterhalt, neutral and administrative, with no image of food.
  • Auskommen: a sufficient income, a livelihood; similar because it names what one earns to live on and often glosses Brot in the phrase sein Auskommen haben; differs because it stresses sufficiency and modest comfort rather than the bare Staff of Life.
  • Kohle: money, “dough”; similar because it is colloquial currency slang and overlaps with the slang use of Brot as earnings; differs because it is much more common in that sense, more informal, and its image is coal, not food.
