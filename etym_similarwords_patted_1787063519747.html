• tapped: Similar because both mean to strike lightly; differs because tapping is a short, sharp, usually repeated touch with fingertips or an object (to get attention), while patting is a softer, flat-palmed, affectionate stroke.
  • touched: Similar because both involve brief physical contact; differs because touching is general and neutral, while patting specifies a light repeated tap of the open hand.
  • stroked: Similar because both are gentle affectionate touches; differs because stroking is a continuous sliding motion along a surface, while patting is a series of light taps.
  • petted: Similar because both are affectionate touches of animals; differs because petting is general fondling and stroking, while patting is the specific light tapping gesture.
  • tapped (on the back): Similar because both describe a light congratulatory touch; differs because "tapped on the back" emphasises the short sound and is rarer as an idiom than "patted on the back" for praise.
  • caressed: Similar because both are tender touches; differs because caressing is soft, lingering, and romantic, while patting is brief, brisk, and often practical or approving.
  • slapped: Similar because both are blows of the open hand; differs because slapping is hard and violent (or strongly negative), while patting is gentle and positive.
  • clapped: Similar because both are light impacts of the hand; differs because clapping is a loud sound-making gesture (applause) or a strike, not a tender touch.
  • dabbed: Similar because both involve light pressing touches; differs because dabbing is a single quick wiping or pressing motion (with cloth or finger), while patting is repeated and soothing.
  • flattened / pressed: Similar because both describe shaping by hand pressure; differs because these are practical, heavier pressures, whereas patting shapes lightly and gently.
  • reassured / congratulated: Similar because both express comfort or approval; differs because these name the abstract intent, while "patted" describes the physical gesture that carries it.
