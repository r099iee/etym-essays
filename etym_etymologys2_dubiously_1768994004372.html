dubiously — from Latin duo "two"; the etymology is the numeral, not an earlier "doubt" root. Stage 1 — PIE: *dwóh₁ / *dwo- "two" (one of the most stable numerals in the family, surviving in nearly every branch; attested in Sanskrit dvá, Greek dúo, Latin duo, and others). Stage 2 — Latin: duo "two" (attested from the earliest Latin records, 6th–3rd centuries BCE). Stage 3 — Latin: dubius "wavering, uncertain, doubtful" — literally "of two minds," formed on duō "two" + -bius "having" (attested from the 1st century BCE). NOTE: some scholars prefer a formation *du-bh- "going two ways" from a root sense "twofold"; the precise suffixal anatomy is debated, but the "two" connection is not. Stage 4 — Latin: dubiōsus "full of doubt, doubtful" (attested Late Latin / classical sources; the adjective built on dubium, "a doubt"). Stage 5 — English: dubious "of doubtful worth, wavering" (borrowed directly from Latin in the mid-16th century). Stage 6 — English: dubiously "in a doubtful manner" (adverb formed with the suffix -ly, attested from the late 16th century). NOTE ON THE PARALLEL: English also has doubt, borrowed through Old French douter from Latin dubitāre, "to doubt, hesitate, waver" (the denominative verb of dubium); dubious/dubiously are the learned direct-from-Latin doublets of the everyday French-derived doubt.
