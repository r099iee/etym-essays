- 15th–16th Century - Derived from Medieval Latin *obituarius* ('pertaining to death'), referring to monastic death registers and commemorative anniversary masses for the dead (*obits*).
- 18th Century - Shifted with the rise of print newspapers into published biographical death notices of prominent public figures.
- 19th Century (Victorian Era) - Developed into a vital journalistic genre, chronicling the moral character, social ancestry, and public achievements of deceased citizens.
- 20th–21st Century - Institutionalized as a celebrated literary art form in major newspapers, and extended metaphorically to describe the demise of technologies, movements, and cultural eras.
