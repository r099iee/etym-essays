• Golden chain / golden rain: Similar because these are the everyday aliases for the same tree, built on the same drooping-yellow-racemes image; differs because they are folk descriptive names, whereas laburnum is the Latin-derived botanical name.
  • Wisteria: Similar because it produces long, dense, pendulous racemes of papilionaceous (pea-type) flowers, the closest visual analogue in gardens; differs because it is a twining climber, not a free-standing tree, the flowers are usually purple, lilac, or white rather than yellow, and it belongs to a different genus of the same family.
  • Broom (Cytisus / Genista): Similar because it is a yellow-flowered member of the pea family with the same warm golden splash in late spring; differs because broom is a low, wiry shrub with scattered, often single flowers rather than dense hanging chains, and it does not form a tree trunk.
  • Gorse / whin (Ulex): Similar because of the intense yellow pea-flowers that light up the landscape; differs because gorse is a fiercely spiny, evergreen, low shrub of heaths and commons, with a strong coconut scent, and its flowers are not arranged in hanging racemes.
  • Robinia / false acacia (Robinia pseudoacacia): Similar because it is a legume tree that hangs white pea-flowers in long racemes; differs because the flowers are white, the racemes shorter and less densely golden, the bark is rough and deeply furrowed, and it is a much larger tree.
  • Mimosa / silver wattle (Acacia dealbata): Similar because it is an ornamental yellow-flowered tree used for spring colour; differs because its flowers are tiny fluffy balls in clusters rather than pea-flowers in chains, it is an evergreen subfamily-Mimosoideae tree from Australia and the Mediterranean, and it is not toxic in the laburnum way.
  • Lilac (Syringa): Similar because it carries showy conical clusters of fragrant flowers on a garden tree; differs because lilac's flowers are usually purple/white, borne in upright panicles rather than hanging racemes, and it belongs to the olive family, not the pea family.
  • Horse-chestnut (Aesculus): Similar because it is a park tree famous for an upright candle-like mass of spring flowers; differs because the flower candles stand erect instead of hanging, the flowers are white with yellow/pink blotches, and it is not a legume.
  • Lupin (Lupinus): Similar because it produces spikes of pea-flowers and is a classic member of the yellow-flowered Fabaceae; differs because it is a herbaceous plant or low shrub, its flowers form upright spikes, not hanging chains, and it is not a tree.
  • Judas tree (Cercis): Similar because it is a small legume tree that blooms early with pea-flowers; differs because its pink-to-magenta flowers appear along the bare twigs and trunk rather than in hanging yellow chains.
  • Yellow-blooming ornamental viburnum / forsythia: Similar because both give a massed yellow spring display in gardens; differs because forsythia's yellow is a scattering of four-petalled flowers along arching stems, not a chain of pea-flowers, and it is a shrub, not a tree of the pea family.
  • 'Vossii' laburnum (Laburnum x watereri): Similar because it is literally a cultivated laburnum with the same core identity; differs because it is a selected hybrid clone bred for exceptionally long, sterile racemes, prized in horticulture over the wild species.
