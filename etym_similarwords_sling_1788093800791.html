• Bandage: Similar because both are cloth supports applied to an injured limb; differs because a bandage wraps directly around a wound for pressure, protection and dressing-holding, whereas a sling suspends the whole limb from the neck to rest it — protection of the wound versus support of the limb.
  • Splint: Similar because both immobilise an injured limb; differs because a splint is a rigid board or mould keeping a fracture immobile, while a sling merely cradles the arm and takes its weight without rigidity.
  • Brace: Similar because both support joints or limbs during recovery; differs because a brace is a structured, often hinged orthopaedic device, whereas a sling is a soft cloth cradle with no framework.
  • Strap: Similar because both are narrow strips of material used for carrying or securing; differs because a strap is the generic flat fastener (shoulder strap, buckle strap) without a hanging-basket function, while a sling is arranged as a closed loop or cradle.
  • Harness: Similar because both bear weight across the body; differs because a harness is an engineered multi-strap frame (climbing, canine, equine, safety) with buckles and load distribution, whereas a sling is a minimal single loop or cradle.
  • Thong: Similar because both are leather strips and a thong can serve as a crude sling; differs because a thong is simply the strip itself, with no pouch or whirling mechanism, and today names footwear and swimwear.
  • Catapult: Similar because both are field weapons that hurl projectiles; differs because a catapult is a mechanical device — a forked stick with elastic (US slingshot) or a siege engine (mangonel, trebuchet) — whereas the sling is a hand-held strap whirled by muscle power.
  • Fling: Similar because both verbs mean to throw with sharp force; differs because flinging suggests wild, careless, whole-body throwing (fling oneself down, fling open the door), while slinging implies a smooth swinging release, often from a hanging or swinging motion.
  • Hurl: Similar because both mean violent projection; differs because hurling is heavier, angrier and less precise (hurl abuse, hurl a spear), while slinging emphasises the swing-and-release technique and can be casual.
  • Lob: Similar because both are throwing verbs; differs because a lob is a gentle, high, arcing throw (tennis, cricket), the opposite of the sling's flat, forceful trajectory.
  • Toss: Similar because both are throwing verbs; differs because a toss is short, light and near at hand (toss a coin, toss the salad), whereas slinging implies greater distance, force and a swinging arm.
  • Cast: Similar because both can mean to throw with a sweeping motion; differs because cast is formal, archaic or specialised (casting a net, a line, a vote, a spell), while sling remains physical, modern and colloquial.
  • Pitch: Similar because both mean to throw; differs because pitching is a targeted ball-delivery (cricket, baseball) whose point is accuracy rather than force, and it is never used of swinging straps or hanging loads.
