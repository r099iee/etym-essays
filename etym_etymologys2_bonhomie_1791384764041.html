Note: Latin bonus derives from Old Latin duenos (demonstrated on the famous 6th-c. BCE Duenos inscription), with regular Latin phonetic shift *dw- -> *b- (compare bellum from *duellum). Latin homō derives from the PIE root for earth *dʰéǵʰōm, literally designating an earth-born mortal (the same root behind English human, humble, and bridegroom). In medieval France, bonhomme was originally a patronizing feudal designation for the rustic peasant ("Jacques Bonhomme"), but softened into an affectionate term for an honest, simple fellow, which was then nominalized with the abstract suffix -ie into bonhomie.
