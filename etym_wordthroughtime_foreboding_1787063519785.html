- c. 1600 - 'a presentiment, especially of evil' - born from 'forebode', the verb 'to announce evil beforehand' - grave, bookish register
- 17th-18th c. - the feeling itself takes over from the message - meaning drifts inward from 'the omen' to 'the dread the omen causes' - subjectified, psychological
- 19th c. - Gothic and Victorian suspense staple - an atmosphere of vague coming doom in Dickens, ghost stories, sensation fiction
- 20th c. - fantasy and horror favourite (Tolkien, Lovecraft) - portentous, uncanny colour; the word names dread before the monster appears
- Today - still literary and journalistic-narrative, rare in casual talk - keeps its singular precision: dread with no definite object
