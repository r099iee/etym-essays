• Arrears: Similar because it is the same word in its modern plural dress, meaning overdue debts; differs because arrears is the living everyday form (in arrears), while the singular arrear is now formal or archaic.
  • Debt: Similar because both name money owed to another; differs because debt covers all owing, punctual or not, while arrear means specifically the part gone overdue.
  • Arrearage: Similar because both name accumulated unpaid sums from the same root; differs because arrearage stresses the whole massed backlog, while arrear can name a single outstanding item.
  • Balance due: Similar because both mean an amount still to be paid; differs because balance due is neutral bookkeeping (it may not be late), while arrear always implies the due date has passed.
  • Outstanding payment: Similar because both describe money not yet handed over; differs because outstanding is polite commercial euphemism, while arrear carries the sterner note of default.
  • Liability: Similar because both are obligations weighing on the debtor; differs because liability is the lawyer’s abstract term for any legal responsibility, while arrear is the concrete unpaid sum.
  • Deficit: Similar because both describe a shortfall in accounts; differs because deficit is the gap between income and spending in a budget, while arrear is a particular debt gone past due.
  • Default: Similar because both belong to the vocabulary of failing to pay; differs because default names the act or event of non-payment, while arrear names the money itself.
  • Back payment: Similar because both mean money paid late for an earlier period; differs because back payment looks backward from the moment of settling, while arrear names the debt while it still stands unpaid.
