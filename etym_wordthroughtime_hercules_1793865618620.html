- Classical Antiquity [Divine Cult, Monster-Slayer & Patron of Athletes] - Replaced older local heroes to become the pan-Hellenic savior demigod (Herakles), worshipped in temples, celebrated in gymnasia, and invoked in everyday Roman oaths (*Mehercle!*).
- Middle Ages [Moral Allegory of Spiritual Fortitude] - Reinterpreted through Christian mythography and Boethian philosophy as a moral allegory: his slaying of monsters represented the soul's triumph over sinful vices, and his labors prefigured Christ hallowing the harrowing of Hell.
- Renaissance & Early Modern [Emblem of Sovereign Majesty & Humanist Reason] - Adopted as the personal heraldic patron of European monarchs (e.g., the 'Gallic Hercules') symbolizing royal authority, eloquence, civic strength, and the triumph of intellect over bestial barbarism.
- 19th c.–Present [Colloquial Epithet for Supreme Brawn & Pop-Culture Hero] - Shifted into an ubiquitous figurative noun for an exceptionally muscular person, an adjective for monumental undertakings ('herculean task'), and an enduring global pop-culture superhero across comics, film, and literature.
