- Unfit and absurd in Latin - ineptus as tasteless, silly, out of place in conduct and speech, a social aesthetic judgement
- Borrowed learned insult - 17th-century English inept for foolish, senseless, unsuitable remarks and persons, formal and rare
- Generalised clumsiness - 19th-century extension to unhandy, bungling work and persons, the incompetence sense takes over
- Social and political workhorse - 20th-century socially inept, politically inept as standard diagnoses of awkwardness and misrule
- Ironic intensifier today - wonderfully inept, gloriously inept used with amused affection as well as censure
