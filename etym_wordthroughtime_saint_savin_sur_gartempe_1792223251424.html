- 5th c. CE (Late Antiquity) — Martyrdom of Saint Savinus and Saint Cyprianus on the banks of the Gartempe River following their flight from religious persecution.
- c. 800 CE (Carolingian Empire) — Charlemagne and Louis the Pious endorse the foundation of a Benedictine abbey over the martyrs' relics, naming the settlement in honor of Saint Savin.
- c. 1060–1115 (High Middle Ages / Romanesque Era) — Construction of the present hall church under Abbots Odon and Gervais; master painters execute the monumental Old Testament fresco cycle across the nave vault.
- 16th c. (French Wars of Religion) — The abbey suffers devastating attacks by Huguenot forces in 1562 and 1568; the monastic dormitories are vandalized, and the church begins a long period of decay.
- 1835–1845 (19th c. Rediscovery) — Prosper Mérimée discovers the endangered frescoes and halts their destruction, directing architect Félix Duban and painter Paul Chenavard to carry out meticulous restoration.
- 1983 to Present — Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site, recognized worldwide as the supreme surviving monument of Romanesque mural painting.
