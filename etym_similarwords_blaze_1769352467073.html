• fire: Similar because both name burning combustion; differs because fire is the general, neutral word for the phenomenon, while blaze emphasises size, fierceness, and brilliant light.
  • flame: Similar because both name burning gas seen as fire; differs because a flame is a single tongue or body of burning gas, while a blaze is the whole bright conflagration of flame plus its radiance.
  • conflagration: Similar because both are large fires; differs because conflagration implies enormous, disastrous scale and a formal register, while a blaze can be smaller yet still fiercely bright and vivid.
  • inferno: Similar because both name a huge, uncontrollable fire; differs because inferno carries hellish, apocalyptic connotations, whereas blaze is more neutral in its vividness.
  • bonfire: Similar because both are large open fires; differs because a bonfire is a controlled, celebratory outdoor fire built deliberately, while a blaze is any fiercely burning fire.
  • furnace: Similar because both imply intense heat; differs because a furnace is an enclosed, engineered chamber of heat, while a blaze is open flame.
  • flare: Similar because both are bursts of flame or light; differs because a flare is brief and localised, while a blaze is sustained and extensive.
  • flash: Similar because both are sudden bright light; differs because a flash is instantaneous and nearly duration-less, while a blaze endures.
  • glow: Similar because both are emitted light and heat; differs because a glow is soft, steady, and gentle, while a blaze is bright, hot, and active.
  • gleam: Similar because both describe shining; differs because a gleam is cool, reflected, or faint light, while a blaze is radiant heat-light.
  • spark: Similar because both are fire; differs because a spark is a tiny flying particle of fire, while a blaze is its massive, consuming form.
  • beacon: Similar because both are bright fires visible from afar; differs because a beacon is a deliberate signal or guiding fire, while a blaze is simply any fierce fire.
  • mark / notch / brand (trail sense): Similar because all are visible marks on a surface; differs because a blaze on a tree is a specific bark-stripped marker for route-finding, a notch is a cut, and a brand implies ownership by heat.
  • outburst / eruption (glory sense): Similar because both are sudden intense manifestations; differs because a blaze of glory emphasises brilliant, dazzling display, while an outburst emphasises release of emotion.
