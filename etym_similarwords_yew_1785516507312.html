• Cypress: Similar because it is an evergreen conifer steeped in graveyard and mourning symbolism; differs because cypress is a resinous, cone-bearing Mediterranean funeral tree, whereas the yew bears no cones and its death-association rests on its own toxicity, longevity, and churchyard planting rather than on the Mediterranean funerary rite.
  • Fir: Similar because it is a needle-leaved evergreen conifer valued for timber; differs because fir has sharp, resinous needles and relatively even, soft wood, while the yew has flat, non-resinous needles and an exceptionally hard, dense, elastic heartwood made for longbows.
  • Pine: Similar because it is a familiar conifer of northern forests used for wood; differs because pine bears woody cones, is resinous, and grows fast with soft wood, whereas the yew is cone-less, non-resinous, extremely slow-growing, and yields the hardest elastic timber of any European conifer.
  • Spruce: Similar because it is a tall, needle-leaved evergreen conifer of northern timber industries; differs because spruce is fast-growing, cone-bearing, and resinous with light resonance wood, while the yew is slow-growing, cone-less, and prized instead for density and spring.
  • Juniper: Similar because it is an evergreen conifer with berry-like fleshy cones and ancient ritual associations; differs because juniper bears its seeds in small bluish "juniper berries" used as spice and gin flavouring, whereas the yew's scarlet aril is the only edible part of an otherwise lethal tree.
  • Cedar: Similar because it is a culturally resonant evergreen tree valued for its durable, aromatic wood; differs because cedar is a resinous, cone-bearing tree of fragrant rot-resistant timber, while the yew is non-resinous, cone-less, and prized for strength and spring rather than scent.
  • Holly: Similar because it is a slow-growing native evergreen of sacred and festive associations with bright red fruit; differs because holly is a broadleaved shrub-tree with glossy spiny leaves and (mildly) poisonous berries, whereas the yew is a true conifer with needles whose every part but the red aril is lethally poisonous.
  • Hemlock: Similar because it is a shade-tolerant evergreen conifer with soft flat needles; differs because hemlock (the tree, Tsuga) is a cone-bearing resinous timber species, while the yew is cone-less and non-resinous and carries the name of no poison plant of its own (unlike the unrelated poisonous herb hemlock).
