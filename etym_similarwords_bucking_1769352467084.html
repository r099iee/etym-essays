• Prancing: Similar because both describe a horse leaping and moving energetically; differs because prancing is a playful, high-stepping, graceful show-off movement, while bucking is violent, arched-back, and meant to dislodge a rider.
  • Rearing: Similar because both are horse movements that threaten to unseat a rider; differs because rearing means rising up on the hind legs with forelegs raised, while bucking is a jump with all four feet leaving the ground and the back arched.
  • Plunging: Similar because both describe violent, uncontrolled horse motion; differs because plunging is a forward dive or lunge, while bucking is a vertical, springing leap.
  • Kicking: Similar because both involve the hind legs in aggressive motion; differs because kicking is a straight backward or sideways strike of a single leg, while bucking is a whole-body jump with arched back.
  • Vaulting: Similar because both describe a springing leap; differs because vaulting implies a deliberate, controlled jump (as in gymnastics), while bucking is an involuntary, violent animal reflex.
  • Jumping: Similar because both describe leaving the ground; differs because jumping is a neutral general term for any leap, while bucking specifies the arched-back, unseating style of a horse or goat.
  • Resisting: Similar because both describe opposition and defiance; differs because resisting is a general word for withstanding, while bucking (figurative) implies aggressive, active pushback against a trend or authority — throwing off a rider.
  • Defying: Similar because both describe refusing to obey or conform; differs because defying stresses open challenge to rules or expectations, while bucking stresses the physical image of throwing something off one's back.
  • Fighting: Similar because both describe active opposition; differs because fighting is prolonged and mutual, while bucking is a specific, sudden, one-sided act of resistance.
  • Bolting: Similar because both describe a horse trying to escape control; differs because bolting means running away suddenly at full speed, while bucking means staying in place and leaping to dislodge the rider.
