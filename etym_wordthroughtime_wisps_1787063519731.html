- Bundle of hay or straw used for wiping, thatching, tinder (14th c.) - purely rural, practical, and unremarkable utility.
- Wisp as a small torch / marsh-light, giving will-o'-the-wisp (16th c.) - turns mysterious and superstitious, linked to ghostly lights and being led astray.
- Wisp of smoke, vapour, steam, hair (19th c.) - the Industrial Age makes chimney smoke and locomotive steam part of daily perception; wisp becomes poetic and atmospheric.
- Wisp of a person — a slender, delicate child or woman (19th–20th c.) - affectionate and diminutive; fragility becomes a gentle compliment.
- Wispy cloud types in meteorology (19th c. onward) - adopted as scientific vocabulary for feathery cirrus.
- Modern use (21st c.) - ghostly minimalism and beauty: wisps of steam, wisps of incense smoke, wisps of hair in art, film, and design; still faint, airy, and elegant.
