- Late 14th century - "inquisitive" reached Middle English from Old French "inquisitif", carrying from Latin "inquirere" ("to seek into") its double charge from the very beginning: eager learning on the one hand, prying curiosity on the other.
- 15th-18th century - the two senses flourished side by side: the positive use belongs to science and letters (an "inquisitive mind"), the censuring use to society (inquisitive neighbours), and both derive from the same root image of actively searching into things.
- Modern English - the word still occupies the middle ground: the strongest positive word for intellectual thirst, yet one that tips easily into censure — a curiosity that is a virtue when the thing sought is knowledge and a liability when it is other people's business.
