Latin (attested from the 2nd c. BCE)
fullo
"fuller, one who tramples cloth"
↓
Medieval Latin (6th–10th c.)
fullāre
"to full cloth, to trample, press"
↓
Old French (10th–12th c.)
fouler
"to trample, crush, press"
↓
Middle English (mid-14th c.)
foilen
"to trample, defile; to spoil a scent"
↓
Early Modern English (1560s–1580s)
foil
"to defeat, frustrate, discomfit"
↓
Modern English
FOILED
"thwarted, defeated"

Note on the debate: the ancestry of Latin fullo is uncertain — no secure Indo-European etymology has been established and a substrate origin is possible — and the sense-shift "trample → defeat" (1560s) was probably reinforced by fencing usage of "foil".
