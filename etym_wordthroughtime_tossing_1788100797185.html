- c. 1500s - tosse appears in English: to throw or fling about violently; also 'to be tossed' by the sea.
- 16th–17th c. - the word extends to brittle chance (the coin toss) and gesture (the toss of the head); the King James Bible fixes 'tossed to and fro' for spiritual instability (Ephesians 4:14).
- 18th–19th c. - domestic and sporting senses: tossing pancakes and salads, tossing the caber; 'toss-up' comes to mean an even chance.
- 20th c. - sleep science adopts 'tossing and turning' for restless nights; 'toss one's cookies' is slang for vomiting; the word stays casual, kinetic, slightly unserious.
- Today - 'tossing' in bed is the emblem of fever, worry, and insomnia — exactly its role in the Happy Prince passage.
