• Tiresome: Similar because both describe things causing fatigue and annoyance; differs because tiresome leans heavily toward petty irritation, minor nuisance, or social boredom (such as a tiresome acquaintance), whereas wearisome conveys deep, heavy, protracted physical, mental, or spiritual exhaustion.
  • Tedious: Similar because both involve the burden of slow, repetitive activities; differs because tedious focuses specifically on dull monotony, lack of variety, and mechanical detail, while wearisome encompasses heavy physical labor, grueling duration, and systemic depletion.
  • Exhausting: Similar because both describe energy depletion; differs because exhausting denotes acute, rapid draining of physical strength to the point of collapse, whereas wearisome emphasizes the prolonged, grinding duration and dreary atmosphere of the ordeal.
  • Onerous: Similar because both describe burdensome tasks; differs because onerous is a formal, legalistic or moral term emphasizing heavy responsibility, severe hardship, or oppressive obligations, rather than physical tiredness or emotional boredom.
  • Arduous: Similar because both involve great difficulty; differs because arduous emphasizes the steepness, labor, and strenuous effort required to overcome an obstacle (often carrying a heroic or ambitious undertone), while wearisome focuses on the dispiriting, energy-draining drag of the effort.
  • Irksome: Similar because both cause negative psychological reactions; differs because irksome focuses narrowly on annoyance, irritation, and exasperation caused by petty constraints, lacking the deep physical or existential fatigue of wearisome.
  • Monotonous: Similar because both cause fatigue through lack of variation; differs because monotonous refers strictly to unchanging acoustic tone or unbroken sameness in routine, whereas wearisome encompasses the full multidimensional burden of the experience.
