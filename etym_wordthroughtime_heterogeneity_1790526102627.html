- 1620s–1680s - Entered Early Modern English through scientific and philosophical treatises (Sir Thomas Browne, Robert Boyle) to describe physical substances and alchemical mixtures composed of fundamentally disparate, non-uniform elements, contrasting with pure, uniform elements.
- 1700s–1800s - Expanded in Enlightenment metaphysics, natural philosophy, and classical chemistry to classify mineralogical compositions, physiological variations, and non-uniform geological strata.
- Early-to-Mid 20th c. - Became a foundational technical parameter across statistical mechanics, genetics, ecology, and chemistry (heterogeneous catalysis, allele frequency distributions, clinical trial variations).
- Late 20th c.–Present - Widely adopted in critical theory, postmodern philosophy, urban sociology, machine learning, and meta-analytic statistics to describe demographic multiplicity or manage high-dimensional variance in complex systems.
