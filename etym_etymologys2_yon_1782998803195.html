PIE *e- / *ey- "this/that, here/there" — the reconstructed distal demonstrative stem (no written PIE records; reconstructed from Latin is, ea, id, Sanskrit ayám, Germanic *jainaz) → PIE *e-no- "that one (over there)" — demonstrative *e- + *-no- extension (reconstructed) → Proto-Germanic *jainaz "that (yonder), the one over there" — reconstructed c. 500 BCE–500 CE; reflected in Gothic jains "that", Old Norse enn, Old High German genēr, Old Saxon genēr → Old English ġeon "that (yonder), yon" — attested c. 8th–11th c. (also ġeond "beyond, throughout", whence yonder and beyond) → Middle English yon "that over there" — attested 12th–15th c. → English YON (determiner, adverb; archaic/literary) — current Note on debate: All stages before Old English are reconstructed rather than attested. The PIE forms *e-, *e-no- and Proto-Germanic *jainaz are standard reconstructions from comparative evidence across Italic, Indo-Iranian, and Germanic; the precise shape of the *-no- extension and its relation to the *-d- forms (yond, yonder) is a matter of scholarly reconstruction, not direct attestation. Latin is, ea, id "he, she, it; that" and Sanskrit ayám "this" (modern Hindi yah) descend from the same PIE pointer *e-/*ey- on sister branches.
