- 12th to 13th Century: Physical spatial relative - Denoted physical containment inside a structure, enclosure, or geographical territory ("the chamber wherein they slept").
- 14th to 15th Century: Abstract and textual containment - Broadened into literary and religious writing to encompass texts, books, parables, and conditions of the soul.
- 16th to 17th Century: Interrogative forensic inquiry and high Renaissance poetry - Featured prominently in rhetorical questioning ("Wherein have we sinned?") and epic verse (Shakespeare, KJV, Milton).
- 18th to 19th Century: Juridical codification and constitutional drafting - Connoted formal authority, embedded in legal deeds, patents, and statutory definitions.
- 20th to 21st Century: Specialized legal drafting and elevated literary prose - Retained in judicial opinions and formal rhetoric to invoke solemnity, while largely replaced by "in which" in ordinary spoken English.
