• Meadow: Similar because it shares identical etymological origin and core meaning with the grassland sense of mead; differs because meadow is the universal, standard modern term in daily English, whereas mead is restricted to archaic, dialectal, and poetic contexts.
  • Pasture: Similar because both refer to open grassland utilized for feeding livestock; differs because pasture specifically designates enclosed or fenced land dedicated directly to grazing, whereas a mead/meadow is often periodically mown for hay crops before grazing.
  • Lea / Ley: Similar because both are poetic and traditional British terms for open grassy fields; differs because a lea often refers to arable land temporarily laid down to grass in crop rotation or an open upland clearing, rather than a damp alluvial river-plain.
  • Hydromel: Similar because it is the exact scholarly synonym for the honey-wine beverage sense of mead; differs because hydromel derives from Classical Greek (hydōr "water" + meli "honey") and is used in formal enological literature, whereas mead is the native Germanic term.
  • Metheglin: Similar because it is a fermented honey-based alcoholic beverage; differs because metheglin (from Welsh meddyglyn, literally "medicinal liquor") is mead specifically spiced and infused with medicinal herbs, roots, cloves, or ginger, rather than pure fermented honey.
