• Attribute: Similar because both assign a cause, quality, or origin to a person or thing; differs because attribute is the broad, everyday, neutral word, while ascribe is more formal, Latinate, and bookish.
  • Impute: Similar because both assign something (often blame or fault) to a person; differs because impute usually carries a negative or incriminating charge, whereas ascribe is neutral in tone.
  • Credit: Similar because both assign praise or cause to someone; differs because credit is colloquial, positive, and conversational ("credit him with..."), while ascribe is formal and dispassionate.
  • Assign: Similar because both allocate or attach something to someone; differs because assign is bureaucratic and can be arbitrary, whereas ascribe implies reasoned, evidence-based attribution.
  • Refer: Similar because both trace something back to a source ("refer a work to an author"); differs because refer is more specialized to provenance and can also mean "send for consultation," a sense ascribe lacks.
  • Accredit: Similar because both assign authorship or cause to a person; differs because accredit also implies official authorization or certification (an accredited diplomat), which ascribe never does.
  • Put down to: Similar because both explain something by a cause; differs because it is informal and everyday — the phrasal counterpart of the formal ascribe.
  • Chalk up to: Similar because both credit an outcome to a cause; differs because it is colloquial and often jocular, with none of ascribe's scholarly weight.
  • Lay at the door of: Similar because both place responsibility or blame on a person; differs because this idiom is figurative, dramatic, and usually implies blame.
  • Attach: Similar because both link a quality or label to a person or thing; differs because attach is physical and general, without the specific "assign cause/origin" force.
  • Blame: Similar because both can assign responsibility; differs because blame is always negative and personal, whereas ascribe is neutral and can assign credit or cause.
  • Pin on: Similar because both fasten responsibility onto someone; differs because it is informal, slangy, and typically accusatory.
