- 11th to 14th Century: Norman feudal game preserve and noble title - In Anglo-Norman England, warren denoted a royal franchise for keeping small game, which evolved into the prestigious aristocratic surname de Warenne.
- 1747: Incorporation of the Rhode Island town - The town was legally incorporated from formerly disputed Massachusetts border territory and named in honor of British naval commander Admiral Sir Peter Warren.
- 1764 to 1770: The cradle of higher education - Celebrated throughout the American colonies as the home of Rhode Island College (Brown University), hosting classes and the first commencement before the college moved to Providence.
- 19th Century: Seafaring shipbuilding, whaling, and textile boom - Renowned as an energetic industrial port city famed for building ocean clipper ships, sending out whaling expeditions, and running cotton textile mills.
- 20th to 21st Century: Architectural preservation and coastal culture - Prized across New England as a picturesque coastal haven characterized by restored 18th-century colonial homes, antique shops, working boatyards, and a celebrated restaurant district.
