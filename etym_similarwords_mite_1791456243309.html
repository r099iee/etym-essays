• speck: similar because it is a very small mark or particle; differs because it is inanimate and emphasizes a visible dot rather than a living creature.
  • particle: similar because it is a minute piece of matter; differs because of its scientific, neutral register and its lack of affection.
  • atom: similar because it is an extremely small unit of matter; differs because of its technical sense and its use in emphatic negatives (“not an atom of proof”).
  • iota: similar because it is a tiny amount, especially in negative constructions; differs because of its Greek-letter origin and its formal, literary tone.
  • jot: similar because it means the least bit; differs because it is almost always used in negatives and because of its informal, idiomatic flavour.
  • whit: similar because it is a very small amount; differs because of its archaic, literary register and its fixed phrase “not a whit”.
  • grain: similar because it is a very small quantity, especially of salt or truth; differs because of its concrete image of a seed and its use in cooking and measurement.
  • crumb: similar because it is a tiny fragment of bread or other food; differs because of its concrete, edible image and its suggestion of leftovers.
  • scrap: similar because it is a small piece left over; differs because of its implication of waste or remains rather than mere size.
  • shred: similar because it is a thin strip or fragment; differs because of its image of tearing and its frequent use with evidence or dignity.
  • bit: similar because it is a small amount or piece; differs because it is far more general and because of its everyday, neutral register.
  • modicum: similar because it is a small quantity, especially of something desirable; differs because of its formal, slightly ironic register.
  • trace: similar because it is a barely detectable amount; differs because it emphasizes detectability rather than size.
  • touch: similar because it is a slight amount, especially of a quality; differs because of its metaphorical, aesthetic use (“a touch of salt”).
  • tinge: similar because it is a slight trace of colour or quality; differs because of its emphasis on hue and its figurative delicacy.
  • smidgen: similar because it is a very small amount; differs because of its colloquial, American, humorous tone.
  • tad: similar because it is a little bit; differs because of its informal, chiefly American register.
  • pinch: similar because it is a small amount held between finger and thumb; differs because of its concrete cooking measure.
  • drop: similar because it is a small quantity of liquid; differs because of its concrete image and its use in “a drop of drink”.
  • dash: similar because it is a small amount added to a drink or dish; differs because of its culinary and cocktail context.
  • tot: similar because it is a small child or a small drink; differs because of its affectionate, colloquial tone and its two distinct senses.
  • tyke: similar because it is a small child, especially a mischievous one; differs because of its regional, dialectal flavour and its impish connotation.
  • waif: similar because it is a homeless or neglected child; differs because of its pathos and its implication of abandonment.
  • urchin: similar because it is a ragged or mischievous child; differs because of its historical association with poverty and street life.
  • tick: similar because it is a bloodsucking arachnid related to the mite; differs because of its larger size and its different feeding habits.
