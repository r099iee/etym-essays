*deik-

*deik-ē-

↓

↓

"to declare publicly, proclaim, impose, announce"
↓

indictare / indictamentum
"to compose, dictate, formally charge in court"
↓
Anglo-French / Old French (c. 13th c.)
enditer / enditement
"to write down, indict, bring formal accusation"
↓
Middle English (c. 1320–1400)
enditement / enditementes
"formal written accusation of crime by grand jury"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1530–1600)
indictment
"Latinate respelling with silent -c-; criminal charge"
↓
Modern English (present)
indictments
"formal criminal grand jury charges; sweeping moral censures"

Note: The bizarre divergence between English spelling (with silent 'c') and pronunciation (/ɪnˈdaɪt.mənts/) arose during the 16th-century Renaissance orthographic revival. Scribes Latinized the Middle English phonetic spelling endite / enditement to reflect classical Latin indicere / dictum, but speakers permanently preserved the Anglo-Norman pronunciation with the long diphthong /aɪ/ derived from Old French.
