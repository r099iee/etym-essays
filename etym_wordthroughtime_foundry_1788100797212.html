- c. 1600 — English borrows "foundery / foundrie" from French fonderie for the art of casting metal; 17th-century spellings wobble (foundery, foundrie, fonderie).
- c. 1700 — the word shifts from the art to the place: the industrial iron foundry is born, and "foundry" becomes a workplace word — a place where metals are melted and cast.
- 18th–19th c. — the foundry of the Industrial Revolution: cupola furnaces, moulding floors, and foundry towns; the art of founding also serves printing, where the "type foundry" casts letters.
- 20th c. — heavy industry dominates the word's core while the type foundry quietly goes digital (font foundries); engineers speak of precision and investment casting.
- 1980s–21st c. — the "semiconductor foundry" arrives (chip factories making others' designs; TSMC, 1987), and "foundry" spreads as a metaphor for anything that pours out products or people — "a talent foundry", "an idea foundry" — while the furnace keeps its ancient heat at the word's centre.
