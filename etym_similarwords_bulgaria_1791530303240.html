• Bulgar: a member of the Turkic steppe people who founded the first Bulgarian state; differs because it names the medieval ethnonym, not the modern nation or country.
  • Bulgarian: the adjective, the language and the nationality; differs because it qualifies people and things rather than naming the territory.
  • Rumelia: the Ottoman name for the Balkan lands of the empire, including Bulgaria; differs because it is a supra-national administrative term with a Turkish frame of reference.
  • Moesia: the Roman province on the lower Danube, roughly northern Bulgaria; differs because it is an ancient administrative unit and does not correspond to modern borders.
  • Thrace: the historical region of south-eastern Bulgaria, north-eastern Greece and European Turkey; differs because it is a geographical and cultural area, not a state.
  • Dobruja: the region of north-eastern Bulgaria and eastern Romania; differs because it is a borderland shared between two states.
  • Balkan: the wider peninsula to which Bulgaria belongs; differs by its far greater scope and by its metaphorical associations.
  • Volga Bulgaria: the medieval Bulgar state on the Volga; differs because it is a different polity with no territorial overlap, sharing only the ethnonym.
  • Bessarabia: a region of modern Moldova and Ukraine; differs geographically and etymologically, despite the superficial resemblance.
  • Sofia: the capital city; differs by being a city, not the country, though it is often used metonymically for the government.
  • Bulgarian Orthodox Church: the national church; differs because it names a religious institution, not the state.
  • Bŭlgariya: the Bulgarian-language name of the country; differs only as the endonym, with a different transliteration.
