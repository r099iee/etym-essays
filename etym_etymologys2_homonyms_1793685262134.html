Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*som- / *sem-
"together, one, same"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE)
homós (ὁμός)
"same, common, equal"
↓
Classical Greek (c. 4th c. BCE)
homṓnymos (ὁμώνυμος)
"having the same name, equivocal"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th c. CE)
homōnymum
"word having the same name but different sense"
↓
Middle French (c. 16th c.)
homonyme
"word of identical sound or spelling"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1620s)
homonym
"word sharing form/sound with another"
↓
Modern English (present)
homonyms
"words identical in form/sound of distinct origin"

Note on Etymological Compounding: Greek homṓnymos is an endocentric compound formed from homós ("same", from PIE *som-) and ónoma (ὄνομα, "name", from PIE *h₁nómn̥, which also yielded Latin nōmen, Sanskrit nā́ma, and English name).
