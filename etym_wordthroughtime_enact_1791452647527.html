- Early 15th century - to act a part or represent something in performance; the theatrical sense is among the word’s earliest English uses.
- Mid-15th century - to decree or establish something into law; the legal sense emerges and soon becomes the dominant public meaning.
- 16th–17th centuries - the word is used for statutes, royal decrees, and stage roles alike; the idea of “putting into action” widens.
- 18th–19th centuries - parliamentary and administrative usage flourishes; enactment becomes a standard term for legislation, while theatrical usage continues.
- 20th century - social scientists and philosophers use “enact” for performing roles, identities, and rituals; cognitive science develops enactive and enactivism.
- 21st century - the word is common in policy, business, and technology for putting a plan or process into operation, as well as in theatre and historical re-enactment.
