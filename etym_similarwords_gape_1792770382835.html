• stare: Similar because both involve looking intently and fixedly at an object or person; differs because stare is a broad, neutral action focused entirely on wide, unwavering eyes that can communicate hostility, intense contemplation, or challenge, whereas gape specifically requires the mouth hanging open in slack-jawed astonishment or dumb wonder.
  • gawk: Similar because both describe staring open-mouthed or foolishly at a spectacle; differs because gawk carries an explicitly clumsy, rude, or boorish connotation, emphasizing the observer's awkward posture and lack of refined social manners.
  • gawp: Similar because both denote staring with an open mouth; differs because gawp is a colloquial British dialectal term that carries an even stronger nuance of mindless, slack-jawed, brainless idling.
  • peer: Similar because both involve directed visual attention toward a curious sight; differs because peer conveys squinting or looking narrowly and intently through an obstruction, fog, or gloom, searching for indistinct details, whereas gape is an involuntary reaction of wide-open shock.
  • yawn: Similar because both describe the wide, involuntary opening of the mouth and jaws; differs because yawn is a physiological reflex driven by fatigue, boredom, or the body's need for oxygenation, whereas gape is triggered by visual surprise or physical splitting.
  • ogle: Similar because both involve prolonged, conspicuous looking; differs because ogle is motivated specifically by impertinent, leering sexual desire or flirtatious appraisal, completely absent from the innocent or stunned astonishment of gape.
  • gaze: Similar because both describe prolonged looking; differs because gaze is poetic, thoughtful, reverent, or dreamy, lacking the dropped jaw, rustic uncouthness, or shock characteristic of gape.
  • yawn (geological): Similar because both describe chasms or fissures that open wide; differs because yawning as a topographic descriptor emphasizes abyssal, cavernous emptiness swallowing space, whereas gape focuses on the raw, split edges of the rupture.
  • part: Similar because both describe surfaces separating to reveal an opening; differs because part is gentle, deliberate, or gradual (as in lips parting to speak), whereas a gaping opening is wide, raw, and conspicuously exposed.
  • rubberneck: Similar because both involve craning the neck to gawk at a dramatic spectacle; differs because rubberneck is modern colloquial slang specifically associated with curious motorists slowing down traffic to stare at roadside accidents.
