• Ascent: Similar because it means an upward movement; differs because an ascent is a deliberate climb or a neutral slope with no emotional charge, while mountings are involuntary surges of the spirit.
  • Elevation: Similar because it means being raised (often morally or religiously); differs because it is static, naming a state rather than the repeated, heaving motion of mountings.
  • Upsurge: Similar because it means a sudden increase or rise; differs because it is more explosive and violent (an upsurge of prices or emotion), less rhythmic than the rising of mountings.
  • Impulse: Similar because it is a mental force that moves one; differs because it stresses the driving pressure behind a motion rather than the upward motion itself.
  • Rising: Similar because it is the plain verbal noun for going up; differs because it lacks mounting's built-in image of a mountain or a horse, and carries none of Wordsworth's sublime elevation.
  • Ascension: Similar because it names an upward going; differs because it is almost exclusively religious and singular (the Ascension of Christ), while mountings are plural and psychological.
  • Rapture: Similar because it is a state of being carried upward spiritually; differs because it names the emotion of being caught up, not the act of climbing that mountings pictures.
  • Mount (noun): Similar because it shares the root and the mountain image; differs because a mount is the height itself, whereas mountings are the acts of rising toward it.
