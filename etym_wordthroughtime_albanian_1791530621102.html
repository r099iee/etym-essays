- Sixteenth century - Albanian enters English from Latin and French as a label for a little-known Ottoman-ruled people and their language; connotation remote, mountainous, obscure.
- Nineteenth century - The word becomes tied to the Rilindja national awakening and the League of Prizren (1878); connotation national rebirth and resistance to empire.
- Twentieth century - Albanian denotes the independent state (1912), King Zog's kingdom, and Enver Hoxha's isolated Stalinist Albania; connotation secrecy and hard-line communism.
- 1990s - Post-communist collapse, mass emigration, and the Kosovo war bring the word into Western headlines, often with tabloid stereotypes of chaos and crime.
- Twenty-first century - A neutral national and linguistic term, used in EU accession debates, Kosovo recognition, and the large Albanian diaspora.
