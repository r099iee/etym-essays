- [Old English 'bōg'] - The word originally meant 'shoulder' or 'arm' as well as a tree's limb; the tree sense is itself a hardened metaphor of the tree's arm reaching out from the trunk, a metaphor so ancient it has settled into the dictionary.
- [Medieval & pre-modern use] - 'Bough' was the ordinary, unceremonious word for a tree's main structural branches, used without poetic decoration in prose as well as verse.
- [Modern register] - With 'branch' now the neutral everyday term for any limb, 'bough' has narrowed to the largest structural arms that spring from the trunk and is the only word of its set with a consistent poetic flavour — it now carries weight, age and shelter, and is favoured by writers cultivating an older, saga-like diction.
