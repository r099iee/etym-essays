- Middle English neutral sense - to purloin is to put or remove something far away, to transport it to a distance; the sense is of removal without any necessary crime
- 15th-16th century narrowed sense - the word narrows to theft: to remove property secretly and artfully, so deftly that the loss may not be noticed; it enters formal discourse and legal prose as the dignified word for stealing
- 17th-19th century literary sense - purloin becomes the elegant, mock-heroic word for theft, at home in detective fiction and polite narrative; Poe's 'The Purloined Letter' fixes it as the connoisseur's name for artful stealing
- Contemporary sense - the word is distinctly literary and faintly archaic, used for ironic, formal or mock-elegant effect rather than in everyday speech, where plain stole or slangy pinched rules
