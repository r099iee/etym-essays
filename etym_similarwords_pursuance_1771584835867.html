• Pursuit: Similar because both share the follow-and-chase core, from the same Latin root sequi "to follow"; differs because pursuit is the active, energetic chasing of a goal or quarry, forward and often competitive, while pursuance is the calm, procedural execution of a rule already adopted, fixed in the phrase "in pursuance of."
  • Execution: Similar because both mean the carrying out of something to completion; differs because execution foregrounds the decisive performance of a task and carries legal and judicial overtones (execution of a will, an executioner), while pursuance stresses ongoing compliance with a principle or instruction and stays soft, dutiful and procedural.
  • Performance: Similar because both denote the carrying out of a duty or plan; differs because performance is the neutral, everyday word for doing or fulfilling something, lacking the legal-administrative gravity that pursuance retains in its fixed formula.
  • Implementation: Similar because both mean putting a plan into effect; differs because implementation is a modern, managerial term for rolling out systems and policies, while pursuance is archaic, procedural, and tied to following an existing rule step by step.
  • Observance: Similar because both denote the keeping of a rule or custom; differs because observance suggests the faithful keeping of ritual, ceremonial, or religious duties, while pursuance is the carrying out of a principle or plan in practical action.
  • Compliance: Similar because both denote conformity with a rule or requirement; differs because compliance is the modern, administrative word for submission to regulations, while pursuance implies the positive, deliberate following of a principle one has adopted as one's own.
  • Fulfilment: Similar because both denote the completing of something; differs because fulfilment emphasizes bringing a promise, duty, or expectation to its full realization, while pursuance emphasizes the continuing process of following a course, not its completion.
