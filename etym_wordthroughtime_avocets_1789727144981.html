- 1760s new borrowing - avocet enters English ornithology from French and Italian; perception exotic rarity, a fine-billed curiosity in cabinets and plates.
- Late 18th to early 19th century county bird - breeding avocets known on East Anglian and Kentish marshes; connotation local-special, prized by collectors and eggers.
- Mid-19th century extinction narrative - drainage and persecution wipe out British breeders; word gains elegiac, lost-bird tone, surviving in books and museum skins.
- 1947 return at Minsmere - wartime floods create lagoons, avocets recolonise; perception transforms to hopeful pioneer, symbol of nature reclaiming war-torn ground.
- RSPB emblem era - avocet head chosen as RSPB logo; public perception iconic, black-and-white badge instantly recognised even by non-birders as conservation success.
- Contemporary Avocets Return - plural evokes thriving colonies, reserve lagoons, and climate-driven spread; connotation celebratory-ecological, from rarity to flagship of wetland restoration.
