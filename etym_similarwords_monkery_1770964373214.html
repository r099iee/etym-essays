• Monasticism: Similar because both denote the monastic system and way of life; differs because monasticism is neutral, academic, and theological, while monkery is pejorative and anti-clerical.
  • Monastery: Similar because both can denote a place of monks; differs because monastery is a concrete building or community, while monkery is chiefly the abstract system or way of life (the place sense being secondary).
  • Cloister: Similar because both suggest secluded religious life; differs because cloister names the architectural covered walkway and, by extension, monastic seclusion, with a neutral or reverent tone that monkery lacks.
  • Abbey: Similar because both name a monastic dwelling; differs because abbey refers specifically to a community governed by an abbot or abbess and its buildings, without the disparaging edge.
  • Convent: Similar because both denote a religious community dwelling; differs because convent usually names a female community and has shed its monastic pejorative charge.
  • Asceticism: Similar because both involve austere religious discipline; differs because asceticism names the practice of self-denial wherever it occurs, monastic or not, without institutional scorn.
  • Monkishness: Similar because both are derived nouns for the monkish quality; differs because monkishness is rarer and emphasises the personal trait, not the institution or place.
  • Seclusion: Similar because both imply withdrawal from the world; differs because seclusion is a general term for privacy with no religious or clerical content.
  • Celibacy: Similar because both touch the unmarried, withdrawn life of monks; differs because celibacy is the specific vow of abstaining from marriage, not the whole monastic system.
  • The religious life: Similar because both describe a vowed life of prayer; differs because "the religious life" is a respectful umbrella term for all vowed vocations, free of contempt.
  • Hermitage: Similar because both can name a solitary dwelling; differs because a hermitage belongs to a lone hermit, not a community, and is neutral or romantic rather than sneering.
