• Cork Oak (Quercus suber): Similar because it is also an evergreen oak native to the western Mediterranean basin with leathery leaves; differs because it produces an exceptionally thick, harvested spongy cork bark and lighter foliage, whereas the holm-oak has hard, furrowed, non-cork bark and much denser, darker wood.
  • English Oak / Pedunculate Oak (Quercus robur): Similar because it belongs to the same genus (Quercus) and bears acorns in cups; differs because it is a deciduous tree of northern and temperate Europe with deeply lobed leaves that shed in autumn, unlike the persistent, spiny evergreen leaves of the holm-oak.
  • Kermes Oak (Quercus coccifera): Similar because it is an evergreen Mediterranean oak with prickly, holly-like leaves; differs because it is typically a low, sprawling shrub or stunted dwarf tree rather than a massive, tall canopy tree like the holm-oak, and was historically famous for hosting scale insects used in scarlet dyeing.
  • Live Oak (Quercus virginiana): Similar because it is a broad-leaved evergreen oak with dense, heavy timber and sprawling limbs; differs because it is native to the southeastern United States and coastal plains rather than the Mediterranean, possessing smooth-margined oval leaves without the sharp holly-like teeth of juvenile holm-oak leaves.
  • Holly (Ilex aquifolium): Similar because its evergreen, dark glossy spiny leaves are visually almost identical to the lower foliage of the young holm-oak; differs because it belongs to an entirely distinct botanical family (Aquifoliaceae rather than Fagaceae), produces red fleshy berries instead of acorns, and has soft white wood rather than dense dark oak timber.
  • Sessile Oak (Quercus petraea): Similar because it is a large, majestic European oak tree with hard timber; differs because it is deciduous, native to temperate upland forests, and its acorns are stalkless (sessile), contrasting with the evergreen habit and Mediterranean climate adaptation of the holm-oak.
  • Turkey Oak (Quercus cerris): Similar because it is native to southern Europe and frequently planted as an ornamental tree in parks; differs because it is deciduous with deeply lobed leaves and mossy-bristled acorn cups, lacking the evergreen, holly-like foliage of the holm-oak.
