- 8th–11th c. — Anglo-Saxon family settlement: Established as a small riparian settlement along the Thames marshlands, named Wappingas after the family or followers of an early Saxon leader named Wæppa.
- 13th c. — Drainage and early river trade: First recorded in surviving medieval charters as Wappinges (c. 1220); local lords begin constructing earthen embankments and river dykes along Wapping Marsh to reclaim fertile agricultural land.
- 16th c. — Flemish drainage and maritime explosion: Cornelius Vanderdelft drains Wapping Marsh in the 1540s; Queen Elizabeth I’s expanding maritime navy transforms the embankment into a continuous strip of shipwrights’ yards, ropewalks, and Execution Dock.
- 17th–18th c. — Classic sailortown of empire: The district grows into the densest maritime community in the world; Jonathan Swift places Gulliver’s surgical practice here; Captain Kidd is hanged at Execution Dock in 1701.
- 19th c. — Monumental enclosed docks: The opening of the massive London Docks (1805) transforms Wapping into a fortified forest of bonded brick warehouses storing rum, wine, and colonial merchandise.
- 1980s — The Battle of Wapping: Rupert Murdoch relocates News International to Wapping, breaking the Fleet Street print unions in a landmark industrial showdown.
- Today — Affluent riverside enclave: Reimagined through dockland redevelopment into an upscale waterfront residential neighborhood of luxury loft apartments, riverside taverns, and peaceful nautical basins.
