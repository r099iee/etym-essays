• Drunk: Similar because both are past forms of the verb drink; differs because drunk is the past participle, used with have/be ("I have drunk", "he was drunk"), while drank is the simple past tense ("I drank").
  • Drunken: Similar because it looks like a drink-derived form; differs because drunken is an adjective meaning intoxicated or given to drink, not a verb form at all.
  • Swallowed: Similar because both describe taking something into the body through the mouth; differs because swallowed covers solids and any liquid at any speed, without the specific act of drinking from a vessel.
  • Gulped: Similar because gulping is drinking, quickly and in large amounts; differs because it names manner and speed, and can apply to air or food, while drank is neutral.
  • Sipped: Similar because sipping is drinking; differs because it specifies slow, small, careful amounts — the opposite of gulping.
  • Quaffed: Similar because to quaff is to drink deeply and heartily; differs because quaff is archaic or literary and implies volume and gusto.
  • Imbibed: Similar because to imbibe is to drink; differs because it is formal and figurative (one can imbibe ideas), without the plain physical frame of drank.
  • Slurped: Similar because it is drinking done noisily; differs because it foregrounds the sound and is informal.
  • Supped: Similar because to sup is to drink or eat, especially at the evening meal; differs because it is old-fashioned and tied to suppers.
  • Tippled: Similar because tippling is drinking, especially liquor, habitually; differs because tipple means to drink alcohol in small regular amounts and carries a disapproving, habit-oriented tone.
