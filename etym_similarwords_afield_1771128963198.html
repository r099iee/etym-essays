• abroad: Similar because both describe being at a distance from one's home; differs because abroad specifically means across national or sea borders into a foreign country, whereas afield only requires departure from one's own accustomed place.
  • afar: Similar because both signal great distance; differs because afar stresses pure, often poetic distance and detached observation (watching from afar), while afield implies active movement or engagement at the distant location.
  • away: Similar because both mean at or to a distance from here; differs because away is the plain, fully neutral everyday adverb, whereas afield is more literary and implies the countryside or a specific base being left behind.
  • astray: Similar because both involve wandering off from a proper or expected position; differs because astray carries a judgment of error or loss, whether moral or navigational, whereas afield is neutral — one can go far afield deliberately and successfully.
  • outside: Similar because both oppose being indoors or within bounds; differs because outside is a general, fully productive preposition and adverb, whereas afield is restricted to literary and figurative talk of distance from home or from the topic.
  • overseas: Similar because both can describe being far from one's own country; differs because overseas specifically requires crossing the sea into another country, an exactness that afield does not possess.
  • yonder: Similar because both are literary words pointing at a distant place; differs because yonder is a demonstrative adverb of place ('over there'), while afield is relational, always measured from home or from the point under discussion.
