PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*h₂ewg- / *aug-
"to increase, enlarge, make grow"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*augos (noun) / *aug-ur
"divine increase, religious power; diviner who observes birds"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
augur → inaugurāre
"to take omens from birds; to consecrate through augury, install in office"
Note: Formed from in- ('into, upon') + augur ('diviner, priest of bird omens').
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th–6th c.)
inaugurātiō / inaugurāns
"ceremonial installation, taking of auspicious auspices"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1600–1620)
inaugurate + -ing
"investing with solemn rites; initiating an office or era"
↓
Modern English (present)
INAUGURATING
