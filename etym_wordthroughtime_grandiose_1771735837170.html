- [19th century Italian grandeur] - [Enters from Italian grandioso and French, meaning grand and imposing in style; connotation initially admiring — the scale of epic subjects]
- [19th-20th century the pretentious turn] - [Applied to schemes, plans, and persons it becomes pejorative — inflated, overweening, disconnected from reality; 'grandiose delusions' enters psychiatry]
- [20th-21st century context decides] - [Split meaning: applied to a vast subject or work it can mean simply immense and impressive; applied to a person or ambition it means inflated and unrealistic — the word's value is settled entirely by context]
