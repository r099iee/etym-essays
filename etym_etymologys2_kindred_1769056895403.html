CHAIN — KINDRED (two elements: KIN + suffix -RED).
ELEMENT 1 of 2 — the element KIN:
1. PIE *ǵenh₁- "to give birth, beget, produce, bear" (c. 4500–3000 BCE; with laryngeal, older notation *gen-; attested in Vedic Sanskrit jánati "begets, gives birth," Greek gígnomai "to become, be born," Latin gignō "to beget").
2. Proto-Germanic *kunją "family, clan, race" (c. 500 BCE–2nd c. CE).
3. Old English cynn "kin, family, race, kind" (attested from c. 8th–9th c.; also the source of cyning "king," literally "son of the royal kin," and cynd "nature, kind").
4. Middle English kin "family, relatives, kind" (12th–15th c.).
5. English kin, joined with the suffix below in kindred.
ELEMENT 2 of 2 — the suffix -RED (from Old English rǣden "condition, state"):
1. PIE *reydʰ- "to ride; to move, to set in order" (c. 4500–3000 BCE; attested in the Germanic family: Old English rīdan "to ride," rād "road," rǣde "ready, prepared"; the "set in order" sense is basic).
2. Proto-Germanic *raidaz "ready, arranged, ordered" (whence English ready, German bereit "ready").
3. Old English rǣden "condition, state, arrangement, rule" (a nominal formation on the "ordering/state" sense; distinct from rǣd "counsel," a different word).
4. Old English cynrǣden "the condition of being kin; kinship, kindred" (compound of cynn + rǣden; attested; siblings: frēondrǣden "friendship," þēowrǣden "servitude").
5. Middle English kynrede "kinship, kindred" (13th c.), the compound fused into one word.
6. English kindred, with the semantic step from the abstract "condition of being kin" to the people who share it — one's family and relations; and the adjective "alike in nature."
7. English kindred (the root word, highlighted at the final node).

NUANCE NOTE: the -rǣden suffix is unrelated to the colour "red" and to Old English rǣd "counsel" (from a different PIE root *h₂reh₁- "to think," whence English read and rede); the rǣden formation descends from the *reydʰ- "set in order" family (ready, road, ride). The PIE *ǵenh₁- root and its reflexes (Latin genus, Greek génos, Sanskrit jāti) are securely attested; no stage has been invented or merged.
