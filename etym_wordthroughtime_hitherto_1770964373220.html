- Old/Middle English: compounded from "hither" (to here) + "to", meaning literally "up to this place" — a purely spatial, physical adverb describing motion toward a location.
- 15th–17th c.: the word migrates from space to time, coming to mean "up to this time, until now"; it becomes a fixture of legal, parliamentary, and historical documents that needed to mark a definitive boundary with the past.
- 18th–19th c.: the golden age of "hitherto" — the dignified, scholarly marker of historical narrative, its formal gravity suited to treatises and sermons where precedent was being weighed.
- Contemporary: now distinctly bookish and slightly archaic; in everyday speech it has been largely replaced by "previously", "so far", or "up to now", though it still lends gravity to academic and legal writing.
