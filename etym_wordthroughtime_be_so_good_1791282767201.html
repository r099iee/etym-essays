- Sincere courtesy — 16th–17th century use as genuine compliment-plus-request among equals; perceived as refined, courtly manners.
- Standard genteel imperative — 18th–19th century normalisation as the公认 polite way for superiors to order servants and clerks; connotation of proper hierarchy with velvet gloves.
- Upper-class signature — Edwardian era freezing as toff diction (Bertie Wooster type); seen as characteristic gentleman's-valet language, slightly pompous but expected.
- Dated and arch survival — mid-20th century decline before plain please and American would you mind; perceived as old-fashioned, posh or bureaucratic.
- Modern ironic revival — contemporary use mainly comic or condescending, savouring its period grandeur to issue orders with a wink.
