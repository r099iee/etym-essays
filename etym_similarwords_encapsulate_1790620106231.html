• enkapsulate (variant) / incapsulate: Similar because it is the same verb in alternate spelling; differs because it is rare or obsolete in modern British and American usage.
  • enclose: Similar because it means to shut in on all sides; differs because it is broader and plainer, lacking the capsule image of small, sealed, portable containment.
  • encase: Similar because it means to put into a case or covering; differs because it suggests a hard outer shell rather than a miniature dose-like vessel.
  • enshroud / envelop / wrap: Similar because they denote surrounding and covering; differs because they suggest draping or surrounding rather than sealing into a discrete capsule.
  • embody: Similar because it means to give concrete form to an abstraction; differs because it stresses incarnation in a body or person, not compression into a small container.
  • epitomize: Similar because it means to be a perfect miniature representative; differs because it stresses typicality rather than enclosure.
  • encapsule (rare): Similar because it shares the capsule root; differs because it is marginal and mostly replaced by encapsulate.
  • summarize / sum up: Similar because they denote compact restatement; differs because they are purely informational and lose the containment metaphor.
  • distil / distill / condense / compress: Similar because they denote reducing to essence; differs because they stress the process of concentration rather than the vessel that holds the result.
  • capture: Similar because journalists use it to mean seize the essence; differs because it is more vivid and less formal, with a hunting metaphor rather than a container metaphor.
  • contain / hold: Similar because they denote having within; differs because they are neutral and static, while encapsulate adds miniaturization and completeness.
