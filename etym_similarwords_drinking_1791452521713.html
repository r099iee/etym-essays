• Sipping: similar in naming the consumption of liquid; differs because it implies small, deliberate mouthfuls and a savouring manner rather than the neutral act of drinking.
  • Quaffing: shares the sense of drinking; differs because it means drinking deeply and heartily, often in a literary or celebratory register.
  • Guzzling: similar in the physical act; differs because it implies greedy, noisy, excessive consumption, usually of alcohol or soft drinks.
  • Imbibing: a formal synonym for drinking; differs in register, being bookish or humorous, and often implying alcohol.
  • Supping: an archaic or dialectal word for drinking in small quantities; differs in being old-fashioned and often associated with broth or ale.
  • Swigging: shares the sense of drinking; differs because it means drinking in large gulps, often straight from a bottle, with a casual or rough tone.
  • Gulping: similar in describing rapid swallowing; differs because it stresses the size of the mouthful and the speed, and applies to food as well.
  • Tippling: shares the alcohol sense; differs because it means drinking small amounts habitually, often with a slightly indulgent or genteel tone.
  • Boozing: similar in the alcohol sense; differs in being slangy and disapproving, with a stronger implication of excess.
  • Carousing: shares drinking as a social activity; differs because it adds noisy revelry, late hours and group merrymaking.
  • Absorbing: similar to the figurative drinking in; differs because it is neutral and intellectual, lacking the bodily thirst metaphor.
  • Savoring: shares the idea of taking something in with pleasure; differs because it stresses taste and duration rather than eager absorption.
  • Ingesting: the technical term for taking something into the body; differs in being clinical and applicable to solids and medicines.
  • Consuming: the broad formal term for eating or drinking; differs in register and in covering everything from food to fuel.
  • Swallowing: the mechanical act that completes drinking; differs because it names only the final stage, not the whole activity.
  • Toasting: a social ritual involving drinking; differs because it requires a verbal tribute and a raised glass, not merely consumption.
  • Hydrating: the health-oriented term for drinking water; differs because it is modern, scientific and concerned with the body's water balance.
