• Inviolable: Similar because both denote something that must never be broken, injured, or infringed; differs because inviolable is a more neutral, legalistic, and objective term regarding rules or treaties, whereas sacrosanct carries profound religious, historical, and emotional undertones of consecrated reverence.
  • Sacred: Similar because both pertain to divine dedication and religious holiness; differs because sacred is the universal general term for that which is dedicated to God or religion, whereas sacrosanct compounds sanctity with active legal or social immunity from challenge and interference.
  • Untouchable: Similar because both signify immunity from interference or assault; differs because untouchable is colloquial and physical, or can denote social outcasts in traditional caste systems, whereas sacrosanct always indicates elevated, inviolable prestige.
  • Hallowed: Similar because both describe that which is treated with profound veneration; differs because hallowed emphasizes historical consecration, antiquity, and quiet devotion (e.g., "hallowed grounds"), whereas sacrosanct emphasizes institutional defense against contemporary skepticism or invasion.
  • Sanctified: Similar because both involve the process of becoming holy; differs because sanctified is a dynamic theological participle denoting purification or spiritual cleansing by divine grace, whereas sacrosanct denotes an established, fixed status of total immunity.
  • Consecrated: Similar because both have roots in religious rites; differs because consecrated describes a specific liturgical act dedicating an object, priest, or altar to sacred service, whereas sacrosanct characterizes an inherent or socially enforced barrier against intrusion.
  • Taboo: Similar because both involve forbidden transgressions; differs because taboo (from Polynesian tapu) emphasizes ritual prohibition and irrational or superstitious dread of pollution, whereas sacrosanct emphasizes positive high status and solemn institutional protection.
