- [c. 1300–1550: Medieval 'Kegeln' & English Skittles] - Evolved from German monastic skittle rituals into a secular diamond-pattern tavern game.
- [c. 1570–1650: Elizabethan Era 'ninepins'] - The compound 'ninepins' entered standard English print to describe pub bowling alleys.
- [c. 1819–1850: Rip Van Winkle & American Ban] - Immortalized by Washington Irving; banned in 1840s New York due to gambling, directly spurring the invention of ten-pin bowling.
- [c. 1820–Present: Modern English & Idioms] - Generated the universal idiom 'to fall like ninepins' (rapid cascading collapse) while surviving as a classic traditional sport.
