reel (noun / verb) — A rotatory cylindrical device, spool, bobbin, spindle, or revolving frame around which continuous flexible material—such as silk thread, cotton yarn, fishing line, electrical wire, cinema film, or magnetic tape—is wound for neat storage, distribution, or controlled payout. In Oscar Wilde’s fairy tale The Nightingale and the Rose, the Professor’s daughter is introduced sitting in the doorway "winding blue silk on a reel", using the word in its classic domestic textile and sewing sense as a hand-held or spinning bobbin.

Primary physical and mechanical senses:

  • Textile winder / spool — In spinning, weaving, lacemaking, and sewing, a reel is a revolving wooden, bone, or metal cylinder or peg designed to hold skeins of thread, silk, or wool, allowing the spinner to wind or unwind the strand smoothly without tangling or snagging.
  • Angling / fishing reel — In sport fishing, a mechanical winding apparatus attached to the butt of a fishing rod, equipped with gears, a drag mechanism, and a revolving spool to cast out line and retrieve hooked fish ("a fly reel", "a spinning reel", "to reel in a trout").
  • Cinematography and media — A circular metal or plastic flanged spool holding motion picture film. By historical metonymy, a reel became the standard unit of measurement for film length (a standard 35mm reel holding approximately 1,000 feet of film, corresponding to roughly 10 to 12 minutes of projection time). In early film distribution, a feature film was described by its reel count ("a five-reel picture", "newsreels").
  • Modern digital video (social media) — In contemporary twenty-first-century digital media (such as Instagram Reels, Facebook, and short-form video platforms), a reel refers to a short, engaging, algorithmic video clip, preserving the historic film-spool metaphor in modern software.
  • Industrial and technical applications — Heavy-duty mechanical spools for industrial fire hoses, electrical extension cords, oceanographic cables, and magnetic recording tape ("reel-to-reel audio tape").

Folk music and traditional dance:

  • Traditional Celtic dance — In Scottish, Irish, and Appalachian folk traditions, a reel (Gaelic righil) is a lively, fast-paced folk dance characterized by circular weaving figures, rapid stepping, and continuous whirling movement, performed in quick duple or quadruple meter (usually 2/2 or 4/4 time).
  • Musical composition — The accompanying energetic dance tune itself, characterized by regular, unbroken sixteenth-note melodic lines ("an Irish reel", "a Highland reel").

Dynamic verb senses (Physical and Psychological):

  • To stagger, lurch, or sway — To lose one's physical balance and sway or stumble backward or sideways, as from a severe blow, acute dizziness, intoxication, or exhaustion ("he reeled under the boxer's heavy punch", "the sailor reeled across the swaying deck"). The semantic bridge connects the spinning rotatory motion of a mechanical winder to the dizzying, revolving sensation of physical imbalance.
  • Psychological shock and cognitive overwhelm — To be emotionally or intellectually stunned, disoriented, or overwhelmed: "his mind reeled at the catastrophic news", "the economy is reeling from skyrocketing inflation".
  • Phrasal verb 'to reel in' — To wind in line on a spool; figuratively, to capture, lure, attract, or secure a customer, investor, or prize.
  • Phrasal verb 'to reel off' — To recite, produce, or list a long series of facts, names, or statistics rapidly and effortlessly from memory, like thread unwinding smoothly from a spinning bobbin.

Register and literary resonance. Reel is a versatile, evocative Germanic root that spans domestic intimacy (Wilde’s blue silk), lively folk joy (Celtic reels), industrial technology (cinematography), and dramatic physical vulnerability (reeling from a blow).
