- 7th–8th c. — the name denotes a Frankish border district settled by Germanic incomers.
- 9th–13th c. — it covers a duchy and then a cluster of imperial cities; “Alsace” is a patchwork, not a province.
- 1648–1681 — French annexation makes Alsace a French province, and the name becomes an ordinary term of French administration.
- 1871–1918 — as an imperial territory of the new German Empire, the name becomes an official German label and a French grievance.
- 1919–1945 — restored to France, annexed again, liberated; the name becomes a symbol of Franco-German conflict.
- Modern — a French region (since 2016 part of Grand Est) known for wine, dialect and cross-border institutions, with the historical name retained locally and commercially.
