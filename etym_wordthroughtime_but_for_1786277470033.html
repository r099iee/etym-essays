- 14th–15th centuries (Middle English) - Formed as a compound prepositional idiom (*but for* / *bote for*) combining the exceptive sense of *but* ("except, without") with *for*, meaning "were it not for" in poetic and narrative verse.
- 16th–18th centuries - Extensively utilized in Early Modern and Augustan literature to establish dramatic counterfactual dependencies in drama, religious theology, and romantic poetry.
- 19th–20th centuries - Adopted into English common law as a foundational legal term of art (*but-for causation*), standardizing the causal inquiry in tort and criminal jurisprudence.
- 21st century - Remains ubiquitous both in high-level jurisprudence and philosophy as a formal causal test and in ordinary literary English as a concise counterfactual conditional.
