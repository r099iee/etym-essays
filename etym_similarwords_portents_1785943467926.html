• Omens: Similar because [both are prophetic signs of future events]; differs because [omens may be auspicious or inauspicious and are often read in routine signs (bird flight, entrails), whereas portents are grave, dramatic signs of momentous and usually threatening change].
  • Presages: Similar because [both denote signs or feelings that foretell a future event]; differs because [presage often names an intuitive internal foreboding or a subtle preliminary indicator, while portent is an overt, objective, dramatic sign demanding public attention].
  • Harbingers: Similar because [both point toward something coming]; differs because [a harbinger is an advance messenger or personified early arrival that directly heralds a coming event (the robin as harbinger of spring), whereas a portent is a symbolic, mysterious, often sinister warning rather than a forerunner].
  • Auguries: Similar because [both belong to the world of divinatory signs]; differs because [an augury is the priestly reading or ritual interpretation of signs — especially bird flight — within sanctioned Roman divination, whereas a portent is the sign itself as a direct communication of the gods].
  • Forewarnings: Similar because [both announce danger in advance]; differs because [a forewarning is a practical, direct, often human notice of danger, while a portent is an impersonal, mysterious, or cosmic sign that implies rather than states what is to come].
  • Prognostications: Similar because [both involve predicting what will happen]; differs because [prognostication is a reasoned forecast or calculation from data and trends, while a portent carries a mysterious, dramatic, non-analytic quality].
  • Intimations: Similar because [both give a hint of something not yet explicit]; differs because [an intimation is a subtle, often indirect hint communicated by a person or small sign, whereas a portent is a large, solemn, publicly significant signal].
  • Tokens: Similar because [both are outward signs of something else]; differs because [a token is a neutral, often conventional mark or keepsake (a token of esteem), whereas a portent is an ominous supernatural or grave indicator of future catastrophe].
  • Indications: Similar because [both are signs pointing toward a state of affairs]; differs because [an indication is a neutral, practical, evidence-based pointer, entirely without the cosmic or sinister weight of a portent].
  • Signs: Similar because [both are phenomena that point beyond themselves]; differs because [sign is the generic, neutral term for any marker or clue, whereas portent names the special, grave, usually supernatural class of signs that foretell momentous events].
  • Warnings: Similar because [both alert people to coming danger]; differs because [a warning is explicit, direct, and typically human or institutional, while a portent is implicit, mysterious, and cosmic — it must be read to be understood].
  • Prodigies: Similar because [both denote extraordinary, unnatural phenomena of ominous significance]; differs because [prodigy in this archaic sense stresses the monstrous or marvellous character of the occurrence itself, while portent stresses its function as a warning of what is to follow].
  • Forebodings: Similar because [both convey a sense of coming evil]; differs because [foreboding is a personal, inward feeling of dread, whereas portent is an external, observable sign or occurrence].
  • Signs of the times: Similar because [both interpret present phenomena as evidence of a coming turn]; differs because [this idiomatic phrase is used of broad social or religious conditions, whereas portents are singular dramatic occurrences, often supernatural].
