• Louis: Similar because Louis is the direct modern descendant of the same Germanic name *Hlodowig borne by Clovis and is the form used by eighteen French kings; differs because Clovis preserves the early Frankish shape while Louis reflects centuries of French phonetic erosion (loss of the medial -d- and the reduction of the first syllable).
  • Charlemagne: Similar because both are the two towering Frankish kings who founded France's royal tradition; differs because Charlemagne reigned three centuries later as founder of the Carolingian Empire and was crowned emperor in 800, while Clovis founded the Merovingian kingdom and never held an imperial title.
  • Theodoric: Similar because both are fifth-sixth-century Germanic kings with two-element royal names and both ruled successor-states of Rome; differs because Theodoric was the Ostrogothic king of Italy and an Arian Christian, whereas Clovis was a Frankish king who converted to Nicene orthodoxy.
  • Merovech: Similar because both are Merovingian royal names; differs because Merovech is the legendary semi-mythical ancestor (perhaps sea-born) from whom the dynasty took its name, whereas Clovis is the first documented king who actually united the tribes.
  • Childeric: Similar because both are early Merovingian royal names; differs because Childeric, Clovis's father, ruled only one small Salian Frankish kingdom, whereas Clovis multiplied that inheritance tenfold.
  • Chieftain: Similar because Clovis began as a Germanic tribal chieftain; differs because chieftain names the modest tribal office, while Clovis is the man who transformed the chieftainship into a royal realm.
  • King: Similar because Clovis is the archetypal early medieval king; differs because king is the generic title, whereas Clovis names the specific first king who united the Franks and converted the West.
  • Warlord: Similar because Clovis rose by conquest and warfare; differs because warlord stresses purely military power without the religious and dynastic legitimacy that Clovis deliberately acquired.
  • Convert: Similar because Clovis's baptism defines him; differs because convert is the general religious term, while Clovis names the king whose conversion permanently aligned the Franks with Catholic Christendom.
  • Founder: Similar because Clovis founded a dynasty and a nation; differs because founder is generic, whereas Clovis is specifically the founder of the Merovingian house and, in French memory, of France itself.
