Note: In archaic Greek anatomy, the phrēn (diaphragm or midriff) was regarded as the physical seat of both respiration and emotional consciousness. The medical noun phrenītis (formed with the disease suffix -ītis) was altered in Late Latin to phrenēsis through analogy with Greek nouns in -sis. In passing into Gallo-Romance, initial Greek ph- (/pʰ/) regularly shifted to f- (/f/). Middle English initially borrowed the trisyllabic frenesie, which by the 15th century underwent syncopation of the unaccented medial vowel to yield the modern disyllable frenzy.
