• on the whole: Similar because both express a general verdict while allowing reservations; differs because “on the whole” is the closest plainer synonym and tends to appear mid-clause, without the idiomatic nautical flavour of “by and large.”
  • for the most part: Similar because both qualify a broad statement; differs because “for the most part” implies a quantitative majority — most cases, most of the time — and is more precise about proportions, whereas “by and large” is airier and sums an impression.
  • generally speaking: Similar because both foreground a generalisation; differs because “generally speaking” is more explicit and grammatical about the act of generalising, while “by and large” is a fixed idiom that simply asserts the general truth.
  • all things considered: Similar because both sum up after weighing everything; differs because “all things considered” explicitly invites the weighing of counter-arguments and circumstances and is the most deliberative of the group.
  • in general: Similar because both assert a general truth; differs because “in general” is the plain, formal phrase used to mark the ordinary rule, whereas “by and large” adds a slightly informal, idiomatic, summative tone.
  • in the main: Similar because both assert that a statement holds for the greater part; differs because “in the main” is more literary and quantitative in feel and less common in modern speech, while “by and large” is the everyday idiom.
  • mostly / largely: Similar because both indicate that a statement applies to most cases; differs because these are single adverbs that directly modify the verb or sentence and quantify the majority, whereas “by and large” is a set phrase that frames the whole assertion.
  • overall: Similar because both give a summed, global judgement; differs because “overall” is a single, colourless adverb used in reporting and assessment, while “by and large” carries a more conversational, idiomatic, slightly old-fashioned flavour.
  • broadly speaking: Similar because both make a wide but not universal claim; differs because “broadly speaking” stresses the width of the claim, whereas “by and large” stresses that the exceptions are set aside and the general rule holds.
  • on balance: Similar because both weigh the whole picture before judging; differs because “on balance” implies an explicit weighing of pros and cons and is more deliberative, while “by and large” sums an impression without itemising the balance.
  • as a rule: Similar because both state what generally happens; differs because “as a rule” describes regular practice or custom, whereas “by and large” is a judgement of how things stand in the general case.
