- Late-antique reputation - Diocletian (reigned 284-305) was praised as the restorer of the Roman world after the Crisis of the Third Century, the emperor who reorganized the army, provinces, and coinage and founded the Tetrarchy.
- Christian tradition - in Christian memory he became the great persecutor, whose Edicts of 303 unleashed the last and fiercest persecution, so his name carried a dark religious charge through the Middle Ages.
- Enlightenment historiography - Gibbon presented Diocletian as the architect of the ceremonious, bureaucratic late empire, and Macaulay's image of the Eastern court that still exhibited the splendour of Diocletian and Constantine made his name a byword for imperial pomp amid slow decay.
- Contemporary sense - Diocletian denotes the reformer-emperor of the Tetrarchy, remembered for the four-man imperial college and for the persecution of Christians, and his palace at Split still bears his name.
