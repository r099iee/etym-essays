pipe + -ing

Modern English (present)
PIPING
"high thin sound; system of pipes; tube-shaped trim"

Note: Latin pīpāre is itself onomatopoeic — it imitates the peeping of chicks — and the “tube” sense of *pipa arises because a pipe, like a bird's throat or a reed, is a hollow passage for air. Debate: some scholars hold that Old English pīpe was borrowed directly from Latin pipare rather than through Vulgar Latin *pipa, and the echoic base *pi- is a descriptive reconstruction, not an attested form.
