No PIE root. *swish- is an imitative, sound-symbolic English base (echoic of something brushing or sweeping through the air). - Imitative base *swish- (echoic; no ancestor, the sound itself is the root) → 1756 English swish (v., intransitive) "to move with a swish or flourish, with a sound like 'swish'" → 1799 English swish (v., transitive) "to cause to swish; to flourish, brandish" → 1820 English swish (n.) "the sound of something moving through the air" → 1856 English swish (slang, v. and n.) "to flog, lash; a whip-stroke or its sound" → Modern English SWISH "onomatopoeic exclamation of a whip cutting through air; the sound or the movement" (also basketball slang from the 20th c., and slang adjective "fashionable" / "effeminate" from the 1930s). Note on debate: the word has no Indo-European or even Germanic pedigree beyond its own imitative creation in modern English; it belongs to the expressive sw- / wh- cluster (swoosh, whish, swash, whoosh) that English has coined repeatedly from sound. No stages before 1756 are attested, and none are reconstructed — the first recorded use in English is the verb in 1756.
