• Pansies: Similar because the pansy is a close relative in the very same genus Viola, a small cheerful garden flower; differs because pansies are large-faced cultivated hybrids (Viola × wittrockiana) while "violets" name the wild plants, and "pansy" has an extra slang sense ("a weakling") that violet never developed.
  • Garden violas: Similar because garden violas are species and hybrids of the very genus that violets belong to; differs because in ordinary English "violet" reserves the name for the small, often fragrant wild plant while "viola" names the modern bedding hybrids — gardeners keep the two trades separate.
  • African violets: Similar because the African violet is a small houseplant with violet-blue, five-petalled flowers that looks like a violet; differs because it is not a violet at all botanically (Saintpaulia, family Gesneriaceae, from East Africa) — the name is a compliment paid on looks alone.
  • Sweet violets: Similar because the sweet violet (Viola odorata) is the archetypal violet species — the fragrant, dark-purple flower of woods and banks; differs because it names one particular species where "violets" is the general plural — a hyponym rather than a rival.
  • Periwinkles: Similar because periwinkles are low ground-hugging plants with small blue-violet flowers; differs because they belong to a different family (Apocynaceae), are creeping evergreen trailers, and roll their flowers into tubes rather than nodding like a violet.
  • Lavender: Similar because it is both a purple-blue flower and the colour term derived from it; differs because it is an aromatic shrub of the mint family (Lamiaceae) and its colour is a paler, greyer blue with no red in it.
  • Lilacs: Similar because they are spring-flowering shrubs with dense clusters of purple flowers and give a purple colour-name; differs because their flowers are four-lobed tubes in panicles on a tall shrub or small tree, and "lilac" names a pinker, lighter purple.
  • Purple: Similar because both describe roughly the same colour-family, bluish-red or reddish-blue; differs because violet is a true spectral colour with its own wavelengths, while purple is not — any perceptual mixture of red and blue light; violet is specific, purple generic.
  • Bluebells: Similar because bluebells are small, early spring woodland flowers with violet-blue nodding bells; differs because they are bulbs (Hyacinthoides) of the asparagus family, and their fuzzy blue differs from the violet's dusky purple.
