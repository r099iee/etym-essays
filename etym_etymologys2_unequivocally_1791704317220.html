"ambiguous, having double meaning"
↓

↓
Early Modern English (c. 1590s)

↓
Modern English (c. 1765)
unequivocal

Modern English (c. 1790)
unequivocally

Note: Built from negative prefix un- attached to equivocal (from Late Latin aequivocus, compounding aequus 'equal' and vox 'voice', translating Aristotle's homōnymos 'having the same name') plus adverbial suffix -ly.
