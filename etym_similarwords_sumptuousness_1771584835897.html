• Luxury: Similar because it denotes wealthy comfort and lavishness; differs because luxury points at comfort, pleasure and self-indulgence rather than solemn, old-fashioned cost, whereas sumptuousness insists on great, formal expense.
  • Lavishness: Similar because it means great abundance and expense; differs because lavishness stresses reckless, open-handed quantity poured out, whereas sumptuousness is about coordinated, dignified costliness.
  • Opulence: Similar because it means conspicuous wealth; differs because opulence emphasises wealth and abundance in display, while sumptuousness adds the notion of expense as an enveloping, coordinated environment.
  • Magnificence: Similar because it means splendid grandeur; differs because magnificence emphasises the scale and splendour of the effect, whereas sumptuousness stresses the costly, luxurious materials and appointments that produce the effect.
  • Richness: Similar because it means abundance of costly or pleasing effect; differs because richness is plainer and broader, covering colour, texture and density in art and prose as well as money, while sumptuousness is confined to costly grandeur.
  • Splendour: Similar because it means impressive brilliance; differs because splendour is the dazzling visual effect itself, without the note of great expenditure, while sumptuousness is explicitly about the expense that produces the brilliance.
  • Extravagance: Similar because it means lavish spending; differs because extravagance carries a note of waste, excess or folly beyond the limits of reason, whereas sumptuousness is dignified, controlled costliness.
