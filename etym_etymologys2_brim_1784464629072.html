Uncertain PIE stage — often linked (speculatively) to *bʰrem- "to roar, hum, buzz" (Pokorny), the "roaring / boiling water" sense; this PIE attachment is debated and not securely established → Proto-Germanic *brim(m)a- / *brim- "surf, sea, swelling water" (reconstructed from Old English and Old Norse evidence) → Old English brim "sea, surf, ocean flood" (attested, e.g. Beowulf, c. 700–1000; also in compounds like brimwudu "ship") → Middle English brim / brymme "brink, edge of water, margin, shore" (c. 14th–15th c.); then "upper edge of a vessel" (15th c.) and "projecting edge of a hat" (16th c.) → Modern English BRIM — "projecting edge of a hat; upper edge of a vessel" Note (debated): whether the modern "edge" senses are the same word as the Old English "sea / surf" sense, or a separate borrowing from Low German / Middle Dutch edge-words (cf. Middle Dutch brem "border, edge"), is genuinely debated; the OED prints one headword, but the bridge from "surf" to "hat brim" is not directly attested and the PIE reconstruction is speculative.
