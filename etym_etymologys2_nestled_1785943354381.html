PIE *ni- 'down, below, into' + PIE *sed- 'to sit' (the root of English sit) -> PIE compound *nisdos (also *nizdo-) 'nest, resting place', literally 'where one sits down' -> Proto-Germanic *nestaz 'nest' (the cluster *-sd- becoming *-st- in Germanic) -> Old English nest 'nest' -> Old English nestlian 'to build a nest; to settle into or occupy a nest' (verb formed from nest with the frequentative suffix -lian) -> Middle English nestlen 'to settle in a nest' -> English nestle 'to settle snugly and comfortably' (the human sense emerging from the 16th century) -> NESTLED (past tense and past participle; the exact form of this card).

Cognates: the same *ni-sed- compound is visible across the family - Sanskrit nida 'nest, resting-place, couch', Latin nidus 'nest' (whence English nest, and the learned derivatives nidify, nidicolous), and the Slavic and Germanic forms - all meaning essentially 'a down-sitting place'. The composition of the root is secure: *ni- 'down' plus *sed- 'sit'.

Note: the vocalism is debated in detail - some scholars reconstruct *nisdos, others *nizdo- with the first element already assimilated; but the two-element compound is universally accepted, and there is no disagreement about the Germanic stage *nestaz onward.
