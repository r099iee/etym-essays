- [Ancient Roman Republic & Empire] — Religious ritual performed by Roman priests (augurs) who took the auspices (reading the flights and cries of sacred birds) to secure divine Jupiter's sanction before a magistrate entered office or a public building was founded — [perception: essential sacred consecration without which any state enterprise was religiously illicit and doomed to disaster]
- [Early 17th c. Early Modern English] — Borrowed directly from Latin inaugurāre during the Renaissance to describe the formal installation, sacred coronation, or solemn investiture of monarchs, prelates, and university chancellors — [perception: majestic ritual of supreme divine right, spiritual blessing, and constitutional legitimacy]
- [Late 18th–19th c. Enlightenment & Industrial Eras] — Extended into constitutional republican governance (e.g., presidential inaugurations in the United States and revolutionary France) and the ceremonial dedication of civil engineering marvels (railways, suspension bridges, public libraries, international exhibitions) — [perception: celebrated as a grand civic milestone marking technological and democratic progress]
- [20th–21st c. Modern & Contemporary Global English] — Abstracted into widespread figurative usage heralding the advent of transformative historical eras, groundbreaking scientific paradigms, medical breakthroughs, or corporate strategic initiatives — [perception: high-register, prestigious verb signifying a decisive, visionary beginning that shapes the future]
