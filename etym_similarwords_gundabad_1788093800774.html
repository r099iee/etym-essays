• Khazad-dûm (Moria): Similar because it is the other great Dwarvish hollow-mountain of the same range, the dwelling of the Longbeards; differs because Moria is in the south, a city built by the Dwarves that became a source of mithril and then fell to the Balrog, while Gundabad is the northern orc-capital.
  • Erebor (the Lonely Mountain): Similar because it is a great northern mountain tied to the Dwarves and to the events of The Hobbit; differs because Erebor is a lone peak in Rhovanion, home of the exiled kings, and is reclaimed by the Dwarves, whereas Gundabad stays in enemy hands.
  • Barad-dûr: Similar because it is the chief fortress-capital of the enemy in the same story-world; differs because it is Sauron's tower in Mordor, built with the power of the Ring, not a natural mountain citadel of Orcs.
  • Dol Guldur: Similar because it is a stronghold of Sauron and his servants in the North, a gathering place of evil power; differs because it is a hill-fort in Mirkwood with a sorcerous, tower-like character, not a hollowed mountain of the Dwarves.
  • Mount Doom (Orodruin): Similar because it is a dread mountain-name of Middle-earth associated with great evil; differs because it is a volcano in Mordor and the very furnace where the Ring was forged, whereas Gundabad is a natural mountain used as a mustering-point.
  • Carn Dûm: Similar because it is the capital of the Witch-king of Angmar in the far North, another northern seat of the enemy; differs because it was a fortress of the corrupt Men of Angmar in the western foothills, not a mountain of Dwarvish origin.
  • Thangorodrim: Similar because it is a great mountain-complex of the enemy in the far North, a byword for dark power in the First Age; differs because it was raised by Morgoth in Beleriand from volcanic debris and was destroyed in the War of Wrath, while Gundabad is a natural peak that survives into the Third Age.
  • Isengard (Orthanc): Similar because it is a mighty fortress in the region of the Misty Mountains whose orc-hosts threaten the free peoples; differs because it was built by the Men of Gondor, held by Saruman, and stands at the southern end of the range, far from the northern orc-capital.
