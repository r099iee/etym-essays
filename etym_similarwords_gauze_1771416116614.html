• Muslin: Similar because it is a fine, light, even translucent cotton fabric; differs because muslin is denser, softer, and more workaday — a plain-weave dressmaking staple — while gauze is more open, sheer, and structurally loose.
  • Tulle: Similar because it is a sheer, lightweight fabric; differs because tulle is a stiff net or mesh with openwork construction — the fabric of ballet tutus and bridal veils — while gauze is loosely plain-woven and softer.
  • Voile: Similar because it is a light, semi-sheer fabric; differs because voile is a crisp, closely woven cotton or synthetic for blouses and curtains, without gauze's loose open weave or its medical role.
  • Netting / Net: Similar because it is an open fabric with visible gaps; differs because netting is knotted or woven mesh, coarser and stiffer than gauze, used for traps, veils, and sport, not for soft dress or wound dressing.
  • Mesh: Similar because it is an open, see-through material; differs because mesh is a modern engineered grid (often synthetic, wire, or metal) with regular holes, lacking gauze's woven cloth character and its medical absorbency.
  • Cheesecloth: Similar because it is a very open, loose-woven cotton cloth, effectively a coarse gauze; differs because cheesecloth is unbleached, utilitarian, and used for straining and wrapping cheese, without gauze's fineness or polish.
  • Scrim: Similar because it is a translucent stage or theatre fabric used to veil scenes; differs because scrim is a special-effects material that turns transparent when lit from behind, a theatrical device rather than a dress or dressing fabric.
  • Gossamer: Similar because it denotes something extremely thin and diaphanous; differs because gossamer is a delicate spider-silk substance — or a poetic word for anything filmy — not a woven cloth with a defined weave.
  • Organza: Similar because it is a sheer, crisp fabric; differs because organza is a stiff, lustrous silk (or synthetic) with a crisp finish used for structured formal dresses, not a soft loose-weave cloth.
  • Chiffon: Similar because it is a light, translucent, draped fabric; differs because chiffon is a soft, slippery, fine-twisted sheer with a floating drape, whereas gauze has an open grid of threads and a firmer hand.
