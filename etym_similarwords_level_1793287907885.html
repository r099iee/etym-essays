• Flat: Similar because both describe surfaces without hills or bumps; differs because flat is the broader everyday word — flat land, flat beer, flat refusal — while level adds the precision of horizontality tested against the horizontal plane.
  • Even: Similar because both praise smoothness and equality of surface or score; differs because even stresses uniformity without lumps or advantage, while level stresses horizontal truth and equal height.
  • Horizontal: Similar because both name the plane at right angles to the vertical; differs because horizontal is the technical geometric term, while level is the working word of roads, voices, and temperaments.
  • Plane: Similar because a planed board is levelled smooth; differs because plane as adjective is literary or mathematical, while level walks the road and builds the wall.
  • Smooth: Similar because smooth roads please like level ones; differs because smooth describes freedom from roughness to the touch, while level describes freedom from slope to the stride — a road may be smooth yet steep.
  • Flush: Similar because both describe surfaces brought to one plane; differs because flush means exactly coterminous — doors flush with frames — while level means horizontal over distance, not merely edge-true.
  • Equal: Similar because level scores are equal scores; differs because equal is the abstract relation of sameness in any measure, while level pictures that equality as two heads at one height.
  • Steady: Similar because a level tone is a steady tone; differs because steady stresses unchanging continuance over time, while level stresses unvarying flatness of temper or grade.
