1870s–1890s — British polo players in India adopt the word for a period of play; it remains a colonial sporting term, coloured by the game’s Indian origins.
1898 — The word is recorded in English in its polo sense, at a time when polo is a fashionable imperial sport.
Early 20th c. — Standardised as six chukkers of seven and a half minutes; the term becomes part of the international vocabulary of the game.
Mid 20th c. — Chukka boots move from the stables into civilian menswear, giving the word a second, fashion life.
Late 20th c. — Polo becomes a niche professional and amateur sport; chukker survives mainly in commentary and club life, while chukka boot is widely known.
21st c. — A technical term in polo and a style word in fashion; occasional figurative use in journalism as a colourful synonym for a round or stint.
