- Antiquity (Assyria, Greece, Rome) - 'the ram' (krios / aries) - the engine of final breach, feared and mythologised by the besieged
- Medieval sieges - 'the ram' swings on ropes under a protective roof - standard siegecraft wherever walls and castles dominate
- 16th-17th c. - 'battering-ram' becomes the standard English name - historians' vocabulary; real military use fades as cannon arrive
- 18th-19th c. - a historical relic of the schoolbook past - reappears in art, poetry, and accounts of antiquity
- 19th-20th c. - figurative birth: 'a battering-ram of change / reform' - any force that smashes opposition; also the verb 'to ram' thrives
- Today - historical and metaphorical at once - epic similes in fantasy and journalism; grand, crushing, unstoppable in connotation
