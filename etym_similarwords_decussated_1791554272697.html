• Intersected: Similar because both describe lines, paths, or bodies crossing one another; differs because intersected is a general geometric term for any crossing at any angle, whereas decussated specifically denotes an X-shaped crossing, diagonal intersection, or reciprocal bilateral crossover.
  • Crisscrossed: Similar because both refer to lines passing over each other in various directions; differs because crisscrossed suggests an informal, dense, or disordered tangle of lines, whereas decussated denotes strict mathematical symmetry, bilateral order, or anatomical precision.
  • Cruciate: Similar because both refer to cross-shaped biological structures; differs because cruciate denotes a cross in the shape of a plus sign or crucifix (cross-shaped at right angles, as in the cruciate ligaments of the knee), whereas decussated denotes an oblique or diagonal crossing in the form of an X or saltire.
  • Chiasmic: Similar because both denote an X-shaped crossing or interchange; differs because chiasmic is often used in rhetoric, genetics (chiasmata during chromosomal crossover), and literary structure (chiasmus), whereas decussated is primarily anatomical, botanical, and geometric.
  • Transverse: Similar because both involve lying across a longitudinal axis; differs because transverse simply means positioned crosswise or perpendicularly (at right angles), without requiring that two opposing strands physically intersect each other.
  • Diagonal: Similar because both deviate from vertical and horizontal planes; differs because diagonal refers to the slanted orientation of a single line, whereas decussated requires the crossing of two or more lines through a common intersection.
  • Intercrossed: Similar because both indicate crossing between members; differs because intercrossed is frequently applied to genetic cross-breeding between distinct strains, while decussated focuses on spatial and structural geometry.
