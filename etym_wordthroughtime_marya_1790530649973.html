- 10th–14th Century: Adopted into Old East Slavic following the Christianization of Kievan Rus' (988 CE) as a premier Christian baptismal name honoring the Virgin Mary.
- 15th–17th Century: Became universally widespread across all strata of Muscovite society, from grand princely and Boyar lineages to peasant villages.
- 18th–19th Century: Flourished as the standard literary and secular Russian spelling (*Марья*), borne by Romanov empresses and immortalized by Pushkin and Tolstoy.
- Early to Mid-20th Century: Gradually replaced in standard Soviet civil registries and official documents by the formal canonical spelling *Maria* (*Мария*).
- Contemporary Usage: Cherished in modern Russian culture as a traditional, literary, and evocative historic variant of *Maria*, beloved in folklore and historical fiction.
