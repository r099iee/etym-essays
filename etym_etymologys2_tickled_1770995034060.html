No attested Proto-Indo-European root exists: the word is imitative/onomatopoeic, formed within Germanic, and a PIE etymon is not reconstructable (stated explicitly rather than guessed). → Proto-Germanic *tikkōną "to touch lightly" (reconstructed, on the imitative base *tik-, expanded with the frequentative suffix *-el-/-le) (c. 500 BCE-500 CE) → Middle English tikelen "to touch lightly, to excite, to tickle" (14th c., c. 1300) → Modern English tickle (verb) (attested from the 14th c. onward) → tickled (past participle; participial adjective "delighted, pleased, amused" by the 17th c.) → tickled (modern English, /ˈtɪkəld/) Note: the imitative origin is generally accepted; the debate is only about details. Latin titillare "to tickle" (English titillate) and Sanskrit kilikila "sounds of joy" are independent imitative formations, not cognates inherited from a common ancestor.
