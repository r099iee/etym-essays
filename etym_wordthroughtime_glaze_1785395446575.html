- [Craft origin (c. 14th c.)] - From glass, "glaze" named the smooth vitreous coating fused onto pottery and ceramics — a neutral, domestic term of craft, admired for its shine.
- [Culinary & decorative extension] - The same glossy finish transferred to food and surfaces — a pastry's glaze or a varnished board — still purely positive, associated with polish and finish.
- [Figurative leap into the body (19th–20th c.)] - "Eyes glaze": the bright, reflective, jewel-like vacancy of a dying or distracted gaze — death described in the language of a potter finishing a bowl, at once cosy and macabre.
- [Modern register] - To "glaze over" now also means to gloss over a difficulty, and "glazed" names the unseeing glassy look of shock or tedium; the shine has come to connote withdrawal of life and attention, not craft.
