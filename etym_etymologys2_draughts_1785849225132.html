CHAIN: FROM PIE TO DRAUGHTS (6 stages)

  1. Proto-Indo-European *dʰreǵʰ- "to draw, pull, drag" (reconstructed, c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE; the precise vocalism is debated in the handbooks — the Germanic family is built on this root, while Latin trahere "to drag" comes from a distinct root *tragh-).
  2. Proto-Germanic *draganą "to draw, pull, carry, lead" (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE; regular changes: PIE *dʰ- becomes PGmc *d-, PIE *ǵʰ becomes PGmc *g).
  3. Old English dragan "to draw, pull, drag, carry" (attested c. 8th–11th centuries; strong verb of Class VI, past tense drōg).
  4. Old English *dræht / *dreaht "that which is drawn; an act of drawing, a pull" (verbal noun built on the past stem of dragan; not directly attested in surviving Old English, but the regular formation appears in the record as early Middle English draht, 12th–13th centuries).
  5. Middle English draught / draght "a drawing, a pull, a portion drawn, a load" (12th–13th centuries); the specialized senses — a swallow of liquid, a current of air, a ship's depth, a portion of medicine — develop through the 14th–16th centuries.
  6. English draught (British spelling; American draft from the 18th–19th centuries); the plural DRAUGHTS is used (a) of repeated pulls or mouthfuls of liquid (the sense of this card) and (b) as the name of the board game from c. 1400, whose pieces are drawn forward across the board.

DEBATED / NUANCED POINTS

  • The Old English noun is reconstructed rather than attested; the earliest written form is Middle English draht/draught, so stage 4 rests on the regular formation of verbal nouns from the strong verb dragan.
  • The British draught / American draft spelling split is purely orthographic, beginning with 18th–19th century American printing practice and Noah Webster's spellings; it is not a sound change.
  • The PIE root is normally given as *dʰreǵʰ-, but the "pull" family also contains *tragh- (source of Latin trahere "to drag"); the two roots are kept distinct by modern etymologists, though older handbooks sometimes blurred them.
