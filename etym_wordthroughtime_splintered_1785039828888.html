- 13th-14th centuries - the noun splinter names a thin sharp fragment of wood or bone; the word is first a thing, not an act.
- 16th-17th centuries - to splinter becomes a verb; the past splintered records the completed act of breaking into long sharp slivers.
- 20th century - figurative use: splintered communities and parties, wholeness shattered into shards along the grain.
- Contemporary - splintered denotes final, finished fragmentation — needle-sharp little bits, in contrast to a simple smashing.
