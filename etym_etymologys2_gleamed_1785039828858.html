PIE root *ghel- "to shine, to be bright" (2nd millennium BCE; attested in derivatives across many branches) → extended stem *ghlei- / *glem- "to shine, gleam" (exact form of the extension debated) → Proto-Germanic *glaimiz "gleam, brightness" (c. 5th c. BCE–1st c. CE) → Old English glæm "beam of light, brightness, splendour" (c. 8th–11th c.) → Old English glæman "to shine, emit light" (denominative verb) → Middle English glemen "to shine, flash" (early 13th c.) → Modern English gleamed (preterite of gleam, weak past in -ed). Note on debate: the precise shape of the PIE extension (*ghlei- vs *glem- vs *ghlō-) is not recoverable with certainty; the Germanic *gl- light-family (glimmer, glisten, glitter, glow, glass, gloaming) all descend from the same root *ghel- through different extensions, so the branch leading specifically to gleam must be reconstructed rather than directly attested.
