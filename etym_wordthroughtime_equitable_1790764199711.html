- 16th Century: Borrowed into English from French *équitable*, derived from Latin *aequitas* (fairness, moderation, evenness).
- 17th Century: Formally integrated into English jurisprudence to designate rights, remedies, and proceedings originating in the Court of Chancery.
- 18th–19th Century: Widely adopted in Enlightenment political philosophy (Locke, Hume, Adam Smith) to describe fair contracts and social justice.
- 20th Century: Becomes a central term in international law, labor negotiations, and civil rights legislation (e.g., equitable distribution of marital property).
- 21st Century: Crucial pillar in public policy and educational theory, explicitly distinguishing context-sensitive *equity* from uniform *equality*.
