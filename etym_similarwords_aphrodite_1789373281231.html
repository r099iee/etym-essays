• Venus: Similar because Venus is the direct Roman mythological counterpart and syncretic equivalent of Aphrodite, presiding over romantic love, beauty, fertility, and gardens; differs because Venus was originally an ancient Italic agricultural goddess of vegetation and cultivated gardens before being fully Hellenized, and in Roman state religion she took on an imperial, dynastic role as *Venus Genetrix*, divine ancestress of Julius Caesar and the Roman people.
  • Ishtar: Similar because both are ancient Near Eastern/Mediterranean deities of sexual passion, beauty, fertility, and cosmic authority; differs because the Mesopotamian goddess Ishtar (Inanna) possessed a vastly more ferocious, explicitly martial, and catastrophic persona as the terrifying queen of war, storm, and divine retribution, whose descent into the Underworld shook cosmic order.
  • Astarte: Similar because Astarte is the Northwest Semitic (Phoenician/Canaanite) goddess of love and fertility who served as the direct historical cultural prototype from which the Greeks adopted and transformed many attributes of Aphrodite; differs because Astarte retained strong astral associations with the morning and evening star (Venus) and shared temples with Phoenician warrior gods in maritime trading ports like Cyprus and Cythera.
  • Freyja: Similar because both are polytheistic goddesses of love, beauty, sex, fertility, and gold who command immense mythic power over mortal and divine desire; differs because the Norse goddess Freyja also presides over *seiðr* (shamanic magic), receives half of all fallen warriors into her heavenly field Fólkvangr, and belongs to the distinct heroic, wintry cosmos of Germanic mythology.
  • Eros: Similar because both personify sexual attraction, passion, and the cosmic impulse of desire; differs because Eros (Cupid) is traditionally depicted either as Aphrodite's mischievous winged archer son or as a primordial cosmogonic deity representing the dynamic, kinetic driving force of attraction itself, whereas Aphrodite is the fully formed sovereign queen and embodiment of beauty, sexuality, and maritime grace.
  • Hathor: Similar because both are ancient Mediterranean/Near Eastern maternal goddesses of joy, feminine love, music, dance, and sensual pleasure; differs because the Egyptian goddess Hathor was symbolized as a celestial cow goddess nourishing the Pharaoh and the cosmos, possessing deep mortuary roles as the Lady of the West welcoming souls into the afterlife.
  • Siren: Similar because both embody the dangerous, irresistible allure of feminine beauty and maritime seduction that draws travelers toward desire; differs because a Siren is a monstrous mythological creature (part woman, part bird or fish) whose deceptive music lures mariners to shipwreck and death, whereas Aphrodite is a supreme Olympian divinity whose power encompasses cosmic creation and noble spiritual contemplation.
