supped is the past tense of sup, which in modern English merges two distinct historical verbs. Complete chains for each in isolation: Chain 1 — sup = "to drink in sips" (native Germanic line): 1. Proto-Indo-European *seuə- / *sū- "to take liquid, press out, suck, extract sap" (c. 4500–3500 BCE) — the root of "juice, liquid, suck"; note this PIE reconstruction is debated (alternative forms *seu-, *seub- are proposed). 2. Proto-Germanic *sūpaną "to drink in sips, swallow liquid" (reconstructed, c. 1st millennium BCE–CE) — a strong verb; cf. Old Norse súpa, Old High German sūfan (modern German saufen "to drink, guzzle"), Middle Dutch sūpen. 3. Old English sūpan "to sip, drink, taste liquid food" (attested c. 8th–11th c.). 4. Middle English suppen "to sip, drink" (attested c. 12th–15th c.). 5. English sup "to drink in small amounts, to sip" (modern); past tense supped — target root word. Chain 2 — sup = "to eat supper" (French/Germanic line): 1. Frankish / Proto-West Germanic *sopp- "sop, bread soaked in liquid" (reconstructed, c. 5th–8th c. CE) — itself from the same Germanic verb-family as *sūpaną "to sip/dip"; the "sop" is what one dips into broth. 2. Old French soupe "broth, slice of bread soaked in broth" (attested c. 10th–12th c.) — a Frankish loan into Gallo-Romance; source of English soup. 3. Old French souper "to take the evening meal" (attested 12th–13th c.) — the evening meal was a broth or sopped-bread repast, so "to make soup/sop" named the meal; also the source of the noun supper. 4. Middle English soupen "to eat the evening meal," converging with native suppen "to sip" (attested 13th–15th c.). 5. English sup "to eat the evening meal (supper)" (modern); past tense supped — target root word. Note on nuance/debate: the two sup verbs were near-homophones by the Middle English period and have since been treated as one verb, with supped serving as the past tense of both senses. The PIE reconstruction in Chain 1 is hypothetical; the Frankish source of French soupe is generally accepted, but the precise Germanic donor form is reconstructed. No attested stage has been invented, merged, or skipped.
