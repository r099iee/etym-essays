• peak: Similar because both name high ground; differs because a peak is a single, sharply pointed summit, whereas a ridge is the elongated crest-line running between or from such heights, emphasising linear extension rather than a point.
  • crest: Similar because both name the very top line of an elevation; differs because crest is the closest of all to "ridge" yet lacks its essential sense of length and continuity — a crest is a line, but a ridge is a line with the country falling away on both sides.
  • hill: Similar because both denote elevated land; differs because a hill is a rounded, isolated rise, whereas a ridge continues for a distance as part of a chain — the ridge is the hill extended into a line.
  • escarpment: Similar because both are continuous lines of elevated ground; differs because an escarpment is the steep, cliff-like edge of a plateau with a scarp face on one side, whereas a ridge has ground falling away on both sides of its crest.
  • spur: Similar because both are prolongations of a mountain mass; differs because a spur is a lateral projection branching off a main ridge, secondary and subordinate, whereas the ridge itself is the main crest-line.
  • arête: Similar because both are sharp mountain ridges; differs because an arête is specifically a narrow, knife-edged ridge carved by glacial erosion on both sides, a technical mountaineering term, whereas ridge is the general word.
  • mountain range: Similar because both involve long lines of high ground; differs because a range is a whole series of peaks and ridges forming a major orographic unit, whereas a ridge is a single crest-line within the landscape.
  • brow: Similar because both name the projecting top of a height; differs because brow is more limited (the brow of a hill is its projecting upper edge) and lacks the ridge's sense of length and the fall of ground on both sides.
  • spine: Similar because both name the backbone image of high ground; differs because spine is metaphorical and technical in geography (a mountain's spine) but literal in anatomy, whereas ridge keeps both the anatomical and the topographical lives of the same image.
  • divide: Similar because both can name a line of high ground; differs because a divide (a watershed) is defined by drainage — the line from which waters run to different seas — whereas a ridge is defined by its own crest-line shape.
