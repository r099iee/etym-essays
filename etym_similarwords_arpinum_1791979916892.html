• Arpino: Similar because it is the same town, continuously inhabited on the same hill, and the direct modern reflex of the ancient name; differs because Arpino denotes the medieval and modern Italian commune, while Arpinum denotes the Roman municipium known from Latin sources.
  • Tusculum: Similar because it was another Latian hill town within easy reach of Rome, famous for the villas of Roman senators, including Cicero's; differs because Tusculum was a Latin town of great antiquity and high prestige, whereas Arpinum was Volscian, smaller, and politically more marginal.
  • Praeneste: Similar because it was a strong hill town of Latium with massive polygonal walls and a long independent history; differs because Praeneste was a Latin city with a famous sanctuary of Fortuna, far larger and more prominent than Arpinum, and it became Palestrina rather than retaining its ancient name.
  • Cereatae: Similar because it was a village in the territory of Arpinum and the birthplace of Marius; differs because it was a dependent hamlet, not a self-governing town, and it has no independent political history.
  • Latium: Similar because Arpinum lay within the wider region the Romans called Latium adiectum; differs because Latium is a region and a cultural-legal category, not a town, and Arpinum was Volscian rather than one of the original Latin communities.
  • Volsci: Similar because the Volsci were the people who inhabited Arpinum before and during its conquest by Rome; differs because the name denotes an ethnic group and a language, not a place, and the Volsci disappear as a political entity after the Roman conquest.
  • municipium: Similar because Arpinum became a Roman municipium after 188 BCE and the term describes its constitutional status; differs because municipium is a legal category shared by many towns, not a proper name, and it tells us nothing about location.
  • patria: Similar because Cicero calls Arpinum one of his two fatherlands, the patria naturae alongside Rome, the patria civitatis; differs because patria is an abstract civic and emotional relationship, whereas Arpinum is the concrete place that grounds it.
