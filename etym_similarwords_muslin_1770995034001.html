• cotton: Similar because both are plant-fibre cloths and muslin is made from cotton; differs because cotton names the fibre and any fabric woven from it, while muslin specifies a fine, plain-weave, light cotton cloth.
  • calico: Similar because both are plain cotton fabrics; differs because calico is coarser and usually printed with patterns, while muslin is finer, plainer, and lighter.
  • gauze: Similar because both are light, open-weave cottons; differs because gauze is much more loosely woven, sheer, and translucent (used for bandages and veils), while muslin has a denser, firmer weave.
  • linen: Similar because both are natural, breathable woven cloths; differs because linen is made from flax fibre, is crisper and heavier, and wrinkles easily, while muslin is cotton and drapes softly.
  • chiffon: Similar because both are fine, sheer, luxurious fabrics; differs because chiffon is a slippery, lightweight weave traditionally of silk or synthetics, while muslin is matte and made of cotton.
  • cambric: Similar because both are fine, light cotton cloths; differs because cambric is a denser, closely woven, more lustrous cotton (originally linen from Cambrai), while muslin is more openly woven and matte.
  • organdy: Similar because both are fine cotton fabrics; differs because organdy is a stiff, crisp, sheer cotton finished with a starch-like sizing, while muslin is soft and unstiffened.
  • lawn: Similar because both are fine, sheer cotton fabrics; differs because lawn is even finer and crisper than muslin, used for handkerchiefs and summer clothing.
  • voile: Similar because both are light, translucent cottons; differs because voile has a more open, curtain-like weave and is often used for drapery, while muslin covers a broader, sturdier range of weights.
  • cheesecloth: Similar because both are open-weave utility cottons used for straining; differs because cheesecloth is extremely loose, coarse, and disposable, while muslin is a firmer, more finished fabric.
