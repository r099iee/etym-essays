• Arrival: Similar because both denote a reaching of a place or point; differs because arrival is the neutral, everyday word with no grandeur — a train arrives — whereas advent is grand, destined, and momentous.
  • Coming: Similar because both mean a coming into view or being; differs because coming is vaguer and more intimate, suited to persons one expects, while advent is impersonal and reserved for epochs, movements, and public figures.
  • Approach: Similar because both involve drawing near; differs because approach emphasizes the process of drawing near and may never be completed, while advent marks the completed, momentous arrival.
  • Emergence: Similar because both name an appearance; differs because emergence stresses gradual appearance from concealment, whereas advent marks a single momentous, long-anticipated arrival.
  • Onset: Similar because both name the beginning of something; differs because onset is used of something unwelcome or neutral (the onset of winter, of illness), while advent carries an aura of importance and providential destiny.
  • Inception: Similar because both name a beginning; differs because inception is a cold, technical word for the start of an enterprise, while advent is warm, grand, and invested with significance.
  • Commencement: Similar because both name a beginning; differs because commencement is a formal, neutral word for the start of any process, while advent insists on the importance and destiny of what comes.
  • Appearance: Similar because both name a becoming visible or present; differs because appearance is neutral and momentary (the appearance of a stain, of a star), while advent is grand and era-changing.
  • Debut: Similar because both name a first coming into public view; differs because debut implies a performance or launch by the one arriving, while advent marks the momentous coming of something that was awaited.
  • Dawn: Similar because both metaphorically mark the beginning of an era; differs because dawn evokes the gradual light of day and is used of beginnings that unfold, while advent is the arrival itself, sudden and complete.
  • Accession: Similar because both name the formal coming of a ruler or era; differs because accession is the specific word for a monarch taking the throne, while advent is general and carries religious resonance.
  • Eve: Similar because both belong to the language of anticipation; differs because eve is the period immediately before an event (the eve of something), while advent is the arrival of the thing itself.
  • Coming-into-being: Similar because both name a coming into existence; differs because coming-into-being is a neutral philosophical phrase, while advent adds grandeur, anticipation, and destiny.
