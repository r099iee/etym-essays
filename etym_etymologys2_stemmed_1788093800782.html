Note on the reconstructed steps (debated): the Proto-Germanic form *stamjan- and its link to PIE *stebh- involve a nasal-infixed zero grade; some handbooks instead posit a separate Germanic root *stam- for this verb, so the deepest two stages are standard reconstructions whose precise shape is debated — the attested record begins with Old Norse stemma. Separate word families of the same shape 'stem': (a) the plant-stem noun — Old English stemn/stefn, from Proto-Germanic *stamniz 'stem, trunk' (whence 'stemmed' = having stems removed, 'long-stemmed', and the phrasal verb 'stem from' = originate); (b) the ship's stem (prow) — from Old Norse stafn, Proto-Germanic *stabna-; source of 'from stem to stern' and of the nautical component of 'stem the tide'. Both belong to the same ancient *stebh- family but are separate lexemes from the dam-verb presented above.
