- 14th to 15th Century: Late Middle English dynamic physical confluence - Signified the physical running together, collision, or mutual flowing of rivers, celestial bodies, or running armies.
- 16th to 17th Century: Human assembly, political congregation, and social confluence - Widely used in political philosophy and Renaissance literature to describe an assembling crowd or meeting of citizens.
- 18th Century: Classical natural philosophy and atomism - Employed in physics and theology (e.g., the 'fortuitous concourse of atoms' in Epicurean cosmology vs. divine providence).
- 19th to 21st Century: Architectural and transportation infrastructure - Shifted primarily toward the spacious open halls of railway stations, airport terminals, and sports stadiums where pedestrian thoroughfares converge.
