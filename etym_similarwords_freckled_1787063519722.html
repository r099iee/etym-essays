• speckled: Similar because both mean marked with small spots; differs because speckled covers any scattered dots and is blander, while freckled usually implies skin or organic markings.
  • spotted: Similar because both mean covered with spots; differs because spotted can imply large or irregular marks, freckled specifically small flat ones.
  • dappled: Similar because both mean marked with patches of colour; differs because dappled suggests mottled light-and-shade, usually in larger patches.
  • mottled: Similar because both describe irregular coloured markings; differs because mottled suggests blotchy, uneven patches, often on skin or foliage, and is more clinical.
  • flecked: Similar because both mean sprinkled with small marks; differs because flecked refers to any small flecks and is more neutral in register.
  • pied: Similar because both describe multi-coloured marking; differs because pied means large contrasting patches (as in magpies), not small freckles.
  • stippled: Similar because both describe a surface of fine dots; differs because stippled is usually a technique or effect (painting, texture) rather than a natural marking.
  • pockmarked: Similar because both describe marked skin; differs because pockmarked implies scars and pits, not flat pigment spots, and is negative.
  • blotched: Similar because both describe discoloured markings; differs because blotched means large irregular patches, not small neat dots.
