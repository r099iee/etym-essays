• Manual: Similar because both describe physical, hand-done labour; differs because manual is neutral and can be skilled (a master craftsman works manually), whereas menial carries the social degradation of low rank.
  • Servile: Similar because both denote lowly subordination; differs because servile emphasizes a fawning, sycophantic attitude of subservience, while menial names the demeaning nature of the tasks themselves.
  • Unskilled: Similar because both describe work requiring little training; differs because unskilled is a purely economic term without the master-servant, domestic hierarchy that menial implies.
  • Demeaning: Similar because both describe work that lowers a person's dignity; differs because demeaning describes the emotional effect of work and applies broadly, while menial identifies the specific low-status class of tasks.
  • Degrading: Similar because both describe work that strips dignity; differs because degrading can apply to any humiliating treatment, whereas menial is firmly rooted in physical, often domestic servitude.
  • Lowly: Similar because both describe low rank or humble status; differs because lowly is gentler and can even carry a modest virtue (a lowly shepherd), while menial is sharply dismissive and disparaging.
  • Humble: Similar because both describe unpretentious, low-status positions; differs because humble may be praised or neutral (humble origins), whereas menial carries contempt and social erasure.
  • Base: Similar because both imply a low, ignoble standing; differs because base suggests moral or material vileness and is far stronger, while menial is limited to the lowliness of the work and station.
