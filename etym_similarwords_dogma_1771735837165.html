• Doctrine: Similar because both are bodies of principles taught within a church, school, or institution; differs because doctrine is the broader, more neutral term for any teaching or set of beliefs, while dogma is specifically a doctrine declared by authority as incontrovertibly true and closed to question.
  • Tenet: Similar because both are single beliefs held by a group; differs because a tenet is merely a principle a group holds, with no implication of authoritative enforcement, while a dogma is a tenet imposed as beyond argument.
  • Creed: Similar because both are formal statements of belief; differs because a creed is a fixed public confession or summary of faith (as in the Nicene Creed), which may be recited by whole communities, while dogma emphasizes the authority that lays the belief down.
  • Principle: Similar because both are general rules or truths underlying conduct and thought; differs because a principle is a foundational rule open to reasoned defense, while a dogma is asserted as immune to debate.
  • Belief: Similar because both involve what one holds to be true; differs because belief is the broadest term, a personal conviction held with or without evidence or authority, while dogma is institutionalized, imposed, and binding.
  • Orthodoxy: Similar because both concern right belief within a community; differs because orthodoxy is the whole body of correct doctrine a community recognizes, while dogma is a specific tenet within that body, typically one authoritatively declared.
  • Precept: Similar because both are authoritative rules; differs because a precept is a rule of conduct or behaviour — a command to act — while a dogma is a rule of belief, a truth to be accepted rather than an action to be performed.
  • Ideology: Similar because both are systems of ideas imposed by a group; differs because ideology is a full worldview or belief-system, often political and secular, while dogma is a single asserted tenet or set of tenets, usually with religious or doctrinal weight.
  • Doctrinaire (adj.): Similar because both describe rigid adherence to theory; differs because doctrinaire stresses the mechanical application of a doctrine to practice, whereas dogmatic stresses the refusal to question the doctrine itself.
