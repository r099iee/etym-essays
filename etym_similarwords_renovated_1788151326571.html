• Restored: Similar because both describe returning something to a good or previous condition; differs because restore implies recovering an earlier or original state (even restoring an artifact), while renovate implies modernising and refreshing rather than merely returning to the past.
  • Refurbished: Similar because both mean cleaned up and renewed, especially of buildings and goods; differs in that refurbish (“to furbish again”) stresses brightening and repair of surfaces, with a slightly commercial ring (refurbished electronics), while renovate suggests deeper structural renewal.
  • Renewed: Similar because both derive from the same Latin notion of making new; differs by being far more general — renewed can describe contracts, friendships, visas, and vigour, while renovated keeps a concrete, artefact-shaped core.
  • Revamped: Similar as informal speech for an overhauled, refreshed result; differs in register (colloquial, punchy) and in suggesting a change of form or style rather than careful repair.
  • Remodelled: Similar because both describe changing a building for current use; differs in that remodel stresses altering the layout or design (walls moved, rooms reconceived), whereas renovate can leave the plan intact while renewing fabric and finishes.
  • Redeveloped: Similar as a large-scale renewal of an area or site; differs in implying demolition and rebuilding or comprehensive urban planning rather than repair of a single structure.
  • Modernised: Similar because renovation usually brings things up to date; differs in that modernise is about updating to current standards and technology, while renovate is about making new again, which may involve respecting the old.
  • Overhauled: Similar because both mean taken apart and thoroughly made good, especially of machinery or systems; differs in that overhaul is mechanical and functional, while renovate is architectural and aesthetic.
  • Rejuvenated: Similar as a figurative renewal of vigour; differs in being almost entirely biological or emotional — people are rejuvenated, houses are renovated.
  • Revivified / revived: Similar as figurative renewal of life or spirit; differ by being strongly animistic and abstract, while renovated covers both the concrete and the spiritual.
  • Refreshed: Similar as a lighter form of renewal; differs in being mild, reversible, and largely mental or physical, with none of the craft or labour of renovation.
