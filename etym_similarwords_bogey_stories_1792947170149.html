• Ghost stories: Similar because both are fright tales told in the dark; differs because ghost stories centre on the returning dead and hauntings, while bogey-stories centre on vague nursery goblins and warnings.
  • Fairy tales: Similar because both are nursery narratives of marvels; differs because fairy tales are wondrous and often moral with fairies and quests, while bogey-stories are purely frightening and cautionary.
  • Old wives tales: Similar because both are dismissed as unscientific nursery lore; differs because old wives tales are superstitions about health and luck, while bogey-stories are monster scares for children.
  • Scare stories: Similar because both aim to frighten into obedience; differs because scare stories is the general modern term (press, politics), while bogey-stories keeps the goblin and the nursery.
  • Bugbears: Similar because a bugbear is itself a nursery goblin and a needless fright; differs because bugbear names the monster or the pet peeve itself, while bogey-stories names the tales told about it.
  • Nursery tales: Similar because both are told by nurses to small children; differs because nursery tales include rhymes, lullabies, and merry tales, while bogey-stories are only the frightening subset.
  • Tall tales: Similar because both exaggerate beyond belief; differs because tall tales boast and amuse with impossible feats, while bogey-stories threaten and warn with lurking things.
  • Hobgoblin lore: Similar because both trade in goblins and household sprites; differs because hobgoblin lore is folk belief collected by scholars, while bogey-stories are the living telling in the nursery itself.
  • Cautionary tales: Similar because both warn the young against danger; differs because cautionary tales are moral and often literary, while bogey-stories are raw fright, wolves and goblins without the moral tag.
