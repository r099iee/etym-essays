• aspect: Similar because both mean a way of looking at or side of a thing; differs because aspect is purely abstract and viewpoint-centred, while facet keeps the concrete image of a cut face.
  • side: Similar because both mean one part of a whole; differs because side is plainer and can mean any boundary or faction, while facet implies a polished, discrete plane.
  • face: Similar because both are surfaces turned outward (and facet literally means 'little face'); differs because face is the whole surface, while a facet is one small plane among many.
  • surface: Similar because both are outer boundaries; differs because surface is a single continuous area, while facets are the separate flat units that together form it.
  • dimension: Similar because both suggest a way in which something can be analysed; differs because dimension is a measure or axis of scale, while facet is a qualitative side or angle.
  • angle: Similar because both name a point of view on something; differs because angle stresses the slant of approach, while facet stresses the many-sidedness of the object itself.
  • viewpoint / perspective: Similar because both are figurative ways of seeing; differ because they name the observer's position, whereas facets are qualities of the thing observed.
  • phase: Similar because both are distinct stages or sides of a thing; differs because phase is temporal (a stage in time), while facet is spatial (a simultaneous side).
  • plane: Similar because both are flat surfaces; differs because plane is mathematical and continuous, while a facet is a small finite face of a cut object.
