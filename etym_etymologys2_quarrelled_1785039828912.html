Earliest proposed stage (debated): PIE *kues- "to hiss, sigh" — hypothesised by some scholars (cf. Sanskrit svas- "to breathe, hiss, snort"); the deeper PIE link is uncertain → Proto-Italic *kwese- "to complain" → Latin queri "to complain, lament, bewail" (1st c. BCE) → Latin querella / querela "complaint, accusation, lamentation" (a legal grievance) → Old French querele "dispute, controversy, grievance; also business, concern" (c. 10th–12th c.) → Middle English querele "dispute, altercation; ground for complaint; also armed combat" (mid-14th c.) → Modern English quarrel "angry contention between persons" (1570s) → QUARRELLED (past tense and past participle; British doubled -l- plus -ed) Debated / nuanced note: The link of Proto-Italic *kwese- to a PIE root *kues- is a hypothesis, not a certainty; no better etymology has been established, but the deeper root is disputed. Do NOT merge this word with the homonym quarrel "crossbow bolt," which descends independently from Latin quadrus "square" < PIE *kwetwer- "four." Related formations: Latin querulus "full of complaints" gave querulous; Old French querelos gave quarrelsome.
