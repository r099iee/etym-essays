• delighted: Similar because both mean feeling strong pleasure; differs because delighted is heavier, more formal, and outwardly expressive, whereas tickled is lighter, more playful, and often privately amused.
  • pleased: Similar because both mean feeling satisfaction; differs because pleased is a calm, steady state of approval with no sudden spark, whereas tickled implies a spontaneous, almost physical burst of joy.
  • amused: Similar because both mean being entertained or charmed; differs because amused is neutral about the source and tone of entertainment, whereas tickled adds warm, personal gratification.
  • flattered: Similar because both involve feeling good about oneself; differs because flattered comes from external praise and hints at vanity, whereas tickled is innocent, delighted amusement.
  • thrilled: Similar because both mean strongly pleased; differs because thrilled is intense, excited, and agitating, whereas tickled is gentle, light, and amusing.
  • charmed: Similar because both mean pleasantly captivated; differs because charmed suggests winning grace or enchantment, whereas tickled suggests humorous amusement.
  • gladdened: Similar because both mean made happy; differs because gladdened is literary and connotes relief from worry, whereas tickled is colloquial and connotes amusement.
  • gratified: Similar because both mean pleased by something; differs because gratified is formal and suggests satisfied desire, whereas tickled is informal and playful.
  • overjoyed: Similar because both mean very happy; differs because overjoyed is ecstatic and overwhelming, whereas tickled is a modest spark of delight.
  • entertained: Similar because both mean experiencing pleasure; differs because entertained is about being diverted by external activity, whereas tickled is an inner state of amusement.
  • happy: Similar because both describe a positive emotional state; differs because happy is broad, enduring, and character-based, whereas tickled is momentary, situational, and light.
  • titillated: Similar because both derive from "tickle" and mean pleasantly excited; differs because titillated implies intellectual or sensual stimulation, whereas tickled implies innocent amusement.
