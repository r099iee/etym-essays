Note on historical etymology and linguistic debate: Modern comparative linguists (including Manfred Mayrhofer in his Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindoarischen and Thomas Burrow) classify the ethnonym Śabara (also written Savara) as an ancient loan from an indigenous non-Indo-European substrate, most likely an Austroasiatic (Proto-Munda) source cognate with the modern ethnonym Sora (or Saora), the indigenous tribe residing in the hill tracts of Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, and central India. While classical Sanskrit grammarians attempted indigenous Indo-Aryan folk-etymologies—such as deriving śabara from the root śav- ("to alter, disfigure") or associating it with śabala ("variegated, speckled")—these are post-hoc scholastic rationalizations. The name is fundamentally an ancient ethnonym designating the indigenous forest inhabitants of central India, and Śabarī represents its regular feminine derivative formed with the standard feminine suffix -ī (technically the ṅīp pratyaya in Pāṇinian grammar).
