- [13th–14th c.] - justly first appears as the adverb of just, meaning "in a just manner, righteously" - [perception: moral and religious register, found beside them in devotional prose]
- [15th–17th c.] - with the rise of law and science, justly also means "exactly, precisely, conforming to rule" - [perception: it becomes the jurist’s and physician’s word for due measure]
- [18th–19th c.] - criticism delights in it: "justly observed", "justly censured" become the critic’s seal of fair dealing - [perception: the adverb gains an air of scholarly even-handedness]
- [20th–21st c.] - the "precisely" sense is absorbed by the adjective just ("just so"); justly narrows to fairness, rightness, deservedness and settles into formal written English - [perception: measured, judicial, a little old-fashioned; speech prefers rightly]
