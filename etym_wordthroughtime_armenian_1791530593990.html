- Fourteenth century - Armenian enters English through Old French as a geographical and religious label for the people and language of Armenia; neutral, remote, Eastern Christian.
- Nineteenth century - The word becomes tied to the "Armenian question" and the massacres of 1894-1896; connotation victimhood, persecution, and European diplomacy.
- 1915 and after - The genocide makes Armenian inseparable from mass death, deportation, and diaspora memory.
- Soviet period - Armenian denotes the Armenian SSR and its culture; the diaspora keeps a rival Western Armenian identity alive.
- Twenty-first century - A neutral national and linguistic term, but heavily charged in Armenian Genocide, Armenian diaspora, and the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict.
