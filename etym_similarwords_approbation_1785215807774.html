• Approval: Similar because both express a favourable judgement; differs because approval is the everyday, less formal term, while approbation is dignified and impersonal — approval given after judgement or testing, retaining the old Latin flavour of "proved good".
  • Commendation: Similar because both are formal praise; differs because commendation stresses spoken or written praise of excellence and often has a ceremonial or military flavour, whereas approbation is quieter and implies endorsement by a trusted judge.
  • Sanction: Similar because both signify official agreement; differs because sanction emphasises authoritative, often legal permission or enforcement, while approbation is moral-intellectual approval rather than institutional permission.
  • Endorsement: Similar because both support a person or work; differs because endorsement in modern use is public, often commercial or political backing (as of a product or candidate), while approbation retains an older, weightier, "proved good" character.
  • Assent: Similar because both indicate agreement; differs because assent is a bare, often passive agreement or consent to a proposition, whereas approbation is an active, approving judgement of worth.
  • Acclaim: Similar because both express praise; differs because acclaim is loud, public, enthusiastic applause, while approbation is measured, considered, and often private — the verdict of one trusted judge rather than the roar of a crowd.
  • Praise: Similar because both commend what is good; differs because praise is general and emotional, spoken or written at any intensity, whereas approbation is formal, weighty, and implies a testing of value.
  • Blessing: Similar because both confer a favourable judgement; differs because blessing carries religious or providential connotations and implies grace conferred, while approbation is the reasoned, secular verdict of approval.
