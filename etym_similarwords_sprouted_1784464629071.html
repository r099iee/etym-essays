• Grew: Similar because both mean to increase in size or develop; differs because growing is general and slower-feeling, while sprouting is the sudden, rapid emergence of new shoots.
  • Sprang up: Similar because both mean to appear suddenly; differs because springing up is sudden appearance from anywhere, while sprouting keeps the shoot-from-below image of germination from seed or root.
  • Blossomed: Similar because both mean to develop or flourish; differs because blossoming stresses flowering, success, and culmination, while sprouting is the earlier, raw, vigorous emergence of shoots.
  • Erupted: Similar because both mean to burst out suddenly; differs because erupting is violent, involuntary, and often destructive, while sprouting is organic, productive, and growth-filled.
  • Proliferated: Similar because both mean to multiply rapidly; differs because proliferating is scientific, abstract, and cold, while sprouting is the lively, organic image of green shoots.
  • Germinated: Similar because both mean to begin growing from seed; differs because germinating is the precise botanical term for the seed's first growth, while sprouting is the wider word covering shoots, buds, and sudden appearance.
  • Budded: Similar because both mean to put forth new growth; differs because budding names the forming of buds rather than the breaking of shoots, and carries none of the "spring up in abundance" force of sprouting.
