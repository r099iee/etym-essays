Origin genuinely uncertain — the earliest stage cannot be securely reconstructed, and no reliable PIE root exists for this family. Stage 1 (conjectural, marked uncertain) — Old English mot "speck, mote, particle of dust" (attested c. 9th-10th centuries), the proposed native base for the idea of "speckled"; alternatively a Frankish / Gaulish base *motta "clod, patch" (compare the "motte" of motte-and-bailey castles). Both proposals are speculative; no PIE root can be reconstructed with confidence. Stage 2 — Anglo-French motteley "variegated, parti-colored; parti-colored cloth" (recorded late 13th-14th centuries), entering Middle English as motley (14th century); the sense "parti-colored dress of the court fool" is fixed by c. 1400. Stage 3 — English mottle (verb) "to mark with spots or blotches of different colors" (back-formation from motley; first recorded 1670s). Stage 4 — English mottled (participial adjective) "marked with irregular blotches, variegated" (first recorded 1670s-1700s). —> mottled. Debate note: The ultimate source of motley / mottled is obscure (the OED records it as "of uncertain etymology"). The link to Old English mot "speck" is plausible but unproven; the often-repeated story of a Latin mota "clod of earth" is speculative. Stage 1 is therefore explicitly conjectural.
