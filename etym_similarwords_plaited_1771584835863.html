• Braided: Similar because both describe strands interwoven over and under into a single band or tail; differs because braided is the everyday American term and may name many small braids, while plaited is the British, slightly old-fashioned word and usually implies the neat three-strand braid of hair.
  • Twisted: Similar because both combine strands into one; differs because twisting rotates strands around one another without the over-under interlacing of a plait, producing a looser, rounder, less structured result.
  • Interwoven: Similar because both describe strands worked into one another to form a single texture; differs because interwoven is the general, technical word for any crossing of threads or elements, while plaited specifically means the regular over-under braid construction.
  • Woven: Similar because both involve interlacing strands; differs because weaving properly uses a loom with warp and weft at right angles, while plaiting interlaces free strands diagonally by hand, without a loom.
  • Pleated: Similar because both come from the same root (Latin plicāre "to fold") and share an orderly, folded look; differs because pleated refers to cloth pressed into parallel folds, while plaited refers to hair or strands braided together.
  • Knitted: Similar because both build texture from the repeated crossing or looping of yarn; differs because knitting works a single continuous thread into loops, while plaiting interlaces distinct strands crossing over one another.
  • Entwined: Similar because both describe things wound together; differs because entwined suggests things twisting about each other, often just two strands or living growth, while plaited implies a neat, structured, multi-strand braid.
  • Coiled: Similar because both arrange a flexible material into an orderly shape; differs because coiling wraps a single strand in circles about a centre, while plaiting interlaces several strands together.
