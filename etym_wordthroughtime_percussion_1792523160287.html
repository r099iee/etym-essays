- [16th c. Early Modern English] — Borrowed via French from Latin percussio, initially applied in medical and surgical tracts to denote physical trauma from a violent blow or the rhythmic pulsation of arteries — [perception: technical surgical descriptor for physical impact or bodily shock]
- [17th c. Scientific Revolution] — Adopted into classical kinematics and Newtonian mechanics to describe the violent collision, elastic rebound, and momentum transfer between physical masses — [perception: fundamental concept in mathematical physics and mechanical philosophy]
- [18th c. Enlightenment & Medical Innovation] — Pioneered by Austrian physician Leopold Auenbrugger as a revolutionary clinical diagnostic technique (Inventum Novum), tapping the patient's chest to determine the resonant or dull health of internal organs — [perception: celebrated clinical breakthrough in non-invasive medical diagnosis]
- [19th c. Industrial & Firearms Revolution] — Revolutionized firearm technology through Alexander Forsyth's invention of the percussion cap (detonating fulminate of mercury), while simultaneously crystallizing in orchestral orchestration as the formal designation for all struck musical instruments — [perception: cutting-edge ballistic mechanism and vital acoustic orchestral family]
- [20th–21st c. Modern Music & Industrial Technology] — Elevated by 20th-century avant-garde composers (Edgard Varèse, John Cage) into the primary voice of modern music, while applying broadly in engineering (percussion drilling, shockwave sensors, electronic drum synthesis) — [perception: universal term for kinetic collision, musical rhythm, and acoustic exploration]
