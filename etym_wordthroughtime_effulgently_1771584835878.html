- [18th century: Latinate poetic coinage] - 'Effulgent' enters English as a deliberately literary adjective meaning shining forth brilliantly; connotation is rarified, classical, and confined to poetry and elevated prose.
- [19th century: Victorian and Romantic radiance] - The word becomes a favourite of Romantic and Victorian writers for divine, celestial, and moral brightness; 'effulgently' connotes not just light but an inner brilliance overflowing its vessel - brightness as virtue and erudition made visible.
- [Modern usage] - 'Effulgently' remains a highly poetic adverb, rare in speech; connotation is solemn, aspirational, and almost liturgical - chosen where 'radiantly' would be too plain and 'dazzlingly' too informal.
