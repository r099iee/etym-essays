• Agape: Similar because both mean wide open, especially of a mouth; differs because agape is an adjective/adverb meaning "with the mouth open" (as in "mouth agape") and is slightly archaic, whereas gaping is a common working adjective for any wide opening.
  • Yawning: Similar because both mean very wide open; differs because yawning stresses the vast cavity or depth (a yawning chasm, a yawning gap), while gaping stresses the split or parted edges of an opening.
  • Open: Similar because both describe an unclosed state; differs because open is neutral and general, whereas gaping adds dramatic width, exposure, and usually an emotional colour of danger or wonder.
  • Wide: Similar because both describe great breadth; differs because wide is a bare measure of extent, while gaping describes the striking openness of a specific opening and the surprise it causes.
  • Ajar: Similar because both relate to doors being open; differs because ajar means only slightly open — the near-opposite of gaping, which means flung fully open.
  • Staring: Similar because both can describe fixed, unblinking attention (gaping at / staring at); differs because staring is a look with wide eyes, while gaping is specifically an open-mouthed look and also a quality of openings themselves.
  • Cracked / split: Similar because both describe damaged openings; differs because they name the act of damage, while gaping describes the resulting wide-open state.
  • Open-mouthed: Similar because both mean with the mouth wide open; differs because open-mouthed is a more literal compound, usually of people only, while gaping also applies to holes, doors, and wounds.
