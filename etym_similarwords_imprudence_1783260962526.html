• Recklessness: Similar in meaning a lack of caution; differs because recklessness implies deliberate disregard of danger and a headlong quality, while imprudence is a want of foresight and need not be deliberate.
  • Carelessness: Similar in denoting a failure to take care; differs because carelessness is lighter and more general and lacks any implication of defective judgment, while imprudence implicates practical wisdom itself.
  • Rashness: Similar in being a lack of caution; differs because rashness stresses undue haste and acting on impulse, while imprudence is the broader failure to foresee consequences.
  • Foolhardiness: Similar in being unwise risk-taking; differs because foolhardiness is courage without judgment — bravery misapplied — while imprudence may involve no courage at all.
  • Negligence: Similar in being a failure of care; differs because negligence is the legal and moral failure to exercise due care against a standard of duty, while imprudence is a failure of foresight and judgment.
  • Indiscretion: Similar in being an imprudent act; differs because indiscretion applies chiefly to speech and social conduct, while imprudence covers judgment generally.
  • Improvidence: Similar in meaning lack of foresight; differs because improvidence is specifically the failure to provide for the future, especially financially, while imprudence is the general want of foresight.
  • Temerity: Similar in denoting rashness; differs because temerity stresses audacity or boldness in taking risks, while imprudence stresses the want of foresight rather than the boldness.
  • Heedlessness: Similar in meaning not paying attention; differs because heedlessness is a failure of attention to what is present, while imprudence is a failure to foresee what is coming.
  • Thoughtlessness: Similar in meaning acting without consideration; differs because thoughtlessness is a lighter, momentary failure to reflect, while imprudence is a blameworthy want of practical wisdom.
  • Injudiciousness: Similar in meaning lack of good judgment; differs because injudiciousness is a failure of discretion in a particular act or remark, while imprudence is the state of lacking foresight.
  • Folly: Similar in meaning a foolish action or quality; differs because folly is broader and often carries overtones of absurdity or moral weakness, while imprudence is specifically the want of foresight and caution.
