• Moisture: Similar because both name a slight degree of wetness, the presence of water in or on something; differs because moisture is the broad, neutral, often welcome term (soil moisture, skin moisture), while dampness is the perceptible chill of absorbed water — felt rather than seen, and usually unwelcome.
  • Wetness: Similar because both describe the presence of liquid; differs because wetness is the state of being covered or saturated, more extreme and usually visible, while dampness is subtle, pervasive, and only felt on skin or stone — dampness stops short of soaking.
  • Humidity: Similar because both involve water in the air; differs because humidity is the technical, quantitative meteorological measure of water vapour as a percentage, carrying no emotional charge, while dampness is the sensory, bodily experience of that water clinging to surfaces and breath.
  • Dankness: Similar because both describe a damp, uncomfortable atmosphere; differs because dankness adds a gloomy, unhealthy, gothic colour — the dampness of a dungeon, cold and mildewed — while dampness itself stays cooler, more clinical, and more neutral.
  • Clamminess: Similar because both name a perceptible moisture on surfaces; differs because clamminess is specifically the bodily, slippery dampness of skin, palms, and fever, while dampness is the broader atmospheric or structural wetness of air, walls, and places.
  • Mugginess: Similar because both describe damp air; differs because mugginess adds oppressive warmth and discomfort (a muggy summer day), while dampness is cooler, more pervasive, and tied to enclosed or subterranean spaces rather than heat.
  • Sogginess: Similar because both name water absorbed by something; differs because sogginess implies heavy, thorough saturation that degrades the object (soggy bread, soggy ground), far beyond the light, suspended wetness of dampness.
  • Fogginess: Similar because both involve condensed water in the air; differs because fog is visible, outdoor, and weather-specific, while dampness is the invisible, felt wetness that can hang in a room or a cave with no fog at all.
  • Steaminess: Similar because both name airborne moisture; differs because steaminess implies heat and visible vapour (a steamy bathroom), while dampness is cool, quiet, and associated with cold stone and stagnant air.
