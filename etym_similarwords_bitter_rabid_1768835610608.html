• Acrimonious: Similar because both describe sharp, resentful feeling (a bitter/acrimonious dispute); differs because acrimonious is a formal, abstract word for verbal sourness, while bitter keeps the physical taste of sting and corrosion.
  • Resentful: Similar because both name lingering ill feeling; differs because resentful names the feeling itself in the person, whereas bitter is the quality of the feeling or rivalry as experienced, with the added taste-metaphor.
  • Fierce: Similar because both describe intense, violent competition; differs because fierce stresses raw intensity and ferocity, while bitter stresses sharp, lingering, corrosive resentment.
  • Acid / acrid: Similar because both are sharply pungent tastes or smells; differs because they are narrowly gustatory and olfactory, connoting burning chemicals rather than emotional history, and cannot describe feelings.
  • Sharp: Similar because both describe a piercing, stinging quality; differs because sharp stresses acute physical sensation or mental keenness and lacks bitter’s persistent, lingering unpleasantness.
  • Pungent: Similar because both are intense taste/smell words; differs because pungent is intense and often appetising (a pungent cheese), while bitter is harsh and unpleasant.
  • Harsh: Similar because both describe rough, unpleasant experience; differs because harsh is general roughness of treatment or sensation, without the specific taste of bitterness or the moral acidity.
  • Sour: Similar because both are sharp tastes opposite of sweet; differs because sour is the fermentative, acid taste (lemon, vinegar) and in mood means peevish rather than corrosive, a lighter and more passing resentment.
  • Embitters / embittered: Similar because both belong to the same family of corrosive resentment; differs because embittered describes the person made bitter, while bitter is the quality of the feeling or the rivalry itself.
  • Fanatical: Similar because both describe extreme, uncritical intensity (rabid/fanatical devotion); differs because fanatical stresses devotion to a cause or doctrine, while rabid stresses maddened, animal frenzy.
  • Extreme: Similar because both go beyond ordinary bounds; differs because extreme is a neutral scale-word (extreme weather, extreme measures), while rabid adds the image of a raving, bitten, contagious madness.
  • Wild: Similar because both describe uncontrolled intensity; differs because wild is general unrestraint (wild enthusiasm), while rabid specifically imports the frenzy and disease of a rabid animal.
  • Mad / deranged: Similar because both suggest the frenzy of rabies; differs because mad and deranged are clinical or general terms for insanity, while rabid keeps the animal-bite image and the note of contagion.
  • Furious: Similar because both mean raging; differs because furious is immediate, explosive anger, while rabid is a settled, fanatical, disease-like intensity.
  • Enraged: Similar because both describe violent anger; differs because enraged is a state of the moment, whereas rabid is a lasting, pathological extremism that infects all the person does.
