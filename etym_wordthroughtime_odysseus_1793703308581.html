- **Ancient Greece (Homeric Epics)** - Portrayed as the archetypal cunning hero (*polytropos*), a master of deception and strategy, but also a man of immense suffering and longing for home. His character was complex, admired for intellect but sometimes viewed with suspicion for his guile.
- **Classical & Hellenistic Greece** - Continued to be revered as a symbol of intelligence and resilience, but also became a subject of philosophical debate regarding the morality of his deception. Playwrights and philosophers explored his character's ambiguities.
- **Roman Era (Ulysses)** - Often depicted in a more negative light, particularly in Virgil's *Aeneid*, where he is seen as a treacherous and cruel Greek, embodying cunning that bordered on villainy, reflecting Roman cultural biases against Greek guile.
- **Medieval & Renaissance Europe** - Re-emerged as a figure of intellectual curiosity and daring exploration, sometimes seen as a proto-humanist figure. Dante's *Inferno* portrays him as a seeker of knowledge beyond human limits, punished for his hubris.
- **Modern Era (19th-21st Century)** - Celebrated as a universal symbol of the human journey, resilience, and the quest for identity and home. His story is reinterpreted through psychological, existential, and post-colonial lenses, emphasizing his adaptability and the transformative power of experience. The term "odyssey" enters common parlance to describe any long, arduous, and meaningful journey.
