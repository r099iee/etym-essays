- 13th–14th c. — Anglo-Norman guild indenture: aprentis enters English records as a legal category for a youth contractually bound to a master of a trade.
- 1563 — Elizabeth I’s national codification: The Statute of Artificers makes a seven-year apprenticeship mandatory across England to practice any craft or trade.
- 17th–18th c. — Universal rite of passage: Commercial and medical careers rely on apprenticeship; Swift's Gulliver binds himself to surgeon Bates to launch his career.
- 19th c. — Industrial crisis and Dickensian critique: Factory exploitation of parish apprentices prompts child labor legislation and union-regulated standards.
- 20th c. — Formal trade credentials: As universities expand, apprenticeship shifts predominantly to skilled manual and mechanical trades (carpentry, electrical, plumbing).
- 21st c. — Vocational renaissance: Modern industries, from tech firms to aerospace, revive apprenticeships as dynamic debt-free alternatives to higher education.
