Note on historical phonology and semantic extension: The lexical path of cupped reveals an organic trajectory from a massive storage container to a handheld drinking vessel, and finally to an organic human anatomical gesture. The ancient Proto-Indo-European root *kewp- originally designated large hollowed storage pits or casks (witness Sanskrit kūpaḥ "hollow, well, pit, cave" and Latin cūpa "large wooden tub, wine barrel"). In Late Latin spoken registers, expressive consonant gemination yielded cuppa, narrowing the referent to a personal handheld drinking vessel. Early Germanic tribes borrowed this culinary vessel directly from Roman merchants and garrison troops across the Rhine. Centuries later, late medieval surgeons turned the noun into a verb (cuppen) to designate the therapeutic application of glass suction cups. Finally, during the Renaissance, the verb discarded its narrow medical bounds to encompass the universal physical act of hollowing one's palms into the organic likeness of a vessel.
