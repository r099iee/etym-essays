CHAIN: FROM LATIN TO ACCEDED (6 stages — PIE stage debated)

  1. PIE source (traditional but debated): Latin cēdere is traditionally set under a PIE root reconstructed as *ked- "to go, move, yield", but modern authorities (notably de Vaan) treat the deeper origin as uncertain; treat *ked- as a debated, not certain, reconstruction.
  2. Latin cēdō, cēdere "to go, move, withdraw, yield, give way" (attested from early Latin, 3rd c. BCE; base of many compounds such as con-, re-, se-, ex-cēdere).
  3. Latin accēdō, accēdere "to go to, approach, come near; to come to agree, accede" (ad- "to" + cēdere; attested 2nd–1st c. BCE).
  4. Old French acceder "to approach, to reach, to arrive" (attested 12th c.; a learned borrowing from Latin).
  5. Middle English acceden "to approach, to enter upon, to agree to" (attested from c. 1400).
  6. English accede (verb) "to agree to a demand or request; to enter upon an office or dignity" (15th c. onwards) → ACCEDED (regular weak past tense and past participle, -ed — the exact form of this card).

DEBATED / NUANCED POINTS

  • The PIE reconstruction *ked- "to go, yield" is traditional but not certain: cēdere may be an isolated Italic formation, and de Vaan treats its ultimate etymology as unknown.
  • Old French acceder is a learned (bookish) loan from Latin rather than a popular development, which is why the English word keeps the Latin cluster -cc- and the educated form.
