- 1620s - the word enters English as the plant name, the flowering shrub itself, from Arabic-Persian via Turkish and French
- 18th-19th century - the blossom's pale violet hue becomes a fashionable color name, used in textiles, interiors, and dress
- 19th century - deeply associated with Victorian delicacy and sentiment; 'lilac' tints poetry and mourning-adjacent fashion
- Contemporary - a standard soft, greyed or pinkish purple in design and fashion, used interchangeably for the plant and the pale-violet color
