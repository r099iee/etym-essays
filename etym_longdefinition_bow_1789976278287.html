bow in your sentence — Maillochon was kneeling in the bow, watching, his gun hidden on the floor — is the nautical bow pronounced /baʊ/ (rhyming with cow): the forward end of a boat or small skiff, the pointed prow where a lookout kneels to watch for game while keeping low. On rivers like the Seine poachers pole silently, one man rowing astern, one kneeling forward in the bow with gun concealed, ready to rise and fire. Naval architecture divides a vessel into bow (front), amidships (middle) and stern (rear); the bow cleaves water, bears the anchor, carries navigation lights (red port, green starboard) and takes the chop first.

English spells three historically distinct words identically as bow, distinguished only by pronunciation and context, and all must be mastered because they collide constantly. Pronounced /baʊ/ like cow: (1) the nautical bow just described, and (2) a bending of the body as reverence — to take a bow, to bow deeply, a bow of the head — plus the curve so made. Pronounced /bəʊ/ like go: (3) the archery weapon — longbow, crossbow, bow and arrow — that shoots arrows by elastic recoil; (4) a knot with loops — shoelace bow, bow tie, gift bow; (5) a curved stick for playing strings — violin bow, cello bow; (6) any curved shape or rainbow arc — rainbow, eyebrow shares the root. All curve-or-bend ideas radiate from to bend, while the ship's bow comes from shoulder — the vessel's shoulder pushing water aside.

  • Nautical /baʊ/ core: forepart of hull — sharp bow, bluff bow, bulbous bow, bow wave, bow thruster, bow line, on the bow, across the bow (warning shot). Near-synonyms prow (poetic, often the very stem), stem (cutwater edge), forebow. Bridge from shoulder: the bow is the boat's shoulder, broad and pushing. Opposite is stern.
  • Reverence /baʊ/: to bow to the audience, to bow one's head in prayer, to bow to pressure (yield). Bridge is bodily bending forward at waist/neck. Socially it encodes respect, submission, gratitude or defeat; theatrically, triumph (curtain-call bow).
  • Weapon /bəʊ/: longbow (Agincourt), recurve bow, compound bow, crossbow, bowman, archer. Oldest projectile technology after spear; mythic (Cupid, Artemis, Odysseus) and medieval-military prestige. Bridge is bent wood under tension.
  • Knot /bəʊ/: to tie a bow, bow tie (formal neckwear), hair bow. Bridge is looped curve holding by tension yet releasable with a pull — beauty plus utility.
  • Music /bəʊ/ and shape /bəʊ/: violin bow (rosined horsehair drawn across strings), rainbow (meteorological arc), eyebrow, elbow (arm-bend). All are elastic curves producing sound, colour or articulation.

Idioms multiply: to have more than one string to one's bow (options), to draw the longbow (exaggerate), shot across the bows (warning), to bow out (withdraw gracefully). For learners the spelling trap is severe: bow of ship and to bow share /baʊ/ despite different roots, while bow and arrow looks identical but says /bəʊ/. In the Maupassant night-poaching scene the low bow hides the gunman from wardens — tactical invisibility, breath held, eyes forward.
