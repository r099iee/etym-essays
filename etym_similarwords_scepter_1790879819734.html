• Staff: Similar because both are long wooden or metal rods held in the hand; differs because a staff is a general utilitarian walking stick or support, whereas a scepter is specifically a sacred, ornate symbol of royal or divine authority.
  • Rod: Similar because both are straight, slender bars signifying discipline or jurisdiction; differs because a rod emphasizes punitive, correctional, or administrative authority (e.g., "rod of correction"), while a scepter emphasizes royal sovereignty and majesty.
  • Baton: Similar because both are short handheld cylinders denoting office; differs because a baton is associated with military field marshals, police officers, or orchestral conductors, lacking the monarchical coronation status of a scepter.
  • Mace: Similar because both are heavy ceremonial weapons/staffs symbolizing institutional power; differs because a mace originated as a bludgeoning weapon and now represents parliamentary or academic authority, carried before a presiding officer.
  • Crozier: Similar because both are ornate ceremonial staffs; differs because a crozier is specifically an ecclesiastical pastoral staff with a hooked or curved top, symbolizing a bishop's role as a Christian shepherd.
  • Diadem: Similar because both are essential pieces of royal regalia; differs because a diadem is worn upon the head (a crown or headband), whereas a scepter is held in the hand.
