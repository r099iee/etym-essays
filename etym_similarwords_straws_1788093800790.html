• Hay: Similar because both are dried grass or cereal material from the farm; differs because hay is grass cut green and dried for animal fodder — fuller, softer and nutritious — while straw is the dry stalk residue of threshed grain, hollow, brittle and mainly used for bedding and thatch.
  • Chaff: Similar because both are light, dry, worthless harvest residue; differs because chaff is specifically the husks and glumes separated by winnowing — dustier, lighter and even more purely “waste” than straw, which at least has structural uses.
  • Stalks: Similar because both name the slender stems of plants; differs because stalk is the generic botanical term for any plant stem (flower, leaf, fruit stalk), whereas straw denotes specifically the dead, dried, harvest-stripped cereal stem.
  • Reeds: Similar because both are slender, hollow, elongated plant material used in thatching and basketry; differs because reeds are living or freshly gathered wetland plants, sturdier and wild, while straw is agricultural residue; reeds also make musical instruments and pens — roles straw cannot fill.
  • Wisps: Similar because both evoke thin, light, insubstantial strands; differs because a wisp is a small loose handful or curl (of hair, smoke, hay) — a measure of quantity, not a material — and is not countably plural in the same way.
  • Splinters: Similar because both suggest thin, brittle fragments that break easily; differs because splinters are sharp broken-off pieces of wood that pierce, whereas straws are whole slender hollow stalks that fold and crush.
  • Matchsticks: Similar because both are thin, straight, fragile slender objects and both feed the simile of skinny legs (“legs like matchsticks”); differs because matchsticks are short manufactured wooden sticks with a chemical tip — stiffer and more comic than the organic, bending straw.
  • Tubes: Similar because both are hollow cylindrical conduits, the generic word for the drinking-straw sense; differs because tube covers manufactured cylinders of any material and any size (rubber, metal, piping), while straw is specifically a slender disposable sipping conduit.
  • Pipes: Similar because both are hollow conduits for liquids; differs because pipes are rigid, durable, large-scale plumbing or smoking implements, at the opposite end of scale and durability from the flimsy straw.
