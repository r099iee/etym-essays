• Scolded: Similar because both find fault with someone's behaviour; differs because scolding is louder, longer and more public - a sustained flood of complaint that blames the person - while chiding is brief, mild and often affectionate, correcting the act rather than damning the actor.
  • Rebuked: Similar because both express sharp disapproval of someone's words or deeds; differs because rebuked is more severe, formal and impersonal - an official or stern reproof - while chiding is the warm, intimate correction of a close elder.
  • Reprimanded: Similar because both are acts of reproof; differs because reprimanded is the formal, institutional word (a headmistress or commanding officer reprimands) carrying official weight, while chiding belongs to the personal, affectionate register of family and friends.
  • Admonished: Similar because both warn and correct someone; differs because admonished stresses giving advice or a warning for the future in a formal, didactic tone, while chiding is a light, affectionate reproach about what has just been done.
  • Reproached: Similar because both express disapproval of someone's action; differs because reproached implies wounded disappointment and is more pointed and personal, while chiding keeps a tender, indulgent tone that cancels the sting.
  • Berated: Similar because both are verbs of scolding; differs because berated is far harsher - a long, loud, angry dressing-down - while chiding is brief, gentle and affectionate.
  • Upbraided: Similar because both rebuke someone; differs because upbraided is heavy, wordy and reproachful, often listing the fault at length, while chiding is light and concise, the tap rather than the tirade.
  • Chastised: Similar because both involve correcting someone's fault; differs because chastised may include punishment or a severe reprimand and carries moral weight, while chiding is purely verbal, mild and forgiving.
