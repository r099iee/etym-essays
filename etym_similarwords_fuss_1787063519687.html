• Ado: Similar because ado is a bustle, a fuss, needless commotion ("without further ado"); differs because it is archaic or literary and usually appears in fixed phrases.
  • Commotion: Similar because both denote noisy disturbance; differs because commotion implies disturbance caused by many people or a crowd, while a fuss can be one person's over-reaction.
  • Bother: Similar because both can mean a state of annoyance or trouble; differs because bother stresses personal inconvenience ("sorry for the bother"), whereas fuss stresses exaggerated excitement.
  • To-do: Similar because "a great to-do" equals a great fuss; differs because it is informal and largely confined to the phrase "a (great) to-do".
  • Stir: Similar because "to cause a stir" is to excite commotion; differs because a stir may be admiring or positive, while a fuss is usually judged needless.
  • Hullabaloo: Similar because both mean a loud clamour; differs because hullabaloo is noisier, more disorderly, and informal or facetious.
  • Kerfuffle: Similar because both mean a minor disturbance; differs because kerfuffle is chiefly British informal, humorous, and implies a small, confused quarrel.
  • Flap: Similar because "to be in a flap" is to be in a state of anxious fuss; differs because it is British informal and focuses on panicky agitation rather than outward ceremony.
  • Fluster: Similar because both involve agitated excitement; differs because fluster is a state of confusion or embarrassment, while fuss is activity and concern directed at something.
  • Trouble: Similar because "don't go to any trouble" is close to "don't fuss"; differs because trouble is far broader — difficulty, effort, risk — and lacks the note of over-excitement.
  • Pandemonium: Similar because both can describe uproar; differs because pandemonium is wild, chaotic noise on a large scale, whereas a fuss is usually small and personal.
  • Fussiness: Similar because it is the noun of the adjective fussy, built on the same word; differs because it describes a demanding, hard-to-please quality in a person, not an episode of commotion.
