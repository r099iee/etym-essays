claret:
- A clear red wine (14th c.) - claret is borrowed as the name of a light, clear red wine, the sense of 'clear' (Old French claret) built into the word itself
- The Bordeaux red (15th-18th c.) - the name fixes on the red wines of Bordeaux and becomes the gentleman's wine, a byword for richness and civilization
- A colour is born (18th-19th c.) - the word doubles as a colour-name: the deep translucent ruby of the wine, used of cloth, glass, and the sky's own refracted light
- Contemporary - claret names both the wine and its clear, liquid hue, a deep red the light passes through rather than one that swallows light

topaz:
- A yellow gem (from the medieval period) - topaz names a golden gemstone prized since antiquity, at first covering yellow stones loosely
- The stone is fixed (18th-19th c.) - mineralogy pins topaz to a single translucent crystal, and the colour-word follows: a golden yellow warmed toward orange, as if sunlight were trapped in the stone
- Contemporary - a jewel-word of the palette, kept for the clear, brilliant yellow of the gem and loved by poetry and fashion alike for its rarity and polish
