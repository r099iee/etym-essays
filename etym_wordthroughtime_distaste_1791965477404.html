- 1570s — First recorded as a noun for a disagreeable quality or flavour, with a matching verb meaning to spoil the taste of something.
- 1590s — The modern sense appears: a feeling of aversion or dislike, at first chiefly of food, then of conduct and ideas.
- 18th c. — Settles into the vocabulary of manners and criticism, the polite and understated form of disapproval.
- 19th–21st c. — Remains markedly milder than disgust; now the standard word for a refined or principled dislike in biography, reviews, and political commentary.
