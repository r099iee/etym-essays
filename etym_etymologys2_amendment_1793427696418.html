Note: Traces back to Latin ēmendāre, compounded from the prepositional prefix ē- (a variant of ex-, ‘out of, away from’) and the noun menda (or mendum, ‘physical defect, bodily blemish, fault’), from PIE *mend- (‘fault, physical defect’). In Old French, the initial unstressed Latin vowel shifted from e- to a- (yielding amender) due to vocalic lowering before nasals and phonological confusion with the frequent prefix ad- (as in aprester, apaisier). The noun suffix -ment, borrowed from Latin -mentum, signifies the act, process, or concrete result of the verb.
