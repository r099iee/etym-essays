Derivation chain for the prefix element — BE-: - PIE *h₁epi "at, near, upon" (preposition), reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE. - → Proto-Germanic *bi "by, near, about," reconstructed, c. 500 BCE – 200 CE. - → Old English be- / bi- (verbal and nominal prefix, from the preposition be "by, about"), attested 7th–11th c. - → Middle English be-, attested 12th–15th c. - → be-, modern English (prefix in bethink, bestir, bemoan, etc.). Derivation chain for the base element — THINK: - PIE *tong- / *(s)tenǵ- "to think, to feel," reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE. - → Proto-Germanic *þankijaną "to think, consider" (the causative counterpart of *þankōną "to thank, to think of with gratitude"), reconstructed, c. 500 BCE – 200 CE. - → Old English þencan "to think, to consider," attested 8th–11th c. - → Middle English thenken "to think," attested 12th–15th c. - → think, modern English. Combination of the two elements: - Old English beþencan "to think about, to consider, to take thought of," attested 10th–11th c. (e.g. in Ælfric's homilies). - → Middle English bithinken / bethenken "to think about, to consider," attested 13th–15th c.; the reflexive construction "to bethink oneself of / that…" emerges in the 14th c. (biblical and Chaucerian usage). - → English bethink "to take thought, to call to mind (oneself)," modern, archaic/literary. - → BETHINKING "the act of coming to think of or reminding oneself of," the present participle / gerund, modern English. Debated / nuanced points: - The be- prefix here is usually analyzed as transitivizing and intensifying, but its precise force in bethink (transitive making, or simple intensification) is debated among grammarians. - The PIE root of think is sometimes cited as *tong- and sometimes as *(s)tenǵ-; the reconstruction is uncertain because the Germanic *þank- has no secure cognates outside Germanic, a point noted in OED and standard handbooks. - The morphological link between think and thank (Old English þencan vs. þancian, both from *þank-) is well attested and explains the early meaning "to think of with feeling, to be grateful.
