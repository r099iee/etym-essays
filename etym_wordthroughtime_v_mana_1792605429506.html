- [Rigvedic Antecedents (c. 1500–1200 BCE)] - celebrated in the foundational solar hymns of the Rigveda (RV 1.22.17: idaṁ viṣṇur vi cakrame tredhā nidadhe padam) celebrating Viṣṇu's three cosmic strides across earth, atmosphere, and heaven, establishing the theological embryo of the legend - [perception: worshipped as the supreme solar deity whose three strides encompass and preserve the living universe]
- [Brāhmaṇa & Early Epic Period (c. 900–400 BCE)] - in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, Viṣṇu explicitly takes the form of a dwarf (vāmana) representing the sacrificial ritual itself, outwitting the Asuras to secure the earth for the gods - [perception: viewed as a profound ritual allegory where sacred sacrifice, though appearing small, triumphs over brute demonic force]
- [Classical Epics & Purāṇas (c. 400 BCE–800 CE)] - fully codified in the Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata, and major Purāṇas (Vāmana Purāṇa, Bhāgavata Purāṇa) as the fifth avatar of Viṣṇu who humbles King Bali, while also establishing Vāmana as the southern world-supporting Diggaja elephant - [perception: revered as the ultimate triumph of dharma, divine grace, and cosmic humility over imperial hubris]
- [Medieval Bhakti, Regional Traditions & Poetics (c. 8th–16th c. CE)] - celebrated in Kerala as the heart of the national Onam harvest festival welcoming King Bali's annual return, while in Kashmir Ācārya Vāmana establishes the Rīti school of poetics (rītir ātmā kāvyasya) - [perception: celebrated in regional folklore, royal temple sculpture (Badami, Mahabalipuram), and high aesthetic theory]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era] - venerated across global Hinduism, depicted in classical temple dances (Bharatanatyam, Kathakali), studied in comparative mythology as a profound archetypal transformation from the miniature to the infinite - [perception: admired as a timeless philosophical allegory of how spiritual truth transcends physical appearance and boundless cosmic pride]
