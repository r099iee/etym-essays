=== coveted === PIE *kwep- "to boil, seethe, smoke; to move in agitation" (the heat of water coming to the boil, of steam rising; reconstructed c. 4500–2500 BCE) → Sanskrit kúpyati "to be angry, to boil with rage" and kopa "anger, wrath" (the boil turned to rage; Vedic onward) → Hindi kop "anger" (a learned tatsama borrowing) → Latin cupere "to desire, to long for" (the heat channelled toward longing; attested 2nd c. BCE) → Latin cupīdō "desire personified" → English Cupid, the god of desire; and Latin cupiditās "inordinate longing, greed" (the moralized form of the same heat) → Vulgar Latin *cupidietāre "to desire inordinately" (late empire) → Old French covoitier "to desire inordinately" (9th–11th c.) → Middle English coveten "to covet" (13th c.) → English covet (13th–14th c.) → English coveted (past participle, present day) Cognate within the same root: Latin vapor "steam, vapour — that which smokes" → English vapour. === battlements === PIE *bhau- "to strike, to beat" (reconstructed; note: the immediate Latin formation is debated) → Latin battuere "to beat, pound" (attested 1st c. BCE; its immediate origin is debated — possibly a borrowing from a Celtic source — though it is traditionally grouped under PIE *bhau-) → Late Latin battalia "fighting exercise, combat" (4th–5th c.) → Old French bataille "battle" (10th–11th c.) → English battle → Old French bataillier "to fight; also to fortify" (12th c.) → Old French bataillement "fortification; a parapet built for fighting" (12th–13th c.) → Middle English batelment "a wall-top fortification" (14th c.) → English battlement (present day) NOTE ON DEBATE/NUANCE: the immediate source of Latin battuere is disputed — it may be a Celtic loan (compare Gaulish forms), though it is traditionally placed under PIE *bhau- "to strike"; the sequence from battalia onward is secure.
