- Old English — the harvest verb: rīpan cuts corn with the sickle, bound to rīpe, ripe, since only mature grain is reaped.
- Medieval — feudal rhythm: reaping is boon-work and harvest-home, the village turning out with sickles while ballads and carols bless the crop.
- Reformation Bible — moralised: whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap fixes the consequence sense for every English ear.
- 17th c. — death's portrait: the Reaper with his scythe enters emblem books and tombstones, humanity as God's standing corn.
- 1830s — iron reapers: McCormick's mechanical reaper multiplies the harvest and empties the fields, turning a communal verb industrial.
- Today — metaphor ascendant: combines do the literal reaping while boardrooms, podiums, and pulpits reap profits, whirlwinds, and what was sown.
