• Relinquish: Similar because both involve voluntarily giving up or releasing something desirable; differs because relinquish often implies surrendering physical possession, legal control, or an entrenched position under pressure or with deep reluctance, whereas forego typically describes a proactive decision not to partake in or experience something in the first place.
  • Abstain: Similar because both convey deliberate self-denial and refraining from an action or pleasure; differs because abstain is predominantly an intransitive verb construed with the preposition "from" (e.g., abstaining from alcohol or voting) emphasizing moral self-restraint, whereas forego is a direct transitive verb that takes an immediate direct object (e.g., foregoing dessert or rights).
  • Waive: Similar because both mean to voluntarily decline to claim or enforce an entitlement or opportunity; differs because waive is primarily a formal legal, technical, or procedural term (such as waiving immunity, fees, or jurisdictional rights), whereas forego operates broadly across personal, moral, and emotional spheres of daily life.
  • Renounce: Similar because both denote the deliberate abandonment of something valuable; differs because renounce implies a formal, solemn, and public declaration of rejection or repudiation (such as renouncing a royal title, citizenship, or heresy), whereas forego applies comfortably to modest, private sacrifices without ceremonial declaration.
  • Forfeit: Similar because both result in being deprived of a benefit or possession; differs because forfeit signifies an involuntary loss imposed as a penalty or legal consequence for an infraction, error, or breach of contract, whereas forego is an entirely voluntary act of personal choice.
  • Precede: Similar because both can mean to go before in chronological time, sequence, or spatial arrangement; differs because precede is the neutral, modern standard English term for temporal priority and possesses no secondary meaning of self-denial or renunciation, whereas forego carries both senses.
  • Eschew: Similar because both mean to deliberately avoid or keep away from something; differs because eschew emphasizes avoidance rooted in moral disdain, philosophical caution, or aesthetic aversion (e.g., eschewing violence or ostentation), whereas forego emphasizes the personal sacrifice of something inherently pleasant or advantageous.
