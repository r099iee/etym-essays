- Old English - adela means liquid filth, dungy mire; perception is physical, foul, agricultural
- Middle English - addle specializes to rotten or barren eggs ('addle egg'); speakers hear spoilage and failed life inside a shell
- 16th–17th centuries - metaphorical leap: addle-headed, addle-pated, addled brains; perception shifts to intellectual emptiness and muddle, still powered by the egg image
- 18th–19th centuries - addled becomes a stock adjective for confused wits from drink, heat, age, or folly in novels and essays; egg sense continues in rural English
- 20th century - cognitive sense dominates newspapers and fiction; slightly quaint or comic flavor versus clinical 'confused' or 'disoriented'
- Contemporary usage - default reading is 'mentally muddled'; literal rotten-egg reading available in farming/cooking or self-conscious metaphor; tone ranges from affectionate exasperation to gentle mockery
