- 16th Century (c. 1530s–1570s): [Acoustic & Kinetic Genesis] - Emerged into Early Modern English as an expressive onomatopoeic verb (*thump*) denoting the action of delivering a heavy, dull blow to a person or object, mimicking the sound of impact.
- Late 16th to 17th Century: [Physiological & Cardiac Extension] - Extended into dramatic literature and poetry to describe the rapid, heavy beating of the human heart caused by mortal fear, romantic passion, or athletic exertion.
- 18th to 19th Century: [Colloquial Altercations & Industrial Soundscapes] - Broadened in British vernacular to signify pugilistic brawling, street fighting, and the percussive, rhythmic operating sounds of Industrial Revolution steam engines and machinery.
- 20th Century to Present: [Sports Slang & Modern Audio Culture] - Adopted in sports journalism to mean crushing athletic defeats (*"thumped 4-0"*), while becoming ubiquitous in modern acoustics and music production to describe low-frequency bass impacts and kick drums.
