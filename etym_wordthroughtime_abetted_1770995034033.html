- 14th–15th c. 'To urge on, to incite' — from Old French abeter (to bait, to set a dog on), originally used of urging hounds to attack; a neutral-to-active word for spurring someone on.
- 16th–17th c. 'To aid in wrongdoing' — hardens into the legal phrase aid and abet, meaning to help commit an offense; the word begins to carry moral and criminal condemnation.
- 18th–21st c. 'Complicity in a crime' — the negative sense dominates completely: to abet is now almost always to encourage or assist wrongdoing; the word sounds legalistic and grave, rare in casual speech.
