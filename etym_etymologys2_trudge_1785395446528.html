Linear derivation chain for TRUDGE (single root word; expressive/echoic — no PIE ancestry): 1. Scots / northern Middle English trudgen 'to walk with laborious effort' (attested in the 1540s). 2. English trudge (verb) 'to travel laboriously, plod' (16th c., used in Spenser and after). 3. TRUDGE (base form, retained exactly as given; the noun 'a trudge', 'a wearisome walk', follows in the early 19th c.). Notes on the record: - The word is classed by the OED as an expressive or echoic formation: its sound enacts the heavy footfall, and no Proto-Indo-European root can be reconstructed for it. - Norwegian truga / trudja 'to tramp, walk heavily' and Swedish trudaska 'to trudge, plod' are parallel echoic formations — NOT cognates; no attested parent links them, and none is needed. - The resemblance to tread (Old English tredan, from PIE *dreh₂- 'to run, step') is real but expressive, not genetic: trudge lacks the regular sound-developments of the *dreh₂- lineage, so the connection is recorded as possible influence only, not ancestry.
