Peripheral is an adjective meaning situated at the edge or margin rather than at the centre, and by extension of secondary importance, incidental, or tangential. In the source sentence — style is not “a peripheral cosmetic accompaniment” — it means merely decorative and dispensable, an optional extra to supposedly self-standing ideas, a view the author rejects.

Principal current senses:

  • Spatial-literal (core sense): at or belonging to the outer boundary. Peripheral districts of a city, peripheral regions of a country, peripheral vision (what you see out of the corner of your eye), peripheral nerves and blood vessels. Neutral and descriptive; opposite is central. Medicine and geography use it daily without value judgment.
  • Figurative — secondary, minor, non-essential: the dominant general sense. A peripheral matter, role, character, or detail is one that does not drive the main action. “Inflation was peripheral to the campaign.” “He played a peripheral role in the negotiations.” Here the spatial metaphor carries evaluation: edge = less important. The source sentence uses exactly this: style demoted to garnish.
  • Computing / technology: (noun, clipping) a peripheral or peripheral device is external hardware connected to a computer — printers, scanners, keyboards, external drives — as opposed to the CPU and memory. The noun use (“plug in your peripherals”) is standard IT jargon, neutral in value despite the etymology.
  • Anatomy / neuroscience: peripheral nervous system (PNS) comprises all nerves outside brain and spinal cord; peripheral resistance, peripheral vision loss, peripheral neuropathy. Technical, precise, indispensable: what is anatomically peripheral (fingertips) can be functionally vital, a useful reminder that edge does not always mean unimportant.
  • Social science / economics: core–periphery models contrast dominant centres with dependent margins — peripheral economies, peripheral states, peripheral communities. Often critical: peripherality implies extraction, neglect, or marginalization. Postcolonial and world-systems theory give the term political charge.

Further nuances:

  • Peripheral vs. marginal: near-synonyms, but marginal more strongly implies exclusion or minimal effect (marginal notes, marginal gains, marginalized people), while peripheral retains a spatial-system image (part of the system but at its edge). A peripheral member still belongs; a marginalized one may be pushed out.
  • Peripheral vs. incidental / tangential: incidental stresses chance occurrence; tangential stresses touching only lightly and veering off; peripheral stresses structural position at the edge. All three demote, but by different metaphors.
  • Peripheral vs. superficial: superficial criticizes lack of depth; peripheral criticizes lack of centrality. A cosmetic accompaniment, as in the source, is accused of being both — but the author’s word choice stresses centrality: style choreographs the whole dance, not the rim.
  • Register: in science and IT the word is neutral-technical; in humanities polemic (“not merely peripheral”) it is evaluative and often contested, because calling something peripheral is itself a power move about what counts as centre.

In sum, peripheral maps space onto value: edge → accessory → dispensable. The literal eye can still need its periphery to detect danger, and the computer still needs its peripherals to work — which is why the metaphor is always available for reversal, as the source sentence reverses it for style.
