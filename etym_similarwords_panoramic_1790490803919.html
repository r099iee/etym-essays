• sweeping: Similar because it describes a broad, unbroken view or survey; differs because sweeping stresses the motion of the eye across space, while panoramic stresses the completeness of the vista.
  • wide-angle: Similar because in photography it means capturing a broad field; differs because wide-angle is a technical lens term, while panoramic is the general visual effect.
  • bird's-eye: Similar because it denotes a comprehensive view from above; differs because bird's-eye specifies elevated viewpoint, while panoramic specifies breadth at any height.
  • all-embracing / all-encompassing: Similar because both mean taking in everything; differs because these are abstract and figurative, while panoramic keeps a visual metaphor of seeing.
  • comprehensive: Similar because a comprehensive survey covers a whole subject as a panorama covers a scene; differs because comprehensive is purely intellectual, while panoramic retains pictorial vividness.
  • vast / expansive: Similar because both praise great spatial extent; differs because vast is mere size, while panoramic adds the idea of being viewable all at once.
  • scenic: Similar because both describe pleasing prospects; differs because scenic means beautiful enough to look at, while panoramic means broad enough to surround.
  • unobstructed / uninterrupted: Similar because a panorama requires clear sight-lines; differs because these name the absence of obstacles, while panoramic names the resulting total view.
  • 360-degree / all-round: Similar because they are near-literal equivalents of the rotunda ideal; differs because they are measured and technical, while panoramic is evocative and literary.
  • epic / sprawling (figurative): Similar because panoramic novels and histories are epic in scope; differs because epic stresses grandeur and sprawling stresses looseness, while panoramic stresses ordered completeness.
