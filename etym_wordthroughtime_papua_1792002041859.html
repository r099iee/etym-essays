- 15th–16th c. — Coined in the Malay trade networks of the Maluku Islands as papuah ("frizzy-haired") or Tidore papa-ua ("unsubordinated territories"), denoting the mysterious eastern island and its people beyond the spice trade.
- 1526–1545 — Adopted into European navigation by Portuguese captain Jorge de Menezes as Ilha dos Papuas; soon joined by Spanish explorer Ortiz de Retes's competitive moniker Nueva Guinea (1545).
- 1884–1906 — Codified into British and Australian colonial administration as British New Guinea, then officially renamed the Territory of Papua upon transfer to Australian governance under the Papua Act 1905.
- 1942–1945 — Emerged as a crucial battleground in the World War II Pacific Theater during the grueling Kokoda Track campaign, forging lasting bonds between native Papuans ("Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels") and Allied troops.
- 1975–Present — Achieved full sovereign independence as Papua New Guinea; concurrently restored in 2001 by the Republic of Indonesia as the official name for the western provinces of the island (Tanah Papua).
