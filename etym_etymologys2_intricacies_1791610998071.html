Note: Latin tricae (plural noun meaning "vexations, delays, trifles, perplexities") was fancifully connected by ancient Roman grammarians and Pliny the Elder to Trica, an obscure, impoverished town in Apulia whose inhabitants supposedly offered trifling excuses. Historical linguistics rejects this folk etymology and traces tricae to the PIE root *torkʷ- / *terk- ("to twist, wind"), cognate with Latin torquere ("to twist"), referring to tangled hairs, snared bird feathers, or twisted cordage that impede movement.
