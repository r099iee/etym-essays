- [3rd c. BCE–1st c. CE] - Originates in the cynic street philosophy of Menippus of Gadara, establishing a Hellenistic genre of seriocomic parody that juxtaposes prose with verse - [connotation: Cynic subversion, street philosophy, intellectual mockery, prosimetrum]
- [Roman Empire (1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE)] - Adopted into Latin literature by Varro, Seneca (Apocolocyntosis), and Petronius (Satyricon), and Greek literature by Lucian - [connotation: scandalous parody, imperial court satire, underworld journeys, carnivalesque feast]
- [Renaissance & Enlightenment (16th c.–18th c.)] - Revived by humanists (Erasmus, Rabelais, Burton's Anatomy of Melancholy, Swift's Gulliver's Travels) to dismantle scholastic pedantry - [connotation: sprawling erudition, encyclopedic comedy, subversion of dogmatism]
- [20th c.–Present] - Canonized by literary theorists Mikhail Bakhtin and Northrop Frye as the foundational ancestor of the modern polyphonic novel and postmodern experimental fiction - [connotation: polyphony, multi-genre medley, ideological carnival, postmodern intellectual satire]
