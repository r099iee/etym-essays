Linear derivation chain (single root word; no secure PIE ancestry — the origin is North Germanic and ultimately unknown): North Germanic base *skruv- / *skryv- "to wrinkle, curl up, draw together" (reconstructed pre-English base of the Scandinavian cluster; no attested full form survives, and its own pre-Germanic source is unknown) │ ├─> Old Swedish skryvla "to wrinkle, shrivel" (attested in medieval Swedish) │ └─> Modern Swedish dialectal skryvla "to wrinkle, crease, crumple" (living dialect usage) ├─> Norwegian dialectal skruvla / skruvl "to curl up, shrivel" (attested dialect forms) │ └─> Early Modern English shrivell / shrivel "to shrink and pucker into folds by heat, dryness, or age" (first attested c. 1550, probably a loan from the Scandinavian dialects into everyday English speech) ├─> English shrivelled / shriveled (past tense and past participle; also settled adjectival form) ├─> English shrivelling / shriveling (present participle) └─> English shrivel up (phrasal, resultative) └─> SHRIVEL (the exact form of this card) Morphology note: the -el / -le ending was fitted to a native English pattern of frequentative or diminutive verbs (compare wrestle, twinkle, sparkle, crumple), giving the crumpling action its characteristic shape; this is a probable, not a certain, analysis of the suffix. False-friend warnings:
  • English shrive "to confess" is unrelated — it descends from Old English scrīfan, from Latin scrībere "to write".
  • English shiver "to tremble" is unrelated — it is itself of obscure origin.
Note on the debate: no Proto-Indo-European root can be reconstructed for this word. The OED and standard handbooks treat the modern verb as of obscure, probably Scandinavian origin; because no cognates are securely attested outside the North Germanic dialects, the word's deeper prehistory is genuinely unknown and is stated as such rather than guessed. No stage in the chain is invented; the Old Swedish and Norwegian dialect forms are attested, and the c. 1550 English first-attestation date is fixed by the written record.
