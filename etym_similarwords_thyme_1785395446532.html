• sage (Salvia officinalis): Similar because it is an aromatic Mediterranean herb of the same family (Lamiaceae), used in cooking and medicine; differs because it is grey-leaved, more savoury and assertive, with soft downy foliage and a stronger, mustier note where thyme is dry, resinous, and more penetrating.
  • marjoram (Origanum majorana): Similar because it is a near relative of thyme sharing the warm Mediterranean character and family; differs because it is milder, sweeter, and more floral, and lacks thyme's dry, haylike pungency and camphor edge.
  • oregano (Origanum vulgare): Similar because it is a robust Mediterranean herb of the same genus cluster, with a warm, savoury bite; differs because it is coarser-leaved, hotter, and more peppery than thyme, and is used in dried form for strongly flavoured sauces and pizzas.
  • rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus): Similar because it belongs to the same family and thrives in the same dry, sunny soil; differs because it is a taller, needle-leaved shrub with a bolder, camphor-and-pine aroma, not the ground-hugging, tiny-leaved herb with thyme's resinous warmth.
  • mint (Mentha): Similar because it is the mint family's namesake aromatic herb and a close relative; differs because it is cool, sharp, and penetratingly fresh where thyme is warm, resinous, and earthy, and mint grows in damp ground rather than arid rock.
  • savory (Satureja): Similar because it is a low Mediterranean herb of the same family with a peppery, thyme-like pungency, so close that the two are often confused; differs because savory is sharper, hotter, and less sweet, with narrower leaves and a stronger peppercorn note.
  • lavender (Lavandula): Similar because it is a woody, aromatic shrub of the same family with fragrant oil used in perfumery and medicine; differs because its scent is floral and soapy rather than culinary, and it is grown for fragrance and oil, not as a seasoning.
  • basil (Ocimum basilicum): Similar because it is a sweet, aromatic culinary herb used fresh in cooking; differs because it is a tender, large-leaved, clove-and-anise-scented annual of warm damp gardens, not a hardy, dry-country shrub of the thyme type.
