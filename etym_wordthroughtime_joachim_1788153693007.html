- [8th–7th c. BCE] - Jehoiakim is a royal name in Judah: the puppet-king who ruled while Babylon rose - [perception: power, then ruin]
- [2nd–4th c. CE] - the Greek-Latin form Joachim is attached to the father of the Virgin Mary in early Christian tradition - [perception: sanctity; a popular saint’s name across Europe]
- [12th–13th c.] - Joachim of Fiore, the Calabrian abbot, imagines an Age of the Spirit; his name becomes a byword for prophecy - [perception: visionary, apocalyptic]
- [16th c.] - Joachim du Bellay, with Ronsard, founds the Pléiade and writes the manifesto of French poetry - [perception: literary éclat; the name sounds cultivated and French]
- [19th c.] - Pater’s Renaissance makes du Bellay the emblem of a “refined and comely decadence” - [perception: nostalgic, refined, belletristic]
- [Today] - an uncommon but dignified European given name; in literature classes it survives chiefly through du Bellay - [perception: bookish, old-world]
