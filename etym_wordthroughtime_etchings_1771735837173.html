- [17th century the acid-bitten print] - [Etching enters as the art and product of biting a design into a metal plate with acid to make an intaglio print; connotation technical and craft-based — the etcher's needle and bath]
- [18th century the golden age] - [The great age of Piranesi's Vedute di Roma; etching is prized for its wiry, freely drawn, pen-like line and dramatic light; connotation of virtuoso draftsmanship and romantic ruin]
- [19th century the etching revival] - [Rembrandt's prints rediscovered and Whistler's work make etching a celebrated artistic medium; connotation of prestige and spontaneity in printmaking]
- [20th-21st century the standing term] - ['Etchings' remains the precise art-historical term for such prints and their technique; as a body of work (a suite of plates) it retains an antiquarian gravitas, fitting for the illustrations of a grand edition]
