- Late 16th c. to 17th c.: Emerged in theological and scholarly English directly from Late Latin and French, applied strictly to biblical descriptions of the Garden of Eden and the heavenly state of redeemed souls.
- 18th c. to 19th c.: Broadened during the Romantic era to describe natural landscapes, pastoral poetry, and sensory experiences of sublime, unblemished earthly beauty.
- Mid-20th c.: Adopted by psychoanalytic theorists to describe pre-verbal, oceanic states of psychic wholeness and maternal unity.
- Late 20th c. to Present: Extended into post-structuralist literary theory, semiotics, and avant-garde criticism (e.g., Roland Barthes) to designate texts of boundless linguistic pleasure, semiotic plenitude, and anti-authoritarian play.
