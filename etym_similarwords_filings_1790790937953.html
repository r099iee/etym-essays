• Shavings: Similar because both are thin fragments or waste material detached from a surface by a tool; differs because shavings are typically longer, curly, ribbon-like strips produced by a plane, drawknife, or lathe (especially in woodworking), whereas filings are fine, powdery, granular particles produced by a serrated abrasive file.
  • Raspings: Similar because both are abrasive particles scraped from a hard surface; differs because raspings are produced by a coarse-toothed rasp and tend to be larger, rougher, and more irregular than the fine metallic dust of filings.
  • Swarf (Swawf): Similar because both consist of metallic debris generated during machining and metalworking; differs because swarf is industrial collective terminology encompassing metal turnings, chips, and curls mixed with liquid coolants and lubricants on machine shop floors.
  • Chippings: Similar because both are small fragments detached from a solid mass; differs because chippings are angular, broken pieces fractured off by impact from a chisel, hammer, or axe (such as stone or ice chippings) rather than steady abrasive friction.
  • Sawdust: Similar because both are fine particulate matter created by cutting through material with a toothed instrument; differs because sawdust is composed specifically of pulverized wood produced by sawing, whereas filings typically refers to metal or hard mineral raspings.
  • Grindings: Similar because both are fine particles created by abrasive material removal; differs because grindings are generated by a rotating abrasive wheel, grindstone, or grinder, often producing extremely fine pulverized dust, sparks, or slurry.
  • Debris: Similar because both refer to loose matter or waste material; differs because debris is a broad, non-specific term for scattered rubble, wreckage, or litter, whereas filings is precise technical and descriptive vocabulary for abraded particulate matter.
