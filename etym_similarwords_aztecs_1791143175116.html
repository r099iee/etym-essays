• Mexica: Similar because they were the dominant Aztec group of Tenochtitlan; differs because Mexica is the precise emic name, while Aztec is the broader exonym.
  • Nahuas: Similar because all Aztecs were Nahuatl speakers; differs because Nahua includes many non-imperial towns before and after the empire.
  • Tenochca: Similar because they were the Tenochtitlan citizens; differs because it excludes Texcoco and Tlacopan allies.
  • Triple Alliance: Similar because it was the Aztec imperial state; differs because it names the political pact, not the peoples.
  • Mayas: Similar because they were another great Mesoamerican civilization; differs because Mayas lived southeast with distinct languages, script, and city-states.
  • Incas: Similar because they built the contemporary Andean empire felled by Spaniards; differs because Incas were Quechua Andeans, not Mexican Nahuas.
  • Toltecs: Similar because they were the revered predecessors at Tula claimed as ancestors; differs because they flourished earlier (c. 900–1150).
  • Teotihuacanos: Similar because they built the earlier great Basin metropolis; differs because their language and identity remain uncertain and predate Aztecs by a millennium.
  • Tlaxcalans: Similar because they were neighbouring Nahuas; differs because they resisted the Triple Alliance and allied with Cortés.
  • Mixtecs: Similar because they were Oaxacan contemporaries under Aztec tribute; differs because they had separate languages and codex traditions.
  • Zapotecs: Similar because they were another Oaxacan civilization; differs because of distinct Valley of Oaxaca roots and language.
  • Mesoamericans: Similar because Aztecs belong to this culture area; differs because it covers dozens of peoples over millennia.
