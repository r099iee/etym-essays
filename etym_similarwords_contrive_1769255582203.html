• devise: Similar because both mean to work out a plan or scheme in the mind; differs because devise is the neutral, purely intellectual planner's word — no craft, secrecy, or difficulty implied — while contrive adds deliberate skill, effort, and often the sense of making do against obstacles.
  • invent: Similar because both name creating something that did not exist before; differs because invent stresses genuine novelty and often playfulness, while contrive stresses arranging existing means toward a goal with craftsmanship.
  • scheme: Similar because both name deliberate planning of a course of action; differs because scheme carries clear underhanded intent and disapproval, whereas contrive is neutral-to-positive when it means "manage to succeed" and only in its adjective form condemns artificiality.
  • plot: Similar because both mean to plan something, often secretly; differs because plot adds clandestine, conspiratorial, narrative intrigue, while contrive covers open crafting, building, and managing as well as scheming.
  • engineer: Similar because both mean to arrange or bring about something by careful behind-the-scenes work; differs because engineer implies technical, systematic, sometimes manipulative arrangement, while contrive is more craft-like, personal, and improvisational.
  • concoct: Similar because both mean to put something together by mental or practical work; differs because concoct is informal and suggests mixing ingredients (physical or figurative) — often a story or excuse — while contrive is more deliberate, precise, and purpose-driven.
  • fabricate: Similar because both can mean to construct or build something by craft; differs because fabricate is now dominated by the sense of manufacturing objects or manufacturing lies (with a clear negative cast), whereas contrive keeps a neutral-to-admiring planning sense.
  • design: Similar because both name planning a form or scheme before it is made; differs because design is neutral, systematic, and aesthetic, while contrive emphasizes effortful, artful management of means and ends.
  • manage: Similar because both can mean to bring about a result despite difficulty ("manage to do"); differs because manage is entirely neutral and broad (running, coping, handling), while contrive implies deliberate crafting and clever arrangement.
  • arrange: Similar because both mean to put things in order toward a goal; differs because arrange is neutral and logistical, while contrive implies skillful or artful working-out against some resistance.
  • maneuver / manoeuvre: Similar because both mean to bring about by strategic action; differs because maneuver emphasizes positioning, outwitting, and movement in relation to opponents, while contrive focuses on the successful crafting of the end itself.
  • wangle / finagle: Similar because both mean to get or arrange something by effort, often clever or shady; differs because these are informal and suggest persuasion or trickery, while contrive is more dignified, intellectual, and craft-like.
  • hatch: Similar because both mean to form a plan, especially a secret one; differs because hatch is chiefly used of schemes or plots and has a conspiratorial, avian-metaphor flavor, while contrive is broader and can be openly constructive.
