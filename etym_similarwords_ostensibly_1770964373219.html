• Apparently: Similar because both mean "to all appearances"; differs because apparently can be neutral or even hopeful, while ostensibly usually implies doubt about the appearance.
  • Seemingly: Similar because both mean "as it seems"; differs because seemingly is less confrontational, while ostensibly flags a likely discrepancy between show and reality.
  • Allegedly: Similar because both introduce a claim open to doubt; differs because allegedly is legalistic and attaches to accusations and reported claims, while ostensibly attaches to appearances and pretexts.
  • Purportedly: Similar because both mean "as is claimed"; differs because purportedly stresses an official or stated claim, while ostensibly stresses the visible surface or show.
  • Professedly: Similar because both describe declared intentions; differs because professedly implies an open declaration that may or may not be doubted, while ostensibly leans toward suspicion.
  • Avowedly: Similar because both describe an acknowledged stance; differs because avowedly means openly and honestly admitted, while ostensibly is about show, not candour.
  • Outwardly: Similar because both describe the surface; differs because outwardly contrasts inner and outer states without necessarily implying deception.
  • On the face of it: Similar because both mean "judging by appearances"; differs because the idiom is neutral and weighs the surface impression, while ostensibly leans toward suspicion.
  • Supposedly: Similar because both mean "as is generally believed or represented"; differs because supposedly reflects public belief or rumour, while ostensibly reflects a presented appearance or pretext.
  • Nominally: Similar because both mean "in name only"; differs because nominally contrasts title with reality in formal or official matters, while ostensibly covers actions and purposes.
  • In name: Similar because both separate appearance from reality; differs because "in name" is used of titles and labels, while ostensibly spans actions, reasons, and purposes.
