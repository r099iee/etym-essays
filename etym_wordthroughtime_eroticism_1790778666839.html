- Antiquity: Greek *erōs* and *erōtikos* denoted passionate, overpowering romantic desire and divine cosmological longing, sublimated by Plato into philosophical love of absolute beauty.
- 18th century: Coined in Enlightenment France as *érotisme* to establish a refined, secular vocabulary for the literary and aesthetic contemplation of amorous pleasure.
- 19th century: Entered English in the 1840s, initially treated with Victorian moral suspicion or catalogued within early psychiatric sexology as hyper-sensuality.
- 20th–21st centuries: Underwent massive intellectual elevation via psychoanalysis (Freud's *Eros*), existential philosophy (Bataille's exploration of taboo and mortality), and literary semiotics (Barthes's amorous textual discourse), becoming a standard cultural term for sophisticated, artistic, and consensual celebrations of desire.
