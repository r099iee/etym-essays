English (17th c.; of uncertain origin)
flurry
"a gust of wind; sudden commotion"
(possibly echoic, or from a dialectal word)
↓
English (sense extension)
flurry
"a sudden brief period of commotion
or excitement"

Debated: The origin of flurry is unknown. No securely attested source exists; both an echoic formation and a link to the dialectal verb flurr "to scatter, agitate" have been proposed, but neither is established. The "sudden fall of snow" sense (chiefly British) arose later from the "gust, sudden commotion" sense.
