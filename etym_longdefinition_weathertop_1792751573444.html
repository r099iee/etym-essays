Weathertop (proper noun, toponym) — A prominent, solitary conical hill rising at the southern extremity of the Weather Hills in the central wilderness of Eriador in J.R.R. Tolkien’s Middle-earth legendarium; known in the Elvish Sindarin tongue as Amon Sûl ("Hill of the Wind"), it was crowned in the Second Age by a magnificent fortress and watchtower that housed the chief palantír of the North Kingdom of Arnor, and later became the dramatic site where Frodo Baggins was stabbed by the Witch-king of Angmar.

1. Geographical Position and Topographic Features
Weathertop occupied a commanding and solitary vantage point across the vast plains of Eriador:

  • Location: Situated north of the Great East Road roughly midway between the Shire and Rivendell, it stood as the highest, southern-most bastion of the Weather Hills (a ridge running northeastward toward the Ettenmoors).
  • Topography: It possessed a conical, truncated crown that rose steep and bare above the surrounding scrubland and peat-bogs, affording an uninterrupted 360-degree panoramic vista across miles of desolate country.
  • The Dell and Approaches: Beneath the windswept crest lay a shallow, sheltered dell scooped out of the western slope, partially shielded by boulders and bushes, where travelers along the Road frequently camped out of the howling winds.

2. Historical Architecture and the Watchtower of Amon Sûl
The summit of Weathertop was rich with tragic historical and military grandeur:

  • Construction under Elendil: In the Second Age, following the Downfall of Númenor, the High King Elendil built a tall, slender stone watchtower on Weathertop to serve as an imperial beacon, defensive citadel, and astronomical observatory.
  • The Chief Palantír of the North: The tower housed the largest and most potent of the three Seeing Stones allotted to the North Kingdom. This stone was the primary medium through which the Kings of Arnor communicated with Gondor and monitored distant borders across Middle-earth.
  • Wars of the Fractured Realm: When Arnor fractured into three rival successor realms (Arthedain, Cardolan, and Rhudaur) in T.A. 861, possession of Weathertop and its palantír became a source of bitter interstate strife, with all three kingdoms coveting its strategic dominance.
  • Destruction by Angmar (T.A. 1409): In the devastating invasion led by the Witch-king of Angmar in T.A. 1409, a great allied army surrounded Weathertop; the tower was breached, looted, and burned to the ground, and King Arveleg I was slain. Although the palantír was saved by the retreating forces of Arthedain and taken to Fornost, the fortress was reduced to a blackened ring of shattered stone cairns.

3. Narrative Crucible in The Fellowship of the Ring
In the late Third Age, Weathertop served as the setting for one of the most suspenseful and fateful encounters of the War of the Ring:

  • Gandalf's Stand: On October 3, T.A. 3018, Gandalf the Grey was besieged on the summit by nine Ringwraiths, fighting through the night with blinding flashes of white light and fire that were visible to Aragorn and the hobbits miles away.
  • The Ambush on Frodo: Three nights later, on October 6, Aragorn and the four hobbits were cornered in the hollow beneath the summit by five Nazgûl. Paralyzed by dread, Frodo succumbed to the temptation to wear the One Ring, revealing his presence to the wraiths; the Witch-king pierced Frodo's shoulder with a splinter from a Morgul-knife before Aragorn drove them off with burning torches.

4. Toponymic Analysis and Germanic Place-Name Mechanics
Linguistically, Weathertop represents an authentic Germanic place-name formulation:

  • Literal Sense: Composed of weather (in its archaic sense of "storm, wind, tempestuous climate") and top (signifying "crest, summit, highest peak").
  • Philological Calque: It functions as an exact English translation of the Sindarin name Amon Sûl (*amon*, "hill" + *sûl*, "wind"), mirroring the Northern European topographic convention of naming wind-blasted knolls and exposed promontories after harsh atmospheric conditions (such as *Windy Brow* or *Gale Fell*).
