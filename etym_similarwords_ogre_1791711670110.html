• Monster: Similar because both refer to terrifying, hideous, and destructive creatures; differs because monster is a vast, generic umbrella term encompassing beasts, dragons, chimeras, and aberrations of any shape, whereas an ogre is specifically an oversized, brutish humanoid renowned for devouring human beings.
  • Giant: Similar because both denote beings of colossal physical size and strength in folklore; differs because a giant can be wise, noble, benevolent, or neutral (such as the Titans or Atlas in Greek myth), while a traditional ogre is inherently malevolent, hideous, and cannibalistic.
  • Fiend: Similar because both describe extremely wicked, cruel entities; differs because a fiend carries strong supernatural, demonic, or infernal connotations of spiritual evil and malicious cunning, whereas an ogre represents physical, gluttonous, and brute primal violence.
  • Brute: Similar because both convey savage cruelty, lack of empathy, and animalistic temperament; differs because brute emphasizes irrational stupidity and bestial insensibility in an ordinary human or animal, lacking the folkloric stature and man-eating nature of an ogre.
  • Tyrant: Similar because both refer to oppressive, feared figures who terrorize others; differs because a tyrant is an explicitly political ruler who abuses sovereign power, laws, or institutions to subjugate citizens, while ogre emphasizes visceral, terrifying monstrousness.
  • Behemoth: Similar because both convey overwhelming, monstrous scale; differs because behemoth (originating in the biblical Book of Job) emphasizes colossal, immovable physical or bureaucratic mass rather than active cannibalistic cruelty.
  • Cannibal: Similar because both share the defining horrific trait of consuming human flesh; differs because a cannibal is an otherwise ordinary human being who engages in anthropophagy, whereas an ogre is a mythical, grotesque race of fairy-tale giant.
  • Ghoul: Similar because both are folkloric human-eating horrors; differs because a ghoul (originating in Arabic folklore) is a grave-robbing creature that feeds primarily on dead corpses and carrion in cemeteries, while an ogre hunts, cooks, and devours the living.
  • Leviathan: Similar because both can metaphorically describe a massive, terrifying state apparatus; differs because Leviathan (from Thomas Hobbes and biblical sea-monster lore) evokes an awe-inspiring, all-encompassing, and legally organized sovereign power, whereas an ogre state evokes crude, feral predation.
