• Sancho Panza: Similar because both serve as earthy, pragmatically minded comic foils to noble, questing idealists (Tamino and Don Quixote); differs because Sancho Panza is a talkative Spanish peasant squire driven by rustic proverbs, physical comfort, and dreams of governing an island in Cervantes's prose epic, whereas Papageno is a whimsical, feather-clad bird-catcher in a magical singspiel whose primary quest is simple romance and companionship.
  • Figaro: Similar because both are beloved Mozartian baritone comic protagonists who celebrate the vitality and wit of the common folk against aristocratic authority; differs because Figaro (in Beaumarchais and Mozart's Le nozze di Figaro) is an intensely cunning, sharp-witted political strategist and master conspirator challenging feudal privilege, while Papageno is delightfully naive, simple-minded, and non-political.
  • Harlequin (Arlecchino): Similar because both trace their theatrical lineage to popular commedia dell'arte folk comedy, utilizing colorful costumes, physical slapstick, and acrobatics; differs because Harlequin is a zany, acrobatic, mischievous trickster servant driven by insatiable hunger and slapstick deceit, whereas Papageno possesses a tender, vulnerable, and deeply romantic soul.
  • Hanswurst: Similar because Hanswurst is the traditional Viennese-German folk clown who directly inspired Emanuel Schikaneder's creation of Papageno for the suburban Viennese stage; differs because Hanswurst was often coarse, gluttonous, vulgar, and scatological, whereas Mozart and Schikaneder refined Papageno into a charming, poetic, and heartwarming universal figure.
  • Leporello: Similar because both are Mozartian bass-baritone servants / companions who provide comic commentary and express terror when confronted by supernatural forces; differs because Leporello in Don Giovanni is cynical, resentful, morally complicit in his master's debauchery, and world-weary, while Papageno is innocent, cheerful, and spiritually pure.
  • Falstaff: Similar because both are larger-than-life comic operatic figures who celebrate wine, food, and earthly survival over martial heroics; differs because Shakespeare and Verdi's Falstaff is an aging, vain, boastful, and manipulative knight, whereas Papageno is youthful, modest, self-effacing, and guileless.
  • Tamino: Similar because both are the twin protagonists of The Magic Flute traveling together through the trials of Sarastro; differs because Tamino represents the solemn, aristocratic, intellectual hero seeking spiritual enlightenment, while Papageno represents the comic, earthly everyman seeking wine, bread, and a wife.
