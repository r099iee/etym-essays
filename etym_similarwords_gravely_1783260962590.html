• Seriously: Similar because both adverbs express the idea of "in a serious manner / to a serious degree"; differs because seriously is the everyday, all-purpose adverb used in speech and slang ("seriously?", "seriously ill"), whereas gravely is formal, weightier, and reserved for solemn and critical contexts.
  • Solemnly: Similar because both describe a grave, dignified manner; differs because solemnly stresses formal, ceremonial, or religious dignity and ritual, while gravely emphasizes the heaviness and seriousness of the matter itself.
  • Somberly: Similar because both describe a dark, serious manner; differs because somberly points to gloom, darkness, and melancholy mood, whereas gravely points to weight and importance.
  • Soberly: Similar because both describe a restrained, earnest manner without levity; differs because soberly emphasizes calm, sensible, abstemious earnestness, while gravely suggests the press of heavy or dangerous significance.
  • Sternly: Similar because both describe a serious manner; differs because sternly carries authority, severity, and reproof toward others, whereas gravely can be entirely inward — the seriousness of a situation, not of a judge.
  • Earnestly: Similar because both describe a sincere, heartfelt manner; differs because earnestly stresses sincerity and zealous intention, while gravely stresses weight and seriousness, which may be solemn without being eager.
  • Severely: Similar because both can mark a critical, dangerous degree ("gravely injured"/"severely injured"); differs because severely emphasizes harshness and intensity of degree across many domains, while gravely adds the specific connotation of life-threatening weight.
  • Critically: Similar because both mark the danger point ("critically ill"/"gravely ill"); differs because critically is clinical and technical — the precise medical triage term — while gravely is the older, more literary and less technical word.
  • Dangerously: Similar because both can mean "to a harmful degree"; differs because dangerously names the risk itself in general terms, whereas gravely names the weight and seriousness of the condition.
  • Portentously: Similar because both describe an impressively serious manner; differs because portentously implies ominous, self-important foreboding, often with irony, while gravely can be sincere and dignified.
  • Weightily: Similar because both descend from the same metaphor of heaviness and mean "in a weighty, serious manner"; differs because weightily is rarer and more literal, still touching the physical sense of weight, whereas gravely has been fully absorbed by the moral and critical senses.
