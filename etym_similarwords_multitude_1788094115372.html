• Crowd: Similar because both denote a large number of people together; differs because 'crowd' stresses physical gathering in one place and is the everyday word, while 'multitude' stresses sheer number and is formal.
  • Throng: Similar because both denote a large gathering of people; differs because 'throng' emphasises density and pressing movement, while 'multitude' emphasises scale and number.
  • Host: Similar because both mean a great number; differs because 'host' is more poetic and archaic, and can be used of angels, armies, or abstract things.
  • Swarm: Similar because both mean a large moving mass; differs because 'swarm' is used of insects or busy people and implies agitation, while 'multitude' is neutral and general.
  • Mass: Similar because both denote a large undifferentiated body of people; differs because 'mass' (esp. 'the masses') is sociological and ideological, while 'multitude' is broader.
  • Horde: Similar because both denote a large (often threatening) crowd; differs because 'horde' carries menace and disorder, and originally meant a nomadic tribe.
  • Legion: Similar because both mean a vast number; differs because 'legion' is hyperbolic and literary (originally a Roman military unit).
  • Army: Similar because both mean a great number; differs because 'army' implies organisation or military force.
  • Mob: Similar because both denote a disorderly crowd; differs because 'mob' is pejorative and turbulent, whereas 'multitude' is neutral or positive.
  • Myriad: Similar because both mean an immense number; differs because 'myriad' is used mostly of things, not people, and is more literary and hyperbolic.
  • Plethora: Similar because both mean an abundance; differs because 'plethora' implies excess (too many), often of things rather than people.
  • Abundance / Profusion: Similar because both mean a large quantity; differs because these are used of things, not of crowds of people.
