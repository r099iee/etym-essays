- 14th–16th century: West Slavic Avian Nickname - Originated in medieval Slavic borderlands as a diminutive byname (*kavka* / *kawka*), designating the jackdaw (*Corvus monedula*), bestowed colloquially upon dark-haired, sharp-eyed, or lively individuals.
- 17th–18th century: Silesian & Bohemian Dialectal Germanization - Underwent phonetic labial rounding from West Slavic *Kavka* / *Kawka* to *Koffka* / *Kofka* in the bilingual administrative territories of Silesia and Bohemia, establishing an enduring family surname.
- 1910s–1920s: The Frankfurt Triumvirate & Gestalt Foundations - Entered the annals of scientific psychology through Kurt Koffka, who collaborated with Max Wertheimer and Wolfgang Köhler at Frankfurt to overthrow atomistic sensory associationism with holistic perceptual field theory.
- 1930s–1950s: American Academic Exilic Synthesis - Immortalized across Anglo-American universities after Koffka's emigration to Smith College, where his monumental 1935 English textbook Principles of Gestalt Psychology established the definitive lexicon of holistic perceptual science.
- Late 20th century to Present: Monument of Cognitive Science - Revered throughout psychology, aesthetics, and cognitive science as an iconic foundational proper noun symbolizing the non-reducibility of structured psychological wholes.
