- Foolishness in medieval English - In the 14th-15th centuries nicety (from Old French niceté) meant folly, foolishness, or excessive simplicity, a sense now wholly extinct.
- Shift to subtle discrimination - In the 16th-18th centuries the word reverses into its modern core: refined delicacy, minute exactness, fastidious discrimination in judgment or manner.
- Modern precision usage - Today nicety means a fine point or subtle distinction ('the niceties of etiquette', 'to a nicety'), still formal and slightly archaic, while the separate modern word niceness (pleasantness) has drifted far away from it.
