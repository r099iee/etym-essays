- 1603–1650: French explorers' label for feared Five Nations enemies — exotic, dangerous thenewcomers' rival and trading partner.
- 1650–1763: Administrative-military term in New France — the Iroquois as diplomatic power to convert, fight or sign treaties with; Jesuit Relations make them famous readers' Indians.
- 1763–1850: Frontier literary Indian — noble savage or bloodthirsty savage in novels, captivity narratives and American histories; longhouse villages romanticised.
- 1851–1920: Anthropological model — Morgan's League celebrated as primitive democracy and matrilineal kinship archetype; Iroquois becomes textbook example.
- 1920–1970: Linguistic-family term — Iroquoian languages studied structurally; ethnic sense static, somewhat museumised.
- 1970–present: Contested exonym yielding to Haudenosaunee — respected historically but increasingly replaced by the endonym in schools, museums and official use.
