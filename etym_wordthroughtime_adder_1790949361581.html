- Proto-Indo-European to Old English - PIE *nē-tr- evolves into Old English nǣdre / nǣddre, meaning snake, viper, serpent, or dragon.
- 14th to 15th Century - Middle English resegmentation (metanalysis): a naddere is misdivided by speakers as an addere, permanently dropping the initial 'n'.
- 16th to 17th Century - Stabilized in Early Modern English and the King James Bible (1611), immortalizing the 'deaf adder' of Psalm 58.
- 18th to 19th Century - Zoology restricts the primary British meaning to Vipera berus, while British colonial expansion encounters Australian 'death adders' and African 'puff adders'.
- 20th to 21st Century - Retained in biological science, ecological conservation in Britain, and literary allusion as an emblem of hidden venom or unyielding obstinacy.
