• secluded: Similar because both mean withdrawn from sight or company; differs because secluded implies chosen privacy, while shut-in implies enforced enclosure.
  • enclosed: Similar because both describe being bounded on all sides; differs because enclosed is neutral physical description, while shut-in adds the idea of being closed off from the outside.
  • confined: Similar because both mean limited within bounds; differs because confined is broader (also mental and abstract), while shut-in specifically evokes walls, doors, and containment.
  • isolated: Similar because both mean cut off from others; differs because isolated stresses solitude and separation, shut-in stresses physical enclosure.
  • cloistered: Similar because both describe being shut away; differs because cloistered has monastic, often voluntary overtones.
  • reclusive: Similar because both describe a person withdrawn from society; differs because reclusive is a chosen temperament, while a shut-in is typically confined by illness or circumstance.
  • housebound: Similar because both name a person confined at home; differs because housebound is the modern neutral term while shut-in is older and more pitying.
  • landlocked: Similar because both describe a place bounded by land; differs because landlocked applies only to countries and bodies of water, shut-in to any enclosure.
  • hemmed in: Similar because both mean surrounded and restricted; differs because hemmed in is more often used of people under pressure, shut-in of physical space.
  • sequestered: Similar because both mean separated from the world; differs because sequestered is formal and often legal or voluntary in flavour.
