- c. 1300s–1400s: Middle English *coppeweb* entered written records, denoting the woven silk trap of a spider (from Middle English *coppe*, "spider").
- 1500s–1650s: The phonetic reduction from *copweb* to *cobweb* stabilized; folklore and early medicine widely used clean cobwebs as a topical styptic to staunch bleeding on open wounds.
- 1700s–1850s: Acquired widespread metaphorical usage referring to mental fog, outdated legal doctrines, superstitious folklore, or neglected intellectual faculties requiring "brushing away."
- 1900s–Present: Solidified in modern culture both as a horror/fantasy trope signifying antiquity and decay, and as an idiom for clearing stagnation or mental fatigue ("blowing away the cobwebs").
