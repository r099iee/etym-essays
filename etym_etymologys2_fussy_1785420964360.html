FUSSY: no PIE ancestry. The word is an expressive, sound-symbolic (imitative/onomatopoeic) formation. Chain: Expressive Germanic sound-root *fuss- "bustle, agitation, puffing" (reconstructed by comparison of North and West Germanic forms; no PIE etymon exists) → Old Norse fuss "eager, willing, desirous" (attested; the "eager, forward" sense) → Dutch / Flemish vuzen "to blow, puff, vent" (attested; the puffing, blowing sense) → Middle Low German fuss "tumult, commotion, bustle" (attested; the "hubbub over nothing" sense) → English noun fuss "tumult, needless commotion or agitation over trifles" (first recorded c. 1701) → English verb to fuss "to bustle about, to fidget, to make much of small matters" (early 18th c.) → English adjective fussy "inclined to make a fuss; over-exacting about details" (fuss + -y, from Old English -ig "characterized by"; c. 1770s) → FUSSY (exact form of this card) Note: because fuss is an expressive formation, the ordinary rules of Germanic sound change do not apply and the relatives above are sound-symbolic kin rather than regular cognates. No Indo-European root is reconstructed for the word, and lexicographers (OED) class it as of imitative or expressive origin; the tree therefore begins with the expressive base and carries the explicit label "no PIE ancestry."
