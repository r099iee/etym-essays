- Early Modern English "fluster" - named a hot, scattered state of agitation and confusion, including the muddled heat of drink, as when a person was "flustered" by strong ale.
- 19th century - the "tipsy" sense faded and "flustered" centred on social and temporal pressure: hurrying, surprise, or too many demands at once putting a person off balance and ruffling their composure.
- Modern English - a homely, comic, mildly sympathetic word: a temporary flutter of nerves and blushing haste under pressure, not trauma, fear, or deep confusion.
