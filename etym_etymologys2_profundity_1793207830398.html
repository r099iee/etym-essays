PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*bhudh-
‘bottom, base, ground’
↓
Latin (c. 700 BCE onward)
fundus
‘bottom of a thing’
↓
Latin (classical, 1st c. BCE)
profundus
‘deep, bottomless, vast’
↓
Late Latin (c. 3rd–5th c.)
profunditas
‘deepness, depth, abyss’
↓
Old French (12th c.)
profundité
‘depth, deepness’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
profundite
‘physical depth, abyss’
↓
Modern English (current)
profundity
‘intellectual or spiritual depth’

Note: Latin fundus is cognate with Sanskrit budhna, Greek pythmēn, and Old English botm, all pointing to the base above; the exact laryngeal shape of the PIE form is debated among specialists.
