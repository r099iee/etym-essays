• United: Similar because both describe things joined into one whole; differs because united states the result without any bonding agent or the slow hardening that cementing implies.
  • Fused: Similar because both mean joined firmly and permanently; differs because fused implies heat and melting (metalwork, glass), too violent for the 'gentle… influence' that cements an alliance.
  • Welded: Similar because both imply a strong, durable join; differs because welding is a metallurgical process of extreme heat and industrial skill, alien to cement's mineral-settling image.
  • Bonded: Similar because both express attachment made secure; differs because bonded is more abstract and can be chemical, emotional, or financial, while cemented keeps the masonry metaphor of setting.
  • Consolidated: Similar because both convey the firming of a union; differs because consolidated is administrative, fiscal, and bureaucratic rather than physical and adhesive.
  • Solidified: Similar because both mean made firm and fixed; differs because solidified is the generic hardening of a liquid to a solid, lacking the specific mortar-and-masonry image.
  • Bound: Similar because both describe being held together; differs because bound suggests rope, ties, or constraints rather than the mineral set of cement.
  • Tied: Similar because both describe being connected fast; differs because tied implies knots and laces that can easily be undone, while cemented implies irreversible hardening.
  • Glued: Similar because both mean stuck together; differs because glued is colloquial, quick, and weak, lacking cement's strength and permanence.
  • Sealed: Similar because both describe something made secure and closed; differs because sealed emphasizes closure against leakage, not the cohesion of parts into one mass.
  • Fixed: Similar because both mean made stable and immovable; differs because fixed is general and can describe nails, bolts, or rules, without cement's bonding-agent image.
  • Fastened: Similar because both mean secured in place; differs because fastened suggests mechanical means (clasps, buckles, screws) rather than chemical setting.
  • Joined: Similar because both describe parts brought together; differs because joined is the most neutral term and says nothing about firmness or permanence.
  • Knit: Similar because both describe parts growing together into one fabric; differs because knit implies interweaving of fibres or healing tissue, not masonry.
  • Locked: Similar because both describe being made immovably fixed; differs because locked implies a mechanism and a key, with the possibility of unlocking, whereas cementing is permanent.
  • Strengthened: Similar because both describe making something firmer; differs because strengthened is about adding force or power, not about binding separate elements together.
  • Anchored: Similar because both describe being fixed firmly in place; differs because anchored implies attachment to a single point, while cemented binds multiple parts into one mass.
  • Soldered: Similar because both describe joining parts with a filler material; differs because soldering uses metal alloy and heat in electronics and metalwork, not stone and lime.
