• paddling: Similar because it means moving through shallow water on foot; differs because paddling is playful splashing at the water's edge with no real forward progress, while wading is purposeful walking through water with the feet on the bottom.
  • fording: Similar because it means crossing water on foot (or by vehicle) at a shallow point; differs because fording is the specific, technical act of crossing a river at a fordable spot, while wading is the more general, leisurely walking through any shallow water.
  • splashing: Similar because it involves moving through water; differs because splashing emphasises the noise and spray of careless motion, while wading emphasises steady, deliberate progress with the feet grounded.
  • trudging: Similar because it means walking slowly and heavily with effort; differs because trudging is about weariness over ground (a long, flat, tiring walk), while wading is specifically about walking through a resisting medium — water — and need not be wearying at all.
  • sloshing: Similar because it means moving in or through liquid; differs because sloshing is looser, wetter and noisier — liquid swilling about — while wading is the calmer, more deliberate act of walking through water that stays around your ankles or calves.
  • slogging: Similar because it means making slow, heavy effort through something resistant; differs because slogging is about hard labour (physical or mental), while wading is about walking through water, with the figurative "wading through" a task borrowed from that water image.
  • plunging: Similar because it means entering water; differs because plunging is a sudden, whole-body dive or drop beneath the surface, while wading keeps your feet on the bottom and the water at ankle or calf height — immersion without surrender.
