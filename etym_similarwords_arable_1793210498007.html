• Cultivable: Similar because both describe land that can be cropped; differs because cultivable is the broader technical term for anything workable by tillage or garden tool, while arable specifically means fit for the plough and field-scale corn crops.
  • Tillable: Similar because both name soil open to tillage; differs because tillable stresses the mechanical act of tilling and suits North American farm listings, while arable is the classic British census and literary word.
  • Farmable: Similar because both describe ground that will repay farming; differs because farmable covers grass, orchard, and buildings as well as crops, while arable is cropped ploughland only, set against grass.
  • Productive: Similar because good arable is productive ground; differs because productive measures output and profit across any enterprise, while arable names the capability for corn crops whether the yield proves rich or lean.
  • Fertile: Similar because fertile soils make the best arable; differs because fertile describes richness in nutrients and life, while arable describes fitness for the plough — thin chalk can be arable though barely fertile, and a fertile water-meadow may never be ploughed.
  • Ploughable: Similar because both mean open to the plough; differs because ploughable is the plain physical statement that the share will go through it, while arable adds the legal, customary, and rotational sense of land held and worked as corn ground.
  • Cropped: Similar because arable fields are cropped fields; differs because cropped reports this season's sown state, while arable reports enduring capability — fallow or stubble is still arable though nothing green stands on it.
