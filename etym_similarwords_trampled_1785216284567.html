• Trod / trodden: Similar because both mean stepping on something with the feet; differs because treading is plain walking on or over something, without the repeated, crushing force that trampling implies.
  • Stamped: Similar because both involve forceful footfalls; differs because stamping is a single deliberate violent step (usually of anger), while trampling is repeated, crushing, and often unintentional.
  • Stomped: Similar because both are heavy-footed verbs; differs because stomping is informal, chiefly American, and expressive of anger, while trampling stresses the repeated crushing of something underfoot.
  • Crushed: Similar because both mean damaging by pressure; differs because crushing is general (by any weight, tool, or force), while trampling is specifically by feet, hooves, or a moving crowd.
  • Squashed: Similar because both mean pressed flat or out of shape; differs because squashing is often done by sitting, squeezing, or dropping, and is informal, while trampling is by treading.
  • Flattened: Similar because both describe something pressed down; differs because flattening stresses the level result and may be achieved by any means, while trampling emphasizes repeated heavy footsteps.
  • Ground: Similar because both mean destroying under pressure; differs because grinding implies a twisting or rubbing abrasion (as in "ground into the dust"), while trampling is a straight stamping crush.
  • Overrun: Similar because both describe a mass passing over something; differs because overrunning is about the spread or invasion itself, while trampling specifies the crushing underfoot.
  • Overwhelmed: Similar because both can mean crushed by superior force; differs because overwhelming is usually figurative (emotions, odds, armies) and need not involve feet at all.
  • Rode roughshod over: Similar because both mean disregarding something brutally; differs because the idiom is specifically about contemptuous disregard of rights or opinions, while trampled keeps a concrete image of feet.
