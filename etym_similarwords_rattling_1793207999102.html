• Clattering: Similar because both name rapid hard knocking sounds in motion; differs because clattering stresses the sharper louder impact (pans, hooves), while rattling stresses looser repeated shaking (windows, dice).
  • Clanking: Similar because both are metallic percussive noises; differs because clanking is the deep ring of heavy metal (chains, engines), while rattling is lighter and quicker, small knocks in series.
  • Jangling: Similar because both describe shaken metal sounding discordantly; differs because jangling stresses harshness on the ear (jangling nerves, keys), while rattling stresses speed and bustle.
  • Rumbling: Similar because both are low continuous travelling noises; differs because rumbling is deep and rolling (thunder, stomachs, lorries), while rattling is higher, broken into distinct knocks.
  • Clinking: Similar because both are bright percussive sounds of things struck; differs because clinking is the clear ring of glass or coin, while rattling is the drier knock of wood, bone, or loose frames.
  • Bustling: Similar because both convey noisy energetic activity; differs because bustling is about busy human movement (bustling streets), while rattling is about the percussive sound that movement makes.
  • Tearing: Similar because to go tearing along matches the rattling pace for speed; differs because tearing stresses violent haste that damages, while rattling stresses noisy haste that announces itself.
  • Bowling along: Similar because both picture brisk smoothish travel; differs because bowling along is easy rolling progress, while rattling adds jolts, noise, and a hint of recklessness.
  • Thunderous: Similar because both describe arrivals that cannot be ignored; differs because thunderous is grand overwhelming noise (applause, wrath), while rattling is everyday percussive noise, comic rather than cosmic.
