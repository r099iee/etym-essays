• Dodged: Similar because both involve quick evasive movement to avoid an incoming hazard; differs because dodged emphasizes lateral or sideways slipping, whereas ducked explicitly requires downward spatial lowering or vertical immersion.
  • Submerged: Similar because both involve being placed under water; differs because submerged is a formal, Latinate term emphasizing total, sustained inundation beneath liquid, while ducked implies a sudden, often temporary dip or plunge.
  • Dived: Similar because both describe plunging beneath water; differs because dived implies a purposeful, headfirst entry into deep water from a height or sustained underwater swimming, whereas ducked can be a passive, momentary, or evasive dip.
  • Stooped: Similar because both involve lowering the upper body; differs because stooped is a slower, deliberate bending forward from the waist or a permanent postural habit, lacking the sudden agility and evasive purpose of ducked.
  • Evaded: Similar because both describe avoiding a threat or question; differs because evaded is a formal, abstract cognitive term describing strategic avoidance, while ducked is visceral, kinetic, and colloquial.
  • Plunged: Similar because both involve forceful entry into liquid or depths; differs because plunged carries far greater violent momentum, dramatic depth, and potential finality than the swift dip of ducked.
  • Shirked: Similar because both describe avoiding responsibilities; differs because shirked specifically implies moral cowardice, laziness, or dereliction of duty, whereas ducked focuses on the clever or quick maneuver of slipping away.
  • Crouched: Similar because both involve lowering the body; differs because crouched denotes holding a sustained, low, squatting posture for concealment or preparation to spring, rather than a momentary evasive dip.
