WRIGGLED is the preterite of the frequentative verb wriggle, which English borrowed from continental West Germanic; its deeper Indo-European link is possible but uncertain. Linear chain: 1. Middle Low German / Middle Dutch wriggelen "to wriggle, to move to and fro" (attested 14th–15th centuries) — a frequentative formed on the base verb wriggen "to move to and fro, twist." 2. Middle Low German / Middle Dutch wriggen "to twist, move to and fro, turn about" (the base verb). 3. Possible PIE *wreik- / *wer- "to turn, twist, bend" (a root proposed for the Germanic wrig- base) — DEBATED: this deep link is not universally accepted, and the Germanic base may be an independent expressive formation; an intermediate Proto-Germanic *wrigōną is reconstructed by some scholars but is not directly attested. 4. English wriggle — borrowed from the Low German / Dutch frequentative in the late 15th century (recorded c. 1490), first meaning "to move with a twisting, writhing motion." 5. English inflected WRIGGLED — past tense and past participle of wriggle; "twisted and turned with quick writhing movements," and figuratively "extricated oneself by evasion" (the idiom to wriggle out of). Note on the debate: the PIE stage is hypothetical. The documented history begins with the attested Low German / Dutch frequentative of the 14th–15th centuries; the reconstruction of a Proto-Germanic *wrigōną is standard but not directly attested; and any link to PIE *wreik- is speculative. No stage beyond the Middle Dutch/Low German forms is directly attested in writing.
