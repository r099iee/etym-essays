PIE *móri-

"sea, standing body of water"
(reconstructed root; cf. Latin mare, Welsh môr,
Old English mere "lake", Gothic marei)

↓

Proto-Germanic *mariz

"sea, lake, standing water"
(reconstructed; the source of the element in
English marsh, and of Old English mere)

↓

Frankish / West Germanic *marisk

"marshy, low-lying wet land; marsh"
(reconstructed adjective-noun from *mari-;
borrowed into Gallo-Romance)

↓

Old French marais

"marsh, swamp, bog"
(attested from the 9th–10th c.; from
Frankish *marisk)

↓

Middle Dutch moeras / moerasch

"marsh, bog, fen, swampy ground"
(14th–15th c.; a loan reshaped from
Old French marais)

↓

Dutch moeras

"marsh, bog, swamp"
(17th c.)

↓

English morass

"tract of soft, wet, boggy ground; marsh"
(borrowed from Dutch, first recorded 1650s)

↓

MORASS

"marshy ground; a complicated, confusing,
or treacherous situation from which escape
is difficult"
(Modern English)

Note: the descent via Frankish *marisk → Old French marais →
Middle Dutch moeras is the standard account; some authorities
allow direct Middle Dutch borrowing. The PIE *móri- stage is
reconstructed, not attested, and is debated for the marsh line.
