• Interval: similar because both denote a space of time between events; differs because "interval" (Latin intervallum, "space between the palisades") is clinical and measurable, the mathematical and scientific default, while "interlude" implies an episode with character and a whiff of play.
  • Intermission: similar because both name a performance break; differs because "intermission" is strictly the pause in a staged event and implies an audience waiting to resume, while "interlude" is the piece that fills such a pause — or a temporal episode far from any theater.
  • Intermezzo: similar because both name a short musical or dramatic piece between larger parts; differs because "intermezzo" (Italian) is the technical musicological word and stays in the concert hall, while "interlude" ranges over time, stage, and life generally.
  • Entr'acte: similar because both mean what comes between acts of a play; differs because "entr'acte" (French, "between the acts") is strictly theatrical and refers to the interval or its entertainment, while "interlude" is far wider in scope but less precise about the theater.
  • Pause: similar because both denote a stop in a sequence; differs because "pause" is momentary, structural, and value-free — the motion resumes at once — while an "interlude" has duration, atmosphere, and often emotional weight.
  • Respite: similar because both can name a welcome break in hardship; differs because "respite" implies relief from suffering or labor (a stay of execution, a rest from toil), whereas an "interlude" may be neutral or delightful, not necessarily a relief.
  • Hiatus: similar because both name an interruption between periods; differs because "hiatus" (Latin "a gap, a wide opening") is formal and suggests an unexplained or undesired break — a hole in the record — while an "interlude" is itself a meaningful little episode.
  • Lull: similar because both name a quiet period between storms or busy stretches; differs because a "lull" is the temporary subsidence of activity (the calm eye of a storm) and is passive, while an "interlude" is more active, even staged — something happens.
  • Breathing space: similar because both denote a welcome period of calm; differs because "breathing space" is purely about relief from pressure and urgency, with no theatrical or musical associations at all.
  • Digression / episode: similar because both name a departure from the main course; differs because a "digression" (speech or writing that wanders off) has to return, and an "episode" is one entry in a continuing series, whereas an "interlude" is defined wholly by the between-ness of its position.
