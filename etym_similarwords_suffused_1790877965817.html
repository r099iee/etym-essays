• Permeated: Similar because both denote spreading completely through something; differs because permeated implies passing through physical pores or structural gaps (often associated with smells, gases, or ideas), while suffused evokes a glowing wash of light, color, or deep emotion.
  • Imbued: Similar because both involve deeply saturating with a quality or feeling; differs because imbued suggests soaking in principles, values, or deep spiritual qualities from an external source, whereas suffused emphasizes the visible, radiant spreading of light, blush, or passion.
  • Infused: Similar because both trace to Latin verbs of pouring (fundere); differs because infused emphasizes introducing a new element, flavor, or energy into a substance (e.g., infusing herbs or infusing courage), whereas suffused describes the resultant all-encompassing spread.
  • Overspread: Similar because both describe spreading across a surface; differs because overspread is purely descriptive, physical, and planar (covering the top), lacking the emotional depth, warmth, and internal welling of suffused.
  • Flushed: Similar because both describe color or heat rising in the skin; differs because flushed is rapid, sudden, and often feverish or athletic, while suffused is more gradual, continuous, and luminous.
  • Saturated: Similar because both mean thoroughly filled; differs because saturated carries a heavy, wet, or maximal technical connotation (soaked to capacity), whereas suffused remains delicate, atmospheric, and radiant.
