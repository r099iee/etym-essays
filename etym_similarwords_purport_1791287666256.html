• gist: Similar because it means main point; differs because gist is informal and conversational while purport is formal and documentary.
  • import: Similar because it means meaning carried by words; differs because import stresses weighty consequence while purport stresses intended gist.
  • substance: Similar because it means essential content; differs because substance is broader (can be physical) while purport is only of messages.
  • tenor: Similar because it means drift or course of discourse; differs because tenor stresses overall direction while purport stresses pointed intention.
  • drift: Similar because it means general meaning; differs because drift is vague and metaphorical while purport is precise and legalistic.
  • claim: Similar because as a verb purport means to assert; differs because claim is neutral while purport often implies doubt or ostensibility.
  • profess: Similar because it means to lay claim to a character; differs because profess can be sincere avowal while purport leans toward alleged appearance.
  • allege: Similar because it reports without endorsing; differs because allege is explicitly legal-accusatory while purport is descriptive of appearance.
  • signify: Similar because it means to mean or convey; differs because signify is semiotic and neutral while purport adds intentional carriage.
  • pretend: Similar because it involves false appearance; differs because pretend is clearly deceptive and childlike while purport is formal and leaves falsity open.
  • purported: Similar because it is the adjectival form meaning alleged; differs because purported functions as wary adjective while purport is noun or verb.
