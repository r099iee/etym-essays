Second element: wrought
PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*werǵ-
"to do, to make, to work"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 1st millennium BCE, reconstructed)
*werką (n.) / *wurkijaną (v.)
"work; to work, make"
↓
Old English (c. 8th–11th c.)
wyrcean — past worhte
"to work, make, perform; wrought, made"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
wroghte / wrouhte
"worked, made, fashioned"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
wrought
"worked; fashioned by hand" — survives as
adjective and past participle
↓
Modern English — un- + wrought:
unwrought
"not worked, not manufactured, raw"

Note: the same negative formation already occurs in Old English as unworht
"not made, not worked" (c. 10th c.): the word is native, not a loan coinage.
