• poured: Similar because both mean "released a liquid"; differs because "poured" is plain and physical, while "effused" implies free, abundant, often graceful or beneficent dispersal, and extends to gases, light, and emotions.
  • spilled: Similar because both mean "released from a container"; differs because "spilled" is accidental and wasteful, while "effused" is deliberate, generous, or natural.
  • disseminated: Similar because both mean "spread widely"; differs because "disseminated" is dry and bureaucratic (information, seeds, propaganda), while "effused" carries a fluid, radiant, life-giving quality.
  • emitted: Similar because both mean "sent out"; differs because "emitted" is mechanical and neutral (light, heat, gas from a source), while "effused" suggests free-flowing spread and, in older usage, generosity.
  • emanated: Similar because both mean "issued forth from a source"; differs because "emanated" is gradual, ghostly, and often abstract (an influence, a fragrance), while "effused" can be a vigorous outpouring of a liquid or quality.
  • radiated: Similar because both mean "extended outward from a centre"; differs because "radiated" suggests linear rays of light or heat, while "effused" suggests a fluid or mist rolling and spreading outward.
  • diffused: Similar because both mean "spread through a space or medium"; differs because "diffused" stresses even dispersion and mixing (of a gas, a scent, an idea), while "effused" stresses the act of pouring out from a source.
  • exuded: Similar because both mean "given off from within"; differs because "exuded" implies slow oozing or secretion (a resin, sweat, a quality), while "effused" implies a fuller, more generous outpouring.
  • discharged: Similar because both mean "released outward"; differs because "discharged" is technical, abrupt, and often implies waste or duty fulfilled, while "effused" is graceful and abundant.
  • spread: Similar because both mean "caused to cover a wider area"; differs because "spread" is entirely neutral and general, losing the specific image of pouring forth from a source.
