Note: The precise Mesoamerican etymology of the Mexican toponym Tabasco remains historically contested. One major hypothesis derives it from the name of the indigenous Chontal Maya halach uinik (cacique) Tabscoob, who ruled the province when Spanish explorer Juan de Grijalva arrived in 1518. An alternate linguistic derivation traces the name to Classical Nahuatl tlapalco ("place of damp earth", from tlapalli "colored earth/damp ground" + locative -co) or tla-huash-co ("place that has a master/lord"). The word was borrowed into Spanish as a territorial designation, passed into 19th-century English as an agricultural descriptor for the capsicum chili pepper, and was subsequently trademarked in 1870 by Edmund McIlhenny for his world-famous condiment.
