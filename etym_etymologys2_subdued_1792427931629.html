Note on historical phonology and semantic contamination: The transmission of subdue into English represents one of the most remarkable instances of lexical blending in Romance philology. In Classical Latin, subdūcere meant "to draw off, withdraw secretly, take away" (or of ships, "to beach or haul ashore"). When passing into Old French, it developed into souduire, which meant "to seduce, lead astray, beguile" (by drawing someone secretly away from duty). However, in Anglo-Norman and early Middle English, the verb was systematically contaminated by and phonetically identified with an entirely different Latin verb: subdere ("to put under, subject, tame, vanquish"), as well as subjugāre ("to subjugate, place under the yoke"). Consequently, English dropped the French meaning of deceitful seduction and permanently absorbed the Latinate sense of martial conquest and moral subjugation, which subsequently softened into modern sensory and psychological muting.
