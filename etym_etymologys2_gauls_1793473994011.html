Note: The etymology of English Gaul represents one of the most famous convergence phenomena in European lexicography. Latin Gallus directly borrows a native Celtic ethnonym based on Proto-Celtic *galn- ('valour, power', cognate with Old Irish gal 'warlike fury' and Welsh gallu 'power'). However, the French form Gaule and English Gaul do not derive phonetically from Latin Gallia (which would have yielded Old French *Jaille). Instead, when Frankish Germanic tribes settled in Roman Gaul, they applied their Germanic term *Walhaz ('foreign Celtic/Roman speaker', from the Celtic tribal name Volcae). In Gallo-Romance, Germanic initial /w/ regularly transformed into /gw/ and then /g/ (as in werra -> guerre, wardon -> garder), yielding Old French Gaule, which Renaissance humanists later harmonized with classical Latin Gallus.
