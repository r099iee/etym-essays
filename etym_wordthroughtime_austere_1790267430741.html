- 1300s–1400s: Middle English Adoption - Borrowed via Old French from Latin/Greek; initially applied to harsh, astringent, bitter tastes, sour wine, and severe medical conditions or bodily humors.
- 1400s–1600s: Monastic and Moral Gravity - Shifted predominantly into religious and ethical discourse, describing unbending spiritual discipline, stern monastic rules, self-denying ascetics, and grave moral demeanors.
- 1600s–1800s: Aesthetic and Stylistic Restraint - Broadened to architectural, literary, and decorative styles characterized by dignified simplicity, geometric purity, and the deliberate absence of frivolous ornament.
- 1900s–Present: Macroeconomic Policy and Modernist Architecture - Expanded into political economy (denoting fiscal spending cuts and public sector retrenchment) while simultaneously serving as high praise for clean, functionalist minimalist design.
