- 14th c. — the French loan arrives: Middle English crevise names the freshwater crustacean of manor fishponds and rivers, a table delicacy.
- 15th–16th c. — folk etymology bites: speakers hear crevise and reshape it toward fish, giving crayfish and crawfish — a crab that is neither cray nor fish.
- 17th–18th c. — colonial spread: settlers carry the word to American creeks and Australian billabongs, breeding crawdad and borrowing yabby beside it.
- 19th c. — science sorts them: naturalists place crayfish among the decapods and map hundreds of species across two hemispheres.
- 20th c. — feast and plague: Louisiana boils and Swedish kräftskiva celebrate them while signal crayfish and plague fungus devastate native stocks.
- Today — indicator and icon: the conservationist’s marker of clean rivers and the festival food of spring boils, beloved and besieged at once.
