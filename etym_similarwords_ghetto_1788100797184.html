• slum: Similar because both denote poor urban districts; differs because slum is purely about poverty and decay, with no reference to ethnic confinement or legal segregation, and none of the Venetian or Nazi history.
  • quarter / Jewish quarter: Similar as a historical urban district; differs as a neutral geographic term carrying no idea of coercion.
  • enclave: Similar as a distinct ethnic district; differs because enclaves (Chinatown, Little Italy) are often voluntary immigrant clusters, whereas a ghetto is imposed and stigmatised.
  • barrio: Similar as a US Spanish-speaking segregated or poor neighbourhood; differs in ethnicity and in the community's own largely positive identification with the term.
  • projects / housing project: Similar as high-poverty, minority-concentrated public housing; differs as a policy institution of the welfare state rather than a historic walled quarter.
  • concentration camp: Similar as a Nazi-era instrument of confinement; differs radically in degree — the camp was lethal imprisonment, the ghetto a walled city district used for exploitation and, later, mass murder.
  • ghettoisation (abstract noun): Similar as the derived process word; differs as a process, not a place — the making of ghettos rather than the districts themselves.
  • hood / 'hood: Similar as slang for the neighbourhood; differs as in-group American slang, warm and reclaimed, whereas ghetto retains its official and segregationist history.
