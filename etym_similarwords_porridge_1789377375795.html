• Oatmeal: Similar because both refer to boiled oat preparations eaten hot for breakfast; differs because oatmeal specifies the raw milled grain meal itself or an American preparation thereof, whereas porridge is a broader dish category that can be made from oats, barley, rye, or other grains in various culinary traditions.
  • Pottage: Similar because pottage is the direct historical ancestor of porridge; differs because pottage refers specifically to a medieval thick savory soup or stew made of vegetables, pot-herbs, and meat cooked in a pot, whereas porridge has specialized into a boiled cereal grain dish.
  • Gruel: Similar because both are made by boiling crushed grains in water or milk; differs because gruel is a very thin, watery, diluted, and low-calorie concoction historically associated with poverty, famine, and harsh workhouses, whereas porridge is thick, hearty, substantial, and creamy.
  • Congee: Similar because both are thick, soothing boiled grain dishes; differs because congee is specifically an East and Southeast Asian preparation made from white or brown rice boiled for hours with copious water until the grains disintegrate, typically seasoned with savory aromatics like ginger, scallions, and soy sauce.
  • Polenta: Similar because both are thick boiled grain mushes; differs because polenta is of Italian origin and made specifically from yellow or white coarsely ground cornmeal (maize), often cooled into a solid block, sliced, and grilled or baked with cheese.
  • Mush: Similar because both are thick porridges made of boiled meal; differs because mush is predominantly an American frontier term for boiled cornmeal, often regarded as coarse, plain, or peasant fare, and lacking the specialized Scottish culinary prestige of oatmeal porridge.
  • Grits: Similar because both are warm grain breakfasts; differs because grits is an American Southern specialty prepared specifically from coarsely ground alkali-treated hominy or dried dent corn, typically served savory with melted butter, cheese, and black pepper.
  • Stew: Similar because both are simmered dishes cooked in a pot; differs because a stew is composed primarily of solid chunks of meat and vegetables cooked slowly in a rich savory gravy, whereas porridge is a homogeneous, starch-gelatinized grain cereal.
