• Anew: Similar because both mean “once more, in a new way”; differs because anew is close in register but more literary and bookish, with less everyday use.
  • Again: Similar because both mean “one more time”; differs because again is the general word for mere repetition, without afresh’s insistence on a new or clean state.
  • Once more: Similar because both mean “a further repetition of an event”; differs because once more stresses the recurrence itself, not the freshness of the new arrangement.
  • From scratch: Similar because both mean “to start over”; differs because from scratch is informal and implies building with no remaining base, whereas afresh simply means starting anew whatever exists.
  • Over: Similar because both can mean “again” (“start over”); differs because over is informal and process-focused, without afresh’s slightly elevated, literary tone.
  • De novo: Similar because both mean “anew, from the beginning”; differs because de novo is a Latinate technical or legal borrowing (“a trial de novo”), while afresh is a native English adverb of ordinary prose.
