• Pablum: Similar because both are soft infant food and both metaphorically mean bland, undemanding content; differs because 'pablum' is a 20th-century trademark (a Canadian baby cereal) whose figurative sense is thoroughly contemptuous, whereas 'pap' keeps a genuinely affectionate literal use.
  • Gruel: Similar because both are thin, semi-liquid food for the sick or weak; differs because gruel is water-based and strained, and has never carried the 'paps = breast' anatomical sense.
  • Porridge: Similar because both are soft cooked cereals; differs because porridge (especially oatmeal) is a hearty adult staple, while pap is specifically infant or invalid food.
  • Mush: Similar because both are soft pulpy foods and both figuratively mean mawkish or soft-headed material; differs because mush is cornmeal-based and American-rustic, while pap is the European bread-and-milk nursery food.
  • Mash: Similar because both describe softened, pulped food; differs because mash keeps the physical process of crushing (mash the potatoes) at its centre and lacks the feeding/breast associations.
  • Baby food: Similar because both are commercially infant weaning foods; differs because 'baby food' is the modern, neutral, commercial term while 'pap' is archaic, homemade, and now mostly metaphorical.
  • Slop: Similar because both name soft semi-liquid food; differs because 'slop' is the food of pigs or prisoners — disgusting swill — where 'pap' is nourishing but infantile.
  • Bland: Similar because both describe the tasteless, undemanding quality of pap's figurative sense; differs because 'bland' is an adjective for personality, food or style, while 'pap' is a noun naming the substance itself.
  • Tripe: Similar because both are contemptuous nouns for worthless writing or talk; differs because 'tripe' (literally the stomach of a ruminant) means foul offal and hence rubbish, where 'pap' implies softness and infancy rather than filth.
  • Drivel: Similar because both condemn trivial, foolish speech or writing; differs because 'drivel' emphasises meaningless chatter (drooling), while 'pap' emphasises the undemanding softness that requires no thought.
  • Twaddle: Similar because both name silly, empty talk; differs because 'twaddle' is pure nonsense talk, whereas 'pap' is bland, non-nutritious content fed to a passive public.
  • Rubbish / Bunkum: Similar as dismissive nouns for worthless material; differs because they condemn falsehood or waste, while 'pap' specifically condemns the soft and the undemanding.
  • Fluff: Similar because both name trivial, insubstantial entertainment; differs because 'fluff' is light and harmless froth, while 'pap' retains the nourishing-food irony.
  • Milk: Similar because both are the natural first foods of infancy, connected to the 'paps = breast' root; differs because 'milk' is literal mother's food with endless nutritious extensions, while 'pap' is its cooked, processed, and eventually contemptuous cousin.
  • Pabulum: Similar because both descend from the Latin/nursery word for food and both mean bland intellectual fare; differs because 'pabulum' is the rarer, more pretentious form (Latin pabulum, 'fodder, food').
  • Paps: Similar because the plural shares the root; differs because 'paps' is the archaic word for breasts, not food.
