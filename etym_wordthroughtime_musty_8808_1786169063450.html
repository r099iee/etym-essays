- 14th century: English takes moist from Old French moiste, "damp," and builds moisty, "damp, wet"
- Early 15th century: moisty drifts toward "damp and beginning to decay" — dampness turning into staleness
- 15th–16th century: under the sound-influence of Old French moisi, "moldy," moisty is reshaped into musty, and the sweet-sour smell of damp decay settles into the word
- 16th century: a settled adjective for the moldy, musty smell of long-closed places — old cloth, old leather, old rooms
- 17th century: figurative life — musty ideas, musty books, musty customs, things stale and kept too long in the dark
- Modern: the smell of time itself — a musty portfolio that has waited for decades in a damp tower, the secret age of sealed and forgotten places
