• Teem: Similar because both describe being abundantly filled or overflowing with living or active things; differs because teem emphasizes a serene, fertile, or harmonious fullness and copiousness (e.g., waters teeming with fish), whereas pullulate emphasizes rapid, energetic, chaotic germination, swarming proliferation, or pestilential multiplication.
  • Swarm: Similar because both convey dense, moving crowds or multitudes in motion; differs because swarm evokes a cohesive, kinetic cloud of flying or crawling insects moving together in flight or aggression, whereas pullulate emphasizes the actual sprouting, breeding, and erupting generation of the multitude from within a host environment.
  • Proliferate: Similar because both describe rapid, exponential multiplication or expansion; differs because proliferate is a clinical, neutral, or scientific Latinate term (e.g., cancer cells proliferate or nuclear weapons proliferate), lacking the visceral, organic, writhing, and slightly chaotic imagery inherent in pullulate.
  • Sprout: Similar because both trace their etymological origins to plant shoots bursting forth from seed or stem; differs because sprout is a simple, gentle, everyday Anglo-Saxon term focusing on individual seedlings emerging from soil, whereas pullulate carries an intense, Latinate literary weight denoting massive, collective, and overwhelming germination.
  • Abound: Similar because both indicate the presence of great numbers or vast quantities; differs because abound is a static, descriptive condition of plenty (e.g., wildflowers abound in the valley), whereas pullulate is an active, dynamic, bubbling process of birth, surge, and frantic multiplication.
  • Infest: Similar because both can describe an overwhelming invasion of unwanted elements, pests, or errors; differs because infest focuses purely on hostile, damaging parasitism and destruction (e.g., termites infesting a house), whereas pullulate describes the exuberant, self-generating fecundity of the entities, whether biological, creative, or defective.
