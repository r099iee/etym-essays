Old English Concrete Path - Wayes (from Old English weg) begins as a physical path or road, the ordinary word for a route through the landscape, and its plural simply names multiple routes.
Figurative Broadening - Already in Old English way extends from road to "course of life" and "manner of doing things," so the plural wayes comes to mean customs, methods, and dealings as well as physical paths.
Early Modern Providential Sense - In 17th-century English the plural form wayes (spelled with -es) is used by Milton of God's providential dealings — "the wayes of God" as the designs and methods of providence — a dignified, moral sense of the word.
Contemporary Sense - Modern ways keeps the full range — roads, routes, manners, methods — in countless phrases (ways and means, by the way, in many ways), while the -es spelling survives only as an archaic echo of early modern orthography.
