Note on morphological evolution and grammatical number: In classical Latin, the noun rēs was an ancient fifth-declension feminine substantive of extraordinary polysemy (giving rise to rēs pūblica "the public thing/commonwealth"). While classical authors used adjectives like vērus ("true") or certus ("sure"), the derivative adjective reālis was specifically coined by late Roman and medieval jurists and scholastic schoolmen to distinguish physical property and things (*rēs*) from personal rights (*personae*). In modern English, realia functions grammatically as a plural noun (construed with plural verbs, though occasionally treated as a collective mass noun in pedagogical jargon), directly mirroring its Latin neuter plural inflection.
