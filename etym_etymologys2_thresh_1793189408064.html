PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*terh₂-
‘to rub, turn, bore’
extended sense ‘to thresh’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*þreskaną
‘to thresh, beat grain’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
þerscan
‘to thresh, beat, strike’
↓
Middle English (13th–14th c.)
threshshen, threshen
‘to thresh grain’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
thresh, thrash
‘to beat grain; to beat’
↓
Modern English (current)
thresh
‘to beat grain from husks’

Note: the deeper PIE connection is debated: handbooks link Germanic *þreskaną with Latin terere (‘to rub, thresh’) and Greek tribein (‘to rub’) under *terh₂-, but the Germanic -sk- extension is unexplained, so some scholars leave the word without a certain deeper root. The thresh/thrash split is a native English vowel variation, not two separate roots.
