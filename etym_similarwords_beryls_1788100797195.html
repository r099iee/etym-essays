• Emeralds: Similar because emeralds ARE beryl — the green crowning variety; differs because the word names only chromium-green beryl of gem quality, far rarer and costlier than "beryl" in general.
  • Aquamarines: Similar because sea-blue beryl; differs because "beryl" unqualified means the species or the antique greenish stone, while aquamarine is a specific blue variety.
  • Morganites: Similar because pink beryl; differs because a refined 20th-century variety (named for J. P. Morgan), not an antique word.
  • Heliodors: Similar because golden beryl; differs by hue and market name alone.
  • Chrysoberyl: Similar because a beryllium gem with a near-identical name, home of the true cat's-eye (cymophane) and alexandrite; differs because it is a different mineral (beryllium aluminate), harder at 8.5 and chemically unrelated.
  • Sapphires / rubies: Similar because premier coloured gemstones of comparable hardness; differs because they are corundum (aluminium oxide), not beryllium silicates.
  • Topaz: Similar because another hard transparent gem, yellow to blue; differs because it is an aluminium fluorosilicate, softer (8), and outside the emerald lineage.
  • Quartz: Similar because a common transparent gem mineral (amethyst, citrine, rock crystal); differs because it is far softer (7), pure silica, and cheap by comparison.
  • Gems / jewels / stones: Similar because the generic trade words for cut precious material; differs because they are categories, while beryl is one named species.
