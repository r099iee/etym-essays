• Simmering: Similar because both describe gentle cooking below the boil; differs because simmering is strictly culinary and lower-heat, while stewing adds the figurative sense of brooding agitation.
  • Boiling: Similar because both involve heating in liquid; differs because boiling implies a much higher temperature and, figuratively, open and explosive anger, whereas stewing suggests slow, contained internal heat.
  • Brooding: Similar because both describe gloomy, inward rumination; differs because brooding is purely psychological and implies prolonged sorrow or melancholy, while stewing suggests simmering anger or worry and keeps its cooking metaphor.
  • Fretting: Similar because both describe anxious worrying; differs because fretting is nervous and restless, often outwardly expressed, while stewing is slower, hotter, and more internalised.
  • Braising: Similar because both involve slow cooking in liquid; differs because braising is a specific culinary technique that first sears food then cooks it in a covered pot, lacking the figurative senses of stewing.
  • Seething: Similar because both describe hidden, building anger; differs because seething implies a barely contained rage about to boil over, stronger and more explosive than the gradual churn of stewing.
