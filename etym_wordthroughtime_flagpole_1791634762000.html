- 16th to 18th c. — English sailors and armies exclusively use 'flagstaff'; flags are flown from ship sterns and military forts, establishing visual recognition across distances.
- Early 19th c. (c. 1826) — American English coins 'flag-pole' as raw timber poles and 'liberty poles' are raised across frontier towns, schools, and courthouse squares.
- Late 19th c. — 'Flagpole' eclipses 'flagstaff' in North American everyday speech as schools mandate daily morning flag-raising ceremonies and patriotic culture surges.
- 1920s — 'Flagpole sitting' sweeps the United States as a spectacular eccentric fad, with daredevils spending weeks atop commercial poles for media fame.
- 1950s — Madison Avenue advertising jargon gives birth to the famous corporate idiom 'run it up the flagpole and see who salutes', meaning to test an idea on an audience.
- Late 20th to 21st c. — Universal global term for freestanding civic and commercial flag structures, as well as an athletic term ('human flagpole') in extreme calisthenics.
