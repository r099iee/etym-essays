Single linear derivation chain for BLAZE (one inherited Germanic root; the "white facial mark" and "trail marker" senses are later historical branches of the same etymon):
1. PIE *bhel- — "to shine, flash, burn, glow white" (reconstructed; c. 4500–3500 BCE; per Pokorny and Etymonline; also the base of *bhleg-, source of Latin flagrāre, Greek phlégō, and English flame, flagrant, conflagration).
2. Proto-Germanic *blas- — "white, shining; a white mark; a torch" (reconstructed; c. 500 BCE – 200 CE; cf. Old Saxon blas "white", Middle High German blas "pale, bald").
3. Old English blæse — "torch, flame, brand" (attested c. 1000–1100 CE, e.g. in Byrhtferth's glosses).
4. Middle English blase — "flame, fire, torch" (attested from the early 13th c.; the verb blasen "to burn brightly, flare" is attested from the same era).
5. Modern English blaze — "a fiercely burning fire; a bright flame; brilliant light" (from the 14th c. onward). The white facial-stripe sense is attested from the early 17th c., the bark-mark sense from the 1610s ("to blaze trees"), and the figurative "blaze a trail" from the 19th c.
Notes on debated / nuanced stages: (a) The link of Germanic *blas- to PIE *bhel- is the traditional assignment but not certain in every detail; the OED conservatively derives blaze from a Germanic base *blas- "bright, white" and does not insist on the deeper PIE root. (b) The homonymous verb blaze "to blow a trumpet, to proclaim" is a separate etymon connected with PIE *bhlē- "to blow" (cf. blazon) and lies outside this chain.
