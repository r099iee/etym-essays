Note: The nominal base caliga (the heavy, open-work leather footwear studded with iron hobnails worn by Roman common soldiers and centurions) is traditionally traced by historical linguists (such as Ernout-Meillet and de Vaan) to the PIE root *kel- ("to cover, protect"), cognate with Latin celare ("to hide") and Germanic words like hall and hell. The feminine hypocoristic diminutive suffix -ula was added playfully by the soldiers of Germanicus on the Rhine frontier, producing caligula ("little bootie"). The transition from an endearing camp pet-name to one of history's most terrifying personal names constitutes one of the most striking ironic inversions in Indo-European onomastics.
