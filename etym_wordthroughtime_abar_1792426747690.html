- [Antiquity & Epic Age: c. 1st millennium BCE] — Emerged in the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* (Araṇyakāṇḍa) as an elderly forest ascetic (*śramaṇī*) of the indigenous Śabara tribe, honored by Sage Mataṅga for her immaculate virtue and rewarded by achieving heavenly liberation upon witnessing Rāma — [perception: venerable indigenous forest ascetic honored for exemplary austerity and dharmic righteousness]
- [Classical Purāṇic Era: c. 4th–10th c. CE] — Maintained in Purāṇic retellings (*Padma Purāṇa*, *Agni Purāṇa*) as an exemplary model of forest hermitage hospitality and the universality of divine grace across all regions of the subcontinent — [perception: holy forest hermit whose hermitage sanctified the wild frontier lands of Kiṣkindhā]
- [Medieval Bhakti Epoch: c. 12th–17th c. CE] — Dramatically reimagined in vernacular bhakti literature (Tulsīdās’s *Rāmacaritamānasa*, Nābhādās’s *Bhaktamāla*), introducing the iconic trope of tasting forest berries (*ber*) to ensure their sweetness for Rāma, thereby dismantling orthodox caste and purity taboos — [perception: ultimate spiritual heroine of casteless, uncalculating, pure loving surrender (*prema-bhakti*)]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era: 19th–21st c.] — Celebrated in subaltern studies, folklore, temple worship (including the Sabarimala pilgrimage traditions of Kerala), and modern literature as an enduring emblem of indigenous dignity, maternal love, and the egalitarian spirit of faith — [perception: timeless cultural icon of humble grace, subaltern empowerment, and boundless maternal devotion]
