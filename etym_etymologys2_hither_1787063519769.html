PIE
(prehistoric)
*ke- / *ki-
"this, here (near-speaker deictic)"
↓
Proto-Germanic
(c. 1st millennium BCE)
*hidrē
"to this place, toward here"
(directive suffix *-dr- + adverb *-ē)
↓
Old English
(c. 8th–9th c.)
hider
"to or toward this place"
↓
Middle English
(12th–15th c.)
hider ~ hithir ~ hyther
"to this place" (final -th- appears
by analogy with thither, whither)
↓
Modern English
(present)
HITHER
"to or toward this place"
(now archaic or literary)

Note: the -th- spelling became the norm only in the 16th c. under the influence of thither and whither; the conservative form hider survives in northern dialects.
