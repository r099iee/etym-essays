- Before the pot (13th–18th c.) — the word chimney supplies the stack, but open hearths and simple flues need no terminal pipe; the compound does not yet exist.
- The pot arrives (early 19th c.) — dense urban terraces and small flues create downdraught and smoke problems; the Staffordshire potteries begin mass-producing earthenware chimney-pots, and the compound chimney-pot is coined.
- The joke of the century (1840s) — a tall silk top hat is nicknamed a “chimney-pot”; cartoons, songs and music-hall gags link gentleman, smoke and pipe in a single silhouette.
- Victorian ornament (mid–late 19th c.) — pots become showpieces: twisted, pierced, castellated cylinders advertise the householder's taste; the roofline becomes a skyline of status.
- Functional twilight (20th c.) — central heating and gas flues shrink the chimney's role; pots persist as safety, conservation and heritage items, and the word turns technical.
- Heritage and memory (21st c.) — listed-building restoration reinstates Victorian pots; the word now carries nostalgic period flavour, its compound structure — chimney + pot — still perfectly transparent.
