• Delimitation: Similar because both involve determining boundaries; differs because in international diplomacy and cartography, delimitation is the diplomatic and cartographic phase of describing and drawing borders on a map, whereas demarcation is the physical marking of those borders on the ground with posts and markers or the rigid conceptual separation of domains.
  • Boundary: Similar because both refer to dividing limits; differs because a boundary is the static limit or border itself, whereas demarcation emphasizes the active process, act, or system of establishing, marking, and maintaining that boundary.
  • Differentiation: Similar because both involve recognizing distinctions between categories; differs because differentiation is a broad cognitive or developmental process of becoming distinct or observing subtle internal differences, while demarcation involves drawing sharp, rigid, and definitive dividing lines.
  • Distinction: Similar because both denote a separation of ideas; differs because a distinction is an intellectual recognition of differences in quality, honor, or meaning, whereas demarcation implies a strict border or exclusionary jurisdictional barrier.
  • Separation: Similar because both involve keeping things apart; differs because separation can be physical distance, emotional estrangement, or mechanical isolation, lacking the specific boundary-marking and jurisdictional connotations of demarcation.
  • Partition: Similar because both involve dividing territory or space; differs because a partition refers to the physical division or fragmentation of a single entity into separate political or physical units (e.g., the Partition of India, a room partition), whereas demarcation focuses on defining the borderline between them.
