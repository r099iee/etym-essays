• Moss: Similar because both are small, low-growing, non-vascular or primitive cryptogamic organisms forming greenish carpets on damp trees, rocks, and soil; differs because moss is a true primitive land plant (bryophyte) with distinct microscopic leaves, stems, and chlorophyll-containing cells, whereas lichen is a composite symbiotic association between a fungus and an alga or cyanobacterium.
  • Mold: Similar because both involve fungal growth forms that colonize surfaces in damp or decaying environments; differs because mold consists of fuzzy, filamented hyphal networks thriving heterotrophically on organic foodstuffs or damp structural materials, lacking symbiotic photosynthetic algae.
  • Fungus: Similar because the primary structural and nutritional body of lichen is composed of fungal mycelium (the mycobiont); differs because fungus is the broad biological kingdom containing mushrooms, yeasts, and molds that obtain nutrients strictly through absorption without obligatory internal algal symbiosis.
  • Algae: Similar because algae comprise the photobiont component of many lichens, providing photosynthetic energy; differs because free-living algae are simple photosynthetic organisms primarily inhabiting aquatic or very wet terrestrial environments without fungal encapsulation.
  • Epiphyte: Similar because both refer to organisms that grow upon trees, rocks, or other plants non-parasitically for physical support; differs because epiphyte is a broad ecological classification including vascular plants such as orchids and bromeliads, whereas lichen is a specific taxonomic composite life form.
  • Patina: Similar because both form a superficial, encrusting layer that accumulates slowly over long periods on weathered surfaces, imparting an antique aesthetic; differs because patina is typically a chemical oxidation layer on metals (such as verdigris on bronze) or a sheen on polished surfaces, rather than a living biological organism.
