- Late-antique Christian writers - Alaric appears as the instrument of catastrophe: the Visigoth who sacked Rome in August 410, an event contemporaries took as proof the world was ending, so his name began as a synonym for annihilating barbarism.
- Post-conversion historiography - the same writers recorded that the Arian Alaric spared the churches of Peter and Paul and returned the sacred vessels, and later historians (Macaulay among them) provocatively ranked him among the zealous Christians, the destroyer who nevertheless acknowledged Rome's God.
- 19th-century romanticism and after - Alaric became the archetypal figure of the barbarian conqueror and the fall of the ancient world, his sack of Rome a fixed landmark, and the name now denotes the moment Rome was first taken in eight hundred years.
