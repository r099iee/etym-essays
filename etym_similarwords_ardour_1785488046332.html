• enthusiasm: Similar because both name eager, lively interest; differs because enthusiasm is modern, social and comparatively light, drained of mystery since the eighteenth century, while ardour keeps a fiery, burning intensity.
  • zeal: Similar because both name intense committed devotion; differs because zeal implies partisan, religious or combative intensity with a hint of excess (zealot), whereas ardour burns without the fanatic’s agenda.
  • fervour: Similar because both name glowing warmth of feeling; differs because fervour leans toward religious devotion, closer to prayer than to argument, while ardour reaches beyond worship into any sustained passion.
  • passion: Similar because both name strong feeling; differs because passion is far broader, covering love, anger and suffering and often turbulent, while ardour is steady, consuming commitment rather than a burst of emotion.
  • eagerness: Similar because both name a desire to act; differs because eagerness is restless, impatient wanting with no sense of depth or burning intensity.
  • vehemence: Similar because both name forceful energy; differs because vehemence stresses violent or angry force, while ardour is a warmer, devoted fire.
  • devotion: Similar because both name committed dedication; differs because devotion is quieter, service-like and may lack ardour’s heat and glamour.
  • intensity: Similar because both name the degree of feeling; differs because intensity is abstract and descriptive, naming strength without ardour’s metaphor of fire.
  • warmth: Similar because both suggest emotion with heat; differs because warmth is gentle and friendly, far below ardour’s consuming burn.
  • fire: Similar because both draw on the metaphor of burning; differs because fire as a noun for emotion is figurative and colloquial, while ardour is a settled, formal term.
  • verve: Similar because both name lively energy; differs because verve is sparkling, social and stylistic (a performance full of verve), while ardour is a deeper, sustained internal fire.
