• Jerked: Similar because both mean a sudden movement; differs because a jerk is harder and more violent, a sharp pull of the whole body or limb, too gross for the fine tail-tip motion Potter describes.
  • Quivered: Similar because both describe trembling motion; differs because a quiver is fine and continuous, lingering, while a twitch is a single discrete flicker over almost as soon as it begins.
  • Flicked: Similar because both describe a quick motion of the tail; differs because a flick implies deliberation and control, while Potter's tail-tip moves "as if it were alive," of its own accord.
  • Convulsed: Similar because both are involuntary contractions; differs because convulse is clinical, violent, and whole-body — catastrophic rather than the delicate nonchalance of a cat's tail.
  • Spasmed: Similar because both are involuntary muscular contractions; differs because a spasm is a sustained, often painful contraction of a muscle group, far too violent and medical for a small flicker.
  • Trembled: Similar because both are involuntary small movements; differs because trembling is a fine, continuous shaking of the whole body or limb, not a single quick flick of a tail-tip.
  • Shuddered: Similar because both are involuntary responses; differs because a shudder is a whole-body response to cold or fear, slow and rippling, not a tiny localized twitch.
  • Fluttered: Similar because both describe quick light movements; differs because flutter suggests rapid repeated waving (like a bird's wings or an eyelid), while a twitch is one brief jerk.
  • Tic: Similar because both denote an involuntary nervous movement; differs because a tic is a habitual, repetitive spasm of a muscle or voice, often clinical, while a twitch is a single spontaneous flicker.
