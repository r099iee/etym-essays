Single root word; one linear chain: PIE *splend- "to be manifest, to shine, gleam" (reconstructed; unattested; the exact shape is disputed in handbooks, also written *splnd- or *spel-) → Latin splendēre "to shine, be bright, glitter" (attested 3rd–2nd century BCE) → Latin resplendēre "to shine back, glow in return, be resplendent" (re- "back, again" + splendēre "to shine"; attested by the Classical period) → Late Latin resplendēns, stem resplendent- "shining back, brilliant" (present participle) → Old French resplendir "to shine brightly" (whence French resplendir, resplendissant) → English resplendent "shining, brightly colored, gorgeously ornamented" (borrowed c. 1480, directly from the Latin participial stem) → Modern English resplendent. DEBATED POINTS: - The PIE root is reconstructed with some uncertainty: handbooks give *splend-, *splnd-, and *spel-, and the exact shape is not agreed. A Sanskrit equation with sphul- "to throb, quiver, burst forth, sparkle" (whence classical sphuliṅga "a spark," and Hindi sphulinga "a spark") is often cited as a sister reflex, but the fit of the initial cluster and semantics, while close, is debated. - No securely verified Germanic cognate is recorded; the root is chiefly confined to the Latin and Greek material, which is itself an argument that the word entered English as a learned Latinism rather than through Germanic inheritance.
