• Vagueness: Similar because both describe meaning that is not clearly fixed; differs because vagueness is a deficit of information with blurred boundaries and no rival precise readings, whereas ambiguity offers two or more genuinely defensible interpretations.
  • Polysemy: Similar because both involve a word with multiple meanings; differs because polysemy is the neutral dictionary fact of several settled, distinct senses (e.g. "bank"), usually resolvable by context, whereas ambiguity is a live uncertainty in a particular utterance.
  • Equivocation: Similar because both involve double meaning; differs because equivocation is the deliberate, often deceptive use of ambiguity to mislead, implying bad faith, whereas ambiguity itself is morally neutral and may be unintended.
  • Double entendre: Similar because both hinge on a phrase with two readings; differs because a double entendre is a narrower, usually humorous and suggestive case built on a single phrase, whereas ambiguity can span whole texts and serious poetry.
  • Ambivalence: Similar because both describe being pulled in two directions; differs because ambivalence is a psychological state of mixed feelings about a person or thing, whereas ambiguity is a property of language or meaning.
  • Obscurity: Similar because both make meaning hard to grasp; differs because obscurity is simple difficulty of understanding, often from darkness or complexity, without the specific structure of two rival readings.
  • Uncertainty: Similar because both involve a lack of fixity; differs because uncertainty is a general state of doubt or unknown outcome, whereas ambiguity is specifically about multiple available meanings.
  • Nuance: Similar because both concern subtle shades of meaning; differs because nuance is a fine distinction or gradation to be appreciated, whereas ambiguity is a genuine fork in interpretation requiring resolution.
  • Amphibology: Similar because both name the double meaning of a sentence or phrase; differs because amphibology is a rare, strictly grammatical term for structural ambiguity (the double parse), while ambiguity is the broad general term.
