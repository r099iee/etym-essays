RECOMMENCED is the past tense and past participle of the verb "to recommence," meaning "to begin again" — to start afresh an action, process or undertaking that had been interrupted or that has come to an end. The word is transparently built from the prefix re- "again" + commence "to begin." In the scene from David Hawkes's translation of Cao Xueqin's Hong lou meng (The Story of the Stone), the verb carries the quiet formality of a narrator observing two young people: "'That means that you should get on well together.' Bao-yu moved over again and, drawing a chair up beside Dai-yu, recommenced his scrutiny." The tone is measured, deliberate, slightly ceremonious — Hawkes's narrator reports Bao-yu's movements in the stately rhythm of the novel's social dance: move over, draw up a chair, recommence. The word signals a formal restart: Bao-yu has been scrutinizing Dai-yu, his attention was interrupted by the conversation, and now he deliberately takes up the looking again, as though it were an undertaking. The Latinate dignity of recommence — much grander than "looked again" — quietly reveals that for Bao-yu this scrutiny is not casual glancing but an act of serious, sustained attention; the formality of the verb is the formality of a boy who is, in his way, ceremonious even in his curiosity, and the comedy of the small gesture performed with such gravity is part of the scene's charm.

The senses of RECOMMENCED, primary and secondary, are these:
  • Core sense: began again after an interruption — the action is started afresh from its initial point, not merely continued mid-course; "the rain recommenced," "the ceremony recommenced."
  • Formal or ceremonial sense: a solemn re-beginning of a proceeding, undertaking or ritual — the sense governing the Hong lou meng sentence, where Bao-yu "recommences" his scrutiny as a deliberate act.
  • Legal and procedural sense: to recommence proceedings, an action, a trial or a debate — the word is at home in law and officialdom, where a process formally stopped is formally begun again.
  • Technical and natural sense: of weather, sounds or processes resuming after a pause — "the rain recommenced with greater intensity," "the noise recommenced" — where the word adds a measured, almost anthropomorphic dignity to natural events.
  • Sports and games: play recommences after a stoppage — a formal restart of a match after the rules or the referee call it to begin again.

The semantic bridge from the ancient to the modern senses is the Latin verb's metaphor of beginning as entering. "Commence" descends from Latin inīre "to go in, to enter upon" and its noun initium "a going in; a beginning" — the Romans conceived of beginning as stepping onto a road — and the prefix re- adds "again," so "recommence" is literally "to enter upon again," to step back onto the road one had left. The word therefore carries a built-in image of an undertaking: recommencing is not passively resuming but deliberately re-commencing, taking up the road once more as an act of will. It is this active, ceremonious quality that distinguishes it from the more neutral "resumed" and that the Hong lou meng sentence exploits — Bao-yu's scrutiny is recommenced as a deliberate undertaking, not merely continued.

Register boundaries against the near-synonyms sharpen the word:
  • "Resumed" is the closest and the most neutral: it means to continue from where one left off, emphasising continuity with what went before, whereas recommence emphasises a fresh beginning — the action is started again from its initial point, not merely picked up mid-course.
  • "Restarted" is modern and mechanical, at home with engines and computers, and would deflate the scene.
  • "Renewed" adds a gust of fresh energy or emotion — renewed interest, renewed attack — and so overstates what a steady, patient scrutiny needs.
  • "Began again" and "took up again" are the plain spoken alternatives; they are correct but surrender the formality that the context wants, and "took up again" drags in a phrasal-verb casualness quite alien to the narrator's register.
  • "Reopened" is reserved for cases, inquiries and wounds.

In short, RECOMMENCED is the ceremonious word for beginning again: an action solemnly undertaken afresh, with the full Latinate dignity of an entering-upon, which is exactly the gravity Hawkes gives to Bao-yu drawing up his chair.
