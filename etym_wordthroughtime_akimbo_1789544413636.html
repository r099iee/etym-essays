- 15th Century - [Middle English Crooked Bow] - First recorded as *in kenebowe* / *a kenebowe*, meaning positioned in a sharp bend or hook like a drawn bow.
- 16th–17th Century - [Elizabethan Posture of Insolence] - Transitioned into *a-kimbo* / *akimbo*, establishing its specialized role in drama to depict blustering soldiers, haughty masters, and combative commoners.
- 18th–19th Century - [Victorian Literary Shorthand for Defiance] - Widely employed by novelists (Dickens, Thackeray, Doyle) as the definitive visual indicator of an obstinate tradesman or resolute authority figure.
- 20th–21st Century - [Classic Body Language Descriptor] - Preserved across modern English as the standard, indispensable anatomical term for the hands-on-hips, elbows-out posture of assertiveness.
