Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*rebʰ- / onomatopoeic echoic root
"to make a rumbling sound, chatter noisily, babble"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*rabb-
"to make rapid noise, babble"
↓
Middle Low German / Middle Dutch (c. 13th c.)
rabbelen / rabbeln
"to chatter, jabber, speak quickly and confusedly"
(frequentative formation in -elen)
↓
Late Middle English (c. 1380)
rablen (verb) / rabble (noun)
"to babble, speak hastily" → "a noisy pack, clamorous troop"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550)
rabble
"a disorderly crowd, mob, the common populace (noun)"
Note: Borrowed from North Sea Germanic (Middle Dutch/Low German) into Middle English; frequentative *-le* applied to acoustic echoic root *rabb-*.
