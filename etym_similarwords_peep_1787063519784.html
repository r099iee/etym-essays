• peek: Similar because it also means a quick, sly, often furtive look; differs because peek never means 'to emerge into view' and has no sound sense at all.
  • peer: Similar because it describes a careful act of looking; differs because peer is a sustained effort to see something indistinct, while peep is a brief glance through an opening.
  • glance: Similar because both name a quick, short look; differs because glance is neutral and casual, with no secrecy, while peep usually involves concealment or a gap.
  • glimpse: Similar because both involve seeing only a little of something; differs because glimpse is a noun/verb of catching sight of a thing, not of peering out yourself.
  • spy: Similar because it can mean watching secretly; differs because spy implies sustained surveillance or espionage, much darker and more deliberate.
  • appear / emerge: Similar because they share the 'come into view' sense; differ because they are neutral, large-scale verbs, while peep stresses that the appearance is partial, gradual, and delicate.
  • cheep: Similar because it names the thin cry of a young bird, matching the imitative sound sense; differs because cheep has no visual 'look' meaning at all.
