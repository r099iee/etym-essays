• Damask: Similar because both are figured luxury fabrics woven with patterns; differs because damask is woven in a single colour with reversible satin-and-sateen contrasts, whereas brocade adds extra floating weft threads to build a raised, non-reversible, embossed pattern.
  • Velvet: Similar because both are richly textured luxury fabrics with a raised surface; differs because velvet's raised surface is a uniform pile with no pattern, while brocade offers pattern without a pile.
  • Embroidery: Similar because both produce raised decorative figures on cloth; differs because embroidery applies the design with a needle after the cloth is woven, whereas brocade builds the pattern into the very structure of the weave at the loom.
  • Tapestry: Similar because both are ornamental woven textiles with pictorial designs; differs because tapestry is a weft-faced wall-hanging or furnishing woven for narrative pictures, while brocade is a figured dress and furnishing fabric with raised supplementary threads.
  • Samite: Similar because both are heavy, rich silks of the medieval luxury trade; differs because samite is a dense twill or satin-weave silk, often plain or with simple gilt thread, without brocade's raised supplementary-weft figures.
  • Lampas: Similar because both use supplementary wefts to figure the cloth; differs because lampas is a compound weave with a ground and a patterning weft that forms a figured, usually flat pattern, whereas brocade floats its extra threads free to create a raised, embossed surface.
