Human sense — the rogue or fool:
Middle Scots (15th c.)
loun
‘idle fellow, rogue, lad’
↓
Scots and Northern English (18th–19th c.)
loon, loun
‘lout, simpleton, fellow’
↓
Modern English (19th–20th c.)
loon
‘foolish or crazy person’
↓
Bird sense — the northern diver, in isolation:
Old Norse (medieval)
lómr
‘diving bird, loon’
↓
Early Modern English (1630s)
loom, loon
‘great northern diver’
↓
Modern English, American (current)
loon
‘diving waterbird, genus Gavia’

Note: the fool-word and the bird-word are independent homographs that converged in spelling; the modern crazy sense is further reinforced by sound-association with lunatic and loony, which are etymologically unrelated, coming from Latin luna, moon.
