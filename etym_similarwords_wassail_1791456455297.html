• Toast: shares the ritual of raising a cup with spoken good wishes; differs because a toast is the act or the words, not the spiced drink, and carries no seasonal or carol dimension.
  • Carouse: shares the communal, alcohol-heavy merrymaking; differs because carouse stresses noisy excess and late drinking, whereas wassail is a fixed seasonal custom with a prescribed drink.
  • Revel: similar in festive merrymaking; differs by being general and untethered to a bowl, a health-formula or Christmas.
  • Merrymaking: similar in festive atmosphere; differs as a broad, wholesome term with no alcoholic or ritual specificity.
  • Jollification: similar in convivial celebration; differs as a humorous, nineteenth-century-sounding word that names no drink.
  • Libation: similar in being a drink used in ritual; differs because a libation is poured out as an offering to a deity, not shared among revellers.
  • Mulled wine / glühwein: similar as a hot spiced festive drink; differs by base liquid and by lacking the English toast-and-carol history.
  • Cider / ale: the usual base liquids of wassail; they differ as ordinary drinks without the ritual and verbal layers.
  • Punch: similar as a communal bowl-drink; differs by being spirit-based and not tied to a health-cry or to Christmas.
  • Eggnog: similar as a rich Christmas drink; differs by its dairy and egg base and its American association.
  • Cheers / skol / prosit / slàinte: similar drinking salutations; they differ as bare interjections with no drink or custom attached.
  • Carol: similar in the Christmas house-visiting tradition; differs because a carol is a song, and only the wassailing carols overlap with the drink.
  • Hail: the archaic greeting embedded in the word; differs as a salutation pure and simple, with no drinking sense.
