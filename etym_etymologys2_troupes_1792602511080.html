Note on historical debate: The ultimate etymology of French troupe is a classic philological crux. The prevailing consensus among Romance etymologists (including Walther von Wartburg and the Trésor de la Langue Française) derives it from the Germanic Frankish superstrate *thorp / *trop ("village, agrarian crowd"), cognate with Old English þorp (Modern English thorpe, "hamlet") and German Dorf. A competing minority theory suggests a derivation from Vulgar Latin *troppus ("flock, herd"), possibly related to Classical Latin turba ("turmoil, crowd, throng"), though the phonetic transition from turba to troppus presents significant phonological irregularities that make the Germanic Frankish derivation far more compelling.
