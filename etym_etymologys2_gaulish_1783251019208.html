Gaulish is a compound English formation: Gaul + -ish. Two independent chains must be traced. Chain 1 — Gaul (via French from Germanic *Walhaz, ultimately from a Celtic tribal name): 1. Celtic tribal name *Volcae — the Gallic confederacy of southern Gaul (the Volcae Tectosages and Volcae Arecomici, attested in Latin records) — the people whom the Germanic tribes first met on the Rhine. 2. Proto-Germanic *Walhaz ‘foreigner; a non-Germanic speaker; esp. a Celt or Roman’ — the Germanic generalization of the tribal name Volcae into a word for ‘the other, the one who is not us’; attested through its descendants (Old English wealh ‘foreigner, Briton’, Old High German walha, Old Norse valr). 3. Frankish *Wālha ‘a Roman/Celtic-speaker, a Gaul’ — the West Germanic form that entered Gallo-Romance. 4. Old French Gaule ‘Gaul’ — the French form, with the characteristic hardening of Germanic w- to gu-; by extension the name of the land of the Gauls. 5. Middle English Gaule / French Gaule → English Gaul ‘Gaul; the ancient country of the Gauls’, attested from the 15th century. 6. (Root chain terminus for the element): English Gaul. Chain 2 — the suffix -ish (Germanic adjectival suffix): 1. Proto-Indo-European *-isko- ‘adjectival suffix of belonging’ (cf. Greek -iskos, Latin -iscus) — the ultimate source of the English suffix. 2. Proto-Germanic *-iskaz ‘adjectival suffix’ — inherited from the PIE form. 3. Old English -isc ‘adjectival suffix (of, belonging to)’ — the direct ancestor; used in Englisc ‘English’, Wēlisc ‘Welsh’, etc. 4. Middle English -ish — the suffix as it appears in Gaulish's formation period. 5. (Root chain terminus for the element): English -ish ‘of, belonging to, native of’. Compound: English Gaul + -ish → English Gaulish ‘of the Gauls; the Gaulish language’, attested from the early 17th century (the adjective), used thereafter as the name of the extinct Continental Celtic language of Gaul. Debated points: the connection of the Celtic tribal name *Volcae to the Germanic *Walhaz is secure in outline (the Germans met the Volcae, and the word generalized), but the exact source dialect and the precise phonetics of the w- to gu- change in Old French are not fully documented; the PIE source *-isko- for the suffix is standard but the intermediate derivations are reconstructed. The parallel Latin-based name — Gallia, Gallic — descends from Latin Gallus/Gallia, of uncertain Celtic origin, and is a separate chain from the English word Gaul.
