Futurists is the plural proper noun for adherents of Futurism — first and paradigmatically the Italian movement launched by Filippo Tommaso Marinetti's Manifesto of Futurism on the front page of Le Figaro (20 February 1909) — extended by analogy to Russian Futurists, English Vorticists touched by Futurism, and later Neo-Futurists. In the present passage the Italian Futurists are cited alongside the Dadaists as continuing Mallarmé's war on print, treating the alphabet as 26 objects for graphic elaboration.

Historically, Futurism was not merely a style but a total program: glorification of speed, the automobile, the aeroplane, electricity, youth, danger and war as “the sole hygiene of the world”; hatred of museums, libraries, academies and feminism (in Marinetti's initial masculinist formulation); free verse, parole in libertà (words in freedom), abolition of syntax, onomatopoeic noise-music, simultaneity and lines of force in painting, and above all typographic revolution — exploding the linear page into dynamic, multisized, diagonal, coloured letter-architecture.

  • 1. Core art-historical sense: Italian Futurists 1909-1944: Marinetti, Boccioni, Carrà, Russolo, Balla, Severini, Sant'Elia; painters of dynamism, sculptors of speed, composers of intonarumori noise, poets of wireless imagination; politically sliding from anarchist-syndicalist provocation to alliance with Fascism, a fate that permanently shadows the term.
  • 2. Russian Futurists (Hylaeans, Cubo-Futurists): Mayakovsky, Khlebnikov, Kruchenykh, Burliuk; authors of A Slap in the Face of Public Taste (1912), inventors of zaum transrational language, book-makers of lithographed samizdat artist-books; sharing typographic iconoclasm but with Slavic archaism and revolution, not Marinetti's nationalism.
  • 3. Typographic sense relevant here: makers of free-word tables, tavole parolibere, where letters vary in size, weight and orientation to paint sound and motion; reading letters as pure shape and sound rather than sense — exactly the book's claim. Marinetti's Zang Tumb Tumb (1914) is the locus classicus.
  • 4. Extended generic sense: any militant modernizer: in criticism, architecture, music and tech, futurist lowercased means visionary anticipator — a futurist architect, a futurist chef — losing the capital and the program, retaining only forward-looking novelty. Polysemy bridges via admiration for the movement's prophetic posture detached from its violence.
  • 5. Pejorative political sense: especially after 1945, Futurist can imply proto-fascist aestheticization of war and misogyny; art historians therefore distinguish aesthetic innovation from ideology, a tension internal to the term's modern reception.

Grammar and usage: always plural with capital F when denoting members (the Italian Futurists); singular Futurist as noun and adjective (Futurist typography, a young Futurist); distinct from futurist meaning futurologist, though dictionaries record overlap. Pronunciation in RP /ˈfjuːtʃərɪsts/, in Italian /futuˈristi/.

Literary and cultural resonance: the Futurists are the hinge between Symbolist Mallarmé and Dada — they literalize his scattered dice-throw into battlefield onomatopoeia, teach Dada and Constructivism how to shout on the page, and teach McLuhan-style media theory (the source book's context) that print is not neutral container but acoustic-visual battlefield. To invoke them is to invoke deliberate rudeness to the bourgeois reader, speed-reading before speed-reading, meme typography before memes.
