• Clothed: Similar because both are participles of clothe meaning "dressed"; differs because clothed is the everyday, neutral form usable of ordinary garments and figurative states, while clad is literary/technical and stresses striking, complete, or material covering.
  • Dressed: Similar because both mean "wearing clothes"; differs because dressed is the plain, universal word for getting into ordinary clothes, whereas clad is elevated and specifies a notable covering, often armour or robes.
  • Covered: Similar because both mean "having a layer over something"; differs because covered is far broader and fully neutral (any covering: dust, paper, insurance), while clad implies an outer skin fitted to a surface or body.
  • Draped: Similar because both can mean "loosely covered with fabric"; differs because draped suggests loose, hanging cloth arranged decoratively, whereas clad suggests a fitted, complete covering.
  • Robed: Similar because both mean "dressed in a distinctive long garment"; differs because robed is limited to robes (judges, priests, kings), while clad covers any garment or material and also all non-fabric coverings.
  • Arrayed: Similar because both are literary words for "dressed in ceremonial finery"; differs because arrayed additionally suggests orderly arrangement and can mean "mustered" (of troops), while clad simply means covered/dressed.
  • Garbed: Similar because both are formal words for "wearing distinctive dress"; differs because garbed belongs to the noun garb (a distinctive costume) and is narrower, while clad extends to armour, materials, and building surfaces.
  • Swathed: Similar because both mean "wrapped up in a covering"; differs because swathed suggests tight, extensive wrapping in bandages or cloth, whereas clad is a neat, fitted covering.
  • Sheathed: Similar because both mean "enclosed in a protective covering"; differs because sheathed is used of blades in scabbards and cables in jackets, suggesting a thin protective sleeve, while clad is used of bodies and buildings.
  • Coated: Similar because both mean "given an outer layer"; differs because coated is the plain technical term for paint, dust, or plating, while clad (in technical use) implies a substantial fitted panel or bonded layer and, for people, clothing.
  • Enveloped: Similar because both can mean "entirely covered"; differs because enveloped suggests being wrapped or surrounded by something (fog, silence), often hiding the object, whereas clad means openly covered with a visible outer layer.
  • Decked: Similar because both are old literary words for "adorned with clothing or covering"; differs because decked stresses decoration (decked with flowers) and not functional covering, while clad stresses the covering itself.
