lumber:
- The pawnbroker's clutter (1550s) - 'lumber' names disused household goods, broken tools, and pawned junk - the stock of medieval Lombard moneylenders; a 'lumber room' is the attic where such cast-offs are heaped
- Heavy stockpiling (17th c.) - to 'lumber' a place is to fill it with useless weight; the word carries the sense of mass and pile
- American transfer (1660s, New England) - the 'stored clutter' becomes the bulky raw material that fills colonial storehouses and wharves: roughly sawed timber, and lumber is reborn as the commercial building material of the New World
- The frontier industry (19th c.) - lumber yards, lumber camps, lumberjacks, and the great log drives of the Pacific Northwest; wood priced by the board-foot, shipped by rail and water
- Contemporary - the commercial American word for wood sawed into boards, planks, and beams, with a separate homophone, 'to lumber along', meaning to move heavily and clumsily
