• Quick: Similar because both describe speed; differs because quick is a neutral or positive measure of promptness, while hasty implies rushing past thought and courting error.
  • Swift: Similar because both denote high velocity; differs because swift suggests smooth, efficient, often graceful motion, whereas hasty suggests impatient, careless speed.
  • Rash: Similar because both involve acting without deliberation; differs because rash stresses reckless disregard for consequences, while hasty stresses premature timing and excessive speed.
  • Precipitate: Similar because both mean done suddenly and without preparation; differs because precipitate is a more formal, literary word for headlong, forced action, while hasty is the everyday term for rushing.
  • Impetuous: Similar because both describe quick, unreflecting action; differs because impetuous stresses emotional force and sudden energy, while hasty stresses brevity of time rather than violence of feeling.
  • Impulsive: Similar because both describe acting without reflection; differs because impulsive points to sudden internal urges, while hasty emphasizes the abbreviated time frame of the decision itself.
  • Hurried: Similar because both mean done in a rush; differs because hurried can be a neutral description of pressing for time, while hasty more strongly implies that the rush caused an error.
  • Premature: Similar because both mean done too soon; differs because premature stresses arriving before the proper time, while hasty stresses the excessive speed of the action.
