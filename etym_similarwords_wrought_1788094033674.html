• Made: Similar because both mean produced or created (a coat of mail wrought for a prince = made for a prince). Differs because made is the plain modern word with no craft or archaism; wrought implies skilled handwork and is literary.
  • Fashioned: Similar because both mean shaped with skill, especially by hand. Differs because fashioned is neutral-formal and can be used of anything shaped; wrought is archaic-solemn and carries the image of working material (especially metal).
  • Forged: Similar because both describe metal shaped by hammering (wrought iron ≈ forged iron). Differs because forged is the modern technical word for hammering hot metal and also means "faked" (a forged signature); wrought is broader (any worked material) and never means fake.
  • Hammered: Similar because both describe metal beaten into shape. Differs because hammered names the specific tool and action; wrought names the working in general, without specifying the tool.
  • Worked: Similar because wrought is literally the archaic past of work. Differs because worked is the modern, neutral form used of labour of any kind; wrought is reserved for skilled making, effect, and fixed phrases.
  • Crafted: Similar because both mean made with skill and care. Differs because crafted is modern and stresses design and artisanal care; wrought is archaic and stresses the physical working of material.
  • Shaped: Similar because both mean given a form. Differs because shaped is generic and physical, with no register; wrought implies skilled working and often an archaic, solemn tone.
  • Built / Constructed: Similar because both mean assembled into existence. Differs because build/construct are modern and structural — of buildings, machines, systems; wrought is of objects worked by hand and of effects caused.
  • Created: Similar because both mean brought into being. Differs because created is abstract and can be divine or artistic; wrought is concrete — material worked, or effects produced.
  • Produced / Caused (for "wrought havoc"): Similar because "wrought havoc" = produced or caused havoc. Differs because produce/cause are plain causal verbs; wrought adds weight, finality and an archaic colour that journalism and history writing exploit deliberately.
  • Beaten (as in beaten gold): Similar because both describe metal worked flat by hammering. Differs because beaten is technical-simple, of sheet metal; wrought covers all working, not just flattening.
  • Overwrought: Similar because it is built from the same participle, meaning over-elaborate or over-agitated. Differs because overwrought is a distinct modern adjective with a critical or clinical tone; wrought alone is neutral-to-solemn.
