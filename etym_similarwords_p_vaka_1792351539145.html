• Agni: Similar because both denote the paramount divine fire in ancient South Asian religion; differs because Agni is the primary personal and ontological name of the deity (cognate with Latin ignis), whereas Pāvaka is a functional agentive epithet emphasizing his purifying, sanctifying capacity.
  • Pāvamāna: Similar because both share the verbal root pū and designate aspects of purification in fire and Soma ceremonies; differs because Pāvamāna is a present middle participle denoting the entity currently undergoing purification or purifying itself (such as Soma flowing through wool), whereas Pāvaka is an active masculine agent noun denoting the external entity that performs the cleansing.
  • Śuci: Similar because both are revered Vedic epithets and personified hypostases of fire; differs because Śuci emphasizes gleaming, spotless, solar radiance and absolute moral purity (from root śuc, to gleam/burn), whereas Pāvaka focuses specifically on the physical and ritual act of burning away gross contamination.
  • Vahni: Similar because both are pervasive poetic and liturgical synonyms for fire in Sanskrit literature; differs because Vahni specifically highlights fire's role as the sacrificial carrier or conveyor who transports oblations to heaven (from root vah, to convey), whereas Pāvaka highlights its cleansing potency.
  • Hutāśana: Similar because both designate Agni in ritual contexts; differs because Hutāśana translates literally as "the oblation-eater" (from huta + aśana), foregrounding fire's voracious sacrificial appetite, whereas Pāvaka emphasizes its spiritual consequence of consecration.
  • Anala: Similar because both refer to fire throughout classical epic and dramatic literature; differs because Anala etymologically signifies the "insatiable" element that can never have enough fuel (from an + ala), pointing toward wild, uncontrolled combustion, whereas Pāvaka denotes sanctified, restorative fire.
  • Jātavedas: Similar because both are ancient Vedic titles for the fire divinity; differs because Jātavedas means "knower of all born beings / possessor of all wisdom," foregrounding Agni's cosmic omniscience as the witness of sacrifices, whereas Pāvaka is grounded in physical and metaphysical purgation.
  • Tejas: Similar because both concepts are intimately linked to fiery energy, heat, and spiritual splendor; differs because Tejas is an abstract subtle element (tanmātra) representing radiant glory, sharp vigor, and spiritual authority, whereas Pāvaka is a concrete nominal agent and personified deity.
