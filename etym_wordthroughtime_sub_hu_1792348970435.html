- [Epic Sanskrit Period: Valmiki Ramayana, c. 500 BCE–200 CE] — introduced in the Bala Kanda as a ferocious rakshasa warrior and chieftain, son of Tataka and brother of Maricha, who terrorizes the Dandaka hermits and is slain by Rama's Agneyastra - [perception: the prototypical demonic antagonist whose rapid destruction validates the young prince Rama's role as cosmic protector of sacrifice and cosmic order (dharma)]
- [Classical Epic & Puranic Period, c. 300–1000 CE] — established in the Mahabharata and Puranas as a recurring heroic and royal name for both noble human kings (e.g. King Subahu of the Chedis; Subahu of the Kulindas) and one of the hundred Kaurava brothers, demonstrating the noble literal meaning ("strong-armed") - [perception: recognized both as an epithet for mighty warriors and specifically as the infamous demon of the Ramayana]
- [Medieval Vernacular Ramayanas, c. 1100–1600 CE: Kamban, Tulsidas, Krittibas] — dramatized in regional vernacular traditions as the ultimate unholy defiler of sacred rites whose dramatic destruction by the fire-missile initiates Rama's martial saga - [perception: an archetypal embodiment of asuric pride and ritual sacrilege extinguished by divine justice]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era] — preserved in Ramlila folk performances across India and Southeast Asian shadow puppetry as the fierce demonic antagonist of the opening episodes, setting the stage for Rama's legendary journey - [perception: a foundational symbol of the triumph of spiritual order over destructive brute force]
