• Secretly: Similar because both denote action out of view; differs because secretly is neutral — it reports an unobserved fact, good or bad, trivial or grave, asserting nothing about wrongness, whereas surreptitiously always implies a culpable, deliberate stealthy intention.
  • Furtively: Similar because both denote stealthy action; differs because furtively focuses on guilty body language and fear of being caught in the very act, while surreptitiously focuses on the controlled, invisible method of the act itself.
  • Covertly: Similar because both denote hidden action; differs because covertly is the institutional, operational word (covert operations, covert surveillance) — detached and professional, lacking surreptitiously's individual, sly, morally charged quality.
  • Stealthily: Similar because both denote quiet, careful concealment; differs because stealthily stresses silence and slow care (a stealthy approach), while surreptitiously stresses the unauthorized, sneaky purpose behind the act.
  • Slyly: Similar because both denote cunning concealment; differs because slyly emphasizes craftiness and wit, often playful or conniving, while surreptitiously is more descriptive of method and carries the legal 'stolen' charge.
  • Clandestinely: Similar because both denote secrecy; differs because clandestinely suggests organized, sustained, group secrecy (clandestine meetings, networks), while surreptitiously usually describes the single stealthy act of one person.
  • Underhandedly: Similar because both denote deceitful concealment; differs because underhandedly implies cheating and deception against an opponent, while surreptitiously focuses on the stealth itself rather than on the deceived party.
  • Unobtrusively: Similar because both denote acting without drawing attention; differs because unobtrusively is neutral or even polite, carrying no implication of wrongdoing — the very opposite of surreptitiously's culpable edge.
  • Privately: Similar because both denote action not done in public; differs because privately implies a legitimate sphere of personal life (done in private), while surreptitiously implies concealment of an act that could not bear publicity.
  • In secret: Similar because both denote concealed action; differs because 'in secret' is a plain, neutral phrase for hidden activity, while surreptitiously adds the sneaking, unauthorized, morally charged quality from its Latin 'to steal' root.
