Note: The PIE connection is plausible but thin: the only firm external cognate is Lithuanian pùlti "to fall", and both the OED and Etymonline treat the deeper Indo-European link as uncertain (a possible Germanic-only innovation). The transitive-causative structure itself is securely attested across Germanic (Old Norse fella, Old Saxon fellian, Old High German fellen, German fällen). Felled is simply the regular weak past/participle of this verb — distinct from fell, the past tense of fall, and from unrelated homonyms (the adjective "fierce" from Old French fel, and the northern noun "hill" from Old Norse fjall).
