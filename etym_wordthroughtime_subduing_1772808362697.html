- [Bringing under] - "Subdue" develops the sense of bringing under control by force, literally pressing something down or quieting resistance.
- [Imperial and martial policy] - In historical prose it becomes the standard verb for conquest as policy: an empire "subduing" nations and territories, emphasising process and settled dominion.
- [Extended quieting sense] - Modern use adds psychological and aesthetic extensions — "subdued lighting," "subdued voice" — where the force is softened into quieted intensity, while the martial sense persists in formal and historical writing.
