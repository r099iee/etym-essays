• Evil: Similar because both denote profound moral wrong; differs because "evil" is the gravest, most absolute term for innate malevolence, while "wicked" often implies wilful, active sinfulness with a slightly less philosophical, more folkloric charge.
  • Sinful: Similar because both mean transgressing moral or religious law; differs because "sinful" is explicitly religious and names violation of divine law, whereas "wicked" works in both religious and wholly secular contexts.
  • Immoral: Similar because both mean contrary to morality; differs because "immoral" is a cooler, more abstract, judgemental term of ethics, while "wicked" is warmer, more visceral, and more condemnatory.
  • Wrong: Similar because both mean "not right"; differs because "wrong" is the neutral, general judgement of incorrectness or injustice, nowhere near the moral intensity of "wicked".
  • Bad: Similar because both describe something objectionable; differs because "bad" is extremely general and mild in this context, while "wicked" is emphatically evil — and "bad" has its own slang "good" sense paralleling "wicked".
  • Villainous: Similar because both describe a person devoted to evil deeds; differs because "villainous" belongs to the vocabulary of melodrama and villain stock-characters, while "wicked" is broader and more religious.
  • Vicious: Similar because both imply harmful cruelty; differs because "vicious" stresses savage, ferocious cruelty, whereas "wicked" can denote sly, calculating evil as well as violence.
  • Malevolent: Similar because both mean "wishing evil"; differs because "malevolent" is a cold, Latinate, psychological word for spiteful intent, lacking the folkloric force of "wicked".
  • Nefarious: Similar because both mean "extremely wicked"; differs because "nefarious" is a learned, dramatic word for villainous deeds and has no slang colour at all.
  • Iniquitous: Similar because both mean grossly unjust or wicked; differs because "iniquitous" is a formal, often legalistic term for grave injustice (iniquity), more abstract than "wicked".
  • Depraved / corrupt: Similar because both denote moral corruption; differs because these suggest a rotten moral character built up over time, while "wicked" can describe a single sinful act or a whole disposition.
  • Base / vile: Similar because both mean morally low and contemptible; differs because these stress lowness and contempt, whereas "wicked" stresses active evil agency.
  • Atrocious / heinous: Similar because both mean shockingly evil; differs because these are reserved for crimes of extreme cruelty and horror, a narrower, more intense band than "wicked".
  • Mischievous / naughty: Similar because in the mild, playful sense both describe charmingly disobedient behaviour; differs because these are far milder and purely secular, lacking any moral-hellfire residue.
  • Awesome / excellent / great (slang): Similar because in the inverted slang sense "wicked" means "very good"; differs because these are the neutral terms of approval, while the slang "wicked" keeps its flavour of extremity and regional colour (especially Boston).
