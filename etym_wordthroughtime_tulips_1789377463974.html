- 16th Century (Tudor & Ottoman Era, c. 1550–1580) - Introduced into Western Europe from Ottoman Turkey via Flemish diplomat Ogier Ghiselin de Busbecq; borrowed into English from French *tulipe* / Turkish *tülbend* (turban), referring to the flower's resemblance to a folded turban.
- 17th Century (Golden Age & Tulip Mania) - Exploded in European fame during the Dutch *Tulpenmanie* (1634–1637); celebrated in Dutch still-life paintings and English Jacobean estate gardens as a symbol of aristocratic wealth and botanical virtuosity.
- 18th Century (Ottoman Tulip Era) - Under Sultan Ahmed III, the flower gave its name to the *Tulip Era* (*Lâle Devri*), a period of artistic renaissance and peaceful court pleasure in Istanbul; cultivated widely in English formal parterres.
- 19th Century (Victorian Era) - Democratized across European domestic gardening; featured prominently in Victorian floriography (yellow tulips for bright cheerful smiles or unrequited love) and children's literature/fairy tales (such as Oscar Wilde's *The Remarkable Rocket*).
- 20th Century (Industrial Floriculture) - Became the centerpiece of the modern multi-billion-dollar Dutch commercial bulb industry centered in Keukenhof; inspired 1950s modernist industrial design (Eero Saarinen's *Tulip chair*).
- 21st Century (Contemporary Usage) - Remains globally preeminent as the quintessential spring bulb, garden flower, and economic metaphor for speculative investor bubbles.
