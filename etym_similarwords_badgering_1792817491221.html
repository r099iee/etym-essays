• Pestering: Similar because both mean nagging someone repeatedly for something; differs because pester is the general everyday word (any annoyance), while badgering stresses wearing the victim down over time until surrender.
  • Nagging: Similar because both are relentless verbal pressure; differs because nagging is scolding about duties and faults (a nagging conscience, a nagging spouse), while badgering presses for a concession or an answer — scolding versus siege.
  • Hounding: Similar because both picture pursuit that will not stop; differs because hounding chases (pursuing, tracking, hunting down), while badgering besieges in place — chase versus siege.
  • Harassing: Similar because both are sustained tormenting pressure; differs because harass is broader and graver (legally actionable, often hostile or discriminatory), while badgering is usually petty and domestic — crime versus nuisance.
  • Plaguing: Similar because both torment with unending demands; differs because plaguing afflicts like a disease (plagued by doubts, plagued with calls), while badgering is pointed at extracting something specific.
  • Importuning: Similar because both beg or demand insistently; differs because importune is formal and often single-scene (importuning a favour), while badgering is colloquial and essentially repeated.
  • Hectoring: Similar because both bully with words; differs because hectoring blusters and intimidates from above (the loud hectoring coach), while badgering worries from below or alongside — bluster versus gnawing.
  • Needling: Similar because both provoke with small repeated jabs; differs because needling teases to irritate (sly digs, sarcasm), while badgering presses to obtain (demands, questions) — irritation versus extraction.
  • Worrying (at): Similar because both gnaw at a victim like a terrier; differs because worrying shakes and tears (the dog worries the slipper), while badgering is purely verbal repetition — teeth versus tongue.
