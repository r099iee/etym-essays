Ancient Greek (c. 6th–5th c. BCE)
κάνιστρον (kánistron)
"wicker basket woven from reeds for bread or fruit"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
canistrum
"reed basket for bread, offerings, or fruits"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th–17th c.)
canister
"reed basket; small case or chest for dry goods"
Note: The material transition from woven wicker to rigid tinplate occurred during the 18th-century expansion of the tea trade, where moisture-proof containers were required to preserve tea leaves.
