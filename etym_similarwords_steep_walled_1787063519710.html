• sheer-sided: similar because both describe sides rising almost vertically; differs because sheer stresses the perpendicular drop and smoothness, while steep-walled adds the wall-like, enclosing quality.
  • precipitous: similar steeply vertical; differs because precipitous is a single-word synonym stressing danger (a precipice), while steep-walled is a compound stressing enclosure.
  • vertical-sided: similar sides at right angles; differs because vertical-sided is technical and geometric, while steep-walled is descriptive and literary.
  • high-walled: similar enclosed by high sides; differs because high-walled stresses height only, while steep-walled stresses the steepness of the angle too.
  • cliff-bound: similar bounded by cliffs; differs because cliff-bound is a rarer compound, while steep-walled is the standard, more transparent one.
  • towering: similar very high and overhanging; differs because towering emphasises height (as a tower), while steep-walled emphasises the near-vertical sides of an enclosure.
  • perpendicular: similar exactly upright; differs because perpendicular is a mathematical, geometric term, while steep-walled is an everyday descriptive compound.
  • abrupt: similar sudden and steep (abrupt cliff); differs because abrupt is general for suddenness, while steep-walled is specifically about walls of rock or land.
  • craggy: similar rough and steep rock; differs because craggy stresses jagged, broken rockiness, while steep-walled stresses smooth vertical enclosure.
  • enclosed: similar shut in on all sides; differs because enclosed is broad (any kind of fencing), while steep-walled specifies the enclosing walls are steep rock.
  • boxed-in: similar hemmed in by sides; differs because boxed-in is colloquial and applies to rooms or traffic, while steep-walled is formal topography.
  • rimmed: similar edged by a raised border; differs because a rim is a lip at the top, while steep-walled describes the vertical sides themselves.
