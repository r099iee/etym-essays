• Board: Similar because both refer to long, flat pieces of sawn lumber; differs because a board is generally thinner (typically under one and a half inches) and lighter, used for shelving, panelling, or fencing, whereas a plank is noticeably thicker, heavier, and engineered for load-bearing walkways or structural hulls.
  • Beam: Similar because both are heavy timber components used in architectural frameworks; differs because a beam has a squarer, stouter cross-section designed to span open spaces horizontally as a primary roof or ceiling support, whereas a plank is distinctly wide, flat, and rectangular.
  • Timber: Similar because both denote wood prepared for building and carpentry; differs because timber is a broad collective or generic term for dressed wood, structural balks, or standing trees fit for felling, whereas a plank is a specific, processed, flat-sawn unit.
  • Slab: Similar because both designate thick, flat, broad pieces of material; differs because a slab is typically made of unyielding stone, marble, concrete, or the rough unedged outer cut of a log with bark intact, whereas a plank is precisely sawn, squared, and finished timber.
  • Slat: Similar because both are narrow, flat pieces of wood; differs because a slat is thin, delicate, and narrow (such as those in window blinds or bed frames) with minimal load capacity, whereas a plank is heavy, broad, and structurally formidable.
  • Platform: Similar because in political terminology both denote policy frameworks; differs because a platform is the complete collective manifesto or foundational stage, whereas a plank is an individual, distinct policy point or planking board within that comprehensive structure.
  • Joist: Similar because both are horizontal wooden structural elements in floors and ceilings; differs because a joist is installed on its narrow edge to support parallel flooring from underneath, whereas a plank lies flat to serve directly as the walking surface itself.
