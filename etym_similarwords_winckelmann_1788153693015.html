• Lessing: similar because both Germans of the eighteenth century theorised Greek art and literature and were friends of Winckelmann; differs because Lessing was a dramatist and critic who argued in Laocoön that poetry and sculpture obey different laws, partly correcting Winckelmann’s single standard.
  • Goethe: similar because Goethe called Winckelmann his master, adopted the ideal of Greek calm, and completed the cult of the antique; differs because Goethe was a poet and statesman who lived the movement rather than founding the discipline.
  • Johann Joachim (his given names): similar as the bearer’s other names; differs in function: “Winckelmann” is the historical surname and eponym, the given names are biographical tags.
  • Pater: similar because Pater’s essay makes Winckelmann’s Hellenism the closing argument of The Renaissance; differs because Pater is the aesthetic critic who transmutes Winckelmann’s scholarship into prose poetry.
  • Winkelmann (single-l variant): similar as an alternative spelling of the same surname; differs orthographically and is rare; both mean “man of the corner”.
  • Winckelmannian (derived adjective): similar because it denominates the same historical complex; differs in being a descriptive adjective for the school, not the man.
