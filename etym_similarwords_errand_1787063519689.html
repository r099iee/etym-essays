• Mission: Similar because both denote a task one is sent to perform; differs because a mission is weightier, longer, and often strategic or dangerous, while an errand is short, routine, and domestic.
  • Task: Similar because both name something that must be done; differs because a task need not involve travel or delegation by another person.
  • Chore: Similar because both are small, routine duties; differs because a chore is typically a recurring household duty performed for oneself, while an errand is a delegated out-and-back journey.
  • Commission: Similar because both are charges entrusted to someone; differs because a commission implies formal appointment and often payment, and may cover a large project.
  • Assignment: Similar because both are tasks given by someone in authority; differs because an assignment is usually educational, work-based, or formal and rarely implies travel.
  • Quest: Similar because both involve a journey toward a goal; differs because a quest is a heroic, prolonged, high-stakes undertaking in romance and legend.
  • Expedition: Similar because both are purposeful journeys; differs because an expedition is long, organised, often exploratory, and demands resources and planning.
  • Delivery: Similar because both involve carrying something to a destination; differs because a delivery is specifically the transport of goods, not a general mission.
  • Job: Similar because both denote a piece of work; differs because a job is broader (occupation or any piece of work) and carries no travel connotation.
  • Business: Similar because both can mean “a matter one attends to”; differs because business is general, commercial, or abstract.
  • Charge: Similar because both are duties placed on a person; differs because a charge is a responsibility or official instruction rather than a journey.
  • Undertaking: Similar because both are matters one commits to doing; differs because an undertaking is larger, longer, and more solemn.
