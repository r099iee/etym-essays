• Suffer: Similar because it implies experiencing something undesirable; differs because 'suffer' can be more passive, simply experiencing misfortune, while 'incur' often implies a more direct causal link to one's actions.
  • Sustain: Similar because it means to undergo or experience (a loss, injury); differs because 'sustain' can also mean to support or keep something going, and when used for negative outcomes, it often implies enduring rather than actively bringing upon oneself.
  • Acquire: Similar in the sense of coming into possession of something; differs because 'acquire' is generally neutral or positive (acquire knowledge, acquire property), whereas 'incur' is almost exclusively negative.
  • Bring upon oneself: Very similar in core meaning, often used as a direct synonym; 'incur' is a more formal and concise single verb for this concept.
  • Draw upon oneself: Similar to 'bring upon oneself,' implying provocation or attraction of a consequence; 'incur' is a more direct and less metaphorical term.
  • Contract: Similar in the sense of acquiring a disease or a debt; differs because 'contract' is often used for agreements or illnesses, while 'incur' has a broader application to various negative consequences.
  • Undergo: Similar because it means to experience or be subjected to something; differs because 'undergo' can be more passive, simply going through an experience, while 'incur' emphasizes the consequence of one's actions.
  • Provoke: Similar when 'incur' is used for wrath or displeasure, as one's actions provoke a reaction; differs because 'provoke' specifically means to stimulate or incite, while 'incur' is the broader act of bringing the consequence upon oneself.
