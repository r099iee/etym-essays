• Dined: Similar because both mean eating a meal; differs because dined names the main formal meal (dinner) of the day, while supped names the light evening meal (supper).
  • Sipped: Similar because both can mean drinking in small amounts; differs because sipped is strictly about taking small tastes of liquid, while supped in its meal sense means eating the evening meal.
  • Ate: Similar because both mean consumed food; differs because ate is the neutral general past of "eat," while supped specifically means ate the evening meal (or drank in sips) and carries a literary, archaic flavor.
  • Drank: Similar because both can mean taking in liquid; differs because drank is the general word for consuming any drink in any quantity, while supped (sip sense) implies small, measured, spoonful sips.
  • Feasted: Similar because both mean eating a meal, often convivially; differs because feasted implies an abundant or celebratory meal, while supped implies a simple, light evening meal.
  • Quaffed: Similar because both are verbs of drinking; differs because quaffed means drank heartily in large gulps, the opposite of the sipping sense of supped.
  • Gulped: Similar because both are drinking verbs; differs because gulped means swallowed quickly in large amounts, while supped (sip sense) is slow and small.
  • Swilled: Similar because both are informal drinking verbs; differs because swilled implies drinking greedily or noisily in quantity, while supped is restrained and old-fashioned.
  • Guzzled: Similar because both are drinking verbs; differs because guzzled means drank voraciously and quickly, entirely opposite to the delicate sipping of sup.
  • Imbibed: Similar because both can mean drinking; differs because imbibed is formal and can be figurative (absorbing ideas), while supped is concrete and literary.
  • Tippled: Similar because both are drinking verbs with an archaic or regional flavor; differs because tippled specifically means drank alcohol repeatedly in small amounts.
  • Sopped: Similar because both are rooted in liquid-bread eating (sop); differs because sopped names dipping bread in liquid to absorb it, while supped names sipping or taking the evening meal.
  • Breakfasted: Similar in being a meal-verb past tense; differs because breakfasted names the morning meal, while supped names the evening meal.
  • Banqueted: Similar because both are formal meal verbs; differs because banqueted implies an elaborate public feast, while supped implies a simple private evening meal.
