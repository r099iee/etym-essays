PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*pleh₂k- / *plāk-
"to be flat, smooth; to please, calm, soothe"
Note: Ancestor of Greek plax ('flat surface') and Latin placēre ('to please, be smooth').
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
placēre
"to please, be agreeable, satisfy, find favor"
↓
Classical Latin (Intensified Compound) (c. 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE)
complacēre
"to please greatly, give mutual pleasure, be very pleasing"
Note: Formed from intensive prefix com- ('completely, thoroughly') + placēre ('to please').
↓
Late Latin / Medieval Latin (c. 4th–12th c.)
complacēns (genitive complacentis)
"being well-pleased, taking great delight, gracious"
↓
Early Modern English (Adjective Adoption) (c. 1640–1650)
complacent
"self-satisfied, contented with one's condition"
↓
Early Modern English (Adverb Formation) (c. 1650–1670)
complacently
"in a self-satisfied, smug, or uncritically tranquil manner"
↓
Modern English (present)
COMPLACENTLY
