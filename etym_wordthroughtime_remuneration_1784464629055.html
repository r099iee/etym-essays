“Remuneration” entered English from Latin as the formal word for payment — reward, recompense, the return due for work or service.
“Remuneration” through the 17th–19th centuries became a legal and commercial word — the money paid for labour or office, appearing in contracts and formal documents rather than casual speech.
“Remuneration” in the 20th century settled as the polite, abstract term for pay — salary, wages, compensation packaged in one formal polysyllable — often used in business and official contexts.
“Remuneration” today remains the formal word for payment for services: to ask about “remuneration” is to translate plain “pay” into ledger language — exactly a respectable hobbit’s way of asking what he will get out of an adventure.
