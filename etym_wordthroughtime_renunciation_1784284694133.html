14th-Century Legal-Religious Import - Renunciation enters English around 1400 from French and Latin renuntiatio, a formal public declaration; its earliest sense keeps the Latin flavor of a solemn announcement made before witnesses.
From Announcement to Abandonment - Following Latin usage (renuntiare amicitiam, "to renounce a friendship"), the English noun shifts from "declaration" to "the formal giving up of a right, claim, belief, or office," acquiring legal and moral gravity.
Religious and Ascetic Colour - Through the monastic tradition renunciation becomes the classic word for monks renouncing the world and for self-denial of one's desires, so it carries moral and spiritual solemnity alongside its legal use.
Contemporary Sense - Today renunciation is the formal term for deliberately relinquishing a right, title, belief, or practice — public, considered, often principled — with the older announcement sense all but lost.
