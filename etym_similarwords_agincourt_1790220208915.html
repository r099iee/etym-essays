• Waterloo: Similar because both are famous French/Belgian battlefields that entered the English language as iconic historical idioms; differs because Waterloo symbolizes a catastrophic, final, and irreversible defeat ("to meet one's Waterloo"), whereas Agincourt symbolizes an underdog's astonishing victory.
  • Thermopylae: Similar because both represent small, disciplined military forces facing overwhelming hordes; differs because Thermopylae is an archetype of heroic last-stand martyrdom and tactical sacrifice, while Agincourt ended in complete, triumphant military victory for the smaller force.
  • Cannae: Similar because both are historic military masterclasses in total annihilation; differs because Cannae is celebrated for Hannibal's double-envelopment tactic against equal or larger forces, whereas Agincourt centers on missile infantry, terrain choke points, and national mythmaking.
  • Hastings: Similar because both are foundational medieval Anglo-French battles; differs because Hastings (1066) represents the conquest and subjugation of Anglo-Saxon England by the Normans, whereas Agincourt represents English military dominance deep in French territory.
  • Balaclava: Similar because both involve iconic British military engagements commemorated in literature; differs because Balaclava (site of the Charge of the Light Brigade) symbolizes tragic blunder and suicidal bravery, while Agincourt represents tactical brilliance and total triumph.
  • Trafalgar: Similar because both represent celebrated British victories against French forces that cemented national identity; differs because Trafalgar was a decisive naval fleet engagement of the Napoleonic era, while Agincourt is a medieval land battle of longbowmen versus knights.
  • Marathon: Similar because both involve a smaller, determined army defeating a massive invading force through tactical innovation; differs because Marathon belongs to classical Greek antiquity and the defense of democratic Athens, whereas Agincourt is situated in late medieval dynastic warfare.
