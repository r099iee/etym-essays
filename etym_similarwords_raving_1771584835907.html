• Ranting: Similar because both describe loud, wild, furious talk; differs because ranting, while angry and repetitive, is still coherent — the ranter has a grievance and presses it — whereas a raver has lost the thread of sense altogether.
  • Babbling: Similar because both describe incoherent talk; differs because babbling is milder and more pitiable — weak, aimless, childish talk — while raving is violent, demented, noisy shouting.
  • Delirious: Similar because both suggest temporary derangement; differs because delirious names the feverish mental state itself, typically from illness, while raving describes the loud, violent outward speech of such a state; one can be delirious yet silent.
  • Frenzied: Similar because both mean wildly agitated; differs because frenzied emphasises frantic, uncontrolled activity and emotion of any kind, while raving focuses specifically on wild, incoherent speech.
  • Hysterical: Similar because both describe uncontrollable emotional excess; differs because hysterical is the clinical and pejorative word for a loss of emotional control (historically linked to hysteria), while raving stresses the loudness and incoherence of the speech itself.
  • Raging: Similar because both mean violent and uncontrolled; differs because raging describes a furious force or fury in general (a raging storm, a raging fire), while raving is characteristically the speech of a furious or mad person.
  • Wild: Similar because both describe uncontrolled behaviour; differs because wild is far broader — it covers all uncontrolled natural or human behaviour — while raving is specifically loud, incoherent talk.
  • Incoherent: Similar because both describe speech that fails to make sense; differs because incoherent is a neutral, clinical term for disconnected speech, while raving adds violence, loudness, and the suggestion of madness.
  • Gushing: Similar because both can mean excessive, effusive expression; differs because gushing is the modern positive or faintly mocking word for excessive enthusiasm and praise, whereas raving is normally the violent, demented or ecstatic extreme.
