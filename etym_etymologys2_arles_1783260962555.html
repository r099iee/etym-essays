CHAIN 1 — THE FIRST ELEMENT (are- "near, before"): Stage 1 — PIE *h₂er- "in front of, near, at" (reconstructed preposition/adverb; status DEBATED — some reconstructions favour a different underlying base for the Celtic "before, for" preposition, so the deepest analysis is uncertain). Stage 2 — Proto-Celtic *are "before, in front of, near, beside" (reconstructed; cf. Old Irish air-/ar "for, before", Welsh ar "on, at"). Stage 3 — Gaulish *are- "near, in front of, beside" (attested in Gaulish compounds such as Aremorica "the land by the sea" and Arduenna "the Ardennes"). Stage 4 — element are- in the Gaulish toponym *Arelate "the place before/beside the marsh" (see Chain 2). CHAIN 2 — THE SECOND ELEMENT (-late- "marsh"): Stage 1 — Proto-Celtic *lati- "marsh, mud, swamp" (reconstructed; cf. Old Irish lathach "mud, puddle", Welsh llaid "mud, dirt"; the exact proto-form is DEBATED, but a Celtic word for wetland is generally agreed). Stage 2 — Gaulish *lati- "marsh, swamp, mud" (the marshy flats at the head of the Rhône delta). Stage 3 — Gaulish toponym *Arelate "the place before the marsh" (the pre-Roman settlement name, formed from are- + late-). Stage 4 — Latin Arelate / Arelas (genitive Arelatis) "Arelate" (attested from the 1st century BC — Caesar's Bellum Civile of 48 BC; the Roman colony founded c. 46 BC). Stage 5 — Old Provençal / Old French Arles (with the loss of the unstressed final syllable; attested from the 10th–12th centuries). Stage 6 — Modern French Arles and English Arles (proper name; in English from the 16th century in historical and geographical writing). DEBATE NOTE: the "place before the marsh" reading is the traditional and most widely accepted etymology, but the second element is not absolutely secure — some scholars have proposed a different Celtic base, and the exact suffixation is debated. The Roman-to-French-to-English chain is secure.
