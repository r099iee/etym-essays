- [Chinese loan into English] - 'Ding' enters English from Chinese ding as the name of the ancient bronze ritual vessel; connotation is exclusively cultural and archaeological - the supreme sacrificial cauldron of Shang and Zhou ancestor worship.
- [19th-20th century: antiquarian adoption] - Western art historians adopt 'ding' as the standard term for these tripod and quadripod bronze vessels; connotation is scholarly, precise, and reverent, distinguishing the ritual bronze from the ordinary cooking pot.
- [Modern usage] - 'Ding' is used in English mainly in art history and Sinology for the ritual vessel; connotation is monumental and dynastic - an emblem of ancestral worship, inherited rank, and the mandate of heaven.
