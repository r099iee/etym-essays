- 16th–17th c. - the verb steepen is coined in English from the adjective steep plus the causative suffix -en; it is rare and technical, used of fortifications and embankments being made more sharply inclined.
- 18th–19th c. - the word stays uncommon while the plain adjective does the descriptive work; where it appears it is in surveying and geology, describing slopes that grow more severe with distance.
- 19th–20th c. - the intransitive sense, 'to become steeper', takes hold in scientific prose; the present participle steepening becomes the standard way to name a gradient changing beneath the feet or across a map.
- 20th–21st c. - steepening is everyday in technical fields - aviation, geomorphology, even finance (a 'steepening yield curve') - while in literary description it converts a static slope into a process unfolding in time, the ground actively tilting away under the walker, which is precisely its effect in this passage.
