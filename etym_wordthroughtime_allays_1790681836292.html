- Old English ālecgan: concrete sense to lay down, put down, suppress — perception practical and physical, laying objects or foes low
- Middle English aleyen, alayen: broadened to abate, subdue, weaken, dilute — connotation still forceful, used of tumults, metals, and appetites alike
- Early Modern English: narrowing toward soothing — to allay fears, passions, pain — perception becomes consolatory and medical, shedding the violent lay-low sense
- 18th–19th c. literary standard: allay as polite formal synonym for calm — connotation genteel, favoured in sermons, novels, and parliamentary speech to signal measured reassurance
- Contemporary allays: third-person analytic use — theories, reports, gestures allay concerns — perception mildly sceptical: relief is acknowledged but its completeness is doubted, as in the source where flattery allays insecurity suspectly
