• bellows: Similar because both terms describe an expandable, compressible pneumatic device used to blow air; differs because bellows is the overarching generic term that includes small, handheld fireplace blowers, musical organ reservoirs, and accordion chambers, whereas forge-bellows specifically designates the massive, heavy-duty workshop apparatus installed in a blacksmith's hearth.
  • blast pipe / tuyere: Similar because both components are involved in delivering a concentrated draft of air to a furnace fire; differs because the tuyere (or blast pipe) is strictly the heat-resistant iron or copper nozzle through which air enters the charcoal bed, whereas the forge-bellows is the entire pneumatic pump that generates the air pressure.
  • blower: Similar because both machines generate a forceful current of air to accelerate combustion; differs because a blower typically refers to a rotary, centrifugal, or motorized mechanical fan introduced during the Industrial Revolution, whereas a forge-bellows operates through the reciprocating expansion and contraction of flexible leather chambers.
  • fan: Similar because both move air to stimulate fire; differs because a fan relies on rotating blades to produce continuous aerodynamic displacement, whereas a forge-bellows relies on volumetric displacement within a sealed bladder governed by one-way flapper valves.
  • furnace: Similar because both are indispensable elements of a metallurgical workshop; differs because the furnace is the thermal refractory structure containing the fire and molten metal, whereas the forge-bellows is the external auxiliary machine that supplies it with oxygen.
  • air compressor / supercharger: Similar because both increase the supply of oxygen to a combustion chamber to produce higher thermal and mechanical power; differs because compressors and superchargers belong to modern pneumatic and internal combustion engineering, utilizing high-speed pistons, screws, or turbines rather than traditional timber-and-leather bellows.
