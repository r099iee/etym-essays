- 15th c. — the noun: Middle English crud or curd names the coagulated part of soured milk, pressed toward cheese.
- 1580s — the verb: curdle appears as the frequentative — to keep making curds — first of milk, then of blood in horror writing.
- 17th c. — horror adopts it: to curdle the blood becomes the standard physiology of fear on stage and page.
- 18th c. — folk physics: thunder curdles milk, witches curdle milk, and sour faces curdle milk — dairy gossip enters proverbs and novels.
- 19th c. — the cook's terror: French haute cuisine makes curdling the cardinal kitchen sin (split sauces, seized custards) while Cheddar makes it industry.
- Today — sour in every register: curdled sauces, curdled blood, curdled friendships — and Tolkien's curdling face keeping the folk joke alive.
