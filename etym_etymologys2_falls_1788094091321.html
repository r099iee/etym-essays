NOTE: The noun derives from the strong verb (Old English feallan, past feoll, past participle gefeallen, German-style class VII ablaut). Germanic cognates are numerous and consistent: Old Norse falla, Old Saxon fallan, Old High German fallan, Dutch vallen, German fallen. The PIE reconstruction *pol- "to fall" (Pokorny) rests chiefly on Baltic and Germanic material — Lithuanian puolti "to fall", Old Prussian aupallai "finds" (literally "falls upon") — and is standard in the handbooks. The geographical sense "a fall of water" is attested in English from the 16th century; the plural "falls" used of a waterfall, and as a place-name form, is established by the 17th–18th centuries.
