• friendly: Similar because both describe a warm, pleasant disposition toward others; differs because friendly is the broad everyday word for behaviour, while amiable adds a settled sweetness of temperament and a slightly formal register.
  • affable: Similar because both describe an approachable, pleasant manner; differs because affable specifically highlights warm conversational ease and readiness to be addressed, while amiable points to overall good nature.
  • genial: Similar because both suggest pleasant warmth; differs because genial carries energetic, radiating cheerfulness, while amiable is quieter and gentler.
  • amicable: Similar because both share the Latin family of amicus “friend” and a basic friendliness; differs because amicable applies to relations, agreements, and settlements between parties, never to a person's temperament.
  • good-natured: Similar because both describe an agreeable, easy temper; differs because good-natured stresses tolerance and lack of ill-will, while amiable stresses the active warmth that draws people in.
  • pleasant: Similar because both describe a person who is agreeable to be around; differs because pleasant is far broader (weather, taste, sound) and says less about character.
  • kind: Similar because both are positive character traits of warmth; differs because kind centres on active benevolence and helpful deeds, while amiable centres on temperament and manner.
  • sociable: Similar because both describe a person easy to be with; differs because sociable stresses liking company and seeking it out, while amiable is about disposition rather than gregariousness.
  • agreeable: Similar because both describe someone pleasant and easy to get along with; differs because agreeable suggests conformability and readiness to please, while amiable implies genuine warmth rather than mere accommodation.
  • sweet-tempered: Similar because both describe a consistently pleasant disposition; differs because sweet-tempered is more informal and implies mildness under provocation, while amiable is more social and outward.
  • warm: Similar because both suggest outgoing friendliness; differs because warm is a casual bodily metaphor, while amiable is the more formal established adjective of character.
  • lovable: Similar because both derive from the same Latin root (amāre “to love”) and originally meant “worthy of love”; differs because lovable focuses on the feeling others have toward the person, while amiable in modern use focuses on the person's own friendly manner.
  • charming: Similar because both describe people who please others; differs because charming implies deliberate, often magnetic attractiveness, while amiable is more about plain good nature.
