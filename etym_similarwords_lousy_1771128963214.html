• Miserable: Similar because both describe a thoroughly bad or unpleasant state; differs because miserable centers on internal suffering and sorrow, while lousy projects an external aura of contamination and worthlessness.
  • Vile: Similar because both express extreme contemptibility; differs because vile implies active, malicious moral corruption, whereas lousy suggests passive, pathetic degradation.
  • Awful: Similar because both mean "very bad"; differs because awful (once "awe-inspiring") carries stronger emotional force, while lousy is a blunter, more casual and tactile insult.
  • Terrible: Similar because both are colloquial intensifiers of badness; differs because terrible evokes fear and dread, while lousy evokes disgust rooted in parasitic vermin.
  • Sordid: Similar because both are grounded in filth and degradation; differs because sordid implies mercenary or sleazy motive, while lousy captures generalized pedestrian badness.
  • Wretched: Similar because both denote a deplorable condition; differs because wretched emphasizes pitiable misery, while lousy emphasizes revulsion and social worthlessness.
  • Contemptible: Similar because both mean deserving of scorn; differs because contemptible is formal and reasoned, while lousy is informal, visceral, and biologically sourced.
  • Rotten: Similar because both are colloquial negatives derived from decay or vermin; differs because rotten suggests decomposition (and is used of luck/situations), while lousy keeps the residue of parasitic filth.
