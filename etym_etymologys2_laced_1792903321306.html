Latin (1st c. BCE)
laqueus
‘noose, snare, looped cord’
↓
Old French (12th c.)
las, laz
‘cord, string, noose’
↓
Old French verb (12th–13th c.)
lacer, lacier
‘to tie, fasten with cord’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
lacen
‘to fasten with lace, tie up’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
lace
‘cord; openwork trim; to intertwine’
↓
Modern English (current, past participle)
laced
‘interwoven, fastened, threaded through’

Note: Latin laqueus itself has no fully certain deeper etymology; older handbooks proposed a PIE snare-word, but de Vaan and modern Latinists leave it without an accepted PIE parent, so the chain honestly begins at Latin rather than inventing a root.
