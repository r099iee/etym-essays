• Home (dwellings): Similar because both name a place where people live; differs because home is warm and emotional, charged with belonging and sentiment, whereas a dwelling is deliberately flat and functional.
  • House (dwellings): Similar because both refer to a place of residence; differs because house is purely physical, the building itself, with no administrative or legal neutrality.
  • Residence (dwellings): Similar because both denote a place where one lives; differs because residence is official and slightly pompous, the word of forms and registers, not of ordinary speech.
  • Abode (dwellings): Similar because both mean a place of dwelling; differs because abode is literary and suggests something temporary, a resting-place, not the fixed, settled, legal permanence of a dwelling.
  • Habitation (dwellings): Similar because both name a place of living; differs because habitation stresses the act of inhabiting an empty or wild place, while a dwelling is a settled residence in an organised society.
  • Domicile (dwellings): Similar because both are legal terms for residence; differs because domicile is a lawyer's word for the place fixed for legal purposes, while dwelling is the plain statutory word for a building where people live.
  • Lodging (dwellings): Similar because both name places where people stay; differs because lodging is temporary — a rented room for the night — while a dwelling is a permanent, settled place of residence.
  • Towered (loomed): Similar because both describe rising high; differs because towered is neutral and physical, stating great height, while loomed adds obscurity, darkness, and menace.
  • Overhung (loomed): Similar because both describe a thing rising above another; differs because overhung suggests a physical cantilever or cliff edge hanging over, while loomed suggests a vague, threatening presence.
  • Impended (loomed): Similar because both describe a threat about to happen; differs because impended applies almost exclusively to events in time (a crisis impends), while loomed is primarily a visual word for shape and size.
  • Hovered (loomed): Similar because both describe a presence suspended over something; differs because hovered implies lightness and motion in the air, while loomed implies massive weight and immobility.
  • Threatened (loomed): Similar because both carry menace; differs because threatened is the plain word for expressing a danger, while loomed achieves menace visually, through obscurity and magnitude.
  • Dominated (loomed): Similar because both describe a thing that overpowers its surroundings; differs because dominated stresses control and superiority, while loomed stresses the indistinct, oppressive bulk itself.
  • Bulged (loomed): Similar because both describe a mass swelling into view; differs because bulged is used of a single protruding shape, while loomed describes a whole dark silhouette appearing at a distance.
  • Appeared (loomed): Similar because both describe something coming into view; differs because appeared is neutral and complete, while loomed specifies the slow, dark, oversized quality of the appearance.
