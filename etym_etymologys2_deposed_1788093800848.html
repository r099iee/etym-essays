Component chain for ponere and its Romance successor
PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*apo
"off, away"
↓
Old Latin (3rd–2nd c. BCE)
posinō
"to put down"
(po- "away" + sinō "to let,
leave"; sinō's origin is debated)
↓
Latin (2nd–1st c. BCE)
pōnere
"to put, place, set"
↓
Late Latin (4th–6th c. CE)
pausāre
"to rest, halt, cease"
(from Greek paúein "to stop";
in Gallo-Romance it takes over
the sense "to place" from pōnere)
↓
Old French (9th–12th c.)
poser
"to put, place, set"
↓
Old French (13th c.)
deposer
"to put down; remove from office"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
deposen
"to remove from office"
↓
English (modern)
DEPOSED
"removed from power;
(of a witness) examined under oath"

Note: The legal sense "to testify" goes back to Latin deponere testimonium "to lay down testimony". English preserves a doublet from two channels: deposed (via pausāre → poser) and deposit/deposition (via positus, the old past participle of pōnere).
