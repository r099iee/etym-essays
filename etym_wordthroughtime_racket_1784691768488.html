- 1560s - "racket" first appears in English as an expressive, imitative word for riotous frolic and noisy merrymaking — a word built to sound like what it describes, allied to the clattering and rattling family of Germanic words.
- 17th century - it came to mean the confused, clashing din of many things breaking or striking together, and then any boisterous disturbance of the peace; a racket is never simply loud, but loud in a way that irritates someone.
- 20th century to modern - in early American English the word made a surprising leap from sound to crime: a dishonest scheme or fraudulent business, as in a "protection racket", producing "racketeer" in the 1920s — while the sporting implement, a separate word from Arabic for "palm of the hand", is spelled and pronounced the same.
