• Carload: Similar because both refer to large quantities of goods transported by an automobile; differs because a carload encompasses the entire interior passenger and cargo space of the vehicle, whereas a trunkload specifically denotes the capacity of the enclosed rear luggage compartment.
  • Truckload: Similar because both are vehicular volumetric load measures; differs because a truckload indicates a vastly larger, commercial freight volume carried by a heavy cargo truck or lorry, whereas a trunkload is a personal consumer-scale quantity.
  • Boatload: Similar because both are compound measure nouns ending in -load used hyperbolically; differs because a boatload is a maritime or nautical container measure conveying massive, overwhelming quantities (e.g., "a boatload of money").
  • Chestful: Similar because both refer to the capacity of a large storage box; differs because chestful evokes stationary antique furniture or pirate treasure chests, whereas trunkload connects directly to automobile travel and transportable luggage chests.
  • Armload: Similar because both designate practical visual units of carrying capacity; differs because an armload is a small amount that an individual can physically hold in both arms, whereas a trunkload requires vehicular or heavy chest storage.
  • Abundance: Similar because a trunkload metaphorically denotes an abundance; differs because abundance is an abstract, formal noun describing general plenitude, whereas trunkload is an earthy, concrete, visual compound noun.
