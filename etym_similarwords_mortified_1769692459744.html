• Humiliated: Similar because both describe wounded pride and loss of dignity; differs because humiliated emphasizes public lowering and abasement, while mortified emphasizes the internal, overwhelming sting of embarrassment.
  • Embarrassed: Similar because both name discomfort at a social misstep; differs because embarrassed is mild and passing, while mortified is intense and crushing.
  • Ashamed: Similar because both involve painful self-consciousness; differs because ashamed carries moral guilt or regret, while mortified is chiefly about social exposure and wounded pride.
  • Chagrined: Similar because both describe vexed embarrassment at a setback; differs because chagrined is a quieter, more internal annoyance, while mortified is hotter and more dramatic.
  • Abashed: Similar because both describe sudden loss of composure from shame; differs because abashed is self-conscious and modest, while mortified is deeply struck and humiliated.
  • Discomfited: Similar because both describe being thrown into confusion; differs because discomfited emphasizes frustration and upset rather than shame.
  • Crushed: Similar because both describe a destroyed spirit or pride; differs because crushed is a broad metaphor of emotional devastation, while mortified specifically involves shame.
  • Abased: Similar because both involve lowering one's dignity; differs because abased is formal and implies deliberate or deserved reduction, while mortified is the involuntary feeling.
  • Shamefaced: Similar because both show shame visibly; differs because shamefaced describes the guilty or bashful appearance rather than the internal death-blow of mortified.
  • Deadened (literal): Similar because both share the literal sense of deprived of life; differs because deadened is neutral about cause, while mortified specifically means "made dead" with the death imagery built in.
  • Gangrenous (medical): Similar because both name dead, decayed tissue; differs because gangrenous is the precise modern clinical term, while mortified is the older, now figurative word.
  • Numbed: Similar because both can describe deadened feeling; differs because numbed implies loss of sensation, whereas mortified implies painful sensation at the moment of shame.
