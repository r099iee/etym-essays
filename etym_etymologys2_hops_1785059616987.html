No secure PIE root is reconstructable for the hop. The West Germanic stem *hopp-ōn- names the climbing, twining hop plant, and is plausibly connected with the Germanic verbal base *hopp- ("to hop, leap, spring"), from the vine's habit of scrambling and "hopping" over hedges, poles, and strings; an alternative view treats it as a loan from an unknown pre-Germanic substrate. Scholarly opinion is divided. - West Germanic *hopp-ōn- ("the climbing, twining hop plant"; origin debated as above; c. 1st millennium CE) → Old High German hopfo / hopho ("hop"; attested 9th–10th c.) → Middle High German hopfe / hopfen → Modern German Hopfen ("hops"; compounds Hopfenzapfen "hop cone", Hopfengarten "hop garden") → (separate continental line) Middle Low German hoppe and Middle Dutch hoppe ("hop plant, hop cone") → Modern Dutch hop. - Old English hop (plural hoppe) ("hop"; attested sparsely, c. 10th c.) → Middle English hoppe / hops (singular) with hoppes (plural; the plural form becomes the brewing standard) → Modern English hop (singular "plant" or "cone") / hops (plural noun, the ingredient, almost always plural). - Medieval Latin (botanical loan from Germanic): humulus / hoppo → Linnaean genus Humulus lupulus (1753), the specific epithet lupulus being Latin "little wolf," from the vine's strangling habit. Note on debate and parallel family: the deeper origin of *hopp-ōn- is genuinely unknown. The "hop" (leap) explanation is attractive because the bine genuinely does scramble and climb, but no direct morphological proof exists, and some scholars posit a substrate Wanderwort for the plant. Independently, the Slavic world has a wholly separate word-family for the same plant — Old Church Slavonic chmelĭ ("hops"), ancestor of Russian хмель (chmelʹ), Czech chmel, Polish chmiel, Serbian хмељ — which is itself probably a Wanderwort of unknown ultimate origin and is not cognate with the Germanic stem. The botanical Latin humulus may derive from this Slavic family (possibly via medieval Germanic mediation) or may be a separate formation; both routes remain under discussion.
