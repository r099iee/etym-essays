• Hustle: Similar because both mean energetic, rapid motion and activity; differs because hustle implies aggressive urgency or commercial ambition, while bustle suggests lively, cheerful, almost effortless activity.
  • Hurry: Similar because both mean moving quickly; differs because hurry implies haste toward a goal and often anxiety, while bustle emphasizes busy, noisy stir without the same urgency.
  • Rush: Similar because both mean rapid movement or a burst of activity; differs because rush implies urgency and crowding, while bustle is more diffuse, cheerful energy.
  • Flurry: Similar because both denote brief intense activity; differs because flurry is sudden and short-lived (like a gust of snow), whereas bustle is a sustained environment of busy motion.
  • Scurry: Similar because both mean quick, busy movement; differs because scurry suggests small, quick, slightly furtive steps (as of small creatures), while bustle is louder and more purposeful.
  • Scuttle: Similar because both mean hurried movement; differs because scuttle suggests short, uneven, slightly panicked steps, while bustle is continuous and energetic.
  • Ado: Similar because both denote fuss or activity; differs because ado focuses on trouble or ceremony ("without more ado"), while bustle centers on physical movement and noise.
  • Stir: Similar because both denote excited activity; differs because stir can be quiet or emotional (a "stir" of feeling), while bustle is audible and physical.
  • Commotion: Similar because both denote noisy activity; differs because commotion implies disorder, alarm, or disturbance, while bustle is orderly, purposeful busyness.
  • Hubbub: Similar because both denote confused noise and activity; differs because hubbub is chiefly a din of voices, while bustle is centered on movement.
  • Hurly-burly: Similar because both denote noisy, busy activity; differs because hurly-burly is more chaotic and turbulent (as of battle), while bustle is energetic but ordered.
  • To-do: Similar because both denote a stir of activity; differs because to-do implies a fuss or an event ("a big to-do"), while bustle names general busy motion.
  • Fashion senses (the garment):
  • Crinoline: Similar because both are structures that expanded a woman's skirt in the 19th century; differs because the crinoline was a hooped cage extending the whole skirt, while the bustle projected only at the back.
  • Pannier: Similar because both are skirt-expanding undergarments; differs because panniers extended the skirt sideways (18th century), while the bustle extended it backward (19th century).
  • Farthingale: Similar because both were skirt-frames worn under gowns; differs because the farthingale was a 16th–17th-century hooped frame, while the bustle was a 19th-century back pad.
  • Tournure: Similar because both denote the back drapery support of a Victorian skirt; differs because tournure is the chiefly French period term for the same structure as the bustle.
