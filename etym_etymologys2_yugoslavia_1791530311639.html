Note: the compound’s second element is Proto-Slavic *slověninū “Slav,” usually derived from *slovo “word” (PIE *ḱlew- “to hear”), hence “those who speak (our language).” The first element, *jugъ “south,” is sometimes connected with *jugъ “yoke” (PIE *yugóm) through the notion of the south wind, but that link is disputed. The name was coined in the nineteenth century and made official in 1929; it has no ancient toponymic ancestor.
