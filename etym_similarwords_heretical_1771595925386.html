• Unorthodox: Similar because both mean departing from accepted belief; differs because unorthodox is the mild, neutral description of anything departing from custom, carrying no charge of censure, whereas heretical carries the gravity of doctrinal error.
  • Heterodox: Similar because both mean divergence from a received system; differs because heterodox is the scholarly, almost clinical word for doctrinal divergence — the polite learned cousin of heretical, without the charge of spiritual error.
  • Blasphemous: Similar because both offend religious authority; differs because blasphemous attacks the sacred directly — insulting God or holy things — whereas heretical attacks the Church's teaching itself, not necessarily the deity.
  • Sacrilegious: Similar because both are grave religious offences; differs because sacrilegious profanes sacred objects or places — an act of desecration — whereas heretical is a matter of belief and doctrine.
  • Iconoclastic: Similar because both flout established belief; differs because iconoclastic smashes venerated images and institutions, an attitude of attack against symbols, while heretical asserts a divergent doctrine of its own.
  • Dissident: Similar because both mean disagreement with an established order; differs because dissident is the political and secular word, carrying none of the theological gravity that heretical retains.
  • Apostate: Similar because both involve departure from a faith; differs because apostate names one who abandons the faith altogether — the step beyond heresy — while heretical describes a belief chosen against doctrine within the fold.
  • Nonconformist: Similar because both mean refusal to follow received practice; differs because nonconformist is a social and historical word (the English Protestant Nonconformists) for refusing forms, whereas heretical is a doctrinal word for refusing belief.
