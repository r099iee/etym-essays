Sanskrit (classical / ancient)
upala (उपल)
"stone, precious stone, jewel"
(inherited from an ancient Indo-European or pre-Indo-European substrate term for rock/gem)
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 1st c. BCE / 1st c. CE)
opallios (ὀπάλλιος)
"opal, iridescent precious stone"
↓
Classical Latin (1st c. CE; Pliny the Elder)
opallus
"opal gemstone"
↓
Old / Middle French (16th c.)
opale
"opal"
↓
Early Modern English (late 16th c.; c. 1590s)
opal
"iridescent silica gemstone"
↓
Modern English (plural form)
opals
"multiple opal gemstones; iridescent gems"

Note: Early Roman etymologists folk-etymologized opallus by linking it to Ops, the Roman goddess of fertility and abundance, while medieval scribes occasionally linked it to Greek ophthalmios ("eye-stone"); historical linguistics confirms direct derivation from Sanskrit upala via Mediterranean gem trade routes.
