Pre-Greek / Mediterranean substrate *topos "place, spot" — no reliable Proto-Indo-European reconstruction; the Greek noun is classed as of uncertain origin, likely a Pre-Greek substrate word [Greek topos attested from the 5th century BCE] > Ancient Greek topos (τόπος) "place, position, location, spot" [attested from the 5th century BCE onward: Herodotus, Thucydides, Plato] > Ancient Greek topikos (τοπικός) "pertaining to a place; local" — topos + adjectival suffix -ikos [attested from the 4th century BCE onward] > Greek ta Topika (τὰ Τοπικά) "The Topics" — Aristotle's treatise on the "places" (topoi) of argument, c. 350 BCE, which develops the rhetorical sense "commonplace, seat of an argument" [4th century BCE] > Late Latin topicalis "pertaining to a place; local" [Late Latin, c. 4th-6th century CE] > Middle French topique "local; of a topic" [16th century] > English topical [first recorded c. 1590 in the locative/rhetorical sense; the medical sense "applied to a spot on the body" and the newsworthy sense "of current interest" (c. 1820s) develop within English] Note: because the Greek etymon topos has no certain PIE ancestry, no earlier stage can be responsibly reconstructed; the chain therefore begins at the attested Greek noun. The English senses (locative-medical and newsworthy) diverge within English history, the second arising from the rhetorical "topics.
