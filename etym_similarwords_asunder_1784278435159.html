• Apart: Similar because both mean separated from one another; differs because apart is the plain, everyday adverb of mere distance or separation (the chairs stood apart), while asunder means separated into pieces or parts by rupture — stronger, more violent, and now chiefly literary.
  • In pieces / to pieces: Similar because both mean broken into fragments; differs because "in pieces" / "to pieces" is the common, concrete expression for the shattered state, while asunder is the single formal adverb of the same idea, used in elevated or archaic diction.
  • Separated: Similar because both name the state of being divided; differs because separated is the neutral, general word for any division (separated by distance, time, or law), while asunder implies violent or complete rupture into parts.
  • Torn: Similar because "torn asunder" is a fixed pair and both describe violent division; differs because torn is the past participle of the verb tear (something tears the fabric), while asunder is the adverb of result (the fabric lies asunder) — one names the action, the other the resulting state.
  • Sundered: Similar because both descend from the same root (Old English sundor) and both mean divided; differs because sundered is the past participle of the verb sunder ("he sundered the chain"), naming a completed action, while asunder is the adverbial state that follows ("the chain burst asunder").
  • Riven / rent: Similar because both appear with asunder and mean violently split; differs because riven and rent are the past participles of the verbs rive and rend, describing the tearing itself, while asunder describes the separated state resulting from the tearing.
  • In twain: Similar because both mean "into two parts"; differs because in twain (from Old English twā, "two") specifically means "in two," while asunder allows separation into two or many parts and is far more common.
  • Broken: Similar because both describe the result of fracture; differs because broken is the general word for any fracture (a broken bone, a broken promise), while asunder is restricted to separation into separate parts and carries dramatic or ceremonial weight.
  • Dismembered: Similar because both mean divided into parts; differs because dismembered is specific to tearing a body limb from limb, while asunder can apply to objects, people, and abstract things alike.
  • Severed: Similar because both mean cut into separate parts; differs because severed implies a clean cutting through a connection (a severed artery, severed ties), while asunder implies ragged tearing or bursting.
  • Divided: Similar because both name separation into parts; differs because divided is the neutral, everyday, often figurative word (divided opinions), while asunder is concrete, physical, and emphatic.
