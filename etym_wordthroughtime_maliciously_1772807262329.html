- [Legal and moral inheritance] - "Maliciously" enters English as the adverb of "malicious," at home in legal and moral prose where ill will is a term of art, colouring an action with deliberate intent to harm.
- [Legal specialization] - By the 17th-19th centuries the adverb anchors tort and criminal law — malicious prosecution, malicious damage — attaching a formal burden of wrongful intent to otherwise ordinary acts.
- [Literary and everyday expansion] - Modern narrative and speech extend it from the courtroom to social conduct (smiled, whispered, damaged "maliciously"), keeping the formal sting of deliberate ill will while remaining stronger and more marked than "meanly" or "nastily."
