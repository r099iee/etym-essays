- [1313–1350s - the merchant’s son in Naples and Florence writes romances, then the Decameron] - [Perception among first readers: brilliant, shocking, dangerously comic]
- [14th–15th c. - the humanists crown Dante, Petrarch and Boccaccio the ‘three crowns’ of Florence; the Decameron is printed c. 1470–71] - [Perception: a classic of the vernacular]
- [16th c. - the Counter-Reformation puts the Decameron on the Index in expurgated form (1573); clergymen purge its jests] - [Perception: a byword for obscenity that must be tamed]
- [17th–18th c. - scholars quietly edit him; his novelle feed the European story-market] - [Perception: an antique author of useful plots]
- [19th c. - Romantic rediscovery; Dante-and-Boccaccio cults; the Decameron’s plague frame read as the origin of modern realism] - [Perception: Renaissance vitality, Italian national pride]
- [20th c. - Pasolini’s The Decameron (1971); the name becomes shorthand for earthy Mediterranean storytelling] - [Perception: sexy, carnivalesque, beloved]
- [Today - ‘Boccaccio’ = the laughter of storytelling itself; scholars study his medieval sources (fabliaux, novellino) and his humanist Latin work] - [Perception: both the founder of the novella and the first modern mythographer of the Renaissance]
