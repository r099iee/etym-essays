Origin debated — no established PIE root; possibly onomatopoeic - Latin palpare 'to stroke, pat, touch gently, fondle' (classical Latin). Ultimate origin uncertain: Watkins proposed PIE *pal- 'to shake, touch gently, quiver', but de Vaan rejects any PIE etymology on phonetic grounds; the word may imitate the soft 'palp' of a patting hand. - Latin palpitare 'to throb, flutter, quiver' (frequentative of palpare, formed with the iterative suffix -itare: repeated, pulsating action; classical Latin, used of the heart and pulse) - Late Latin palpitatus (past participle) 'throbbed, fluttered' - English palpitate 'to beat or pulse rapidly, to throb' (borrowed 1620s from Latin palpitare / palpitatus, into the language of physicians) - English palpitating (present participle) 'throbbing, trembling with agitation' — the form under consideration - Modern English palpitating 'beating rapidly and irregularly, quivering with excitement or fear'
