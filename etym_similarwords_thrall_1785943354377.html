• Bondage: Similar because both denote a state of captivity; differs because bondage stresses physical chains, legal slavery, or restraint, while thrall emphasizes psychological or magical captivity.
  • Servitude: Similar because both name forced or bound labour; differs because servitude is a formal socio-legal condition, whereas thrall is poetic and emotional.
  • Enslavement: Similar because both concern loss of freedom; differs because enslavement names the act of making someone a slave, while thrall names the resulting state of being gripped or held under a spell.
  • Captivity: Similar because both mean being held prisoner; differs because captivity implies physical confinement in a cell or cage, whereas thrall can exist in the open air, under hypnotic or emotional influence.
  • Subjection: Similar because both mean being under authority; differs because subjection is political or administrative submission to a ruler, without the emotional or magical grip of thrall.
  • Slavery: Similar because both historically described an unfree person; differs because slavery is the institutional term for legal chattel bondage, while thrall has become a metaphor for fascination or obsession.
  • Serfdom / Vassalage: Similar because both are feudal bonds; differs because serfs were tied to the land and vassals to a lord by homage, while a thrall was personal property, a slave pure and simple.
  • Yoke: Similar because both image oppressive control; differs because yoke is the harness of a draught animal and implies burdensome service, while thrall names the state of being held, often spellbound.
  • Spellbound: Similar because both describe being captivated or entranced; differs because spellbound is limited to fascination and enchantment, while thrall can also mean harsh servitude or bondage.
