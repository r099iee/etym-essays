Root 1 — HAULED:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*kelh₁-
"to call" (or *kel- "to drive")
(link uncertain)
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*halōną
"to fetch, summon"
↓
Frankish (c. 4th–8th c.)
*halōn
"to fetch, haul"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
haler
"to pull, haul"
↓
Middle French (15th c.)
haler
"to haul, pull"
↓
English (16th c.)
haul
"to pull with great force"
↓
English (past tense)
hauled
"pulled with great effort or force"

Note: the PIE stage is uncertain — PGmc *halōną is sometimes linked to PIE *kelh₁- "to call" or *kel- "to drive", but neither connection is securely established.

Root 2 — REINS:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*ten-
"to stretch, hold"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
tenēre
"to hold"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
retinēre
"to hold back, restrain"
(re- "back" + tenēre "to hold")
↓
Old French (12th c.)
rene
"a rein"
↓
English (13th c.)
rein
"a strap for guiding a horse"
↓
English (plural form)
reins
"straps used to guide a horse"

Note: the Old French form was also written resne / reigne; the sense "rein" grew from retinēre "to hold back" — reins hold the horse back. The plural reins is the normal form.
