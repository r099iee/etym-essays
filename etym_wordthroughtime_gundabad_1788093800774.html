- Age of awakening (legend) - sacred origin: the mountain where Durin the Deathless, eldest of the Seven Fathers, first opened his eyes in the deep earth; in Longbeard memory it is a holy place, the cradle of their race.
- Later ages (legend) - slow desecration: as the Dwarves spread south to Khazad-dûm, Gundabad passes from sanctity into shadow; Orcs tunnel beneath it, and northern lore begins to say its name with dread rather than reverence.
- 1937 (The Hobbit) - the name enters print: Gundabad appears as a far-northern place-name in the world of Thorin's quest, part of the goblin geography that menaces the travellers; to first readers it is an exotic, ominous-sounding name of the North.
- 1954 (The Lord of the Rings) - strategic pivot: at the Council of Elrond, Gandalf reveals that around and beneath Gundabad a vast host has assembled - the capital of the Orcs of the North; the mountain is now, in reader perception, the storm-centre of the coming war.
- 1955 (Appendix A) - tragic depth: linked to the awakening of Durin, the mountain gains pathos: the same earth that bore the eldest of the Dwarves now holds the mustering of their enemies.
- 1996 (The Peoples of Middle-earth) - philological puzzle: Tolkien's posthumous notes treat the Dwarf-names, and scholars reconstruct *gund(u) 'underground hall, delve'; Gundabad becomes an object of linguistic scrutiny rather than pure menace.
- 2000s-2020s - cultural landmark: the mountain appears in maps, wikis, role-playing games, and the 2021 LOTRO expansion 'Gundabad'; perception settles as one of the great, grim landmarks of Middle-earth's northern marches.
