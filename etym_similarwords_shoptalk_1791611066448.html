• jargon: Similar because both involve specialized vocabulary peculiar to a trade, profession, or group; differs because jargon refers specifically to the linguistic terms, technical phrases, and obscure vocabulary itself (often with a pejorative connotation of unintelligibility), whereas shoptalk refers to the active, informal conversation and social exchange among colleagues.
  • patter: Similar because both involve rapid, specialized talk associated with a particular trade; differs because patter denotes the glib, rehearsed sales pitch or rapid patter of a street hawker, magician, or comedian designed to persuade an audience, whereas shoptalk is unscripted, peer-to-peer discourse.
  • argot: Similar because both represent specialized insider speech; differs because argot historically designates the secretive, underworld slang of criminals, vagabonds, or subcultures designed to conceal meaning from outsiders, whereas shoptalk is ordinary occupational dialogue without malicious secrecy.
  • parlance: Similar because both refer to a distinctive manner of speaking associated with a given field; differs because parlance is a formal, elevated term describing an idiomatic mode of expression (e.g., "in common parlance", "in legal parlance"), whereas shoptalk is casual, conversational, and collegial.
  • cant: Similar because both describe the characteristic speech of a particular sect or professional class; differs because cant strongly implies pious hypocrisy, sanctimonious catchphrases, or monotonous specialized whining, whereas shoptalk represents honest, substance-driven craft discussion.
  • gossip: Similar because both occur in casual, informal workplace settings; differs because gossip focuses on personal rumors, scandalous secrets, and private lives of individuals, whereas shoptalk focuses on the work, craft, business operations, and technical problems of the profession.
  • vernacular: Similar because both denote specialized, natural speech; differs because vernacular refers to the native regional dialect or ordinary everyday language of a people, as opposed to formal literary standards, whereas shoptalk is strictly vocational.
