- Bronze & Archaic Age (c. 1200–500 BCE) - Originally known as Aeolia or Pelasgiotis; renamed after the migratory northwestern Thessaloi entered and conquered the plains; perceived as a semi-mythical frontier of horse-breeders, lapiths, and centaurs.
- Classical & Hellenistic Eras (c. 500–146 BCE) - Organized into a powerful federal tetrarchy led by an aristocratic tagos (such as Jason of Pherae); celebrated for unmatched cavalry forces while simultaneously stigmatized across Greece as the dark epicenter of witchcraft and lunar sorcery.
- Roman Republic & Empire (c. 146 BCE–330 CE) - Integrated as a crucial Roman province and the staging ground for monumental civil wars (most notably the Battle of Pharsalus in 48 BCE between Julius Caesar and Pompey); immortalized by Roman poets (Lucan, Apuleius) as the land of terrifying necromancy.
- Byzantine & Ottoman Eras (c. 500–1881 CE) - Designated in medieval chronicles as Great Wallachia (Megale Blachia) due to its pastoral Vlach populations; later governed under Ottoman rule as the sanjak of Tesalya, dominated by large feudal agricultural estates (chifliks).
- Modern Era (1881–Present) - Formally annexed and integrated into the Kingdom of Greece following the Convention of Constantinople in 1881; established today as the core agricultural breadbasket and one of the 13 official administrative peripheries of the Hellenic Republic.
