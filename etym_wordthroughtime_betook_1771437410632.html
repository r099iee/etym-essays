- [Middle English] - Betake means to commit, entrust or deliver; the reflexive 'betook himself' becomes the polite, deliberate way to say a person directed his own steps somewhere.
- [16th-18th century golden age] - The standard literary verb for solemn departure: betook himself to his chambers, betook to flight, betook to study; dignified and ceremonious.
- [19th-century decline] - Falls out of everyday speech, surviving in novels as a period-flavoured formality, often for resolute or hasty departure.
- [Contemporary register] - Archaic and literary; used for elevated or mock-heroic effect - 'he betook himself to the pub' is now jocular.
