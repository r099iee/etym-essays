• armband: Similar because both are bands worn around the arm; differs because armband is a broad, utilitarian term typically referring to a fabric strip, badge, mourning band, or identification sash worn around the sleeve, whereas armlet is specifically a rigid ornamental piece of jewelry or a protective metal ring worn directly on the upper arm.
  • bracelet: Similar because both are ornamental jewelry worn on the arm; differs because a bracelet is worn almost exclusively on the wrist or lower forearm, whereas an armlet is worn higher up encircling the bicep or upper arm.
  • bangle: Similar because both are rigid circular ornaments; differs because a bangle is a rigid ring of metal, wood, or glass that hangs loosely around the wrist, whereas an armlet fits snugly around the muscular contour of the upper arm.
  • cuff: Similar because both encircle the limb; differs because a cuff is an open, wide metal band that slips on without a clasp (or the fabric end of a shirt sleeve), whereas an armlet can be a closed ring, hinged clasp, or spiral coil specifically tailored for the bicep.
  • torc / torque: Similar because both are ancient rigid metal rings of high prestige; differs because a torc is worn primarily around the neck (though occasionally around the upper arm in Celtic contexts), whereas an armlet is exclusively designed for the arm.
  • vambrace: Similar because both can refer to protective arm wear; differs because a vambrace is a specific piece of medieval plate armor protecting the lower arm from wrist to elbow, whereas armlet encompasses upper-arm ornaments and jewelry as well as general protection.
  • rerebrace: Similar because both relate to the upper arm; differs because a rerebrace is strictly a piece of medieval plate armor protecting the upper arm between shoulder and elbow, whereas armlet is predominantly an ornamental piece of decorative jewelry.
  • wristlet: Similar because both are small bands worn on the limb; differs because a wristlet is worn strictly around the wrist (as a close-fitting band, knit glovelet, or small bracelet), whereas an armlet is positioned on the upper arm.
  • anklet: Similar because both are metallic bands worn around limbs; differs because an anklet is worn around the ankle and foot, whereas an armlet is worn on the arm.
  • inlet: Similar because both describe narrow waterways extending inland; differs because an inlet is a general coastal indentation or recess in the shore, whereas an armlet in geography specifically highlights a narrow, branch-like tributary extending from a larger primary body of water like an arm.
  • creek: Similar because both designate small watercourses; differs because a creek is a natural stream of water or narrow tidal channel, whereas armlet emphasizes the branching, limb-like relationship to a larger parent river or bay.
  • tributary: Similar because both represent water branches; differs because a tributary is a freshwater river or stream that flows into a larger river or lake, increasing its volume, whereas a geographical armlet is an offshoot, secondary channel, or coastal arm.
