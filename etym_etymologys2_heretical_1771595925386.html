Chain for HERETICAL: 1. Proto-Indo-European *ser- "to take hold of, grasp, seize" (Pokorny's slender root, attested chiefly in Greek; Beekes allows a possible Pre-Greek origin for the Greek verb — note the debate: the PIE connection is the standard hypothesis but not beyond dispute). 2. Greek hairein (αἱρεῖν) "to take, seize, grasp," and in the middle voice haireisthai (αἱρεῖσθαι) "to take for oneself" → "to choose" (Homeric and classical Greek). 3. Greek hairesis (αἵρεσις) "a taking, a choosing; a chosen school of thought, a sect, a party" (classical Greek; the medical "sects" of the Hippocratics and of Galen were called haireseis). 4. Greek hairetikos (αἱρετικός) "able to choose; given to choosing for oneself; heterodox" (classical; in Christian writers "heretical"). 5. Late Latin haereticus "of a sect; heretical" (ecclesiastical Latin, from the early Christian centuries). 6. Old French eretique / heretique "heretic, heretical." 7. Middle English heretik "a heretic" (14th c.). 8. English heretic + Latinate suffix -al → HERETICAL (adjective, recorded from c. 1500) — OUR CARD. Notes: - The noun heresy travelled a parallel route: Latin haeresis "school of thought, sect" → Old French heresie → English heresy (c. 1225). - The Greek medical and philosophical sense of hairesis ("a chosen school") is not pejorative; the religious meaning of "false doctrine" is a Christian development of the word. - Debate to state explicitly: the ultimate link of Greek hairein to PIE *ser- "to seize" is the standard hypothesis (Pokorny, Etymonline) but Beekes allows a possible Pre-Greek origin; the derivation from haireisthai onward is entirely secure.
