• Territory: Similar because both denote bounded land under some control; differs because territory is the neutral, geographical word with no necessary implication of rule or ownership, while dominion carries the full sense of lordship and possession.
  • Realm: Similar because both mean a monarch's lands; differs because realm is romantic and feudal, usually a single kingdom under a king, while dominion stresses lordship and the aggregate of many lands under one rule.
  • Empire: Similar because both name vast territories under one sovereignty; differs because empire emphasizes the composite, hierarchical structure of many peoples under one imperial power, while dominion is the word for the territories themselves.
  • Colony: Similar because both are lands under a distant power; differs because a colony is specifically a body of settlers planted in new land and dependent on the mother country, while a dominion is a whole realm under one lordship, and the British Dominions were self-governing, not ruled, colonies.
  • Possession: Similar because both name land held by a power; differs because possession is the strictly colonial and legal term, suggesting property held at a distance and often disputed, while dominion is the broader word for sovereign territory.
  • Domain: Similar because both descend from the same Latin root and both name a sphere of authority; differs because domain is the everyday and modern doublet (a field of expertise, an internet address), while dominion survives in the formal and historical register.
