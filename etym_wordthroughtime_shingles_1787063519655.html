- 16th c. - pebble sense appears in dialectal English; the sound-word for stones that 'sing' as they shift; coastal and folkish.
- 17th-18th c. - "shingle beach/bank" established in nautical and geographical prose; the collective, weathered character sets.
- Separate track - roof-tile scindel (Old English) 'thin split piece of wood': by the 18th-19th c. 'shingled' roofs become an American vernacular icon.
- 14th-15th c. - the disease sense arrives from medieval Latin cingulus 'girdle'; plural in form, singular in use.
- 1920s - 'shingle' names the bobbed layered haircut, borrowing the roofing image.
- 21st c. - all three senses coexist; the vaccine era makes 'shingles' (disease) a household word, while the pebble sense lives on in literature and coastal place names.
