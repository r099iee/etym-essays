- [Vedic & Archaic Mythological Roots (c. 1200–800 BCE)] — the constituent *śarabha* originated in the Atharvaveda as an invincible eight-legged mountain beast, while Vedic ascetic lore developed the concept of severe physical penance (*tapas*) breaking or mastering bodily limbs (*aṅga*) — [perception: revered as symbols of untamable primal strength and yogic endurance]
- [Epic Rāmāyaṇa Period (c. 5th c. BCE–2nd c. CE)] — canonized in Vālmīki's Araṇyakāṇḍa as the exalted hermit *Śarabhaṅga* who postpones his heavenly ascent with Indra to see Rāma, dramatically renouncing his physical body in a consecrated fire — [perception: venerated as the supreme ideal of voluntary spiritual departure (*ichhā-mṛtyu*) and devotion to the avatāra]
- [Classical Sanskrit Golden Age (c. 4th–8th c. CE)] — celebrated in Kālidāsa's Raghuvaṃśa (Canto 13) and Purāṇic literature, where his blazing ascension was immortalized as the moment that inspired Rāma's solemn vow to protect the ascetics from demons — [perception: celebrated as the catalyst for the righteous war against demonic evil]
- [Medieval Vernacular Rāmāyaṇas (c. 12th–17th c. CE)] — beautifully dramatized in Kamban's Tamil epic and Tulsīdās's Rāmacaritamānasa, where the sage's self-immolation is depicted with intense devotional fervor (*bhakti*) as an act of ecstatic reunion with the supreme divine essence — [perception: honored as a shining saint whose total surrender of body in the fire revealed eternal spiritual youth]
- [Modern Philological & Indological Era (19th–21st c.)] — examined in critical editions of the Rāmāyaṇa (such as the Baroda Critical Edition and the Princeton Translation) for its complex manuscript variants (*Śarabhaṅga*, *Śarabhan̄ga*, *Śarabhan-*), while remaining an enduring symbol of yogic detachment across modern Hindu discourse — [perception: admired as a timeless exemplar of fearless mastery over life, death, and the physical elements]
