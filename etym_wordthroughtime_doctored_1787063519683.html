- 18th c. - Doctored first means “treated medically, cared for by a physician”; a homely, trusted word for receiving care.
- Early 19th c. - The participle extends to repair: a doctored boot, roof, or stew is patched up and made serviceable; the sense is still positive or neutral.
- Mid-late 19th c. - The dark turn: doctored food, drink, and accounts are adulterated or falsified; the word acquires its dishonest charge, especially for watered wine, doctored milk, and padded figures.
- 20th c. - Doctored photographs, films, and documents become the model case as media technology spreads; the verb also gains the polite veterinary sense “neutered”.
- 21st c. - Doctored is above all the word for digital falsification — doctored images, videos, and statistics — while the medical sense sounds dated and colloquial; the old healing and the new faking now seem like two different words.
