Linear chain for EGERIA (a mythonym: the underlying origin is debated; no secure PIE root is reconstructed): Stage 1 — Non-PIE (Italic/Etruscan substrate): theonym *Egeria (name of a local Italic water-/spring goddess, worshiped at the grove of Aricia near Lake Nemi and at a spring outside the Porta Capena at Rome). No attested earlier stage survives; the name is generally treated as pre-Latin substrate, likely influenced by Etruscan religious culture. Exact form and meaning at any deeper level cannot be reconstructed and are unknown. Stage 2 — Latin: Egeria (proper noun, theonym of the nymph and goddess; attested in Livy, Ovid, and Plutarch, 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE) "the nymph who counseled King Numa Pompilius; a divinity of springs, childbirth, and counsel." Stage 3 — Latin proper noun → French appellative: égérie (common noun, "female counselor, muse, inspiratrice"; the transferred use crystallized in French salon and literary culture of the 18th–19th centuries, applied to women such as Madame de Staël and Madame Récamier; modern French retains une égérie, "a muse, an iconic inspirational figure"). Stage 4 — French → English: Egeria (appellative, "a wise female adviser, a source of inspiration, an inspirer"; adopted in English learned and literary prose from the 18th century and fully established in the 19th) → Modern English EGERIA. Note on debate: the ancient folk etymologies connecting Egeria to Latin egere "to lack" or egredior/egerere "to come forth" (fitting a goddess of birth and flowing water) are popular explanations, not demonstrable derivations. A proposed connection to the PIE root *wreg- "to push, to work" is generally rejected. The name is best treated as a pre-Latin (likely Etruscan-influenced) substrate item whose deeper origin is unknown.
