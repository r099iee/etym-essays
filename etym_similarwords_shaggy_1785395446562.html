• Hairy: Similar because both mean covered with hair; differs because hairy is neutral and purely factual, merely possessing hair, with no sense of texture, length, or care, whereas shaggy specifies long, coarse, disordered hair.
  • Furry: Similar because both describe a dense covering of hair or fur; differs because furry implies softness, density, and pleasantness — a word of caress — while shaggy describes coarse, loose, unkempt growth.
  • Woolly: Similar because both describe a thick coat of animal fibre; differs because woolly means the fine, crimped fleece of a sheep — soft, warm, and curly — not the long, coarse, dry disorder of shag.
  • Bristly: Similar because both describe rough, protruding hair; differs because bristly implies short, stiff, prickly hairs standing upright (a day-old beard, a hedgehog's back), the opposite of shaggy's long, lank, dangling masses.
  • Unkempt: Similar because both describe untidy hair; differs because unkempt adds an overtone of personal neglect, dirt, and lack of care, whereas shaggy suggests healthy, natural abundance without contempt.
  • Dishevelled: Similar because both describe disordered hair; differs because dishevelled implies hair ruffled or disordered after the fact, as by wind or struggle, not naturally long and rough like shaggy.
  • Ragged: Similar because both describe a rough, irregular, worn appearance; differs because ragged applies to torn or frayed cloth, edges, or a person's clothes, not to hair or fur.
  • Hirsute: Similar because both mean covered with hair; differs because hirsute is the formal, scientific term for marked hairiness (in botany and zoology), without the texture, length, or affection of shaggy.
  • Fleecy: Similar because both describe a thick coat of hair or wool; differs because fleecy implies the soft, white, woolly coat of a sheep — clean, soft, and cloud-like — not the rough, matted, unkempt mass of shag.
