Proto-Semitic (reconstructed, c. 2500 BCE)
*ḥamm-
"father-in-law; kinsman"
↓
Amorite (c. 1900 BCE)
ḫammu
"kinsman, paternal uncle"
↓
Akkadian (c. 1792 BCE)
ḫa-am-mu-ra-pí
"Hammu heals" (syllabic spelling)
↓
Akkadian (c. 18th c. BCE)
Ḫammurabi
"the kinsman is a healer"
↓
Assyriological transliteration (19th c.)
Hammurabi
"Babylonian lawgiver-king"
↓
English (20th c.)
Hammurabi
"king of Babylon; his law code"

Note: the second element is disputed — usually Akkadian rāpi’u “healer” (root rpʾ), but a connection with rabû “great” or with a divine name has also been proposed. Amorite is known mainly from personal names, so the parse of ḫammu- as “kinsman” is probable rather than certain.
