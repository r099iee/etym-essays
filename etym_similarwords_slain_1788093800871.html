• Killed: Similar because both mean caused to die; differs because killed is the neutral, all-purpose term from police reports to biology, while slain is violent, deliberate and literary — always a blow, and usually battle or murder.
  • Murdered: Similar because both denote unlawful deliberate killing of a person; differs because murdered is a legal term with premeditation and illegality at issue, while slain has no legal content — dragons and enemies in war are slain, not murdered.
  • Slaughtered: Similar because both describe large-scale or pitiless killing; differs because slaughtering implies mass killing (or animal butchery) and evokes carnage, while slain can be a single heroic death.
  • Butchered: Similar because both describe killing with violence; differs because butchered emphasizes the cutting up of the body (or brutal messiness) and is pejorative, while slain is austere and dignified.
  • Massacred: Similar because both describe killing in numbers; differs because massacred implies the wholesale killing of the helpless, with horror, while slain is neutral as to numbers and carries epic dignity.
  • Dispatched: Similar because both mean to put to death; differs because dispatched is clinical, brisk, even bureaucratic (a job "seen to"), often of executions or animals, and lacks any grandeur.
  • Executed: Similar because both describe carrying out a death; differs because executed is judicial, ordered by law after sentence, while slain is extra-legal — battle, ambush, monster-slaying.
  • Assassinated: Similar because both describe a killing; differs because assassination is the targeted political murder of a named figure, while slain carries no motive and is not restricted to politics.
  • Felled / cut down: Similar because both mean struck down to the ground; differs because felled and cut down keep the tree-felling image and are used of persons abstractly or collectively, while slain names the killing itself.
  • Destroyed: Similar because both can describe utter ruin of a person or thing; differs because destroyed covers places, machines, arguments and hopes, while slain is restricted to living beings done to death.
  • Mowed down: Similar because both describe killing in numbers; differs because mowed down is colloquial, modern and mechanical (gunfire), while slain is archaic, noble and weapon-neutral.
  • Eliminated / liquidated: Similar because both are euphemistic verbs of killing; differs because eliminate and liquidate are cold bureaucratic slang for political or criminal killing — the exact opposite of slain's heroic openness.
  • Done in / finished: Similar because both are slang for killed; differs because done in and finished are colloquial and informal, while slain is the most formal and archaic of the kill-words.
  • Smitten: Similar because both come from the old verbs of striking (smitan, slēan) and mean struck down; differs because smitten survives chiefly as a participle of love or disease ("smitten with"), while slain never lost its lethal meaning.
