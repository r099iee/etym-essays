Biblical Mountain of the Covenant - Sinai is the proper name of the mountain where God gave the Law to Moses (Exodus 19-20), a Semitic toponym whose earliest English appearance in biblical tradition fixes it as the site of theophany and covenant.
English Transmission - Entering English around 1300 through the Vulgate and Anglo-Norman, Sinai becomes the standard name for the holy mountain, used in scripture and sermon for the place where a people heard the voice of God.
Miltonic & Literary Symbol - In Paradise Lost Milton pairs "Oreb, or of Sinai" as the true, superior rival to pagan Helicon — the site of the Spirit's inspiration of the lawgiver — making Sinai a symbol of prophetic and biblical inspiration.
Contemporary Sense - Today Sinai names the mountain, the wilderness, and the peninsula in geography and history, and stands figuratively for divine law, covenant, and dramatic theophany.
