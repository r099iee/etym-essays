- Old English: wægn / wain — farm cart, war chariot and stars (Charles's Wain); everyday and celestial.
- 1520s–1600s: Dutch wagon reborrowed — heavy continental military and freight vehicle; foreign, sturdy, modern.
- 1700s–1800s: American frontier icon — Conestoga, covered wagon, wagon train; freedom, hardship and expansion.
- 1840s–1900s: Railway goods wagon (BrE) — coal and freight trucks; industrial, working-class.
- 1950s–1980s: Station-wagon family car — suburban load-lugger; respectable, capacious, then unfashionable.
- Present: Heritage + idiom — historic wagons in museums and circle-the-wagons metaphors; on/off the wagon for drinking; literal farm wagons nostalgic.
