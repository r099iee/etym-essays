Kṛta (proper noun, adjective, and technical noun; Sanskrit कृत, past passive participle of the root kṛ "to do, make, accomplish, perform") — In Hindu cosmology, philosophy, literature, and mathematics, a highly significant term signifying "done, accomplished, completed, perfected," and specifically designating the first and most perfect of the four cosmic world ages (the Kṛta Yuga, also universally revered as the Satya Yuga or Golden Age). In ancient Indian culture, the term possesses profound polysemy, originating conceptually in the ancient Indian game of dice as the winning, supreme four-point throw, which served as the numerical and metaphorical paradigm for cosmic perfection and holistic righteousness across Vedic, Epic, Purāṇic, and philosophical systems.

1. Cosmological Sense — The Kṛta Yuga (The Primordial Golden Age of Truth). In the cyclical Hindu cosmological framework of cosmic epochs (Mahāyugas), the Kṛta Yuga is the inaugural and longest epoch, enduring for 1,728,000 solar years (or 4,800 divine years). It represents the absolute summit of moral purity, cosmic order, and human perfection. During this age, Dharma (the cosmic moral order and righteousness) is envisioned as a sacred bull standing completely balanced upon all four of its legs: truthfulness (satya), ascetic purity (tapas), compassionate mercy (dayā), and charity (dāna). In the Kṛta Yuga, humans live in spontaneous harmony with natural and cosmic laws: there are no diseases, physical deformities, lies, hatred, grief, or agricultural toil, as the earth yields bountiful sustenance without exploitation. There is no distinction of caste hierarchy, no commercial trade, no taxation, and no warfare. Longevity extends for thousands of years, and meditation (dhyāna) is the sole effortless means of spiritual communion with the Supreme.

2. Ludic and Numerological Origin — The Winning Throw of Four in Ancient Dice Games. The cosmological designation of the four yugas directly originated from the ancient Indian game of dice (akṣadyūta), played with the brown nuts of the vibhitaka tree. In this game, throws were calculated by multiples of four:

  • Kṛta designated the winning, perfect throw where all four dice fell face-up or the total number of dice was divisible by four with zero remainder, symbolizing wholeness, total victory, and completion.
  • The subsequent throws were named Tretā (three points / second best), Dvāpara (two points / compromised balance), and Kali (one point / the losing, ruined throw).
  • This ancient gaming terminology was elevated by Vedic and epic thinkers into the grand architecture of cosmic time, wherein the Kṛta Yuga represents four-quarters (4/4) of perfection, gradually declining to three-quarters in Tretā, two-quarters in Dvāpara, and only one quarter (1/4) in our current dark age of Kali.

3. Lexical and Grammatical Sense — Accomplished, Perfected, and Cultivated. In general Sanskrit morphology, kṛta functions as the past passive participle of kṛ, meaning "made, fashioned, performed, accomplished, cultivated, or prepared." It stands in fundamental philosophical contrast to akṛta ("unmade, uncreated, eternal, self-existent"), an essential epithet used in the Upaniṣads for the supreme absolute (Brahman) and the transcendental state of liberation (Mokṣa) which cannot be produced through physical action or ritual works.

4. Moral, Ethical, and Legal Idioms in Classical Literature. In Sanskrit ethical treatises (such as the Dharmaśāstras and Hitopadeśa), kṛta forms essential compounds defining character and virtue:

  • Kṛtajña (literally "knowing what has been done") — Grateful, mindful of benefits received, acknowledging past kindness.
  • Kṛtaghna (literally "destroying what has been done") — Ungrateful, malicious, a traitor to benefactors, considered one of the gravest spiritual sins in epic thought.
  • Sukṛta and Duṣkṛta — Righteous deeds / positive karmic merit, versus evil actions / sinful demerit.
  • Kṛtakṛtya ("having done what was to be done") — A person who has fulfilled all earthly obligations and attained supreme spiritual enlightenment and tranquility.

5. Mathematical and Astronomical System (Bhūtasaṅkhyā). In classical Indian mathematics, astronomy, and prosody, kṛta serves as a standard chronogrammatic word-numeral symbolizing the number four (4), based upon the four points of the winning die throw and the four pillars of the primordial yuga.

  • In Epic Literature (Rāmāyaṇa & Mahābhārata) — Invoked as the supreme benchmark of societal flourishing; an ideal reign (such as Rāmarājya) is consistently described as transforming the troubled present into the pristine flawlessness of the Kṛta Yuga, where fire, flood, theft, and early death vanish from the earth.
  • Semantic Bridge — The development from the verbal participle *kṛta* ("completely finished, accomplished") to the winning die throw of four, and subsequently to the Golden Age, reflects the ancient Indian metaphysical intuition that perfection consists in complete, unblemished wholeness and moral fulfillment.
