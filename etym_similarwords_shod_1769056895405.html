• Booted: Similar because both describe feet furnished with covering; differs because booted implies high, rigid, military or riding footwear and can never describe an animal — a horse is shod, never booted.
  • Footed: Similar because both concern the state of the feet; differs because footed describes how a creature walks or what its feet are like (sure-footed), not what covers them — a weak, mostly poetic near-synonym.
  • Sandaled: Similar because both mean wearing a foot-covering; differs because sandaled names one specific open, light covering and is narrower than the general shod, which spans shoes, boots, and horseshoes.
  • Covered: Similar because both imply a protecting layer over a surface; differs because covered is far too general — anything can be covered — and says nothing of feet or of being equipped for walking.
  • Sheathed: Similar because both image a thing wrapped or protected; differs because sheathed metaphorically pictures the foot as a blade in a scabbard and works only in figurative speech, losing shod's practical, completed-furnishing idea.
  • Clothed: Similar because both concern dress or covering; differs because clothed applies to the whole body and says nothing of the feet or of hard-travel readiness.
  • Equipped / Furnished: Similar because both mean provided with what is needed; differs because these are generic words of readiness, while shod is the foot- and hoof-specific member of the set, always pointing at a protective covering on the extremity.
  • Harnessed: Similar because both are used of a horse prepared for work; differs because harnessed concerns the pulling gear — traces, collar, bridle — whereas shod concerns the hooves; a horse can be shod without being harnessed.
  • Barefoot: The direct antonym; differs because barefoot denotes unprotected, earth-touching feet, the exact opposite of shod's crafted layer of separation from the ground.
