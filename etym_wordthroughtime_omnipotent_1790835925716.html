- c. 1300s-1400s (Middle English): Omnipotent entered from Old French and Latin as a supreme theological epithet for God Almighty in prayers and mystery plays.
- c. 1500s-1600s (Early Modern English): Flourished in Renaissance theology and epic poetry (Milton's Paradise Lost) to depict cosmic divine warfare and creation.
- c. 1700s-1800s (Enlightenment): Utilized in rational theology (Addison, Clarke) and political philosophy (parliamentary omnipotence in constitutional theory).
- c. 1900s-Present: Maintained as the definitive term in philosophy of religion (the omnipotence paradox) and figurative high-register prose.
