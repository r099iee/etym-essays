• Dragging: Similar because both mean pulling something along behind; differs because dragging emphasizes force and effort in moving a heavy object and carries no suggestion of provocation, whereas trailing adds continuous contact, a wake left behind, and in the idiom a studied intent to tempt opposition.
  • Towing: Similar because both describe drawing something along behind a moving thing; differs because towing implies a mechanical or deliberate coupling — a vehicle, boat, or rope under tension — while trailing is passive, loose, and often incidental.
  • Hauling: Similar because both involve drawing something along; differs because hauling stresses heavy lifting, great effort, and a destination, while trailing suggests something that simply follows in the wake without being borne.
  • Straggling: Similar because both describe spread-out motion behind a main body; differs because straggling implies disorder, falling behind, and lack of intent, while trailing can be deliberate, purposeful, and even provocative.
  • Dangling: Similar because both describe something hanging loosely; differs because dangling implies suspension and swaying without forward motion along a ground, lacking trailing's moving contact and wake.
  • Lagging: Similar because both can mean falling behind; differs because lagging stresses slowness or delay relative to a pace-setter, whereas trailing in competition implies a measurable deficit in a race or count.
  • Following: Similar because both can mean coming behind; differs because following is neutral and voluntary — going after someone — while trailing in the tracking sense is covert and in the physical sense describes what is pulled, not what chooses to come.
  • Sweeping: Similar because both can describe a long, drawn-out motion over a surface; differs because sweeping implies a broad, clean, deliberate arc, while trailing is narrow and passive, drawn along in the wake of something else.
  • Trolling: Similar because both derive from dragging (cf. trawl); differs because trolling and trawling are the deliberate dragging of a line or net through water, a technical act, whereas trailing is the general word for anything dragged behind.
  • Streaming: Similar because both describe something spread out in a long wake; differs because streaming implies a flowing, fluid, or continuous medium (hair in the wind, data transmitted live), while trailing implies contact with a surface or ground along the way.
