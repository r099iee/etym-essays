• threat: Similar because both name an indication or source of danger; differs because "threat" is the plain, everyday word for an expression of intent to harm, while "menace" adds a sense of looming, embodied, atmospheric danger.
  • danger: Similar because both can name something that can cause harm; differs because "danger" is a neutral, broad condition of risk, whereas "menace" is more active, personal, and threatening.
  • hazard: Similar because both name a source of potential harm; differs because "hazard" is a technical, environmental word (a hazard on the road), lacking menace's deliberate or looming quality.
  • peril: Similar because both name a serious danger; differs because "peril" is a lofty, literary word for grave risk, while "menace" usually involves a threatening agent.
  • intimidation: Similar because both involve frightening another; differs because "intimidation" names the act of bullying by fear, whereas "menace" can be a mere presence without action.
  • nuisance: Similar because both can name a troublesome person or thing; differs because "nuisance" is mild and legalistic (a public nuisance), whereas "menace" implies serious harm.
  • scourge: Similar because both name a person or thing causing widespread suffering; differs because "scourge" is archaic and biblical in register, implying punishment, while "menace" is modern and general.
  • plague: Similar because both can name a widespread affliction or danger; differs because "plague" is a concrete disease or pestilence metaphor, whereas "menace" stays abstract.
  • threaten (verb): Similar because both verbs mean to express or present a danger; differs because "threaten" is the common everyday verb, while "menace" as a verb is more literary and suggests a darker, looming threat.
  • endanger (verb): Similar because both verbs can mean to put at risk; differs because "endanger" is neutral and factual (it endangers lives), whereas "menace" implies an active, felt threat.
  • imperil (verb): Similar because both mean to put in danger; differs because "imperil" is formal and passive in feel, while "menace" suggests an agent doing the threatening.
  • terrify / intimidate (verbs): Similar because both aim to frighten; differs because they focus on the effect of fear, while "menace" focuses on the source or gesture of danger.
