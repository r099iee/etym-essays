• Bob: British slang for a shilling, exactly equivalent in value; differs because it is informal, affectionate and largely obsolete, whereas shilling is the neutral official term.
  • Penny: the shilling's twelfth part, a coin of the same older decimal-free world; differs because it names the smallest unit, not the middle-sized one, and its abbreviation d. marks it as the base of the system.
  • Sixpence: half a shilling and therefore directly convertible; differs because it names a specific coin (the tanner) rather than the accounting unit, and its value is precisely half.
  • Florin: worth two shillings; differs because it is a coin denomination named after a Florentine gold coin, not a unit of account, and it was introduced as a decimalisation experiment.
  • Half-crown: worth two shillings and sixpence, a familiar silver coin; differs because it is a compound denomination with no independent status in the reckoning system.
  • Crown: worth five shillings; differs in scale, being a commemorative rather than an everyday coin, and in register, sounding ceremonial.
  • Sovereign: worth twenty shillings and equal to one pound in gold; differs because it is a prestige bullion coin and the anchor of the gold standard, not small change.
  • Guinea: worth twenty-one shillings; differs because it was a professional and aristocratic unit of account used long after the coin stopped circulating, carrying a distinctly upper-class and archaic flavour.
  • Pound: twenty shillings and the parent unit of the whole system; differs in magnitude and in continuing official life, since the pound still exists while the shilling does not.
  • Quid: slang for a pound, the shilling's superior unit; differs in register and scale, being informal and twenty times larger.
  • Cent: the decimal subdivision of the modern Kenyan, Ugandan or Tanzanian shilling; differs because it belongs to a decimal system and is worth one hundredth of a shilling.
  • Schilling: the German and Austrian cognate, historically the same Germanic unit; differs because it is a foreign-language form with its own national coinages, including the Austrian currency replaced by the euro.
  • Skilling: the Scandinavian cognate, a small coin of Denmark, Norway and Sweden; differs in national context and in being a separate descendant rather than the English word.
  • Solidus: the late Roman gold coin whose name supplied the abbreviation s.; differs because it is a historical Latin term for a different, far more valuable coin, linked only through accounting convention.
  • Denarius: the Roman silver coin that gave the penny its d.; differs in that it is the ancestor of the penny's notation rather than of the shilling itself.
  • Coin: the general class to which the shilling belonged; differs because it names any stamped piece of metal money, without specifying value, nation or period.
  • Currency unit: the abstract category of the shilling's function; differs because it is a technical numismatic phrase rather than a name with a social and literary history.
