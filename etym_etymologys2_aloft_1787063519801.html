Proto-Germanic
(c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*luftuz
"air, sky; the upper space"
↓
Old Norse
(c. 800–1200 CE)
lopt
"air, sky; an upper room
or loft"
↓
Old Norse
(c. 800–1200 CE)
á lopt
"in the air, up on high"
↓
Middle English
(c. 1200)
aloft

↓
English
(modern)
aloft
"up in the air, high above

Note: the ultimate origin of *luftuz is debated. Some scholars link it to an Indo-European root meaning "to peel, strip" (the air as a "cladding" of the world), but no pre-Germanic form is securely attested; the safest statement is that the Germanic word is very old and its earlier ancestry is unknown.
