• Ambushed: Similar because both mean attacked by surprise from concealment; differs because an ambush is military or predatory and nearly always violent, while being waylaid may be a mere, unarmed interception, and waylaying specifically involves a route or path.
  • Intercepted: Similar because both mean stopped or caught on the way; differs because interception is the neutral, technical word for blocking passage (of messages, signals, or persons), while waylaid stresses stealth, concealment, and the deliberate choice of the road.
  • Accosted: Similar because both mean stopped and addressed or challenged unexpectedly; differs because accosting is direct and face-to-face, often in a public place, while waylaying is secretive, lying in wait, and implies a halted journey.
  • Cornered: Similar because both mean trapped so that escape is difficult; differs because cornering is the act of driving someone into a position of no escape, while waylaying is the act of lying in wait on their path — one is ambushed before movement, the other trapped after it.
  • Stopped / Detained: Similar because both mean prevented from proceeding; differs because 'stopped' and 'detained' are neutral, lacking waylaid's implication of concealment, surprise, and the deliberate ambush of a traveller.
  • Held up: Similar because both describe stopping a traveller or vehicle, often to rob it; differs because 'held up' is modern, colloquial, and usually implies armed robbery on the spot, while waylaid is older, broader, and need not involve a weapon or theft.
  • Mugged (colloquial): Similar because both describe robbing someone by force; differs because mugging is urban, sudden, and usually violent in the moment, while waylaying is premeditated lying-in-wait upon a route and has an older, more literary register.
