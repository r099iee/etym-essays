- [14th century church doctrine] - [Enters as the adjective of heresy — beliefs contrary to established church doctrine; connotation grave, carrying anathema, exile, and historically the fire]
- [16th century Reformation] - [Heresy becomes the central charge of religious conflict; 'heretical' is a weapon both sides hurl, connotation of mortal spiritual error]
- [18th-19th century secular extension] - [Ruskin calls his own aesthetic opinions 'heretical to the ordinary connoisseur', casting the art establishment as a secular church and himself as the man who questions its dogmas; the tone is defiant, ironic, faintly amused — the heresy is in the ear of the orthodox judge]
- [20th-21st century broadened provocation] - [Extended to any opinion that flouts the received truth of a community — artistic, political, scientific; connotation ranges from genuine scandal to playful provocation]
