• Remnants: Similar because both refer to remaining leftover parts after the greater portion has been consumed or removed; differs because remnants is a more formal, dignified term often applied to surviving fabrics, cultural traditions, or defeated armies, whereas scraps denotes small, irregular, disconnected, or discarded fragments.
  • Fragments: Similar because both denote broken or detached constituent parts; differs because fragments implies pieces broken off from a once-unified brittle structure (such as glass, pottery, or ancient manuscripts), whereas scraps applies naturally to flexible materials (paper, cloth, meat) or loose leftovers.
  • Morsels: Similar because both describe small quantities of food; differs because morsels emphasizes delicacy, delicious flavor, or choice small bites intended for appetizing enjoyment, whereas scraps conveys discarded leftovers, trimmings, or refuse fit for animals.
  • Crumbs: Similar because both denote minute food particles left behind after eating; differs because crumbs specifically refers to tiny baked grain particles (bread, cake, biscuits) crumbled during eating, whereas scraps encompasses meat bones, vegetable rinds, paper cuttings, and metal waste.
  • Shreds: Similar because both denote narrow, torn strips of material; differs because shreds emphasizes long, ragged, fibrous tears (as in shredded cabbage or paper), whereas scraps refers to broader, irregular remnants of any shape.
  • Ort / Orts: Similar because both denote food remnants left over after a meal; differs because orts is an archaic, dialectal term restricted strictly to feeding refuse, whereas scraps possesses wide industrial, metaphorical, and colloquial applicability.
  • Debris: Similar because both denote scattered discarded material; differs because debris is an uncount collective noun denoting extensive, widespread wreckage or rubble resulting from destruction or storm damage, rather than small individual remnants.
  • Skirmishes / Brawls: Similar because both can describe minor physical fights (in the colloquial noun sense of scraps); differs because skirmishes denotes formal minor military clashes, whereas a scrap is an informal, scrappy, unarmed fistfight or petty dispute.
