Ancient Greek place-name (3rd c. BCE)
Πέργαμον (Pergamon)
“the city of Pergamum in Asia Minor”
↓
Hellenistic Greek phrase (2nd c. BCE)
περγαμηνὴ (pergamēnē)
“(skin) of Pergamum; Pergamene”
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
(charta) pergamēna
“Pergamene (sheet); skin for writing”
↓
Late Latin, Medieval Latin (6th–12th c.)
pergamenum, parcamenum
“parchment skin; a deed”
↓
Old French (13th c.)
parchemin
“prepared skin; written deed”
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
parchemin, parchment
“writing skin; title-document”
↓
Modern English (plural)
parchments
“prepared skins; legal deeds”

Note: the familiar tale that Pergamum invented parchment under an Egyptian papyrus embargo is the traditional account repeated by Pliny; scholars nuance it — prepared skins long predate Pergamum, whose workshops perfected rather than invented the craft — and the intrusive -t in English parchment comes by association with words like pavement rather than from the Latin.
