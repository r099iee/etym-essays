• Aeschylus: Similar because Aeschylus is the earliest of the three great tragedians and the author of the Oresteia, sharing Sophocles' canon status in classical tragedy; differs because Aeschylus introduced the second actor and favoured vast, choral, theological drama, while Sophocles introduced the third actor and centred the drama on the individual hero.
  • Euripides: Similar because Euripides is the youngest of the three great tragedians and Sophocles' near-contemporary, the third pillar of the same canon; differs because Euripides is known for psychological realism, social critique, and darker questioning (Medea, The Bacchae), whereas Sophocles perfected the formal, ironic tragic structure of fate and hubris.
  • Aristophanes: Similar because Aristophanes was the leading Athenian dramatist of the same era and a contemporary of Sophocles; differs because Aristophanes wrote Old Comedy — satirical, fantastic, and obscene — while Sophocles wrote tragedy.
  • Oedipus: Similar because Oedipus is the tragic hero of Sophocles' most famous play, Oedipus Rex, and of the Theban cycle; differs because Oedipus is a character of myth, while Sophocles is the playwright who shaped the myth into the canonical drama.
  • Antigone / Electra: Similar because these are the heroines of Sophocles' surviving tragedies and the titles of his plays; differs because they are characters and play-titles, while Sophocles is their author.
  • Tragedian / playwright: Similar because Sophocles was a tragedian and a playwright; differs because these are the general names of the professions, while Sophocles is the specific canonical exemplar of classical tragedy.
  • Sophoclean (adjective): Similar because the adjective derives from his name and denotes his style or themes; differs because Sophoclean modifies art or fate (dramatic irony, hamartia, hubris), while Sophocles is the historical person.
  • Dramatic irony: Similar because Sophocles is the acknowledged master of dramatic irony, the device whereby the audience knows more than the hero; differs because dramatic irony is a technique used by many dramatists before and after, whereas Sophocles is the specific master credited with perfecting it in the Oedipus plays.
