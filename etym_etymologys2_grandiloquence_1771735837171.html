This word joins TWO independent Latin roots. Each root gets its own linear chain; they merge only in the compound grandiloquus. CHAIN 1 — the "grandis" ingredient: Latin grandis "great, grand, full-grown" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE, Plautus) ├── pre-Latin origin UNCERTAIN: no secure PIE etymon is reconstructed; the OED marks the origin obscure │ └── merges with CHAIN 2 at the compound grandiloquus "speaking in a grand style" (grandis + loquī), the educated Latin of imperial Rome │ └── Late Latin grandiloquentia "the quality of grand speaking" (abstract noun from grandiloquus) │ └── English grandiloquence (borrowed via French grandiloquence, late 16th c., first attested c. 1580; adjective grandiloquent mid-17th c.) NOTE (debate): no PIE etymon for grandis can be established with confidence; the chain therefore begins at Latin. The transmission of the noun into English through French is the standard account. CHAIN 2 — the "loquī" ingredient: Latin loquor, loquī "to speak" (origin uncertain; possibly sound-symbolic; no secure PIE etymon is agreed upon) ├── grandis + loquī → grandiloquus "speaking in a grand style" │ └── Late Latin grandiloquentia "the quality of grand speaking" │ └── English grandiloquence (late 16th c., via French) NOTE (debate): the origin of loquī is disputed; some scholars suspect sound symbolism in its repeated liquid consonants, but no reliable PIE etymon is agreed upon, so the chain begins at Latin. The two roots (grandis and loquī) share no common Indo-European ancestor within this word, so no single master root is invented.
