• you: Similar because both function as second-person pronouns referring to the person addressed; differs because you is the universal modern standard pronoun used for singular and plural, nominative and objective, lacking the specific singular, archaic, intimate, or liturgical qualities of thee.
  • thou: Similar because both belong to the same historical second-person singular pronominal paradigm; differs because thou is strictly the nominative (subject) form ("thou art"), whereas thee is the objective (accusative/dative) form ("I love thee").
  • ye: Similar because both are archaic second-person English pronouns; differs because ye originally served as the second-person plural nominative pronoun ("hear ye") and later formal singular, whereas thee is strictly singular and objective.
  • thine: Similar because both are second-person singular pronominal forms sharing the same Germanic root; differs because thine functions as an absolute possessive pronoun ("this is thine") or possessive determiner before vowel sounds ("thine eyes"), indicating ownership rather than objective grammatical relation.
  • thy: Similar because both share the same second-person singular root paradigm; differs because thy functions exclusively as a pre-nominal possessive adjective ("thy father") modifying a noun, whereas thee functions independently as a pronoun object.
  • yourself: Similar because both can refer to the addressee in an objective or reflexive relation; differs because yourself is a modern compound reflexive/emphatic pronoun, whereas thee is a simple personal pronoun.
  • oneself: Similar because both represent pronominal objects in discourse; differs because oneself is an impersonal, third-person generalized reflexive pronoun, whereas thee is specific, second-person, and personal.
