PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*weh₂dh-
‘to go, advance, stride’
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
vadere
‘to go, rush, walk’
↓
Latin compound (classical)
evadere
‘to go out, escape’
↓
Late Latin (3rd–4th c.)
evasio
‘an escape, a getting out’
↓
French (14th c.)
evasion
‘escape, subterfuge’
↓
English (1620s–1720s)
evasion, evasive
‘dodging answer, tending to evade’
↓
Modern English (19th c., adverb)
evasively
‘in a manner that avoids the point’

Note: the deeper PIE parent of Latin vadere is given by de Vaan as *weh₂dh- with some uncertainty, and the English adjective evasive is a transparent 18th-century formation from evasion plus -ive, with -ly added for the adverb, so the modern tail of the chain is morphological rather than borrowed.
