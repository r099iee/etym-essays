• Kabandha: Similar because both are monstrous, bizarre Daṇḍaka demons slain by Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa who are revealed upon death to be cursed celestial beings (gandharvas) requiring specific funerary rites (burial for Virādha, cremation for Kabandha) to regain their radiant heavenly bodies; differs because Virādha is a towering, weapon-wielding giant who snatches Sītā and is buried alive, whereas Kabandha is a headless monster whose face is embedded in his chest and whose eye is in his belly.
  • Khara: Similar because both are ferocious rākṣasas inhabiting the Daṇḍaka forest who attack Rāma; differs because Khara is a demonic prince, brother of Rāvaṇa and commander of an army of 14,000 demons at Janasthāna who fights with conventional warfare, whereas Virādha is an isolated solitary giant and cursed gandharva possessing a mystical boon of invulnerability.
  • Dūṣaṇa: Similar because both are demonic terrors encountered by Rāma in the wilderness; differs because Dūṣaṇa is Khara's chief general who leads the demonic assault against Rāma at Janasthāna and is slain by arrows, while Virādha operates alone and must be buried alive due to Brahmā's boon.
  • Mārīca: Similar because both are rākṣasas who attempt to separate Sītā from Rāma in the forest; differs because Mārīca is a deceptive sorcerer who assumes the form of a golden deer (māyā-mṛga) to lure Rāma away, whereas Virādha uses direct, brutal physical violence to snatch Sītā right before Rāma's eyes.
  • Kumbhakarṇa: Similar because both are rākṣasas of colossal, gargantuan proportions who devour immense amounts of meat and strike terror by their physical size; differs because Kumbhakarṇa is the noble, slumbering titan brother of King Rāvaṇa who fights in the great war of Laṅkā, whereas Virādha is a forest cannibal encountered early in the exile.
  • Tāṭakā: Similar because both are demonic forest obstacles encountered by Rāma early in a narrative kāṇḍa; differs because Tāṭakā is a yakṣī turned demoness slain by young Rāma in the Bālakāṇḍa on the orders of Viśvāmitra, whereas Virādha is slain by mature Rāma in the Araṇyakāṇḍa to defend his wife.
  • rākṣasa: Similar because Virādha physically lives and acts as a member of the rākṣasa race; differs because rākṣasa is the broad generic taxon for all demonic nocturnal beings in Hindu mythology, whereas Virādha is an individual proper character with a distinct backstory as the cursed gandharva Tumburu.
  • Tumburu: Similar because Tumburu is the true celestial soul imprisoned within Virādha's demonic body; differs because Tumburu is the immortal, sweet-singing gandharva courtier of Kubera, whereas Virādha is the degraded, man-eating physical monster caused by Kubera's curse.
  • Baka: Similar because both are man-eating forest giants slain by epic heroes; differs because Baka (Bakasura) is a monster in the Mahābhārata slain by the Pāṇḍava hero Bhīma to protect the villagers of Ekacakrā, whereas Virādha belongs to the Rāmāyaṇa cycle.
  • asura: Similar because both are dangerous supernatural enemies of cosmic righteousness; differs because asuras are cosmic titans and step-brothers of the gods fighting for dominion of the heavens, whereas Virādha is an earthly forest demon stalking travelers in the woods.
