• Mortar: Similar because pestle and mortar are the inseparable grinding pair, always named together; differs because the mortar is the bowl that holds while the pestle is the club that strikes.
  • Pounder: Similar because both crush by repeated downward blows; differs because pounder is the generic word for any pounding thing, while pestle is the specific hand implement shaped for bowl-work.
  • Mallet: Similar because both are heavy hand clubs for striking; differs because a mallet drives chisels and stakes with flat-face blows, while a pestle crushes and grinds inside a cup.
  • Muddler: Similar because both bruise aromatics in a vessel, the bartender's tool and the cook's; differs because a muddler presses mint and fruit gently in a glass, while a pestle pounds hard solids to powder.
  • Grinder: Similar because both reduce solids to powder; differs because grinder suggests rotary mills and machines, while pestle is purely the hand club and its circular work.
  • Rolling pin: Similar because both are hand tools that crush and flatten food by pressure; differs because the rolling pin rolls dough flat on a board, while the pestle pounds downward in a bowl.
  • Millstone: Similar because both grind grain and spice between hard stones; differs because the millstone is the great paired wheel of the mill, while the pestle is its small hand-held cousin.
  • Hammer: Similar because both strike with weight; differs because a hammer drives nails with a hard flat face, while a pestle crushes soft matter with a rounded grinding head.
  • Club: Similar because a pestle is literally a small club; differs because club is the weapon or cudgel for hitting out, while pestle is the domesticated club turned to cookery.
