- The old seed (Old English compounds) - the sea-folk enter English one maiden at a time: mermaid (OE mere + mægden) and its rare male counterpart merman - singular, romantic figures, each a curiosity of folklore.
- 18th–19th c. - the collective idea forms: merfolk and sea-people appear in folklore writing, and the mer- prefix begins to generalise from a single sea-maiden into a whole species.
- 20th c. - merpeople crystallises as a modern, gender-neutral, collective coinage (back-formed from mermaid and merman via merperson) in fantasy and children's literature; the plural people makes them a society with songs and customs, not curiosities.
- 20th–21st c. - merpeople becomes the standard word for the sea-folk as a singing nation, clearly set against the dangerous sirens of myth; its transparency - simply 'sea-people' - means a reader needs no gloss to hear the ocean made conscious and harmonious.
