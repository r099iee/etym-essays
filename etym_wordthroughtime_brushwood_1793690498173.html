- Late 14th/Early 15th Century - First attested as a compound, referring to dense, low-lying woody growth or collected small branches. - Connotation: Practical utility as fuel and building material; associated with wild, untamed landscapes.
- 16th-19th Century - Common term in rural life and literature for undergrowth and firewood. - Connotation: Continued practical importance, often associated with humble living or poverty (gathering brushwood for fuel); also a symbol of natural wildness and impediment.
- 20th Century - Retains primary meaning in ecological and forestry contexts; develops metaphorical uses for minor obstacles or complexities. - Connotation: Still practical, but also used to describe challenges or distractions; maintains association with natural, uncultivated areas.
- Contemporary Usage - Standard term for small woody vegetation or collected debris; used in environmental management, landscaping, and metaphorically. - Connotation: Neutral, descriptive in literal sense; slightly negative when used metaphorically for impediments or trivial matters.
