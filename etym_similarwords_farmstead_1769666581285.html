• Homestead: Similar because both name a farmhouse with its land and outbuildings; differs because homestead emphasizes the family dwelling and settled home, while farmstead emphasizes the whole agricultural working unit of buildings.
  • Farm: Similar because both refer to land used for agriculture; differs because farm names the land and business itself, while farmstead specifically denotes the buildings and occupied site.
  • Grange: Similar because both name a country farming estate with buildings; differs because grange historically carried monastic or noble granary associations (Latin granica), while farmstead is a plain, general term.
  • Croft: Similar because both name a small farm with its land; differs because a croft is specifically a small Scottish or northern-English enclosed holding, often tenanted.
  • Smallholding: Similar because both name a farm unit; differs because smallholding stresses the small size, while farmstead stresses the building cluster.
  • Steading: Similar because both derive from stede and name the farm buildings; differs because steading is chiefly Scottish and often excludes the house, while farmstead includes it.
  • Ranch: Similar because both name a livestock farm with buildings; differs because ranch is the North American term for an extensive cattle or horse operation (from Spanish rancho), while farmstead is a general, smaller-scale term.
  • Plantation: Similar because both name a large agricultural estate; differs because plantation historically means a large commercial crop estate (often with coercive labour history), while farmstead is a family-scale unit.
  • Acreage: Similar because both name a piece of rural land; differs because acreage names only the extent of land, not the buildings.
  • Estate: Similar because both name a large holding of land; differs because estate implies a grand property with mansion, while farmstead is modest and agricultural.
  • Farmyard: Similar because both belong to a farm; differs because farmyard names the yard between the buildings, not the whole complex.
