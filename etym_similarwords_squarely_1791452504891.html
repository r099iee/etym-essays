• Directly: the closest general synonym, meaning straight and without intermediary; differs because it can also mean "immediately" or "in a direct line", whereas squarely adds a note of firmness and moral straightness.
  • Straight: shares the idea of without deviation or evasion; differs because it is more colloquial and can mean heterosexual or undiluted, senses squarely lacks.
  • Frankly: similar in the sense of speaking plainly; differs because it concerns speech and candour rather than position, responsibility or physical alignment.
  • Honestly: shares the moral sense of fair dealing; differs because it names truthfulness as a virtue, while squarely describes the manner of an action.
  • Fairly: close in the moral sense; differs because it stresses justice and impartiality rather than directness or firmness.
  • Bluntly: similar in directness of speech; differs because it implies a lack of tact bordering on rudeness, which squarely does not.
  • Forthrightly: shares the combination of directness and honesty; differs because it is more formal and more clearly about speech.
  • Unequivocally: similar in the sense of leaving no doubt; differs because it is abstract and legalistic, with no physical or geometric image.
  • Point-blank: similar in directness, especially of a refusal or a question; differs because it is more abrupt and idiomatic.
  • Firmly: shares the sense of stability and decision; differs because it concerns strength of hold or resolve, not alignment or honesty.
  • Exactly: shares the positional sense of precision; differs because it is purely quantitative and carries no moral colour.
  • Precisely: close to exactly; differs in being more formal and in lacking the sense of physical squareness.
  • Dead (as in dead centre): colloquial for exactly; differs in register and in being restricted to position.
  • Flatly: similar in the sense of a blunt refusal; differs because it suggests a level, uninflected tone rather than upright honesty.
  • Openly: shares the absence of concealment; differs because it concerns visibility rather than directness or fairness.
  • Candidly: similar to frankly; differs because it emphasises the speaker's willingness to reveal, not the firmness of the action.
  • Justly: shares the moral sense of fairness; differs because it is a legal and ethical term, more formal than squarely.
  • Equitably: the technical term for fair dealing; differs in being confined to law and administration.
  • Four-square: an archaic emphatic cousin meaning solid and unyielding; differs in being literary and much rarer.
