Part 1 — WHIRL:
↓
PIE (uncertain)
*kʷerp-
"to turn"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*hwerbaną
"to turn; to whirl"
↓
Old Norse (Viking Age)
hvirfla
"to whirl"
↓
English (13th c.)
whirl
"to turn or spin rapidly round"


Part 2 — GIG:
↓
Middle English (15th c.)
gigge
"a spinning top"
↓
English (16th c.)
gig
"a whipping-top; a whirligig"


Compound — WHIRL + GIG:
↓
English (15th c.)
whirligig
"a toy that spins round; a spinning device"
↓
English (present)
whirligigs
"toys that spin round; objects that whirl"

Debate: gig is of uncertain, possibly echoic origin; the link of whirl to PIE *kʷerp- is uncertain; and whirligig is a reduplicative compound of whirl + gig.
