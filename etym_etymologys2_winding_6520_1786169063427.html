PIE *wendʰ- (to turn, wind, weave, bend) [earliest known stage]
├── Proto-Germanic: *windaną (strong verb: to turn, twist, coil; class 3 ablaut)
│ └── Old English: windan (to turn, twist, plait, weave; strong verb windan-wand-wunden)
│ └── Middle English: winden (to wind, twist, coil)
│ └── English: wind (to coil, turn, move in a twisting course)
│ └── winding (present participle, gerund, and participial adjective) ◄ TARGET
├── Cognates of *wendʰ-: Latin viere (to bend, weave) and vincire (to bind); Lithuanian výti (to twist, wind); Old Church Slavonic veno (wreath); Old Irish fet (chain); Tocharian B wänt (turban); Sanskrit vandhura- (curved, bent; the curved frame of a chariot seat, cited on this card as vehicle seat frame)
└── Note 1: The noun wind (moving air) descends from a DIFFERENT PIE root, *h₂weh₁- (to blow), and is entirely unrelated to the coiling verb; the participle winding belongs only to the turn-verb.
└── Note 2: The exact ablaut grade and laryngeal configuration of the PIE root are matters of minor scholarly debate, but the Germanic chain *windaną → windan → winden → wind is historically secure.
