• Kosi: Similar because both represent spiritual entities originating from a deceased Trobriand individual; differs because a kosi is a fleeting, earthbound, malevolent phantom that lingers near the burial site for only a few days before evaporating forever, whereas the baloma is the immortal, revered ancestral soul that journeys to Tuma and eternally reincarnates into the maternal lineage.
  • Shade / Manes: Similar because both denote departed ancestral spirits residing in a subterranean or overseas underworld who demand respect, offerings, and ritual remembrance from living kin; differs because Roman manes or classical Greek shades belong to Western Mediterranean antiquity and do not undergo the specific cyclic skin-sloughing rejuvenation into an embryonic spirit-child (*waiwaia*) that causes human conception.
  • Atman: Similar because both characterize an immortal, permanent spiritual essence that survives the death of the biological body and undergoes cyclical reincarnation; differs because atman in Hindu metaphysics is an abstract, universal soul bound to the cosmic law of karma and spiritual liberation (*moksha*), whereas baloma operates strictly within local Melanesian clan geography and ancestral lineage continuity without moral retribution.
  • Waiwaia: Similar because both represent stages of the exact same spiritual entity in Trobriand eschatology; differs because baloma is the mature, adult ancestral spirit dwelling on Tuma, whereas waiwaia refers specifically to the tiny, rejuvenated spirit embryo produced after the baloma bathes in the sea and sloughs its aged skin, ready to enter a woman's womb.
  • Anima: Similar because both signify the vital animating principle or soul that gives life to a human being; differs because anima is a Western philosophical and theological construct linked to breath, intellect, and Christian salvation, whereas baloma is fundamentally anchored in matrilineal clan reproduction, seaside bathing, and harvest festivities.
  • Apparition / Wraith: Similar because both convey the visible or invisible post-mortem manifestation of a deceased person; differs because an apparition is an anomalous, terrifying specter in folklore, whereas a baloma is a normal, structurally essential, and honored member of the extended Trobriand societal community.
