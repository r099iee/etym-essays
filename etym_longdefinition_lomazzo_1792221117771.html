Lomazzo (/ləˈmæt.soʊ/, Italian: [loˈmattso]) is an Italian historical proper noun, habitational surname, and art-historical descriptor designating Gian Paolo Lomazzo (1538–1592), the Milanese painter and preeminent art theorist of late Italian Mannerism. Struck by total, irreversible blindness at the height of his career in 1571 at the age of thirty-three, Lomazzo redirected his creative energies from visual painting to encyclopedic theoretical writing, producing the most comprehensive, complex, and philosophically ambitious codifications of artistic theory, academic pedagogy, emotional psychology, and astrological mysticism in the late Renaissance.

The multifaceted significance of Lomazzo unfolds across several pivotal intellectual domains:

  • Author of the *Trattato dell'arte della pittura* (1584): Lomazzo's monumental seven-book *Treatise on the Art of Painting, Sculpture, and Architecture* is the largest, most exhaustive art-theoretical encyclopedia produced during the sixteenth century. Across its extensive chapters, Lomazzo systematically codified every aspect of visual art into a rigorous academic curriculum: Book I covers Proportion (synthesizing Albrecht Dürer and classical harmonic ratios); Book II analyzes Motion (*moti*) and emotional expression; Book III addresses Color and pigment properties; Book IV explores Light and optical illumination; Book V codifies Linear Perspective; Book VI details Practical Composition and iconographical decorum; and Book VII catalogues History Painting, mythology, and religious genres.
  • The Doctrine of *Moti* (Physical Movement as Spiritual Emotion): Deeply indebted to Leonardo da Vinci's investigations into *i moti mentali* (the motions of the mind), Lomazzo formalized the doctrine that every internal human emotion (grief, terror, rage, ecstasy, devotion) produces an exact, mathematically determinable physical configuration of limbs, facial muscles, and bodily posture. His systematic classification of expressive gestures became the foundational pedagogical textbook for subsequent seventeenth-century academic theorists, most notably Charles Le Brun in France.
  • The Cosmological Architecture of the *Idea del tempio della pittura* (1590): In his second great work, Lomazzo synthesized Mannerist art theory with Marsilio Ficino's Neoplatonism, Renaissance Hermeticism, and planetary astrology. He conceptualized painting as a colossal sacred temple supported by seven monumental pillars, each governed by a planetary deity and personified by one supreme Renaissance master:
    • Michelangelo: Governed by Saturn (representing profound melancholia, intellectual depth, and monumental anatomical design).
    • Gaudenzio Ferrari: Governed by Jupiter (representing divine majesty, spiritual piety, and narrative grandeur).
    • Polidoro da Caravaggio: Governed by Mars (representing martial vigor, bold invention, and heroic Roman chiaroscuro).
    • Leonardo da Vinci: Governed by the Sun and Saturn (representing universal illumination, anatomical clarity, and optical wisdom).
    • Raphael: Governed by Venus (representing grace, harmonious proportion, pleasantness, and compositional balance).
    • Andrea Mantegna: Governed by Mercury (representing intellectual sharpness, archaeological erudition, and linear precision).
    • Titian: Governed by the Moon (representing luminous color, moist optical radiance, and naturalistic verisimilitude).
  • The Primary Conduit for Leonardo's Lost Writings: Because Lomazzo worked in Milan and studied directly under Giovan Battista della Cerva (pupil of Gaudenzio Ferrari, who in turn emerged from the Leonardo circle), he had direct access to Leonardo da Vinci's original studio notebooks, treatises, and oral studio traditions. Lomazzo's treatises preserve invaluable quotes, technical observations on sfumato and optics, and personal anecdotes regarding Leonardo that would otherwise have been lost to history.
  • Head of the Counter-Cultural *Accademia dei Facchini della Val di Blenio*: In sharp contrast to his solemn academic writing, Lomazzo presided as *abbà* (abbot) over a secretive, carnivalesque society of Milanese artists, musicians, and poets who spoke and wrote in the grotesque, rustic dialect of the Ticinese porters of the Blenio Valley (*Rime dei Facchini*). This dual identity reveals Lomazzo as a complex Renaissance figure navigating both high Counter-Reformation academic dogmatism and subversive, Dionysian popular culture.
  • Toponymic Origin: Beyond the historical theorist, *Lomazzo* is an ancient toponym designating a historic town located in the province of Como, Lombardy, from which the noble Lomazzo family originally migrated to Milan.

The historical bridge connecting Lomazzo to modern art history was forged through his international transmission. In 1598, Oxford scholar Richard Haydocke translated Lomazzo's *Trattato* into English as *A Tracte Containing the Artes of Curious Paintinge, Carvinge, and Buildinge*, directly inspiring the iconic Elizabethan miniaturist Nicholas Hilliard and establishing Renaissance art theory in the British Isles. In the twentieth century, seminal art historians Erwin Panofsky (*Idea*, 1924) and Rudolf and Margot Wittkower (*Born Under Saturn*, 1963) demonstrated that Lomazzo was the first thinker in European history to link an artist's personal psychology and planetary temperament directly to their individual visual style.
