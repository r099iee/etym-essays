• hurried: Similar because both describe moving or acting quickly; differs because hurried suggests flustered, disorderly speed and possible carelessness, while hastened implies deliberate, purposeful urgency.
  • rushed: Similar because both describe acting with speed; differs because rushed emphasizes being pressed for time and often sloppy haste, while hastened stresses intentional acceleration toward a goal.
  • quickened: Similar because both involve an increase of speed; differs because quickened is more neutral and often internal (a quickened pulse or interest), while hastened describes outward movement or bringing an event about sooner.
  • expedited: Similar because both make something happen sooner; differs because expedited is a formal, official term for speeding up a process or task, while hastened is general and applies to any action or event.
  • accelerated: Similar because both mean increased speed; differs because accelerated is technical and rate-focused, while hastened adds human urgency and purpose.
  • urged: Similar because both push toward action; differs because urged is purely verbal persuasion of another person, while hastened is the actual rapid movement or acceleration itself.
  • sprinted: Similar because both describe fast movement; differs because sprinted means running at top speed over a short distance, while hastened is rapid but not necessarily running.
  • dashed: Similar because both describe quick movement; differs because dashed suggests a sudden, impulsive, short burst, while hastened implies sustained urgent speed with purpose.
  • precipitated: Similar because both cause something to happen sooner; differs because precipitated suggests triggering suddenly and often with force or catastrophe, while hastened is gentler and more deliberate.
  • foreshortened: Similar because both compress time; differs because foreshortened is artistic or technical (to shorten in time or perspective), while hastened is about acting quickly or accelerating an event.
  • barreled: Similar because both describe rapid purposeful motion; differs because barreled is informal and implies forceful, headlong speed, while hastened is formal and controlled.
