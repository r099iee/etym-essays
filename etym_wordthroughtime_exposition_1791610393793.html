14th c. — Middle English ecclesiastical adoption: entered English (e.g., Chaucer, Wycliffite tracts) primarily as the scholarly interpretation and theological glossing of sacred Scripture and canonical law.
- 15th–16th c. — Humanist and educational expansion: broadened to encompass any formal, methodical discourse, treatise, or lecture that laid out the principles of a secular science, rhetoric, or grammar.
- 17th–18th c. — Systematic philosophy and legal codification: crystallized as the gold standard for methodical, rigorous presentation of philosophical systems (e.g., Locke, Hume) and constitutional or statutory laws.
- 19th c. — Industrial spectacles and musical formalism: dramatic expansion of scope; applied to colossal global trade fairs (giving birth to the international 'expo'), and formalized in musicology for the opening theme-stating section of classical sonata form.
- 20th–21st c. — Screenwriting and narrative critique: heavily specialized in cinematic, television, and literary criticism, where 'exposition' often carries a critical nuance regarding how clumsily or elegantly background narrative lore is delivered to the audience.
