- [Vedic Period, c. 1500–800 BCE; Ṛgveda & Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa] — celebrated as the son of Vivasvān, the first sacrificer to establish ritual fire, and the sole righteous survivor of the cosmic deluge guided by the divine Fish (*Matsya*) — [perception: revered as the ultimate human ancestor, the primal priest, and the father of human worship]
- [Epic Period, c. 500 BCE–200 CE; Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa & Mahābhārata] — immortalized as the royal architect-founder of the imperial city of Ayodhyā, father of King Ikṣvāku, and fountainhead of both the Solar and Lunar royal dynasties — [perception: celebrated as the heroic sovereign archetype and the celestial builder of civilization]
- [Classical Dharmaśāstra Era, c. 200 BCE–1000 CE] — codified as the supreme cosmic lawgiver through the *Mānava Dharmaśāstra* (*Manusmṛti*), setting forth the timeless principles of socio-religious duties (*dharma*), kingly governance, and cosmic cycles (*Manvantaras*) — [perception: regarded across the subcontinent as the infallible legal authority on cosmic and social order]
- [Modern & Contemporary Global Era, 19th–21st c.] — analyzed in comparative Indo-European mythology alongside Germanic *Mannus* and biblical Noah; subject to intense modern sociological and political debate regarding the caste ordinances of the *Manusmṛti*, while remaining linguistically immortalized in the everyday Hindi/Sanskrit word for human beings (*mānava*) — [perception: complex civilizational figure representing ancient humanity's earliest reflections on cosmogony, law, and human identity]
