• remainder: Similar because both mean what is left after the rest goes; differs because "remainder" is the neutral, arithmetic word (also the leftover number in division), with no pathos and no cloth-trade sense.
  • rest: Similar because both denote what is left after part is taken; differs because "the rest" is broad, neutral, everyday and usually the larger part, whereas "remnant" is small and grave.
  • residue: Similar because both name leftover matter; differs because "residue" is scientific or chemical — deposits after processing — with no human or historical dimension.
  • leftovers: Similar because both name what remains, especially food; differs because "leftovers" is colloquial, domestic and homely.
  • leavings: Similar because both name what is left behind; differs because "leavings" is archaic or literary and pejorative — worthless scraps others have thrown away.
  • vestige: Similar because both are a surviving trace of something gone; differs because "vestige" is more abstract and biological (vestigial organs), implying a mark or organ, not a counted remainder.
  • trace: Similar because both are a small survival; differs because "trace" is tiny, often intangible, and lacks the sense of a numbered group or weighed portion.
  • fragment: Similar because both are a piece of a broken whole; differs because "fragment" emphasizes breakage, while "remnant" emphasizes what was not taken away.
  • scrap: Similar because both are small leftover pieces (especially of cloth); differs because "scrap" is colloquial, vaguer and implies near-worthlessness.
  • offcut: Similar because both name a piece left after cutting material; differs because "offcut" is modern industrial trade jargon for any cut remnant.
  • relic: Similar because both are survivals from a vanished whole; differs because "relic" implies age and veneration — a museum object, not a usable remainder.
  • survivor(s): Similar because both name those who remain after destruction; differs because "survivor" applies to living individuals and emphasizes the ordeal, while "remnant" is collective and often impersonal.
  • rump: Similar because both name the reduced residue of a larger body (e.g. a "rump parliament"); differs because "rump" is pejorative and institutional — a leftover legislature, not a people or fabric.
  • dregs: Similar because both name the last undesirable remainder; differs because "dregs" is strongly pejorative sediment-imagery (of liquids, or of society).
