- Early 15th-century sense - meant "lacking strength, weak, infirm" of persons; a pitiable physical weakness rather than a property of things.
- 16th-17th-century shift - "awkward, clumsy, hard to manage"; now applied to actions and objects, taking on a critical, mildly contemptuous tone.
- 18th-19th century - "bulky, heavy, ponderous to handle"; a practical complaint about physical objects such as furniture and books.
- Modern sense - "too large, heavy, or awkwardly shaped to be easily handled, moved, or managed"; now extends metaphorically to arguments, methods, and whole organizations, in a formal, dryly disparaging register.
