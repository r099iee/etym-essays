OREB (proper noun; standard form Horeb). Milton's spelling of Horeb, the biblical name of the mountain of God in the wilderness — the desert mount where Moses saw the burning bush, struck the rock for water, and where Elijah heard the still, small voice (Exodus 3; 17; 1 Kings 19). Milton's "That on the secret top Of Oreb, or of Sinai, didst inspire" (Paradise Lost, Book I, 1667) uses Oreb as the poetic, Vulgate-derived form, pairing it with Sinai as the twin names of the one holy mountain. As a proper noun its polysemy is a growth of reference from a dry mountain to a synonym of Sinai, together with a rich load of theological and literary association. Senses and uses:
  • The dry mountain (etymological and topographical sense). The Hebrew name Ḥōrēb means "dry place, waste, desert," from the root ḥ-r-b, "to be dry, waste, desolate" — a bare piece of desert description for the barren, waterless, solitary peak.
  • The mount of the burning bush (primary biblical sense). At Horeb, Moses keeping the flock "behind the desert" sees the bush that burns without being consumed and hears the voice that names itself "I AM THAT I AM" (Exodus 3:1–14) — the mount of the call of Moses and the revelation of the divine name.
  • The mount of the rock and the water (Exodus 17:6). At Horeb Moses strikes the rock and water flows for the murmuring people — the desert mount of provision as well as of revelation.
  • The mount of Elijah's still, small voice (1 Kings 19). To Horeb, "the mount of God," Elijah flees forty days and nights from Jezebel, and there God passes by — not in wind, earthquake, or fire, but in the still small voice — making Horeb the mount of gentle, inward theophany.
  • Synonym of Sinai / the Law (Deuteronomic and traditional sense). Deuteronomy repeatedly locates the lawgiving at Horeb (Deuteronomy 1:6; 4:10, 15; 5:2), so that Horeb/Oreb functions as the alternative name of the mountain of the Law, traditionally identified with Sinai (or with a region of it).
  • The Miltonic Oreb (17th-century literary sense). "The secret top Of Oreb, or of Sinai" is where the Spirit inspired Moses the lawgiver — the wild, cloud-veiled desert mount of biblical inspiration, set against the pagan "Aonian Mount" of the Muses.
  • Modern usage. Horeb is the standard English form (KJV, scholarship); Oreb survives as an archaic, poetic, Vulgate-derived variant essentially confined to Milton and older texts, and in historical criticism names the Elohist source's designation of the sacred mount.
Historical bridges and reasoning:
  • The polysemy grows from the bare topographical meaning upward: a dry waste mountain becomes the mount of the burning bush, then of the rock-water, then of the still small voice, then the alternative name of the lawgiving Sinai — each sense a layer of revelation on the same desert peak.
  • Transmission: Hebrew Ḥōrēḇ → Greek Χωρήβ Chōrēb (Septuagint) → Latin Horeb and the variant Oreb (Vulgate) → English Horeb (standard) beside Oreb (Milton's poetic form).
  • Critical scholarship explains the double naming (Horeb/Sinai) by the sources of the Pentateuch: the Elohist prefers Horeb, the Yahwist and Priestly sources prefer Sinai — an insight that illuminates Milton's paired line.
Meaning today: Horeb is the standard scholarly and biblical name; Oreb is an archaic literary variant, still echoing, in Milton's line, the "dry waste" that revelation transformed.
