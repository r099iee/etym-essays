• Hellenes: Similar because both refer to the people of Greece; differs because Hellenes is the native, authentic autonym used by the Greeks themselves across all eras, whereas Grecians is an English exonym derived via Latin and Anglo-Norman.
  • Greeks: Similar because both denote persons belonging to the Greek ethnic and cultural lineage; differs because Greeks is the universal modern vernacular ethnonym, whereas Grecians carries an elevated classical, historical, or academic register associated with ancient philosophy and classical scholarship.
  • Hellenists: Similar because both can designate individuals steeped in Greek culture or Greek-speaking populations; differs because Hellenists specifically emphasizes non-Greek people who adopted Greek speech and culture during the Hellenistic period, or modern academic specialists in Greek literature.
  • Byzantines: Similar because they were Greek-speaking inhabitants of the eastern Roman Empire; differs because Byzantines refers specifically to the medieval Christian Greek realm centered on Constantinople from 330 to 1453 CE, whereas Grecians evokes classical and Hellenistic antiquity.
  • Achaeans: Similar because both refer to ancient Greeks; differs because Achaeans is specifically the Homeric and Mycenaean designation for the Greek warriors who fought in the Trojan War, whereas Grecians encompasses classical and Hellenistic philosophers and historical peoples.
  • Ionians: Similar because both designate ancient Greek populations; differs because Ionians refers specifically to one of the four major ancient Greek tribal subdivisions (along with Dorians, Aeolians, and Achaeans), primarily inhabiting the Aegean islands and the coast of Asia Minor.
  • Classicists: Similar because both can designate scholars devoted to ancient Greek literature; differs because classicists encompasses scholars of both ancient Greek and Roman Latin civilizations, whereas a Grecian focuses specifically on Greek language and culture.
  • Humanists: Similar because both describe scholars devoted to classical learning; differs because humanists refers to the Renaissance intellectual movement emphasizing secular classical humanities, rather than an ethnonym or specialized Greek scholar.
