• supply: Similar because it means goods provided for use; differs because it is general and immediate, while provision stresses foresight and stockpiling.
  • provisions (victuals): Similar because it is almost identical in food sense; differs because victuals is archaic and purely edible, while provisions includes equipment and stores.
  • rations: Similar because they are allotted food supplies; differs because rations stress fixed measured shares, especially military, not general stock.
  • stores: Similar because they are accumulated supplies; differs because stores emphasizes warehousing, not necessarily future contingency.
  • stock: Similar because it is accumulated goods; differs because it is commercial and neutral, lacking the prudential nuance.
  • stipulation: Similar because it is a condition laid down in an agreement; differs because it stresses negotiated demand rather than preparatory arrangement.
  • clause: Similar because it is a distinct article in a legal text; differs because it is purely textual, while provision implies purposeful arrangement.
  • proviso: Similar because it is a conditional provision; differs because it is specifically a qualifying if-clause, narrower than any provision.
  • arrangement: Similar because it is preparation made beforehand; differs because it is informal and general, not legal or logistic.
  • preparation: Similar because it is readiness for the future; differs because it is the act, not the stored thing or written article.
  • prerequisite: Similar because it is something required beforehand; differs because it is a condition for something else, not a supply.
  • accoutrements: Similar because they are equipment supplied; differs because they are personal gear, not consumable stores.
  • purveyance: Similar because it is historical supply of provisions; differs because it is archaic and tied to royal requisition.
