Classical Arabic (7th–8th c. CE)
al-ʿūd (العود)
"the wood, timber, wooden staff; the lute"
(referring to its wooden soundboard, distinguishing it from skin-topped chordophones)
↓
Andalusi Arabic / Old Spanish (10th–13th c.)
al-ʿūd / laúd
"plucked musical chordophone with rounded body"
(Arabic definite article al- absorbed into Spanish laúd, Portuguese alaúde, Provençal laüt)
↓
Old French (late 13th c.)
leüt / lut
"lute, plucked stringed instrument"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
lute
"stringed instrument played with fingers or plectrum"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–present, plural form)
lutes
"pear-shaped plucked stringed musical instruments"

Note on homonymy: The musical instrument lute (from Arabic al-ʿūd) is entirely distinct etymologically from the chemical, metallurgical, and masonry noun and verb lute ("a tenacious adhesive clay or cement used to hermetically seal joints and retorts"), which derives from Latin lutum ("mud, mire, loam"), from PIE *lew- ("dirt, mud").
