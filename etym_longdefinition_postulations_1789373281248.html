Postulations is a plural noun denoting fundamental assumptions, foundational premises, principles, or hypotheses advanced, claimed, or taken for granted as true, self-evident, or necessary without empirical proof, serving as the essential bedrock for an argument, theoretical system, scientific paradigm, or aesthetic doctrine. The word also possesses specialized historical and institutional meanings, particularly within Roman Catholic canon law, where it designates formal petitions or requests for an ecclesiastical appointment or canonization.

Derived from the Classical Latin verb postulāre ("to demand, ask, claim, require"), the term occupies an indispensable position across logic, geometry, epistemology, and literary aesthetics. In literary criticism—as illustrated in the context sentence examining Marcel Proust's position between the nineteenth and twentieth centuries—aesthetic postulations refers to the foundational artistic credos, theoretical premises, and doctrinal manifestos that govern creative movements. Proust stands suspended between two monumental postulations: the objective postulation of nineteenth-century Realism (Balzac, Flaubert, the Goncourt brothers, which posits that literature can meticulously transcribe social reality) and the subjective postulation of late-nineteenth-century Symbolism (Baudelaire, Huysmans, Mallarmé, which posits that reality is an enigmatic forest of symbols accessible only through poetic analogy and musical suggestion).

The term displays rich polysemy across philosophical, scientific, mathematical, legal, and theological domains:

  • Aesthetic and Literary Doctrinal Premises (The Context Sense): In cultural history and literary criticism, postulations refers to the core aesthetic dogmas, assumptions, or manifestos through which an artistic movement organizes its vision of the world. An aesthetic postulation dictates what art is, what truth it can reveal, and what stylistic instruments it may deploy. Proust's genius lay in refusing to settle comfortably into either the realist postulation (literature as social mirror) or the symbolist postulation (literature as transcendent allegory), instead forging an unprecedented synthesis between etymology and allegory, sensory immediacy and structural memory.
  • Mathematics, Logic, and Axiomatic Systems: In deductive logic and formal geometry, postulations (or postulates) are primary propositions that are assumed without demonstration, from which subsequent theorems and proofs are logically deduced. Since Euclid's Elements, geometry has rested upon foundational postulations (such as the parallel postulate). While modern formal logic often treats axioms and postulates as interchangeable synonyms, classical philosophy distinguished axioms as self-evident universal truths of reason (e.g., the law of non-contradiction), whereas postulations were specific, operational assumptions accepted as a necessary foundation for a particular discipline.
  • Epistemology and Kantian Critical Philosophy: In the philosophy of Immanuel Kant, particularly the Critique of Practical Reason (1788), the postulates of pure practical reason (German: Postulate der reinen praktischen Vernunft) designate those necessary presuppositions that cannot be theoretically proven by speculative intellect, but which must be postulated as moral necessities for practical human agency: the freedom of the will, the immortality of the soul, and the existence of God.
  • Theoretical Physics and Scientific Paradigm Building: In scientific methodology, postulations are bold theoretical conjectures or foundational hypotheses advanced to account for observed phenomena. Albert Einstein's 1905 Special Theory of Relativity rests upon two core postulations: the principle of relativity (the laws of physics are identical in all inertial frames) and the constancy of the speed of light in a vacuum. Quantum mechanics similarly advances foundational postulations concerning wave-particle duality and state vectors in Hilbert space.
  • Canon Law and Ecclesiastical Appointments: In Roman Catholic canon law, a postulation (Latin: postulatio) is a formal, petitionary vote cast by an electoral chapter or college requesting a religious superior (such as the Pope) to appoint a candidate who is canonically ineligible due to a legal impediment (such as age, birth, or existing episcopal vows) that the superior has the power to dispense. In causes of sainthood, the postulator is the official advocate appointed to petition the Holy See and guide the investigation into the candidate's heroic virtues and miracles.
  • Everyday Academic and Analytical Discourse: In scholarly essays and general debate, postulations designates tentative theories, reasoned assertions, or claims put forward for debate and analytical exploration, functioning as educated hypotheses awaiting verification.

The historical semantic bridge linking Roman courtroom demands, ecclesiastical petitions, Euclidean geometry, Kantian ethics, and aesthetic credos is the concept of a necessary demand: a postulation demands that the listener grant a foundational premise so that an entire universe of thought, law, or art may be constructed upon it.
