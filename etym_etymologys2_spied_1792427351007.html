Note on historical derivation: The English verb spy (past tense spied) was borrowed into Middle English from Old French espiier (Modern French épier), which itself was borrowed from the Germanic language of the Franks (*spehōn). Germanic *spehōną traces directly to the prolific Proto-Indo-European verbal root *speḱ- ("to look, observe"). It is thus an indirect Germanic doublet of native Old High German spehōn (Modern German spähen, "to peer, spy") and an etymological cognate of Latin specere ("to look at", yielding spectator, respect, despise), Greek skopein ("to examine", yielding telescope), and Sanskrit paśyati / spaś ("to see, scout").
