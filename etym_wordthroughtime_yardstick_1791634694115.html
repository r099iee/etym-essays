- Early 19th c. (c. 1823) — Emerges in American English as a physical compound to denote a merchant's or carpenter's literal three-foot measuring stick, distinguishing the graduated wooden tool from the abstract legal unit of length.
- Mid-19th c. — Widely integrated into retail drapers' shops, tailoring parlance, and carpentry across North America and Britain; celebrated as the emblem of fair, honest retail transactions.
- Late 19th c. (1880s) — Undergoes a major semantic expansion into American political journalism, economics, and social philosophy, becoming a widespread metaphor for an objective, impartial standard of evaluation or comparison.
- Early to Mid-20th c. — Cemented in corporate accounting, educational testing, and sociological measurement; the concept of an empirical 'yardstick' becomes a cornerstone of policy assessment.
- Late 20th to 21st c. — Codified into specialized economic terminology ('yardstick competition' / 'yardstick regulation' in public utility oversight), while remaining a universally recognized idiom in public discourse for any reliable criterion of judgment.
