- 14th century - the hind part of an animal: hip and thigh taken together
- 15th-16th century - extended to human anatomy, especially for squatting or sitting back 'on one's haunches'
- 17th-19th century - also culinary (a haunch of venison) and architectural (the haunch of an arch)
- Contemporary - chiefly animal or culinary in ordinary use; applied to a human it sounds coarse, physical, and slightly rural, more literary than plain 'hips'
