- 17th Century: Introduced into Early Modern English as a formal legal term derived from Latin, describing the initiation and procedural prosecution of a civil lawsuit or judicial dispute before a magistrate.
- 18th to 19th Century: Retained a primarily technical, juridical register in common law treatises, corporate contract disputes, and property litigation, often carrying connotations of costly, protracted courtroom conflict.
- Mid-20th Century: Began appearing in political commentary and historical scholarship as a metaphorical term for treating ideological debates, historical traumas, and election results as adversarial legal trials.
- Late 20th to 21st Century: Widely adopted in media, cultural criticism, and colloquial discourse in phrases like "litigating the past," referring to endlessly rehashing, dissecting, and assigning blame for historical decisions, cultural controversies, or interpersonal fallout as if conducting a trial in the court of public opinion.
