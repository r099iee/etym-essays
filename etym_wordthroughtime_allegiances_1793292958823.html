- c. 1300 — feudal homage: allegiaunce names the sworn duty of the liege man to his lord, sealed by oath and service.
- 14th–16th c. — law of the realm: the bond migrates from lord to crown, and breach of allegiance becomes treason in statute and trial.
- 17th–18th c. — citizen duty: oaths of allegiance cross the Atlantic and multiply with revolutions, turning subjects into citizens who pledge republics.
- 19th c. — widening to causes: churches, parties, and nations all claim allegiances, and emigrants learn the pain of divided ones.
- 20th c. — metaphor everywhere: scholars, fans, and consumers hold allegiances to theories, teams, and brands, half earnest and half playful.
- Today — formal but flexible: the civic and legal core survives in pledges and courts, while the plural flourishes for every tribe of politics, fandom, and thought.
