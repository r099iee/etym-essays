- late 18th century scientific coinage - coined in the 1790s as an optical and mineralogical term for the rainbow-like play of colour in opals, mother-of-pearl, feathers, and oil films; the word is descriptive and scientific, free of emotional colour.
- 19th century natural-history prose - spreads through natural science and travel writing as the precise word for spectral surface sheen, and begins to be applied to anything that flashes changing hues.
- 19th-20th century figurative transfer - borrowed for personalities and prose (Carlyle uses it of Harriet Taylor) to suggest a dazzling but unstable surface that cannot be pinned to a single tint; admiration and elusiveness sit side by side.
- Contemporary use - an everyday decorative adjective for shimmering rainbow colour, and still available for the figurative sense of brilliance that catches the eye but does not hold it.
