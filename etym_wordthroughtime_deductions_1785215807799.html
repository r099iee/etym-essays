- 15th century arithmetic and legal adoption - the English plural first means subtractions from a total, amounts drawn off from a sum in accounts and law; the meaning is purely financial and concrete.
- 16th-17th century logical turn - borrowed into the vocabulary of reasoning: a deduction becomes a conclusion drawn downward from general principles to particular cases, the rigorous method of the syllogism and of the new philosophy.
- 18th-19th century philosophical currency - Mill's age contrasts deduction with induction and makes the word a technical instrument of method; it is used, as here, of the laborious and exacting inferences a biographer must make.
- Contemporary use - the logical sense is formal and technical, while the financial sense (tax deduction, payroll deduction) is everyday; the two live side by side with little of the logical word's scientific glamour left.
