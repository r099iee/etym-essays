• Consternation: Similar because the closest overall synonym — sudden fear and shock that paralyse; differs because consternation stresses shocked confusion and paralysis, while dismay stresses the loss of courage with a load of disappointment.
  • Alarm: Similar as sudden fear; differs because alarm is the immediate fear of danger (etymologically “to arms!”) and can be light and fleeting, while dismay is deeper and includes dejection.
  • Horror: Similar as a powerful negative reaction; differs because much stronger and rooted in revulsion and dread — dismay is disappointment-laden, horror is gut-level and horrified.
  • Dread: Similar as strong fear; differs because dread is anticipatory — fear of what is coming — whereas dismay is the reaction to what has just happened.
  • Panic: Similar as sudden overwhelming fear; differs because panic is irrational, contagious and action-impairing flight, while dismay can be quiet, sad and self-possessed.
  • Fright: Similar as a sudden scare; differs because milder and momentary — fright passes; dismay settles in.
  • Fear: Similar as the genus of apprehension; differs by complete generality — fear is any fear; dismay is the specific complex of alarm plus shattered confidence.
  • Apprehension / Unease / Trepidation: Similar as anxious states; differ because they are milder, future-oriented and present even before anything happens, without the note of hopes destroyed.
  • Discouragement / Disheartenment: Similar as loss of confidence; differ because they are gradual and hope-focused, with no component of shock or alarm — the disappointment without the fright.
  • Dejection / Despondency: Similar as low spirits; differ because they are chronic states rather than sudden reactions — sadness that stays, not courage knocked out.
  • Distress: Similar as acute suffering; differs because it is broader (physical and emotional pain, longer-lasting) and lacks the specific “shock plus discouragement” blend.
  • Discomfiture: Similar as being ruffled or thrown off; differs because it is milder, chiefly social, and often comic in modern use.
  • Perturbation: Similar as agitation; differs because formal, mild and intellectual — mental disturbance without the emotional collapse of dismay.
  • Appall / Overwhelm (verbs): Similar as “to overcome with emotion”; differ because appall is horror-heavy and overwhelm is any emotion at full force, while dismay specifically conjoins alarm and discouragement.
  • Daunt / Intimidate (verbs): Similar as “to make someone lose courage”; differ because they aim to deter a person in advance, while dismay names the achieved state of lost heart.
