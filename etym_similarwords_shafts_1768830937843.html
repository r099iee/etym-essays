• beams: Similar because both name columns of light in the sky; differs because a beam is the broadest term — originally the structural timber of a house — suggesting a massive, weighty, often horizontal column (a sunbeam), while a shaft is narrower, more directed, and carries the image of the spear-stem.
  • rays: Similar because both name lines of light radiating from a source; differs because a ray is thin, geometric, and scientific, a line without body, while a shaft has thickness, structure, and direction.
  • streaks: Similar because both name narrow lines of light; differs because a streak is a mark on a surface, not a volume of light in the air, while a shaft occupies space as a column.
  • pillars: Similar because both name vertical columns of light; differs because a pillar is architectural, massive, and necessarily vertical, while a shaft is slender, aimed, and may fall at any angle.
  • lances: Similar because both derive from the weapon-image and name penetrating light; differs because a lance is a specific shaft-bearing weapon whose metaphor stresses piercing, while shaft is the more neutral and structural word for the straight body itself.
  • spears: Similar because both carry the weapon image of straight, aimed light; differs because a spear is the whole weapon, while a shaft is the straight stem that the weapon lends to light.
  • columns: Similar because both name vertical, structural forms of light; differs because a column implies width and support, while a shaft implies slender straightness and aim.
  • needles: Similar because both name thin, piercing lines of light; differs because a needle stresses thinness and a point, while a shaft stresses length and a straight body.
  • bars: Similar because both name straight bands of light; differs because a bar implies width and an edge, often from a shadowed opening, while a shaft implies a directed column from a source.
  • blades: Similar because both name flat, straight, penetrating forms of light; differs because a blade implies flatness and cutting, while a shaft implies a round straight stem.
  • darts: Similar because both name quickly aimed projections of light; differs because a dart is small and thrown, while a shaft is long, structural, and steady.
  • arrows: Similar because both name aimed, straight projections; differs because an arrow is the projectile, while a shaft is the straight stem of which the arrow is made — the light takes the stem, not the flight.
