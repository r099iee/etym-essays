• Judas: Similar because both are the supreme betrayers of their traditions, synonymous with treachery itself; differs because Judas is the biblical disciple who sold Christ for silver, a spiritual and monetary betrayal, whereas Mordred is a blood-kinsman whose rebellion is dynastic and martial.
  • Brutus: Similar because both are kin-betrayers who strike down a founding hero (Caesar/Arthur) and die in the ensuing catastrophe; differs because Brutus was Caesar's protégé rather than a claimant to the throne, and his act was a principled, debated tyranny-cide while Mordred's was self-interested usurpation.
  • Ganelon: Similar because both are the arch-traitors of medieval romance who bring down a heroic king's enterprise; differs because Ganelon betrays out of personal spite during the campaign (Song of Roland), whereas Mordred seizes power at home in the king's absence.
  • Traitor: Similar because Mordred embodies the category; differs because traitor is the general common noun applying to any betrayer, while Mordred names the specific Arthurian archetype with all its legendary resonance.
  • Usurper: Similar because Mordred unlawfully seizes the throne; differs because usurper is a functional term (anyone who seizes power illegitimately), whereas Mordred is the legendary usurper whose seizure is dynastic, incest-poisoned, and apocalyptic.
  • Betrayer: Similar because Mordred betrays kin and king; differs because betrayer is neutral and general, while Mordred implies the full romantic machinery of Round Table, fatal blow, and mutual death.
  • Regicide: Similar because Mordred's rebellion kills the king; differs because regicide names the act or agent of killing a king, while Mordred's crime is broader — usurpation, kin-treason, and the destruction of a realm, with the killing itself shared and mutual.
  • Turncoat: Similar because Mordred changes allegiance against his lord; differs because turncoat suggests pragmatic switching of sides and lacks the dynastic, apocalyptic weight of Mordred.
  • Renegade: Similar because Mordred abandons loyalty to the crown; differs because renegade emphasises desertion and outlaw status, whereas Mordred remains at the centre of the court and strikes from within it.
  • Quisling: Similar because both name collaborator-traitors who destroy a regime; differs because quisling is a modern political term for a foreign-collaborating leader, while Mordred is the medieval kin-betrayer who destroys his own house.
  • Lancelot: Similar because both are Arthurian figures whose actions destroy the Round Table; differs because Lancelot's tragedy springs from forbidden love and fidelity to Guinevere, whereas Mordred's is cold, calculating treason with no redeeming motive.
  • Gawain: Similar because both are Arthur's nephews; differs because Gawain is the loyal, chivalrous nephew who fights for Arthur, the foil that throws Mordred's treachery into relief.
