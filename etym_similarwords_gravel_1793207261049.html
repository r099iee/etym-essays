• Pebbles: Similar because both are small rounded water-worn stones; differs because pebbles pictures the individual stones (a child pockets pebbles), while gravel names the massed material for surfacing.
  • Shingle: Similar because both are beds of loose rounded stones; differs because shingle is specifically beach or river-bank accumulation (and, confusingly, roof tiles), while gravel is the quarried or dug commercial material.
  • Ballast: Similar because railway ballast literally is graded gravel packing the sleepers; differs because ballast stresses the stabilising function (ships, tracks, balloons), while gravel stresses the stony material itself.
  • Aggregate: Similar because builders’ aggregate includes gravel mixed with sand and crushed rock; differs because aggregate is the technical umbrella term for the concrete mix, while gravel is the homely word for the stones.
  • Grit: Similar because both are small hard stone particles for traction and surfacing; differs because grit is finer and sharper (gritted roads, gritstone), while gravel is coarser and rounded.
  • Rubble: Similar because both are broken stone used in foundations and fill; differs because rubble is angular demolition waste and rough walling stone, while gravel is small rounded or crushed pieces for surfaces.
  • Scree: Similar because both are accumulations of loose rock fragments; differs because scree is the mountain talus slope of fallen shards, while gravel is lowland, rounded, and usable.
  • Crushed stone: Similar because most commercial gravel today is quarried rock mechanically broken; differs because crushed stone names the industrial product explicitly, while gravel keeps the natural river-bank flavour.
  • Chippings: Similar because both dress paths and drives with small stones; differs because chippings (slate, granite) are decorative graded fragments, while gravel is the general workaday heap.
