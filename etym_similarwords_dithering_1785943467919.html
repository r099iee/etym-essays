• Vacillation: Similar because both denote repeated, uncommitted wavering between options; differs because vacillation swings between major opposing positions or loyalties, while dithering is flustered, petty, anxious fiddling over details.
  • Hesitation: Similar because both denote a pause before committing; differs because hesitation is the general, brief act of pausing, while dithering is hesitation amplified into prolonged nervous wavering.
  • Procrastination: Similar because both result in delay and failure to act; differs because procrastination is deliberate postponement out of laziness or aversion, while dithering is active but fruitless wrestling with a decision out of indecision.
  • Wavering: Similar because both denote unsteady movement between alternatives; differs because wavering is a neutral, often measured instability, while dithering is nervous, agitated and petty.
  • Equivocation: Similar because both can delay commitment; differs because equivocation is the deliberate use of ambiguous language to conceal the truth, while dithering is genuine internal indecision rather than verbal evasion.
  • Deliberation: Similar because both involve thinking before deciding; differs because deliberation is careful, rational, structured thought, while dithering is chaotic, inefficient, unproductive wavering.
  • Floundering: Similar because both denote confused, ineffectual struggling; differs because floundering is clumsy struggle in any difficulty, while dithering is specifically indecisive hesitation in making a choice.
  • Trembling (older sense): Similar because dithering once literally meant trembling and the words share the body-image of shaking; differs because trembling is physical quivering, while dithering (modern) is mental indecision, though it retains the older tremor sense.
  • Shilly-shallying: Similar because both denote indecisive, ineffectual hesitation (both are also slightly whimsical words); differs because shilly-shallying suggests dawdling and delaying games, while dithering suggests nervous agitation and fluster.
  • Waffling: Similar because both denote failure to commit clearly; differs because waffling is the evasive, wordy shifting of position (especially in speech), while dithering is anxious hesitation regardless of speech.
