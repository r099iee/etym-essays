- c. 1400 — tousle appears: Middle English touselen means to handle roughly, to rumple and pull about.
- 1620s — the diminutive is born: tussle emerges as the frequentative of tousle, a little tousling-match, first recorded in the sense of a minor struggle.
- 17th–18th c. — country pastime: wrestlers, schoolboys, and puppies tussle in poems and jest-books; the word smells of straw and schoolyards.
- 19th c. — sport adopts it: newspaper writers find tussle ideal for rucks, scrums, and election contests too small for battle but too lively for dispute.
- 20th c. — the headline favourite: cabinet tussles, courtroom tussles, transfer-window tussles — conflict miniaturised for daily consumption.
- Today — scuffle with a smile: still the gentlest word for a fight, equally at home in nurseries, newsrooms, and Tolkien pantries.
