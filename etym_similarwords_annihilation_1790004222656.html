• obliteration: Similar because it means complete wiping out leaving no trace; differs because it emphasizes erasing marks or memory rather than existential reduction to nothing.
  • extermination: Similar because it means utter destruction of a group; differs because it is typically used for living pests, peoples, or species and implies deliberate killing.
  • eradication: Similar because it means rooting out entirely; differs because it is used for diseases, problems, or practices and suggests removal of cause rather than explosive destruction.
  • extinction: Similar because it means ceasing to exist; differs because it is specific to species, flames, or customs dying out, often gradual, not violent instant erasure.
  • destruction: Similar because it means ruin; differs because it is broader and allows remains and repair, while annihilation allows none.
  • decimation: Similar because it suggests severe defeat; differs because literally it means removal of one-tenth and today means heavy loss, not total erasure.
  • liquidation: Similar because it means elimination; differs because it is bureaucratic or financial — liquidating assets, liquidating enemies — cold and administrative.
  • abolition: Similar because it means bringing to an end; differs because it applies to laws, institutions, slavery — formal repeal, not physical blast.
  • nullification: Similar because it means making null and void; differs because it is legalistic and abstract, canceling validity rather than matter.
  • vaporization: Similar because it means turning to vapor leaving nothing solid; differs because it is vividly physical and often hyperbolic in sci-fi and military slang.
  • oblivion: Similar because it means state of being forgotten or destroyed; differs because it stresses forgetting and neglect over active destruction.
