“Dare” entered English meaning to be bold enough to do something, and “say” joined it naturally: to “dare say” something is to be bold enough to utter it.
“Dare say” in the 16th–18th centuries fused into a fixed conversational formula — “I daresay” meaning “I venture to think,” “I should suppose,” a mild, polite hedging of an opinion.
“Daresay” through the 19th century was a staple of genteel spoken English — the comfortable, slightly fussy assertion of probability used by hosts, narrators, and Austen characters alike.
“Daresay” today survives as a dated but recognizable conversational idiom: “I daresay you will…” softens a prediction into a polite venture — the speaker’s own mild boldness in offering an opinion, not a sworn claim.
