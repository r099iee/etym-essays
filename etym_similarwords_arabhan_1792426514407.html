• Sutīkṣṇa: Similar because both are venerable forest ascetics (*ṛṣis*) residing in the Daṇḍaka wilderness whom Rāma visits in direct succession in the Araṇyakāṇḍa; differs because Śarabhaṅga immediately ends his earthly incarnation upon meeting Rāma by entering a sacrificial fire to ascend to Brahmaloka, whereas Sutīkṣṇa remains alive in his hermitage to instruct and host Rāma for an extended stay.
  • Agastya: Similar because both are great Vedic sages of the southern forests who assist Rāma during his exile; differs because Agastya is the foremost civilization-bringer of the south who presents Rāma with divine celestial weapons (the golden bow of Viṣṇu, inexhaustible quivers, and the sun-sword) and advises him to build a hermitage at Pañcavaṭī, whereas Śarabhaṅga is an anchorite whose narrative focuses on his personal spiritual liberation (*mokṣa*).
  • Bharadvāja: Similar because both are exalted sages who welcome Rāma with boundless hospitality during his exile; differs because Bharadvāja maintains a large, prestigious hermitage at the holy confluence of Prayāga near Ayodhyā on the northern edge of the journey, while Śarabhaṅga dwells in the deep, dangerous Daṇḍaka wilderness.
  • Atri: Similar because both are primordial ṛṣis visited by Rāma and Sītā in forest hermitages; differs because Atri is one of the Saptarṣis (Seven Sages) who lives with his celebrated wife Anasūyā at Citrakūṭa, whereas Śarabhaṅga is an isolated anchorite living without a consort in the southern forest.
  • Vālmīki: Similar because both are revered sages appearing in the narrative of the Rāmāyaṇa; differs because Vālmīki is the inspired author and poet-composer (*ādikavi*) of the entire epic who provides shelter to Sītā in her later exile, whereas Śarabhaṅga is an actor within the epic story who ascends to heaven in the third book.
  • ṛṣi: Similar because Śarabhaṅga is the quintessential embodiment of a Vedic ṛṣi; differs because ṛṣi is the broad, generic Sanskrit title denoting any seer, poet, or sage of Vedic revelation, whereas Śarabhaṅga (Śarabhan-) is an individual historical-mythological personality.
  • muni: Similar because both denote holy ascetics practicing silence and spiritual contemplation; differs because muni is a general functional title for an ascetic or recluse, whereas Śarabhaṅga is the specific proper name of the sage who entered the fire.
  • tapodhana: Similar because Śarabhaṅga is repeatedly praised in the text as a *tapodhana* (one whose sole wealth is his penance); differs because tapodhana is a descriptive epithet applied to any austere yogi, while Śarabhaṅga is the specific sage who met Indra and Rāma.
  • Śarabha: Similar because *śarabha* forms the foundational first morphological half of the sage's compound name; differs because śarabha is the mythical eight-legged beast or the fierce avian-leonine avatāra of Lord Śiva, whereas Śarabhaṅga is the human sage whose limbs possessed such power.
  • Tumburu: Similar because both are spiritual figures in the same section of the Rāmāyaṇa who achieve liberation (*mokṣa*) upon meeting Rāma; differs because Tumburu was a gandharva trapped in the monstrous demon Virādha who had to be buried alive to regain his form, whereas Śarabhaṅga was already a pure, holy sage who voluntarily entered the fire.
