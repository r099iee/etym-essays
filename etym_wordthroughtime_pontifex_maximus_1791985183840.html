- 1st c. BCE — the title of the head of the Roman priestly college, held for life and central to public religion.
- 12 BCE — Augustus takes the office after Lepidus; it becomes an imperial prerogative.
- 382 CE — Gratian refuses the title, and it fades from the imperial style.
- Middle Ages–Renaissance — pontifex is applied to bishops; Pontifex Maximus reappears in papal coins, inscriptions and humanist Latin.
- 16th–17th c. — Reformation polemic turns the title into evidence of papal paganism, giving the phrase a controversial edge.
- 18th–19th c. — classical scholarship fixes it as the standard name of the Roman office.
- 20th–21st c. — used for the pope, for Roman religion, and jocularly for the dominant figure in any field.
