Core meaning.Precocious describes something that develops, flowers, or matures earlier than is usual or expected — most often a child whose mind, talent, or manner is advanced beyond its years. In the source passage above, Will Durant speaks of the tropics' precocious "maturity and decay": because life ripens too early there, it also begins to fall apart too early. That double edge — brilliance and risk in the same word — is built into its history, for precocious things come before their time and therefore also fail, fade, or are forced before their time.

The botanical root. The English word was coined from Latin praecox, "early-ripening", literally "cooked before" (prae- "before" + coquere "to cook, ripen"). In its first, seventeenth-century use it applied to plants: a precocious tree bears fruit early; a precocious season ripens corn ahead of the calendar. This horticultural sense remains live — "precocious varieties" of fruit and vine — and it explains everything that followed: the human senses of the word are metaphors of ripening, with all of ripening's mixed messages about readiness, sweetness, and perishability.

  • Of children: mentally or socially advanced beyond one's years — a precocious reader, a precocious wit.
  • Of parts and faculties: prematurely developed — precocious talent, precocious intelligence, precocious growth of the body.
  • Of plants and seasons: early-flowering or early-fruiting (the original sense, still standard in horticulture).
  • Of behaviour and culture: precocious sophistication — a child who talks like an adult; an artwork, movement, or venture said to be "ahead of its time".

Polysemy and the semantic bridge. Every secondary meaning is the botanical metaphor extended step by step: fruit that ripens early → a mind that matures early → a body that matures early → anything that arrives too soon. The modern ambivalence is inherited. To call a child precocious can praise ("remarkably advanced") or gently warn ("dangerously pushed; too clever by half"). In paediatrics, precocious is now the technical word for growth that is pathologically early — precocious puberty, precocious adrenarche — a clinical, value-free usage that sits oddly beside the admiring "precocious talent" of the concert hall and classroom. In ecology and zoology, the cognate precocial (from the same Latin stem) describes animals born or hatched remarkably mature — ducklings that walk and feed at once, literally "ripened early" — a striking terminological cousin of the child-prodigy sense.

Register and collocation. A dignified, slightly literary adjective; it collocates with child, talent, intelligence, puberty, development. The nouns are precocity (usual) and precociousness (rater). The word almost always measures a child or a thing against a calendar, and that is its point: precocious means early, not merely able — it names timing, and timing, as the tropics passage insists, can be as much a curse as a gift.
