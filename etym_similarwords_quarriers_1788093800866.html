• quarrymen: Similar because identical occupation — workers in a stone pit; differs because "quarryman" is the modern standard term while "quarrier" is now historical or regional.
  • stonecutters: Similar because both work with building stone; differs because stonecutters usually shape finished blocks in a yard or shop, not extract raw stone at the face.
  • stonemasons: Similar because both are stone trades; differs because masons dress and lay stone in structures — a construction craft, not an extraction trade.
  • miners: Similar because both extract rock and minerals from the earth; differs because miners work underground in ore, coal or salt seams, while quarriers work open faces for building stone.
  • hewers: Similar because both cut material loose with striking tools; differs because "hewer" is archaic or literary and generic (wood or stone), with no quarry setting.
  • excavators: Similar because both dig matter out of the ground; differs because "excavator" today means earth-moving machinery or archaeological diggers, not skilled stone extraction.
  • delvers: Similar because both dig into the earth; differs because "delver" is poetic and antiquarian (a favourite of Tolkien's for underground workers), with no trade meaning.
  • rock-splitters / knappers: Similar because both break stone by hand; differs because these are narrower, craft-specific terms (flint knapping, splitting for paving) rather than the full occupation.
  • labourers: Similar because both are manual workers; differs because "labourer" is a generic, unskilled category with no stone specialization.
  • quarry-owners / quarrymasters: Similar because both belong to the quarry industry; differs because they direct and manage the works rather than work the face.
