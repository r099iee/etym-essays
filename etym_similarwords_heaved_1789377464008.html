• Hoisted: Similar because both involve raising heavy objects upward against gravity; differs because hoisted typically implies the use of mechanical aids, pulleys, ropes, cranes, or tackle (e.g., *hoisted the royal standard*, *hoisted the cargo into the hold*), whereas heaved emphasizes raw, direct bodily strain, leverage, and brute physical force.
  • Lifted: Similar because both denote elevating something from a lower to a higher position; differs because lifted is the generic, neutral term applicable to any weight—including light objects like feathers or teacups—whereas heaved universally demands substantial weight, arduous exertion, or deep respiratory labor.
  • Hauled: Similar because both describe the strenuous movement of heavy mass; differs because hauled specifically emphasizes dragging or pulling across a horizontal surface or transporting over distance (e.g., *hauled the timber overland*), whereas heaved focuses on lifting upward, pitching with momentum, or undulating.
  • Exhaled (Respiratory sense): Similar because both describe breathing air outward from the lungs; differs because exhaled is a clinical, neutral biological term for expelling breath, whereas heaved conveys intense emotional drama, deep thoracic expansion, and audible, labored psychological release (*heaved a sigh*).
  • Hurled (Ballistic sense): Similar because both involve propelling an object vigorously through the air; differs because hurled emphasizes violent rage, high speed, and aggressive intent (e.g., *hurled an insult*, *hurled a spear*), whereas heaved emphasizes the ponderous weight of the object and the full-body mechanical momentum required to launch it.
  • Swelled (Undulatory sense): Similar because both describe expanding or rising upward in volume; differs because swelled emphasizes gradual inflation, rounded bulging, or increasing size, whereas heaved conveys dynamic, rolling, rhythmic up-and-down motion like the churning waves of a stormy sea.
  • Gasped: Similar because both are involuntary, emotionally charged respiratory actions; differs because gasped involves a sudden, sharp, spasmodic *inhalation* caused by shock, terror, or lack of air, whereas heaved a sigh represents a deep, heavy, prolonged *exhalation* releasing grief or relief.
