• Optimus Princeps: Similar because both names label the same ruler and signal his idealised standing as "best of princes"; differs because Optimus Princeps is a senatorial honorific-title, not a personal name, and never appears in English as a common noun the way Trajan does.
  • Marcus Ulpius Traianus: Similar because it is the full Latin nomenclature of the same emperor; differs because Traianus is the cognomen (family third name) and Marcus Ulpius the praenomen and nomen, whereas "Trajan" is the anglicised short form used in narrative history and this translation.
  • Nerva: Similar because Nerva is Trajan's adoptive father and the immediately preceding emperor in Gibbon's chain; differs because Nerva reigned only two years (96–98), was elderly and civilian, and is remembered for choosing Trajan as heir rather than for conquest or building.
  • Hadrian: Similar because Hadrian follows Trajan in the list and is a fellow soldier-administrator; differs because Hadrian consolidated and renounced some of Trajan's eastern conquests, built the famous wall in Britain, and is remembered more as a consolidator and traveller than a conqueror.
  • The Five Good Emperors: Similar because Trajan is one of the five; differs because the group label (Nerva, Trajan, Hadrian, Antoninus Pius, Marcus Aurelius) is a later historiographical collective, not a name of the man.
  • Conqueror / warrior-emperor: Similar because Trajan's military fame (Dacia, Parthia) makes "conqueror" a fair descriptor; differs because it is a generic role-word usable of many generals, whereas Trajan is a unique proper name, and Gibbon's passage stresses administration, not conquest.
  • Emperor / Caesar: Similar because Trajan was emperor; differs because "emperor" and "Caesar" are generic or titulary labels applicable to dozens of rulers, with no reference to Trajan's specific reign, adoption, or Antonine context.
