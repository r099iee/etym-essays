[Root 1: COLLIE (Scots colley / Old English col "coal, black-faced")]
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–1st c. CE)
*kulą
"coal, live ember, charcoal"
↓
Old English (c. 8th–11th c.)
col / colig
"coal, blackened with soot, dark-colored"
Note: Connected to Scottish dialect colley, designating black-faced Highland sheep, whence the pastoral dog herding them was named colley dog.
↓
Early Scots / Middle Scots (c. 14th–16th c.)
coly / colley
"black-faced sheep; Scottish shepherd's dog"

[Root 2: DOG (Old English docga "powerful hound, mastiff")]
Old English (c. 10th–11th c.)
docga
"dog, powerful canine, mastiff"
Note: Origin disputed; expressive hypocoristic nickname with geminated -cg- that progressively displaced native Proto-Germanic *hundaz (hound).
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–15th c.)
dogge / dog
"domestic canine, hound, working dog"

[Compound Synthesis: COLLIE-DOG]
Early Modern English / Victorian English (c. 18th–19th c.)
collie dog → collie-dog
"Scottish sheep-herding pastoral dog"
↓
Modern English (present)
COLLIE-DOG
