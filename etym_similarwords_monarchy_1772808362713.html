• Empire: Similar because both describe large-scale single rule; differs because empire emphasizes dominion over many peoples and provinces rather than the form of the head of state.
  • Kingdom: Similar because both name a realm under one ruler; differs because kingdom implies a traditional crown, hereditary succession, and a more compact medieval or early-modern realm.
  • Realm: Similar because both denote a territory ruled by a sovereign; differs because realm is the poetical or legal word for the domain itself, not the system of rule.
  • Tyranny: Similar because both mean rule by one; differs because tyranny carries the moral judgement of illegitimacy and oppression, which monarchy does not necessarily imply.
  • Autocracy: Similar because both stress untrammelled personal power; differs because autocracy evokes modern absolute and bureaucratic rule rather than the formal constitutional institution of monarchy.
  • Despotism: Similar because both mean rule by a single supreme authority; differs because despotism suggests arbitrary, capricious, household-style rule with an Orientalist flavour, not a structural form of government.
  • Absolutism: Similar because both can describe unlimited personal rule; differs because absolutism is a modern political-philosophical term for unlimited executive power, not the constitutional type itself.
  • Sovereignty: Similar because both involve supreme authority; differs because sovereignty is the abstract quality of supreme power (which a republic also possesses), not rule by one person.
  • Crown: Similar because both can metonymically name royal government; differs because crown is the concrete symbol and office of the monarch, not the system of one-ruler government.
  • Kingship: Similar because both mean rule by a single royal head; differs because kingship is specifically a king's office and dignity, narrower than monarchy's general one-ruler concept.
  • Dominion: Similar because both denote sovereign rule over territory; differs because dominion emphasizes the territory and its control, not the constitutional form of the ruler.
  • Principality: Similar because both name a state under a single ruler; differs because principality denotes a small state ruled by a prince, not a system of government.
  • Diarchy: Similar because both are Greek-derived -archy forms naming rule; differs because diarchy means rule by two, the opposite of monarchy's rule by one.
