- 1791: Viennese Popular Premiere - Embraced by suburban audiences at the Theater auf der Wieden as a charming fairy-tale princess in a spectacular comedic-mechanic Singspiel.
- Early 19th Century: German Romantic Apotheosis - Revered by Goethe, E.T.A. Hoffmann, and early Romantics as the archetype of German lyrical maidenhood, moral purity, and poetic pathos.
- Mid-20th Century: Psychological & Hermeneutic Deepening - Reinterpreted through Jungian psychoanalysis and Masonic scholarship as the vital anima figure bridging the unconscious night with conscious solar reason.
- Late 20th to 21st Century: Modern Feminist & Dramaturgical Revision - Celebrated as the active, morally autonomous hero of the opera who guides Tamino through the sacred elements and embodies egalitarian humanism.
