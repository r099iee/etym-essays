- 500s-800s Frankish / Salic Law baro - ordinary freeman, warrior, or husband; legal status indicating full tribal rights and wergild
- 1000s-1200s Norman Feudalism - direct tenant-in-chief holding landed estates per baroniam from the King; military obligation and council duty
- 1215 Magna Carta Barons - collective aristocratic force checking royal tyranny; political rebellion and constitutional governance
- 1300s-1700s British Hereditary Peerage - lowest rank of the five peerage orders in the House of Lords; social prestige and legislative entitlement
- Late 1800s Gilded Age America - "Robber barons" and corporate monopolists; ruthless capitalist power, wealth accumulation, and market dominance
- 20th c. Media & Commodities - press barons (Hearst, Beaverbrook, Murdoch), cattle barons, drug barons; autocratic control over information and illicit networks
- Contemporary English - established parliamentary title and widespread metaphor for any domineering, powerful industry titan
