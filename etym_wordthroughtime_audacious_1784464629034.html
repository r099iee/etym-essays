“Audacious” entered English meaning daring, bold, and confident — willing to take risks, with no fixed moral colour.
“Audacious” in the 16th–17th centuries could lean toward the bad: audacity meant impudent, shameless boldness, the nerve that oversteps manners.
“Audacious” in the 18th–19th centuries saw the two strands coexist — audacious deeds admired as magnificent daring, audacious lies condemned as cheek — the word balanced between praise and blame.
“Audacious” today still carries that double edge at once: an audacious plan is bold and admired, an audacious claim is brazen — daring joined with a hint of shameless nerve.
