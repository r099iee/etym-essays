- [14th century: aphetic of 'display'] - 'splay' is born as a clipped form of 'display,' meaning to spread out or unfold — a sense of opening wide.
- [16th-17th century: architectural use] - in building, splayed describes walls, jambs, and window-openings cut to slope outward; the word enters the mason's lexicon as a precise technical term of angled widening.
- [18th-19th century: anatomy and heraldry] - splayed limbs, splayfeet: the adjective names legs and hands bent outward and flattened, a stance found in heraldry and medicine — wide, outward-spreading, and pressed flat.
- [Contemporary: feral and grotesque] - in modern writing splayed connotes a body spread flat and wide, hands paw-like and open, something animal or inhuman pressed against the ground; the word has gained a faintly disturbing, tactile charge.
