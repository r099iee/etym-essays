Single root word: dabbled (from the English verb dabble, a frequentative formation on the imitative verb dab). Because the base is onomatopoeic / sound-symbolic, there is NO securely reconstructable PIE root; this is stated explicitly rather than forced. Derivation chain: 1. Base root: imitative / sound-symbolic *dab-, "to strike lightly, pat, stamp, splash softly" — a native Germanic expressive formation mimicking the sound and feel of a light repeated impact on a surface or liquid. Earliest attestations: Middle Dutch dabben "to strike, stamp, pat" (13th–14th c.) and Middle English dabben "to strike, stamp" (c. 1300). NO PIE source is reconstructable: sound-symbolic roots stand outside regular etymology by their very nature. 2. Frequentative derivative: Middle English dabbelen (also dablen), "to splash about, to wet by repeated light strokes, to move the hands or feet gently in water" — formed by adding the Germanic frequentative / diminutive suffix -el- (from Proto-Germanic *-ilōnan, source of modern English -le) to dabben. Compare the parallel Middle Dutch dabbelen "to dabble, splash," which may have reinforced the English form. Date: c. 1300. 3. Modern English dabble (verb), "to dip partially and repeatedly in water; to wet by gentle splashing; figuratively, to engage in an activity superficially" — the literal aquatic sense is continuous from Middle English; the figurative "superficial engagement" sense is attested from c. 1590s–1600s. 4. English dabble + the regular past-tense / past-participle suffix -ed —> dabbled, "dipped or splashed gently in water; engaged with in a casual, shallow way." DEBATED / NUANCED ETYMOLOGY: The OED treats dab as imitative and dabble as a native frequentative formation, but the early record shows strong Middle Dutch parallels (dabben, dabbelen), so it cannot be excluded that English borrowed or blended with the Dutch forms rather than forming the word internally. The frequentative analysis itself is standard and secure. Because the root is onomatopoeic, no PIE chain exists to reconstruct; the chain above therefore begins at the first attested Germanic forms and is complete as far as the record allows.
