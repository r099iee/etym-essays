Note on morphological compounding and substrate hypotheses: Sanskrit Citrakūṭa is an endocentric descriptive compound (karmadhāraya samāsa), joining citra ("bright, variegated, wondrous, painted") and kūṭa ("summit, ridge, peak"). Indo-Europeanists trace citra- back to the PIE root *kʷey- ("to observe, perceive"). Regarding the noun kūṭa, philologists note two potential etymological vectors: while some historical linguists connect it to PIE *(s)kew- ("to bend, project"), Manfred Mayrhofer and Burrow have suggested that Sanskrit kūṭa ("summit, horn, hammer") may also reflect an early convergence with a Dravidian or Austroasiatic substrate root denoting rounded elevations (compare Proto-Dravidian *kōṭṭu, "peak, horn, summit").
