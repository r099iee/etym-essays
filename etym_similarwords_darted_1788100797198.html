• Bolted: Similar because both name sudden, rapid departure; differs because bolting implies breaking free of restraint (a horse bolting, a prisoner bolting) and headlong, uncontrolled flight — darted is lighter, more precise and more deliberate.
  • Dashed: Similar because both mean moving at speed over a short space; differs because dashing is more violent and urgent, with force, and can imply collision or smashing ("dashed against the rocks"), which darting never does.
  • Sprinted: Similar because both are quick, deliberate motion; differs because sprinting is sustained athletic top-speed running over a course, while darting is a single brief straight burst, often of an animal or glance rather than a runner.
  • Scurried: Similar because both describe fast, fussy movement; differs because scurrying is short-stepped, bustling and pattering (mice, waiters, insects) with no single straight line, whereas darting is one decisive flight in one direction.
  • Scampered: Similar because both describe light, quick movement; differs because scampering is playful and jaunty — children and puppies scamper — while darting is purposeful and sudden.
  • Shot: Similar because both evoke missile-like speed ("shot across the room"); differs because "shot" is more abrupt and can be motion or projection (shot out, shot past), and it belongs to a lower, more informal register than the literary darted.
  • Zipped: Similar because both mean fast, smooth motion; differs because zipping is informal, effortless and frictionless (a car zips by), without the pointed suddenness of a dart.
  • Whisked: Similar because both mean quick, sweeping motion; differs because whisking implies smoothness and often removal or transport ("whisked away"), not the straight-line attack of darting.
  • Flitted: Similar because both describe rapid light movement (birds flitting, glances flitting); differs because flitting is fluttery, hovering and multi-directional, whereas darting is a single straight decisive course.
  • Hurried: Similar because both mean moving with haste; differs because hurrying is sustained, anxious haste without the missile image or the sudden single burst.
  • Raced: Similar because both mean moving very fast; differs because racing is sustained competitive top speed, often of vehicles or athletes, not the instantaneous flash of a dart.
  • Rushed: Similar because both mean moving fast under pressure; differs because rushing is urgent, disorderly haste — it lacks the precision, lightness and straightness of darting.
