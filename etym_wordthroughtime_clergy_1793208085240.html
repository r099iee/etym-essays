- Apostolic age — the sacred lot: Greek klēros, a cast lot and allotted inheritance, becomes the Christians name for the ministry as God portion.
- 3rd–5th c. — the order hardens: bishops, priests, and deacons emerge as the clerus distinct from the laicus, the people; dress, discipline, and courts follow.
- Medieval — the First Estate: the clergy pray, the nobles fight, the commons work — and the praying estate owns a third of England, tries its own criminals, and taxes in tithes.
- Reformation — the order splits: reformers keep ministers but attack priestcraft; the word clergy itself becomes a small confessional battleground.
- 1790s — the radical indictment: as in the source sentence, the established clergy appear as pensioned sanctifiers of repression, and anticlerical fury peaks.
- Today — ministry without monopoly: an inclusive, professional clergy serving voluntary congregations, while journalists lend the word to the functionaries of every faith.
