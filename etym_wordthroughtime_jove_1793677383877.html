- Late 14th Century - Integrated into Middle English through Anglo-Norman translations and courtly poetry (notably Geoffrey Chaucer's Troilus and Criseyde), serving as the supreme pagan sovereign of cosmic fate and planetary fortune.
- 16th to 17th Century - Celebrated in Elizabethan and Jacobean drama (Shakespeare, Marlowe, Milton) as the preeminent symbol of imperial majesty, monarchical authority, and theatrical wonder, whose thunder symbolized irresistible power.
- 18th Century - Neoclassical poetry maintained Jove as an elevated mythological reference, while popular speech began converting the deity's name into the mild secular exclamation "by Jove!" to avoid taking the Christian God's name in vain.
- 19th Century (Victorian & Edwardian Era) - "By Jove!" reached its cultural peak as an emblematic gentlemanly interjection among the British upper classes, expressing polite astonishment or sporting camaraderie in colonial and domestic literature.
- 20th to 21st Century - Largely retired from everyday modern speech into affectionate period pastiche, while surviving permanently in astronomical science (Jovian studies) and classical scholarship as the venerable name of Rome's greatest god.
