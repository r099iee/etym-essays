• Peeked: Similar because both name a quick, secretive, partial look, and peek is the probable sibling of peep; differs because peek is a playful look round a corner or over a shoulder, while peep leans on the aperture — a look through a crack, chink, or leaf-screen, with concealment built into the image.
  • Glanced: Similar because both are brief looks; differs because a glance is quick but open and casual, with no secrecy or concealment, whereas peeping is furtive, partial, and hidden.
  • Spied: Similar because both involve covert observation; differs because spying adds sustained, deliberate surveillance and the gathering of intelligence, while a peep is a momentary, bird-like act of curiosity rather than espionage.
  • Stared: Similar because both are acts of looking; differs because staring is long, fixed, and unashamed — the exact opposite of peeping's brief, furtive, partial vision.
  • Gazed: Similar because both are ways of looking; differs because gazing is prolonged, absorbed, and open, an act of admiration or deep attention, while peeping is snatched, cautious, and incomplete.
  • Glimpsed: Similar because both name a partial, imperfect view; differs because glimpsing happens to you — something passes briefly into sight — whereas peeping is a deliberate action the looker performs from hiding.
  • Peered: Similar because both involve looking hard at something; differs because peering is an effort to see clearly (squinting through dimness or distance), while peeping is an effort not to be seen, the looker's own concealment being the point.
  • Squinted: Similar because both may be small, half-closed looks; differs because squinting is an effort of the eyes themselves against glare or poor vision, with no element of secrecy or hiding.
  • Pried: Similar because both can mean looking where one should not; differs because prying is sustained, nosy intrusion into private affairs, while a peep is a single brief stolen glance.
  • Keeked: Similar because keek is the Scots and Ulster-Scots member of the very same look-word family; differs because it is regional and dialectal, confined to Northern speech, while peeped is the standard English form.
