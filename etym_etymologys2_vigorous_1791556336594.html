Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*weǵ-
"to be lively, alert, fresh, strong"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*weg-ē-
"to be lively, thrive"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
vigēre
"to be lively, flourish, be strong, thrive"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
vigor
"liveliness, activity, force, energy"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th–5th c. CE)
vigōrōsus
"full of vigor, strong, energetic"
↓
Old French (c. 12th c.)
vigorous / vigoreus
"strong, sturdy, active, vehement"
↓
Anglo-Norman (c. 13th c.)
vigorous
"forceful, courageous, energetic"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
vigorous
"physically strong, energetic, forceful"

Note: The PIE root *weǵ- is also the ancestor of English wake, watch, and vigilant, sharing the archaic root concept of watchful, aroused vitality and alertness that blossomed into physical force and flourishing growth in the Latin stative verb vigēre.
