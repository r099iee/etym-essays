Note on the debate: the origin of Latin pīla "pillar" is debated. Some scholars connect it with pīlum "javelin, pestle" and a PIE root of crushing or pounding (cf. Greek ptissein "to pound grain"), so that a pillar is literally "compacted, pounded matter"; others treat pīla as of uncertain derivation. No PIE ancestor is securely attested, so the chain begins at Latin. Note that English also has unrelated homographs pile (a sharp stake, from Latin pīlum) and pile (fine surface fibre, from Latin pilus "hair"); "piled" in this card belongs to the pīla "heap" family only.
