• Firkin: Similar because both refer to staved wooden coopered vessels historically used for packing and measuring commercial salted butter; differs because a firkin is a precise statutory unit of capacity and weight (traditionally 56 lbs of butter or 9 imperial gallons of ale), whereas buttertub is a broader descriptive container term.
  • Tub: Similar because both are wide, low, open-topped or lidded cylindrical containers; differs because tub is a generic hypernym used for washing, bathing, or general utilitarian storage, whereas buttertub is specialized exclusively for dairy preservation.
  • Crock: Similar because both are traditional household containers for storing butter and perishable food; differs because a crock is made of glazed earthenware or heavy stoneware ceramic rather than coopered wooden staves.
  • Churn: Similar because both are wooden or metal vessels essential to the traditional dairy industry; differs because a churn is an active mechanical device with a plunger or paddle used to agitate cream into butter, whereas a buttertub is purely a passive storage and transport vessel.
  • Kilderkin: Similar because both are staved wooden coopered containers; differs because a kilderkin is an eighteen-gallon cask primarily designed for storing and shipping liquids (such as ale, wine, or liquid soap) rather than dense packed dairy solids.
  • Pail: Similar because both are portable wooden or metal dairy containers; differs because a pail is fitted with an overhead swinging wire or rope bail handle and designed for carrying fresh milk or water rather than airtight packing.
  • Pot: Similar because both held butter in domestic kitchens; differs because a pot is a small domestic ceramic or metallic container of variable dimensions rather than a sturdy coopered bulk export container.
