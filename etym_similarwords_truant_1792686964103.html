• Absentee: Similar because both refer to an individual who is not present at a required location or duty; differs because absentee is a neutral, formal administrative designation that includes excused and unexcused absences, whereas truant specifically implies willful, illicit avoidance.
  • Fugitive: Similar because both are runaways fleeing from institutional authority or confinement (Wordsworth explicitly pairs them: "Keen as a Truant or a Fugitive"); differs because a fugitive is actively fleeing criminal prosecution or severe physical capture, whereas a truant is avoiding daily scholastic or civil duty.
  • Runaway: Similar because both have escaped from their rightful custody or designated place; differs because a runaway has permanently or semi-permanently abandoned home and family, whereas a truant typically returns home after evading school for the day.
  • Shirker: Similar because both deliberately avoid their assigned duties or responsibilities; differs because a shirker focuses primarily on evading hard physical or mental labor (often while remaining physically present), while a truant physically flees the scene.
  • Malingerer: Similar because both evade compulsory work or education; differs because a malingerer specifically feigns illness or injury as an excuse, whereas a truant simply skips attendance without providing any excuse.
  • Slacker: Similar because both exhibit indolence and neglect of required productivity; differs because slacker is an informal, low-intensity colloquialism describing half-hearted effort, whereas truant involves the unauthorized physical act of absence.
  • Deserter: Similar because both abandon their sworn post without authorization; differs because deserter is a severe military or legal crime carrying extreme penal consequences, whereas truant applies primarily to school-age youth or light duty.
  • Vagrant: Similar because historically in Middle English, truant meant an idle wanderer or beggar; differs because in modern usage, a vagrant is an impoverished, unhoused person with no fixed abode, while a truant is an errant student.
  • Loafer: Similar because both prefer idle leisure to disciplined routine; differs because a loafer idly hangs about without purpose, whereas a truant actively defies school regulations and authority.
  • Hooky-player: Similar because both describe a student skipping school without permission; differs because hooky-player (from "playing hooky") is an informal, colloquial American idiom, whereas truant is the formal legal, educational, and high-literary standard term.
