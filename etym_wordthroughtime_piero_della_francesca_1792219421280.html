- 15th c. (Quattrocento) — Celebrated throughout central Italy by princely patrons (Federico da Montefeltro, Sigismondo Malatesta) and mathematician Luca Pacioli as both the "monarch of painting" and the foremost geometer of his age.
- 16th c. — Biographized by Giorgio Vasari in *Le Vite*, who noted that his early blindness saddened his final years, though his mathematical treatises were partially eclipsed and absorbed by Pacioli's published works.
- 17th–18th c. — Fell into relative obscurity during the Baroque and Rococo eras, as artistic taste favored theatrical emotionalism, dynamic diagonal motion, and illusionistic painterly drama over serene geometric calculation.
- Mid-to-late 19th c. — Rediscovered with immense enthusiasm by British connoisseurs (Sir Austen Henry Layard, John Addington Symonds), culminating in the National Gallery's acquisition of *The Baptism of Christ* in 1861.
- 20th c. — Hailed by modernist critics (Roger Fry, Kenneth Clark, Charles Bouleau) and avant-garde painters (Cézanne, the Cubists, Mondrian) as the quintessential Renaissance precursor to modern abstract spatial construction.
- 21st c. — Universally canonized in global cultural heritage as the ultimate master of perspective, geometric harmony, and timeless Renaissance humanism.
