• posthumous: Similar because both relate to matters arising after death; differs because posthumous is purely a chronological marker with no legal force, whereas testamentary implies the legal force of a will.
  • hereditary: Similar because both involve transmission after death; differs because hereditary describes passive, automatic transmission by bloodline, whereas testamentary involves the deliberate, authored act of a will.
  • probate: Similar because both belong to the legal settlement of a deceased person's estate; differs because probate names the official court process of proving the will, whereas testamentary is the adjective describing whatever pertains to the will.
  • testatory: Similar because both relate to making a will; differs because testatory is rare and archaic, whereas testamentary is the standard legal form.
  • testamental: Similar because both are adjectives formed on "testament"; differs because testamental is a rare variant, whereas testamentary is the accepted usage.
  • intestate: Similar because both are legal adjectives about wills; differs because intestate means dying WITHOUT a will, whereas testamentary means relating TO a will.
  • bequeathal: Similar because both concern disposing of property at death; differs because bequeathal names the act of leaving a gift, whereas testamentary is the adjective of the will itself.
  • mortuary: Similar because both relate to death; differs because mortuary concerns the body, burial, or funeral, whereas testamentary concerns the legal will.
  • final: Similar because both suggest something conclusive and last; differs because final is general and non-technical, whereas testamentary specifically means "embodied in a witnessed will."
  • authoritative: Similar because both mean commanding or binding; differs because authoritative commands through present power or expertise, whereas testamentary derives its binding power from the absent author's witnessed will.
