• tattered: Similar because both describe worn cloth or garments; differs because tattered means ripped into hanging, jagged shreds by violent tearing or severe decay, while frayed is the localized unspooling of edge fibers that leaves the cloth's core intact.
  • threadbare: Similar because both denote damage from rubbing; differs because threadbare means the surface nap is worn completely away until the warp and weft show through as thin, semi-transparent fabric, whereas frayed focuses on the loose border threads at the edges, not the thinning of the whole surface.
  • ragged: Similar because both suggest a rough, worn appearance; differs because ragged implies general jagged tearing, unevenness, and overall dishevelment across a garment, whereas frayed is a precise, localized description of edge-wear.
  • worn: Similar because both mean damaged by use; differs because worn is a broad, neutral term covering any kind of use-erosion (thinning, smoothing, fading), while frayed names the specific phenomenon of threads unspooling at an edge.
  • shabby: Similar because both can signal poverty and neglect in appearance; differs because shabby refers to a general state of dingy, rundown, ill-kept condition, whereas frayed is a precise physical descriptor that can describe a single edge of an otherwise clean garment.
  • raveled / unravelled: Similar because both describe threads coming undone; differs because raveled means the weave or knit itself is coming undone (threads pulled loose and separating), while frayed refers specifically to the edge abraded by rubbing, which may or may not then unravel further.
  • dog-eared: Similar because both describe worn edges; differs because dog-eared is specific to paper pages folded and crumpled at the corners by handling, whereas frayed applies to fabric, rope, cable, and any material edge worn by friction.
  • moth-eaten: Similar because both describe damaged cloth; differs because moth-eaten means riddled with holes eaten by insect larvae, a chemical/biological destruction, whereas frayed is purely mechanical wear from rubbing.
  • scuffed: Similar because both denote surface wear from contact; differs because scuffed means scratched or marked on the surface (of shoes, floors, paint), while frayed means fibers physically separating at the edge.
  • shredded: Similar because both involve fibers separating; differs because shredded implies violent tearing into narrow strips or pieces, while frayed is gradual attrition leaving the material substantially intact.
