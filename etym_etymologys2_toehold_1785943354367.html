CHAIN FOR THE FIRST CONSTITUENT, TOE (3 attested/reconstructed stages)

  1. Proto-Indo-European *deyḱ- "to point out, to show" (reconstructed, c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE). The same root yields Latin digitus "finger, toe" and Greek deiknūmi "to show, point out." The toe is conceived as a "pointer" — the small pointing member of the foot.
  2. Proto-Germanic *taihwǭ "toe" (reconstructed). Regular operation of Grimm's Law shifts PIE *d- to PGmc *t- and PIE *ḱ- to PGmc *h-. The family survives in Old Norse tā, Old High German zēha, and modern German Zehe.
  3. Old English tā "toe, each of the terminal members of the foot" (attested from the 8th century; plural tān). The form tō in Middle English and toe in Modern English (with the spelling fixed in the 16th century) continues it directly.

CHAIN FOR THE SECOND CONSTITUENT, HOLD (3 attested/reconstructed stages)

  1. Proto-Indo-European *kel- "to drive, urge, set in motion; to tend" (reconstructed, c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE). The same root yields Latin colere "to cultivate, to tend" (whence colony, culture) and Greek kellein "to drive." The link to "hold" is the idea of keeping something in one's care and preventing it from straying.
  2. Proto-Germanic *haldaną "to keep, to hold, to tend" (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE; attested in Gothic haldan "to tend, keep, pasture" and Old Norse halda "to hold").
  3. Old English healdan "to hold, keep, retain, guard" (attested from the 8th century; a class VII strong verb, past hēold). Middle English holden and Modern English hold continue it, and the noun hold "a place to grip, a grasp" develops from the verb in the medieval period.

COMPOUND FORMATION

  1. English noun toe + English noun hold combined as a compound in the 19th century (first recorded in the 1880s in British mountaineering literature) to denote a minimal foothold for the toes on a rock face. The figurative senses (business, military, wrestling) follow in the 20th century.

→ Modern English TOEHOLD (the exact form of this card).

DEBATED / NUANCED POINTS

  • The PIE root of "hold," usually given as *kel- "to drive, tend" (Watkins), is a reconstruction, and its semantic link to "hold / keep" is inferred rather than directly attested.
  • The derivation of "toe" from *deyḱ- is standard (it matches Latin digitus), but some scholars treat the Germanic word as an independent formation; the details of the PGmc suffix and the earliest vocalism are debated.
