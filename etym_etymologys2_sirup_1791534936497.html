Proto-Semitic (c. 3rd millennium BCE)
*š-r-b
"to drink, sip, absorb"
↓
Classical Arabic (pre-7th c. CE)
šariba
"to drink, swallow"
↓
Arabic medical noun (c. 8th–9th c. CE)
šarāb
"drink, beverage, medicinal potion"
↓
Medieval Latin (Salerno) (c. 11th–12th c.)
siruppus / syrupus
"medicinal sweetened beverage"
↓
Old French (late 13th c.)
sirop
"sweet medicinal draft"
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
sirup / sirop
"medicinal thick sweet potion"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–present)
sirup
"thick viscous sweet liquid"

Note: The root is directly cognate with Arabic šarba(t), which gave rise to English sherbet and sorbet, as well as shrub (a vinegar-fruit cordial). While the spelling syrup became dominant in the 19th and 20th centuries due to Renaissance humanists hyper-correcting the vowel to match Greek syrma or classical orthography, sirup represents the unbroken direct descendant of Middle English scribal tradition and remains an authoritative standard variant.
