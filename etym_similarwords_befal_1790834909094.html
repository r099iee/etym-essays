• Happen: Similar because both express the occurrence of an event; differs because happen is a neutral, everyday, high-frequency verb applicable to any minor or major incident, lacking the elevated, poetic gravity and ominous nuance of befal.
  • Occur: Similar because both denote an event taking place; differs because occur is formal, objective, and analytical, emphasizing factual realization or presence without the personal impact or fateful weight carried by befal.
  • Betide: Similar because both are archaic/literary verbs meaning to happen to someone as their destiny; differs because betide is even more archaic, almost exclusively surviving in fixed idioms ("woe betide him") or romantic verse.
  • Transpire: Similar because both describe events coming to pass; differs because transpire originally meant to leak out or become known gradually and is often used formally for complex developments over time, rather than a sudden event falling upon someone.
  • Overtake: Similar because both involve an event or force coming upon an individual; differs because overtake emphasizes catching up with someone, surprising them, or overpowering them through superior speed or force.
  • Chance: Similar because both can mean to happen without human intent; differs because chance specifically highlights accidental occurrence, sheer coincidence, or random luck, whereas befal often carries connotations of fate, consequence, or destiny.
