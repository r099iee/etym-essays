Note: Formed from Latin re- ("back, again") and capere ("to catch, grasp", displaying standard Latin vowel weakening from -căp- to -cĭp- in compound verbs). The Anglo-Norman diphthong -ei- was preserved in English spelling (producing the famous 'i before e except after c' orthographic rule), while central Old French evolved into recevoir. In the Ruskin context, the participle bears the martial naval sense of 'sustaining, enduring, or absorbing incoming fire or impacts'.
