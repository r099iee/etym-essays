• Sleeps: Similar because it is the same verb in the same person and number; differs because it is the modern neutral form, while sleepeth is archaic, sacred or mock-solemn in register.
  • Slumbers: Similar because it denotes sleeping, especially gentle, peaceful sleep; differs because it is poetic-literary, implies serene or light sleep, and is a distinct verb rather than an inflection.
  • Dozes: Similar because it means to sleep; differs because it denotes brief, half-awake sleep and a colloquial register.
  • Naps: Similar because it means to sleep; differs because it implies a short daytime sleep and relaxed informality.
  • Drowses: Similar because it names the edge of sleep; differs because it describes growing heavy-eyed rather than actual sleeping.
  • Rests: Similar because rest implies ceasing activity, as sleep does; differs because rest does not require unconsciousness and is far broader.
  • Reposes: Similar because it means to lie at rest, often asleep, and shares the solemn tone; differs because it stresses posture and tranquility rather than unconsciousness, and is formal-elevated rather than archaic.
  • Lies / Lieth: Similar because a sleeper lies down; differs because it names only the recumbent posture, not sleep itself.
  • Kips / Dosses (British slang): Similar because both also mean to sleep; differs because they are rough, casual slang for sleeping anywhere — the polar opposite of sleepeth's solemnity.
