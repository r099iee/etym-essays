• Articulate: Similar because both describe speech, writing, or thought that is clearly understood and effectively communicated; differs because articulate emphasizes vocal delivery, fluid elegance of phrasing, and phonetic clarity of expression, whereas coherent focuses on the underlying logical architecture, structural integrity, and absence of internal contradiction.
  • Lucid: Similar because both characterize discourse that is easily grasped by an audience without confusion; differs because lucid emphasizes radiant, luminous clarity, simplicity, and transparency that makes complex ideas effortlessly intelligible, whereas coherent emphasizes the rigorous, systematic interlocking of parts within an organic whole.
  • Consistent: Similar because both denote freedom from internal conflict, contradiction, or discrepancy; differs because consistent is often narrower, meaning that elements do not clash or that a person's conduct remains uniform over time, whereas coherent requires that the parts not only avoid clashing but actively integrate into a meaningful, comprehensive pattern.
  • Logical: Similar because both involve sound reasoning and rational deduction; differs because logical denotes strict formal compliance with the technical rules of syllogistic or inductive validity, whereas coherent encompasses aesthetic, narrative, psychological, and physical unity as well.
  • Cohesive: Similar because both derive from Latin haerēre and denote parts sticking together; differs because cohesive is predominantly applied to physical matter, molecular attraction, or interpersonal group solidarity (e.g., a cohesive military unit or cohesive soil), whereas coherent is primarily reserved for intellectual systems, texts, speech, and wave-phase alignment.
  • Rational: Similar because both signify mental sanity, reasonableness, and the presence of order; differs because rational characterizes the fundamental cognitive faculty of sound judgment and sensible motives (contrasted with mad or emotional), whereas coherent describes the structural intelligibility and interconnectedness of the expression itself.
