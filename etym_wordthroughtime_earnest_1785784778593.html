- Old English eornoste / eornost — Perceived as resolute intensity and zealous seriousness, including martial or spiritual firmness; "in earnest" precursors in intent action.
- Middle English ernest — Clear contrast with jest and game; seriousness of purpose in speech and bargains; earnest as pledge money enters commercial English.
- Early Modern English earnest — Moral and religious prose elevates earnestness as sincerity before God and neighbor; adjective describes persons of gravity and good faith.
- Victorian earnest — Cultural keyword of high seriousness, duty, and anti-frivolity; also ripe for satire when earnestness seems pompous or naive.
- 20th c. earnest — Standard positive term for sincere effort; "in earnest" fully idiomatic; earnest money fixed in legal/real-estate jargon.
- Contemporary English earnest — Still praises committed sincerity; mild pejorative available ("a bit earnest") for humorless zeal; core denotation stable.
