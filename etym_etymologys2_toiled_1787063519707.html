NOTE — DEBATED / NUANCED STAGES: (1) The Latin chain: tudes "hammer" is securely built on the PIE root *(s)teu- (cf. Sanskrit tudati "he thrusts, pushes", Old Norse støkkva "to spur", Old English þȳdan "to thrust, press"). The diminutive tudicula "small hammer, mallet" and the denominative verb tudiculāre "to crush with a hammer" are the standard bridge into Romance, but the exact Vulgar Latin intermediate is not directly attested in every dictionary — some authorities reconstruct *todicare — so this stage is marked as reconstructed, not guessed. (2) The Old French form appears in the manuscripts as toillier, toeillier, toiller; the headword spelling varies. (3) The homonym "toils" (nets) is a different word from Latin tela and is included only to prevent confusion; it has no toiled form.
