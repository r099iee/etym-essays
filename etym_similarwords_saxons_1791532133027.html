• Angles: A fellow Germanic people of the same North Sea coast and Bede's first-named settler group; differs because the Angles gave their name to England and English, while the Saxons gave their name to Wessex, Essex, Sussex and to the continental Saxons.
  • Jutes: The third people in Bede's triad, closely associated with the Saxon migration; differs because the Jutes were a much smaller group, settling mainly Kent and the Isle of Wight, and left no lasting territorial name in England.
  • Frisians: Coastal Germanic neighbours who shared the North Sea seafaring culture and probably contributed settlers to Britain; differs because the Frisians retained a distinct homeland and language in the Netherlands rather than becoming a name for the English.
  • Anglo-Saxons: The compound term for the merged Germanic peoples of early England; differs because it is a later scholarly construct joining two names, whereas Saxons is the historical name of one constituent people.
  • Old Saxons: The continental Saxons of Germany, speakers of Old Saxon; differs because it specifies the homeland branch and its language, not the migrant branch that settled Britain.
  • Teutons: An ancient Germanic people used loosely by Latin writers for Germanic groups generally; differs because it is a broader, often archaic umbrella term, not the name of this specific confederation.
  • Germans: The modern national grouping to which the continental Saxons belong; differs because it is a nineteenth-century national category, far wider and politically different from the early medieval tribal name.
  • Norsemen: Scandinavian raiders and settlers who later attacked England; differs because they came from Scandinavia in the Viking Age and are linguistically and culturally distinct from the Saxon settlers.
  • Sassenach: The Gaelic and Irish term for an Englishman, derived from Saxon; differs because it is a loanword in a Celtic language with a hostile or mocking register, not an English word for the tribe.
  • Saxony: The territory named after the Saxons; differs because it is a place-name, and the modern German state covers a different area from the original tribal homeland.
