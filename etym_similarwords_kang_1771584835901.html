• Bed: Similar because both are places to sleep; differs because a bed is unheated, individual, and for lying, while the kang is a heated multi-purpose platform serving the whole household by night and day.
  • Platform: Similar because both are raised horizontal surfaces; differs because a platform is a thing one stands on and says nothing of heat, while a kang is a heated surface one lives upon.
  • Daybed: Similar because both are low seats for lying; differs because a daybed is unheated and individual, missing the essential heating and the communal function of the kang.
  • Divan: Similar because both are long low seats that can serve for reclining; differs because a divan is upholstered, unheated, and a portable piece of furniture, while the kang is a fixed masonry platform heated from within.
  • Ondol: Similar because both are flue-heated masonry surfaces; differs because the ondol heats the whole floor of a Korean room while the kang is a raised platform, and ondol has never entered English as freely as kang.
  • Kotatsu: Similar because both solve the same East-Asian winter-heating problem; differs because a kotatsu is a low table with a heat source beneath a quilt that people sit under, not a platform one sleeps on.
  • Futon: Similar because both are East-Asian sleeping surfaces; differs because a futon is a soft mattress laid on the floor — unheated and portable — while the kang is a fixed, heated brick platform.
  • Bench: Similar because both are long communal seats; differs because a bench is unheated and for sitting only, while the kang is heated and used for every activity of daily life.
