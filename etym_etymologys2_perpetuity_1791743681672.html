Note: Latin perpetuus was formed from the prefix per- ("through, throughout") and the root of petere ("to seek, rush toward", from PIE *peth₂- "to fly, rush"). The radical sense was of something that "rushes completely through" without interruption or obstruction. In Roman law, in perpetuum denoted perpetual grants, a formula that entered English feudal law and eventually required the celebrated common law "Rule Against Perpetuities" to prevent aristocratic families from locking up land in perpetuity.
