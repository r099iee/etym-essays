• Settler: a person who settles in a new region; nearly synonymous, but more neutral and less tied to a metropolitan or imperial framework, and it is now the preferred term in much academic writing.
  • Colonizer: an agent or architect of colonisation; similar because both words describe people who establish a colony, but "colonizer" foregrounds power, policy and dispossession, whereas "colonist" foregrounds residence and settlement.
  • Pioneer: an early settler or developer of a new area; similar in describing first arrivals, but it is celebratory and individualist, while "colonist" is collective and political.
  • Migrant: a person who moves from one place to another; similar in involving relocation, but a migrant need not settle permanently or belong to a colony, and the word carries no imperial relation.
  • Immigrant: a person who comes to live permanently in a foreign country; similar in permanence, but immigration is usually into an established polity, whereas colonisation involves founding or reshaping one.
  • Expatriate: a person living outside their native country; similar in residence abroad, but it usually implies privilege, temporary stay and no claim to found a colony.
  • Planter: a settler, especially a plantation owner in the colonial Atlantic; similar as an early modern colonial term, but it is narrower, agrarian and often associated with slavery.
  • Homesteader: a settler granted land under a homestead law; similar in farming settlement, but it is specific to nineteenth-century frontier legislation, especially in the United States.
  • Frontiersman: a person living on a frontier; similar in the imagery of first settlement, but it is masculine, adventurous and mythologised, and not necessarily tied to a colony.
  • Occupier: a person or force holding territory; similar in the fact of presence on others' land, but it stresses military control rather than settlement.
  • Colonial: an adjective or a person of a colonial society; similar in reference to colonies, but the adjective describes institutions and styles, and the noun is rarer and more old-fashioned than "colonist".
  • Colon: a punctuation mark or a body part; identical in spelling only, and unrelated etymologically, a classic false friend.
