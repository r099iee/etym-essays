- [c. 1000–500 BCE: Vedic & Early Epic Formation] — Emerged in late Vedic and early epic literature as a patronymic title (*jāhnavī*, from *Jahnu*), establishing the legend of the sage who swallowed and released the impetuous celestial river — [perception: sacred title demonstrating the awesome authority of ascetic *tapas* to master even cosmic elemental deities]
- [c. 500 BCE–800 CE: Classical Sanskrit Kāvya & Purāṇic Systematization] — Fully canonized in the Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata, and major Purāṇas as an elite, poetic name for the Ganges, recurring throughout ornate court poetry (*kāvya*) and pilgrim itineraries — [perception: exalted name evoking royal lineage, maternal grace, and supreme ritual purification]
- [c. 800–1800 CE: Medieval Stotra Liturgy & Bhakti Movement] — Centralized in pan-Indian devotional hymnology (such as Ādi Śaṅkarācārya's *Gaṅgā Stotram*) as an affectionate, intimate name invoked by pilgrims seeking absolution and *mokṣa* — [perception: beloved mother goddess and supreme purifier of sins throughout devotional Hinduism]
- [19th c.–Present: Modern Pan-Indian & Global Usage] — Maintained in literary, religious, and geographic discourse alongside widespread adoption as a popular Indian feminine given name (Jahnavi/Janhavi) — [perception: celebrated symbol of timeless sacred tradition, feminine nobility, and cultural continuity]
