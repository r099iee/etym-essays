• uniform: Similar because both are full garments that dress a whole body for a role; differs because the uniform displays institutional rank and membership outward, while the overall conceals and protects privately.
  • apron: Similar because both are protective coverings worn over ordinary clothes; differs because the apron guards only the front of the body and leaves the wearer's own clothes visible, while the overall engulfs the entire figure.
  • smock: Similar because both are loose coverall garments for work; differs because the smock is the bohemian, aesthetic cover of the painter or studio worker, while the overall is the dust-grey, custodial livery of a keeper.
  • boiler suit: Similar because both are one-piece protective working garments; differs because the boiler suit stresses industrial, engine-room labour, while the overall here belongs to the museum and the servant's ritual of dust.
  • dungarees: Similar because both are hard-wearing protective workwear; differs because dungarees are bib-and-brace trousers of farm and industry, while the overall is a one-piece suit covering the trunk as well as the limbs.
  • coveralls: Similar because coveralls is the standard American one-piece protective suit, essentially the same garment as the British overall; differs because coveralls is strictly plural and strictly industrial, while overall also carries its adjective and adverb senses of totality.
  • frock: Similar because both are loose outer garments; differs because the frock is a woman's or child's dress or a loose smock-like garment, not a protective work layer, and carries no connotation of guarding one's clothes from dirt.
