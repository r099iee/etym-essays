- c. 1200 — Ecclesiastical Arrival: Enters Middle English exclusively as a liturgical and theological term denoting Christ's redemption of humanity from sin, death, and damnation.
- 14th–15th c. — Liturgical and Homiletic Dominance: Permeates medieval mystery plays, sermons, and devotional poetry; associated with profound spiritual anxiety and the eternal fate of the soul.
- 16th–17th c. — Reformation Polemics: Becomes the central battlefield of theological controversy, fiercely debated in terms of faith, works, predestination, and divine grace across Protestant and Catholic Europe.
- 18th c. — Secular and Institutional Expansion: Broadens into political, philosophical, and aesthetic prose (as in Reynolds's Discourses), signifying the preservation of cultural standards and civil order against chaotic impulses.
- 19th c. — Social Gospel and Moral Activism: Applied to movements rescuing the destitute from poverty and moral vice, famously institutionalized in William Booth's founding of the Salvation Army.
- 20th–21st c. — Metaphorical Ubiquity and Modern Nuance: Frequently applied to economic bailouts, corporate turnarounds, sports victories, and environmental conservation, while retaining its aura of decisive, existential rescue.
