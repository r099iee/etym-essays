- [15th century: from Latin sumptuosus] - 'Sumptuous' enters English meaning costly, magnificent, lavish; connotation is aristocratic - the expense of a great household or feast.
- [18th-19th century: moral ambivalence] - In an age of frugal virtue, 'sumptuousness' carries a hint of reproach: excess, expense, and self-indulgence - the display that moralists mistrusted even while admirers marvelled.
- [Modern usage] - 'Sumptuousness' denotes the quality of being expensive-looking, luxurious, and lavish; connotation is admiring but hushed - wealth as a total environment, old-fashioned grandeur rather than modern flash.
