CHAIN 1 — THE ELEMENT "ZEUS" (Dio-): Stage 1 — PIE *dyew- "sky, heaven, daylight" (reconstructed root of the bright sky and of the sky-god; extended as *dyews, attested across the family: Sanskrit dyaus, Greek Zeus, Latin Iuppiter and diēs "day"). Stage 2 — Proto-Hellenic *dyews "sky-god Zeus" (the ancestral form of the Greek god's name). Stage 3 — Greek Zeus (Ζεύς) "Zeus, the sky-god" (attested from Mycenaean di-we and classical Greek) with the compound stem Dio- (Διο-), the oblique base used in compounds and in the genitive Dios, "of Zeus". Stage 4 — Greek Dioklēs (Διοκλῆς) "glory of Zeus" — a compound of Dio- ("of Zeus") + -klēs ("glory"; see Chain 2) — attested as an ordinary Greek personal name from the classical period (e.g. the mathematician Diocles of Syracuse, 2nd century BCE). CHAIN 2 — THE ELEMENT "GLORY" (-cles): Stage 1 — PIE *ḱlew- "to hear" (reconstructed root of hearing, from which "fame" = "that which is heard"; the source of Greek kluein "to hear", kleos "glory, fame", and English loud, via *ḱlutó-). Stage 2 — Proto-Hellenic *kléwos "fame, glory" (abstract noun, "what is heard of one, renown"). Stage 3 — Greek kleos (κλέος) "fame, glory, renown" (attested from Homer, who made kleos aphthiton, "imperishable glory", the hero's goal in the Iliad). Stage 4 — Greek -klēs (-κλῆς) "glory" (the contracted nominal stem used in the great class of compound names: Periklēs "far-famed", Hēraklēs "glory of Hera", Themistoklēs "glory of right", and Dioklēs "glory of Zeus"). THE FULL NAME: Stage 5 — Latin Diocles "Diocles" (the Greek personal name borrowed into Latin; the emperor's birth name, recorded for the future emperor born in Dalmatia c. 244 CE). Stage 6 — Latin Diocletianus "Diocletian" (the full imperial name, formed from Diocles + the adjectival suffix -ianus, "belonging to, of Diocles"; the emperor's official name Caius Aurelius Valerius Diocletianus, assumed at his accession in 284 CE). Stage 7 — English Diocletian "the emperor Diocletian" (adopted from Latin Diocletianus in English historical writing by the 16th–17th centuries; the standard English form, naturalized in Gibbon and all later historians). FINAL NODE: DIOCLETIAN. DEBATE NOTE: the two elements of Dioklēs — Dio- "of Zeus" and -klēs "glory" — are securely attested and standard; the only nuance is that the future emperor came from humble Dalmatian stock and bore the common Greek name Diocles with no implication of Greek cultural identity, and that the -ianus suffix merely converts the personal name into the dynastic designation by which history knows him.
