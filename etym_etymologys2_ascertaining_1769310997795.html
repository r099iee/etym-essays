1. PIE *krei- "to sieve, separate, distinguish, decide" (reconstructed, c. 4500–3500 BCE). 2. Latin cernere "to sift, separate, perceive, distinguish" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE, e.g. Plautus). 3. Latin certus "fixed, settled, sure, certain" — past participle of cernere (attested 2nd–1st c. BCE; Cicero). 4. Old French certain "sure, fixed, determined" (via Gallo-Romance from certus; 9th–12th c.). 5. Old French acertener / ascertener "to make sure, certify, establish" (a- + certain + -ener; 11th–13th c., with manuscript variants such as asçertener). 6. Middle English ascertainen "to make certain, to inform officially, to establish (a fact, claim, or boundary)" (borrowed via Anglo-Norman; 14th c.). 7. English ascertain "to find out for certain, to discover and establish by investigation" (semantic reversal 17th–18th c.) → present participle/gerund ASCERTAINING. Debate / nuance notes: - The PIE root *krei- is securely reconstructed (Greek krī́nein "to judge, separate", Latin cernere), but the exact "sifting → deciding" semantic chain is an inference, not a documented shift. - The Old French verb shows spelling fluctuation (acertener / asçertener / asçertener); the a-/as- alternation is a normal Old French prefix variation, not a distinct root. Some scholars posit a hypothetical Vulgar Latin intermediate (*certāre, *certināre); such forms are asterisked reconstructions, best treated as inferred rather than attested. - Ascertaining is the ordinary English participial/gerund form of ascertain, produced by regular inflection.
