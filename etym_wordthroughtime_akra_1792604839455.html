- [Early Vedic Period (c. 1500–1000 BCE)] — Attested abundantly in the Ṛgveda as an evocative adjectival epithet of Indra, denoting his irresistible strength, cosmic potency, and capability to slay Vṛtra and release the rains — [perception: terrifying and glorious storm-warrior of divine capability]
- [Late Vedic & Brāhmaṇa Era (c. 1000–600 BCE)] — Transitioned from a descriptive modifier into an independent divine proper name, firmly cemented in sacrificial liturgies and royal consecration rituals (rājasūya) — [perception: divine archetype of the consecrated king and patron of royal power]
- [Classical Epic Period (Rāmāyaṇa & Mahābhārata, c. 500 BCE–400 CE)] — Consolidated as the undisputed king of Svarga and the universal literary benchmark of supreme earthly wealth, luxury, and palace splendour — [perception: supreme monarch of the celestial court, magnificent yet vulnerable to mortal austerities]
- [Early Buddhist & Jain Traditions (c. 5th c. BCE–5th c. CE)] — Transformed into Sakka in Theravāda Buddhism, revered as the humble, righteous protector of the Buddha and the Sangha, and as Saudharmendra in Jainism, inaugurator of the Tīrthaṅkaras' bathing ceremonies — [perception: enlightened celestial disciple and devoted guardian deity of spiritual truth]
- [Medieval & Pan-Asian Transmissions (c. 6th–14th c. CE)] — Disseminated along the Silk Road into China as Dìshìtiān and Japan as Taishakuten, guarding Buddhist temples, while in Sanskrit kāvya poetry he remained the classic trope for imperial grandeur — [perception: trans-continental guardian deity and immortal symbol of sovereign authority]
- [Modern Indology & Comparative Mythology (19th–21st c.)] — Studied by global comparative mythologists and linguists as the premier South Asian reflex of the ancient Indo-European thunder-sovereign archetype — [perception: iconic mythic figure representing the majestic golden age of ancient Indian religion]
