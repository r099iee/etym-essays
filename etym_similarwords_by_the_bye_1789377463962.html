• By the way: Similar because both serve as conversational discourse markers to introduce an incidental topic or parenthetical thought; differs because by the way is the universal, modern, neutral idiom in everyday speech, whereas by-the-bye has an elegant, literary, slightly archaic or formal Victorian flavor.
  • Incidentally: Similar because both indicate that a remark is supplementary or tangential to the primary conversation; differs because incidentally is a formal Latinate adverb suitable for academic, journalistic, and professional prose, whereas by-the-bye is an idiomatic Germanic prepositional phrase with stronger conversational and dramatic texture.
  • En passant: Similar because both denote something mentioned or done in passing; differs because en passant is a French loan phrase carrying sophisticated aristocratic or chess-related connotations, whereas by-the-bye is rooted in native English idiomatic parlance.
  • Parenthetically: Similar because both signal an aside or explanatory insertion; differs because parenthetically emphasizes structural separation or editorial containment (as if enclosed in punctuation marks), whereas by-the-bye flows organically as a conversational transition.
  • Apropos: Similar because both are used to introduce an associated thought or observation; differs because apropos (derived from French à propos) strictly signifies that the new topic is directly relevant and opportune to what was just said, whereas by-the-bye explicitly introduces a tangent, side-track, or casual detour.
  • As an aside: Similar because both step outside the primary discourse to share a secondary observation; differs because an aside often mimics theatrical staging where a character addresses the audience out of earshot of other characters, whereas by-the-bye is spoken directly to the conversational partner.
