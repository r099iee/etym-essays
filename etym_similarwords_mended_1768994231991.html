• repaired: Similar because both put something back in working order; differs because repair is the formal, mechanical term for machines, buildings, and infrastructure — a systematic operation — while mend is homely, used of clothes, tools, and convalescent bodies.
  • fixed: Similar because both make something usable again; differs because fix is the quick, unceremonious catch-all of modern speech, applicable from a car to a relationship, while mended implies patient, careful restoration.
  • restored: Similar because both return something to a sound state; differs because restore implies returning to original, often pristine condition, with conservationist or antiquarian overtones, while mend asks only that a thing be whole enough to use again.
  • patched: Similar because both repair damage; differs because patching is a temporary, partial, and often visible repair — a stopgap — while mending restores working order more thoroughly.
  • darned: Similar because both mend fabric; differs because darning is the specific needlework of filling a hole in cloth with woven stitches, while mending is the general word for any repair of clothes or other things.
  • stitched: Similar because both repair by sewing; differs because stitching is the physical act of needlework and also surgical (stitching a wound), while mending names the whole operation of restoring soundness.
  • healed: Similar because both mark recovery from illness or injury; differs because healing is biological and internal, invoking medicine, time, and narrative restoration, while mended treats the body as an object patched to working order — economical, unheroic.
  • cured: Similar because both end an illness; differs because cure implies a decisive medical or therapeutic intervention that eliminates disease, while mend is gradual, modest, and domestic — a body climbing back to usefulness.
  • amended: Similar because both improve what is defective; differs because amend is the formal word for correcting texts, laws, and behaviour, with official and legislative weight, while mend is plain and physical.
  • rectified: Similar because both put right what is wrong; differs because rectify is formal and precise, used of errors, circuits, and accounts, while mend is informal and material.
  • corrected: Similar because both remove fault; differs because correct is about accuracy and error — answers, manuscripts, manners — while mend is about wholeness and function in material things and bodies.
  • reformed: Similar because both improve conduct (mend one's ways); differs because reform implies systematic, institutional, or moral change on a large scale, while mend one's ways is individual, modest, and domestic.
  • renewed: Similar because both restore condition; differs because renew suggests freshness, vigour, or a new lease on life, while mend implies repair of what was damaged rather than a fresh start.
  • reconciled: Similar because both restore a damaged relationship (mend fences); differs because reconcile names the explicit making-up of estranged parties, while mending fences is the idiom for quietly restoring goodwill.
