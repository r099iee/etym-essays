CHAIN 1 — the first element, metá ("change, after"): - PIE *me- "with, among, in the middle" (spatial particle), reconstructed pre-PIE date; often written *meth₂- or *met- in modern handbooks. - Proto-Hellenic *metá "with, among" (reconstructed, 2nd millennium BCE). - Ancient Greek μετά (metá) "in the midst of, among; after, with, beyond" (Homeric Greek, 8th c. BCE). - Greek combining prefix μετα- (meta-) "after, beyond; changed; higher-order" (Classical, 5th c. BCE). - Greek μετωνυμία (metōnumía) "a change of name" (rhetorical compound, Hellenistic, 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE). - Latin metōnymia (borrowed by Roman rhetoricians, 1st–2nd c. CE). - Old French metonymie (medieval, 12th–14th c.). - English metonymy (first attested 1547, in Ralph Lever's The Arte of Reason). CHAIN 2 — the second element, ónuma ("name"): - PIE *h₁nómn̥ "name" (nominal root attested in every major branch of the family). - Proto-Hellenic *ónomə "name" (reconstructed, 2nd millennium BCE). - Ancient Greek ὄνομα (ónoma) "name; noun; fame" (Homeric, 8th c. BCE). - Greek dialectal variant ὄνυμα (ónuma) "name" (Doric/Aeolic; the form used in compounds). - Greek combining suffix -ωνυμία (-ōnumía) "naming, name-ness" (Hellenistic). - Employed as the second member of μετωνυμία (metōnumía) "a change of name," which passes through Latin metōnymia and Old French metonymie into English metonymy (1547). NOTE ON DEBATE: The exact shape of the PIE ancestor of metá is reconstructed rather than attested and is written variously *me-, *met-, or *meth₂-; and the compound metōnumía is a late Hellenistic rhetorical coinage (not a Homeric word), so the "change/beyond" force of metá in it is a technical extension of the older spatial sense. The two chains are independent above the level of the compound itself.
