- [18th century: equestrian origin] - Taken from French saccade, 'a sudden jerk of a horse's reins'; in English it described the sharp tug a rider gives to control or spur the mount.
- [19th century: transferred to abrupt motion] - Widened to any sudden, jerky movement or abrupt change of direction in gesture, writing, and the arts; still a word about sharp, impulsive pulls.
- [Mid-20th century: ophthalmology and neuroscience] - Adopted as the standard technical term — together with micro-saccades — for the rapid, ballistic, jump-like repositioning of the eyes from one fixation point to another; the word migrated from the stable to the laboratory.
- [Contemporary sense] - A fixture of vision science, psychology, and eye-tracking research, now familiar in discussion of reading and screens; its register is coolly mechanistic, denoting an abrupt jump, never a smooth glide.
