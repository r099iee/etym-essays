- 18th c.–19th c. - Originated in English horsemanship treatises, foxhunting memoirs, and pastoral agricultural literature to record subtle equine body language and auditory orientation in workhorses and hunters.
- Late 19th c. - Featured prominently in English translations of Russian animal realism (especially Leo Tolstoy's Kholstomer) and classic Victorian equestrian novels like Anna Sewell's Black Beauty, popularizing the phrase among the general reading public.
- 20th c.–Present - Maintained in modern equine ethology, veterinary behaviorism, and literary prose as a precise, descriptive marker of animal emotion, selective listening, and subtle resistance.
