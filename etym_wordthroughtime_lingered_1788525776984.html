- [10th–13th c. OE to ME] - Native verb lengan means "to prolong, delay, lengthen, or belong" - [connotation: temporal extension and geographical dwelling]
- [14th–15th c. Middle English] - Formed with frequentative suffix -eren as lengeren / lingeren, meaning "to dwell continually, hesitate, dally" - [connotation: repetitive hesitation and idle tarrying]
- [16th–17th c. Early Modern] - Vowel shifts firmly to i (linger); widespread in Shakespeare and Spenser for romantic longing and slow dying - [connotation: poignant emotional delay and mortal fading]
- [18th–19th c.] - Mastered by Romantic and Victorian poets (Wordsworth, Keats, Wilde) for atmospheric twilights, fading celestial bodies, and memory - [connotation: sublime aesthetic melancholy, dreamy reluctance to vanish]
- [20th–21st c.] - Retains universal literary, emotional, and sensory resonance, alongside medical and computer science jargon - [connotation: evocative persistence of traces, doubts, and fragrances]
