• totter: Similar because both describe unsteady motion from loss of balance; differs because totter is the vertical unsteadiness of one about to fall — weak knees, fading balance, a gesture toward imminent collapse — whereas wobble is a sustained lateral rocking on a loose pivot, without the same finality.
  • stagger: Similar because both describe unsteady, reeling movement; differs because stagger is whole-body movement while still attempting to walk, the lurching of a person struck, fatigued, or drunk, whereas wobble is a smaller, faster, rockier oscillation that can happen in place.
  • teeter: Similar because both describe precarious imbalance; differs because teeter is being poised on the very edge of tipping over, a single threshold about to be crossed, whereas wobble is repeated back-and-forth oscillation born of poor fit or lost control.
  • sway: Similar because both are side-to-side motions; differs because sway is smooth, regular, and often voluntary (a tree swaying in the wind, a crowd swaying to music), whereas wobble is jerky, loose, and involuntary, the rocking of something not properly fixed.
  • quiver / tremble: Similar because both name fine, rapid, unsteady motion; differs because quiver and tremble are fast vibrations in place, usually from emotion, cold, or tension, with no sense of lateral rocking or poor fit, whereas wobble rocks side to side on a loose base.
  • rock: Similar because both involve back-and-forth tilting; differs because rock is often gentle, regular, and deliberate (rocking a cradle), whereas wobble is irregular, jerky, and uncontrolled.
  • waver: Similar because both can mean figurative hesitation and physical unsteadiness; differs because waver leans toward the mental and emotional sense (wavering resolve, wavering flame) and a gentler unsteadiness, whereas wobble is more physical, jokier, and more about loose mechanics.
  • shake: Similar because both mean unsteady movement; differs because shake is a generic word for any rapid agitation and lacks wobble's specific image of lateral rocking on a loose pivot — a table shakes, but a loose wheel wobbles.
  • lurch: Similar because both describe unsteady movement; differs because lurch is a single violent sideways jerk or lunge, whereas wobble is a series of smaller, quicker oscillations — lurch is one event, wobble a condition.
  • vacillate: Similar because both cover the figurative sense of wavering between positions; differs because vacillate is the formal, intellectual word for indecision between alternatives, whereas wobble is informal and keeps the physical image of rocking loose.
  • jiggle / wiggle: Similar because both are small quick motions; differs because jiggle and wiggle are often playful or deliberate and need no loss of control or poor fit, whereas wobble implies something loose and out of balance.
