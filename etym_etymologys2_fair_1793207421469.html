Noun sense — market fair:
PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*dʰh₁s-
‘sacred, holy, divine’
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE)
feriae
‘festival days, holidays’
↓
Late Latin (4th c.)
feria
‘weekday; feast-day market’
↓
Old French (12th c.)
feire
‘fair, market, festival’
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
feire
‘fair, market, festival’
↓
Modern English (current)
Fair
‘periodic market’

Adjective sense — fair, beautiful and just:
PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*pak-
‘to fasten, join, fit’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*fagraz
‘suitable, fitting, nice’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
fæger
‘beautiful, pleasant’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
fair
‘beautiful; just; light’
↓
Modern English (current)
Fair
‘lovely; just; clear’

Note: the two modern fairs are complete homonyms from opposite ends of Europe — feast-day Latin and fitting-Germanic — whose spellings converged in Middle English; the exact PIE shape behind the adjective (*pak- or *peh₂ǵ-, to attach) is debated among handbooks, though the Germanic stages are secure.
