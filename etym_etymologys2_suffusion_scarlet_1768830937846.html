ROOT WORD 1 — SUFFUSION PIE *ǵʰew- "to pour" (c. 4000-3000 BCE) — the pour-root of the family; attested by Sanskrit juhóti "he pours (an oblation)" and Greek khéō "I pour" ↓ Latin fundō, fundere "to pour, to melt, to cast" (attested c. 3rd-1st c. BCE) — the unprefixed Latin verb; the extended senses "to melt" and "to cast metal" were already present ↓ suffundō, suffundere "to pour beneath or over" (sub- "under" + fundere "to pour"; attested c. 1st c. BCE - 1st c. CE) — the compound verb meaning to pour up from beneath and spread over ↓ suffūsiō, suffūsiōnis "an overspreading, a suffusion" (Late Latin, c. 4th-5th c.; in medical Latin, a suffusion of blood or fluid) — the noun formed on the supine stem suffus- ↓ English suffusion "a spreading of light, color, or fluid from within" (borrowed in the 16th c. from Latin suffūsiō) ROOT WORD 2 — SCARLET Persian saqirlāt "a rich cloth" (Sasanian Persian, pre-Islamic; the word names a costly fabric, not yet a color) ↓ Arabic siqillāṭ "a fine brocade, a costly cloth" (c. 8th-10th c.) — the Persian word borrowed into Arabic with the luxury-cloth sense ↓ Medieval Latin scarlatum "scarlet cloth" (c. 11th-12th c.) — the word entered Latin via the Arabic trade routes of the medieval Mediterranean ↓ Old French escarlate "scarlet cloth, costly red cloth" (12th c.) — passed through French on the way to England ↓ Middle English scarlat / scarlet "scarlet cloth; scarlet color" (13th c.) — the color sense develops from the dye of the prized cloth ↓ scarlet (Modern English) — the color name, retaining the cloth's associations of rank and ceremony Note: The ultimate origin of the Persian word is debated; the connection to Arabic siqillāṭ is well attested, and the earliest meaning everywhere is "costly cloth," with the color sense a later English and European development.
