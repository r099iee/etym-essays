• SPURNED: Similar because both mean refusing with contempt; differs because spurn keeps a trace of its physical origin in "striking with the foot" — pushed away like a beggar from the door — while scorn reaches back to the Old French image of cutting a reputation to shreds and can be expressed in a cold look rather than an action.
  • DESPISED: Similar because both mean holding in low esteem; differs because despise is the quieter, enduring, passive state of long-continued low estimation, while scorn is an active, passionate, often momentary eruption of contempt that may carry mockery in it.
  • REJECTED: Similar because both mean refusing something offered; differs because rejected is the neutral verb of refusal that says nothing about the refuser's feeling, while scorned adds the full apparatus of contempt, derision, and the sense that the thing was beneath consideration.
  • MOCKED: Similar because both mean expressing contempt for a person; differs because mocking nearly always means public verbal ridicule and laughter, while scorn can be expressed in a look or a silence and carries a colder, prouder, more decisive judgment.
  • DERIDED: Similar because both mean treating with contemptuous laughter; differs because deride stresses mockery and ridicule aimed at something as absurd or laughable, while scorn stresses proud disdain and a judgment of worthlessness without necessarily any laughter.
  • DISDAINED: Similar because both mean treating as unworthy; differs because disdain is a haughty sense of one's own superiority toward something beneath one (the disdain of the aristocrat), while scorn is more violent, with contempt, anger, or derision in it.
  • SNEERED AT: Similar because both express contempt; differs because sneer names the physical expression — a curled lip, a jeering remark — while scorn is the underlying attitude that may be expressed by the sneer or by silence.
  • DISPARAGED: Similar because both involve lowering a person's worth; differs because disparage means to belittle or speak slightingly of, often in report or gossip, while scorn is a direct, decisive rejection or contemptuous treatment of the person or thing itself.
  • REPUDIATED: Similar because both mean rejecting; differs because repudiate is a formal, deliberate disowning or refusal to accept (a debt, a doctrine, a child), while scorn is emotional — contemptuous rejection of what is felt to be cheap or shameful.
  • SHUNNED: Similar because both involve treating a person as unworthy of regard; differs because shun means to avoid systematically, to keep one's distance, while scorn confronts and judges — it can be active mockery, not mere avoidance.
