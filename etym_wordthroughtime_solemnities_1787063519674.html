- Latin sollemnia (annual fixed religious festivals) - Civic and religious weight: rites prescribed by sacred custom, recurring year after year.
- Medieval Christian usage (solempnete) - A solemnity is one of the Church's high feasts (Christmas, Easter); the plural names the great festival observances of the calendar.
- 14th–16th century English - "Solemnity" also means grave, earnest seriousness and the ceremonial pomp surrounding a rite; the word is at once the event and the mood.
- 17th–18th century - Broadens to secular state functions: coronations, funerals, official receptions; "solemnities" = the formal programme of a grand occasion.
- 19th century - Literary prose (Dickens, Thackeray) uses "solemnities" of imposing, occasionally pompous formalities; a faint ironic edge appears.
- 20th–21st century - Survives mainly in liturgical, legal, and formal registers; in ordinary speech it sounds archaic or mock-formal — Tolkien's Fili is "impatient at these solemnities.
