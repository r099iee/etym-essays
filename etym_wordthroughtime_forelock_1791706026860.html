- 14th Century (Late Middle English): Anatomical & Equine Coinage - Compounded from Germanic roots to describe the natural tress of hair growing above the forehead in humans and horses.
- 16th–17th Century (Renaissance & Early Modern): Classical Proverbial Transformation - Fused with Greek and Roman iconography of Kairos/Occasio in Spenser, Shakespeare, and Bacon to create the ubiquitous proverb "to take Time by the forelock."
- 18th Century: Maritime & Naval Rigging Jargon - Adopted by Royal Navy shipwrights for a wedge-shaped iron pin securing a through-bolt on timber knees and naval gun carriages.
- 19th Century (Victorian Era): Deferential Class Salute - Codified into the rural gesture of "tugging the forelock" as a humble salute given by tenant laborers and servants to landlords and squires.
- 20th–21st Century: Socio-Political Pejorative & Enduring Idiom - Sustained as a scathing political metaphor for obsequious servility ("forelock-tugging") alongside enduring equine grooming and literary proverbial usage.
