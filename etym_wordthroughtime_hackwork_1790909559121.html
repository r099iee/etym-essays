- 17th c. - The noun hack (shortened from hackney horse, a hired carriage horse) was applied in Grub Street London to mercenary writers who churned out pamphlets for hire.
- 18th & 19th c. - The compound hackwork (and hack-writing) crystallized in literary criticism (Alexander Pope, Samuel Johnson, Thomas Carlyle) to denounce commercial, uninspired literary production.
- Early 20th c. - Adopted in acting theory by Stanislavski and theatrical critics to describe clichéd, textbook stage acting that imitates emotion rather than living it.
- Late 20th c. to Present - Expanded into journalism, commercial design, and software programming to describe rushed, derivative, or low-quality commercial labor.
