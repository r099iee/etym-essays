- [Old English & Early Germanic Era (c. 8th–11th c.)] — As individual elements, fealu described a pale brownish-yellow, dusky, or pale golden color (often applied to autumn leaves, horses, or stormy seas), while hȳd denoted animal pelt or human skin — [perception: vivid, descriptive tactile descriptors grounded in agrarian life and natural observation]
- [Middle English Period (c. 12th–15th c.)] — Fallow evolved into an agrarian term for ploughed land left unsown (due to its pale turned earth) and an adjective for pale-tinted deer or horses, while hide became standard for tanned leather or skin — [perception: common rural terminology in agriculture and wildlife husbandry]
- [20th-Century Tolkienian Coinage (1954–Present)] — Compound formulated by J.R.R. Tolkien in The Fellowship of the Ring to name the fair-skinned, forest-dwelling Hobbit branch — [perception: universally recognized in literary fantasy as the aristocratic, courageous, and adventurous kindred of the Shire from whom Bilbo and Frodo descended]
- [Contemporary Fantasy Philology & Pop Culture (Late 20th–21st c.)] — Analyzed by Tolkien scholars as an exquisite Old English calque (*fealu-hȳd*), embodying Northern European aristocratic and elf-friendly archetypes in miniature form — [perception: celebrated as a masterstroke of historical linguistic worldbuilding]
