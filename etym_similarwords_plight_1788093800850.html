• Predicament: Similar because both mean "a difficult situation"; differs because predicament is about a hard choice or awkward fix one is in, often self-made, while plight is a state of suffering, usually imposed from outside.
  • Dilemma: Similar because both mean "a hard situation"; differs because dilemma is specifically a choice between two bad options, while plight involves no choice at all — it is a condition, not a decision.
  • Quandary: Similar because both mean "a state of difficulty"; differs because quandary is mental — a state of puzzlement about what to do — while plight is material suffering or danger.
  • Hardship: Similar because both name suffering and difficulty; differs because hardship is the difficulty itself (enduring hardship), while plight is the whole condition in which one suffers.
  • Adversity: Similar because both mean "difficult circumstances"; differs because adversity is abstract and general (trials of adversity), while plight names a concrete, particular bad situation.
  • Distress: Similar because both mean "a state of suffering"; differs because distress emphasizes the emotional or physical pain (in distress, distress signal), while plight emphasizes the objective situation.
  • Trouble: Similar because both mean "difficulty"; differs because trouble is the everyday, all-purpose word for any problem, small or large, while plight is serious, large-scale, and usually collective.
  • Straits (as in "dire straits"): Similar because both mean "a difficult, dangerous condition"; differs because straits is plural and usually intensified by "dire", with a maritime metaphor of a narrow passage, while plight is a standalone noun.
  • Misfortune: Similar because both name bad luck and suffering; differs because misfortune is the unlucky event or fate itself, while plight is the resulting condition one is caught in.
  • Misery: Similar because both name great suffering; differs because misery is the felt wretchedness, while plight is the situation that causes it.
  • Suffering: Similar because both involve pain and hardship; differs because suffering is the experience of pain, while plight is the external condition, which may or may not be dwelt on emotionally.
  • Ordeal: Similar because both name a severe test of endurance; differs because ordeal is a single terrible experience that happens to someone, while plight is a prolonged condition.
  • Tribulation: Similar because both mean "great trouble and suffering"; differs because tribulation is biblical and religious in register, while plight is secular and journalistic.
  • Woe: Similar because both name deep sorrow and trouble; differs because woe is the sorrow itself (woe is me), archaic and poetic, while plight is the factual condition.
  • Bind (informal): Similar because both mean "a difficult situation"; differs because bind is colloquial and personal (in a bit of a bind), while plight is formal and often collective.
  • Jam / Fix / Pickle (informal): Similar because all mean "an awkward or bad situation"; differs because all are light, comic, everyday words for temporary personal trouble, while plight is grave and sustained.
