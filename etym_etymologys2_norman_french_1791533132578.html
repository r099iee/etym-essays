Note: the etymology of Frank is disputed. The usual explanation derives it from a Germanic weapon-word, *frankō “javelin, lance,” a characteristic Frankish arm; a rival derives it from *frankaz “bold, fierce.” The sense “free” (as in franchise, enfranchise) is a Romance development from the legal privileges of Franks living in Roman territory, not the original meaning. The compound Norman-French itself is a modern English descriptive formation, not a medieval name: medieval writers said françois, le françois des Normanz, or simply Normanz. The hyphen is modern.
