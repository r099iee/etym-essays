• Colony: Similar because both name a territory under a mother state; differs because colony specifically implies settlement by transplanted people (Latin colōnia, from colere "to cultivate"), while a dependency stresses subordination and reliance regardless of settlement.
  • Dominion: Similar because both name territory within an empire; differs because dominion implies self-governing autonomy (the historical Dominions of the British Empire), the very quality a dependency lacks.
  • Possession: Similar because both name land held by a power; differs because possession is a neutral, property-like term with no political structure, while dependency names a governed, reliant relation.
  • Protectorate: Similar because both are subordinate territories; differs because a protectorate is a nominally sovereign state under another's protection with only partial control surrendered, while a dependency is directly subject.
  • Province: Similar because both are administrative divisions of a state; differs because a province is an integral part of a single state, not a separate territory hanging from the crown.
  • Territory: Similar because both name land under a power; differs because territory is the neutral geographical term with no implication of subordination or reliance.
  • Satellite state: Similar because both describe states controlled by a greater power; differs because satellite evokes modern Cold War politics and ideological control, not constitutional allegiance.
  • Client state: Similar because both describe a dependent political entity; differs because client state implies voluntary or economic dependence in geopolitics rather than formal sovereignty or colonial rule.
  • Vassal: Similar because both imply subordination to a superior; differs because vassal names a person or medieval state bound by homage and service, while dependency is a modern territorial-administrative category.
  • Tributary state: Similar because both describe states paying submission to a greater power; differs because tributary centres on the payment of tribute, a fiscal relation, rather than general subordination.
  • Dependence (abstract): Similar because both derive from the same root and concern reliance; differs because dependence is the abstract noun for the state of relying, while dependencies are concrete territories or, in computing, concrete packages.
  • Appendage: Similar because both suggest something attached to and hanging from a main body; differs because appendage is a metaphor from anatomy, informal and non-political.
  • Puppet state: Similar because both describe a state controlled by another; differs because puppet state is pejorative and implies a regime kept in power by a foreign patron, unlike the formal imperial relation of a dependency.
