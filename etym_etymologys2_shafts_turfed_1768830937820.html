Linear derivation chain for SHAFTS (root word one of two): PIE *(s)kep- (also reconstructed *(s)kap- or *skēp-; to cut, scrape, hew) — reconstructed root, c. 4500–3500 BCE; the root of cutting and shaping wood, attested in Latin scāpus (shaft, stem, stalk) and Greek skēptron (staff, hence English sceptre) and skaptein (to dig). → Proto-Germanic *skaftaz (a shaved or cut rod; spear-handle, pole) — reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE. → Old English sceaft (pole, spear-shaft, shaft of an arrow) — attested from the 9th century CE. → Middle English shaft (pole, spear; arrow; ray of light) — 12th–15th century. → Modern English shaft → shafts (plural noun) ◄ TARGET. Note on the debate: The PIE reconstruction is disputed in detail — some scholars write *(s)kep-, others *(s)kap-, others *skēp- (Pokorny gives *skēp-/*skep-, to cut); the s-mobile alternation and the vowel grade are debated, but the Germanic chain *skaftaz → sceaft → shaft is secure. The sense "shaft of light" arises in English from the 16th century as a transfer of the spear image.
Linear derivation chain for TURFED (root word two of two): Origin debated: possibly PIE *dʰer- (to hold, fasten) or a non-Indo-European substrate; NOT securely established — the earliest stage is uncertain and is reported as such rather than guessed. → Proto-Germanic *turbą / *turba- (turf, sod, peat) — reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE. → Old English turf (sod, grass surface, piece of peat) — attested from the 8th century CE. → Middle English turf — 12th–15th century. → Modern English turf (noun: sod, the grass-covered ground; verb: to lay sod) → turfed (past participle and participial adjective) ◄ TARGET. Branches and cognates: Old Norse torf (turf, sod), Old High German zurba → German Torf (peat), Old Frisian turf, Middle Dutch turf — all continuing the same Germanic base. Proposed cognate: Sanskrit darbhá (tuft of sacred grass, used in ritual) is sometimes cited as a distant relative, but the connection is widely regarded as uncertain and is therefore not part of the secure chain. Note: ZERO GUESSWORK — the Germanic stage *turbą and the English chain are secure; the Indo-European or substrate origin of the Germanic base is not established, and no stage beyond Germanic is asserted.
