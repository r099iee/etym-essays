• Beseechingly: Similar because both describe earnest entreaty; differs because beseechingly is more archaic and solemn, too heavy for mild domestic coaxing.
  • Imploringly: Similar because both describe urgent requesting; differs because imploringly raises the stakes toward desperation or crisis, overstating a small favour.
  • Entreatingly: Similar because both describe polite, earnest requesting; differs because entreatingly is formal and literary, without the slightly childlike colour of 'pleadingly'.
  • Wheedlingly: Similar because both persuade by soft appeal; differs because wheedlingly implies crafty flattery and sounds more cynical than sincere affection.
  • Cajolingly: Similar because both coax with soft words; differs because cajoling is more manipulative and flattering, closer to persuasion than to a humble plea.
  • Piteously: Similar because both evoke an emotional response; differs because piteously focuses on exciting pity for suffering, not on winning a small concession.
  • Supplicatingly: Similar because both mean beseeching with humility; differs because supplicatingly is more solemn and religious in tone, appropriate to prayer or abject need.
  • Wistfully: Similar because both suggest a gentle, emotional appeal; differs because wistfully expresses longing and regret rather than an active request to be granted something.
