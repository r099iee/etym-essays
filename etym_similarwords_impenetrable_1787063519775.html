• Impervious: Similar because it also means not allowing passage or entry; differs because it usually describes resistance to an external agent (impervious to water, impervious to criticism) and rarely describes physical piercing or understanding.
  • Impermeable: Similar because it means not allowing something through; differs because it is technical and concrete, used of membranes, soils, and fabrics that block liquids or gases, never of people or arguments.
  • Impassable: Similar because it means impossible to travel through or across; differs because it is reserved for routes and terrain (roads, rivers, mountain passes), not for barriers that resist being pierced or understood.
  • Inaccessible: Similar because it means impossible to reach; differs because it is about remoteness or unreachability (an inaccessible island, an inaccessible account) rather than about refusing entry to understanding.
  • Opaque: Similar because both mean hard to see into or understand; differs because “opaque” stresses lack of transparency (visual or figurative) and is much weaker — an opaque text is merely unclear, not walled off.
  • Inscrutable: Similar because it means impossible to understand; differs because it is used almost exclusively of faces, expressions, and demeanour — a mystery you cannot read, rather than a barrier you cannot pass.
  • Unfathomable: Similar because it means impossible to understand or measure; differs because it evokes depth — fathom was a unit for measuring water depth — so it fits mysteries, voids, and extremes (unfathomable sorrow), not fortifications.
  • Invulnerable: Similar because it means cannot be harmed or attacked successfully; differs because it is about immunity to damage, not about refusing passage or defeating comprehension.
  • Unbreakable: Similar because it means cannot be broken; differs because it is simpler and more concrete, usually of objects (glass, promises) and carries no intellectual or emotional sense.
  • Dense: Similar because, informally, a dense person or a dense text is hard to get through; differs because it is informal, blunt, and usually implies slowness or over-packedness rather than a deliberate barrier.
