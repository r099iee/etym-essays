• Sedative: Similar because both pharmacologically depress central nervous system activity to promote calm and relieve anxiety; differs because sedative is a broad modern clinical classification spanning tablets, injections, and gas, whereas sleeping-draught specifically denotes a swallowed liquid potion.
  • Hypnotic: Similar because both have the explicit pharmacological objective of initiating and sustaining sleep; differs because hypnotic is formal medical terminology for modern sleep-inducing drugs without any archaic, liquid-formulation connotation.
  • Soporific: Similar because both denote agents or qualities that produce drowsiness; differs because soporific is primarily an adjective describing anything (a dull lecture, warm climate, heavy meal) that induces sleep, whereas sleeping-draught is a concrete physical medicine.
  • Potion: Similar because both refer to a medicinal, herbal, or magical liquid prepared for drinking; differs because potion carries broad magical or lethal associations (love potion, deadly poison), whereas sleeping-draught is restricted to sleep-inducing or sedative outcomes.
  • Nightcap: Similar because both describe a drink consumed shortly before retiring to bed to encourage sleep; differs because a nightcap is typically a casual, comforting alcoholic drink (brandy, whiskey, warm spiced wine) rather than a medicinal or pharmacological sedative.
  • Laudanum: Similar because historically it was the standard liquid opiate formulation taken as a sleep-inducing draught; differs because laudanum specifies a precise chemical preparation (tincture of opium in alcohol) rather than the functional hypernym sleeping-draught.
  • Tranquilizer: Similar because both reduce tension and induce tranquility; differs because a modern tranquilizer is primarily designed to mitigate psychiatric anxiety and agitation without necessarily rendering the subject completely unconscious or asleep.
  • Narcotic: Similar because both dull the senses, relieve pain, and produce stupor or slumber; differs because narcotic focuses strictly on psychoactive, pain-deadening alkaloid properties and carries legal connotations of illicit dependence.
