In 16th–17th-century England the viol was a courtly instrument of the first rank — the fretted, bowed consort family (viola da gamba and kin) whose players set the standard of refined ensemble music.
“Viol” through the 18th century was displaced by the violin family in concert life, and by the 1800s the word had become a historical name for an old, superseded instrument.
“Viol” returned to the stage in the 20th-century early-music revival, and the word again names living instruments played by consorts worldwide.
“Viols” today carries a deliberate archaic, ceremonial flavour — heirloom instruments of an older music — which is exactly the register Tolkien calls up when Dwalin and Balin bring in “viols as big as themselves.”
