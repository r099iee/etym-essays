- 6th-century Byzantium - Belisarius (c. 505-565) was Justinian's most celebrated general, reconquering the Vandal kingdom of North Africa and most of Ostrogothic Italy, so his name entered history as the last of the great Roman generals.
- Procopius's double portrait - in the Wars he appears as the brilliant loyal commander, but the Secret History shows him repeatedly recalled and disgraced by Justinian's jealousy, so his name carries the drama of brilliance shadowed by suspicion.
- Legend and art - a later legend (popularized in 18th-century painting) reduced the victorious general to a blind beggar crying give alms to Belisarius, making his name a moral emblem of ingratitude and the fall of greatness.
- Contemporary sense - Belisarius names the greatest soldier of the Byzantine recovery, a figure of military genius and political misfortune, and in Gibbon's text a contemporary of Procopius amid the marvels of rich Constantinople.
