• Palisades: Similar because both are walls of upright stakes driven side by side; differs because palisade is the older general term (and the fence of suburban gardens), while stockade stresses the military fort or prison enclosure.
  • Forts: Similar because a stockade is the wall of a frontier fort; differs because a fort is the whole defended place with buildings and garrison, while a stockade is specifically its timber enclosure.
  • Ramparts: Similar because both are defensive walls manned against attack; differs because ramparts are broad earthen or stone embankments, while a stockade is a vertical wall of timber posts.
  • Barricades: Similar because both are improvised defensive barriers under threat; differs because a barricade is hastily heaped in streets from carts and furniture, while a stockade is deliberately built of set posts.
  • Enclosures: Similar because both shut an area in; differs because enclosure is the neutral general word (fields, compounds), while stockade bristles with sharpened military menace.
  • Compounds: Similar because both are fenced areas holding people or stores; differs because a compound is any walled yard (embassies, factories), while a stockade is timber-built and martial or penal.
  • Pens: Similar because both confine bodies behind barriers; differs because pens hold livestock with light rails, while stockades hold men with war-grade posts.
  • Stockades versus barracks: Similar because both house soldiers under discipline; differs because barracks are the roofed quarters men sleep in, while the stockade is the post wall — or the punishment cells — around them.
