Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*ḱers-
"to run, race, hurry"
↓
Proto-Celtic (c. 800 BCE)
*karros
"wagon, wheeled vehicle, chariot"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
carrus / carrum (borrowed from Gaulish/Celtic)
"two-wheeled Celtic transport cart"
↓
Late Latin / Gallo-Romance (c. 6th–9th c.)
*carrietta / *carrote
"cart, wheeled carriage"
↓
Old French / Old Northern French (c. 12th–13th c.)
char (from Latin carrus) + -iot → chariot
"stately wagon, ceremonial car, war cart"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.)
chariot / charyotte / charyot
"chariot, war vehicle, triumphant carriage"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–present)
chariot
"ancient war/racing car; celestial vehicle of sun and moon"
