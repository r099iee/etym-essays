- 14th–16th c. [Apothecary & Herbal Liquid Formulation] - Regarded as a vital physician's compounding of opium, wine, mandrake, or poppy to relieve agony or insomnia.
- 16th–17th c. [Dramatic & Theatrical Plot Device] - Gained immense literary currency in Renaissance drama as a narrative tool for feigning death, escaping captivity, or subduing guards.
- 18th–19th c. [Domestic & Victorian Patent Medicine] - Associated with widely consumed household laudanum, chloral hydrate solutions, and sedative cordials.
- 20th–21st c. [Archaic Literary Trope & Figurative Metaphor] - Largely replaced in clinical settings by "hypnotic/sleeping pill", surviving in fantasy fiction and figurative political commentary.
