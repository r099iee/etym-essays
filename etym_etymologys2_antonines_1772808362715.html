Antonius — Roman gentilicium (family/clan name), etymon opaque; deep origin unknown, widely suspected to be Etruscan or otherwise non-Latin (Italic substrate); no secure PIE reconstruction exists and none should be invented → Latin Antōnius, the name of a famous plebeian gens (attested from the 3rd century BCE; most famous member Marcus Antonius, Mark Antony, 83–30 BCE) → Latin Antōnīnus 'of the Antonii, Antonine' (cognomen and derivative formed with the regular -īnus suffix, as in Latīnus, Sabīnus; attested as a personal name and as the imperial title, notably Titus Aurelius Fulvus Boionius Arrius Antoninus, the emperor Antoninus Pius, r. 138–161, and Marcus Aurelius Antoninus, r. 161–180) → English Antonine (adjective) and Antonines (plural noun) 'the Antonine emperors; by extension their age' (the dynastic plural and its Gibbonian period-sense, 'the age of the Antonines,' 98–180 CE). Note: this is an onomastic (name) lineage, not a lexical etymology; the chain from the unknown Etruscan/Italic base of Antonius to the English plural is documented in inscriptions and imperial usage, but no meaning can be assigned to the original gentilicium. The same gens name, transmitted through the Christian cult of St Anthony (d. 356 CE), produced the European name family Anthony, Antoine, Antonio, Anton — a separate onomastic branch, not a stage in the imperial label's own chain.
