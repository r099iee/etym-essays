INDUSTRY descends from a PIE root meaning 'to spread out / build', through the Latin compound industria 'diligence'. CHAIN: - PIE *sterh₃- 'to spread out, extend, strew; to build by laying out' (reconstructed; c. 4500-3500 BCE) — source also of Greek stórnymi 'I spread', Sanskrit stṛṇāti 'he strews' → Proto-Italic *strō- 'to spread, build' (reconstructed; c. 1000-500 BCE) → Latin struere 'to build, construct, arrange, pile up' (attested 2nd-1st c. BCE) — source of English construct, instruct, obstruct → Latin industrius 'diligent, painstaking, active' (attested 2nd-1st c. BCE) — formed with the archaic prefix indu- 'in, within' (an old by-form of in-) + struere 'to build': literally 'one who builds within' → Latin industria 'diligence, purposeful activity, assiduity' (attested 1st c. BCE) — the abstract noun of industrius → Old French industrie 'skill, activity, diligence' (attested c. 1100-1200) → Middle English industrie 'skill, diligence, activity' (attested c. 1400) → Modern English industry (1500-present; the economic sense develops from the 18th century onward) → INDUSTRY NOTE ON DEBATE: the internal analysis of Latin industrius (indu- 'within' + struere 'to build') is standard and well established; the archaic prefix indu- is an old by-form of in- preserved in this word and in indutiae 'truce'. The PIE root *sterh₃- 'spread, strew' is well attested across Indo-European (Greek, Sanskrit, Latin sternere 'spread out', English strew), and its connection to struere is accepted; the exact grade of the root in struere is a matter of standard historical phonology rather than serious debate.
