• Condemning: Similar because both express strong, emphatic disapproval of something; differs because condemning is the strongest and most general term — it can pronounce guilt and assign punishment — whereas reprobating is a measured verdict delivered in formal language after testing.
  • Censuring: Similar because both are formal, official expressions of disapproval; differs because censuring is the institutional form (a censure motion), cooler and procedural, while reprobating is a reasoned condemnation of character, manner, or style.
  • Denouncing: Similar because both declare disapproval publicly; differs because denouncing is loud, public, and accusatory, aimed at rousing others to outrage, whereas reprobating weighs the matter and finds it wanting without hot anger.
  • Decrying: Similar because both publicly express disapproval of a wrong; differs because decrying laments and protests a public evil without necessarily arraigning a person, whereas reprobating passes a formal judgment on a person, text, or manner.
  • Castigating: Similar because both punish a fault with words; differs because castigating punishes at length and often harshly, with a corrective whip, whereas reprobating is a cooler, judicial verdict.
  • Reproving: Similar because both come from the same Latin family of testing (probare) and both blame; differs because reproving is the milder sense of scolding or rebuking a person gently, whereas reprobating rejects outright as unsound or blameworthy.
  • Reprehending: Similar because both express formal blame; differs because reprehending is largely literary and means to find fault with, without the systematic testing-and-rejection image that reprobating carries.
  • Criticizing: Similar because both are adverse appraisals; differs because criticizing is the mildest term — a mere adverse assessment without condemnation — whereas reprobating condemns with judicial severity.
  • Excoriating: Similar because both severely denounce; differs because excoriating tears the object apart with fierce language, whereas reprobating is severe but measured, a verdict rather than a flaying.
  • Impugning: Similar because both call something into question; differs because impugning challenges a claim's truth or validity rather than condemning a person's manner or conduct, and it is narrower and more technical.
