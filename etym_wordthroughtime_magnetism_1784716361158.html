- 17th century - 'magnetism' names the physical property of the lodestone and magnet — an invisible force of attraction studied by natural philosophers and celebrated as a wonder of nature.
- late 18th century - Mesmer's 'animal magnetism' borrows the scientific term for an invisible influence of one person upon another's body and mind; the word leaves the laboratory for the parlour and the medical controversy.
- 19th century - the figurative sense stabilizes: 'personal magnetism' becomes the standard phrase for an inexplicable, quasi-physical power of attraction that an orator, performer, or leader exercises over a crowd — men are pulled toward the speaker, not merely charmed.
- 20th-21st century - magnetism remains firmly figurative (stage presence, aura of command) while its physical sense retreats into physics; it now suggests a forceful, irresistible pull of personality — more earthy and physical than the divine halo implied by charisma.
