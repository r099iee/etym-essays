The word is a compound participle: over- + hung (the past and past-participle form of hang). Two root chains follow, then the compound formation. Chain 1 — "over": - PIE *upér "over, above, beyond" (the comparative-extension form of the adverbial root *upo / *eup "up, under," attested across the family by Greek hypér, Latin super, Sanskrit upári) → Proto-Germanic *uber "over, above" (reconstructed) → Old English ofer "over, above, across, beyond" (preposition and adverb, attested from the earliest Old English) → Middle English over, ouer "over, above, beyond" (12th–15th centuries) → Modern English over "above, across, beyond; excess" → the first element of overhung. Chain 2 — "hang" (past and past participle "hung"): - PIE *ḱenk- (also reconstructed *konk- or *kenk-) "to hang, to be suspended" — a root of disputed exact shape; the traditional reconstruction connects the Germanic words for hanging, but the Indo-European distribution is thin and the laryngeal shape is debated → Proto-Germanic *hanhaną "to hang, to be suspended" (strong verb of class VII; reconstructed; attested through its descendants) → Old English hōn "to hang (transitive, strong), to suspend, to crucify" and hangian "to hang (intransitive, weak), to be suspended" (both attested from the earliest Old English; the strong and weak pair coexisted and later merged) → Middle English hangen "to hang, to suspend, to be suspended" (12th–15th centuries) → Modern English hang "to suspend, to be suspended, to extend downward" (16th century onward) with the past and past participle hung (the form continues the Old English strong past plural alternant; hanged is preserved only in the sense of execution by hanging) → Modern English hung "past tense and past participle of hang." Chain 3 — the compound "overhung": - Old English oferhōn / oferhangian (the verb formed with ofer- + hōn / hangian, "to hang over, to overhang"; attested) → Middle English overhangen "to overhang, to project over, to hang over" (12th–15th centuries) → Modern English overhang "to project or hang over" (16th century onward) → overhung (the past and past participle of overhang, formed exactly as hang gives hung, i.e. over- + hung; attested from the 16th century) = OVERHUNG "covered, roofed, or shaded by something hanging from above." Note on debate: the PIE root of hang is not securely attested in its exact laryngeal shape, *ḱenk- versus *konk-, and the Germanic strong past was remodelled in English, with hung generalised by analogy and hanged reserved for executions. The existence of the Old English compound oferhōn is attested, and the modern participle overhung is simply over- + the modern past participle hung, a transparent and uncontroversial formation.
