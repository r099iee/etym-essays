Compound Word Analysis:
Bonfires is an ancient compound of two distinct Germanic root words: bone + fire.

[Root 1: Bone]
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*bʰeyg- / *bʰey-
"to strike, split; rigid bone"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*bainą
"bone, leg, straight bone"
↓
Old English (c. 750 CE)
bān
"bone, ivory, skeletal element"
↓
Middle English (c. 1250 CE)
bane / bon
"bone (northern 'bane', southern 'bon')"

[Root 2: Fire]
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*péh₂wr̥
"fire (inanimate / elemental force)"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*fōr / *fūriz
"fire, blaze"
↓
Old English (c. 750 CE)
fȳr
"fire, flame, lightning"
↓
Middle English (c. 1250 CE)
fir / fyr / fire
"fire, flame"

[Compound Formation]
Middle English (c. 1483 CE)
bane-fyre / bone-fyre
"fire of bones (ignis ossium)"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550 CE)
bonefire / bonfire
"large public celebratory open-air blaze"
↓
Bonfires
Note: The first written attestation occurs in the Catholicon Anglicum (1483): 'A bane fyre; ignis ossium.' Folk etymology later attempted to derive the prefix from French bon ('good'), but historical phonology confirms bone-fire.
