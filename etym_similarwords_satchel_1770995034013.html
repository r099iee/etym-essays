• backpack: Similar because both are bags for carrying books and personal items; differs because a backpack is larger, worn on both shoulders with padded straps, and built for weight-bearing travel, while a satchel hangs from one shoulder at the hip and is softer and more informal.
  • knapsack: Similar because both are small portable carrying bags; differs because a knapsack is a rugged traveller's or soldier's pack for outdoor gear, worn on the back, while a satchel is soft, civilian, and worn over one shoulder.
  • rucksack: Similar because both are carried bags for belongings; differs because a rucksack is a large backpack for hiking and camping, while a satchel is small and urban.
  • haversack: Similar because both are single-strap canvas shoulder bags; differs because a haversack is the soldier's or hiker's provision bag, rugged and utilitarian, while a satchel has a scholarly, bookish character.
  • briefcase: Similar because both carry documents and papers; differs because a briefcase is stiff, formal, and hand-carried, whereas a satchel is soft, informal, and shoulder-slung.
  • messenger bag: Similar because both are one-strap shoulder bags for papers and a laptop; differs because messenger bag is the modern, often larger courier version, while satchel is the older, smaller, school-associated word.
  • purse: Similar because both are personal carryalls; differs because a purse is much smaller, for money and cosmetics, while a satchel has volume for books and documents.
  • pouch: Similar because both are small bags held close to the body; differs because a pouch is a little drawstring or flap bag, while a satchel is large enough for books.
  • bag: Similar because both name a container for personal items; differs because bag is the general word for any such container, while satchel specifies the single-strap, hip-worn shape.
  • tote: Similar because both are soft open carriers; differs because a tote is carried by two short handles, is open-topped and casual, while a satchel closes (flap or zip) and hangs from a long strap.
  • duffel: Similar because both hold a traveller's gear; differs because a duffel is a large cylindrical drawstring bag for bulky clothing and sports kit, while a satchel is small and neat.
  • grip: Similar because both are small travelling bags; differs because grip is an older, now dated American word for a small suitcase or travel bag, lacking the shoulder-strap and scholarly associations of satchel.
