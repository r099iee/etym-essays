- Old English — the fortified place: fæstnes names both firmness and the fortress itself, the safe enclosure where people and treasure hold out.
- Middle Ages — castles and vows: fastnesse guards loyalties as well as lands, describing locked gates, tied knots, and promises that do not slip.
- Early modern — speed sneaks in: fast gains its modern sense of swift, and fastness quietly inherits it, so firmness and fleetness live in one skin.
- 18th–19th century — romance rediscovers the fortress: Gothic novels and histories revive fastness for Highland, Alpine, and desert strongholds, wild and picturesque.
- 20th century — the laboratory takes a share: dye chemists standardise fastness for colours that survive washing and sun, a technical everyday sense beside the poetic one.
- Today — two worlds in one word: fantasy keeps the mountain stronghold (Tolkien's Mordor fixed it forever), while industry keeps the test certificate; context tells which fortress you are in.
