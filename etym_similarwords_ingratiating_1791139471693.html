• obsequious: Similar because both mean fawning to please; differs because obsequious stresses servile obedience, ingratiating stresses charming flattery.
  • sycophantic: Similar because both mean toadying for favour; differs because sycophantic is stronger and more contemptuous, implying gross flattery.
  • fawning: Similar because both mean cringing praise; differs because fawning evokes a dog-like display, ingratiating can be smoother and verbal.
  • unctuous: Similar because both mean oily pleasantness; differs because unctuous adds smug, greasy insincerity of manner, not just motive.
  • oleaginous: Similar because both mean slimily flattering; differs because oleaginous is literary and stresses repulsive oiliness.
  • servile: Similar because both imply low seeking favour; differs because servile stresses slavish submission, ingratiating stresses pleasing charm.
  • subservient: Similar because both mean overly compliant; differs because subservient is about obedience to authority, ingratiating about winning liking.
  • toadying: Similar because both mean flattering a superior; differs because toadying is blunt noun/verb for the act, ingratiating is adjectival style.
  • smarmy: Similar because both mean falsely charming; differs because smarmy is informal British and stresses creepy sliminess.
  • winning: Similar because both mean gaining favour by charm; differs because winning is positive and sincere, ingratiating hints at calculation.
  • endearing: Similar because both mean arousing affection; differs because endearing is warm and genuine, ingratiating may be strategic.
  • charming: Similar because both mean pleasing; differs because charming is neutral-positive, ingratiating adds motive to please for gain.
  • flattering: Similar because both involve praise to please; differs because flattering is the tactic itself, ingratiating is the overall favour-seeking manner.
