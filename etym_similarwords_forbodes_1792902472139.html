• Portends: Similar because both announce a future event through present signs; differs because portends usually takes the sign itself as subject and stays formal and ominous, while forbodes can also name the inward feeling of the heart.
  • Presages: Similar because both mean to foreshow something momentous ahead; differs because presages is stately and often neutral or even hopeful, while forbodes leans almost always toward gloom and dread.
  • Augurs: Similar because both read omens of what is to come; differs because augurs recalls Roman priests studying birds and entrails, deliberate divination, while forbodes is an involuntary heaviness of spirit.
  • Bodes: Similar because both declare what an omen promises, sharing the same root; differs because bodes can be good or ill and takes the sign as subject, while forbodes reaches further forward and almost always fears the worst.
  • Foretells: Similar because both speak of the future before it arrives; differs because foretells stresses explicit statement of what will happen, while forbodes stresses uneasy sensing rather than clear detail.
  • Foreshadows: Similar because both cast a shadow of coming events over the present; differs because foreshadows is the narrator’s literary device hinting at plot, while forbodes is a character’s lived dread inside the story.
  • Herald: Similar because both announce an approach; differs because a herald proclaims openly and often joyfully, while what forbodes creeps in unwanted and unwelcome.
  • Threatens: Similar because both warn that harm gathers ahead; differs because threatens is blunt and immediate, a fist raised, while forbodes is misty and prophetic, a chill felt before the fist is seen.
