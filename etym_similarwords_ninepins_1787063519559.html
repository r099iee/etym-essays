• Ten-Pin Bowling: Similar because both involve rolling a ball down a wooden lane to knock down wooden pins; differs because 'ninepins' uses 9 pins in a diamond formation without finger holes in the ball, whereas 'ten-pin bowling' uses 10 pins in a triangle with a three-holed ball.
  • Skittles: Similar because both are traditional British and European pub bowling games using wooden pins; differs because 'skittles' is a broader regional family of games (with varying pin counts and throwing cheese/balls), whereas 'ninepins' specifically denotes the standardized 9-pin diamond game.
  • Bowls / Lawn Bowls: Similar because both involve rolling heavy balls toward a target; differs because 'bowls' aims to roll biased balls as close as possible to a target jack on flat grass, while 'ninepins' aims to violently knock over standing pins.
  • Duckpin Bowling: Similar because both use smaller balls without finger holes; differs because 'duckpin' uses 10 smaller, squatter pins in a triangle, while 'ninepins' uses 9 taller pins in a diamond.
  • Candlepin Bowling: Similar because both are traditional regional bowling variants; differs because 'candlepin' uses 10 tall, cylindrical, straight-sided pins and smaller balls where dead wood remains on the lane.
  • Quoits: Similar because both are traditional English throwing/aiming sports; differs because 'quoits' involves tossing rings through the air onto a single upright peg, while 'ninepins' involves rolling a ball along the ground at nine pins.
  • Bocce: Similar because both involve rolling balls along a court; differs because 'bocce' is a target-boules game without pins, played on gravel or natural earth.
