- Old English to Middle English - from "welig/wilig" through "wilghe" and "wilowe" to modern "willow", the word has always named the same thing: the pliant, bending riverside tree whose supple wood twists without snapping, a tree named after motion and pliancy.
- Early modern period - "willow" accumulated a second, wholly literary life: from the harps hung upon the willows of Psalm 137 to the drooping "weeping" willow of European poetry, the tree became the very emblem of sorrow, mourning, and forsaken love.
- Modern English - the word works in both registers at once: a precise botanical type of flexible tree used in craft (osiers, withes, cricket bats) and a storehouse of literary sadness — the tree that bends and sighs in poetry.
