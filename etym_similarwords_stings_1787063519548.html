• Bites: Similar because both refer to acute, painful punctures inflicted by animals or insects; differs because *bites* are inflicted using mouthparts, jaws, or mandibles to crush, tear, or clamp, whereas *stings* are delivered by a specialized posterior ovipositor, tail spine, or microscopic cnidocyte injecting venom.
  • Pricks: Similar because both describe sharp, localized puncturing sensations or wounds; differs because a *prick* is a minor, dry puncture by a sharp point (like a pin or rose thorn) that causes brief mechanical pain without venom, whereas a *sting* involves intense chemical burning and lingering irritation.
  • Pangs: Similar because both describe sudden, sharp spasms of pain; differs because *pangs* are internal, visceral sensations of hunger, grief, or longing ("pangs of conscience"), whereas *stings* are acute, surface or lancinating burning pains.
  • Barbs: Similar because both are sharp, piercing projections; differs because a *barb* is a backward-facing point designed to prevent withdrawal of a hook or arrow, whereas a *sting* is primarily a biological weapon or acute burning wound.
  • Smartings: Similar because both denote burning or stinging sensations on the skin; differs because *smarting* describes the persistent, diffuse burning aftereffect of a wound or chemical contact rather than the initial piercing impact.
  • Wounds: Similar because both represent physical trauma inflicted on living tissue; differs because *wound* is a broad general category for lacerations, incisions, and blunt trauma of any scale, whereas *stings* are small, precise, venomous punctures.
  • Thorns: Similar because both are sharp biological defenses that puncture skin; differs because *thorns* are rigid, modified plant stems that cause passive mechanical injury, lacking active venom glands and muscular injection apparatus.
