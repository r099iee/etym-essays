• premonition: Similar because both are a sense that something will happen; differs because premonition is a specific flash of warning about a named or namable event, while foreboding is a vaguer, more diffuse dread.
  • presentiment: Similar because both mean an anticipatory feeling; differs because presentiment is neutral or even hopeful in theory, while foreboding is almost always negative.
  • dread: Similar because both are intense fear of what is coming; differs because dread is the raw emotion itself, while foreboding is the anticipatory sense that precedes and produces it.
  • apprehension: Similar because both describe anxious anticipation; differs because apprehension can be mild and everyday, whereas foreboding implies a weighty, often irrational certainty of evil.
  • misgiving: Similar because both are uneasy doubts about the future; differs because misgiving is plural-prone and scruple-like, pointing at a specific choice, while foreboding is a general atmosphere of doom.
  • unease / uneasiness: Similar because both name low-grade discomfort; differs because unease is broad and can have many causes, while foreboding is specifically oriented toward future misfortune.
  • omen: Similar because both relate to signs of the future; differs because an omen is an external sign or event, while foreboding is an inner feeling.
  • portent: Similar because both suggest evil to come; differs because a portent is a thing that signals doom, not the feeling it stirs.
  • presage: Similar because both bridge sign and feeling; differs because presage is chiefly a verb and a more intellectual, sign-reading word.
  • forewarning: Similar because both mean a sign or sense of what is coming; differs because forewarning is a concrete, often spoken notice, whereas foreboding is wordless inner dread.
