- Late 13th to 14th c. — welcoming and formal acceptance: enters Middle English as the present participle and verbal noun of receiven, designating the formal taking in of guests, payments, sacraments, or feudal tribute.
- 15th to 16th c. — legal custody and physical receipt: develops specific legal definitions regarding the possession of goods, inheritance, and holding property in trust for another.
- 17th to 18th c. — martial endurance and passive absorption: develops the military and physical sense of sustaining incoming blows, enemy fire, or broadsides without yielding, immortalized in naval accounts and dramatic literature.
- 19th c. — institutional administration and postal communication: becomes central to industrial commerce, post offices, freight docks, and formal societal receptions hosted by high society.
- 20th to 21st c. — telecommunications, computing, and athletics: expands into radio wave reception, input-output signal processing, data packets, and athletic specialty roles such as American football wide receivers.
