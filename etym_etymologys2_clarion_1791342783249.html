Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*kelh₁- / *kl̥h₁-ró-
"to shout, summon, call out"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*klāros
"audible, distinct, clear"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
clarus
"clear, loud, bright, renowned"
↓
Medieval Latin (c. 12th c.)
clario
"shrill trumpet with a clear tone"
↓
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 13th c.)
clarioun / clairon
"high-pitched military horn or trumpet"
↓
Middle English (c. 1325)
clarioun
"a shrill war trumpet"
↓
Modern English (c. 16th c.)
clarion
"shrill trumpet; clear, inspiring call"

Note: Rooted in PIE *kelh₁- ('to call, cry aloud'), the source of Latin calare ('to summon'), clamor, and concilium. In Latin, clarus moved synesthetically from loud acoustic clarity to visual brilliance, before Medieval Latin clario re-specialized the acoustic sense for a piercing military instrument.
