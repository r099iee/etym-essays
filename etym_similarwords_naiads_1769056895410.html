• Dryads: Similar because both are female nature-spirits of Greek myth, so fixed in the pairing "Naiads and Dryads" that the two read almost as one compound idea — the spirits of water and wood; differs because Dryads are arboreal, born with their tree and dying with it, while a Naiad belongs to fresh flowing water.
  • Nereids: Similar because both are water-nymphs of Greek religion; differs because the Nereids are the fifty daughters of Nereus who ride the open sea with dolphins, while a Naiad is confined to fresh water — the spring, fountain, well, stream, river, or pool.
  • Oceanids: Similar because both are classes of Greek water-spirits; differs because the Oceanids are the daughters of Oceanus, the great earth-encircling river, grander and more cosmic in scale, while a Naiad is a small, localised nymph of one particular spring or stream.
  • nymph: Similar because a naiad is one species of the general class of minor female nature-spirits the Greeks called nymphs; differs because nymph spans land, sea, and wood indifferently, while a Naiad is bound to fresh water — literally "the flowing one."
  • Hamadryads: Similar because both are nymphs whose identity is fused with a particular natural object; differs because a Hamadryad is a tree-nymph born and dying with her tree, while a Naiad is the spirit of a spring or stream, tied to flowing water rather than to a fixed trunk.
  • Oreads: Similar because both are nymphs of a specific landscape; differs because the Oreads haunt mountains and rocky heights, while the Naiads are the fresh-water members of the nymph class.
  • Undines: Similar because both are water-spirits; differs because the Undines are the later, alchemical spirits of the element of water imagined by Paracelsus in the Renaissance, while the Naiads are the older classical nymphs of springs and streams, rooted in Greek cult and poetry.
  • Sirens: Similar because both are female, water-adjacent beings of Greek myth; differs because the Sirens are the dangerous bird-women (later pictured as mermaid-like) who lure sailors to death, while Naiads are benign guardians of fresh water — companions rather than temptresses.
