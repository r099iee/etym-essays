- 10th–13th c. (Old Norse / Old East Norse) — Gautstafr: warrior and chieftain compound name signifying "staff/pillar of the Geatish people", invoked as a marker of heroic descent and tribal leadership.
- 16th c. (Vasa Dynasty & Swedish Nation-Building) — With Gustav I Vasa liberating Sweden from the Kalmar Union, Gustaf became the national royal name par excellence, symbolizing Swedish sovereignty, Lutheran reform, and independence.
- 17th c. (Thirty Years' War) — Latinized throughout Europe as Gustavus Adolphus, the name came to connote military genius, Protestant championing, and geopolitical majesty.
- 18th–19th c. (Enlightenment, Classicism, & Cultural Patronage) — Under Gustav III, the name became synonymous with the "Gustavian era" of opera, theatre, neoclassicism, and academy-building; the traditional Swedish spelling Gustaf was codified alongside continental Gustav.
- Early 20th c. (German Gestalt & Art Theory) — Associated with pioneering intellectual figures such as Gustaf Britsch (1879–1923), whose posthumous 1926 work established the foundational cognitive laws of visual artistic development.
- Contemporary Era — Retained as an enduring Swedish royal and civil name, maintaining associations with Scandinavian heritage, artistic patronage, and historical statesmanship.
