• Populated: Similar because both mean furnished with inhabitants; differs because populated is a modern, analytical, demographic term typically used for statistical, ecological, or administrative contexts, whereas peopled is a warmer, more literary and narrative term emphasizing human community and culture.
  • Inhabited: Similar because both indicate the presence of occupants in a space; differs because inhabited emphasizes the state of dwelling or living within a location, whereas peopled emphasizes the collective act of filling or supplying that space with human beings.
  • Settled: Similar because both describe establishing a population in a territory; differs because settled stresses the establishment of fixed, orderly, permanent domestic and agricultural life, often implying prior nomadic wandering or pacification, while peopled focuses on the presence and density of the population itself.
  • Colonized: Similar because both involve the arrival and establishment of people in a new land; differs because colonized carries strong political, institutional, and often exploitative connotations involving sovereignty, governance, and power dynamics, whereas peopled is purely demographic and generative.
  • Occupied: Similar because both describe physical presence within a space; differs because occupied frequently implies military conquest, temporary physical holding, or filling a vacant seat, lacking the generational, community-building essence of peopled.
  • Tenanted: Similar because both refer to occupants living in a space; differs because tenanted is a formal, legal, or landlord-tenant term emphasizing contractual or temporary residence rather than organic demographic settlement.
