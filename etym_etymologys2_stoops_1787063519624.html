Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*stewb- / *steup-
"to project, lean over, stand stiffly, strike"
(Root extension of PIE *sta- "to stand")
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*stūpōną / *steupaną
"to bend down, incline forward, tower over"
↓
Old English (c. 10th c.)
stūpian
"to bend down, bow, incline the body"
↓
Middle English (c. 1250)
stoupen
"to bend forward; to condescend; to incline downward"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550)
stoop
"to bend forward; to dive (falconry); to lower oneself morally"
↓
Modern English (c. 1700)
stoops
"bends forward/down; condescends; slopes down in shadow"
Note: Present 3rd pers. sing. form; cognate with Old Norse stúpa ('to project, tower over, stoop').
