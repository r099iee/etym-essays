- 15th c. — a technical term for a judge’s award of disputed property; confined to writs, plea rolls, and learned commentary.
- 16th–17th c. — widened to any judicial determination of a claim or status, while the older verb adjudge competed with the newer adjudicate.
- 18th–19th c. — fixed as the term for declaring a debtor bankrupt, and acquired a bureaucratic, administrative colour.
- 20th c. — spread across the administrative state — disability claims, immigration, taxation, insurance — and produced the job title adjudicator.
- 21st c. — extended to non-state deciders such as platforms, ombudsmen, and construction adjudicators, so that the word now names any structured, authoritative resolution of a dispute.
