- [14th c.] - "in as moche as" is an ordinary prepositional phrase, literally "in so much as" - [perception: no fixed spelling; a mere collocation]
- [16th–17th c.] - the phrase fuses into the single word inasmuch; writers use "inasmuch as" to open heavy, legal-sounding reasons - [perception: formal, connective, weighty]
- [18th–19th c.] - philosophy, religion, and law rely on it for exact causal scope - [perception: a precise instrument for "since, and to that extent"]
- [20th–21st c.] - journalism and speech prefer because and insofar as; inasmuch as survives in literary and academic prose - [perception: elegant, bookish, mildly archaic]
