• Diocletian: Similar because both are the two great late-Roman emperors whose joint splendour the context evokes (the Tetrarchy and the Constantinian monarchy), and both divided and reorganised the empire; differs because Diocletian (r. 284–305) persecuted Christians and established the Tetrarchy, while Constantine (r. 306–337) legalised Christianity and founded the single eastern capital.
  • Justinian: Similar because both were emperors who ruled a revitalised Roman/Byzantine state and presided over monumental building (Constantinople's churches; Hagia Sophia) and legal consolidation; differs because Justinian (r. 527–565) ruled two centuries later, was the codifier of Roman law, and completed rather than initiated the Christian empire.
  • Theodosius: Similar because both were Christian emperors who made Christianity official and shaped church-state relations; differs because Theodosius I (r. 379–395) made Nicene Christianity the sole state religion (Edict of Thessalonica, 380) and was the last emperor to rule a unified empire, whereas Constantine was the first Christian emperor.
  • Constantius: Similar because the name is the same family stem and both were emperors of the Constantinian dynasty; differs because Constantius (Constantius I Chlorus, and later Constantius II) did not found a new capital or initiate the Christian revolution, and the name marks the family rather than the epoch.
  • Augustus: Similar because both are the two archetypal "founders" of the Roman empire — Augustus of the Principate, Constantine of the Christian empire — and both names became imperial titles; differs because Augustus (d. 14 AD) founded the pagan empire, while Constantine founded its Christian successor and shifted the centre east.
  • Byzantium: Similar because the city-name is inseparable from Constantine's foundation of Constantinople (he refounded Byzantium); differs because Byzantium is the earlier Greek city and later a historiographical name for the whole Eastern Empire, whereas Constantine is the emperor who renamed it.
