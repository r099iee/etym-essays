• Constraints: Similar because both denote limitations that restrict freedom of action or artistic choice; differs because constraints is a broad, neutral operational term applicable to any limitation (budget, time, physics), whereas servitudes carries formal legal, philosophical, and architectural gravity emphasizing submission to an external master or structure.
  • Encumbrances: Similar because both describe burdens or legal liabilities attached to real property; differs because encumbrance is an umbrella financial and legal term including mortgages, liens, and unpaid taxes, whereas a servitude is a specific non-possessory right of use or restriction running with the land.
  • Easements: Similar because both represent the legal right of one party to use or restrict another's real estate for a specific purpose; differs because easement is the traditional terminology of Anglo-American common law, whereas servitude is the broader concept rooted in Roman civil law and international jurisprudence.
  • Bondage: Similar because both signify an utter absence of freedom and subjection to another's will; differs because bondage carries visceral physical connotations of chains, emotional enslavement, and oppressive captivity, whereas servitudes frequently operates as an abstract legal or aesthetic category.
  • Subjections: Similar because both denote being placed under the dominion, governance, or control of an external power; differs because subjection emphasizes the dynamic act or political hierarchy of being conquered, whereas servitude emphasizes the ongoing institutional status or structural rule.
  • Thralldoms: Similar because both denote complete submission and lack of personal autonomy; differs because thralldom is an archaic, poetic Scandinavian-derived word evoking Norse slave-taking and intense psychological or romantic obsession, unlike the clinical precision of servitudes.
  • Serfdoms: Similar because both represent institutional unfree labor; differs because serfdom refers specifically to the feudal socioeconomic system binding peasant laborers to a feudal estate or lord's land, whereas servitude applies universally to law, philosophy, art, and penal institutions.
  • Vassalages: Similar because both involve structural subordination within a hierarchy; differs because vassalage involves a reciprocal feudal contract where a subordinate nobleman owes military service and loyalty in exchange for land protection, whereas servitude lacks reciprocal nobility.
  • Limitations: Similar because both denote boundaries beyond which one cannot act; differs because limitations is a generic, everyday term, whereas servitudes denotes profound structural imperatives woven into the fabric of a monument or civil code.
