• Fireworks: Similar because sparklers are a kind of firework; differs because fireworks is the broad umbrella term (rockets, shells, fountains), while sparklers names only the hand-held wire-stick variety.
  • Squibs: Similar because both are small, modest fireworks in Tolkien's catalogue; differs because a squib hisses and pops or fizzles out, while a sparkler burns steadily with a shower of sparks.
  • Crackers: Similar because both are small celebratory fireworks lit by hand; differs because crackers explode with a bang, while sparklers are prized precisely for burning silently without detonating.
  • Roman candles: Similar because both eject coloured sparks and stars in festive displays; differs because a roman candle is a tube that fires projectiles skyward, while a sparkler is waved in the hand.
  • Fountains: Similar because both are non-explosive fireworks producing showers of sparks; differs because a fountain sits on the ground and erupts upward, while sparklers are held and waved.
  • Catherine wheels: Similar because both are sparkling display fireworks that spin or shower light; differs because a Catherine wheel rotates on a pin throwing a circle of sparks, while a sparkler is a static burning stick.
  • Flares: Similar because both are hand-held burning sticks giving bright light; differs because a flare gives one steady intense flame for signalling or distress, while a sparkler gives branching crackling sparks for delight.
  • Glow sticks: Similar because both are hand-waved novelties of light at parties; differs because glow sticks shine by chemiluminescence with no heat or sparks, while sparklers are true pyrotechnics that burn.
  • Torches: Similar because in the source sentence both are hand-held sources of festive flame; differs because a torch is a sustained flame for light or ceremony, while a sparkler is a short-lived shower of sparks for play.
