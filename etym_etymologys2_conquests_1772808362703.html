Linear derivation chain for CONQUESTS (single root word): 1. PIE *kwo- / *kwe- "who, what" (interrogative-relative pronominal stem; the base of "question" words across the family). Debate note: this traditional OED/Etymonline link — deriving Latin quaerere from the "question" stem — is NOT secure; de Vaan and others judge the origin of quaerere as uncertain. The chain from *kwo- should be read as a plausible but debated reconstruction. 2. Latin quaerere "to seek, ask, inquire, strive for, gain" (attested from the 2nd century BCE, e.g., Plautus). 3. Latin conquirere "to seek thoroughly, hunt up, procure, win" (com- intensive + quaerere; attested). 4. Latin conquisita (feminine past participle of conquirere; "that which has been sought out / won"). 5. Vulgar Latin *conquesta (nominalized feminine participle; "the act of conquering; what is won"). 6. Old French conqueste "conquest; thing conquered" (attested from the 11th century; the verbal line Old French conquerre gives English conquer, conqueror). 7. Middle English conquest (attested 13th century, via Anglo-Norman; after 1066 the word was culturally inevitable in England). 8. English conquest; plural CONQUESTS "territories won by force; a series of annexations" (the present root word). Sister lines (not ancestors): Latin quaestio > English question; acquirere > acquire; requirere > require, request; exquirere > exquisite; all preserve the "seek" sense without the military con- intensive.
