• Compensation: Similar because both mean payment or something given to offset a loss; differs because compensation is the neutral, often legal term for money or goods given to offset a loss without necessarily implying fault, while reparation implies making amends for a wrong and carries a moral dimension.
  • Restitution: Similar because both mean making good a loss or wrong; differs because restitution specifically means returning something to its rightful owner or restoring it to its original condition, while reparation may be satisfied by payment or other amends rather than return of the thing itself.
  • Amends: Similar because both mean the repair of a wrong or injury; differs because amends is a more general, often moral word for actions that satisfy a duty or repair a relationship, while reparation can be concrete, material, and legal, as well as moral.
  • Damages: Similar because both denote compensation for harm; differs because damages is the narrow legal award imposed by a court in civil cases to compensate proven injury, whereas reparation may be voluntary and moral as well as legal, and need not be quantified by a court.
  • Redress: Similar because both mean the setting right of a wrong or grievance; differs because redress emphasizes the removal or correction of an injustice, often by authority, while reparation emphasizes the giving of compensation or amends.
  • Atonement: Similar because both involve making good a wrong; differs because atonement is chiefly moral and religious, the expiation of guilt before God or conscience, while reparation is secular and practical, aimed at the injured party.
  • Indemnity: Similar because both denote compensation for loss or damage; differs because indemnity is a formal legal and financial term for security or compensation against loss or liability, while reparation is broader and more moral in connotation.
  • Satisfaction: Similar because both denote the fulfilling of a claim or the settling of a wrong; differs because satisfaction is a general word for the contentment or settling of an obligation, while reparation is specifically the making of amends for a wrong or injury.
  • Requital: Similar because both mean giving something in return for a wrong or a service; differs because requital is a poetic and archaic word for repayment or retaliation that can be negative as well as positive, while reparation is specifically constructive amends.
  • Repair: Similar because both come from the same Latin source and mean restoring something damaged; differs because repair is the plain, concrete verb and noun for mending an object, while reparation is the abstract, elevated noun for making amends for a wrong.
