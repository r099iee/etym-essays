- [1937, first appearance] - Coined in The Hobbit as the name of an 'ancient' Elvish sword; within the fiction the word is already millennia old (Gondolin, First Age) — readers meet it as venerable.
- [In-universe First Age] - Made in Gondolin, the hidden city: the name carries High-Elven prestige, and goblins dread it, calling the blade 'Biter'.
- [Textual history, 1937] - Carried through the quest: named by Elrond, wielded in Goblin-town, confiscated in Mirkwood, and finally laid on Thorin's tomb — remembered in songs as gleaming when foes press near.
- [1960s–80s] - Tolkien's linguistic papers (the Etymologies, published 1987) reach readers, and 'Orcrist' is re-read as pure Sindarin, built from the roots of 'orc' and 'cleaver'.
- [2000s–present] - Pop-culture afterlife: Jackson's films (2012–2014), games, and replicas make the name a canonical artifact of fantasy; 'Orcrist' now signifies Tolkienesque deep-world naming rather than an obscure prop.
