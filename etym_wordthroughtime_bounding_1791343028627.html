- 14th c. "rebounding, bouncing back from an impact" - Middle English bounden inherited the Old French sense of physical recoil and acoustic reverberation.
- 15th–16th c. "leaping forward, springing with athletic force" - the sense broadened from passive rebounding to active muscular propulsion by animals and humans.
- 17th–18th c. "pulsing, surging with vitality" - applied in natural philosophy and medicine to surging waves, leaping torrents, and the vigorous rhythmic beats of a "bounding pulse."
- 19th c. "prospering rapidly; exuberant" - extended metaphorically to describe surging health, national prosperity, and uncontainable high spirits.
- Late 19th–early 20th c. "arriving with pushy, vulgar exuberance" - colloquial British usage linked to the social label bounder; used with comedic disdain in Wodehouse to satirize tactless, bustling entrances.
- Contemporary usage "moving by leaps; escalating swiftly" - established in both physical descriptions of athletic locomotion and economic/metaphorical contexts of rapid growth.
