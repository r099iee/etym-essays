• Birding / bird-watching: Similar because both concern observing birds; differs because "birding" is a recreation (listing and observation for pleasure), while ornithology is a science with methods, publication, and theory.
  • Aviculture: Similar because both deal with birds; differs because aviculture is the keeping and breeding of captive birds (pets, collections, poultry), not the study of wild ones.
  • Ornithomancy: Similar because both are built on the same Greek root "ornitho-"; differs because ornithomancy is divination from birds — the ancient religious practice that the science named after it replaced.
  • Ornithopter: Similar because both share the Greek "ornitho-" element; differs because an ornithopter is an engineering artifact (a flapping-wing aircraft), not a discipline.
  • Zoology: Similar because both are scientific "ologies" of animals; differs because zoology covers the whole animal kingdom, ornithology being its bird-shaped subset.
  • Natural history: Similar because both describe the old observational study of living things; differs because "natural history" is the older, amateur-friendly tradition that preceded the -ologies, while ornithology is the institutionalised science.
  • Ethology: Similar because both study animal (including bird) behaviour; differs because ethology is the behaviour science of all species, while ornithology is bird-focused and includes anatomy, taxonomy, and ecology, not only behaviour.
  • Ecology: Similar because modern ornithology leans heavily on ecology; differs because ecology is the general science of organisms and environments, with no bird specificity.
  • Avifauna: Similar because both belong to the bird vocabulary of a region; differs because "avifauna" names the birds themselves (the species list of a region), not the study of them.
  • Falconry: Similar because both involve raptors and expertise about birds; differs because falconry is a hunting sport with trained hawks, not a science.
  • Poultry science: Similar because both are bird disciplines; differs because poultry science is applied agriculture focused on domestic fowl, while ornithology spans all ~11,000 species in the wild.
