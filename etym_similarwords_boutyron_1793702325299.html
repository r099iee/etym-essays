• Butter: Directly synonymous as 'boutyron' is the Greek word for butter; differs only in language of origin.
  • Ghee: Similar because it is a clarified butter product, widely used in South Asian cuisine; differs because it is anhydrous (water-free) and has a distinct nutty flavor due to the removal of milk solids.
  • Margarine: Similar because it is a fat spread used as a butter substitute; differs fundamentally because it is typically made from vegetable oils and water, not dairy.
  • Lard: Similar because it is an animal fat used in cooking and baking; differs because it is rendered pork fat, not a dairy product.
  • Tallow: Similar because it is a rendered animal fat, usually from beef or mutton, used in cooking and for other purposes; differs because it is not a dairy product and has a higher melting point than butter.
  • Olive Oil: Similar in its function as a primary cooking fat in many Mediterranean cultures; differs because it is a plant-derived oil, not a dairy product, and was the preferred fat over butter in ancient Greece.
