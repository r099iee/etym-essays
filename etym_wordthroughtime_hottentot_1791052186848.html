- Neutral colonial label - 1650s-1700s Dutch and English: simply the name for Cape herders; condescending but ordinary in travel logs; no alternative term in European languages.
- Scientific racial type - 18th-early 19th c.: Linnaean anthropology makes Hottentot a textbook race; exhibited, measured, mythologised; dehumanising curiosity, as in Hottentot Venus.
- Stock figure of idleness/savagery - Late 19th-early 20th c.: popular economics and adventure books use Hottentot as byword for lazy, improvident, comic savage; paternalistic amusement as in source context.
- Generic insult - Same era: to call a child or glutton a Hottentot means crude and ill-mannered; pejoration complete.
- Rejected slur - Mid-late 20th c. with decolonisation and anti-apartheid: Khoikhoi speakers and scholars denounce term; dictionaries add offensive, avoid; official South Africa drops it.
- Historical quotation only - Present: appears only in quotes, etymology notes, and discussions of racism; use outside quotation causes offence; replaced by Khoikhoi/Khoekhoe.
