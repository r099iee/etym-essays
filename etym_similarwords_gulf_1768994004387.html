• Bay: Similar in being an indentation of the sea into the land; differs because a bay is open and usually hospitable to ships, its mouth wide to the ocean, while a gulf is deeper, larger, and more nearly enclosed — almost a sea in itself, with land on most sides.
  • Bight: Similar in being a curved indentation of coast; differs because a bight is a broad, gentle, shallow curve of coastline, more open than a bay and rarely enclosed, while a gulf is deep-set, basin-like, and nearly shut in.
  • Inlet: Similar in being a body of water running into the land; differs because an inlet is narrow and tongue-like, a small opening of water along a coast, while a gulf is vast, deep, and basin-shaped.
  • Cove: Similar in being an indentation of coast; differs because a cove is small, sheltered, and often semicircular — a snug corner of the shore — while a gulf is enormous and its far shore may be invisible to the eye.
  • Estuary: Similar in being a body of water linked to the sea; differs because an estuary is the tidal mouth of a river where fresh and salt water meet, a mixing zone, while a gulf is purely marine and has no river feeding its basin.
  • Sound: Similar in being a broad body of water between land masses; differs because a sound is a long, relatively narrow passage of sea separating islands or linking seas, while a gulf is an enclosed basin of sea pushing into one landmass.
  • Lagoon: Similar in being a body of water sheltered by land; differs because a lagoon is shallow, often separated from the open sea by a reef or sandbar, while a gulf is deep, open at the surface, and bounded only by land.
  • Chasm: Similar in being a deep fissure; differs because a chasm is a deep crack or split in the rock, narrower and more jagged, rarely reachable across, while a gulf is a wide hollow or basin, water-filled or waterless, and can be leagues in extent.
  • Ravine: Similar in being a deep cut in the earth; differs because a ravine is a narrow, steep-sided valley cut by a stream, a channel you can often see across, while a gulf is far wider and deeper — a hollow whose far shore defeats the eye.
  • Abyss: Similar in being a vast, unfathomable depth; differs because an abyss is the bottomless depth itself — the vertical extreme, more terrifying and more abstract than gulf — while a gulf is a hollow with shores, a measurable (if immense) extent rather than a void.
  • Gorge: Similar in being a deep valley; differs because a gorge is a narrow, steep-walled valley with a river at the bottom, long and linear, while a gulf is basin-shaped and hollow — a depression rather than a trench.
  • Gap: Similar in being an opening or space between things; differs because a gap is the general word for any opening or break, small or large, while a gulf (figuratively) is the widest kind of gap — one so wide it seems uncrossable, as light cannot cross the underground gulf.
  • Rift: Similar in being a separation; differs because a rift is a crack or fissure, often a break in a relationship or a geological fault line, while a gulf is a broad, deep separation — not a crack but a basin of distance.
  • Divide: Similar in being a separation between groups; differs because a divide is a boundary or line of separation (a "political divide"), while a gulf is the wide open space between them — not the line but the unbridgeable distance.
  • Breach: Similar in being a break in something that should be whole; differs because a breach is a specific rupture, often reparable or openable, while a gulf is a standing, structural separation that resists crossing.
  • Hollow: Similar in being a depression in a surface; differs because a hollow is the plain, everyday word for any low place, small or large, while a gulf is an enormous, deep, and usually enclosed hollow, watery or cavernous.
  • Basin: Similar in being a depression that holds water; differs because a basin is the neutral geographic term for a drainage hollow or a depression holding a body of water, while a gulf is specifically the sea-filled basin open to the ocean at a mouth.
