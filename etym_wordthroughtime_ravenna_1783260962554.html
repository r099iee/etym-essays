- Late-antique imperial capital - Ravenna became the capital of the Western Roman Empire in 402 and then of Theodoric's Ostrogothic kingdom (493-526), so the name entered history as a seat of imperial and royal government on the marshy Adriatic plain.
- Byzantine exarchate - after Justinian's reconquest Ravenna was the seat of the Byzantine exarchate (584-751), and the name came to denote the last stronghold of Roman administration and mosaic art in Italy.
- 18th-19th-century historiography - Macaulay and Gibbon invoked Ravenna among the great continental courts where Germanic princes became the reverent pupils of Christian bishops, making it an emblem of the fusion of Germanic power with Roman and orthodox civilization.
- Contemporary sense - Ravenna survives as a living Italian city and UNESCO site, celebrated for its Byzantine mosaics, and in English remains a proper name freighted with late-antique and art-historical associations.
