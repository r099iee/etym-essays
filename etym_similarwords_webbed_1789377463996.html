• Palmate: Similar because both describe digits connected by a continuous skin membrane; differs because palmate is a specialized anatomical and botanical term specifically designating the joining of the forward-pointing toes or a hand-shaped leaf structure, whereas webbed is the universal descriptive and everyday term.
  • Syndactylous: Similar because both refer to digits fused or joined together; differs because syndactylous is a formal clinical, teratological, and evolutionary descriptor denoting congenital skeletal or cutaneous fusion between fingers or toes, whereas webbed highlights the functional, paddle-like connecting skin flap.
  • Lobate: Similar because both represent avian aquatic foot adaptations aiding propulsion; differs because lobate feet possess separate, scallop-shaped flexible flaps along each individual toe (as in grebes and coots) rather than a single continuous sheet connecting adjacent toes.
  • Reticulated: Similar because both denote patterns resembling an interwoven network; differs because reticulated describes an intersecting grid-like surface texture, net pattern, or veining (such as on python scales or leaf veins), whereas webbed emphasizes an unbroken connecting membrane or structural strap.
  • Interlaced: Similar because both evoke strands crossing over and under one another; differs because interlacing refers to the process or pattern of weaving separate threads or elements into an open mesh, whereas webbed denotes the resulting continuous sheet, biological flap, or finished heavy strap.
  • Membranous: Similar because both describe thin, flexible sheets of tissue; differs because membranous is a broad histological adjective describing any pliable layer or lining (e.g., mucous membranes, insect wings), whereas webbed specifically denotes membrane stretched across the intervals between digits or structural ribs.
