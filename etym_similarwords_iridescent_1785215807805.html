• Opalescent: Similar because both describe a play of colour in a material that shifts with the light; differs because opalescent names the milky, cloudy, internal glow of the opal, a soft play within a translucent body, while iridescent names a surface sheen of distinct spectral hues that shift with the viewing angle.
  • Pearlescent: Similar because both describe a luminous, colour-shifting sheen; differs because pearlescent is narrower and paler — the lustrous pinkish-white shimmer of mother-of-pearl, usually delicate and calm, lacking the full rainbow range of iridescence.
  • Prismatic: Similar because both evoke the colours of the rainbow; differs because prismatic suggests the clean, separated bands of a spectrum laid out in order by a prism, while iridescent colour is fluid, metallic, and changes with the angle of view.
  • Luminescent: Similar because both describe things that appear to glow; differs because luminescent means emitting one's own light, with no play of changing colour at all, while iridescence is a reflected, shifting play of hues.
  • Nacreous: Similar because both describe a shimmering, lustrous play of colour; differs because nacreous specifically denotes the pearly, rainbow-tinged surface of mother-of-pearl (nacre), a subset of the wider iridescence.
  • Chatoyant: Similar because both describe a changing, light-dependent sheen; differs because chatoyant is the specialist cat's-eye effect of certain gems — a single shifting band of light — rather than the full spectral play of iridescence.
  • Irised: Similar because both name colour effects of the iris-rainbow family; differs because irised is a rarer, technical term for displaying rainbow colours (also used of the eye's iris), while iridescent is the standard, productive adjective.
  • Shimmering: Similar because both describe light that trembles and shifts; differs because shimmering is a soft, quivering gleam without necessarily any colour change, while iridescent specifically involves a play of rainbow hues.
  • Glistening: Similar because both describe surfaces that catch and reflect light; differs because glistening names a wet, gleaming sparkle with no change of colour, while iridescent names an angle-dependent rainbow sheen.
  • Gleaming: Similar because both describe bright surfaces that catch the eye; differs because gleaming is a steady, soft brilliance, while iridescent is a shifting, multi-coloured play that alters with every movement.
  • Rainbow-hued: Similar because both evoke the colours of the rainbow; differs because rainbow-hued is an everyday descriptive phrase for displaying several rainbow colours at once, while iridescent specifically names the changing, light-dependent shimmer of those colours.
  • Polychromatic: Similar because both describe many colours; differs because polychromatic simply means multi-coloured, with no implication of changing with the angle, while iridescent centres on the shifting play of hues.
  • Iridescently shimmering / holographic: Similar because both describe rainbow-coloured, light-refracting surfaces; differs because holographic is a modern marketing word for a diffractive rainbow effect on film and foils, while iridescent covers the natural optical phenomenon as well as the manufactured one.
