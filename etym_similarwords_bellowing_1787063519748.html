• Roar: Similar because both name a loud, deep, sustained sound produced with force from the throat; differs because "roar" is broader — of lions, engines, crowds, and flames — while "bellow" specifically implies the deep, prolonged, resonant quality of a bull or a voice thrown like a bull's, and usually carries a more animalistic, swelling register.
  • Bawl: Similar because a loud, unrefined cry; differs because "bawl" usually describes a harsh, throaty shout or a person crying noisily, and lacks the deep resonance and sheer volume implied by "bellow".
  • Shout / Yell: Similar because both are loud human vocalizations; differs because they are human-specific and may be high- or low-pitched, whereas "bellow" emphasizes depth and is freely used of animals and even of inanimate thunderous sounds.
  • Holler: Similar because a loud call or cry; differs because "holler" is casual, chiefly American, and usually a simple summons, without the animal depth and menace of a bellow.
  • Howl: Similar because a long, loud animal cry; differs because "howl" is a rising, wavering, often mournful wail (as of wolves or wind), whereas a bellow is a blunt, sonorous, sustained blast.
  • Low / Moo: Similar because both describe cattle sounds; differs because "low" is the ordinary soft sound of cattle, while "bellow" is the intensified, aggressive, trumpet-like version used in anger, distress, or rutting.
  • Bray: Similar because a loud harsh animal cry (as of a donkey); differs because a bray is flat, coarse, and piercing, the opposite of the deep resonance of a bellow.
  • Thunder / Boom: Similar because both are deep, resonant, far-carrying noises; differs because they describe sounds produced by objects or forces (sky, cannon, drum) rather than by a living throat, and "bellow" keeps its link to a source that can swell and fall like breath.
  • Blare: Similar because a loud, harsh, unpleasant noise; differs because "blare" is usually a strident, flat sound (trumpet, alarm), without the bodily depth and muscular surge of a bellow.
  • Scream / Shriek: Similar because a loud cry; differs because these are high-pitched and piercing, the exact opposite in pitch and character of the low, booming bellow.
  • Clamor: Similar because a loud and persistent noise; differs because "clamor" is usually a confused uproar of many voices, whereas "bellow" is a single deep cry or voice.
