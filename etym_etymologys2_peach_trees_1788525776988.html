=== ELEMENT 1: PEACH ===

Old Persian (6th–4th c. BCE)

Pārsa

"Persia, the Persian land"

↓

Ancient Greek (4th–3rd c. BCE; Theophrastus)

Mēlon Persikón (Μῆλον Περσικόν)

"Persian apple / Persian fruit"

(so named because the fruit reached the Greco-Roman world via Persian trade routes from China)

↓

Classical Latin (1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE; Pliny the Elder)

mālum Persicum / persicum

"Persian apple; the peach fruit"

↓

Late Latin / Vulgar Latin (4th–6th c. CE)

pessica / pesca

"peach"

(regular phonetic assimilation of -rs- to -ss- in colloquial Western Romance)

↓

Old French (12th c.)

pesche

"peach (fruit and tree)"

↓

Anglo-Norman / Middle English (14th c.)

peche

"peach"

↓

Modern English (present)

peach

"sweet velvety stone fruit (Prunus persica)"



=== ELEMENT 2: TREE ===

PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed)

*dóru- / *deru- / *drew-

"tree, wood, oak; firm, solid, steadfast"

(ancient nominal root for standing timber; also source of true, trust, druid, and tar)

↓

Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)

*trewą

"tree, wood, timber"

↓

Old English (pre-10th c.)

trēow / trēo

"tree, wood, timber, beam; the Cross"

↓

Middle English (12th–14th c.)

tree / tre (plural trees / treen)

"living tree, timber, wooden post"

↓

Early Modern to Present English (compound attested c. 1380s)

PEACH-TREES

"deciduous orchard trees (Prunus persica) bearing pink blossoms and peaches"
