- [Old English–medieval] - Practical land word: mersc, 'marsh', names waste ground, rough pasture, and watery borders; the plural 'marshes' names whole dangerous districts that flood, hide outlaws, and swallow travellers.
- [Early modern] - Feared as sickly: 'marsh fever' (malaria) and miasmas are blamed on marsh air; drainage becomes a civic virtue — 'the marshes' connote disease and death.
- [18th–19th c.] - Romantic re-enchantment: drained where profitable, sublime where left — Dickens's 'marsh country' in Great Expectations (1861) makes the marshes a landscape of guilt, fog, and fate.
- [20th c.] - Ecological reversal: science reveals marshes as nurseries, filters, and flood-breaks; Ramsar (1971) protects them; 'the marshes' shift from wasteland to treasure.
- [Contemporary] - Conservation icons and climate actors: the Mesopotamian Marshes, the Camargue, coastal salt marshes; in ordinary speech 'the marshes' now sounds wild, fragile, and worth saving.
