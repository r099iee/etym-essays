- Early 1500s: to pelt as to strike with missiles - earthy street and battlefield slang for stoning, mud-slinging, seen as rowdy and violent.
- Late 1500s–1600s: pelting as contemptible, paltry - Shakespearean extension where what is pelted is worthless; moral contempt colours the word, later obsolete.
- 1600s–1700s: to pelt with words and to pelt along - respectable literary widening to verbal attack and headlong rushing; perception shifts from purely physical to energetic and importunate.
- 1800s: pelting rain as stock weather image - Dickensian era fixes the brutal-storm sense; readers hear noise, sting, and pitilessness, with faint admiration for nature's honesty.
- Present: vivid journalistic adjective for storms and metaphor for information overload - pelted with emails; perception is forceful but familiar, slightly British, punchy rather than scientific.
