- Judicial sentence - medieval courts condemning to death, prison and hell, awesome and terrifying, backed by sword and scripture
- Administrative doom - early modern officials condemning houses, meat and ships as unfit, bureaucratic and practical rather than moral
- Moral-public censure - 18th-19th century press, pulpit and public condemning manners, vices and policies, righteous and often partisan
- Modern ubiquitous judgment - contemporary media condemning with statements, looks and reports, instant and global, ranging from solemn human-rights verdicts to hasty pile-ons
