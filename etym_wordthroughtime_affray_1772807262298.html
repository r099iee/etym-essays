- Middle English 'affray' - alarm, fright, the fear of sudden attack (borrowed as a noun meaning 'fear, terror')
- 15th-16th century - shifts from the terror itself to the noisy disturbance that provokes it: a quarrel or brawl that alarms the public
- 17th-18th century - the fight sense hardens; the word moves into law, naming a public breach of the peace
- Contemporary - formal, legal, and slightly archaic-legal; everyday speech says 'brawl' or 'fight', while 'affray' survives as a statutory offence in British law
