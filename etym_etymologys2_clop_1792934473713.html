Modern English (echoic formation) (attested c. 1920s)
clop
“sound of a hoof striking”
↓
Modern English (verb use) (20th c., alongside noun)
to clop
“to go with a hoofbeat sound”
↓
Modern English (extended) (current)
clop
“hollow beat; to move beating”

Note: clop is imitative in origin and has no ancestor chain: it was coined from the sound itself, not inherited. It belongs to a wider echoic family (clap, click, clash, clip, clatter) sharing the cl- impact cluster, but no authority derives it stepwise from any one of them, so no deeper stages are given.
