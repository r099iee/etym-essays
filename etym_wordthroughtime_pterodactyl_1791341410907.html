- 1809–1812 - Coined in scientific French by Georges Cuvier (*ptéro-dactyle*) and Latinized by Samuel Thomas von Sömmerring (*Pterodactylus*) to designate the first recognized flying reptile fossil from the Bavarian Solnhofen limestone.
- 1820s–1850s - Entered English paleontological and Victorian public discourse, electrifying society as an unimaginable chimera that shattered the biblical boundary between birds, bats, and reptiles.
- Late 19th to Mid-20th Century - Cemented in pulp adventure fiction (Arthur Conan Doyle's *The Lost World*, Jules Verne) and cinema as a terrifying, screeching prehistoric monster; colloquialized in British humor (P.G. Wodehouse) as a metaphor for an ancient, gloomy, leathery curmudgeon.
- Late 20th Century to Present - Displaced in rigorous scientific literature by the broader term *pterosaur*, but retained as a ubiquitous cultural unigram in popular media and childhood paleontology.
