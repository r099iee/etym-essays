- [Writing and the alphabet] - In its current form since Middle English (from Latin littera), the plural 'letters' first named the letters of the alphabet and writing itself - the very elements of literacy.
- [Classical institutional sense] - In the Renaissance and Augustan age the plural hardened into a frozen institutional meaning of 'learning, literature, scholarship' - 'a man of letters', 'arts and letters' - dignified and humanistic.
- [Modern narrowing] - As 'literature' narrowed in the 19th century to imaginative writing, 'letters' remained the broader, more elevated term covering history, oratory, philosophy and correspondence.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'letters' survives chiefly in set phrases ('arts and letters', 'man of letters') and in academic titles (Doctor of Letters), carrying a slightly old-fashioned, honorific flavour.
