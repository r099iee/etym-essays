- [5th c. BCE: the name of the Athenian statesman who led Athens in its greatest generation] - [Perception: in life — awe, mockery, and fear ('Olympios', head-onion jokes); the name means 'far-famed' and lives up to it]
- [4th–2nd c. BCE: historians and philosophers debate his democracy and his war] - [Perception: admired by Thucydides, criticised by Platonists; already a case-study]
- [Roman era (2nd c. CE): Plutarch's 'Life of Pericles' fixes his double image — wise patron of the arts, architect of war] - [Perception: the great example of statesmanship, for good and ill]
- [Renaissance: Plutarch translated into English (1579); Shakespeare's 'Pericles, Prince of Tyre' (1609) makes the name a stage romance] - [Perception: name enters popular culture, half myth]
- [18th–19th c.: 'the age of Pericles' becomes the label of the classical golden age (Grote, Arnold, Pater)] - [Perception: cultural benchmark — the measure of all great eras]
- [Today: 'Periclean' still means democratic greatness; the Funeral Oration is quoted at Gettysburg's descendants] - [Perception: aspirational and schoolbook; the name is a byword for civilisation's bright height]
