• Hesitate: Similar because both involve a pause or delay before action; differs because hesitate is a mental pause before starting, while falter is an unsteady weakening of something already under way.
  • Stumble: Similar because both are physical missteps; differs because stumble is strictly a tripping of the feet, while falter can also be vocal, moral, or institutional weakening.
  • Waver: Similar because both are unsteady; differs because waver implies oscillation back and forth between options or opinions, whereas falter implies a single collapse of strength or momentum.
  • Flounder: Similar because both describe struggling unsteadily; differs because flounder implies frantic, confused thrashing (often in mud or water), while falter is a quieter, momentary loss of steadiness.
  • Flag: Similar because both describe losing energy or vigour; differs because flag is a gradual tiring (as of a flag dropping), while falter is a sudden stumble or interruption of forward movement.
  • Totter: Similar because both describe unsteady movement; differs because totter is a teetering, swaying motion on the edge of falling, while falter is an interruption of forward progress.
  • Vacillate: Similar because both involve uncertainty; differs because vacillate is a formal, repetitive oscillation between two positions, while falter is a physical or energetic weakening.
  • Dither: Similar because both involve indecision; differs because dither is a nervous, trivial fluster about small choices, while falter suggests a genuine loss of strength or resolve.
  • Stammer / stutter: Similar because both describe broken speech; differs because stammer and stutter are clinical terms for speech disorders, while falter's speech sense is situational, caused by emotion.
  • Quaver: Similar because both describe a trembling voice; differs because quaver is specifically a tremulous vibration of tone, while falter is a breaking off or hesitating of the flow of words.
  • Teeter: Similar because both suggest instability; differs because teeter is a swaying on a brink (physical or figurative), while falter is a loss of forward progress or strength.
  • Weaken: Similar because both describe loss of strength; differs because weaken is a gradual reduction of power, while falter is a specific unsteady interruption of a continuous motion.
  • Lurch: Similar because both describe sudden unsteady movement; differs because lurch is a violent, abrupt jerk, while falter is a lesser, hesitant stumble.
  • Flicker: Similar because both describe a failing continuous process; differs because flicker is used of light and weak flames, while falter is used of movement, speech, and abstract processes.
