• Compensation: Similar because both mean payment for loss or harm; differs because “compensation” is the modern, neutral, legal-industrial word, while “recompense” is formal and literary.
  • Repayment: Similar because both mean paying back; differs because “repayment” implies a debt of money, while “recompense” covers losses, injuries and services.
  • Remuneration: Similar because both are payments made in return; differs because “remuneration” is specifically payment for work or services, with no sense of making good a loss.
  • Requital: Similar because both mean repayment in kind, good or ill; differs because “requital” is rarer and more archaic, often with a tone of vengeance.
  • Restitution: Similar because both mean restoring what was taken or lost; differs because “restitution” is a legal term for restoring property or rights, not merely paying money.
  • Indemnity: Similar because both are compensation against loss; differs because “indemnity” is contractual and technical (insurance, legal protection), while “recompense” is general.
  • Damages: Similar because damages are court-awarded compensation; differs because “damages” is strictly legal and plural, while “recompense” is broader and non-technical.
  • Redress: Similar because both mean remedy for a wrong; differs because “redress” emphasises setting the grievance right, often by action rather than money.
  • Reparations: Similar because both are making good a wrong; differs because “reparations” is collective and historical (war reparations), paid by nations or institutions.
  • Amends: Similar because “to make amends” is to compensate for a wrong; differs because “amends” is moral and personal, and exists mainly in the fixed phrase.
  • Reward: Similar because both can mean a return for merit or service; differs because a reward is positive only and implies no prior loss.
  • Reimbursement: Similar because both are paying back; differs because “reimbursement” repays exact expenses incurred, with no idea of injury or justice.
  • Retaliation / vengeance: Similar because archaic “recompense” could mean requital of evil; differs because these are emotional and punitive, while “recompense” is measured and just.
