• Ousted: Similar because both mean "forced out of a position"; differs because ousted is broader and less grand (ousted from a committee, ousted from power), with no throne-imagery, while deposed is reserved for rulers and supreme leaders.
  • Dethroned: Similar because both mean "removed from a throne"; differs because dethroned is metaphorical and almost playful outside monarchy (a dethroned champion), while deposed is the serious political and legal word.
  • Toppled: Similar because both mean "brought down from power"; differs because toppled focuses on the violence or suddenness of the fall (a regime toppled by protests), while deposed names the formal act of removal and its result.
  • Overthrown: Similar because both mean "removed from power by force"; differs because overthrown describes the destruction of a whole government or regime, while deposed focuses on the person — the ruler himself is put down.
  • Removed: Similar because both mean "taken away from a position"; differs because removed is the colorless general word (removed from office, removed from the board), while deposed adds gravity, force, and usually a throne.
  • Unseated: Similar because both mean "dislodged from a position"; differs because unseated is used of legislators, officials and jockeys, often by election or accident, while deposed is for supreme power.
  • Dismissed: Similar because both mean "sent away from a post"; differs because dismissed is everyday and bureaucratic (dismissed from a job), lacking the political and historical weight of deposed.
  • Displaced: Similar because both mean "moved out of a place or position"; differs because displaced is about location and population (displaced persons), not about authority, while deposed is specifically about power.
  • Defrocked: Similar because both mean "stripped of office" (of clergy); differs because defrocked is specific to the priesthood and its vestments, while deposed covers kings, presidents, bishops and popes alike.
  • Cashiered: Similar because both mean "removed from a position of authority"; differs because cashiered is military jargon for the dishonourable dismissal of officers, while deposed is civilian and political.
  • Uncrowned: Similar because both mean "deprived of royal power"; differs because uncrowned is mostly figurative and literary (an uncrowned king = a de facto ruler), while deposed describes the actual act of removal.
  • (Legal) examined under oath: Similar because "deposed" in law means giving sworn recorded testimony; differs from "interrogated", which implies hostile questioning by police or captors, while a deposition is a formal civil procedure with counsel present.
