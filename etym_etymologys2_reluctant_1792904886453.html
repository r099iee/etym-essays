Latin (classical, 1st c. BCE)
luctari
‘to wrestle, struggle’
↓
Latin compound (classical)
reluctari
‘to struggle against, resist’
↓
Latin participle (classical)
reluctans, reluctantem
‘struggling back, resisting’
↓
Early Modern English borrowing (1590s)
reluctant
‘struggling against, unwilling’
↓
Modern English (current)
reluctant
‘unwilling, holding back’

Note: Latin luctari itself has no fully certain PIE parent; older works linked it to words for bending or lock, but de Vaan leaves it without an accepted deeper root, so the chain honestly begins at Latin. The English word is a direct learned borrowing of the Latin present participle, not passed through French.
