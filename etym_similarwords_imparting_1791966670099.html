• conveying: Similar because it describes the transfer of information, meaning or feeling from one mind to another; differs because convey stresses the message or impression that arrives and can be unintentional, whereas imparting stresses a deliberate grant by someone who possesses the thing.
  • communicating: Similar because both involve making something known; differs because communicating is the broad, neutral term covering any exchange, while imparting is formal, one-directional and implies an unequal relationship between giver and receiver.
  • transmitting: Similar because both suggest passing something along a channel; differs because transmitting is mechanical or technical — signals, disease, data — and carries no implication that the receiver is edified or entrusted with a secret.
  • divulging: Similar because it can mean making information known; differs because divulging specifically implies disclosing something previously secret or private, often against an expectation of confidence, whereas imparting is neutral about secrecy.
  • disclosing: Similar because both can introduce information to a recipient; differs because disclosing is legal and impersonal, focused on the revelation itself, while imparting keeps the personal, beneficent relation between the two parties in view.
  • bestowing: Similar because both mean granting something to someone; differs because bestowing is used of honours, gifts and rights, and implies condescension and ceremony, whereas imparting is used of qualities and knowledge and need not involve status.
  • conferring: Similar because it can mean granting a quality or benefit; differs because conferring is the formal word for degrees, titles and rights, and in the sense "consult" it is reciprocal, while imparting is always a one-way transfer.
  • sharing: Similar because both involve giving others a portion of what one has; differs because sharing is warmer, more reciprocal and at home in everyday speech, while imparting is bookish and hierarchical.
  • disseminating: Similar because both spread something to others; differs because disseminating implies wide, often impersonal distribution — ideas, propaganda, seed — and not a face-to-face grant to a particular recipient.
  • instilling: Similar because it describes putting a quality or belief into someone's mind; differs because instilling is slow, repeated and formative, working on the receiver's character, whereas imparting presents the transfer as a single act of giving.
