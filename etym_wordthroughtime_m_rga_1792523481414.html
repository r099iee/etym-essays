- [Vedic & Early Upaniṣadic Period, c. 1500–600 BCE] — originated as a hunter's term for the physical footprint, slot, or track of wild game (*mṛga*), shifting in late Vedic literature to denote the solar course and the mystical pathway trodden by ancestral seers into the afterlife — [perception: perceived as a physical trail and an intuitive method of tracking unseen cosmic order]
- [Classical Epic & Philosophical Era, c. 500 BCE–500 CE] — codified in the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, *Bhagavad Gītā*, Buddhism, and Jainism into distinct soteriological pathways (*Jñāna*, *Bhakti*, *Karma*, and the Buddhist *Aṣṭāṅgika-mārga*) — [perception: universally venerated as an authentic, disciplined regimen of spiritual self-realization and moral righteousness]
- [Classical Aesthetic & Musicological Period, c. 2nd c. BCE–13th c. CE] — established in Bharata's *Nāṭyaśāstra*, Daṇḍin's *Kāvyādarśa*, and Śārṅgadeva's *Saṅgītaratnākara* as the pristine, divinely ordained mode of music (*mārga-saṅgīta*) and poetic style (*Vaidarbha mārga*), sharply distinguished from regional folk styles (*deśī*) — [perception: revered as high sacred classical art capable of purifying the soul and invoking divine presence]
- [Medieval & Modern Period, 14th–21st c.] — while ancient *mārga* music evolved into the dual classical traditions of Hindustani and Carnatic music, *mārga* persisted as the scholarly designation for classical high art, royal boulevards (*rājamārga*), and spiritual ways across modern Indian languages — [perception: esteemed as the timeless emblem of rigorous, orthodox, and transcendent classical excellence]
