ROCKROSES is a genuine English compound of two loanwords, rock and rose, joined in English in 1578 to name the shrubs of the genus Cistus. Neither element has a secure PIE pedigree; both chains are given in full below. CHAIN 1 — ROCK (no PIE ancestry; a pre-Roman substrate loan): Medieval Latin *rocca "rock, mass of stone" (8th c. CE; first attested in this form; origin debated — generally taken to be a pre-Roman substrate word of Gaulish/Celtic or Italic origin; NOT from Latin petra "rock" or rūpes "cliff"; no accepted PIE reconstruction) → Old North French roque "rock" (9th–12th c. CE) → Old French roche "rock, cliff, crag" (10th–13th c. CE; cf. rocher "crag") → Anglo-Norman → Middle English rokke "rock, stone mass" (attested c. 1300) → English rock "rock, stone mass" (14th c. CE onward) → English rock-rose / rockrose "Cistus" (1578, compound of rock + rose) → ROCKROSES (plural in -s, the note's form) SISTER BRANCHES (from the same substrate source, not ancestors of English rock): Italian rocca "rock, fortress" (whence roccaforte "stronghold"), Spanish roca "rock", Portuguese roca, Occitan roca. CHAIN 2 — ROSE (non-PIE; a Greek word of probable Old Iranian or Semitic origin): Greek ῥόδον (rhódon) "rose" (attested in Sappho, c. 600 BCE; no accepted Indo-European etymology; ancient grammarians called it Phrygian; modern scholars posit an Old Iranian or Semitic source — see note) → Latin rosa "rose" (borrowed from Greek, probably through a South Italian/Greek dialect, 3rd–1st c. BCE) → Old English rōse "rose" (borrowed from Latin rosa, 9th c. CE) → Middle English rose "rose" (12th–15th c. CE) → English rose "rose" (15th c. CE onward) → English rock-rose / rockrose "Cistus" (1578, compound of rock + rose) → ROCKROSES (plural in -s, the note's form) NOTE ON DEBATE: Neither element descends from an Indo-European root within English. The rock-word is a Western European substrate loan that entered Latin only late, with no accepted PIE reconstruction; the rose-word is famously etymologically opaque — Sappho already uses it, and its origin is disputed between an Old Iranian source (Avestan varǝδa- "rose", Armenian vard "rose") and a Semitic source (Aramaic wrd', Akkadian wurtinnu), with the "Phrygian" label of the ancient grammarians reflecting their own uncertainty. The compound rock + rose itself is a late, transparent English coinage (1578), and its plural rockroses (the form of this note) is a simple inflection added in English.
