• Obviously: Similar because both mark clear perception and plain understanding; differs because obviously is the more conversational everyday twin, slightly less judicial or measured than evidently.
  • Apparently: Similar because both introduce a conclusion drawn from what is seen; differs because apparently is weaker and provisional ('so it seems'), allowing error, while evidently claims plain, grounded proof.
  • Clearly: Similar because both express plain understanding; differs because clearly is broader and less tied to the metaphor of visible evidence.
  • Manifestly: Similar because both stress formal demonstration; differs because manifestly is more legalistic and elevated, emphasizing overt display rather than visible inference.
  • Plainly: Similar because both mean 'in a plain, obvious way'; differs because plainly is more direct and earthier, less tied to the evidential chain of proof.
  • Patently: Similar because both claim something is obvious from the evidence; differs because patently often collocates with absurdity or wrongness ('patently false') and is narrower in distribution.
  • Seemingly: Similar because both present an appearance to an observer; differs because seemingly stresses outward appearance and often implies the reality may differ, a hedged quality evidently lacks.
  • Ostensibly: Similar because both describe how things appear; differs because ostensibly signals that appearances may be deceptive and hides the true reason, while evidently asserts the conclusion is sound.
