- [Late 14th-15th Century] - First recorded in late Middle English as *trappe-dore*; primarily denoted concealed floor flaps or hinged pit covers designed to trap animals or access hidden subterranean vaults.
- [Late 16th-17th Century] - Emerged as a fundamental term of Elizabethan and Jacobean theatre architecture, designating stage apertures through which supernatural characters entered and exited dramatically.
- [18th-19th Century] - Expanded into vernacular domestic, industrial, and maritime architecture (cellar doors, warehouse hoists) and natural history (trapdoor spiders c. 1820s).
- [Late 20th-21st Century] - Underwent profound mathematical and technological abstraction with the invention of asymmetric cryptography (Diffie-Hellman, RSA trapdoor functions) and cybersecurity vulnerability analysis.
