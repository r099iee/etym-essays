• Doghouse: Similar because it is the American word for the same small shelter for a dog; differs because kennel is the standard British term and also names the boarding/breeding establishment, which doghouse never does — and doghouse lives in the idiom 'in the doghouse' (in disgrace).
  • Pen / Run: Similar because both are enclosures where dogs are kept; differ because a pen or run is an open fenced area for exercise, while a kennel is the sheltered, usually roofed structure for sleeping.
  • Cage: Similar because both can confine a dog; differ because a cage is of bars or wire, suggesting imprisonment and transport, while a kennel is a dog's proper shelter, though neglect can turn it into a prison.
  • Shelter: Similar because a kennel is a shelter and rescue establishments are 'animal shelters'; differ because shelter is general for any protection and, in the rescue sense, houses many animals awaiting homes, while a kennel is specifically a dog's place.
  • Hutch: Similar because it is the comparable small shelter for a rabbit or small animal; differs because it belongs to rabbits and rodents, not dogs.
  • Sty / Byre / Stable: Similar because they are the animal-housing words built with the same Latin suffix pattern as kennel; differ because they name shelters for pigs, cattle, and horses respectively.
  • Den / Lair: Similar because a dog's kennel is like a wild animal's den; differ because a den or lair is natural and self-made, while a kennel is a human-built structure.
  • Hovel: Similar because both can describe a mean, cramped dwelling; differ because hovel is applied to human housing as a term of contempt, while kennel names the dog's shelter first and its contemptuous human use is secondary and dated.
