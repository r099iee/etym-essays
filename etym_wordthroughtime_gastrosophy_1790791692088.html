- 1820s–1830s: Coined by French utopian socialist Charles Fourier as a visionary social science harmonizing culinary pleasure, agriculture, and communal passions.
- 1850s: Systematized in German by Baron Eugen von Vaerst as the cultural philosophy and ethical art of table pleasures.
- Late 19th to Mid-20th Century: Used primarily in academic, philosophical, and gourmet circles to distinguish enlightened culinary appreciation from mere gluttony.
- 1970s–Present: Re-interpreted by Roland Barthes and contemporary food studies scholars as a sophisticated semiotic system linking bodily orality with literary pleasure.
