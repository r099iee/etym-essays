• Cut down: Similar because both mean "to bring a tree or person down"; differs because cut down is the slower, deliberate act of sawing or hewing, while felled is the single blow or collapse — and felled is the standard verb of formal prose.
  • Hew: Similar because both are verbs of chopping down; differs because hew stresses the axe-work itself (to hew timber into shape), while fell stresses the result — the making of something fall.
  • Chop down: Similar because both mean "to bring a tree to the ground"; differs because chop down is colloquial and physical, while felled covers battle, sport and metaphor as well as timber.
  • Knock down: Similar because both mean "to cause to fall by a blow"; differs because knock down is casual and everyday, while felled is weightier and more final.
  • Strike down: Similar because both mean "to disable or kill with a blow"; differs because strike down emphasises the blow itself and is dramatic, while felled simply states the fall.
  • Floor: Similar because both mean "to knock to the ground"; differs because floor is informal and physical (he floored him), while felled carries the weight of timber and battle.
  • Prostrate: Similar because both mean "to lay flat"; differs because prostrate is formal or reflexive (to prostrate oneself) and stresses the lying-flat state, while felled stresses the violent act.
  • Topple: Similar because both mean "to bring down something tall"; differs because topple suggests losing balance (a statue topples), while felled suggests a deliberate or massive blow.
  • Bring down: Similar because both mean "to cause to fall"; differs because bring down is general (bring down a government, an aircraft), while felled is physical first and metaphorical second.
  • Level: Similar because both mean "to make flat"; differs because level is used of buildings and terrain (level the site), while felled is used of trees, people and reputations.
  • Raze: Similar because both mean "to destroy down to the ground"; differs because raze applies to buildings and settlements, while felled applies to trees, people and living things.
  • Demolish: Similar because both mean "to destroy utterly"; differs because demolish is formal and technical (demolish a structure), while felled is the physical, native English word for what falls.
  • Mow down: Similar because both mean "to knock down in great numbers"; differs because mow down suggests sweeping destruction of many (gunfire, vehicles), while felled usually describes single blows to single things.
  • Overthrow: Similar because both mean "to bring down from standing or power"; differs because overthrow is political and abstract (overthrow a regime), while felled remains concrete — the body or the tree that actually hits the ground.
