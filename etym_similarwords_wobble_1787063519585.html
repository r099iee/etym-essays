• Totter: Similar because both denote unsteady movement; differs because totter specifically implies being on the very brink of falling or collapsing entirely due to severe feebleness or top-heaviness (e.g., a tottering toddler, a tottering regime), whereas wobble emphasizes continuous side-to-side oscillation without necessarily falling.
  • Waver: Similar because both describe vacillation and instability; differs because waver is primarily used for light, sound, flickering flames, mental resolve, or lines that lose straightness (e.g., wavering courage, wavering candle), whereas wobble is more mechanical, somatic, and tactile.
  • Teeter: Similar because both involve balancing precariously; differs because teeter emphasizes rocking back and forth across a fulcrum or edge (like a seesaw), conveying imminent danger of toppling over an edge (e.g., teetering on the brink of disaster).
  • Vacillate: Similar because both indicate indecision and fluctuating movement; differs because vacillate is a formal Latinate term used almost exclusively for shifting between opposing opinions, choices, or emotional states.
  • Stagger: Similar because both describe irregular walking; differs because stagger denotes heavy, clumsy, uncontrolled steps with sudden drunken or concussed lunges in random directions, rather than the rhythmic side-to-side rocking of a wobble.
  • Oscillate: Similar because both describe back-and-forth movement; differs because oscillate is a precise technical term implying smooth, regular, mathematically predictable sinusoidal motion between two fixed extremes (e.g., a pendulum).
  • Reel: Similar because both involve loss of balance; differs because reel conveys a dramatic sensation of dizziness, spinning, or being thrown violently backward by a blow.
