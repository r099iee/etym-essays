Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*hwistlōjan
"to whistle"
(imitative of the sound)
↓
Old English (c. 725)
hwistlian
"to whistle, hiss"
↓
Middle English (12th–14th c.)
whistlen
"to whistle; to sound shrilly"
↓
Modern English (from c. 1500)
whistle
"to make a clear shrill sound by forcing air through a small opening"
↓
Present-participle inflection (-ing)
whistling

Note on origin: the word is imitative/onomatopoeic — its sound-mimicking core (the hiss-and-blow of *hw-) is reconstructable back only to Proto-Germanic, and no safe PIE root can be posited. It is related within Germanic to Old Norse hvisla "to whisper" and Old English hwisprian "to whisper", all built on the same sound-symbolic initial cluster; beyond Germanic, any further ancestry is unknown and must not be guessed.
