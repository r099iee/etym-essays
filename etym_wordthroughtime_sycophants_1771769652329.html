- [Ancient Greece informer] - ['Sycophant' (fig-showing informer) first names an informer or denouncer who exposed smugglers of figs; a despised type of public accuser]
- [17th-18th century flattering dependent] - [The sense shifts entirely: a sycophant is now the flatterer who courts favour in court or patronage society, as in Gibbon's literary world]
- [19th century obsequious self-server] - [Settles as a formal insult for those who buy advantage with exaggerated praise]
- [20th century to present bootlicker] - [Means a parasitic flatterer, a 'yes-man' in elevated dress; the old informer sense is lost and only the fawning-toady meaning survives]
