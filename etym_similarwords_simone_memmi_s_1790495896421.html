• Simone Martini: Similar because it is the same painter under his correct modern name; differs because Martini is the current scholarly form, while Simone Memmi's is the obsolete Vasarian alias with possessive.
  • Lippo Memmi: Similar because he was Simone's brother-in-law, partner, and fellow Sienese gold-ground painter; differs because Lippo is a distinct artist, not Simone himself.
  • Duccio di Buoninsegna: Similar because he was Simone's master and founder of Sienese painting; differs because Duccio is earlier, more Byzantine and hieratic, while Simone is more Gothic and courtly.
  • Ambrogio Lorenzetti: Similar because he is another great Sienese Trecento master of the same generation; differs because Ambrogio is famed for secular allegory and naturalism, not Simone's lyrical devotion.
  • Pietro Lorenzetti: Similar because he is Ambrogio's brother and Sienese contemporary; differs because Pietro leans to sculptural Giottesque weight, while Simone leans to linear elegance.
  • Giotto di Bondone: Similar because he is the Florentine Trecento counterpart and rival standard; differs because Giotto is Florentine, massive and dramatic, while Simone is Sienese, refined and decorative.
  • Martino di Bartolomeo: Similar because the names overlap confusingly (Martino and Martini); differs because he is a later Sienese painter, not the great Simone.
  • Hans Memling: Similar only in confusingly similar surname sound (Memmi and Memling); differs because Memling is 15th-century Netherlandish oil painter, a wholly different school and century.
  • Fra Angelico: Similar because he is another devout gold-ground religious painter beloved by Ruskin; differs because Angelico is early-15th-century Florentine Dominican, a century later and more Renaissance.
  • Gothic Sienese manner: Similar as a stylistic paraphrase for Simone Memmi's qualities; differs because it is a general period label, while Simone Memmi's points to one master's hand.
