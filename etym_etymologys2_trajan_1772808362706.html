TRAJAN is not a transparently derivable Indo-European word; it is a Latin proper name (a cognomen) whose deeper prehistory is genuinely uncertain, and the honest chain therefore records attested forms and explicitly flags the unknown point rather than inventing a PIE root. Chain for TRAJAN (single root word, the cognomen): 1. Unknown onomastic origin — Traius / Traianus type name-stock; debated whether of provincial Italic (possibly Osco-Umbrian-type), Etruscan-influenced, or local Iberian/Hispanic origin; no secure PIE etymology is reconstructable; scholars treat the ultimate meaning as opaque (attested as Roman gentilic and cognomen stock). 2. Latin Traianus — cognomen meaning "of/belonging to the Traii (Traia family)"; the -ianus suffix forms a relational/adjectival name (as in Hadrianus from Hadria); attested from the 1st century BC/AD in Roman onomastics. 3. Marcus Ulpius Traianus — full Roman name of the emperor's father and of the emperor himself (nomen Ulpius from gens Ulpia + cognomen Traianus); the emperor was born at Italica in Hispania Baetica in AD 53. 4. Marcus Ulpius Traianus, emperor — ruled AD 98–117 after adoption by Nerva (AD 97); his official style became Imperator Caesar Nerva Traianus Augustus, but the familiar designation remained Traianus / Trajan. 5. English TRAJAN — the conventional English short form of the cognomen, used in Gibbon's narrative and modern historiography; also rendered Trajano (Italian/Spanish), Traian (Romanian), Trajan (French/German). Debated/nuanced note: The ultimate source of the name is unknown. Older popular works once suggested meanings such as "endure" or "three" (connecting to Latin tres or to Slavic/Illyrian roots), but modern scholarship rejects these as folk etymology. No Proto-Indo-European root can be securely assigned; the responsible position is to state that the name's pre-Latin etymology is opaque.
