- [1780s adoption from French] - Borrowed as a neutral, bookish word for "origin or source"; high-register but with no special prestige.
- [19th century: art & antiquarian world] - Shifts toward the documented history of an object's ownership; begins to carry an aura of authentication and scholarly authority.
- [20th century: museums & auction houses] - Becomes the indispensable term for a thing's documented biography; connotes genuineness, connoisseurship, and market value.
- [Modern scholarly & general use] - Extends to texts (the provenance of a manuscript or edition) and even data; retains an air of archival expertise and curatorial seriousness.
