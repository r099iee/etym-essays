• Proximity: Similar because both denote spatial nearness or closeness; differs because proximity indicates that objects are near each other but may still be separated by a substantial intervening void, whereas contiguity strictly demands actual physical contact, a shared border, or an uninterrupted boundary.
  • Adjacency: Similar because both describe objects positioned side by side; differs because adjacency allows for items lying next to each other across a small gap or street without directly touching, while contiguity emphasizes literal surface-to-surface touching or topological continuous joining.
  • Juxtaposition: Similar because both involve placing entities in close spatial contact; differs because juxtaposition specifically implies deliberate placement or side-by-side comparison to highlight artistic contrast or intellectual divergence, whereas contiguity denotes an ambient physical or topological state of touching.
  • Abutment: Similar because both involve direct boundary contact between adjacent entities; differs because abutment is a specialized architectural, engineering, or legal term emphasizing the point or structural surface of physical support where one heavy mass leans against or terminates on another, while contiguity is a broader descriptive state.
  • Contact: Similar because both involve physical touching between surfaces; differs because contact can be momentary, localized, or incidental (such as an electrical contact or two billiard balls colliding), whereas contiguity typically implies a sustained, linear, or extensive border along shared margins.
  • Apposition: Similar because both refer to placing things in close physical alignment; differs because apposition denotes the active process or grammatical positioning of placing one element alongside another for syntactic explanation, or biological layer deposition, whereas contiguity is an established spatial condition.
  • Vicinity: Similar because both describe regional closeness; differs because vicinity denotes a general surrounding neighborhood or vague ambient radius without any requirement of physical connection, while contiguity is exact and topologically rigorous.
  • Propinquity: Similar because both signify nearness; differs because propinquity carries rich connotations of kinship, psychological affinity, emotional warmth, or chronological proximity, whereas contiguity leans toward objective, geometric, or territorial touching.
  • Coterminousness: Similar because both describe shared geographic boundaries; differs because coterminousness specifies that two distinct entities share identical outer borders or cover precisely the same coextensive area or time span, whereas contiguous entities merely touch along one common border.
