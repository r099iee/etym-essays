Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*stebh- / *stob-
"post, stem, to support, step firmly"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*staupaz / *stōpaną
"high step, projecting bank, to step"
↓
Old Dutch (c. 9th c.)
*stōp
"step, flight of steps, threshold"
↓
Middle Dutch (c. 13th c.)
stoepe / stoep
"raised sidewalk, steps before a house"
↓
Early Modern Dutch (c. 17th c.)
stoep
"raised platform or entry steps
at front of residence"
↓
American English (c. 1755)
stoop
"exterior front steps, entrance porch"

Note: English independently possessed a native Germanic verb 'stoop' (Old English 'stūpian', to bend or bow down) from PIE *steup- ('to push, stick out'). The architectural noun meaning 'porch/steps' was directly borrowed in colonial New York from Dutch 'stoep', reinforcing the homograph in American English.
