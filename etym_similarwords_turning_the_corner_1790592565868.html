• recovering: Similar because it denotes return toward health or normality after illness or setback; differs because it describes the gradual process itself without foregrounding the decisive inflection point that turning the corner emphasizes.
  • rallying: Similar because it means gathering strength after weakness, especially in medicine and markets; differs because it suggests a spirited fight-back and can be temporary, while turning the corner implies a more structural change of direction.
  • bouncing back: Similar because it conveys resilience and return to form after adversity; differs because it is more colloquial and abrupt, suggesting elasticity, whereas turning the corner is more gradual and narrative.
  • turning point: Similar because it also marks a decisive change of trajectory; differs because it is a noun naming the moment itself and is neutral about direction, while turning the corner is verbal, progressive, and inherently positive.
  • getting over the hump: Similar because it images passing the hardest part of a task or illness; differs because hump stresses exertion over an obstacle, while corner stresses emergence from blindness into visibility.
  • seeing the light at the end of the tunnel: Similar because it expresses hope after prolonged darkness or difficulty; differs because it is purely perceptual and anticipatory, while turning the corner claims the change is already underway.
