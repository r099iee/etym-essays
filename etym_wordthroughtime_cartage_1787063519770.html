- 1610s: "the act of carting goods" - born as the plain "-age" noun beside postage, towage, and wharfage in the commercial vocabulary of early modern trade.
- 1731: "the charge for carting" - the fee sense takes root in ledgers, customs, and freight documents; the word becomes a standard line-item of trade.
- 18th–19th centuries: peak utility - "cartage and warehousing" in dock and railway records; the essential road-leg connecting ship and railhead to warehouse and shop.
- 20th century: with motorisation the everyday act becomes "haulage"; cartage narrows to logistics contracts, moving-and-storage services, and legal definitions.
- Present: a slightly antique specialist term - stable in meaning (act + fee) for 400 years, its horse-and-cart image now giving it period flavour.
