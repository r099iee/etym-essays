Note: The historical root 'slave' represents one of the most famous ethnonymic semantic shifts in European history. In the early medieval period, the vast numbers of Slavic captives captured by Byzantine, Frankish, and Venetian traders transformed the tribal ethnonym *Slověninъ into the common noun for a human held in bondage, eventually displacing the classical Latin term 'servus' (which had weakened into 'serf'). The verb 'enslave' was coined in the 17th century by prefixing the productive causative morpheme 'en-' (from Latin 'in-').
