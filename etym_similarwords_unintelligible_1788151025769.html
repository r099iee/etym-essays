• Incomprehensible: similar because both mean "cannot be understood"; differs because incomprehensible is the grander, more philosophical word (beyond comprehension, vast or profound), while unintelligible is the precise engineering term for a failed signal or text.
  • Indecipherable: similar because both describe something that will not yield its meaning; differs because indecipherable focuses on codes, scripts, and handwriting — a puzzle of signs — while unintelligible covers speech and thought too.
  • Illegible: similar because both describe unreadable writing; differs because illegible is strictly physical (the letters cannot be made out), whereas unintelligible may survive perfect letters and still defeat the reader.
  • Inscrutable: similar because both describe what cannot be penetrated; differs because inscrutable is reserved for faces, intentions, and mysteries — a person's motives — and carries wonder where unintelligible carries factual failure.
  • Incoherent: similar because both describe content that fails to make sense; differs because incoherent means internally disconnected (thoughts that do not join), while the words can each be clear while the whole is unintelligible.
  • Meaningless: similar because both deny sense; differs because meaningless declares that no meaning exists, while unintelligible leaves open that meaning exists but is inaccessible.
  • Nonsensical: similar because both describe statements that cannot be accepted as sense; differs because nonsensical is livelier and more contemptuous — absurd, silly — where unintelligible is neutral.
  • Opaque: similar because both describe prose that will not let meaning through; differs because opaque is a style metaphor — dense, obscure, resistant — while unintelligible describes the outcome, complete failure.
  • Garbled: similar because both describe corrupted messages; differs because garbled implies damage in transmission (static, mangling), while unintelligible may describe an original, unsullied obscurity.
  • Unfathomable: similar because both describe what the mind cannot reach; differs because unfathomable suggests depth — an ocean without bottom — while unintelligible suggests disorder or foreignness.
