• Traitorous: Similar because both mean guilty of betrayal; differs because "traitorous" is specifically about treason or betrayal of one's country, allegiance, or oath, with stronger legal and political colour, while "treacherous" is broader (any betrayal of trust).
  • Perfidious: Similar because both mean faithless and deceitful; differs because "perfidious" is a high literary-register term emphasising deliberate violation of faith or promise, while "treacherous" is everyday and also covers physical danger.
  • Faithless: Similar because both mean without loyalty or trustworthiness; differs because "faithless" is milder and also means "without religious faith," lacking the active deception of "treacherous."
  • Disloyal: Similar because both mean unfaithful to a commitment; differs because "disloyal" implies mere lack of loyalty, while "treacherous" implies active betrayal and deceit.
  • Deceitful: Similar because both involve deception; differs because "deceitful" describes a habit of lying and misleading generally, while "treacherous" specifically implies betraying established trust.
  • Deceptive: Similar because both can describe things that mislead; differs because "deceptive" needs no moral agent — appearances are deceptive — while "treacherous" implies faith or safety being betrayed.
  • Insidious: Similar because both describe hidden, harmful deception; differs because "insidious" emphasises the gradual, stealthy approach of harm, while "treacherous" emphasises the breaking of trust or false safety.
  • False: Similar because both describe a person who betrays; differs because "false" is very broad (untrue, artificial, fake) and milder, while "treacherous" is specific and intense.
  • Unreliable: Similar because both mean not to be depended on; differs because "unreliable" is far milder and often used of things (an unreliable car), without the moral betrayal or hidden danger of "treacherous."
  • Dangerous: Similar because both describe something that can harm; differs because "dangerous" merely states hazard, while "treacherous" adds that the hazard is hidden or deceptive.
  • Double-dealing: Similar because both mean acting deceptively while appearing loyal; differs because "double-dealing" is narrower (secretly working both sides) and mostly personal, while "treacherous" also covers places and conditions.
