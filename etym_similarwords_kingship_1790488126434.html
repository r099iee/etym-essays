• monarchy: Similar because it denotes rule by a king or queen; differs because monarchy names the system, while kingship names the office, aura, and ideal.
  • kingship / kingliness: Similar because both mean royal dignity; differs because kingliness is personal bearing, while kingship is institutional and moral office.
  • sovereignty: Similar because it means supreme authority; differs because sovereignty is legal and can be popular, while kingship is personal and hereditary.
  • reign: Similar because it means the period and act of ruling; differs because reign stresses time and exercise, while kingship stresses status and duty.
  • throne: Similar because it stands for royal power; differs because throne is metonymic furniture, while kingship is abstract office.
  • crown: Similar because it symbolises royal authority; differs because crown is the emblem and revenues, while kingship is the condition and art.
  • royalty: Similar because it means royal persons and quality; differs because royalty is collective or qualitative, while kingship is the specific male office.
  • queenship: Similar because it is the female counterpart office; differs because queenship belongs to a queen regnant or consort, kingship to a king.
  • princedom / principality: Similar because they are lesser monarchical offices; differs because prince is lower rank, king supreme.
  • lordship: Similar because it shares the -ship suffix for dominion; differs because lordship is feudal and manifold, kingship uniquely royal.
  • empire / emperorship: Similar because they are supreme rule; differs because empire is multi-national and imperial, kingship national and paternal.
  • chieftainship: Similar because it is tribal leadership; differs because chieftainship rests on kin and prowess, kingship on consecration and law.
