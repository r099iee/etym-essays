• mein: Identical in meaning — mein is an older variant spelling of mien; differs only in spelling, with mien standard and mein now archaic or rare.
  • countenance: Similar because both describe the face's expressive appearance; differs because countenance centres on the face and its current expression of moral state, while mien encompasses the whole figure, posture, and air.
  • demeanor: Similar because both describe a person's outward bearing; differs because demeanor covers overall conduct and behaviour, while mien focuses on the look, expression, and carriage that reveal character.
  • bearing: Similar because both describe how a person carries themselves; differs because bearing emphasises physical posture, carriage, and dignity, while mien is broader, including the face and mood.
  • carriage: Similar because both describe bodily presence; differs because carriage is specifically about posture and the way one moves, while mien includes facial expression.
  • air: Similar because both describe a general aura a person gives off; differs because air is more diffuse and vague, while mien is more specific and literary, tied to look and bearing.
  • presence: Similar because both describe the impression a person makes; differs because presence suggests commanding charisma or personal impact, while mien is quieter, about visible bearing.
  • appearance: Similar because both describe how a person looks; differs because appearance is neutral and factual, while mien implies expressive, character-revealing bearing.
  • aspect: Similar because both can denote look or appearance; differs because aspect is more formal and can apply to things, while mien applies to a person's bearing.
  • look: Similar because both denote appearance or expression; differs because look is informal and common, while mien is elevated and literary.
  • deportment: Similar because both concern how one carries oneself; differs because deportment stresses learned, proper behaviour, while mien is the natural readable bearing.
  • manner: Similar because both describe how a person presents themselves; differs because manner covers behaviour and speech as well, while mien is specifically the visible look and carriage.
  • expression: Similar because both relate to the face; differs because expression is the specific current look on the face, while mien is the habitual overall bearing.
  • attitude: Similar because both describe how a person is disposed or posed; differs because attitude today suggests a mental stance or opinion, while mien stays physical and visible.
