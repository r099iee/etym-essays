• Cavity: Similar because both name an empty space inside something solid; differs because cavity is clinical and small (teeth, walls, geology), while hollow is broader, warmer, and often a landscape.
  • Depression: Similar because both describe ground that sinks inward; differs because depression is technical and measured (meteorology, economics, terrain), while hollow is homely and visual.
  • Dip: Similar because both are small downward curves in land or surface; differs because a dip is slight and passing, while a hollow can hold a copse, a cottage, or an echo.
  • Pit: Similar because both are holes dropping below the surface; differs because a pit is steep-sided and often dug (sawpit, coalpit), while a hollow is gently cupped and often natural.
  • Hole: Similar because both are empty openings, Tolkien's own cousin-word; differs because a hole pierces through or goes deep, while a hollow cups and shelters.
  • Valley: Similar because both are low ground between highs; differs because a valley is long and drained by water, while a hollow is compact, roundish, and nest-like.
  • Dell: Similar because both are small sheltered wooded dips; differs because dell iselfish and poetic, while hollow is everyday and covers trees, teeth, and promises too.
  • Empty: Similar because hollow things are empty within; differs because empty is the general state of holding nothing, while hollow adds the cupped shape and the echo it makes.
  • Vain: Similar because hollow promises and vain promises both fail; differs because vain stresses futility and pride, while hollow stresses the missing inside, the sounding board with nothing behind it.
