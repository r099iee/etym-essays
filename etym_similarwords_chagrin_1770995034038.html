• mortification: Similar because both name acute distress caused by humiliation; differs because mortification is more intense, suggesting pride "killed" by shame (with a religious undertone), while chagrin is the sharper chafing irritation of a blow to one's self-esteem.
  • embarrassment: Similar because both name discomfort at being exposed; differs because embarrassment is milder and social, an awkward feeling among others, while chagrin involves sharper disappointment and can sting even in private.
  • vexation: Similar because both name annoyance and distress; differs because vexation is irritation at external annoyances without any humiliation, while chagrin follows a failure or slight to one's pride.
  • humiliation: Similar because both name the pain of being brought low; differs because humiliation emphasizes a public lowering of dignity imposed by others, while chagrin emphasizes the internal, self-directed distress at one's own mistake.
  • shame: Similar because both name painful self-consciousness at one's failing; differs because shame is a deeper moral sense of personal worthlessness, while chagrin is a sharper, lighter smart of wounded pride.
  • discomfiture: Similar because both name the awkwardness of being thwarted; differs because discomfiture is broader (confusion, defeat, unease), while chagrin is specifically the irritation of disappointed self-regard.
  • dismay: Similar because both name distress at an unwelcome result; differs because dismay is a shock of discouragement, while chagrin carries the sting of personal failure.
  • annoyance: Similar because both name irritation; differs because annoyance is mild and external, while chagrin is sharper and self-directed.
  • frustration: Similar because both name the feeling of being thwarted; differs because frustration is about blocked goals generally, while chagrin is about wounded pride after failure.
  • displeasure: Similar because both name displeasure; differs because displeasure is often directed outward at others, while chagrin is turned inward at oneself.
  • regret: Similar because both follow a misstep; differs because regret focuses on wishing an action undone, while chagrin focuses on the present sting of embarrassment or failure.
