• Historically: Similar because both situate things in development over time; differs because historically is the broad everyday word, while diachronically is the technical term marking the through-time axis against the synchronic one.
  • Longitudinally: Similar because both follow subjects across successive observations; differs because longitudinally belongs to surveys and studies over time, while diachronically belongs to language and cultural theory.
  • Developmentally: Similar because both trace growth from earlier to later stages; differs because developmentally centres maturation of organisms or minds, while diachronically centres historical succession of forms.
  • Evolutionarily: Similar because both explain present traits by past transformations; differs because evolutionarily invokes biological descent and selection, while diachronically invokes documented linguistic or cultural change.
  • Chronologically: Similar because both order events along time; differs because chronologically stresses sequence of dates, while diachronically stresses comparison of stages and mechanisms of change.
  • Philologically: Similar because philology classically works through textual history; differs because philologically names the discipline of texts and languages, while diachronically names only the temporal direction of inquiry.
  • Etiologically: Similar because both seek origins and causes in the past; differs because etiologically pursues causation of conditions, while diachronically describes the method of staging periods in series.
  • Retrospectively: Similar because both look backward from the present; differs because retrospectively is a backward glance of judgment, while diachronically is a forward march through stages in their order.
