ROOT CHAIN 1 — the base element mal- "bad": PIE *mel- "bad, wrong, false" (reconstructed; Pokorny *mel-; the prehistory of Latin malus is debated) → Latin malus "bad, evil, wicked, wrong" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE; Plautus and Ennius) → Latin malīgnus "ill-natured, evil-natured, spiteful" (attested from the 1st c. BCE; formed from malus + the suffix -gnus "born"; first attested in Cicero and Lucretius) → Anglo-Norman / Old French maligne "wicked, ill-natured" (12th c.) → Middle English malign(e), maligne "evil, baleful" (adjective, 14th c., borrowed from Old French) → English MALIGN (verb "to speak evil of, defame", 17th c.; the verb develops out of the adjective) ROOT CHAIN 2 — the suffix -gnus "born": PIE *ǵenh₁- "to beget, give birth, produce" (reconstructed; Pokorny *gen-, Beekes *ǵenh₁-) → Latin gignere, genitus "to beget, to bear, to produce" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE) → Latin suffix -gnus "born, of such a kind" (as in benignus "well-born, kind" and malignus "ill-born, evil-natured"; the suffix derives from the participle of gignere and survives in classical Latin in only a handful of adjectives) → (the suffix combines with malus to form malīgnus — see Chain 1) → Old French maligne "wicked" (12th c.) → Middle English malign(e) "evil, baleful" (adjective, 14th c.) → English MALIGN (verb, 17th c.) Note (debated): The reconstruction *mel- for the ancestor of Latin malus is traditional (Pokorny) but debated; some scholars treat the prehistory of malus as uncertain. Greek mélas "black, dark" is a colour-word sometimes brought into the family but is generally kept separate. The derivation of -gnus from *ǵenh₁- "to beget" is standard.
