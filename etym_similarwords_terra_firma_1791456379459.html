• Dry land: land as opposed to water; similar core sense, but plainer and more geographical, without the classical or humorous colouring.
  • Solid ground: firm surface underfoot; very close in meaning, but more literal and less bookish than the Latin phrase.
  • Mainland: the principal land mass as opposed to islands; similar in the territorial sense, but specifically geographical rather than a general term for firm earth.
  • Firm ground: a direct English rendering of the Latin; identical meaning, but it lacks the phrase’s learned or wry tone.
  • Land: the general term; broader and neutral, whereas terra firma emphasises stability and relief.
  • Ground: the surface beneath the feet; similar, but ground can include any surface, while terra firma implies solid, dry earth.
  • Earth: the planet or its soil; similar in the literal sense, but far wider and without the contrast with sea or air.
  • Shore: the land at the water’s edge; similar in maritime contexts, but it names the boundary rather than the solid interior.
  • Footing: a secure place to stand, often figurative; similar in the metaphor of stability, but it does not denote land itself.
  • Bedrock: solid rock beneath the soil; similar in implying firmness, but geological and technical rather than a general phrase for land.
