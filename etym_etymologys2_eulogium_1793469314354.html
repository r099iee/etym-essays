Note: The word eulogium is an etymological hybrid resulting from lexical cross-contamination. Ancient Greek eulogía (from eu- 'well' + logos 'word') was borrowed into Christian Latin as a feminine noun denoting spiritual blessings. However, during the Renaissance and late antiquity, Latin scholars formally conflated it with an entirely separate Latin word: ēlogium (a short tombstone inscription, legal clause, or epitaph, perhaps derived from Greek elegeîon or Latin ex- + legere). This contamination produced the neuter noun eulogium, embedding both the meaning of spoken praise and the permanence of an engraved memorial monument.
