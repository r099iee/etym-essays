Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*ǵʰeh₂i- / *ǵʰēy- (*ghei-)
"to yawn, gape, open wide"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*gaisipōną / *geispōną
"to yawn, gape with open mouth"
(Iterative/intensive elaboration of *gīnaną "to gape")
↓
Old Norse (c. 900 CE)
geispa
"to yawn, gape, pant"
↓
Middle English (Danelaw loan) (c. 1350 CE)
gaspen / gaispen
"to open mouth wide, pant, gasp for breath"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550s)
gasp (past: gasped)
"to inhale sharply from shock; catch breath"
↓
Modern English (present)
GASPED
"inhaled sharply from shock; spoke breathlessly"
