• Push: Similar because a thrust is a forcible push; differs because "push" is the general verb of pressure, while "thrust" adds suddenness, direction and a pointed force.
  • Shove: Similar because both are rough pushes; differs because a shove is blunter and often sideways or from the body, while a thrust is straight ahead and point-first.
  • Propel: Similar because both drive something forward; differs because propel is technical and indifferent to suddenness, while thrust is sudden, manual or military.
  • Drive: Similar because both force something forward; differs because drive implies sustained control (drive a nail, drive cattle), while a thrust is one sharp event.
  • Ram / Jam: Similar because both force something in with violence; differs because ram is the repeated battering of a head, and jam is wedging fast, while a thrust is a single directed push.
  • Press: Similar because a thrust presses; differs because press is steady, continuous pressure, while thrust is sudden and directional.
  • Stab / Lunge: Similar because all are point-first attacks; differs because stab is the blow itself (weapon into flesh) and lunge the body's forward spring, while "thrust" is the push that carries the point — the verb of fencing.
  • Poke / Prod: Similar because all push at something with a point; differs because poke and prod are light, repeated, even playful, while a thrust is forceful, serious and committed.
  • Jab: Similar because both are short sharp pushes or blows; differs because a jab is quick and often boxing-specific, while a thrust is deeper, stronger and directional.
  • Assault / Onslaught / Offensive: Similar because a military thrust is an offensive attack; differs because these name the whole operation, while a thrust is specifically the deep, concentrated push within it.
  • Attack: Similar because a thrust can be an attack; differs because attack is the general word of aggression, while thrust keeps the image of the pointed driving force.
  • Gist / Core / Essence: Similar because "the thrust of X" means its essential direction or point; differs because gist, core and essence name the content of the matter, while "thrust" names the forward pressure of its main argument — the direction, not the substance.
  • Purport: Similar because both denote the main import of what is said; differs because purport is formal and static (what is claimed), while thrust is dynamic (where it is driving).
  • Momentum / Impetus: Similar because both are forward-driving forces; differs because momentum is the physical quantity of motion and impetus the force that sets it going, while a thrust is the single push that supplies either.
