Derivation chain for bough (plural boughs): 1. PIE *bheghu-s / *bhaghu-s 'arm, shoulder' (well attested: Sanskrit bahu 'arm, foreleg, side', Avestan bazu 'arm', Greek pekhys 'forearm, elbow, arm', Armenian bazuk 'arm', Tocharian A poke 'arm'; the precise original meaning is debated - 'arm' versus 'forearm/elbow'). 2. Proto-Germanic *bogaz 'arm, shoulder; a tree-limb, bough' (reconstructed from Old English bog, Old Saxon bog, Old High German buog 'shoulder', Old Norse bogr 'shoulder of an animal; the bow of a ship'; the tree-limb sense is already present in the Germanic ancestor, transferred from the body). 3. Old English bog / boh (8th-11th c.) 'shoulder, arm; a bough, branch of a tree' (also boga 'a bough, a bent thing, a bow'; the arm-shoulder sense and the tree-limb sense coexist in Old English). 4. Middle English bogh / bough / bow (12th-15th c.) 'a bough, a branch, a large limb of a tree' (the spelling bough, with the digraph -gh, becomes fixed from the 13th c.; the body sense drops away in the later Middle English period). 5. Modern English bough, plural boughs (16th c. onward; the plural is formed by the regular -s plural suffix; bough is homophonous with bow). Note on the debate: the original referent of the PIE root is disputed - 'arm' and 'elbow/forearm' both have supporters, and the Sanskrit and Greek evidence points in slightly different directions. The transference from the body to the tree ('the arm of a tree') is complete already in Proto-Germanic and is not contested. The homophone bow 'to bend; a bent thing' is unrelated: it descends from Old English bugan 'to bend', a different root, though the two have influenced each other's spelling over the centuries.
