• Xiamen: Similar because both refer to the exact same geographical port city in southeastern Fujian, China; differs because Xiamen is the standard Modern Pinyin transliteration based on Standard Mandarin pronunciation (Xiàmén), whereas Amoy is the historical postal and dialectal romanization based on the local Zhangzhou/Quanzhou Hokkien (Southern Min) pronunciation (Ē-mn̂g).
  • Treaty Port: Similar because Amoy was one of the prime historical examples of such an entity; differs because treaty port is a generic institutional term for any of the Chinese, Japanese, or Korean ports opened to foreign foreign trade under unequal treaties in the 19th century.
  • Canton (Guangzhou): Similar because both were major southern Chinese maritime trading ports prominent in 19th-century British commerce and tea trade; differs because Canton is located in Guangdong Province along the Pearl River, with Cantonese as its language, whereas Amoy is located in Fujian Province, with Hokkien as its language.
  • Foochow (Fuzhou): Similar because both are major coastal cities in Fujian Province opened as treaty ports under the 1842 Treaty of Nanking; differs because Foochow is the provincial capital located further north on the Min River, speaking Eastern Min, while Amoy is in the south.
  • Hokkien: Similar because Amoy was the cultural and linguistic capital of the Hokkien speech community; differs because Hokkien designates the Southern Min language group and its people, whereas Amoy designates the specific island and port city itself.
  • Macau: Similar because both were historic southern Chinese coastal trading enclaves; differs because Macau was a Portuguese-administered territory dating from the 16th century, whereas Amoy was a Qing imperial port later opened as a treaty port.
  • Formosa (Taiwan): Similar because Amoy sits directly across the Taiwan Strait and maintains deep ancestral and linguistic ties with Taiwan; differs because Formosa is the historical Western name for the island of Taiwan itself.
