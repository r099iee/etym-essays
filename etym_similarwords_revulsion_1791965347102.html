• disgust: Similar because it names strong revulsion at something offensive or unclean; differs because disgust is the broader, more bodily emotion and can be mild or comic, while revulsion implies a more violent, whole-body recoil.
  • repugnance: Similar in denoting strong antipathy; differs in being more formal and more often a settled attitude than a sudden physical reaction.
  • repulsion: Similar because both are built on the image of being driven back; differs because repulsion is also a physical force and can be drier or more intellectual, whereas revulsion is almost always emotional.
  • loathing: Similar in intensity; differs because loathing is a sustained hatred of the object, while revulsion is the recoil itself, often momentary.
  • abhorrence: Similar as strong moral detestation; differs in being more judicial and principled, less visceral.
  • aversion: Similar as a turning away; differs in being milder and more general, often a settled preference against something.
  • detestation: Similar in intensity; differs in emphasising active hatred rather than physical shrinking.
  • horror: Similar in the shudder it describes; differs because horror is aroused by the dreadful or terrifying, revulsion by the disgusting or morally foul.
  • nausea: Similar in the bodily sensation; differs as a literal symptom or a weaker figurative distaste, without the moral charge.
  • odium: Similar as intense dislike; differs as public hatred directed at a person, not a physical recoil.
