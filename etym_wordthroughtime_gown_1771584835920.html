- [14th century: the loose outer robe] - 'Gown' enters English as a loose outer garment worn over other clothes; connotation is generic and practical.
- [16th-19th century: the professional and ceremonial dress] - The word attaches to the dress of scholars, judges, and clerics - 'town and gown' - and to formal women's attire; connotation becomes professional and ceremonial, the garment of learning, law, and occasion.
- [Contemporary sense] - Either a woman's long formal dress (evening gown, ball gown) or a loose robe of office and convenience (academic gown, hospital gown); connotation is formal and ceremonial, though the loose, lived-in 'worn-looking gown' of private comfort keeps a softer, domestic life as well - the body's release from the public self.
