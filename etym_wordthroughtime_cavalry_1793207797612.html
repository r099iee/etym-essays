- Antiquity — chariots and companions: Egyptians and Assyrians fight from chariots, Alexander’s Companions invent true shock cavalry at Gaugamela.
- Middle Ages — the knight: armoured horsemen own the battlefield, and cavalry’s cousin chivalry turns horse-power into a moral code.
- 16th–17th c. — gunpowder’s answer: pistols and sabres replace the lance; Gustavus Adolphus and Cromwell’s Ironsides make cavalry the battle-winner again.
- Napoleonic — the golden age: Murat’s massed charges shake Europe, and the hussar becomes the romantic hero of a continent.
- 1794 — the volunteers: England mounts its yeomanry cavalry against France abroad and radicals at home — Cobbett’s hated horsemen.
- 1914–42 — the long dismount: machine guns, wire, and tanks end the horse; titles march on into armour, helicopters, and Hollywood rescue-music.
