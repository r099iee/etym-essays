• Pagan: Similar because both denote non-Abrahamic, polytheistic believers; differs because pagan derives from Latin paganus (rustic villager) and often carries broader classical (Greco-Roman) or romanticized nature-worship connotations, whereas heathen is of native Germanic origin and historically carried harsher, fiercer Germanic connotations.
  • Infidel: Similar because both describe non-believers in an orthodox faith; differs because infidel (from Latin infidelis, unfaithful) specifically denotes an active rejecter or enemy of Christianity or Islam (a person without faith), carrying strong theological hostility and holy-war connotations.
  • Gentile: Similar because both translate biblical non-believers; differs because gentile (from Latin gens / gentilis) is a technical biblical term denoting anyone who is not Jewish (or in Mormon theology, not Latter-day Saint), completely lacking the derogatory or uncivilized overtones of heathen.
  • Idolater: Similar because both refer to non-monotheistic worshippers; differs because idolater focuses specifically and narrowly on the religious practice of bowing down before physical carved idols or statues.
  • Barbarian: Similar because both describe people perceived as uncivilized or outside the dominant culture; differs because barbarian emphasizes lack of advanced civilization, language incomprehensibility (originally Greek barbaros), or physical brutality, whereas heathen centers primarily on religious and moral otherness.
  • Philistine: Similar because both are used as insults for unrefined people; differs because a philistine specifically attacks someone who is smugly hostile or indifferent to high art, literature, aesthetics, and intellectual culture.
  • Heretic: Similar because both are religious outsiders; differs fundamentally because a heretic is an baptized insider who holds doctrinal opinions contrary to orthodox dogma, whereas a heathen stands completely outside the faith altogether.
