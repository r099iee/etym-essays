• Chubby: Similar in describing soft, rounded fullness; differs because chubby is cutesy and usually reserved for babies, children or round cheeks, while plumpish is a more neutral, grown-up hedging word.
  • Stout: Similar in denoting a solid, full figure; differs because stout suggests a heavier, often middle-aged and durable bulk, while plumpish stays at the gentler threshold of 'somewhat plump'.
  • Overweight: Similar in measuring a body beyond a normal weight; differs because overweight is a clinical, modern word that judges against medical norms, while plumpish is affectionate and aesthetic, with no medical charge.
  • Portly: Similar in describing a full-bodied figure; differs because portly adds a dignified, stately, often male bulk - the prosperous gentleman - while plumpish is lighter and gender-neutral in its softness.
  • Fat: Similar in denoting bodily fullness; differs because fat is blunt, often censorious, and suggests sloppiness or excess, while plumpish is hedged, kindly, and firmly on the pleasant side of the boundary.
  • Full: Similar in suggesting a rounded, well-fed form ("full-figured"); differs because full is broader, used of faces, lips, flavours, and sounds, while plumpish is specifically about pleasing bodily roundness.
  • Round: Similar in evoking a soft, curved bodily shape; differs because round is neutral and geometric, while plumpish adds the evaluative note of comfortable, attractive fullness.
  • Well-fed: Similar in implying a body nourished into comfortable fullness; differs because well-fed stresses the cause (ample food and prosperity) rather than the pleasing appearance, and can sound blunter or more practical.
