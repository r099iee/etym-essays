MULTIPLE ROOT WORDS: WEIR + WOOD

Chain 1: WEIR

PIE *wer-
"to cover, protect, ward off, defend"
(reconstructed; see debate note below)

↓

Proto-Germanic *warjaną / *werjaną
"to defend, ward off, protect, enclose"
(reconstructed)

↓

Old English wer
"weir, dam, fish-fence, enclosure"
(attested in Anglo-Saxon charters; homonym of wer "man")

↓

Middle English were / weir
"dam, fish-trap, barrier across water" (13th-15th c.)

↓

Modern English weir
"river dam, fish-fence, overflow barrier"


Chain 2: WOOD

PIE *widhu-
"tree, wood, forest"
(reconstructed; alternative *weydʰ- also proposed)

↓

Proto-Germanic *widuz
"wood, timber, tree"
(reconstructed)

↓

Old English widu / wudu
"tree, wood, forest" (attested)

↓

Middle English wode / wood
"forest, woodland, timber"

↓

Modern English wood
"timber, forest, trees"


Compound synthesis (George R.R. Martin, 1996)
weir + wood

↓

Modern English WEIRWOOD
"sacred white tree with red leaves and carved face,
embodying fate, protection, and the old gods"

DEBATE NOTE: The PIE root *wer- is notoriously ambiguous: Pokorny lists several homonymous *wer- roots ("to cover/protect," "to turn/bend," "to notice"). The weir family is assigned to the "cover/protect/ward" set, but the reconstruction is debated; the Old English form wer "dam" is securely attested, and its connection to warian "to guard" is widely accepted. The root of "wood" is likewise reconstructed variously as *widhu- or *weydʰ-.
