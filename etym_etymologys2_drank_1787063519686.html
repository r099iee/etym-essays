PIE (c. 4000 BCE) *dʰreĝ- / *dʰreg- "to drink" (root reconstruction debated) ↓ Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE) *drinkaną "to drink" (class III strong verb) ↓ Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE) *drank "drank" (past tense, ablaut stem) ↓ Old English (8th–11th c.) drincan "to drink" (infinitive) ↓ Old English (8th–11th c.) dranc "drank" (past singular) ↓ Middle English (13th–15th c.) drank / dronk "drank" (past tense) ↓ English (16th c. onwards) DRANK "simple past tense of to drink" Note: the precise Indo-European root behind the Germanic "drink" family is debated; some scholars reconstruct *dʰreg- ("to drink") or link it to *dʰrebʰ-, but Germanic alone attests the form securely, so stages before Proto-Germanic must be treated as conjectural.
