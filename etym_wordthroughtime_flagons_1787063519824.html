- Medieval serving vessel — a large handled jug for ale or wine at feasts and taverns; everyday, communal, plentiful
- 16th–17th c. pewter and silver flagons — tavern ware and church communion vessels; material prestige rises while the word stays ordinary
- 19th c. archaism — historical fiction and the ballad revival use “flagon” for antique colour; the word begins to sound old-fashioned
- 20th c. fantasy (Tolkien) — flagons in ruined halls; romantic, elegiac, epic register
- Modern retail — the large handled glass “flagon” of cheap wine returns the word to shop shelves; casual, slightly humorous
