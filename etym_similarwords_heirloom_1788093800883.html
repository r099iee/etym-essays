• Legacy: Similar because both involve something transmitted from the past to successors; differs because a legacy is usually intangible (reputation, influence, money) and need not be a physical object, while an heirloom is a concrete possession.
  • Inheritance: Similar because both denote what is received through succession; differs because inheritance is the entire body of property received at death and is legally neutral, while an heirloom singles out objects with familial pedigree and sentimental continuity.
  • Bequest: Similar because both are things handed down after death; differs because a bequest is any specific gift made by will, whether or not it has family history, while an heirloom emphasises generational transmission, not the legal instrument.
  • Patrimony: Similar because both evoke inherited family property; differs because patrimony is the collective estate inherited from father or ancestors — often grand and legal, even national — while an heirloom is usually a single intimate object.
  • Keepsake: Similar because both are objects kept for personal, emotional reasons; differs because a keepsake needs no inheritance at all (a souvenir of a trip or romance will do), while an heirloom is defined by descent through generations.
  • Memento: Similar because both are objects treasured as memories; differs because a memento is a private reminder of an event or person and is often inexpensive (a ticket, a medal, a lock of hair), while an heirloom is a valued, often valuable family possession.
  • Relic: Similar because both are survivals from the past that carry reverence; differs because a relic connotes religious or historical sanctity (a fragment of a saint, a relic of a lost era), while an heirloom is intimate and familial.
  • Antique: Similar because both are old and potentially valuable objects; differs because an antique is defined by age and market value (typically over a hundred years and collectible), while an heirloom is defined by family transmission and may be worthless on the open market.
  • Hand-me-down: Similar because both pass from one user or generation to the next; differs because a hand-me-down is casual, often worn clothing or goods passed on without ceremony, whereas an heirloom implies reverence and curated preservation.
  • Family treasure: Similar because both denote prized inherited possessions; differs because this phrase is informal and has no legal backbone, and it names a category rather than the single object that "heirloom" singles out.
  • Birthright: Similar because both involve what belongs to one by right of birth; differs because a birthright is a right or privilege (the Biblical first-born's portion) rather than a specific physical object.
