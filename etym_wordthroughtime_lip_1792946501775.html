- Old English — the mouth's rim: lippa names the fleshy folds, among the most kissed and cursed inches of the body.
- Middle Ages — the rim of things: potters, coopers, and poets lend lip to cups, wounds, and pits; the hollow world grows mouths.
- Early modern — speech itself: on every lip, sealed lips; the organ stands for the words it shapes, from prayer to treason.
- 19th c. — the scold's word: American slang coins none of your lip, and the tender organ becomes the name of backchat.
- 20th c. — technical and tender: surgeons suture lips of wounds, geologists map lips of craters, lovers read lips — one syllable for scalpel, survey, and kiss.
- Today — brink and body together: hikers walk the lip of the hollow while singers watch lips on screens, the oldest rim still edging every sense.
