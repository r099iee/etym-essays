• Copse: a small wood, especially one managed by coppicing; similar in size and scale, but more neutral and more common in American English.
  • Coppice: a wood whose trees are periodically cut for poles and firewood; similar in being small and managed, but technical and productive rather than merely descriptive.
  • Thicket: a dense tangle of shrubs and small trees; similar in density and thorniness, but implies impenetrability rather than a bounded wood.
  • Holt: an old term for a wood or grove, often an animal's refuge; similar in rural flavour, but more archaic and more specific to foxes or otters.
  • Grove: a small group of trees, often open and ornamental; similar in size, but lighter, more formal and less scrubby.
  • Covert: a thicket planted for game; similar in function and often in appearance, but explicitly tied to hunting and estate management.
  • Brake: a clump of bushes, especially brambles; similar in tangled growth, but smaller and less tree-like.
  • Hurst: an archaic word for a wood or wooded hill; similar in rural and toponymic register, but rarer and more place-name-like.
  • Wood: a larger area of trees; similar in kind, but broader in scale and not necessarily small or dense.
  • Woodland: tree-covered land as a habitat or land-use category; similar in meaning, but ecological and general rather than a specific small patch.
  • Plantation: a deliberately planted wood or stand of trees; similar in being managed, but implies deliberate establishment and often a larger scale.
  • Shrubbery: a collection of shrubs, often in a garden; similar in density, but domestic and ornamental rather than rural.
  • Bosquet: a formal grove in a garden or park; similar in being a small wooded area, but French-derived and landscape-garden specific.
  • Clump: a small group of trees; similar in size, but looser and without the implication of undergrowth or enclosure.
