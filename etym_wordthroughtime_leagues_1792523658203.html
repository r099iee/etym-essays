- [Late Antiquity & Gallo-Roman Era (c. 1st–5th c. CE)] — absorbed from Celtic *leuga into Latin as an administrative measure of 1,500 paces — [perception: regarded by Roman administrators as a practical provincial metric of an hour's marching distance]
- [Medieval French & Anglo-Norman Transition (c. 11th–14th c.)] — evolved into Old French lieue and Middle English leuge, standardizing in English common law as three miles — [perception: celebrated in chivalric romances and chronicles as the standard measure of knights' journeys and estate boundaries]
- [Age of Exploration & Maritime Cartography (c. 15th–18th c.)] — elevated into the primary unit of global oceanic navigation, treaty borders (Treaty of Tordesillas), and naval law (the three-mile cannon shot limit) — [perception: viewed as the indispensable global currency of maritime discovery, exploration, and geopolitical sovereignty]
- [19th Century Romantic Literature & Epic Translations] — widely adopted by Indologists and translators to render epic Asian measures (yojanas, parasangs), while celebrated in poetry (Tennyson) and science fiction (Jules Verne) — [perception: acquired an evocative, nostalgic patina of heroic expanse, mythical grandeur, and deep-sea mystery]
- [20th–21st Century Idiomatic & Contemporary Usage] — largely retired from civil surveying in favor of metric units, while flourishing in everyday speech through sports associations (Major League) and idioms ("in a league of one's own," "out of one's league") — [perception: universally recognized as a symbol of elite distinction, categorical superiority, and organized athletic competition]
