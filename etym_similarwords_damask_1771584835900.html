• Brocade: Similar because both are rich, figured, decorative woven fabrics with patterns; differs because brocade's pattern is raised by extra weft threads, often glinting with gold or silver, is thicker and more ornate, and is not reversible, while damask's pattern lies flat in the ground weave and is reversible and more austere.
  • Satin: Similar because both are glossy luxury weaves of silk; differs because satin is a smooth, single-weave silk with no pattern at all — a weave rather than a decorative art — while damask's very point is the figured design woven into the cloth.
  • Silk: Similar because both denote luxurious textiles with an aristocratic register; differs because silk names the material itself (which may be plain or patterned, fine or heavy), while damask names a specific weave technique, classically executed in silk.
  • Tapestry: Similar because both are decorative woven textiles; differs because a tapestry is a picture woven in thick weft and hung as a wall-covering, while damask is a flat dress or furnishing fabric with a reversible, single-colour pattern.
  • Jacquard: Similar because both are figured woven fabrics produced on looms; differs because jacquard is the nineteenth-century mechanical descendant of figured weaving (named for Joseph-Marie Jacquard), usually multi-colour or raised, while damask is the older hand-loom, single-colour, reversible technique.
  • Velvet: Similar because both are rich dress and furnishing fabrics; differs because velvet's richness is a raised pile of cut loops of thread rather than a weave reversal, and velvet is not reversible in the way damask is.
  • Toile: Similar because both are patterned furnishing fabrics of one colour; differs because toile (toile de Jouy) carries a printed, typically pastoral scene on plain cloth, while damask's pattern is woven, never printed.
  • Linen: Similar because damask is also woven in linen for tableware; differs because linen names the flax fibre and any cloth made from it, while damask names the weave pattern regardless of fibre.
