CLAMMY = clam + adjectival suffix -y. Chain:
1. PIE *gley- "to glue, to stick together, to smear; clay, mud" (reconstructed; with variant *glei-) — c. 4000 BCE; cf. Latin glūten "glue", Greek glía "glue".
2. Proto-Germanic *klaimaz "mud, clay, sticky substance" — 1st millennium BCE; the soft, adhesive earth.
3. Old English clām "mud, clay, mortar, plaster" — 7th–11th centuries; alongside the related clǣg "clay" (both from the same root with different vowel grades).
4. Middle English clam "mud, soft sticky earth; any sticky substance" — 12th–15th centuries.
5. clam + -y → clamy / clammie "sticky, adhesive, viscous to the touch" (of earth, of hands) — late 15th century (first recorded c. 1450–1500).
6. Modern English clammy "unpleasantly damp, cold, and sticky" — 16th century onward; the coldness and revulsion enter in the 16th–17th centuries.
7. clammy — Modern English.
Note: the etymological family (clay, glue, glutinous, cleave) is secure; the semantic drift from "sticky like clay" to "cold, damp, clinging" is the word's documented modern path.
