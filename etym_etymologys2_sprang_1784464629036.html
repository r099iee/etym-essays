PIE *sprengʰ- / *spergʰ- "to move quickly, hasten, spring, burst forth" (reconstructed root of sudden rapid motion; both shapes are proposed in the handbooks — see note) → Proto-Germanic *springaną "to spring, jump, burst" (reconstructed) → West Germanic *springan (reconstructed) → Old English springan "to leap, burst forth, fly up" (attested from c. the 8th–9th c.; class III strong verb, past sprāng, pl. sprungon, p.p. sprungen) → Middle English springen "to spring, leap" (attested from the 12th–13th c.) → Modern English spring, with the strong past sprang (this card) and past participle sprung. Debated/nuanced stage: The PIE reconstruction is not settled. Pokorny's dictionary gives a root in the area of *sp(h)er(e)gh- "to move quickly, hasten," and the form *sprengʰ- is often cited, but the full Indo-European cognate set is thin (some look to Greek spérkhesthai "to hurry"); the Germanic verb is securely reconstructible as *springaną regardless of the precise PIE shape. Note that the mechanical noun spring (an elastic coil) is a later English development of the same verb, and the season Spring derives from the "bursting forth" of plants — neither is a stage of the derivation chain itself.
