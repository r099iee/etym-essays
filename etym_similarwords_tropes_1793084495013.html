• Metaphors: Similar because metaphor is the queen of the tropes, turning one thing into another; differs because metaphor names one specific figure (likeness asserted), while tropes is the whole family including irony and metonymy.
  • Figures of speech: Similar because tropes are the central kind of figurative language; differs because the phrase covers both tropes (turns of meaning) and schemes (turns of arrangement), while tropes strictly means the meaning-turners.
  • Images: Similar because the source sentence pairs them — language is made of images or tropes; differs because images appeal to the senses and imagination broadly, while tropes are the classified rhetorical mechanisms of that appeal.
  • Motifs: Similar because both name recurring artistic devices audiences learn to recognise; differs because a motif is a repeated theme or image within a work, while a trope is a stock device shared across works and genres.
  • Clichés: Similar because overused tropes become clichés — the dead metaphors of the source sentence; differs because cliché always condemns staleness, while trope is neutral — a trope well handled delights.
  • Irony: Similar because irony is one of the four master tropes, turning meaning to its opposite; differs because irony is the single device of saying what is not meant, while tropes embrace metaphor, metonymy, and synecdoche besides.
  • Metonymy: Similar because metonymy is a canonical trope, naming the institution by its thing (the crown for the monarchy); differs because it is one member of the family, while tropes name the family itself.
  • Allegories: Similar because both extend figurative meaning beyond the literal; differs because an allegory sustains its second meaning across a whole narrative, while a trope turns meaning locally, word by word.
  • Conventions: Similar because genre tropes function as the shared conventions writers and audiences understand; differs because conventions are broad agreements (sonnet form, happy endings), while tropes are the specific turns and devices within them.
