- Antiquity (c. 3rd c. BCE – 5th c. CE) - Latin *tempus* functions as the fundamental Roman term for a division of time, season, opportune moment, weather condition, and grammatical inflection.
- 9th to 12th Century - Evolves into Old French as *tens* or *tans*; unaccented final syllables weaken, while the double meaning of time and weather becomes deeply rooted in Romance vernacular speech.
- 14th to 16th Century - Renaissance French humanists deliberately reintroduce the silent etymological Latin 'p' into spelling, transforming *tens* into *temps* to display classical heritage.
- 17th to 18th Century - Classical French literature and Enlightenment philosophy (Descartes, Voltaire, Rousseau) establish *temps* as the central metric of rationalist science and subjective existential reflection.
- Late 19th to Early 20th Century - Revolutionized in philosophy by Henri Bergson (*temps* vs *durée*) and in physics by Albert Einstein (*espace-temps*), fundamentally reshaping human understanding of reality.
- 1913 to 1927 - Transfigured into the supreme aesthetic protagonist of world literature through Marcel Proust's *À la recherche du temps perdu* and its crowning conclusion, *Le Temps retrouvé*.
- 21st Century - Retains ubiquitous presence in contemporary French daily life (weather, time, grammar, music) while remaining the ultimate literary symbol of reclaimed human memory.
