Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*(s)kew- / *skeu-
"to cover, conceal, hide; vessel"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*skawwō / *skald-
"punting boat, shallow craft"
↓
Old Dutch (c. 10th c.)
*scouwe
"flat-bottomed punt, ferryboat"
↓
Middle Dutch (c. 13th c.)
schouwe / scouwe
"ferryboat, flat-bottomed barge
propelled by poles"
↓
Early Modern Dutch (c. 17th c.)
schouw
"flat-bottomed canal barge, punt"
↓
American English (c. 1748)
scow
"flat-bottomed, square-ended freight barge"

Note: Dutch 'schouw' is historically connected to Low German 'schouwe' and West Frisian 'skou', denoting pole-propelled shallow craft used across the wetlands of the North Sea coast. The diphthong /ɔu/ in Dutch was naturalized into English as /aʊ/.
