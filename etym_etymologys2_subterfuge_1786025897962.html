SUBTERFUGE = Latin subter "beneath, secretly" + fugere "to flee", via Late Latin subterfugium "an evasion". Two PIE roots converge in the Latin compound.

PIE *(s)upo
"under, below, up from below" - the ancestor of Latin sub (and Greek hypo)

▼

Latin sub
"under, below" - preposition and prefix (Classical Latin)

▼

Latin subter
"beneath, below, secretly" - sub + comparative suffix -ter (Classical Latin)

▼ (compounds with the fleeing-verb branch below)

PIE *bheug-
"to flee, to escape" - the root of Latin fugere (also Greek pheugein "to flee")

▼

Latin fugere
"to flee, to run away, to escape" (1st c. BCE)

▼

Latin subterfugere
"to flee secretly, to evade, to escape by stealth" - subter + fugere (Classical Latin, e.g. in Cicero)

▼

Late Latin subterfugium
"an evasion, a device for escaping" (4th-5th c. CE)

▼

Middle French subterfuge
"an evasion, a trick, a dodge" (15th c., from Late Latin subterfugium)

▼

English subterfuge
"a deceitful device or stratagem used to evade or conceal" (1570s)

▼

SUBTERFUGE
"deceit used in order to achieve one's goal" (the modern English noun on this card)

Note: The root *bheug- "to flee" (source of Greek pheugein) is distinct from the Indo-European root *bheugh- "to bend"; Latin fugere is securely traced to the "flee" root, though the exact reconstruction of the PIE laryngeal/aspiration is debated.
