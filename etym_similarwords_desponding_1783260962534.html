• Despondent: Similar because both denote loss of hope; differs because despondent is the settled modern adjective for a stable, ongoing low state, while desponding keeps the active participle sense of hope in the very process of being surrendered.
  • Dejected: Similar because both are cast down in spirits; differs because dejected pictures a person thrown down by a specific sudden blow and tends to be more momentary, while desponding is a slow, deliberate giving-up.
  • Disheartened: Similar because both lose courage or spirit; differs because disheartened bluntly names the internal loss of heart caused by disappointment, without desponding's solemn, almost contractual flavour of having promised hope away.
  • Despairing: Similar because both involve loss of hope; differs because despairing denotes total, complete, absolute hopelessness — the abandonment of any expectation — whereas desponding is a gloomier, more passive discouragement that may stop short of full despair.
  • Discouraged: Similar because both have reduced confidence or courage; differs because discouraged is the everyday, milder word for a temporary dip in morale caused by obstacles, while desponding is literary and implies a deeper resignation of hope.
  • Dispirited: Similar because both describe low spirits; differs because dispirited suggests cheer or animation drained away by events, whereas desponding suggests the will to hope itself given up.
  • Disconsolate: Similar because both describe profound sadness; differs because disconsolate means unable to be comforted (inconsolable) and is more about grief, while desponding is about abandoning expectation for the future.
  • Forlorn: Similar because both describe a pitiably hopeless state; differs because forlorn suggests loneliness and desolation as well as hopelessness, while desponding is purely the inner surrender of hope.
  • Gloomy: Similar because both describe a dark, pessimistic outlook; differs because gloomy can describe atmospheres and weather as well as persons and is vaguer, while desponding specifically names the relinquishing of hope.
  • Dour: Similar because both describe a heavy, cheerless disposition; differs because dour is more about stern severity and hardness of mood, while desponding is about the interior collapse of hope.
