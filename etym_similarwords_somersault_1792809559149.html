• Flip: Similar because both name a quick overturning of the body in the air; differs because flip is the small, casual, everyday word (a flip off the diving board), while somersault is the full formal revolution, technical and complete.
  • Tumble: Similar because tumblers are the very performers who turn somersaults; differs because tumble stresses falling, rolling confusion (down the stairs, in the dryer), while somersault stresses the controlled aerial wheel landed upright.
  • Cartwheel: Similar because both are playground revolutions of the body through the air; differs because a cartwheel turns sideways like a wheel (hands touching ground, legs splitting), while a somersault turns end-over-end, heels directly over head.
  • Handspring: Similar because both are gymnastic aerial revolutions; differs because a handspring vaults over the hands with arms extended and body arched, while a somersault tucks tight and rotates free of the ground.
  • Loop-the-loop: Similar because both describe a full vertical circle drawn in motion; differs because loop-the-loop belongs to aircraft and rollercoasters (machines inscribing the circle), while somersault belongs to bodies doing it under their own power.
  • Volte-face: Similar because both name a complete reversal of position or policy; differs because volte-face is the French borrowing of diplomacy and fencing (a wheeling about), while the political somersault is the English picture — the minister literally upside down.
  • U-turn: Similar because both picture a course completely reversed; differs because a U-turn is driven on the ground (traffic, policy driven back), while a somersault is thrown in the air — sudden, showy, acrobatic.
  • Capriole: Similar because both are leaps in which the body hangs suspended and kicking; differs because the capriole is the dressage horse’s high buck (all four legs tucked, a kick at the height), while the somersault is the human body’s full rotation.
  • Gambol: Similar because lambs gambolling and children somersaulting share one spirit of leaping play; differs because gambol is any frolicsome springing about, while somersault is the single precise trick — the wheel within the gambol.
