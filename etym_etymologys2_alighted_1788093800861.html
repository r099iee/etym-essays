PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*legʷʰ-
"light in weight, easy"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 100 CE)
*linhtaz
"light (of weight)"
↓
Proto-Germanic
*līhtijaną
"to make light, lessen a load"
↓
Old English (9th c.)
līhtan
"to lighten; to dismount, descend"
↓
Old English (9th–11th c.)
ālīhtan
"to alight, come down from the air"
↓
Middle English (13th–15th c.)
alighten / alyghte
"to dismount; to settle after flight"
↓
Modern English
alighted
"descended and settled; landed"

Note: the "dismount" sense arose because a rider who got down was said to "light" — i.e. lighten — his horse. The homograph adjective alight "on fire" (16th c.) comes from a different root and is not part of this chain.
