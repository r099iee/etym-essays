• Achilles: Similar because he is a leading Achaean hero at Troy; differs because Achilles is supreme, wrathful, semi-divine, Diomedes steadier and more pious.
  • Odysseus: Similar because he is a cunning Achaean counsellor-warrior and night-raid partner; differs because Odysseus is wily and talkative, Diomedes blunt and youthful.
  • Ajax (Aias): Similar because he is a massive Achaean bulwark; differs because Ajax relies on size and endurance, Diomedes on speed, horses, and Athena favour.
  • Agamemnon: Similar because he is an Argive overlord at Troy; differs because Agamemnon is commander and flawed king, Diomedes subordinate exemplary king.
  • Menelaus: Similar because he is an Atreid war leader; differs because Menelaus fights for Helen with middling prowess, Diomedes excels independently.
  • Hector: Similar because he is a noble champion and charioteer; differs because Hector is Trojan defender and humane family man, Diomedes Achaean attacker.
  • Patroclus: Similar because he is a young loyal warrior-companion; differs because Patroclus is Achilles friend and tragic substitute, Diomedes independent king.
  • Nestor: Similar because he is a wise Argive counsellor; differs because Nestor is aged adviser, Diomedes young doer who heeds advice.
  • Sthenelus: Similar because he is Diomedes charioteer-companion; differs because Sthenelus is squire and sidekick, Diomedes principal hero.
  • Glaucus: Similar because he exchanges armour with Diomedes; differs because Glaucus is Lycian ally of Troy, Diomedes his Achaean counterpart.
  • Aeneas: Similar because he duels Diomedes and founds Italy; differs because Aeneas is Trojan survivor and Roman ancestor, Diomedes Greek founder in Italy.
  • Tydeus: Similar because he is Diomedes father and Seven-against-Thebes hero; differs because Tydeus is savage older generation, Diomedes tempered son.
  • Heracles: Similar because he also fights a Diomedes (of Thrace); differs because Heracles is panhellenic strongman, Argive Diomedes mortal king.
  • Palladium-hero: Similar because both Odysseus and Diomedes steal the Palladium; differs as joint epithet, not separate person, stressing cunning mission.
