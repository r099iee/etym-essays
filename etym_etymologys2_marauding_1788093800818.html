Middle French (15th c.)
maraud
"rogue, vagabond" (origin uncertain)

↓

French (17th c.)
marauder
"to prowl, go marauding, plunder"

↓

English (late 17th c.)
maraud (verb)
"to rove in quest of plunder"

↓

English (18th c. – present)
marauding
"roaming in search of plunder (participle/adjective)"

↓

Modern English (present)
MARAUDING
"raiding and looting while roving"

Note: The origin of French maraud is unknown; proposals include a dialectal French word for "tomcat" (possibly echoic) and various Germanic sources, but the OED marks the etymology as uncertain.
