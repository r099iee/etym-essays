- Old English seamestre - female sewer (with feminine -estre); everyday and honourable
- Medieval sempstress / seamer - makers and menders of linen; guild and household workers
- 17th–18th c. needlewoman coined - needle + woman on the model of gentlewoman; polite, encompassing plain and fancy work
- Regency school prize (Ruskin's mother) - best needlewoman as moral-accomplishment crown; evangelical, genteel, didactic
- Victorian distressed needlewoman - Hood's Song of the Shirt, sweated slop-work; pitiable, political, iconic
- Modern garment worker / seamstress - needlewoman retreats to history books and craft guilds; quaint yet respectful
