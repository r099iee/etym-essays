• Clamber: Similar because both mean hauling oneself up using hands and feet; differs because clambering emphasizes effort over obstacles without the panic or competition of a scramble.
  • Climb: Similar because both mean ascending; differs because climbing is the neutral, dignified term for ascending, while scrambling is rapid, disordered, all-fours haste.
  • Crawl: Similar because both mean moving on hands and knees; differs because crawling keeps low to the ground without the upright reaching and urgency of a scramble.
  • Scale: Similar because both mean ascending a steep surface; differs because scaling implies technical, deliberate ascent, while scrambling is improvised and rushed.
  • Scrabble: Similar because both describe frantic, ungraceful movement; differs because scrabbling pictures scratching and clawing ineffectually at a surface, while scrambling makes real progress in a rush.
  • Claw: Similar because both mean using the hands in a raking, grasping effort; differs because clawing is a single repeated scraping action, while scrambling is a whole-body climb or rush.
  • Scuttle: Similar because both mean moving quickly and urgently; differs because scuttling is a short, sidling, furtive motion, while scrambling is a larger, climbing, all-fours effort.
  • Scuffle: Similar because both mean moving or contending in disorder; differs because a scuffle is a confused struggle or fight between people, while a scramble is an urgent climb or competitive jostle.
  • Jostle: Similar because both mean contending in a crowd; differs because jostling is pushing and shoving among rivals without the climbing or ground-covering motion of a scramble.
  • Hasten: Similar because both mean moving in haste; differs because hastening is a purposeful, dignified urgency, while scrambling is disorderly, ungraceful, all-limbs effort.
