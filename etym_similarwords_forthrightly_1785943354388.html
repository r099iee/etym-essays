• FRANKLY: Similar because both mean speaking honestly and openly without evasion; differs because frankly emphasizes openness and freedom of speech (from the medieval "Frank" who paid for free access, hence "free"), while forthrightly emphasizes direct, straight-ahead movement toward the point, with a stronger sense of purposeful candour and moral courage.
  • CANDIDLY: Similar because both mean speaking honestly and plainly; differs because candidly stresses freedom from bias and the honest disclosure of one's true opinion (from Latin candidus "white, pure, sincere"), often implying a willingness to say what others would rather not hear, while forthrightly stresses the direct, unevasive manner of the delivery itself.
  • DIRECTLY: Similar because both mean without deviation or intermediaries; differs because directly is the general adverb of directness, spatial or procedural (go directly, respond directly), and carries no inherent moral weight, while forthrightly specifically implies honesty and candour about something difficult.
  • OPENLY: Similar because both mean without concealment; differs because openly emphasizes the absence of secrecy and hidden motives (openly admitted, openly criticized), while forthrightly emphasizes the direct, straight-talking manner and often adds a tone of resolute honesty.
  • HONESTLY: Similar because both describe truthful, straightforward conduct; differs because honestly is the general adverb of truthfulness and integrity, covering every kind of honesty, while forthrightly is more specific, describing a direct, unevasive, straight-to-the-point manner of speaking or acting.
  • PLAINLY: Similar because both mean speaking without ornament or evasion; differs because plainly emphasizes simplicity and clarity (speak plainly, put it plainly), sometimes with bluntness, while forthrightly emphasizes the straight-ahead honesty and courage of direct address.
  • BLUNTLY: Similar because both mean saying something directly and without softening; differs because bluntly implies abruptness and a disregard for tact or feelings (to speak bluntly is to be abrupt), while forthrightly is the dignified, positive word for candid directness that can still be courteous.
  • STRAIGHTFORWARDLY: Similar because both mean directly and without complication, sharing the "straight" image of forthrightly; differs because straightforwardly is the plainer, more everyday word for simple and clear conduct, while forthrightly carries a stronger, more formal tone of honesty and resolve.
  • UNRESERVEDLY: Similar because both mean without holding back; differs because unreservedly emphasizes giving one's whole opinion without reserve or qualification, while forthrightly emphasizes the direct, straight-ahead manner of the expression.
  • OUTSPOKENLY: Similar because both mean speaking one's mind; differs because outspokenly emphasizes bold, free, often public speech without fear of opinion (an outspoken critic), while forthrightly emphasizes directness and candour in the manner of the speech, whether public or private.
  • UNEQUIVOCALLY: Similar because both mean without ambiguity or evasion; differs because unequivocally emphasizes the absence of doubt or double meaning in what is said, while forthrightly emphasizes the direct, honest manner of the saying itself.
  • EXPLICITLY: Similar because both mean stated clearly and without vagueness; differs because explicitly stresses that something was spelled out in full detail (explicitly stated, explicitly banned), while forthrightly stresses the candid, straight-talking manner of the statement rather than its completeness.
  • POINT-BLANK: Similar because both mean saying something directly and without mincing words; differs because point-blank (from shooting at close range) is emphatic and often confrontational — refusing, denying, telling point-blank — while forthrightly is the calmer, dignified adverb of honest directness.
  • WITHOUT BEATING AROUND THE BUSH: Similar because both describe speech that goes straight to the point; differs because this idiom is informal and vivid, describing the avoidance of preliminaries and prevarication, while forthrightly is formal and positive, describing a virtue of candour rather than merely the absence of hedging.
