• smooth pursuit movements: Similar because both are coordinated, conjugate movements of the two eyes that redirect the line of sight; differs because pursuit is a slow, continuous tracking that keeps the fovea locked onto a moving target, whereas a saccade is an abrupt, ballistic jump to a new point of fixation.
  • vergence movements: Similar because both move the eyes to change what is fixated; differs because vergence rotates the two eyes in opposite directions to fixate near or distant objects and maintain binocular fusion, while saccades shift both eyes in the same direction toward a new target at a similar depth.
  • fixation: Similar because both are stages of the same visual sampling cycle; differs because fixation is the steady pause during which the fovea acquires detail from a stationary target, whereas a saccade is the rapid jump that terminates that pause and repositions the fovea.
  • micro-saccades: Similar because both are saccadic eye movements governed by the same brainstem machinery; differs because micro-saccades are the tiny, largely involuntary fixational saccades made during attempted steady gaze, whereas a full saccade is a larger, often voluntary relocation of gaze between different objects or locations.
  • drift: Similar because both are small eye movements that occur between fixations; differs because drift is a slow, continuous wandering of the eye during attempted fixation, whereas a saccade (even a micro-saccade) is a rapid, jerky, ballistic repositioning.
  • nystagmus: Similar because both are rhythmic or repeated movements of the eyes; differs because nystagmus is an involuntary, often pathological oscillation (typically a slow drift followed by a fast corrective phase), whereas saccades are the normal, purposeful (or fixational) jumps of healthy vision.
  • vestibulo-ocular reflex (VOR): Similar because both stabilise or redirect the visual axis; differs because the VOR is a reflex that moves the eyes opposite to head motion to keep the world steady, while a saccade is a goal-directed movement that shifts gaze to a new target, driven by cortical and collicular circuits rather than by the vestibular system.
  • glance: Similar because both denote a quick redirection of the eyes to take in something; differs because glance is the ordinary, intentional, literary word for a brief look, whereas saccade is the technical, neutral name for the underlying ballistic movement, carrying no implication of attention or intent.
  • jerk / twitch: Similar because both name an abrupt, sudden movement; differs because jerk and twitch are general terms for any rapid muscular motion, whereas saccade is the precisely defined oculomotor event with measurable amplitude and velocity.
  • eye movement (generic): Similar because saccades are a species of eye movement; differs because “eye movement” covers all classes — pursuit, vergence, VOR, drift, nystagmus — whereas saccade names only the ballistic refixation jumps.
