PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*per- + *wehdʰ-
"through, across" + "to go, advance"
↓
Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
vadere
"to go, walk, advance hastily"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
pervadere
"to go through, penetrate, spread through"
↓
English (c. 1650s)
pervade
"to spread throughout, permeate"
↓
English (c. 1670s–1680s)
pervading
"spreading throughout; permeating; present everywhere within a medium"

Note: Formed from the present participle of the verb pervade. The Latin base vadere ('to go, walk') is cognate with English wade. Prefixed with intensive/spatial per- ('through'), it originally meant 'to walk completely through or traverse', which naturally shifted into 'to penetrate and diffuse throughout all parts'.
