• Branches: Similar because both denote woody structural members dividing from the tree's main stem to bear leaves and fruit; differs because *branch* is a universal, general botanical term encompassing any division from huge boughs down to small shoots, whereas *bough* is restricted exclusively to the largest, primary main limbs.
  • Limbs: Similar because both refer to large, prominent structural divisions of a tree trunk; differs because *limb* is an anatomical metaphor also applied to animal appendages and large mechanical extensions, whereas *bough* is specifically arboreal, evocative, and poetic.
  • Twigs: Similar because both are woody parts of a tree's vegetative canopy; differs because *twigs* are the smallest, thinnest terminal offshoots at the very ends of branches that bear leaves and buds, whereas *boughs* are massive, thick, structural beams.
  • Sprigs: Similar because both are offshoots from woody plants; differs because a *sprig* is a tiny shoot, twig, or spray of leaves or flowers often plucked for decoration or culinary garnish, standing at the opposite pole of scale from a heavy *bough*.
  • Shoots: Similar because both are vegetative growth forms arising from a plant body; differs because a *shoot* is a young, flexible, newly emerged stem or sprout, whereas a *bough* is an ancient, thick, lignified, load-bearing wooden limb.
  • Stems: Similar because both are vegetative axes supporting leaves and reproductive structures; differs because *stem* refers to the central slender stalk of an herbaceous plant or small branch, lacking the massive girth and woody mass of an arboreal *bough*.
  • Offshoots: Similar because both represent secondary structures radiating from a central body; differs because *offshoot* is heavily abstract and figurative (referring to subsidiaries, lateral developments, or doctrinal branches) rather than a physical woody limb.
