Chain for INHABITANTS (single root word, from Latin inhabitāns): 1. PIE *ghabh- / *gʰebʰ- — reconstructed root meaning "to give, to take, to grasp"; the root behind Latin habēre "to have/hold" and, distantly, Germanic give/gift; exact reconstruction debated (see note), attested indirectly across the family. 2. Proto-Italic / Latin habēre — "to have, hold, possess"; attested throughout Latin from the earliest records (6th–5th century BC in the Duenos inscription; classical from 1st century BC). 3. Latin habitāre — "to dwell, to inhabit, to reside" — a frequentative built on habēre (hab- + frequentative -itāre): literally "to keep holding/occupy" a place; attested in Classical Latin. 4. Latin inhabitāre — "to dwell in, to inhabit" (in- "in" + habitāre); attested in Latin from the classical/post-classical period. 5. Latin inhabitāns, inhabitantis — present participle of inhabitāre, meaning "dwelling in," and as a noun "one who dwells in"; attested in post-classical Latin. 6. Old French inhabitant / inhabitans — borrowed from the Latin participle with the sense "inhabitant, resident"; attested in Old French. 7. Middle English inhabitaunt / inhabitant — borrowed from Anglo-French/Old French; recorded in English from the 15th century. 8. English inhabitant, plural INHABITANTS — "persons who live in a place"; the note's rootWord. Debated/nuanced note: The PIE reconstruction *ghabh- / *gʰebʰ- is traditional but not universally accepted; some scholars prefer to link Latin habēre to a different root or to treat the deeper etymology as uncertain. The Latin-to-English chain, however, is fully documented and secure from habēre onward.
