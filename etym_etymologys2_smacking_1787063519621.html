Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*(s)magʰ- / *(s)mak-
"to taste, savour, make a sound of tasting"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*smakkijaną / *smakkuz
"to taste, perceive flavor" / "taste, scent"
↓
Old English / Middle Low German (c. 10th-13th c.)
smæccan / smacken
"to taste; to strike with a flat noise, kiss noisily"
(Synthesis of OE taste-root and MLG onomatopoeic kinetic verb)
↓
Middle English (c. 1380)
smacken
"to taste of; to strike sharply, produce a loud slap"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1570)
smack
"to slap, hit sharply, kiss with noise, carry a trace of"
↓
Modern English (c. 1750)
smacking
"striking with sharp flat impact; tasting; vigorous, brisk"
Note: Exemplifies lexical convergence between an ancient PIE taste root and a Low German onomatopoeic kinetic root.
