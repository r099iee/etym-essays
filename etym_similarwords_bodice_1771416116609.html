• Corset: Similar because both are fitted torso garments; differs because a corset is an undergarment of stiffened cloth or boning worn beneath the dress to mould the figure, while a bodice is the visible outer garment, even when itself stiffened.
  • Blouse: Similar because both are a woman's upper garments; differs because a blouse is loose and a separate garment in its own right, while a bodice is integral to the dress hanging below it.
  • Shirt: Similar because both cover the upper torso; differs because a shirt is an everyday, often loose and separate garment, while a bodice is the fitted, structural part of a woman's gown.
  • Top: Similar because both are upper garments; differs because top is modern, informal, and unstyled, carrying none of the historical tailoring and structure of a bodice.
  • Camisole: Similar because both are women's torso garments; differs because a camisole is a loose undergarment, while a bodice is a structured, fitted outer garment.
  • Bustier: Similar because both cover the torso from chest to waist; differs because a bustier is a modern, often strapless and revealing garment with an erotic tone, while a bodice is the dignified, integral part of a dress.
  • Waist (American historical): Similar because both name the upper part of a dress; differs because waist stressed the waistline as the defining point, while a bodice covers the whole torso down to that line.
  • Stomacher: Similar because both belong to the front torso of historical dress; differs because a stomacher is the decorative triangular panel worn over the front of the bodice, an accessory to it rather than the bodice itself.
  • Bodysuit: Similar because both are close-fitting garments covering the torso; differs because a bodysuit is a modern one-piece undergarment with no historical tailoring, while a bodice is a dress section with a long sartorial history.
