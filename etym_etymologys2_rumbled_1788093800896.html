Middle Dutch (13th–14th c.)
rommelen
"to rumble, roar, make a low sound"
↓
Middle Low German (14th c.)
rummelen
"to make a rumbling noise"
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
rumblen
"to make a low heavy continuous sound"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–)
rumble
"to make a deep rolling noise; to move noisily"
↓
Modern English (past tense)
RUMBLED
"made a low continuous sound; moved with it"

NOTE: The word is imitative (onomatopoeic) — no PIE ancestor is reconstructable; the Germanic forms are parallel coinages echoing the same sound (cf. German rummeln “to make a noise”). The slang sense “to see through, detect” (17th-c. cant; modern British “rumbled” = exposed) is probably a separate word, of uncertain and possibly Romani origin, that converged on the same form.
