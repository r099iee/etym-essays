CONSTITUENT 1: LICK (Verb)
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*leyǵʰ-
"to lick, lap up with the tongue"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*likkōną
"to lick, lap"
↓
Old English (c. 9th c.)
liccian
"to lick, lap with the tongue"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
likken / liken
"to lick, pass the tongue over"
↓
Modern English (c. 1500)
lick
"to lick, pass tongue over"

CONSTITUENT 2: SPITTLE (Noun)
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*(s)pyēw- / *sp(y)u-
"to spit, spew forth"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*spīt- / *spēwaną
"to spit, eject saliva"
↓
Old English (c. 10th c.)
spittan + -el
"to spit" + diminutive/instrumental suffix → "saliva, spittle"
(alongside cognate spātl "spittle")
↓
Middle English (c. 1350)
spitel / spittel
"saliva ejected from the mouth, spittle"
↓
Modern English (c. 1550)
spittle
"saliva, ejected spit"

COMPOUND SYNTHESIS & PLURAL:
Early Modern English (c. 1620s)
lickspittle
"an abject parasite; one who licks spittle to flatter"
(verb-noun agentive compound: lick + spittle)
↓
Modern English (plural)
lickspittles
"abject sycophants, servile flatterers (plural)"
