• Pinwheel: Similar because it is a toy that spins; differs because a pinwheel is specifically a flat paper wheel spun on a stick by the wind, while a whirligig is the broad generic term for anything that whirls round.
  • Spinning top: Similar because it is a toy that revolves; differs because a top is a balanced toy spun on its point, whereas a whirligig need not balance at all — it is any whirring, gyrating contrivance.
  • Teetotum: Similar because it is a small spinning toy; differs because a teetotum is specifically a gambling top marked with letters for games of chance, while a whirligig is any spinning plaything, often wind-driven.
  • Merry-go-round: Similar because it is a whirling, revolving device; differs because a merry-go-round is a fixed fairground machine on a circular course, not a free-spinning handheld toy.
  • Carousel: Similar because it is a revolving fairground ride; differs because carousel is the grander, more ornate word for the same ride and also names a revolving luggage conveyor, whereas whirligig keeps its toy-like, whimsical sense.
  • Roundabout: Similar because both can name a revolving machine; differs because a British roundabout is primarily a road junction (a whirl of traffic), while a whirligig is a spinning toy or garden spinner.
  • Fidget spinner: Similar because it is a handheld spinning toy; differs because the fidget spinner is a modern, functional gadget for restless hands, while the whirligig is an archaic-whimsical word carrying an oath-like, fairy-tale flavour.
  • Weathercock: Similar because both can mean a fickle, inconstant person or thing that turns with circumstances; differs because a weathercock literally turns with the wind on a rooftop, while a whirligig spins of its own motion and its fickleness is giddier and more capricious.
  • Whirl: Similar because it names rapid circular motion; differs because whirl is the plain verb and noun for the motion itself, while a whirligig is a specific object (a toy or contrivance) that performs the whirling.
  • Spinner: Similar because it is something that rotates; differs because a spinner is a general name for anything that spins (a fishing lure, a fidget gadget, a rotating machine part), while a whirligig is specifically a toy or whimsical contraption.
