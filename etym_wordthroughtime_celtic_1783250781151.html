- [Ancient tribal label] - In its current form (from Greek Keltoi and Latin Celtae), 'Celtic' was first a learned name for a family of ancient peoples who once filled the map of western Europe - a sweeping, scientific label.
- [18th and 19th century philology] - In the age of comparative philology 'Celtic' became the umbrella term for a whole branch of the Indo-European languages, used with calm confidence by scholars tracing the triumph and retreat of tongues.
- [Cultural revival] - From the 19th century onward it took on romantic and political colour: the Celtic revival, the Celtic twilight, the Celtic fringe - identity, poetry and national assertion, no longer merely linguistic.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'Celtic' names the modern Celtic nations, languages and identities (and the football club), a word that has travelled from ancient tribe to living cultural badge.
