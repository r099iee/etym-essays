• Comedy: Similar because farce is a species of comedy and shares its purpose of provoking laughter through fictional incident; differs because comedy is the broad genre covering everything from wit to satire to romantic comedy, while farce is a narrower, high-speed mode defined by extreme improbability, physical machinery, and plot collapse.
  • Slapstick: Similar because both thrive on physical humour, knockabout violence, and rapid pacing, and slapstick moments are typical ingredients of farce; differs because slapstick names the physical comedy itself (pratfalls, pie-throwing, collisions), whereas farce is a full dramatic structure with plot, stock roles, and identity machinery that can include slapstick but is not limited to it.
  • Parody: Similar because both are comic forms that exaggerate for ridicule and can satirize institutions or styles; differs because parody deliberately imitates and mocks a specific prior work, author, or genre, while farce invents its own absurd situations and does not require a target text to invert.
  • Burlesque: Similar because the historical burlesque also travesties serious subjects for laughter and overlaps farce in low comic theatre; differs because burlesque works by degrading an elevated original or style through incongruous treatment, whereas farce achieves comedy through plot frenzy and overstuffing without needing a high model to parody.
  • Mockery: Similar because both dismiss something as unworthy of seriousness and both can be applied to persons, works, or events; differs because mockery names the act or tone of contemptuous ridicule, while farce names a chaotic or absurd structure — the spectacle of the thing, not the sneer directed at it.
  • Sham: Similar because both denote things that fail to be what they pretend to be and both are used to condemn bogus proceedings; differs because sham stresses deliberate fraud or hollow imitation (a fake, a pretence), whereas farce may be a genuinely intended process that collapses through ineptitude — bungled sincerity rather than planned deception.
  • Charade: Similar because both label an insincere or absurd performance and both are used of meetings, trials, or relationships that are going through the motions; differs because charade emphasizes the acted pretence or empty ritual (going through the motions), while farce emphasizes chaotic incompetence and the sheer quantity of absurd incident.
  • Travesty: Similar because both condemn a proceeding that has been grotesquely distorted from its proper form (a travesty of justice, a farce of a trial); differs because travesty focuses on the distorted or debased version of something serious, while farce focuses on the ludicrous, slapstick absurdity of the collapse.
