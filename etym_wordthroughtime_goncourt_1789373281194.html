- [1850s–1880s] - Goncourt functions in Parisian high culture as the shared surname and joint collaborative moniker of Edmond and Jules de Goncourt, pioneers of documentary naturalism, art criticism, and intimate social memoirs - [connotation: obsessive aestheticism, naturalist realism, elite Parisian cultural salon life]
- [1896–1900] - Following Edmond's death, his testament endows the Académie Goncourt to rival the conservative Académie française, legally establishing the surname as an independent literary institution - [connotation: philanthropic rebellion against academic orthodoxy, avant-garde patronage]
- [1903–1919] - The inaugural Prix Goncourt is awarded, rapidly ascending in public estimation to become France's premier literary accolade; Proust's controversial 1919 victory over Roland Dorgelès seals its mythic national status - [connotation: ultimate literary validation, fiery cultural controversy, commercial kingmaker]
- [Mid-20th c.–Present] - Goncourt operates in modern global literary discourse as the gold standard of Francophone novelistic achievement and a metonym for the legendary annual autumn banquet at the Restaurant Drouant - [connotation: highest literary prestige, classic Gallic salon tradition, guaranteed bestseller status]
