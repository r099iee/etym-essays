• Neigh: Similar because both are equine vocalizations; differs because a neigh is the loud, full-throated, trumpet-like call, proud and commanding and audible across a field, while a whinny is soft, high, tremulous, and anxious.
  • Whicker: Similar because both are soft horse sounds; differs because a whicker is the low, throaty, friendly muttering of greeting or anticipation of food, quiet and contented, while a whinny is higher, more tremulous, and more urgent.
  • Nicker: Similar because both are quiet equine calls; differs because a nicker is a deep, soft, almost purring sound of contentment or welcome, while a whinny rises in pitch and carries nervousness or longing.
  • Bray: Similar because both are animal cries; differs because a bray belongs to the donkey and ass — a harsh, sawing, discordant blast — while a whinny is the soft, quivering call of the horse.
  • Squeal: Similar because both are high-pitched animal cries; differs because a squeal is the sharp, shrill protest of a hurt or fighting animal, while a whinny is tremulous, softer, and pleading.
  • Snort: Similar because both are equine sounds; differs because a snort is the explosive expulsion of air through the nostrils, often of alarm or challenge, while a whinny is a voiced, vibrating call through a partly closed throat.
  • Whine: Similar because both name a thin, high, plaintive sound and share the same imitative root; differs because a whine is a sustained human or mechanical complaint, while a whinny is a musical, trembling equine call.
  • Call / cry (general): Similar because both name a voiced animal sound; differs because call and cry are generic across species, while whinny names specifically the soft, tremulous cry of a horse.
