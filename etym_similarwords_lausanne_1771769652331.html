• Switzerland: Similar because both locate the same political frame — Lausanne is a Swiss city; differs because Switzerland is the larger sovereign nation, while Lausanne names the specific lakeside city and its educational-religious milieu rather than the whole confederation.
  • the Swiss Confederation: Similar because both refer to the Swiss polity; differs because this is the formal constitutional name of the whole country, whereas Lausanne is a single city within it, so the two are in no sense interchangeable.
  • Lake Geneva: Similar because both evoke the same geographic setting on Lac Léman; differs because the lake is a body of water shared with France, while Lausanne is the city on its shore where Gibbon's tutoring and reconversion actually took place.
  • Lac Léman: Similar because it is the French name for the same lake that fronts Lausanne; differs because it names the water itself, not the settlement, and carries a distinctly Francophone, Vaudois flavour.
  • Vaud: Similar because Lausanne is the capital of the canton of Vaud; differs because Vaud is the modern administrative canton — a container, not the biographical scene of Gibbon's formation.
  • Geneva: Similar because it is a Swiss city on the western end of the same lake, with a famous Reformation and Enlightenment history; differs because Geneva is a different city with a separate polity and story (Calvin's republic), not the site of Gibbon's re-education.
  • the Pays de Vaud / Vaudois country: Similar because it is the regional landscape around Lausanne; differs because it denotes the broader countryside of the canton rather than the urban centre where the tutor and academy were located.
  • Helvetia: Similar because it is a learned, Latin-derived name for Switzerland; differs because it is an archaizing or allegorical label (on coins, stamps) for the whole confederation, entirely without Lausanne's specific local reference.
  • the Lausanne years: Similar because both refer to Gibbon's residence in the city; differs because the phrase is a chronological biography shorthand for a period of his life, whereas the bare name denotes the place itself.
  • exile / re-education abroad: Similar because both capture the function of the episode in Gibbon's story — forced removal and correction; differs because they are generic descriptions that lose the concrete European place, the lakeside setting, and the Francophone scholarly world that biographers treat as Gibbon's intellectual cradle.
