• Köhler: Similar because Wolfgang Köhler was Koffka's lifelong peer and co-founder of the Berlin Gestalt school; differs because Köhler focused on physical field analogies, primate insight experiments on Tenerife, and psychophysical isomorphism, whereas Koffka served as the systematic theoretician, developmental psychologist, and primary English-language synthesist of the movement.
  • Wertheimer: Similar because Max Wertheimer was the foundational visionary of Gestalt theory whose 1910 phi phenomenon experiments initiated the collaboration with Koffka and Köhler; differs because Wertheimer concentrated primarily on productive thinking, grouping laws, and mathematical logic, whereas Koffka systematically articulated Gestalt principles across child development, memory traces, and general perception.
  • Lewin: Similar because Kurt Lewin was another brilliant member of the Berlin Gestalt school who emigrated to the United States; differs because Lewin adapted Gestalt field theory into social psychology, topological dynamics, action research, and group life-space dynamics, whereas Koffka remained dedicated to core perceptual systems, developmental psychology, and epistemological philosophy.
  • Ehrenfels: Similar because Christian von Ehrenfels laid the direct philosophical groundwork for Gestalt theory with his 1890 concept of *Gestaltqualität* ('form-quality'); differs because Ehrenfels maintained an Austrian Brentanian view that elemental sensations still underpin form-qualities, whereas Koffka and his Berlin colleagues insisted that the holistic Gestalt is primary and irreducible from the very onset of sensation.
  • Kafka: Similar because it is the direct West Slavic (Czech) etymological doublet of Koffka, both originating from the Common Slavic noun *kavka* ('jackdaw'); differs because *Kafka* retained the open unrounded vowel /a/ in Czech and is immortalized worldwide by the existential novelist Franz Kafka, whereas *Koffka* underwent Silesian Germanized vowel rounding to /ɔ/ and belongs to the history of scientific psychology.
  • Brentano: Similar because Franz Brentano's act psychology and phenomenology profoundly influenced the philosophical lineage leading to Gestalt theory; differs because Brentano belonged to nineteenth-century Austrian scholastic philosophy focusing on intentionality, whereas Koffka operated as an empirical twentieth-century experimental psychologist.
  • Stumpf: Similar because Carl Stumpf was the eminent Berlin psychologist under whom both Koffka and Köhler completed their doctoral dissertations; differs because Stumpf worked within late-nineteenth-century acoustic psychology and nativism, whereas Koffka broke away from Stumpf's elemental legacy to pioneer radical Gestalt holism.
