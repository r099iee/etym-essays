- 1550s–1620s: Classical Renaissance Borrowing - Entered Early Modern English as a scholarly Latinate/Hellenic neologism during the dawn of English transoceanic exploration, denoting anything pertaining to ships or sailors.
- 1650s–1780s: Institutionalization in Scientific Navigation - Solidified into formal maritime science through the establishment of the Royal Observatory and the Board of Longitude, becoming anchored in navigational mathematics and cartography.
- 1800s–1900s: Technical Standard & Lexicographical Domain Label - Adopted by major dictionaries (such as Webster and the Oxford English Dictionary) as a standardized subject field label marking specialized seamanship terminology.
- Mid-20th Century: Everyday Civilian & Military Distinction - Maintained strict differentiation from 'naval' (military) while denoting standard civilian nautical charts, radio licensing, and seamanship manuals.
- Late 20th–21st Century: Aesthetic & Lifestyle Generalization - Expanded into popular consumer culture to designate coastal interior design, striped navy-and-white apparel, and leisure yachting aesthetics while preserving its rigorous navigational definitions.
