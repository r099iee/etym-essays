- 18th to Early 19th Century: Post-Revolutionary intellectual genesis - Emerged following the French Revolution to denote political and theological counter-revolutionaries (such as Joseph de Maistre and Louis de Bonald) who argued that human society must rest upon divine revelation and hereditary monarchy rather than Enlightenment rationalism.
- Mid-19th Century: Catholic theological disputes - Used in Catholic dogmatics to describe the theological faction (condemned in part by the Papacy) that asserted that all human religious and moral knowledge was originally derived from primitive divine revelation handed down through tradition, rather than reachable through human natural reason.
- Early to Mid-20th Century: Cultural, philosophical, and aesthetic polarization - Broadened into literature, aesthetics, and metaphysics; applied to the Perennialist metaphysical school of René Guénon, as well as literary critics defending classical metrics and the Western canon against emerging modernist and cubist avant-gardes.
- Late 20th Century: The Culture Wars and Canon Debates - Became a central battleground term in Anglo-American universities during the 1980s and 1990s ("Canon Wars"), contrasting defenders of the Great Books curriculum with post-structuralist and feminist academic reformers.
- 21st Century: Liturgical and sociopolitical realignment - Heavily applied to young religious demographics returning to ancient liturgical rites (e.g., the Latin Mass) and cultural critics pushing back against hyper-accelerated digital progressivism and algorithmic homogenization.
