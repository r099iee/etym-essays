- Vedic Era (c. 1500–800 BCE) — Sacred & Enigmatic Sacrificial Associate: Appears in the famous Vṛṣākapi hymn of the Ṛgveda (RV 10.86), representing an enigmatic, hyper-potent draught-animal or pet monkey belonging to Indra, evoking rustic ritual potency, solar fertility, and fierce domestic controversy between Indra and Indrāṇī.
- Epic Sanskrit Era (c. 500 BCE–200 CE) — Heroic, Devout Simian Champions: Solidified in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata as the noble designation for Sugrīva, Hanumān, and the simian host (*kapi-sainya*), where *kapi* is celebrated for steadfast devotion, colossal physical strength, supernatural agility, and battlefield courage.
- Classical & Scholastic Era (c. 300–1200 CE) — Cosmological Symbol & Botanical Pillar: Codified in Sanskrit lexicons like the Amarakośa as a multi-tiered term meaning not only monkey, but also an epithet of the Sun (drinking water from the earth), the god Vishnu (*Kapīndra*), and appearing in Ayurvedic naming for bristly, medicinal forest flora (*kapikacchū*).
- Medieval Bhakti & Tantric Eras (c. 1200–1700 CE) — Divine Avatar of Fidelity: Re-conceptualized in devotional theology, where Hanumān as *Kapīśvara* ("Lord of Monkeys") became the supreme archetype of humble surrender (*dāsya-bhakti*) and invincible spiritual protection, celebrated in the Hanumān Cālīsā and vernacular poetry.
- Modern Scholarly & Indological English (19th–21st c.) — Philological & Comparative Paradigm: Studied by comparative linguists and Indo-Europeanists as a crucial lexical bridge linking Sanskrit *kapi* to Ancient Greek *kēpos*, Hebrew *qōf*, and Germanic *apa* (*ape*), reflecting ancient international Indian Ocean trade routes and primate symbolism.
