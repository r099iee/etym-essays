- Medieval Iberian Period - Emerged as a rustic toponymic identifier in northern Spain (Castile and León) designating homesteads or villages situated near patches of stinging nettles (Latin *urtica*) or grounds frequented by the sandgrouse bird (*ortega*).
- Early 20th Century (1914–1925) - Rose to international intellectual prominence with José Ortega y Gasset, leading voice of the *Generación del 14*, whose foundational works *Meditaciones del Quijote* (1914) and *La deshumanización del arte* (1925) redefined Spanish philosophical aesthetics and European modernist theory.
- Mid-20th Century (1930s–1950s) - The publication and worldwide translation of *The Revolt of the Masses* (*La rebelión de las masas*, 1930) made *Ortega* a central reference in global political philosophy and sociology, sparking intense debates on democracy, mass culture, and totalitarianism.
- Late 20th Century - Re-evaluated in academic philosophy and cultural theory for his pioneering formulations of perspectivism, ratiovitalism, and media ecology through his influential journal *Revista de Occidente*.
- 21st Century - Continues to be widely cited in political science, literary criticism, and philosophy of technology for his prescient warnings regarding hyper-specialization, the alienation of the mass-man, and the situated nature of human knowledge.
