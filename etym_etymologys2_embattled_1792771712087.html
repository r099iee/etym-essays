Latin (late, 4th c.)
battuere
‘to beat, strike’
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 5th–8th c.)
*battualia
‘military exercises, combat’
↓
Old French (11th–12th c.)
bataille
‘battle; battalion’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
embataillen
‘to fortify; array for battle’
↓
Modern English (current)
embattled
‘beset; in battle array’

Note: Latin battuere (‘to beat’) is itself of likely Gaulish (Celtic) origin, borrowed into soldiers' Latin — so the word for battle may have entered Latin from the very barbarians Rome fought. The deeper PIE source is uncertain; de Vaan marks battuere as a probable Celtic loan without a secure PIE root.
