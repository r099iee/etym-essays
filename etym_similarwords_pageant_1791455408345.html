• Parade: similar because it is a public procession with costumes, floats, and spectators; differs because a parade is primarily a moving line of participants, while a pageant usually has a stronger narrative or theatrical structure.
  • Procession: similar because it involves a formal, ordered movement of people; differs because a procession need not have scenes, costumes, or a story, and can be purely religious or funerary.
  • Spectacle: similar because it is a striking public display; differs because spectacle is broader and can be accidental or non-narrative, while a pageant is planned and performative.
  • Tableau: similar because a pageant often contains static, costumed scenes; differs because a tableau is a single frozen scene, while a pageant is usually a sequence or a whole event.
  • Ceremony: similar because both are formal, public, and often traditional; differs because ceremony stresses ritual and protocol, while pageant stresses display and performance.
  • Ritual: similar because both can be repeated, symbolic, and communal; differs because ritual is primarily about sacred or social order, while pageant is primarily about representation and spectacle.
  • Masque: similar because it is a costumed, allegorical court entertainment; differs because a masque is a specific Renaissance dramatic form with music and dance, not a general public spectacle.
  • Play: similar because a pageant can be a dramatic performance; differs because a play is a scripted work for the stage, while a pageant often includes processions, tableaux, and civic celebration.
  • Extravaganza: similar because it suggests a lavish, spectacular production; differs because extravaganza stresses lavishness and entertainment, often with fantasy or music, rather than historical or civic representation.
  • Show: similar because a pageant is something put on for an audience; differs because show is far broader and can be commercial, televised, or non-ceremonial.
  • Display: similar because both involve something presented to be seen; differs because display can be static and non-performative, while a pageant implies organised performance.
  • Pomp: similar because pageants often involve ceremony and splendour; differs because pomp stresses ostentatious grandeur and can be pejorative, while pageant can be neutral or celebratory.
  • Pageantry: similar because it is the collective noun for pageants and their display; differs because pageantry refers to the whole apparatus or quality of spectacle, not a single event.
  • Beauty contest: similar because it is the plain synonym for a beauty pageant; differs because it is more direct and less formal, and lacks the historical and theatrical overtones of pageant.
  • Historical re-enactment: similar because it recreates past events with costumes and performance; differs because re-enactment stresses fidelity to a specific event, while a pageant may be allegorical, symbolic, or celebratory.
  • Carnival: similar because it is a colourful public festivity with costumes and performance; differs because carnival is typically seasonal, participatory, and festive, while a pageant is usually staged and narrative.
