“Snort” has always meant to force air explosively through the nose — the short, harsh, involuntary blast of breath, first of animals like horses, then of people.
“Snort” from the animal sound was borrowed by humans as a gesture of contempt, disbelief, or derision — a “snort” of dismissal or laughter — and as a noun for the sound itself.
“Snort” in the 20th century took a slang turn: to “snort” came to mean inhaling drugs sharply through the nose, and as a noun it named the dose taken that way.
“Snort” today covers the whole range at once — the horse’s blast, the rude human dismissal, the derisive laugh, and the slang inhale — all sharing one sharp nasal explosion.
