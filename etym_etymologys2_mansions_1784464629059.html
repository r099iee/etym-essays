↓ PIE (reconstructed)
*men-
"to stay, remain"

↓ Latin (1st c. BCE)
manere
"to remain, stay"

↓ Latin (classical)
mansio
"a staying,
dwelling, abode"

↓ Latin (classical)
mansiōnem
"dwelling,
abode (accus.)"

↓ Old French (12th c.)
mansion
"dwelling, abode"

↓ Middle English (14th c.)
mansion
"dwelling, abode"

↓ English (16th c.)
mansion
"large, stately house"

↓ English (modern)
mansions
"large, grand houses"

Note: English 'mansion' narrowed in
the 16th c. from 'dwelling, abode' to
'grand house'. The PIE root *men-
'to remain' is distinct from the
homophonous *men- 'to think'.
