PIE *men- "to remain, stay, wait" (a root distinct from the homophonous *men- "to think"; attested in Greek ménō "I remain" and, with debated connections, Sanskrit man- "to wait, stay") → Latin manēre (to remain, stay, abide), attested from the 2nd century BCE → Latin mānsiō (noun, accusative mānsiōnem: a staying; night's lodging; halting-place; station on a journey; dwelling-place), built on the perfect participle stem māns- of manēre, attested from the 1st century BCE → Old French / Anglo-Norman mansion (dwelling, abode, residence), attested from the 10th–12th centuries CE → Middle English mansion / mansioun (dwelling-place, abode; stage or station on a journey), attested from the 13th century CE → Modern English mansion (a large, grand, impressive house; formerly any dwelling or abode), hence the plural MANSIONS of the card. NOTE: sister English words from the same Latin stay-root — remain, permanent, manor, manse — are collaterals, not intermediate stages of mansion. The PIE root *men- "to remain" is distinct from the homophonous *men- "to think" (source of mind, memory).
