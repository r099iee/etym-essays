- 15th–16th c. - “stocking” = the lower leg-part of hose (the “nether stock”) - a plain workday clothing word worn by men and women alike
- 1560s–17th c. - Elizabethan silk stockings - a luxury marker of courtly status (tradition gives Queen Elizabeth I the first silk knit stockings in 1561)
- 17th–18th c. - the stocking-frame knitting machine transforms the trade; “blue-stocking” clubs - the word enters intellectual history as a label for learned women
- 19th c. - stockings worn by everyone, almost always concealed under long skirts - mundane, propriety-coded, universal
- 1940s - nylon stockings, wartime scarcity, the “nylon black market” - the word becomes glamorous, rationed, desirable
- 1960s – present - displaced by tights and pantyhose for daily wear - “stockings” now reads vintage, festive (Christmas stockings, stocking-stuffers) and nostalgic
