- [Ancient and Roman period - the river Liger is a frontier and a trade-way of the Gauls] - [Perception: a boundary, a barrier, a military fact more than a beauty]
- [Medieval period - the Loire marks the border between the langue d’oïl and langue d’oc, and between rival lordships] - [Perception: a political river, guarding castles at every ford]
- [15th–16th c. - the kings of France settle on the Loire; Amboise, Blois, Chambord rise] - [Perception: the river becomes the stage of royal power and Renaissance art]
- [16th–17th c. - poets of the Pléiade (Ronsard near Vendôme, du Bellay) make the valley a literary homeland] - [Perception: ‘the country of the Loire’ stands for sweetness, civil elegance, a French Arcadia]
- [19th c. - the railway by-passes the river; Balzac and travellers discover its quiet picturesqueness] - [Perception: from highway to heritage — a nostalgic, romantic river]
- [20th c. - ‘the garden of France’ tourism, wine routes, château-tourism] - [Perception: the Loire is marketed as the essence of old France]
- [2000 - the central valley is listed by UNESCO] - [Perception: a living landscape, protected and celebrated]
- [Today - floods, drought and tourism debate the river's future; ‘Loire’ also names wines, departments and a way of slow travel] - [Perception: beloved emblem and fragile ecosystem at once]
