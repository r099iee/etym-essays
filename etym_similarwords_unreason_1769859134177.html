• Irrationality: Similar because both denote a lack of sound reasoning; differs because irrationality is a dry, analytical term for a specific logical failure, while unreason names a broad, systemic breakdown of rational order.
  • Folly: Similar because both denote foolishness; differs because folly suggests lighthearted misjudgment or indiscretion, while unreason carries existential weight and the suppression of thought.
  • Madness: Similar because both denote a loss of reason; differs because madness implies clinical insanity or violent derangement, while unreason designates the atmospheric vacuum in which rationality decays.
  • Absurdity: Similar because both denote meaninglessness or a departure from logic; differs because absurdity emphasizes the ridiculous nature of existence, while unreason points to the absence and active suppression of rational order.
  • Nonsense: Similar because both name the absence of sense; differs because nonsense is everyday, playful, and refers to absurd speech or ideas, while unreason is grave, formal, and philosophical.
  • Foolishness: Similar because both describe behavior lacking good sense; differs because foolishness is mild and quotidian, while unreason is systemic and dramatic.
  • Unreasonableness: Similar because both are formed on "reason"; differs because unreasonableness describes a quality of being hard to deal with in a particular case, while unreason names the general state of irrationality.
  • Illogic: Similar because both denote a failure of reasoning; differs because illogic points to a flaw in an argument's structure, while unreason is broader, naming the collapse of the rational faculty itself.
  • Inanity: Similar because both denote emptiness of sense; differs because inanity stresses silliness and emptiness of content, while unreason stresses the darkness of a mind deprived of reason.
  • Delirium: Similar because both describe a derangement of the mind; differs because delirium is a clinical state of confused, feverish raving, while unreason is a philosophical or literary condition.
  • Lunacy: Similar because both name a departure from reason; differs because lunacy is a vivid, older word for madness, while unreason is an abstract, dignified negation.
  • Darkness (figurative): Similar because both name the absence of mental light and clarity; differs because darkness is a general metaphor for ignorance and evil, while unreason specifically names the absence of the reasoning faculty.
