• Model: Similar in being a pattern to be imitated or compared; differs because model is the broadest term — any representation, person, or plan — while paradigm is a model invested with the authority of a standard exemplar.
  • Exemplar: Similar in being a typical instance that serves as an example; differs because an exemplar is an individual case or person worthy of imitation, whereas a paradigm is the abstract pattern such cases embody.
  • Archetype: Similar in being an original pattern; differs because an archetype is the primal, mythic image from which copies descend — older and Jungian — while a paradigm is a contemporary working model.
  • Prototype: Similar in being a model; differs because a prototype is the first concrete working specimen of a thing (a prototype engine), while a paradigm is an abstract or intellectual pattern.
  • Template: Similar in providing a shape to be copied; differs because a template is a mechanical or material pattern with little intellectual depth, while a paradigm carries assumptions and values.
  • Pattern: Similar in denoting a regular arrangement or model; differs because pattern is a plain word for any recurring arrangement, whereas paradigm implies a pattern invested with exemplary authority.
  • Standard: Similar in being something set up as a measure; differs because a standard is a criterion of comparison or quality, while a paradigm is a model of how things are done.
  • Ideal: Similar in being a perfect model; differs because an ideal is a perfect target that may be unattainable, while a paradigm is a working framework actually in use.
  • Schema: Similar in being a structured framework; differs because a schema is a neutral diagram or outline of structure, lacking paradigm's connotation of exemplary authority.
  • Framework: Similar in being a supporting structure of ideas; differs because a framework is the skeleton of an approach, while a paradigm is the whole constellation of assumptions, methods, and values built around it.
  • Benchmark: Similar in being a reference point for comparison; differs because a benchmark is a standard of measurement to be tested against, not a model to be imitated.
  • Blueprint: Similar in being a detailed plan for imitation; differs because a blueprint is a specific technical plan for construction, while a paradigm is a general intellectual framework.
