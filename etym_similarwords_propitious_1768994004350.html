• Auspicious: Similar because it promises success; differs because auspicious describes the signs and omens observed (from Latin auspicium, "bird-watching"), the evidence of coming success, while propitious describes the disposition or favor itself — a day can be full of good omens without the powers being propitious, and vice versa.
  • Favorable: Similar because it indicates a good chance of success; differs because favorable is neutral and secular — circumstances incline helpfully — with no supernatural agent or will involved.
  • Opportune: Similar because it is well-timed for success; differs because opportune is purely temporal — the right moment has arrived — with no sense of a power disposed toward you.
  • Fortunate: Similar because it carries a sense of good luck; differs because fortunate describes outcomes or persons blessed by blind chance, whereas propitious implies an active will inclined toward you.
  • Lucky: Similar because it denotes favorable chance; differs because lucky is casual and modern, naming random good fortune with no will, no omen, and no dignity.
  • Gracious: Similar because it describes a superior being or person showing favor; differs because gracious implies condescension and charm from above, while propitious implies active goodwill toward one's hopes.
  • Well-disposed: Similar because it names a favorable attitude; differs because well-disposed is plain, modern, and matter-of-fact, losing the religious, augural weight of propitious.
  • Benign: Similar because it describes a kindly, favorable influence; differs because benign is clinical and medical in modern use (a benign tumor), and describes nature rather than intention.
  • Promising: Similar because it suggests future success; differs because promising is general and impersonal — the evidence looks good — with no agent granting or withholding favor.
  • Encouraging: Similar because it raises hope of success; differs because encouraging implies an active sign or message that heartens, not a disposition of fortune toward you.
  • Timely: Similar because it denotes a good moment; differs because timely is purely practical — coming at the right time — with none of the sense of divine or fateful goodwill.
  • Seasonable: Similar because it denotes what comes at the proper season; differs because seasonable is agricultural and meteorological, referring to the weather or the harvest, not to fortune's disposition.
