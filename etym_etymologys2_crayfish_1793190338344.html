PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*grebh-
‘to dig, scratch, bury’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*krabitaz
‘crab, scratcher, digger’
↓
Frankish (c. 5th–8th c.)
*krebit
‘crab’
↓
Old French (12th c.)
crevice
‘crab, freshwater crayfish’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
crevise
‘freshwater crustacean’
↓
Modern English (folk etymology) (16th c.)
crayfish
‘freshwater lobster-like animal’

Note: the -fish ending is pure folk etymology, shared with crawfish and jellyfish — the animal is a crustacean, not a fish — reshaped by English speakers from crevise; the deeper link of the Germanic crab-word to PIE *grebh- (‘to dig’) is the standard handbook connection but is debated, since burrowing is only one of several proposed motives for the name.
