• Wrinkled: Similar because both describe linear ridges, puckers, or irregularities formed on a surface; differs because wrinkled denotes irregular, small, chaotic crumples (especially of dry skin or unpressed cotton), whereas creased typically refers to distinct, sharper, and often more structural or deliberate linear folds.
  • Furrowed: Similar because both describe linear depressions or grooves across a surface; differs because furrowed evokes deep, parallel trenches resembling plowed agricultural earth, applied almost exclusively to brows expressing solemnity, deep anxiety, or concentrated intellect.
  • Folded: Similar because both involve bending a pliable material back upon itself; differs because folded is the broad physical act of doubling material over, whereas creased specifically refers to the permanent or visible linear mark or ridge left behind by the pressure of that fold.
  • Crinkled: Similar because both describe fine surface indentations; differs because crinkled denotes delicate, paper-thin, crackling, or rustling micro-wrinkles (such as crushed cellophane, tissue paper, or laughing eye corners), whereas creased implies deeper, firmer, or more defined linear boundaries.
  • Pleated: Similar because both describe folds in textile garments; differs because pleated refers strictly to deliberate, regular, uniform folds stitched or pressed permanently into cloth (such as in a kilt, curtain, or accordion skirt), whereas creased can be accidental, chaotic, or individual.
  • Crumpled: Similar because both describe surfaces crushed out of flatness; differs because crumpled denotes violent, multi-directional compression into a messy, three-dimensional mass, whereas creased preserves recognizable, distinct directional lines.
  • Corrugated: Similar because both describe ridges and grooves; differs because corrugated is an engineered, industrial term for alternating parallel ridges pressed into heavy materials (such as galvanized steel or cardboard) to maximize structural stiffness.
  • Lined: Similar because both refer to markings across a surface; differs because lined can denote superficial visual marks or printed rules without any physical relief, whereas creased always denotes a physical indentation or structural ridge caused by bending or compression.
