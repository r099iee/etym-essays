• Apollo: Similar because it refers to a god of beauty, often associated with classical male perfection; differs because Apollo also embodies light, music, and prophecy, making his beauty more intellectual and divine, whereas Adonis is purely about physical, often mortal, allure.
  • Beau: Similar because it denotes a handsome man, often a suitor; differs because 'beau' is more colloquial and less formal, lacking the mythological depth and tragic undertones of 'Adonis'.
  • Heartthrob: Similar because it describes a man who is widely admired for his attractiveness; differs because 'heartthrob' is a modern, informal term focused on popular appeal and romantic interest, without the classical or literary weight of 'Adonis'.
  • Hunk: Similar because it refers to an attractive, often muscular man; differs because 'hunk' is very informal and emphasizes physical build and raw attractiveness, lacking the refined, idealized, or potentially tragic connotations of 'Adonis'.
  • Matinee idol: Similar because it describes a handsome male actor who is adored by audiences; differs because 'matinee idol' is specific to the entertainment industry and implies a broader appeal based on charisma and performance, not solely physical beauty in the classical sense.
  • Pin-up: Similar because it refers to an attractive person whose image is widely displayed; differs because 'pin-up' is a more general term for an idealized image, often sexualized, and can apply to either gender, lacking the specific mythological male beauty reference.
  • Casanova: Similar because it refers to a man known for his charm and attractiveness to women; differs because 'Casanova' emphasizes seduction and romantic conquests, whereas 'Adonis' focuses purely on physical beauty, without necessarily implying a specific romantic prowess.
