• Rotherhithe: Similar because both are venerable maritime dockland districts facing each other across the River Thames (Rotherhithe on the south bank, Wapping on the north bank), steeped in shipyard heritage and seafaring populations; differs because Rotherhithe was the historic home of Captain Christopher Jones and the berth from which the Mayflower departed in 1620, retaining a slightly quieter village feel, whereas Wapping was far more densely urbanized, directly adjoining the City of London, and home to Execution Dock.
  • Limehouse: Similar because both are historic East End maritime riverside quarters in Tower Hamlets shaped by river commerce and foreign trade; differs because Limehouse was renowned as London's historic nineteenth-century Chinatown and the river entrance to the Regent's Canal, whereas Wapping was the primary bastion of native naval trades, enclosed bonded docks, and naval hangings.
  • Shadwell: Similar because both are adjacent riverside districts in East London sharing maritime working-class roots and dockland commerce; differs because Shadwell lies immediately inland and east of Wapping and was renowned in the Victorian era for extreme poverty and coal-heaving, whereas Wapping possessed direct waterfront prominence and the monumental enclosed London Docks.
  • Bermondsey: Similar because both are Thames-side industrial quarters characterized by massive nineteenth-century brick wharves and warehousing; differs because Bermondsey sits on the south bank of the river and was celebrated as the historic hub of the leather tanning and food processing trades, whereas Wapping was almost exclusively tied to ocean shipping and naval victualling.
  • Deptford: Similar because both were vital centres of English naval power on the Thames; differs because Deptford, situated further downstream in southeast London, was the site of the Royal Naval Dockyard founded by Henry VIII and the royal victualling yard, whereas Wapping catered primarily to private merchant shipping, privateers, and river craft.
  • Billingsgate: Similar because both are historic waterfront districts on the north bank of the Thames near the City; differs because Billingsgate was the world-famous wholesale fish market and customs wharf inside the City walls, renowned for profane speech, whereas Wapping was a sprawling residential and industrial sailortown downriver.
  • Greenwich: Similar because both are world-famous London maritime locations; differs because Greenwich represents the grand royal and scientific heritage of the Royal Navy (the Royal Naval College, the National Maritime Museum, and the Royal Observatory), whereas Wapping represents the rough-and-tumble commercial frontline of working mariners.
