- c. 650-500 BCE (Archaic Greece): Túrannos referred neutrally or mixedly to an autocrat who seized power without hereditary title, often championing commoners against aristocrats.
- c. 450-300 BCE (Classical Greece): Demonized by Athenian democracy, Plato, and Aristotle as the ultimate moral monster and corrupt perversion of kingship.
- c. 1300s-1500s (Middle English): Entered via French as tyrant / tiraunt, representing the prototypical cruel usurper, pagan persecutor, and ungodly oppressor in mystery plays and chronicles.
- c. 1600s-1700s (Enlightenment): Central symbol in political philosophy (Milton, Locke, American Revolution) representing arbitrary power violating natural law and the social contract.
- c. 1800s-Present: Broadened to include domestic bullies, oppressive workplace managers, and figurative forces of custom or time, while retaining its supreme political gravity.
