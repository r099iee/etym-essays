• previously: Similar in meaning 'before this time'; differs because previously is plain and neutral with no legal flavour, while heretofore is bookish, formal, and ceremonial.
  • formerly: Similar in denoting past time; differs because formerly points to an older state of affairs that no longer holds, whereas heretofore points up to the present moment, inclusive of it.
  • hitherto: Similar in meaning 'until now'; differs because it is built on hither ('to this place') and is slightly more literary and self-conscious than the workmanlike heretofore.
  • beforehand: Similar in meaning 'in advance, earlier'; differs because beforehand points to a time before a specific event and is informal, while heretofore seals a whole closed past up to the present.
  • before: Similar in meaning 'at an earlier time'; differs because before is a general, informal, unemphatic preposition-adverb, while heretofore is a formal compound used at the opening of a sentence or clause.
  • earlier: Similar in meaning 'at a prior time'; differs because earlier is relative and neutral, while heretofore names the boundary point of the present itself.
  • so far: Similar in meaning 'up to this point'; differs because so far implies a measured stretch with an eye toward continuation, while heretofore regards the past as a closed ledger.
  • thus far: Similar in meaning 'up to this point'; differs because thus far is more literary than so far and also hints at continuation, while heretofore carries the finality of a sealed past.
  • up to now: Similar in meaning 'until now'; differs because up to now is spoken and neutral, without the legal or ceremonial weight of heretofore.
  • until now: Similar in conversational meaning; differs because until now is informal and spoken, while heretofore belongs to statute and formal prose.
  • as yet: Similar in meaning 'up to this time, still'; differs because as yet is used chiefly with negatives and implies that the state may soon change, while heretofore simply seals the past.
  • yet: Similar in meaning 'up to now'; differs because yet is a tiny grammatical adverb governed by negatives and questions, while heretofore is a full formal compound with its own rhetorical weight.
  • once: Similar in meaning 'at a former time'; differs because once points to a specific past occasion or a single past state, while heretofore sums up the whole stretch up to the present.
  • erstwhile: Similar in meaning 'former, at an earlier time'; differs because erstwhile is now chiefly an adjective (an erstwhile friend) and archaic, while heretofore is a living formal adverb.
