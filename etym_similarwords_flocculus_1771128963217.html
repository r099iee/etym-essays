• Tuft: Similar because both denote a small clump of hair, grass, or fiber; differs because tuft is generic and often firmly rooted, while flocculus is a diminutive, softer and more woolly.
  • Flock: Similar because both are textile terms for tufts of wool; differs because flock is a mass or waste of fibers, not a tiny singled-out tuft.
  • Wisp: Similar because both mean a small light loose piece; differs because wisp suggests smoke or thin strands, while flocculus implies soft woolly clumping.
  • Lock: Similar because both can name a tress of hair; differs because a lock is a longer gathered portion, while a flocculus is a small fluffy tuft.
  • Nodule: Similar because both denote a small round mass; differs because a nodule is hard and solid, while a flocculus is soft, fibrous, woolly.
  • Clump: Similar because both mean a small mass; differs because clump implies density or stickiness, while flocculus implies dry airy fluffiness.
