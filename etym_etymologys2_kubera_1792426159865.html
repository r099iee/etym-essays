Kubera

Note on etymological debate: Ancient Sanskrit grammarians (in the Nirukta and Puranas) rationalized Kubera as a compound of ku- ("bad, deformed") + bera ("body"), citing his mythological physical defects (three legs, single yellow eye, dwarfish frame). However, modern comparative historical linguists (such as F.B.J. Kuiper, Thomas Burrow, and Manfred Mayrhofer) recognize this as a classical folk etymology. They demonstrate that Kuvera / Kubera is almost certainly a loanword from an indigenous Austroasiatic (Munda) or Dravidian substrate representing an ancient local tutelary earth-god of subterranean minerals and wealth who was absorbed into late Vedic and early epic religion.
