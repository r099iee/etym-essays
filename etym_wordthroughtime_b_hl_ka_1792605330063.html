- [Late Vedic Era (c. 1000–800 BCE)] - recorded in the Atharvaveda (5.22) as Bālhika, a distant, cold northern frontier beyond the borders of Āryāvarta to which fevers and ailments (takman) are ritually banished - [perception: viewed with awe and suspicion as an exotic, freezing borderland inhabited by heterodox tribes]
- [Epic Sanskrit Period (c. 500 BCE–300 CE)] - celebrated in the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa as a wealthy, prestigious kingdom producing peerless cavalry warhorses (bāhlīka-haya), blankets, and chariot warriors; featured in the Kurukṣetra war through King Bāhlīka - [perception: admired for supreme equestrian excellence, martial power, and aristocratic kinship with the Kuru dynasty]
- [Greco-Bactrian & Indo-Greek Era (3rd–1st c. BCE)] - ruled by Hellenistic kings who struck Greek coins, founded poleis (Ai-Khanoum), and synthesized Greek artistic naturalism with Persian and Indian traditions - [perception: celebrated across Eurasia as the prosperous "Empire of a Thousand Cities" (Bactria)]
- [Kushan & Buddhist Flowering (1st–7th c. CE)] - served as the imperial core of the Kushan Empire, transforming Balkh into a magnificent metropolis of the Silk Road and a world center of Mahāyāna Buddhist scholarship (Navavihāra monastery, visited by Xuanzang) - [perception: revered as a radiant cosmopolitan capital of international trade, art, and spirituality]
- [Islamic Golden Age (8th–13th c.)] - evolved into Islamic Balkh, celebrated throughout the Persian world as Umm al-Bilād ("Mother of Cities"), a premier center of Islamic learning, science, and the birthplace of the mystical poet Rumi - [perception: honored as one of the supreme intellectual and cultural jewels of the Islamic East]
- [Modern Era (19th–21st c.)] - an evocative toponym in classical Indology, Central Asian archaeological excavations (e.g., the Bactrian Gold of Tillya Tepe), and historical equestrian studies - [perception: recognized as a vital civilizational bridge between India, Persia, Greece, and Central Asia]
