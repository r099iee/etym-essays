• fortress: Similar because both belong to the world of fixed military defense; differs because a fortress is the physical fortification or stronghold, while a garrison is the body of troops stationed in it (and the act of stationing them).
  • outpost: Similar because both denote stationed military forces at a defended position; differs because an outpost is a small, forward position for early warning, while a garrison is a substantial force inside a main stronghold.
  • detachment: Similar because both refer to a body of troops; differs because a detachment is a temporary segment of a force sent away on a specific mission, while a garrison is a permanent or semi-permanent stationed defense force.
  • post: Similar because both name a place where troops are stationed; differs because post emphasizes the geographic location of duty, whereas garrison emphasizes the troops and the fortified character of the place.
  • encampment: Similar because both denote a military body located somewhere; differs because an encampment is a temporary field camp, while a garrison implies fixed fortification and long-term stationing.
  • barracks: Similar because both refer to military housing; differs because barracks are the buildings themselves, while a garrison is the stationed troops (who occupy the barracks) and the fortified post as a whole.
  • citadel: Similar because both belong to fortified defense; differs because a citadel is the strong inner fortress of a city, while the garrison is the defending force within it.
  • troops: Similar because both denote military personnel; differs because troops is the general term for soldiers, while garrison specifies soldiers assigned to hold a particular fortified place.
  • soldiers: Similar because both name military personnel; differs because soldiers is the neutral generic word for any members of an army, whereas garrison names a role: the soldiers who defend a fixed stronghold.
  • reinforcement: Similar because both concern military defense of a position; differs because reinforcement is the additional troops sent to strengthen a force, while a garrison is the existing stationed defensive body.
