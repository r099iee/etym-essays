IDLE is a Germanic word of uncertain deeper ancestry; its certain history begins in Proto-Germanic, and no accepted PIE etymon exists. CHAIN: → Proto-Germanic *īdalaz 'empty, vain, idle' (reconstructed; c. 500 BCE-500 CE) — Germanic-only; no certain cognates outside Germanic → Old English īdel 'empty, void, vain, useless, unemployed' (c. 800-1100) → Middle English idel / ydle 'unoccupied, vain, futile, worthless' (c. 1200-1500) → Modern English idle 'inactive, groundless, futile' (1500-present) → IDLE NOTE ON DEBATE: the ultimate origin of Proto-Germanic *īdalaz is unknown. A link to PIE *h₁eydh- 'to burn, blaze, shine' has been proposed — on the theory that 'idle' originally meant 'clear, bright' and thence 'clear of work, empty' — but this is speculative and not generally accepted; the standard dictionaries mark the deeper etymology as uncertain or unknown. The word's West Germanic family is solid: Old Saxon īdal 'empty, worthless', Old Frisian īdel 'empty, void', and Old High German ītal 'empty, vain, bare', which developed into modern German eitel 'vain, conceited' while also preserving an archaic positive sense 'pure, unmixed'.
