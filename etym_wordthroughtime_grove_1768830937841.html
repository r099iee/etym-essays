- The Old English word (pre-1100) - grove is the plain native name for a small wood or thicket, a neutral landmark of the landscape
- A place apart (medieval period) - it settles on the bounded, open cluster of trees, a sheltered clearing set off from the open field, often kept for game or shade
- The sacred and the pastoral (16th-18th c.) - the classical revival makes the grove the seat of ceremony: the sacred groves of antiquity, the grove of Academe, the haunt of Dryads and lovers in pastoral verse
- Contemporary - the word has narrowed to planted, managed clusters - orange grove, olive grove, memorial grove - keeping a residue of quiet seclusion but shedding its old sacredness
