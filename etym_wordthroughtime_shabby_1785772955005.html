- [OE–ME background: Scab & mange] - English has sceabb/scabby for diseased crust and mangy look; insult uses prepare contempt for ‘unclean’ surfaces and persons.
- [1660s–1700s: Shabby as mangy → worn] - New form shabby peels away from strict disease toward clothes and appearance that look scurfy, seedy, or threadbare; urban and class-conscious speech adopts it fast.
- [1700s–1800s: Genteel poverty & moral meanness] - Public perception pairs shabby rooms/coats with failed respectability; ‘shabby treatment’ makes ethics visible as worn cloth—mean, ungenerous, low.
- [Victorian–early 20th c.: Novelistic staple] - Character sketch default for down-at-heel clerks, misers’ offices, and dishonorable bargains; strongly judgmental of both poverty’s display and petty vice.
- [Late 20th–21st c.: Dual + chic] - Ordinary senses unchanged; ‘shabby chic’ rebrands deliberate fade as style, even as ‘shabby way to treat someone’ remains a sharp social rebuke.
