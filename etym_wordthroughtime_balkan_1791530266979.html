- Fourteenth to eighteenth centuries - Ottoman Turkish Balkan names a wooded mountain range, above all the Stara Planina; it is a local geographical word, not a political one
- 1808 - the German geographer August Zeune coins Balkanhalbinsel, "Balkan Peninsula", extending a mountain name to a whole region; the term enters the atlas
- Nineteenth century - the peninsula becomes a diplomatic object, and "the Balkans" acquires an exotic, orientalising colour in Western travel writing
- 1912–1918 - the Balkan Wars and the First World War fix the word as a byword for ethnic conflict; "balkanisation" is coined for the break-up of empires into small states
- Cold War - the peninsula is divided between NATO Greece and Turkey and the non-aligned Yugoslavia, and "Balkan" often implies backwardness and communist intrigue
- 1990s - the Yugoslav wars revive the darkest associations; "Balkan" becomes a live metaphor for atrocity and fragmentation
- Twenty-first century - "Western Balkans" becomes an EU administrative category, while scholars such as Todorova critique "Balkanism"; the word is now used with visible self-consciousness
