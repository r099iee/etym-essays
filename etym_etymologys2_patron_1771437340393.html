Stage 1 — PIE *ph₂tér- "father" (also transcribed *pəter-; Pokorny *pətēr; one of the oldest and most stable kinship reconstructions in Indo-European). Stage 2 — Latin pater, patris "father" (attested from the earliest Latin inscriptions; the plural patres, "the fathers," names the senate). Stage 3 — Latin patronus "protector, advocate, patron; legal guardian" — derived from pater with a suffix of office; first the former master of a freedman who remained his protector (attested in Plautus, 2nd century BCE). Stage 4 — Old French patron "patron, protector; model, sample" (11th century; the double sense "protector" and "model" arises because the patron who commissions a work is also its pattern). Stage 5 — Middle English patron "protector, defender" (c. 1300, borrowed from Old French). Stage 6 — English PATRON "a protector, a supporter, a benefactor; later a regular customer" (this card's word; patron saint 16th c., patronage 14th c., patronize 16th c. with the condescending sense from the 18th c.). NOTE ON THE PATTERN SPLIT: the Old French double sense entered English as one word; around 1600 the spellings diverged — patron kept the protector (French spelling), while pattern became the model (re-spelled on the sound). The two are the same word historically.
