• Barrow: Similar because it is the root noun for a carrying frame with handles; differs because it is the older general term, vague about wheels, and survives mainly inside compounds like handbarrow.
  • Handbarrow: Similar because it is a barrow for carrying loads; differs because it has no wheel and is carried by two people at its ends, whereas a wheelbarrow has a single front wheel and is pushed by one.
  • Cart: Similar because it is a vehicle for moving loads; differs because a cart has two or more wheels and is pulled (by a person or animal) rather than pushed on one wheel.
  • Handcart: Similar because it is a small wheeled vehicle pushed by hand; differs because it has two wheels and a simpler box without the front-wheel-tilt dumping action.
  • Trolley: Similar because it is a wheeled device for carrying loads; differs because it moves on two or four wheels, often over smooth floors, and does not tip to discharge its load.
  • Dolly: Similar because it is a wheeled platform for moving heavy items; differs because it is low and flat, without sides, rear handles, or the single-wheel design.
  • Tumbrel: Similar because it is a tipping cart; differs because it is a two-wheeled, usually horse-drawn historical cart, far larger than the one-person wheelbarrow.
  • Rickshaw: Similar because it is a one-person human-powered vehicle; differs because it carries people and is pulled or pedalled, not a load-bearing, wheel-and-handles garden tool.
