• Gaps: Similar because both denote an opening or unfilled break between two parts; differs because gaps can be large, accidental, or irregular ruptures, whereas interstices specifically implies narrow, systemic, or structurally regular spaces between components.
  • Crevices: Similar because both refer to narrow openings or recesses; differs because crevices typically implies jagged, cracked, or fractured openings in rocks or walls, while interstices denotes structural or natural spaces within an organized lattice or tissue.
  • Chinks: Similar because both describe small, narrow apertures that allow light or air to pass; differs because chinks is a more colloquial, rustic term often associated with gaps between cabin logs or armor plates, whereas interstices is formal, scientific, and analytical.
  • Fissures: Similar because both describe linear separations between parts; differs because fissures suggests a deep, splitting fracture or structural cleavage resulting from tension or tectonic stress, rather than routine intervening pores.
  • Apertures: Similar because both designate openings; differs because an aperture is typically an engineered, intentional hole or diaphragm designed to admit light or passage, while interstices are ambient voids between constituent parts.
  • Crannies: Similar because both refer to tiny, out-of-the-way recesses; differs because crannies is informal and often paired with nooks to describe hard-to-reach corners, whereas interstices belongs to high-register literary and scientific prose.
  • Intervals: Similar because both can describe intervening physical or temporal distances; differs because intervals leans heavily toward temporal pauses or linear spatial spans between distinct points, whereas interstices emphasizes the cellular, woven, or spatial matrix enclosing voids.
  • Lacunae: Similar because both denote missing spaces or voids; differs because lacunae specifically signifies missing gaps, omissions, or lost passages in texts, manuscripts, or legal frameworks, rather than physical structural pores.
  • Voids: Similar because both denote empty space; differs because a void suggests complete, cavernous emptiness or total absence of substance, whereas interstices are small, localized openings nestled between solid entities.
