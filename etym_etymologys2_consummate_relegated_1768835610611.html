Chain for CONSUMMATE
1. Proto-Indo-European *upér, 'over, above' — reconstructed; c. 4500–3000 BCE
2. Latin super, 'above, over' — 3rd c. BCE (from PIE *upér; also the source of English super, 17th c., and superior, 14th c.)
3. Latin superus, 'upper, that which is above' — 1st c. BCE (adjective from super; also superī, 'the gods above')
4. Latin summus, 'highest, utmost' — 1st c. BCE (Cicero; the superlative of superus; also the source of English summit, 15th c., via Old French som, and of supreme, 16th c., via supremus)
5. Latin summa, 'the top; the total, the whole amount' — 1st c. BCE (neuter plural of summus used as a noun — the 'top' of a column of figures, hence the sum; the source of English sum, 13th c.)
6. Latin consummāre, 'to add up, to bring to a total, to complete' — 1st c. BCE (the completive prefix con- 'together, wholly' + summa)
7. Latin consummātus, 'completed, finished, perfected' — past participle of consummāre — 1st c. CE (the form famous as the Vulgate's consummatum est, 'it is finished')
8. Middle English → English consummate, adjective 'perfect, complete in the highest degree' and verb 'to finish, to complete' — 15th c. CE (from Latin consummātus)
9. Modern English consummate — present day


Chain for RELEGATED
1. Proto-Indo-European *leg-, 'to collect, gather; by extension to choose' — reconstructed; c. 4500–3000 BCE
2. Latin legere, 'to gather, choose, read' — 3rd c. BCE (Plautus; the sense 'to read' developed because reading was 'gathering' letters; the Greek cognate λέγειν, légein, 'to gather, speak,' gives logic, dialogue)
3. Latin lēgāre, 'to send with a commission, to appoint as deputy, to dispatch as an envoy' — 1st c. BCE (traditionally derived from lēx, lēgis, 'law' — a legate is one sent with legal standing — but see the debate note below)
4. Latin relegāre, 'to send back or away, to banish, to remove' — 1st c. BCE (the prefix re-, 'back, away,' + lēgāre)
5. Latin relegātus, 'sent back, banished, consigned' — past participle of relegāre — 1st c. BCE/CE
6. English relegate, 'to consign to an inferior position, to send away' — c. 1500 CE (from Latin relegātus)
7. Modern English relegated, past tense and past participle — present day

Debated / nuanced points: The derivation of Latin lēgāre is debated. The traditional view connects it with legere, 'to gather, choose' — an envoy is one 'chosen' and dispatched; the modern scholarly view (e.g. de Vaan) treats lēgāre as a denominative of lēx, lēgis, 'law' — one sent with legal commission — while noting that lēx itself is of disputed origin (frequently, but not certainly, linked to legere). The chain above follows the traditional route through legere while flagging the debate.
