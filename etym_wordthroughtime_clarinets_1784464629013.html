“Clarinet” arrived in European music in the early 18th century as a development of the single-reed chalumeau, and the English word settled as the name for this clear, singing, single-reed woodwind.
“Clarinet” through the 18th–19th centuries became a standard of concert orchestras, wind bands, and chamber music, prized for its range from dark chalumeau tones to brilliant high notes.
“Clarinet” in the 20th century moved into jazz and popular music, where its flexible, vocal quality made it one of the most expressive of woodwinds.
“Clarinet” today is the everyday, unremarkable name of a familiar orchestral instrument — even in fantasy prose simply stocking a clear-voiced reed instrument beside fiddles, flutes, and drum.
