- Late-medieval entry - Nicene, formed from the city-name Nicaea (modern İznik, Turkey), entered English with the theology of the church councils, above all the Council of Nicaea of 325, and for centuries denoted orthodox Trinitarian Christianity as fixed in the Creed.
- Reformation and post-Reformation usage - as Christendom argued over the Trinity, Nicene hardened into the badge of orthodoxy against Arianism and became a synecdoche for the philosophically engaged, literate Christianity of the Roman world.
- Contemporary sense - Nicene is a specialized, chiefly ecclesiastical and historical adjective, encountered almost always in Nicene Creed, still standing for the orthodox definition that the Son is of one substance with the Father.
