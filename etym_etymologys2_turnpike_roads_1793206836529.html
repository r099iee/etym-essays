PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*tere-
‘to rub, turn, bore’
↓
Ancient Greek (8th c. BCE)
tornos
‘lathe, circle, tool’
‘for turning’
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
tornare
‘to turn on a lathe’
↓
Old French (12th c.)
torner + pique
‘to turn’ + ‘sharp point’
↓
Middle English (14th–15th c.)
turnen + pike
‘to rotate’ + ‘spiked bar’
↓
Early Modern English (15th c.)
turnpike
‘revolving spiked barrier’
↓
Modern English (18th c., plural)
turnpike-roads
‘tolled highways with gates’

PIE (reconstructed)
*reidh-
‘to ride, go, move’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
rad

↓
Middle English (13th c.)
rode

Note: French pique rests on popular Latin *piccare, to prick, whose deeper origin is uncertain and possibly imitative, so the pike half of the chain begins securely only at Old French.
