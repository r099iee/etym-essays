Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*déḱm̥ + *leh₂b- / *slab-
"ten" + "to take, seize"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE)
δέκα (deka) + συλλαβή (syllabe)
"ten" + "that which is taken together"
↓
Hellenistic Greek (c. 2nd c. BCE)
δεκασύλλαβος (dekasyllabos)
"of ten syllables"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th c. CE)
decasyllabus
"having ten syllables"
↓
French (c. 16th c.)
décasyllabique / décasyllabe
"verse of ten syllables"
↓
Modern English (c. 1750s)
decasyllabic
"consisting of ten syllables"
