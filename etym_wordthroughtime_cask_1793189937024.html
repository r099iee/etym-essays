- 15th c. — the cooper's vessel: cask enters English for the wooden barrel of wine and naval stores, the age of sail in a word.
- 16th–17th c. — ships and cellars: casks of water, salt pork, and powder fill every hold; broaching a cask marks feasts, mutinies, and survival alike.
- 18th c. — measure as well as object: writers order a cask of claret or Madeira as a quantity, and great house cellars catalogue butts, hogsheads, and tuns.
- 19th c. — industry and empire: brewers and distillers standardise the cask while steel and bottle begin their slow challenge; sherry butts season the whisky of Scotland.
- 20th c. — the real-ale crusade: CAMRA makes cask-conditioned beer a cause, rescuing live beer in wooden casks from pressurised keg uniformity.
- Today — heritage as flavour: cask strength, cask finish, and cask-aged labels turn the old barrel into a promise of craft, patience, and oak.
