• box: Similar because both mean a container; differs because box is the general, practical container of any size and material, while casket is specifically a small chest associated with precious contents or with burial.
  • chest: Similar because both mean a storage container with a lid for valuables; differs because chest is larger, furniture-like and usually of strong wood or metal, while casket is small, portable and precious.
  • case: Similar because both descend from the same Latin ancestor (capsa) and both mean a protective container; differs because case is flat, functional and for specific objects (a watch case, a violin case), while casket is a lidded chest for accumulate treasure or the dead.
  • coffer: Similar because both mean a strong box for valuables, money or treasure; differs because coffer stresses security and wealth (a locked strongbox for coin) and has bureaucratic senses (public coffers), while casket stresses preciousness and, in America, burial.
  • strongbox / safe: Similar because both mean a secure container for valuables; differs because strongbox and safe are modern security equipment with locks and armour, while casket is a precious or ceremonious container without the security-industry sense.
  • jewel box: Similar because both mean a small container for jewellery; differs because jewel box is a modern, specifically commercial word, while casket carries historical, literary and funerary weight.
  • coffin: Similar because in American usage both mean the burial container; differs because coffin is the plainer, tapered, six-sided box and the older, blunter word, while casket is the rectangular, upholstered and euphemistic funeral-industry term.
  • sarcophagus: Similar because both are burial containers for the dead; differs because sarcophagus is the massive carved stone coffin of antiquity, monumental and above ground, while casket is a portable wooden or metal box lowered into the ground.
  • urn: Similar because both contain the remains of the dead; differs because the urn holds cremated ashes, is small and jar-like, and lacks the full-body containment of a casket.
  • reliquary: Similar because both are precious containers for something venerated; differs because reliquary is the ecclesiastical container for saints’ relics, strictly religious, while casket holds jewels or bodies in secular or general use.
  • receptacle: Similar because both mean a container; differs because receptacle is an abstract, technical word for any container and carries none of casket’s value, ceremony or mortality.
