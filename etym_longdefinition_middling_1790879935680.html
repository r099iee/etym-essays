Middling is an adjective, adverb, and substantive noun describing that which occupies an intermediate position, rank, size, quantity, or quality—remaining central, moderate, or average, and neither remarkably superior nor egregiously defective.

Depending on the semantic register and communicative context, middling can function as an objective, neutral descriptor of intermediate dimension or commodity grading, or as a mildly disparaging, critical assessment of uninspired mediocrity, artistic adequacy, and unexceptional performance.

Primary, Technical & Multi-Faceted Senses:

  • Moderate or Average Quality (Mediocrity): Describing performance, talent, artistic execution, or intellectual output that fails to reach greatness while remaining passable or ordinary (e.g., "a middling actor," "a middling novel that neither delights nor offends"). This is the critical sense famously emphasized by French philosopher Denis Diderot in his Paradox of Acting (Paradoxe sur le comédien), where he argued that extreme emotional sensibility produces merely middling actors who remain prisoners of their own erratic feelings, whereas true sublime mastery demands cold, calculated technical detachment.
  • Intermediate Physical Size, Extent, or Quantity: Characterizing physical dimensions or quantities of moderate scale that fall squarely between large and small (e.g., "a market town of middling size," "a middling harvest that met basic domestic needs").
  • Socio-Historical Sense ("The Middling Sort"): In early modern British and colonial American social history, the middling sort denoted the emerging socioeconomic class positioned between the landed aristocracy/gentry and the impoverished laboring peasantry—comprising prosperous yeomen farmers, merchants, master craftsmen, guild artisans, shopkeepers, and educated professionals. This concept served as the direct historical precursor to the modern concept of the "middle class."
  • Commercial & Commodity Grading ("Middlings"): In agricultural commerce, grain milling, and the textile industry, middlings refers to an intermediate grade of flour, grain screenings, or raw cotton containing a mixture of bran, coarse endosperm, and germ, typically utilized in animal feed or secondary industrial manufacturing.
  • Colloquial Adverbial & State of Health: In regional vernacular (notably in British dialects, Scots, and Southern and Appalachian American English), used adverbially in conventional idioms like fair to middling to denote tolerable health, ordinary spirits, or moderate financial fortunes (e.g., "How have you been keeping? Just fair to middling, thank you.").
  • Nautical & Historical Naval Jargon: In classical naval architecture and maritime warfare, describing intermediate-sized tackle, sails, or rigging that fall between the heavy main courses and light topgallants.

Semantic Tone and Connotation:

  • When applied to creative, intellectual, or athletic endeavors, middling carries an inherent aesthetic critique: it emphasizes the lack of distinction, brilliance, or divine inspiration. It delineates that vast, unmemorable middle stratum of practitioners who populate a profession without ever leaving a lasting mark upon it.
