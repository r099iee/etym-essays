Proto-Algonquian (c. 1000 BCE)
*pōxkʷahw-
"to break open or crush by pounding"
↓
Virginia Algonquian (Powhatan) (early 17th c.)
pawcohiccora
"milky liquor prepared from pounded hickory nuts"
↓
Colonial Virginian English (c. 1612–1653)
pohickory
"the nut-milk drink, then the tree itself"
↓
American English (late 17th c.)
hickory
"the North American hardwood tree and its timber"

Note: Recorded by Captain John Smith (1612) and William Strachey as pawcohiccora, describing a white, rich liquor made by beating hickory kernels in a mortar with water. The word underwent aphesis (loss of the initial unstressed syllable po-) in colonial colloquial speech by the 1670s, yielding modern hickory.
