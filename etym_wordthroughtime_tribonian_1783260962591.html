- Justinianic Byzantium - Tribonian (c. 500-542) served Justinian as quaestor of the sacred palace and presided over the commissions that compiled the Corpus Juris Civilis - the Digest, the Institutes, and the revised Code.
- Mixed ancient reputation - in the legal tradition he was the master codifier who preserved nearly a thousand years of Roman jurisprudence, while Procopius's Secret History accused him of avarice, so his name carried both honor and suspicion.
- Later legal fame - for the whole history of European law, from the glossators to the modern codes, Tribonian became a byword for the preservation and systematization of Roman law at the very moment the West was collapsing.
- Contemporary sense - Tribonian denotes the jurist of the Digest, the intellectual pillar of Justinian's age, linked in Gibbon with Belisarius and Simplicius among the marvels gravely related in rich and polite Constantinople.
