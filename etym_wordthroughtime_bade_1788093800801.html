- The ordinary strong past of “bid” — throughout Middle English “bade/bad” means simply “commanded, invited, said a greeting”; fully standard and unremarkable.
- The literary past — in Early Modern English (Shakespeare: “he bade me… call thee Thane of Cawdor”; the King James Bible) it is the natural past of command, invitation and farewell.
- Retreat into the archaic — as “bid” gained the regular weak past “bid” (and participial “bidden”), “bade” receded from speech and became a marker of formal, scriptural or old-fashioned diction.
- Today — an archaic literary fossil: fantasy and period prose, “bade farewell”, “bade welcome”; in ordinary speech, dead except in quotation.
