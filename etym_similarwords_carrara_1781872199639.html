• Marble: Similar because both name the material associated with the place; differs because marble is the generic metamorphic stone found anywhere, while Carrara localizes excellence, history, and prestige.
  • Quarry: Similar because both relate to the extraction of stone; differs because quarry is industrial and process-focused, lacking the poetic contour of "the hills of Carrara."
  • Paros: Similar as a famous classical marble source; differs because Paros evokes rival Greek marble (Parian) and shifts the cultural map away from Italian Renaissance continuity.
  • Pentelic: Similar as another classical marble source (Mount Pentelicus, Athens); differs because it belongs to the Greek classical world, not the Italian Renaissance world Pater's list assumes.
  • Tuscany: Similar in situating the region; differs because Tuscany is a much broader geographic frame without the stone-specific fame of the Carrara district.
  • Apuan Alps: Similar in naming the mountains where Carrara lies; differs because it names the range, not the quarrying district or the marble brand that the toponym carries.
  • Statuario: Similar in denoting the finest grade of Carrara marble; differs because it is the technical trade grade, not the place-name or the literary touchstone.
  • Marmor Lunense: Similar in naming the Roman marble of the same district; differs because it is the ancient Latin term (marble of Luna), historical and technical, rather than the modern toponym.
