• Flemish: Similar because it is the adjective formed from the same territory and is often used where a noun might be expected (“the Flemish”); differs because it describes language, art or people rather than the place, and it cannot stand for the region in a phrase like “in Flanders”.
  • Fleming: Similar because it names a native of Flanders and shares the same Germanic root; differs because it is a demonym for a person, not a territory, and in English it also survives as a surname.
  • Vlaanderen: Similar because it is the Dutch name of the same region and is used officially in Belgium alongside the English form; differs because it belongs to Dutch-language discourse and carries the modern community's own political framing.
  • Flandre: Similar because it is the French name of the region and appears in English historical writing on the ancien régime; differs because French usage often prefers the plural Flandres and can denote French Flanders specifically.
  • Low Countries: Similar because it is the broader geographical term covering Flanders with Brabant, Holland, Zeeland and the rest; differs because it names the whole delta region, not one county or community within it.
  • Belgium: Similar because Flanders is now the northern region of Belgium and the two names are constantly used together; differs because Belgium is a sovereign state including Wallonia and Brussels, whereas Flanders is a region and community within it.
  • Wallonia: Similar because it is the counterpart region of federal Belgium, French-speaking and formerly industrial; differs because it lies to the south, has a different language and a different historical name and self-image.
  • Brabant: Similar because it is another historic Low Countries territory adjoining Flanders and often confused with it; differs because its capital was Brussels and its name survives mainly in the provincial divisions of Belgium and the Netherlands.
  • Artois: Similar because it is a neighbouring historic county, long contested between France and the Habsburgs; differs because it lies south of Flanders, was annexed by France in the seventeenth century, and is now a French département.
