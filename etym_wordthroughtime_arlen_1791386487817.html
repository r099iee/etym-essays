- 1919–1920 - Dikran Kouyoumdjian invents “Michael Arlen” for his first book - the name is a practical solution to an unpronounceable foreign surname and a bid for literary fame
- 1920s - The Green Hat makes “Arlen” a society brand - the word connotes Riviera glamour, exotic sophistication and mild scandal
- 1930s - Arlen turns to plays, journalism and political writing - the name loses some of its frivolous sparkle and acquires a more serious, worldly tone
- 1940s–1950s - he works in Hollywood and civil defence, then settles into relative obscurity - “Arlen” becomes a period reference, a name from the vanished twenties
- Late twentieth century - the novels fall out of fashion and the surname is often confused with Harold Arlen - the word survives mainly in literary histories and film credits
- Twenty-first century - biographies, reissues and Armenian diaspora studies revive interest - “Arlen” now signals self-invention, immigrant assimilation and Jazz Age nostalgia
