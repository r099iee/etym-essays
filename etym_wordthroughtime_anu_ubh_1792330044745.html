- Vedic Samhitā Era (c. 1500–1000 BCE) — Receptive exclusively as a sacred liturgical chant-meter in the Ṛgveda, perceived as a mystical acoustic vehicle whose 32 syllables harmonized the singer with cosmic and seasonal orders.
- Brāhmaṇa & Aranyaka Period (c. 900–600 BCE) — Theological and ritual codification where priests identified the meter with speech (Vāc) and bodily physiology, regarding each syllable as an indivisible unit of sacrificial currency.
- Epical Transition Period (c. 500 BCE–200 CE) — The rigid Vedic rules softened and crystallized into the narrative cadence of the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa, democratizing the meter from elite ritual altars into universal storytelling.
- Classical Sāstric Age (c. 200–1200 CE) — Established as the indispensable pedagogical format for all technical, legal, scientific, and philosophical treatises across the Indian subcontinent.
- Modern Philological & Global Era (19th c.–Present) — Analyzed in comparative Indo-European linguistics as a prime window into Proto-Indo-European octosyllabic verse, while revered by scholars and practitioners as the enduring rhythmic heartbeat of Sanskrit culture.
