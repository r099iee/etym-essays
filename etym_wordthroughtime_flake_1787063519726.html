- Thin flat chip or piece that splits off (snow, rock, paint, skin) - a homely, physical word for fragments breaking away.
- 16th century: verbal "flake (off)" = to peel or come away in layers - describes weathering, decay, and surfaces failing.
- 19th-20th century: transferred to character in American slang - a "flake" is a vague, unreliable, eccentric person; "flaky" = temperamental, undependable.
- 1940s US military slang: "flake out" = to collapse from exhaustion or fall asleep - the word moves from things to bodies.
- 1920s-1950s underworld: a "flake" of cocaine; "to flake" someone = to plant evidence and frame them - criminal slang built on the image of a dropped fragment.
- Late 20th century computing: "flaky" software and tests fail intermittently - the metaphor of unreliable cohesion goes technical.
- 21st century: "snowflake" = the unique individual, later a dismissive insult for the over-sensitive - a cultural battleground in one word.
