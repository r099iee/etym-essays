• Edge: Similar because both name the boundary line where a surface ends; differs because edge is the general term, sharp or straight, while lip is specifically a curved projecting rim, often of a hollow.
  • Rim: Similar because both describe the circular border of a cup, crater, or hollow; differs because rim is neutral and mechanical, while lip adds the flesh-like curve and the suggestion of a mouth.
  • Brim: Similar because both are the upper rim of a vessel or hollow, full to the brim; differs because brim stresses fullness to the top edge, while lip stresses the projecting shape itself.
  • Brink: Similar because both mark the line where level ground gives way to a drop; differs because brink stresses the verge of falling or crisis, while lip stresses the curved anatomy of the hollow.
  • Margin: Similar because both denote a border zone around something; differs because margin is flat, written, or figurative, while lip is raised, rounded, and three-dimensional.
  • Mouth: Similar because both are openings rimmed with flesh or earth, and each lends the other its metaphors; differs because the mouth is the whole opening and cavity, while the lip is only its rim.
  • Verge: Similar because both describe the extreme edge of a place or state; differs because verge is abstract and often temporal, on the verge of tears, while lip is concrete and curved.
  • Border: Similar because both mark where one territory ends and another begins; differs because a border is a drawn or political line, while a lip is a physical projecting fold.
  • Insolence: Similar because giving lip is a classic form of insolence; differs because insolence names the attitude of contempt in general, while lip names only its spoken backchat.
