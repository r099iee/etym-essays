Early Modern English (1520s)
badger (bager)
“burrowing mammal”
“white-marked head”
↓
Early Modern English (16th–18th c.)
badger-baiting
“sport of setting dogs”
“on the badger”
↓
Late Modern English (1790s)
badger (verb)
“to bait, torment”
“pester like the dogs”
↓
Modern English (19th c.–current)
badgering
“pestering repeatedly”
“pressuring with demands”

Note: the animal-name itself is debated: the standard account derives badger from badge (mark, emblem) + -ard, for the white blaze on the beast's head, but the ultimate origin of badge is unknown and a French source has also been proposed — so the chain above begins at the first securely attested English form rather than inventing a deeper root.
