• vendetta: Similar because both denote a prolonged, retaliatory hostility, usually between families; differs because vendetta is specifically the Italian code of blood revenge and often implies a formal obligation to kill, whereas feud is the broader English term for any such lasting enmity.
  • quarrel: Similar because both involve open disagreement and hostility; differs because a quarrel can be a single argument or a short-lived falling-out, while a feud is defined by duration and inherited obligation.
  • dispute: Similar because both concern a disagreement between parties; differs because dispute is neutral and often legal or commercial, with no implication of violence or honour, while feud implies deep personal enmity.
  • rivalry: Similar because both describe ongoing antagonism between two sides; differs because rivalry is usually competitive and can be respectful, whereas a feud is hostile, personal, and often violent.
  • enmity: Similar because both denote deep-seated hostility; differs because enmity is the emotional state itself and can be one-sided or silent, while a feud is a social relationship carried on through reciprocal acts.
  • hostility: Similar because both involve antagonism; differs because hostility may be a passing attitude or a state of war, while feud specifies a long, kin-based cycle of retaliation.
  • animosity: Similar because both involve bitter feeling; differs because animosity is an internal emotion, whereas feud is an external, socially organised conflict.
  • strife: Similar because both suggest conflict and discord; differs because strife is a general, often collective condition of trouble, while feud names a particular relationship between identifiable parties.
  • grudge: Similar because both preserve the memory of an injury; differs because a grudge can be held silently by one person, while a feud requires open, mutual, and continuing retaliation.
  • bad blood: Similar because both describe lasting ill feeling between people; differs because bad blood is informal and emotional, whereas a feud implies a structured pattern of action.
