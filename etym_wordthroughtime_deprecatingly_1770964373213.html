- Latin root "deprecari" (to pray against, avert by prayer) - The adverb's earliest sense is devotional: acting as if praying to ward off evil or danger, tied to religious pleading.
- 17th-18th century sense (to express disapproval of, protest against) - Shifts from prayerful aversion to verbal objection, so "deprecatingly" comes to mean with mild but unmistakable reproach or entreaty against something.
- 19th century Victorian sense (self-effacing modesty) - Gains its signature nuance of humble, apologetic disparagement, especially of oneself, used to deflect praise while still signaling disapproval.
- Contemporary sense (soft, self-deprecating modesty) - Retained mainly in formal, literary, and jocular registers as "self-deprecatingly", denoting gentle self-mockery that softens a reproach rather than aggressive condemnation.
