• Equestrian: Similar because both are Latinate and horse-related. Differs because equestrian centres on riders, riding, and mounted display (equestrian sport, equestrian statue), while equine centres on the animal itself — its body, biology, and species identity.
  • Horse / horsey: Similar because both refer to horses. Differs because horse is the basic everyday noun and horsey is informal (fond of horses, or stable-like in smell), whereas equine is formal and scientific; "equine companion" versus "horse" changes register and sometimes dignity of tone.
  • Bovine: Similar because both are taxonomic domestic-animal adjectives built on the same Latin pattern (bovine, ovine, porcine, canine, feline, equine). Differs in connotation: bovine often implies dull sluggishness, while equine often implies grace, speed, or noble animality.
  • Hippic: Similar because it is a Greek-derived synonym meaning "of horses." Differs because hippic is rare and obscure, while equine is the standard term; they draw on different classical source words (hippos vs equus).
  • Caballine: Similar because it means "of horses" and is also Latinate. Differs because caballine, from Latin caballus (the popular "nag, workhorse" word), is almost never preferred to equine in modern English; it highlights the equus vs caballus split in Latin.
  • Canine / Feline / Porcine / Ovine: Similar because they are the parallel taxonomic adjectives for other animals in scientific and clinical English. Differs because each names a different species family; equine is specifically the horse family (Equidae).
