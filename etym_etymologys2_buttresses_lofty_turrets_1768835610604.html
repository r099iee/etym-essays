Root 1 — BUTTRESSES:

Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*buttan
"to push, strike"
(origin uncertain)
↓
Old French (12th c.)
bouter
"to thrust, push"
(from a Germanic source)
↓
Old French (12th c.)
bouterez
"a buttress"
↓
English (14th c.)
buttress
"a projecting stone support"
↓
buttresses
"projecting stone supports"

Debate: the Germanic verb behind Old French bouter is of uncertain ultimate origin; the English form was reinforced by Old French boteresse "a supporting structure".

Root 2 — LOFTY:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*lewp-
"to peel, scale"
(uncertain)
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*luftuz
"air, sky"
↓
Old Norse (9th–13th c.)
lopt
"a loft, sky, upper room"
↓
English (13th c.)
loft
"an upper room, a gallery"
↓
English (15th c.)
lofty
"very high, elevated, towering"

Debate: the link of PGmc *luftuz to PIE *lewp- "to peel, scale" is uncertain; *luftuz is securely attested through German Luft "air" and Old Norse lopt, but its pre-Germanic source is debated.

Root 3 — TURRETS:

Greek (1st millennium BCE)
τύρσις (tyrsis)
"a tower"
(possibly a Mediterranean loanword)
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE)
turris
"a tower"
(origin uncertain)
↓
Old French (11th c.)
tor
"a tower"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
torete
"a turret"
(diminutive of tor)
↓
English (14th c.)
turret
"a small tower"
↓
turrets
"small towers"

Debate: Latin turris is of uncertain origin — the Greek τύρσις "tower" (perhaps itself a non-Indo-European Mediterranean loanword) is the most likely source, but the direction of borrowing is debated.
