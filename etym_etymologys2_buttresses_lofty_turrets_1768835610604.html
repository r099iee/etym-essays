CHAIN 1 — BUTTRESSES PIE *bhau- 'to strike, to beat' (reconstructed; also written *bhōu-; attested by Old Norse bauta 'to beat', Old English bēatan 'to beat') → Proto-Germanic *bautaną 'to beat, push, strike' → Frankish *bōtan 'to push, to strike' → Old French bouter 'to push, to thrust' (9th–12th c.) → Old French bouterez 'buttresses, supporting arches' (plural; from bouter — the masonry that pushes against a wall; 12th–14th c.) → Middle English butres 'buttress' (14th c.) → English buttress 'a masonry support built against a wall'; plural buttresses (target root word) CHAIN 2 — LOFTY (Proto-Germanic root; PIE ancestry unknown — the family is securely Germanic only) Proto-Germanic *luftuz 'air, sky' (reconstructed; PIE etymology debated, none established) → Old Norse lopt 'air, sky; upper room, loft' (attested from the Viking Age) → Middle English loft 'upper room, gallery, air' (13th c.; also the phrase on lofte 'in the air', giving English aloft) → Middle English loft + adjectival suffix -y → English lofty 'high, elevated, in the air; exalted' (14th c.; target root word) NOTE ON NUANCE: The PIE ancestry of *luftuz is unknown; an old suggestion linking it to PIE *leup- 'to peel, break off' is too weak to rely on and is omitted. German Luft 'air' and Dutch lucht 'air' are cognates of the same Germanic stem, and Old English lyft 'air, atmosphere, sky' is a close relative. The English noun loft (the upper room of a house) and the verb to lift (from Old Norse lypta 'to raise') come from the same Germanic family. CHAIN 3 — TURRETS (Pre-Greek / Mediterranean substrate word; no PIE ancestry) ? Pre-Greek *turs- 'tower, fortified height' (substrate word, reconstructed; the origin of Greek týrris) → Greek týrris / týrsis 'tower, fortified stronghold' (Pre-Greek substrate loan, per Beekes; attested from the 5th c. BCE) → Latin turris 'tower' (loan from Greek; Classical Latin) → Old French tour / tur 'tower' (9th–12th c.) → Old French tourette 'little tower' (diminutive of tour) → Middle English touret 'turret' (14th c.) → English turret 'a little tower'; plural turrets (target root word) NOTE ON DEBATE: No PIE etymology for Greek týrris is established; the word is generally treated as a Pre-Greek or Mediterranean substrate term. An older guess deriving turris from PIE *dwer- 'door' is rejected. Latin turris also gave Old English torr 'tower, rock, peak' (our tor, the rocky peak) and German Turm 'tower'.
