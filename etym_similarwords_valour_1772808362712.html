• Courage: Similar because both name the capacity to face fear and danger; differs because courage is the broad moral quality needing no martial setting, while valour is specifically courage under arms.
  • Bravery: Similar because both mean boldness in danger; differs because bravery often stresses conspicuous, momentary daring rather than the trained, sustained fighting quality of valour.
  • Prowess: Similar because both celebrate outstanding fighting ability; differs because prowess emphasizes skill and brilliant deeds, not the moral virtue of facing danger.
  • Gallantry: Similar because both name courage in a martial or knightly context; differs because gallantry adds chivalric, courtly colour and can verge on show or courtesy, while valour stays focused on fighting quality.
  • Fortitude: Similar because both mean strength in adversity; differs because fortitude is endurance under suffering and hardship, not the aggressive front-line energy of valour.
  • Heroism: Similar because both imply exceptional courage; differs because heroism is grand, noble, and often self-sacrificing, tied to heroic deeds, while valour is a soldier's professional courage.
  • Intrepidity: Similar because both mean fearlessness in danger; differs because intrepidity is cool, undaunted fearlessness, more intellectual and detached than the combative valour.
  • Fearlessness: Similar because both describe absence of fear; differs because fearlessness is a psychological trait without the ethical or military value judgement valour carries.
  • Mettle: Similar because both name the strength of spirit shown under pressure; differs because mettle is temperamental grit in any trial, not specifically courage in battle.
  • Daring: Similar because both involve risk-taking bravery; differs because daring suggests audacity and thrill-seeking boldness, sometimes reckless, while valour is disciplined and honourable.
  • Audacity: Similar because both mean bold courage; differs because audacity implies boldness that may be reckless or disrespectful, lacking valour's honourable charge.
  • Guts: Similar because both mean courage in facing danger; differs because guts is blunt, colloquial, and informal, the opposite of valour's elevated, ceremonial register.
  • Nerve: Similar because both name the steadiness to face danger; differs because nerve stresses cool self-control and composure, not the martial fighting spirit.
  • Spirit: Similar because both name energetic courage; differs because spirit is general vigour and morale, not the specific virtue of bravery in battle.
