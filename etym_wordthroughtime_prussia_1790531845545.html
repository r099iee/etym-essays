- 9th–13th c. CE (Baltic Pagan Era) - Referred exclusively to Prūsa, the Baltic geographic homeland of the Old Prussians, a Western Baltic pagan people linguistically related to Lithuanians and Latvians.
- 13th–15th c. (Teutonic Monastic State) - Conquered and Christianized by the Teutonic Order during the Northern Crusades, establishing a German settler state (Deutschordensstaat) governed by monastic knight-monks.
- 1525–1618 (Duchy of Prussia) - Grand Master Albert of Brandenburg converted to Lutheranism and secularized the territory into the Duchy of Prussia, creating the first official Protestant state in Europe as a Polish fief.
- 1618–1701 (Brandenburg-Prussia) - Inherited by the Hohenzollern Electors of Brandenburg; in 1701, Elector Frederick III crowned himself 'King in Prussia' in Königsberg, uniting the Brandenburg state with the Baltic kingdom.
- 18th c. (Age of Frederick the Great) - Emerged as a dominant European Great Power through rigorous military reforms, bureaucratic efficiency, and Enlightenment philosophy (Kant in Königsberg), giving rise to Mirabeau's adage that 'Prussia is an army with a state.'
- 19th c. (Napoleonic Defeat, Reform & German Unification) - Recovered from defeat at Jena (1806) through sweeping Stein-Hardenberg reforms; under Chancellor Otto von Bismarck and King Wilhelm I, Prussia defeated Denmark (1864), Austria (1866), and France (1870–71), proclaiming the German Empire at Versailles.
- 1918–1947 (Weimar Free State to Allied Abolition) - Following the 1918 German revolution and the Hohenzollern abdication, became the democratic 'Free State of Prussia' within the Weimar Republic; formally abolished by Allied Control Council Law No. 46 in 1947 as the bearer of German militarism and reaction.
