• Affectations: Similar because both refer to artificial, unnatural manners adopted specifically to impress onlookers or feign superior taste; differs because affectations is a formal Latinate term encompassing specific idiosyncratic behaviors, accents, or habits (e.g., *literary affectations*), whereas airs typically functions as a broader behavioral demeanor in idioms like *putting on airs*.
  • Pretensions: Similar because both involve laying claim to qualities, talents, or social distinctions that one does not actually possess; differs because pretensions focuses on the underlying false claims, intellectual ambition, or aspirational illusions of grandeur, whereas airs highlights the outward physical strutting, haughty glances, and haughty carriage.
  • Haughtiness: Similar because both express blatant condescension and an assumption of elevated rank over others; differs because haughtiness denotes a genuine, deeply ingrained emotional attitude of cold aristocratic pride and disdain, whereas airs often implies a comical, flimsy, or transparently phony performance of superiority.
  • Mannerisms: Similar because both describe distinctive outward gestures, speech patterns, or physical habits; differs because mannerisms are frequently unconscious, natural physical quirks unique to an individual, whereas airs are always consciously or semi-consciously assumed to project false grandeur.
  • Melodies / Tunes (Musical sense): Similar because both denote a coherent sequence of musical notes that produce a recognizable song or theme; differs because tunes is an informal, catchy, rhythmically direct designation, whereas airs (or *ayres*) conveys an elegant, historical, lyrical, or classical vocal movement distinguished by melodic sweetness.
  • Arias (Musical sense): Similar because both designate an elaborate vocal piece for solo voice within an opera, cantata, or oratorio; differs because an aria is typically an expansive, technically demanding operatic set-piece accompanied by an orchestra, whereas an air can be a simple folk melody, lute song, or modest instrumental line.
  • Posturing: Similar because both involve adopting an insincere stance designed to mislead observers about one's true status or intentions; differs because posturing emphasizes political, ideological, or tactical maneuvering in negotiations or public debate, whereas putting on airs is rooted in drawing-room vanity and social snobbery.
