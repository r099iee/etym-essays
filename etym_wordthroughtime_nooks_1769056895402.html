- Early sense - a nook is a small corner or angle where two walls or surfaces meet; a purely spatial, neutral word for an indentation in any surface
- 16th–17th c. - the domestic snug corner: the sheltered, tucked-away place in a room or garden, as in the "snug nook" of a window-seat or hearth
- 18th–19th c. - the word moves out of doors: nooks of field, wood, and coast become hidden sheltered pockets of landscape, secret and faintly romantic
- Contemporary - mostly plural and set-phrasal ("every nook and cranny"), meaning every conceivable hiding place; in literary use a nook is a small, enclosed, protective pocket — the landscape's own place of refuge for something hidden
