- Old English to Middle English (c. 1000–1400) - Concrete Botanical Foundations: *Rōt* (borrowed from Old Norse) and *word* referred strictly to the underground botanical organ of plants and spoken vocal utterances respectively.
- 16th to 17th Century - Metaphorical & Grammatical Extension: *Root* was adopted by Renaissance grammarians as a metaphor for the foundational ancestral element or radical base of words in Hebrew, Latin, and Greek.
- 18th to 19th Century - Historical Linguistics & Philology: The compound *root-word* became established in 19th-century comparative philology (e.g., Max Müller, Grimm) to describe the primitive monosyllabic roots of Proto-Indo-European.
- 20th to 21st Century - Structural Morphology & Computational NLP: Standardized in formal linguistic morphology (distinguished from stem and base) and deployed in computer science for algorithmic stemming, lemmatization, and lexical databases.
