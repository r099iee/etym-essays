- [Old English & metallurgical sense] - Sinder named the fused, stony dross thrown off in iron-smelting — industrial waste of the forge, value-neutral, the crust of metal-working.
- [Hearth & household fires] - The word settled onto the partially burned, still glowing fragments left in fireplaces and stoves — hard, brittle, blackened remnants of coal or wood, coarser than fine ash.
- [Railway & steam age] - "Cinders" flew from locomotive fireboxes — flying sparks and burnt fragments stinging the eye; the plural became idiomatic (a cinder in one's eye, a cinder track), a mass rather than single pieces.
- [Modern register] - Today cinders is the emphatic plural for the dead residue of a fire "that has done its worst" — the word of burnt remains and total annihilation, grim and final rather than neutral.
