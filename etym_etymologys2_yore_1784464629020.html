Chain for root word yore: PIE *yeh₁r- / *yer- "year, season" (reconstructed; also given as *yōr-; c. 4500–3500 BCE) → Proto-Germanic *jērą "year" (reconstructed; pre-common era) → Old English ġēar "year" (attested from the 7th–8th c. CE; ancestor of modern year) → Old English ġēara "of years" (genitive plural of ġēar) → Old English ġēara used adverbially: "years ago, formerly, long since" (attested in prose and verse, 9th–11th c. CE) → Middle English yore / yoor "long ago, formerly; the distant past" (13th–14th c. CE) → Modern English yore (archaic/literary noun; this card) Debated note: the PIE reconstruction of the "year" root is variously given as *yeh₁r-, *yer-, or *yōr-, with the laryngeal under debate; a proposed connection to a verbal idea "to go, travel" (the year as a going-round) is plausible but unprovable. The English line itself, however, is securely attested: the genitive-plural adverb is the documented bridge from year to yore.
