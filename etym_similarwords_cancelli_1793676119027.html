• Balustrade: Similar because both serve as architectural railings or barriers delineating boundaries between spaces; differs because a balustrade typically consists of a row of upright, sculpted balusters supporting a coping or handrail, whereas cancelli specifically feature an open crisscrossed lattice, grating, or mesh.
  • Chancel screen (or Rood screen): Similar because both represent physical partitions in ecclesiastical architecture separating the clergy and altar from the congregation; differs because a rood screen is often an elaborate, full-height timber or stone structure surmounted by a crucifix (rood), whereas cancelli originally denoted low Roman latticework barriers or rails.
  • Grille: Similar because both describe protective openwork barriers formed of metal or timber bars; differs because a grille is primarily a functional security barrier or vent covering, lacking the specific legal, historical Roman judicial, or anatomical trabecular associations of cancelli.
  • Latticework: Similar because both designate an ornamental or structural network of intersecting crossbars or laths; differs because latticework is a broad, generic descriptive term for any crisscrossed mesh (wooden trellises, garden arbours, mechanical trusses), whereas cancelli carries specific classical judicial, ecclesiastical, and histological connotations.
  • Trellis: Similar because both employ an open framework of crossed bars or laths; differs because a trellis is predominantly an outdoor horticultural support designed for climbing plants, rather than a formal civic barrier, legal partition, or biological matrix.
  • Trabeculae: Similar because in anatomy both refer to the intersecting microscopic beams and bars that compose spongy bone tissue; differs because trabecula (diminutive of Latin trabs, a beam) refers to an individual structural beam, bar, or strut, whereas cancelli refers to the collective lattice-like network of spaces and crossbars formed by them.
  • Parapet: Similar because both function as architectural boundaries or protective barriers; differs because a parapet is a solid or low defensive wall positioned at the edge of a roof, bridge, or balcony to prevent falls or incoming projectiles, lacking the openwork crisscross pattern inherent to cancelli.
  • Grating: Similar because both are frameworks of parallel or crossed bars that admit light and air while barring physical passage; differs because grating is a utilitarian, industrial term (such as a sewer or cell grating), devoid of the solemn judicial, liturgical, and histological nuances of cancelli.
