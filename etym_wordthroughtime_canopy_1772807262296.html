- [14th-15th century mosquito net] - ['Canopy' first means a net or cloth hung over a bed to keep off insects and drafts]
- [16th century ceremonial covering] - [Extends to any ornamental cloth held or hung over a throne, altar, or dignitary; a canopy of state marks rank and ceremony]
- [17th-19th century overhanging shelter] - [Transferred to trees, skies, and mountains: the 'canopy' of a forest or of heaven — anything arching overhead]
- [Present three-way life] - [Bed canopies, ceremonial baldachins, and the natural 'tree canopy' and canopy of clouds all thrive; the word still pictures something hanging protectively overhead and enclosing]
