• Copses: Similar because a copse is likewise a small wood or thicket; differs because copse is simply a little wood of any kind, while coppice properly implies wood cut and regrown on rotation.
  • Thickets: Similar because both name dense tangled growth; differs because thicket stresses impenetrable tangling, while coppices suggests managed, compartmented woodland with a forester's order.
  • Cops: Similar because cop in dialect is a clipped form of the same word for a small plantation; differs because it is a clipped regional variant, while coppices is the full standard form.
  • Underwoods: Similar because both name the low growth beneath or beside taller timber; differs because underwood is defined by position under standards, while coppices is defined by the cutting regime.
  • Groves: Similar because both are small groups of trees with a literary glow; differs because a grove is open and often sacred or ornamental, while a coppice is dense, low, and working.
  • Spinneys: Similar because a spinney is a small covert or plantation sheltering game; differs because spinney belongs to sporting estate vocabulary, while coppice belongs to woodmanship and forestry.
  • Bosquets: Similar because both are formal or semi-formal compartments of trees; differs because bosquet is a French garden term for clipped wilderness, while coppice is a working English wood.
  • Plantations: Similar because both are deliberately grown tree-crops; differs because a plantation usually means even-aged high forest planted in rows, while coppices regenerate from living stools.
