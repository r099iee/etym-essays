*steh₂-
"to stand, set, be upright, be firm"

*stāō / *stistō
"to stand" / "to cause to stand"

Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
sistere (reduplicated from stare)
"to cause to stand, place, plant oneself"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
insistere (in- + sistere)
"to stand upon, plant oneself, press on, urge"
↓

insistens (gen. insistentis)
"standing upon, persisting, urging"
↓

insistentia
"standing firm, persistence, persevering"
↓
Middle French (16th c.)
insistence
"pressing demand, urging, firmness"
↓
English (c. 1605–modern)
insistence
"the act of persisting in a demand or assertion"

Note: Latin sistere is the reduplicated present stem (*si-st-) of the primary Indo-European root *steh₂- ('to stand'), which also produced Classical Latin stare (whence state, status, station). The prefix in- ('upon, on') formed insistere, which shifted from literal physical stance ('to set foot upon') to mental and rhetorical persistence ('to dwell upon, press urgently').
