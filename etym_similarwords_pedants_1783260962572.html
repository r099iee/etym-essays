• Sticklers: Similar because both insist with rigid exactness on rules and details; differs because a stickler insists on standards generally (etiquette, punctuality), whereas a pedant specifically parades academic learning and corrects minor scholarly details.
  • Purists: Similar because both demand strict adherence to a standard of correctness; differs because a purist seeks uncorrupted purity in language or art out of principle, while a pedant fusses over petty rules for display and self-importance.
  • Dogmatists: Similar because both are rigidly certain about their positions; differs because a dogmatist asserts doctrines or beliefs with arrogant finality, while a pedant obsesses over minor formal details and rules of learning.
  • Formalists: Similar because both privilege form and rule over content; differs because "formalist" is often a neutral or technical label (literary theory, law, art), while "pedant" is a term of contempt for ostentatious learning.
  • Hair-splitters / quibblers: Similar because both dwell on tiny distinctions and trivialities; differs because a hair-splitter is cleverly evasive about distinctions in argument, while a pedant is concerned with the show of learning and bookish rules.
  • Nitpickers: Similar because both focus obsessively on tiny faults or details; differs because nitpicking is casual, fussy fault-finding, whereas pedantry implies a pompous academic manner and a claim to superior knowledge.
  • Literalists: Similar because both take the exact letter of a text seriously; differs because a literalist reads at face value (often in religion or law), while a pedant imposes scholarly rules and parades book-learning.
  • Pedagogues: Similar because both come from the same Greek root (paidagōgos, "child-leader") and both denote a teacher; differs because a pedagogue is the professional educator (neutral), while a pedant is the pejorative caricature of the rule-bound, self-displaying teacher.
  • Scholastics: Similar because both belong to academic/bookish traditions and can connote arid formalism; differs because a scholastic is a medieval philosopher of the schools or a systematic academic, without the personal sting of fussy self-display carried by "pedant."
  • Bookworms: Similar because both are deeply immersed in books and learning; differs because a bookworm loves reading for its own sake (usually affectionately), while a pedant uses learning as a weapon of superiority and misses its spirit.
  • Experts: Similar because both possess detailed knowledge; differs because an expert applies knowledge to produce understanding or results, while a pedant hoards details and insists on them without judgement — knowledge without "taste, sense, and spirit."
