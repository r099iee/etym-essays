• Entrusted: Similar because it is the identical word sharing the exact same meaning, etymology, and pronunciation; differs only in modern orthographic convention, as entrusted became the prevailing 20th-century standard while intrusted represents the classical 17th–19th-century legal and literary standard.
  • Confided: Similar because both involve committing something private or precious into another's keeping; differs because confided (from Latin confidere, to have full trust) is strongly associated with sharing secrets, intimate thoughts, and emotional vulnerabilities, whereas intrusted frequently involves formal legal custody, financial property, or official duty.
  • Committed: Similar because both denote placing something into another's custody or charge; differs because committed is a broader, more functional term (e.g. committing someone to prison, committing a body to the earth, committing funds) that does not necessarily carry the deep personal covenant of moral reliance inherent in intrusted.
  • Delegated: Similar because both involve transferring responsibility or authority to a proxy; differs because delegated specifically emphasizes the administrative handover of tasks, decision-making powers, or official duties down a hierarchy, lacking the emotional or moral sanctity of personal trust.
  • Consigned: Similar because both describe delivering an object into the keeping of another; differs because consigned carries a distinct commercial or fatalistic tone (such as consigning goods to a carrier for sale, or consigning an idea to oblivion), often implying that the original owner is washing their hands of the matter.
  • Commended: Similar because both involve entrusting something precious to another's care; differs because commended belongs to a high liturgical, biblical, or formal register (e.g. "into Thy hands I commend my spirit"), often carrying an overtone of praise or prayerful recommendation.
  • Assigned: Similar because both allocate tasks or assets to an individual; differs because assigned is an authoritative, routine administrative act (assigning homework, assigning a beat) that carries none of the delicate vulnerability or fiduciary covenant of being intrusted.
  • Vested: Similar because both grant authority or rights to a person; differs because vested is a technical constitutional and legal term signifying that an absolute, non-forfeitable legal right or sovereign power is permanently placed in someone's hands (e.g. "executive power is vested in the President").
  • Deposited: Similar because both involve placing a valuable into a safe location or hands; differs because deposited is mechanical and financial (such as depositing money in a bank or sediment in a river), lacking the human moral dimension of reliance upon character.
