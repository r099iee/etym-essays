- Late 16th c. — Gian Paolo Lomazzo composes his monumental Trattato dell'arte della pittura (1584) and Idea del tempio della pittura (1590) in Milan after suffering total blindness, becoming the premier theoretical authority for late Mannerist academies.
- 1598 — Translated into English at Oxford by Richard Haydocke as A Tracte Containing the Artes of Curious Paintinge, Carvinge, and Buildinge, exerting a direct, profound influence on Elizabethan and Jacobean court painters, theorists, and miniature portraitists (including Nicholas Hilliard).
- 17th–18th c. — Widely studied across European royal academies (notably the French Académie Royale de Peinture et de Sculpture under Charles Le Brun) as the standard textbook for the rules of proportion, color, light, and the codified expression of human passions.
- 19th c. — Viewed with ambivalence by Romantic critics who rejected late Mannerist academic dogma, yet prized by archival historians for preserving irreplaceable quotes and fragments from Leonardo da Vinci's lost Milanese notebooks.
- 20th–21st c. — Brilliantly re-evaluated in modern Renaissance scholarship (Erwin Panofsky's Idea, Rudolf and Margot Wittkower's Born Under Saturn, Roberto Longhi) as an extraordinary pioneer of artistic psychology, astrological symbolism, and counter-cultural carnival verse.
