• Sullan: Similar because it is the adjective derived from the same name and denotes everything belonging to him — his laws, veterans and colonies; differs in being a common adjective rather than a name, and in describing his works rather than the man.
  • Marius: Similar because he was Sulla's great rival in the same struggle for Rome, a general who also broke constitutional norms; differs because Marius was the popularis outsider and seven-times consul, whereas Sulla was the patrician who made himself dictator and then resigned.
  • Caesar: Similar because both marched on Rome and destroyed the Republic's conventions; differs because Caesar made his dictatorship permanent and was assassinated, while Sulla abdicated and died in bed.
  • Pompey: Similar because he began his career as a Sullan commander and was nicknamed the boy butcher; differs because Pompey never seized supreme power by law and spent his later years defending the Senate against Caesar.
  • dictator: Similar because Sulla held the Roman dictatorship and gave the word its modern colouring; differs because the Roman office was constitutional and, in Sulla's case, formally laid down, whereas the modern word denotes an unelected ruler who keeps power.
  • tyrant: Similar because both words are used of rulers who seize power and rule by force; differs because tyrant carries a moral judgment from Greek political thought, while Sulla is a name and only implies the judgment through his deeds.
  • Cornelius: Similar because it is Sulla's nomen, the clan name he shared with the Scipios and the Lentuli; differs because it identifies the gens, not the man, and is borne by many unrelated Romans.
  • Felix: Similar because it was Sulla's adopted agnomen, “the fortunate”, and is inseparable from his self-presentation; differs in being an adjective meaning lucky or blessed, applicable to anyone, and in pointing to his claim of divine favour rather than to his acts.
  • proscriber: Similar because Sulla instituted the great proscription and his name is bound to the practice; differs because it is a generic agent noun for anyone who outlaws opponents, and carries no historical specificity.
