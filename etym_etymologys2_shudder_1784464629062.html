SHUDDER is an English verb whose pre-Germanic ancestry is uncertain and debated; the honest chain documents what is attested and flags what is not. (Origin uncertain; no secure PIE etymon reconstructed; probably a borrowing from continental Germanic — see note) → Middle Dutch schudden 'to shake, jolt' (attested 13th c.) and/or Middle Low German schodden 'to shake' (attested) → Middle English schoderen / schudderen 'to shake, tremble' (c. 1300; built with the frequentative suffix -eren, as in quaver, totter) → English shudder 'to tremble or shake convulsively' (verb; the fear-and-horror sense established by the 16th–17th c.) → back-formation noun shudder 'a convulsive trembling' (17th c.) → SHUDDER (card form). Note on the debate: the Middle Dutch schudden / Middle Low German schodden family is itself of unclear further origin; some scholars connect it to the Germanic root behind 'shoot' (Proto-Germanic *skeutan- 'to shoot', as if a shudder is a sudden shooting movement), but this is not generally accepted, and no secure PIE etymon has been reconstructed. The Middle English to modern stages, by contrast, are solidly attested.
