• Bell: Similar because both name the instrument or sound of a ringing metal vessel; differs because bell is the generic term for any such instrument, while chime usually implies a musical, patterned, or set-member ringing rather than a single toll.
  • Gong: Similar because both are struck metal instruments producing a ringing sound; differs because a gong is a flat disc with a deep, broad resonance, often ceremonial or East Asian, while a chime is higher, lighter, and more musical.
  • Toll: Similar because both describe the sound of a bell being struck; differs because a toll is slow, solemn, and usually single or funereal (or marking an hour), whereas a chime is quicker, often patterned, and not mournful.
  • Peal: Similar because both involve ringing bells; differs because a peal is a loud full-scale ringing of many bells, while a chime can be a single bell or a short tune.
  • Knell: Similar because both are bell sounds; differs because a knell is specifically the bell rung for death or a funeral, a meaning chime does not carry.
  • Clang: Similar because both are metallic ringing sounds; differs because a clang is harsh, loud, and without musical tuning, while a chime is clear, resonant, and musical.
  • Clash: Similar because both are sounds of struck metal; differs because a clash is discordant and accidental, while a chime is patterned and intentional.
  • Ring: Similar because both describe a clear ringing sound; differs because ring is the generic verb/noun for any bell sound, while chime adds a musical, often set-like quality.
  • Tinkle: Similar because both describe small bell sounds; differs because a tinkle is light, high, and discontinuous, while a chime, even a small one, rings with a sustained musical tone.
  • Jingle: Similar because both are light metallic sounds; differs because a jingle is a rapid succession of small tinkles, while a chime produces a fuller, more tuneful ringing.
  • Carillon: Similar because both involve a set of bells playing music; differs because a carillon is the large keyboard-played instrument of many bells, while a chime is a smaller set or a single bell.
  • Cymbal: Similar because both derive historically from the same bowl-shaped instrument family and produce ringing metal sounds; differs because a cymbal is a flat disc struck to make a crash or shimmer, while a chime is a bell or tuned tube that rings on its own.
