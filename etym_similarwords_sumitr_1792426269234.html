• Kausalyā: Similar because both are virtuous queen consorts of King Daśaratha and devoted mothers of royal princes (Rāma and the twins respectively); differs because Kausalyā is the eldest senior queen whose narrative arc is dominated by deep maternal grief (*śoka*) and tragic endurance, whereas Sumitrā embodies serene philosophical wisdom (*jñāna*) and detached acceptance.
  • Kaikeyī: Similar because both are junior wives of King Daśaratha who play decisive roles in the events of the *Ayodhyākāṇḍa*; differs fundamentally because Kaikeyī succumbs to possessive jealousy, ambition, and fear, demanding Rāma's exile, whereas Sumitrā displays absolute selflessness and sends her own son to serve Rāma in the wild forest.
  • Urmilā: Similar because both are epic women belonging to the royal household of Ayodhyā who make quiet, unsung sacrifices; differs because Urmilā is Sumitrā's daughter-in-law (wife of Lakṣmaṇa) who endures fourteen years of solitary slumber and separation in the palace, while Sumitrā is the queen-mother who counsels and guides the family.
  • Sītā: Similar because both are exemplars of unyielding dharmic fortitude in Vālmīki's *Rāmāyaṇa*; differs because Sītā is the divine heroine and consort of Rāma whose trials, abduction, and fire-ordeal form the central plot of the epic, whereas Sumitrā operates as a wise matriarch in the background.
  • Mandodarī: Similar because both are virtuous queens who offer righteous moral counsel to their husbands in the *Rāmāyaṇa*; differs because Mandodarī is the chief queen of the demon-king Rāvaṇa in Laṅkā whose wise counsel is ignored by her hubristic husband, whereas Sumitrā's wisdom is revered by her sons.
  • Arundhatī: Similar because both are celebrated in Sanskrit literature as paragons of wifely devotion and spiritual wisdom; differs because Arundhatī is the ascetic wife of the sage Vasiṣṭha who lives in a forest hermitage, whereas Sumitrā is a royal queen in the palace of Ayodhyā.
  • Gandhārī: Similar because both are epic queen-mothers who witness devastating dynastic conflicts involving their sons; differs because Gandhārī in the *Mahābhārata* blindfolds herself and helplessly laments the wicked arrogance of her Kaurava sons, whereas Sumitrā actively instills supreme righteousness in Lakṣmaṇa and Śatrughna.
  • Kuntī: Similar because both are noble epic mothers who raise heroic warrior sons; differs because Kuntī in the *Mahābhārata* is plagued by the agonizing secret of her firstborn Karṇa and actively manages the political survival of the Pāṇḍavas, whereas Sumitrā remains detached from political ambition.
  • Anasūyā: Similar because both are venerated for freedom from envy (*an-asūyā*); differs because Anasūyā is an ascetic sage's wife living in Citrakūṭa who grants divine gifts to Sītā, whereas Sumitrā is an Ikṣvāku queen.
  • Śabarī: Similar because both are exemplars of pure spiritual devotion toward Rāma; differs because Śabarī is an aged forest ascetic who awaits Rāma's arrival in devotion, whereas Sumitrā is a royal mother whose devotion is expressed through the noble upbringing of her sons.
