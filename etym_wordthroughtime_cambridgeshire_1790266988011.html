- 8th–10th c. (Old English Grantebrycgscīr) - Anglo-Saxon administrative territory - Established as the shire jurisdiction surrounding the strategic bridge crossing on the River Granta.
- 1086 (Domesday Book Cantebrigiscire) - Norman feudal county - Recorded by Norman surveyors following sound changes from Grante- to Cante-.
- 13th–17th c. - Academic and commercial county - Renowned globally for the University of Cambridge and the thriving agricultural trade of Ely and the Fens.
- 1839 - Inauguration of the Cambridgeshire Handicap - Elevated into an iconic sporting fixture in British flat horse racing at Newmarket.
- Early 20th c. - Turf culture and comedic literature - Frequently referenced in P.G. Wodehouse and Edwardian fiction as a defining cultural milestone for bets and social seasons.
- Late 20th–21st c. - "Silicon Fen" and ceremonial county - Celebrated internationally for premier scientific innovation, biotechnology, and agricultural heritage.
