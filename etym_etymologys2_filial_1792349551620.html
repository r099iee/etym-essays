Note on semantic evolution: The semantic trajectory of filial provides an extraordinary window into ancient kinship cognition. The Proto-Indo-European verbal root *dʰeh₁(y)- ("to suckle") originally described the physical physiological act of lactation and maternal nursing. In Proto-Italic, the derived noun *feīlyos literally designated "the suckling one" or "the one at the breast." Over centuries of Roman cultural institutionalization, this intimate physiological descriptor broadened into the primary legal and social terms for offspring (fīlius, "son"; fīlia, "daughter"), simultaneously shedding its purely physical connotation to become the anchor of Roman family law (patria potestas) and ethical piety (pietas).
