• Speak plainly: similar because it means saying what one means without ornament; differs because it stresses clarity rather than the naming of an awkward thing.
  • Call things by their proper names: similar because it insists on exact naming; differs because it is more formal and lacks the homely image of the spade.
  • Mince no words: similar because it means not softening one’s language; differs because it emphasises the absence of restraint rather than the accuracy of the label.
  • Not mince matters: similar because it means dealing with a subject bluntly; differs because it is a slightly dated British formulation and focuses on the issue rather than the word.
  • Tell it like it is: similar because it claims unvarnished honesty; differs because it is American, conversational, and often implies a whole attitude of straight talking.
  • Be blunt: similar because it means speaking without tact; differs because bluntness can be careless, whereas the idiom usually implies deliberate accuracy.
  • Be direct: similar because it avoids evasion; differs because directness is neutral about delicacy, while the idiom often concerns indelicate subjects.
  • Be frank: similar because it means open and honest speech; differs because frankness is a general disposition, not a specific act of naming.
  • Be candid: similar because it means truthful disclosure; differs because it is more formal and often implies revealing something about oneself.
  • Be forthright: similar because it means straightforward and forceful; differs because it stresses strength of character rather than the refusal of euphemism.
  • Lay it on the line: similar because it means stating something plainly and risking offence; differs because it often implies a warning or ultimatum.
  • Say it straight: similar because it means without embellishment; differs because it is colloquial and less tied to naming a taboo subject.
  • Call a fig a fig: similar because it is the Greek ancestor of the same idea; differs because it is learned and archaic, and few recognise it.
  • Give it to someone straight: similar because it means honest, unsoftened speech; differs because it is directed at a person and often implies delivering criticism.
  • Pull no punches: similar because it means unrestrained criticism; differs because it is about force, not lexical precision.
  • Name the elephant in the room: similar because it means raising an avoided truth; differs because it concerns a shared silence, not the choice of a blunt word.
