• Command: Similar because it directs or enjoins an action; differs because command is blunt, military, and unambiguous, while bid softens authority with the residual politeness of its ancient "to ask" meaning and sounds courtly rather than harsh.
  • Order: Similar because it tells someone to do something; differs because order is a formal, sometimes harsh directive issued by rank, whereas bid is an elevated, courteous summons that preserves the fiction of a request.
  • Direct: Similar because it gives guidance or instructions for action; differs because direct is neutral and managerial, lacking both the archaic dignity of bid and its connotation of a person in high authority speaking.
  • Instruct: Similar because it imparts an order or guidance; differs because instruct emphasises teaching or procedure (how to do something), while bid is a pure command or invitation without didactic content.
  • Enjoin: Similar because it urgently orders or prohibits an action; differs because enjoin is legalistic and formal (to enjoin by injunction), whereas bid is archaic-poetic and courteous rather than juridical.
  • Charge: Similar because it lays a duty or responsibility on someone; differs because charge is weighty and moral (to charge with a mission), while bid is lighter, more ceremonial, and closer to a polite summons.
  • Summon: Similar because it calls someone to attend or appear; differs because summon stresses official or urgent calling (a summons to court), whereas bid implies invitation or courteous command without legal force.
  • Invite: Similar because it asks someone to come or do something; differs because invite is neutral, symmetrical, and optional, while bid carries superior authority and a ceremonial formality the modern verb has lost.
  • Request: Similar because it asks for something; differs because request is the humble, symmetrical modern word and retains the oldest sense of biddan, while bid has risen to a superior's word for directing others.
  • Ask: Similar because it is the direct descendant of the ancestor sense of bid (Old English biddan meant "to ask"); differs because ask is neutral between equals, while bid has climbed the social ladder and now belongs to authority, except in fossil phrases like "bid farewell."
  • Demand: Similar because it insists on something being done; differs because demand is forceful and peremptory, the very opposite of bid's courteous compulsion, and carries none of the archaic politeness.
  • Entreat: Similar because it requests earnestly, preserving the ancient "pray/beseech" meaning of biddan; differs because entreat is used of a suppliant towards a superior, while bid is used by a superior towards an inferior.
  • Offer: Similar because it is the everyday counterpart of the auction sense of bidding a price; differs because offer is broad and neutral, while bid as noun and verb is the precise technical term of auctions, commerce, and card games.
