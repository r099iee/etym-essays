• Odious: Similar because it also provokes strong negative feeling; differs because odious describes what is hated for its intrinsic repulsiveness or detestableness, whereas invidious stirs resentment because it appears unjustly preferential — the sting is in the inequality, not in the object's badness.
  • Objectionable: Similar because it is also displeasing; differs because objectionable is far milder — merely open to objection — while invidious carries the wound of unfair discrimination and the envious look it draws.
  • Discriminatory: Similar because it also treats persons unequally; differs because discriminatory denotes systematically prejudicial treatment in modern legal usage, while invidious names the emotional consequence — the rankled resentment — that such treatment stirs, and it is the adjective in the legal collocation "invidious distinction."
  • Envious: Similar because it shares the same Latin root family; differs because envious describes the person who feels envy, whereas invidious describes the act or remark that causes resentment: one is the emotion, the other the provocation.
  • Unfair: Similar because it names an unjust judgment; differs because unfair describes the quality of the judgment itself without the emotional consequence, whereas invidious carries the rankled feeling of the singled-out onlooker inside it.
  • Partial: Similar because it names a biased preference; differs because partial can be merely descriptive of a leaning or a preference, while invidious adds the note that the preference is perceived as wounding and provokes ill-will.
  • Galling: Similar because it describes something that irritates and offends; differs because galling describes the chafing feeling caused, whereas invidious names the provoking quality of the act itself — its power to single out and sting.
  • Offensive: Similar because it gives displeasure or resentment; differs because offensive is much broader — anything that affronts — while invidious is specific to the resentment aroused by apparently unjust preference or discrimination.
