• Harvest: Similar because both name gathering the ripe crop and its rewards; differs because harvest is the whole season and yield (noun and verb) while reap stresses the cutting and collecting act and its moral consequences.
  • Gather: Similar because reapers gather sheaves and rewards alike; differs because gather is general collecting of anything while reap implies a sown crop and a deserved outcome.
  • Mow: Similar because mowing cuts grass and grain as reaping does; differs because mow is the cutting stroke itself (lawns included) while reap includes gathering the harvest home.
  • Glean: Similar because both belong to the harvest field; differs because glean is picking leftover ears after the reapers, humble and secondary, while reap is the main harvest itself.
  • Garner: Similar because garnering stores grain and praise as reaping wins them; differs because garner stresses storing up in the granary while reap stresses cutting and obtaining.
  • Collect: Similar because both obtain and bring in; differs because collect is neutral assembly of items or debts while reap carries the seed-to-harvest logic of earned consequence.
  • Obtain: Similar because to reap profits is to obtain them; differs because obtain is formal and effort-neutral while reap insists the gain grew from prior sowing.
  • Earn: Similar because earned rewards are reaped rewards; differs because earn stresses meriting through labour while reap stresses receiving the matured yield, for good or ill.
  • Mow down: Similar because the scythe cuts men like wheat in war poetry; differs because mow down is violent mass killing while reap keeps, even in its Grim Reaper sense, a solemn harvest dignity.
