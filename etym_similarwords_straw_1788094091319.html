• Hay: Similar because both are dried plant material used to feed or bed animals; differs because hay is whole grass or legumes cut green and cured as fodder, while straw is the leftover stalks after the grain has been threshed — drier, less nutritious, and used mainly for bedding, thatch, and packing.
  • Chaff: Similar because both are threshing residues of the harvest; differs because chaff is the light husks and scales winnowed off the grain — finer, looser, and proverbially worthless ("separate the wheat from the chaff") — while straw is the stiff stalks.
  • Stubble: Similar because both are post-harvest stalk remnants; differs because stubble is the short cut stalks left standing in the field after reaping, not gathered or used as straw is.
  • Haulm: Similar because both are dried plant stems; differs because haulm (chiefly British dialect) is the stems of peas, beans, or potatoes — not cereal stalks — and has no idiom family.
  • Husk: Similar because both are outer coverings of grain; differs because husks are the small outer coats of the kernels (or of fruit), loose and fine, while straw is the long stalk; the words share the "worthless residue" flavour but little else.
  • Litter: Similar because both are bedding materials for animals; differs because litter is the general category (straw, shavings, sand) and also a brood of newborn animals, while straw is one specific material among several.
  • Fodder / Forage: Similar because both are feed for livestock; differs because fodder is the functional category (hay, silage, grain) and straw is only poor, low-nutrition fodder of last resort; straw's main jobs are non-feeding ones.
  • Thatch: Similar because both denote roofing material of dried vegetation; differs because thatch is the roof covering and the craft (of reed, straw, or heather), while straw is one material among several; "thatch" is never a tube, a man of straw, or a poll.
  • Reed: Similar because both are hollow-stemmed plants put to similar uses — roofing, weaving, tubing; differs because reed is a specific wetland grass (Phragmites) with its own etymology (Old English hrēod), and it is a musical instrument part, which straw never is.
  • Trifle / Jot: Similar because all three are the standard of triviality in "not worth a ___"; differs because trifle and jot are abstract standards of smallness while straw is a concrete cheap object; the straw idiom is homelier and older than "jot" (from Greek iota) and blunter than "trifle".
