- Old French fourrager era (pre-English) — Perceived as military-logistical: ranging for horse feed and army food; associated with campaign necessity and potential coercion of peasants.
- Late Middle English foragen / foraged — Enters English with that martial provisioning sense; chroniclers' word for supply raids and countryside collection.
- Early Modern–18th century — Still primarily war, siege, and colonial expedition vocabulary; "foraged" narrates what armies did to landscapes.
- 19th century perception shift — Naturalists and novelists apply foraging/foraged to animals and to human wilderness survival; the word begins to sound ecological as well as military.
- Late 20th–21st century — Split prestige: disaster/survival foraged (gritty) vs culinary foraged (artisanal, Instagram-era wild food). Public perception of the exact form foraged now often triggers foodie "wild-harvested" before "looted for the cavalry," though historical writing keeps the older sense available. No taboo; positive eco-connotation is currently strong in lifestyle media.
