• Hollows: Similar because both name dipped places in rolling ground; differs because a hollow is simply sunken, while a fold suggests draped, enfolding slopes that wrap what they hold.
  • Valleys: Similar because valleys are the low ground between heights; differs because valley is the geographer's scale-word for whole systems, while folds are intimate creases within them.
  • Dells: Similar because a dell is a small sheltered wooded hollow; differs because dell is smaller, woodier, and more secret, while folds can roll open and pastoral.
  • Combes: Similar because a combe is a short valley in chalk or moorland; differs because combe is dialectally West Country and geologically specific, while folds is general literary English.
  • Creases: Similar because both are bends where a surface doubles back; differs because crease is sharp and small (paper, cloth, skin), while folds in land are broad and soft.
  • Sheepfolds: Similar because the sheepfold is the ancestral noun behind the word; differs because sheepfold names the built pen of hurdles or stone, while folds of land are the earth's own enclosures.
  • Pleats: Similar because pleats are regular pressed folds in fabric; differs because pleat is tailored and deliberate, while land-folds are natural and irregular.
  • Wrinkles: Similar because both describe creased surfaces on skin or maps; differs because wrinkle carries age or accident, while folds suggest designed drapery or shelter.
