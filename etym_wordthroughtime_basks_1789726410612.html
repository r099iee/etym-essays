- Late medieval wallow - basken means to wallow in warm water or blood; perception visceral, battlefield and bathhouse, bodies soaked.
- Shakespearean sunshine turn (1600) - bask'd him in the sun in As You Like It; connotation of idle fool happy in Fortune's glow, warmth replaces water.
- 17th–18th century genteel leisure - ladies bask, travellers bask in Italian sun; perception of Grand Tour ease, healthful airing.
- Victorian natural history - basking sharks, basking lizards and seals; scientific yet affectionate, cold-blooded joy observed.
- Contemporary glow-word - streams bask in gold, winners bask in glory; from blood to sun to praise, retaining passive blissful exposure, as in streams basking in waning gold.
