PIE (reconstructed Proto-Indo-European)
*keglo- / *kagle-
"pebble, small stone, hailstone"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*haglaz / *hagla-
"hail, hailstorm"
↓
Old English (c. 700–1100 CE)
hægl / hagal
"hail, precipitation of ice pellets; the runic letter H"
↓
Middle English (c. 1150–1450)
hayle / haile / hail
"hail, hailstorm; shower of missiles"
↓
Modern English (standard weather noun and verb)
Hail
"pellets of frozen rain; a barrage; personified winter storm"

Note: The precipitation word hail is of native Germanic descent (Old English hægl). It is etymologically distinct from the homophonous greeting and verb to hail (e.g. "hail a cab", "hail the king"), which was borrowed from Old Norse heill ("healthy, whole, sound").
