- [14th-15th century the small book] - [Enters meaning a small, unbound booklet devoted to a single subject; connotation neutral — the medium of one argument]
- [16th-17th century weapon of controversy] - [Becomes the standard weapon of polemic and religious controversy — cheap to print, quick to circulate, striking while the argument is hot; the 'pamphlet war' is born]
- [18th-19th century age of the squib] - [The great age of the political and critical pamphlet; Ruskin's intended 'short pamphlet' signals modest, pugnacious intention — the angry voice in a pamphlet war, contrasted with the five-volume treatise it unexpectedly became]
- [20th-21st century slow eclipse] - [Displaced in everyday use by report, brief, and leaflet, but survives with its old character: a short, pointed, usually argumentative booklet for a single cause]
