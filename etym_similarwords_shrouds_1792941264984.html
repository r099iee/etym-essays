• Veils: Similar because both are thin coverings that half hide what lies beneath; differs because a veil is light, often bridal or religious, and meant to be seen through, while shrouds are heavier, greyer, and touched with death and weather.
  • Winding-sheets: Similar because both wrap the dead for burial; differs because winding-sheet names only the funeral linen itself, while shrouds extends to mist, rigging, secrecy, and machinery.
  • Palls: Similar because both drape the dead and darken the living (a pall of smoke); differs because a pall is the cloth spread over a coffin or the gloom itself, while a shroud wraps round the body or thing.
  • Mantles: Similar because both cloak hills and shoulders (a mantle of snow); differs because mantle is dignified and often warm or honoured, while shrouds keep a chill of concealment and the grave.
  • Cloaks: Similar because both cover and conceal the wearer; differs because a cloak is worn deliberately for warmth or disguise, while shrouds of mist or secrecy settle without anyone choosing them.
  • Rigging: Similar because ships' shrouds are part of the rigging; differs because rigging is the whole system of ropes, sails, and spars, while shrouds name only the lateral standing ropes that steady the masts.
  • Stays: Similar because both are standing ropes bracing a mast; differs because stays run fore-and-aft along the ship's length, while shrouds run athwartships from masthead to the rails on either side.
  • Wrappings: Similar because both enclose a thing in covering layers; differs because wrappings are everyday and neutral (paper, cloth), while shrouds are solemn, literary, or technical, never used for parcels.
  • Covers: Similar because both hide or protect what lies beneath; differs because covers is the plain general word, while shrouds adds poetry, mist, mourning, or seamanship to the act of covering.
