Old High German (9th c.)
thunkōn
“to dip, plunge in”
↓
Middle High German (12th–14th c.)
dunken, tunken
“to dip bread, immerse”
↓
Pennsylvania German (18th–19th c.)
dunke
“to dip food in drink”
↓
American English (1919)
dunk
“to dip doughnuts, plunge”
↓
Modern English (current)
dunk
“dip; basketball slam”

Note: deeper origin beyond Old High German is debated; handbooks suggest a possible link to PIE *teng-, to moisten, but several scholars treat thunkōn as imitative or of obscure origin, so the chain above starts where attestation is firm.
