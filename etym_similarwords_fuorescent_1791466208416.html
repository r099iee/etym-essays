• Fluorescent: the correctly spelled form of the same word, identical in meaning; differs only orthographically, by retaining the l of fluor.
  • Luminescent: the broad category term for emitting light not caused by heat; differs by covering fluorescence, phosphorescence and chemiluminescence alike.
  • Phosphorescent: similar in glowing after absorbing light; differs because the glow persists after the source is removed, whereas fluorescence stops almost at once.
  • Incandescent: similar in being a light source; differs by producing light through heat, as in a tungsten filament, and by carrying connotations of warmth rather than clinical harshness.
  • Glowing: the plain everyday word for emitting light; differs by being vague about mechanism and often positive in tone.
  • Radiant: similar in giving out light; differs by suggesting warmth, beauty or happiness rather than a physical process.
  • Neon: similar as a bright artificial light source; differs by referring to gas-discharge signage and its colours, not to phosphor-converted ultraviolet.
  • Day-glo: similar in describing lurid artificial colour; differs as a commercial/street term for pigments that glow under UV, with no physics precision.
  • Vivid: similar in intense colour; differs by describing saturation of colour, not emission of light.
  • Garish: similar in the negative judgement of harsh brightness; differs as a purely evaluative word with no technical sense.
  • Iridescent: similar in shifting, unnatural colour; differs because iridescence is interference and reflection, not absorption and re-emission.
  • Electroluminescent: similar in converting electricity to light; differs by the mechanism (direct electrical excitation) and its association with LEDs and EL panels.
