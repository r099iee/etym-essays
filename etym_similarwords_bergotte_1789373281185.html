• Elstir: Similar because both are fictional genius artists created by Marcel Proust in In Search of Lost Time who serve as aesthetic mentors to the young narrator Marcel; differs because Elstir represents the visual art of impressionist painting (modeled on Claude Monet, James McNeill Whistler, and Paul-César Helleu), whereas Bergotte represents literature and the melodic architecture of French prose.
  • Vinteuil: Similar because both belong to Proust’s sacred triad of artistic creators whose work reveals the transcendent essence of reality; differs because Vinteuil is a secluded, tragic composer whose music (notably the evocative 'little phrase' of his sonata) communicates non-verbal, emotional truths through sound, whereas Bergotte communicates philosophical and descriptive truths through written language.
  • Adrian Leverkühn: Similar because both are monumental fictional artists in modern European literature whose creative struggles embody the crisis and tragedy of modern art; differs because Leverkühn is Thomas Mann’s tragic protagonist in Doctor Faustus, who strikes a demonic pact for musical genius, whereas Bergotte is a Proustian figure dedicated to classicist refinement and lyrical aestheticism.
  • Anatole France: Similar because Anatole France provided the direct historical, biographical, and stylistic inspiration upon which Proust constructed the fictional author; differs because Anatole France was a living, flesh-and-blood historical novelist and Nobel laureate, while Bergotte is an idealized fictional creation existing within the narrative fabric of Proust’s novel.
  • Stephen Dedalus: Similar because both are central literary figures in high-modernist fiction who represent the artist's dedication to aesthetic form; differs because Stephen Dedalus in James Joyce’s works is a struggling, youthful autobiographical avatar seeking artistic self-exile, whereas Bergotte is depicted as an established, aging master who achieves canonical greatness before his death.
