• Goethean: Similar because it derives from the man and belongs to the same cultural field; differs because it names a manner, worldview, or morphology of thought (Goethean science, Goethean optics) rather than the person and his oeuvre.
  • Faustian: Similar because it is inspired by his drama and carries his cultural weight; differs because it names restless, overreaching striving and the bargain motif, not the surname or the man himself.
  • Weimar Classicism: Similar because it is inseparable from his mature career; differs because it is a period term for a phase of German literature shared with Schiller, not a personal name.
  • Sturm und Drang: Similar because it marks the movement of his youthful fame; differs because it is an earlier German literary movement, one phase of Goethe's development, not a label for the man.
  • Schiller: Similar because he was Goethe's near-contemporary Weimar partner and co-founder of Weimar Classicism; differs because he is a distinct writer and dramatist, not a substitute for the name Goethe on a list of almost-clean artists.
  • Shakespeare: Similar because both function as absolutes of literary genius in European culture; differs because Shakespeare is the English (often the universal) benchmark while Goethe is the modern continental universal man of letters and science.
  • Byron: Similar because he is the other name in Pater's list of near-absolute artists; differs because Byron signals English Romantic intensity and celebrity while Goethe signals German breadth, longevity, and integrative intellect.
  • Weltliteratur: Similar because the concept is attached to Goethe's name and fame; differs because it names his idea of world literature and international circulation, not the person.
  • Universal genius / polymath: Similar because it describes what Goethe is held to be; differs because it is a generic descriptive phrase applicable to Leonardo or Humboldt, not a proper name anchored to one man.
