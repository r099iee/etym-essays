• Cushion: the generic padded support; similar as the base of the compound, but a cushion is for sitting or leaning, whereas a pin-cushion is specifically a receptacle for pins.
  • Pad: a soft, stuffed mass used for protection or support; broader and more neutral, without the specialised sewing function.
  • Pillow: a cloth case stuffed for resting the head; similar construction, but domestic and bed-related rather than a pin repository.
  • Bolster: a long, firm cylindrical cushion; similar stuffing and support, but its shape and scale are quite different.
  • Hassock: a thick cushion for kneeling or resting the feet; similar padded object, but used on the floor and in church or furniture contexts.
  • Beanbag: a fabric bag filled with loose pellets; similar soft filling, but designed as seating rather than for holding pins.
  • Needle-book: a small folded case for needles; functionally similar in sewing, but it stores needles flat in leaves rather than sticking them into a pad.
  • Emery bag: a small bag of abrasive powder for cleaning needles; often attached to a pin-cushion, but its purpose is sharpening, not storage.
  • Sewing kit: a complete set of needles, thread, and scissors; a pin-cushion is one component of such a kit, not the whole collection.
  • Target: figurative for a person or thing repeatedly attacked; similar to the “human pin-cushion” metaphor, but without the image of physical piercing.
