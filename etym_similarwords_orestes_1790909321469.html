• Electra: Similar because both are children of Agamemnon who conspire to avenge their father's assassination; differs because Electra embodies prolonged domestic mourning, obsessive grief, and psychological instigation from within the palace, whereas Orestes is the exiled physical executor of the matricidal act.
  • Hamlet: Similar because both are royal sons visited by a mandate to avenge a murdered father whose throne and marital bed have been usurped; differs because Shakespeare's Hamlet is paralyzed by intellectual introspection, philosophical melancholy, and existential hesitation, whereas Orestes operates within the deterministic framework of divine Hellenic oracles and direct ritual action.
  • Oedipus: Similar because both are iconic Greek tragic heroes burdened by ancestral house curses (the House of Atreus and the House of Cadmus) and catastrophic familial blood-guilt; differs because Oedipus commits parricide and incest unwittingly in tragic ignorance of his identity, whereas Orestes slays his mother fully conscious of her identity under direct divine command.
  • Pylades: Similar because both share the perilous journey to Argos and the execution of the vengeance plot; differs because Pylades is the steadfast, unflinching companion and moral anchor whose brief, pivotal speech in Aeschylus reminds Orestes of Apollo's oracle, rather than the tormented bearer of blood-guilt.
  • Avenger: Similar because both denote an agent who inflicts retribution for a severe grievance or crime; differs because 'avenger' is an abstract, generic descriptive role, whereas 'Orestes' carries the profound mythological weight of matricide, divine madness, the pursuit of the Furies, and trial by jury.
