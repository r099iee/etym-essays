- 4th–3rd c. BCE — Arpinum is a Volscian hill stronghold above the Liris, known to Rome through the Volscian wars and captured in 305 BCE.
- 3rd–1st c. BCE — after receiving citizenship without the vote in 303 and full citizenship in 188, it is a Roman municipium; the name denotes a loyal Italian town rather than a great city.
- 1st c. BCE — Marius and Cicero make Arpinum a byword for Italian talent and the new man; Arpinas becomes both a badge of pride and an aristocratic sneer.
- Middle Ages — the town survives as Arpino, a hilltop commune; the ancient name recedes into Latin charters and the memory of Cicero.
- Renaissance–18th c. — humanists and travellers return to Cicero's Arpinum, seeking the landscape of De Legibus and the statesman's birthplace.
- 19th–21st c. — Arpino is an Italian municipality and Arpinum a scholarly name for the ancient town, kept alive mainly by Cicero studies and by the town's Roman walls.
