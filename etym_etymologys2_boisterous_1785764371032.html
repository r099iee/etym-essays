BOISTEROUS entered English from Anglo-Norman French; its deeper origin is genuinely uncertain and must be stated honestly. The chain: 1) (Origin of the French form, uncertain and debated) — Old French / Anglo-Norman boistous (also boisteus, boisteux) “rough, coarse, clumsy, fierce” (adjective; its deeper etymology is disputed — see the NOTE below; proposed sources include a connection with Old French boiste “box”, from Latin buxis, from Greek pyxís “a box”, via the idea of awkward stiffness, or with Old French bois “wood”, from Latin boscus, with the sense of rustic roughness — none of these is universally accepted) — attested in Old French and Anglo-Norman from the 12th–13th c. 2) Middle English boistous / boystous “rough, coarse, clumsy, violent, fierce” (borrowed from Anglo-Norman; used of coarse cloth, rude manners, and violent force) — attested from c. 15th c. 3) Early Modern English boisterous (the form reshaped with the inserted -er-, a re-syllabification common in early modern English) “rough, violent, turbulent; loud, noisy, high-spirited” — attested 16th–17th c. 4) Modern English boisterous “noisy, energetic, unruly, and wildly cheerful or stormy” — BOISTEROUS. NOTE ON DEBATE: The English side of the chain — Middle English boistous from Anglo-Norman boistous, reshaped to boisterous in early modern English — is secure and fully documented. The genuinely uncertain point is the deeper origin of the Anglo-Norman/Old French adjective itself. Three hypotheses have been advanced, none with universal assent: (1) a connection with Old French boiste “box” (Latin buxis ← Greek pyxís “box”), with the sense “stiff, awkward as a box, cramped”; (2) a connection with Old French bois “wood” (Latin boscus ← Germanic *busk-), with the sense “woody, rough, uncultivated”; (3) a connection with Old French boisteux “lame, limping”, with the sense “awkward, unsteady”. Because no single proposal has won general acceptance, responsible etymology begins the secure record at the Anglo-Norman stage and marks the deeper origin as unknown, rather than inventing a false PIE pedigree.
