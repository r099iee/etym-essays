• show: Similar because both make something visible; differs because show is the plain everyday verb making no claim about certainty, whereas evince asserts proof beyond doubt.
  • display: Similar because both exhibit something; differs because display implies deliberate arrangement for an audience, while evince claims an involuntary, demonstrable presence.
  • demonstrate: Similar because both prove by evidence; differs because demonstrate is methodical, step-by-step and procedural, too logical for something as unselfconscious as a passion.
  • betray: Similar because both reveal involuntarily; differs because betray adds exposure against one’s will and fits a hidden secret rather than an openly held quality.
  • reveal: Similar because both bring to light; differs because reveal can disclose anything hidden, neutral in register, while evince is formal, forensic and restricted to evidence-based showing.
  • indicate: Similar because both point to something; differs because indicate is weak, tentative and merely suggestive, far below evince’s claim of demonstrable certainty.
  • manifest: Similar because both make something evident; differs because manifest is more solemn and can be used of things that become obvious of themselves, while evince stresses the act of showing by evidence.
  • exhibit: Similar because both display an outward sign; differs because exhibit implies deliberate putting-on-show of a quality, closer to display than to evince’s proof-by-evidence.
  • evidence (verb): Similar because both ground a claim in proof; differs because to evidence something is to supply the supporting material, while to evince is the quality itself showing through the material.
  • prove: Similar because both establish beyond doubt; differs because prove requires formal demonstration of a proposition, while evince asserts that a quality is readable from outward signs.
