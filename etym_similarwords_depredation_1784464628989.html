• Plundering: Similar because both name the seizure of goods by force; differs because plundering is the active physical looting, while depredation names the act as a grave, often recorded ravaging with stress on the resulting ruin.
  • Pillaging: Similar because both denote spoiling a place or people; differs because pillaging emphasizes stripping a town of valuables during war, while depredation is broader and often used in the plural for a chronic series.
  • Looting: Similar because both involve seizure of goods; differs because looting is informal, often post-disaster or mob seizure, whereas depredation carries a legal-historical or formal gravity.
  • Ravaging: Similar because both mean violent destructive laying waste; differs because ravaging stresses the violent damage itself, while depredation stresses the predatory taking that causes it.
  • Despoliation: Similar because both name the stripping of possessions; differs because despoliation is a rarer, purely learned word for spoiling, while depredation is in wider general and technical use.
  • Spoliation: Similar because both denote wrongful taking of property; differs because spoliation is especially legal and art-historical (the looting of artworks and documents), while depredation covers military, natural, and animal damage.
  • Devastation: Similar because both denote wide ruin; differs because devastation stresses total destruction and emptiness, while depredation emphasizes predatory pillaging as the cause.
  • Sacking: Similar because both denote the storming and stripping of a town; differs because sacking is the concrete military event, while depredation is the formal noun of record for such acts generally.
  • Raid: Similar because both involve hostile seizure; differs because a raid is the sudden attack itself (often a single event), while depredation is the ravaging consequence viewed as a crime or calamity.
  • Predation: Similar because both denote the taking of prey; differs because predation is the modern scientific term for animals feeding on animals, while depredation retains the formal, ruinous, historical weight.
  • Damage: Similar because both denote harm; differs because damage is neutral and general, while depredation implies deliberate, predatory, often systematic ravaging.
