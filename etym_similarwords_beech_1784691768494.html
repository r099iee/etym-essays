• Birch: Similar because it is a smooth-barked hardwood of the northern woods, often confused with beech; differs because birch bark peels in papery white sheets and its timber is lighter and quick-burning, while beech is closer-grained, heavier, and better for turnery and lasting fire.
  • Oak: Similar because it is a great English timber and mast tree of the woodland; differs because oak is far heavier and more durable, with gnarled fissured bark, and was the shipwright’s and builder’s wood, while beech served the craftsman and the cook.
  • Hornbeam: Similar because it is a dense, smooth-trunked tree of the same family (Carpinus, in the Fagales) with hard wood; differs because hornbeam is still harder and is chiefly used for mill-work and cog-wheels, while beech is the all-round wood of furniture, bowls, and fire.
  • Fagus: Similar because it is the scientific genus name for the beech; differs because “fagus” (from Latin fāgus) is the technical botanical label, while “beech” is the native English tree-name.
  • Book: Similar because it is the doublet of beech from the same Germanic root (both from the writing-tablet sense of *bōkō); differs because “book” has long since diverged completely into “a written work,” while “beech” names the tree and its wood.
  • Mast: Similar because both belong to the old forest economy of feeding pigs and game; differs because “mast” is the collective name for the fallen nuts (beech-mast, acorns) of forest trees, while “beech” is the tree that bears one kind of mast.
  • Wood: Similar because beech is a kind of wood; differs because “wood” is the general material name, while “beech” names the specific hardwood tree and its timber.
  • Copper beech: Similar because it is a variety of the same species; differs because the copper (purple) beech is a cultivated colour mutant with deep purple leaves, prized as an ornamental, while “beech” names the ordinary green-leaved tree.
