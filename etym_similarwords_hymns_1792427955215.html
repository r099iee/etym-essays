• psalms: Similar because both are sacred lyrical songs of praise and prayer addressed to God; differs because psalms specifically designates the 150 canonical poems of the Hebrew Bible (Book of Psalms / Tehillim) and their strict liturgical settings, whereas hymns encompasses non-canonical, metrical, and newly composed songs of worship across global faiths.
  • canticles: Similar because both are liturgical chants of adoration; differs because canticles are non-metrical biblical prose songs drawn from scriptural texts outside the Book of Psalms (such as the Magnificat, Benedictus, or Song of Moses), chanted responsively in canonical hours.
  • carols: Similar because both are religious songs sung by congregations; differs because carols are festive, joyous seasonal songs with dance-like meters traditionally associated with specific calendar feasts (particularly Christmas and Easter), whereas hymns are solemn, doctrinally weighty, and sung year-round.
  • anthems: Similar because both are choral or congregational musical pieces expressing reverence; differs because anthems are typically elaborate choral compositions set to prose texts with instrumental accompaniment for performance by a trained choir, or secular patriotic pieces (national anthems).
  • odes: Similar because both are formal lyric poems celebrating a noble subject; differs because an ode is a classical poetic genre celebrating athletic champions, statesmen, or abstract ideals (e.g., Keats's *Ode on a Grecian Urn*) without inherent musical congregational worship or theological worship.
  • stotras: Similar because both are devotional hymns of praise chanted to deities; differs because stotra is the specific Sanskrit and Hindu genre of melodic, metrical laudatory praise (such as the *Viṣṇu Sahasranāma* or *Śiva Tāṇḍava Stotra*), whereas hymn is the universal English and Judeo-Christian term.
  • doxologies: Similar because both offer praise to divine majesty; differs because a doxology is a short, formulaic liturgical ejaculation of praise to the Trinity (e.g., the *Gloria Patri* or "Praise God from whom all blessings flow") functioning as a conclusion rather than a freestanding multi-stanza song.
  • paeans: Similar because both are songs of joyous adoration; differs because a paean historically originated as a Greek choral ode of thanksgiving for deliverance or victory addressed to Apollo, carrying a triumphantly martial and celebratory character.
  • spirituals: Similar because both are Christian sacred songs; differs because spirituals (Negro spirituals) are folk-religious oral compositions forged under American plantation slavery, combining biblical typology with coded desires for physical and spiritual liberation, whereas hymns are structured, written, metrical works.
  • encomiums: Similar because both express high, lavish praise; differs because an encomium is a rhetorical, spoken prose oration in praise of a person or deed, lacking musical melody, stanzaic meter, and congregational singing.
  • dirges: Similar because both are solemn ritual songs; differs because a dirge is an expression of mourning, grief, and funeral lamentation, whereas hymns primarily celebrate adoration, glory, and divine majesty.
