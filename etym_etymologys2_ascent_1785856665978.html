PIE *skand- 'to leap, spring, dart, climb' (reconstructed; attested across the family — Sanskrit skándati 'leaps, springs', Greek skandalon 'stumbling-block, trap' from the notion of springing) —>
Latin scandere 'to climb, mount, ascend' (attested in classical Latin) —>
Latin ascendere 'to climb up, mount, rise' (compound of ad-, 'to, toward', assimilated as as- before sc-, + scandere; attested in classical Latin) —>
Latin ascēnsus 'an ascending, a climbing up' (noun formed from the perfect passive participle stem ascēns- of ascendere; attested in classical and later Latin) —>
English ascent 'the act of rising' (formed in English c. 1610 from ascend on the model of descend / descent; the spelling ascent settled in the 17th century) —>
ASCENT 'an act of rising or climbing upward; progress to higher status or power' ◄ TARGET.

Debate note: the noun was formed in English by analogy with descent — ascend : ascent :: descend : descent — rather than borrowed whole from Latin or French (French had ascension); this analogical formation is the account given by the OED, though older sources sometimes derived it directly from Latin ascensus via French. The PIE root *skand- for Latin scandere is generally accepted; the Sanskrit and Greek cognates are widely cited, though their precise ablaut relationships are debated.
