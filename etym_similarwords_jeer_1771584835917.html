• Taunt: Similar because both denote mocking, derisive remarks aimed at someone; differs because a taunt is aimed and verbal — a specific insult hurled at a specific person ("they taunted him with his cowardice") — whereas a jeer is often a wordless or near-wordless collective noise, a howl or hoot of contempt from many throats at once.
  • Scoff: Similar because both express mockery and derision; differs because scoff expresses contemptuous disbelief, often at an idea or a claim rather than a person, and is quieter and more intellectual — a sarcastic remark or a curled lip — whereas a jeer is loud, public, collective noise.
  • Deride: Similar because both mean to mock and ridicule; differs because deride is more formal and reasoned mockery, often of a person's claims or efforts from a position of intellectual contempt, while a jeer is vulgar, loud, and usually collective, the shout of a crowd rather than the judgement of an individual.
  • Sneer: Similar because both express contempt; differs because a sneer is silent and facial — a curled lip, a contemptuous smile that asserts superiority without a sound — whereas a jeer is shouted, vocal, and public, mockery made theatrical.
  • Gibe (or Jibe): Similar because both are mocking remarks; differs because a gibe is a witty, clever, pointed sneer — articulate and often sarcastic — whereas a jeer is crude, loud, and frequently wordless, the roar of a hostile audience rather than the cut of a wit.
  • Hoot: Similar because both are loud, contemptuous noises raised at someone; differs because hoot names the sound itself — an owl-like call or cry of derision — and is narrower than jeer, which covers articulate mocking remarks as well as noise.
  • Boo: Similar because both are collective sounds of disapproval from an audience; differs because boo is a single syllable of pure disapproval, while a jeer adds mockery, contempt, and often articulate taunting to the sound of rejection.
  • Mock: Similar because both mean to treat with contemptuous derision; differs because mock is far broader — it includes imitation, mimicry, and ridicule in any mode, quiet or loud, verbal or physical — whereas jeer is specifically the loud, public, collective cry of a crowd.
  • Ridicule: Similar because both mean to hold someone up to derision; differs because ridicule is the general term for contemptuous laughter at someone and can be private, subtle, or written, while a jeer is by definition audible, public, and usually many-voiced.
