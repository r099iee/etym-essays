- [Vedic Samhitā Period (c. 1500–1000 BCE)] — venerated as a primordial Rigvedic seer (*ṛṣi*) of twenty-six sacred hymns, born miraculously from a water jar alongside Vasiṣṭha, engaging in profound philosophical dialogues with his wife Lopāmudrā - [perception: supreme Vedic composer and mediator between divine forces]
- [Epic & Early Purāṇic Era (c. 500 BCE–300 CE)] — celebrated as the heroic conqueror of nature who humbled the Vindhya mountain range, drank the cosmic ocean dry to defeat hidden demons, and bestowed inexhaustible divine weaponry upon prince Rāma - [perception: cosmic civilizer, defender of dharma, and bridge between northern and southern India]
- [Classical Tamil Sangam & Post-Sangam Era (c. 300–800 CE)] — enthusiastically adopted in southern culture as *Akattiyar*, patron of Tamil language, composer of the first Tamil grammar (*Agattiyam*), and leader of the First Sangam - [perception: revered father of Tamil literary culture and guardian of peninsular identity]
- [Medieval Siddha & Tantric Era (c. 900–1600 CE)] — elevated to the paramount seat among the eighteen supreme Siddhas (*Patinen Siddhars*), credited with pioneering pulse diagnosis, mineral alchemy, and herbal medicine - [perception: divine alchemist, immortal master physician, and mystic avatar]
- [Maritime Southeast Asian Diffusion (c. 7th–14th c. CE)] — worshipped across Java, Sumatra, Bali, and Cambodia as the divine guru *Bhaṭāra Guru*, whose shrines anchored monumental Shaivite temple complexes - [perception: universal patron of royal wisdom, agriculture, and spiritual guidance across the Indian Ocean]
- [Modern Astronomy & Contemporary Piety (19th–21st c.)] — permanently identified with the bright southern star Canopus whose rising marks water purification, while remaining an enduring symbol of spiritual wisdom and medical heritage - [perception: timeless emblem of cosmic vision, scientific antiquity, and cultural unity]
