CHAIN: FROM GERMANIC TO MAGGOT (5 stages — PIE stage probable but debated)

  1. PIE source (probable but debated): Proto-Germanic *maþô "worm, maggot" is usually set under a PIE base reconstructed variously as *math- or *moth- "worm, maggot" (with possible Baltic material, e.g. Latvian mātīte "maggot"); the reconstruction is not securely established outside Germanic and its exact vocalism is debated.
  2. Proto-Germanic *maþô "worm, maggot" (reconstructed; reflected in Old High German mado, German Made "maggot", Gothic maþa "worm").
  3. Old English maþa "worm, maggot" (attested in the Anglo-Saxon period, c. 8th–11th c.).
  4. Middle English maddok "maggot" (attested 13th c., probably from Old Norse maðkr "maggot, worm", cognate with the same Germanic stock) and, alongside it, the altered form magot, maggot "fly-larva" (attested 14th c.). The relationship of magot to maddok is debated: magot may be a different suffixation of the same base, or an independent formation.
  5. English maggot "fly larva" (16th c. onwards, modern spelling standardised) → MAGGOT.

DEBATED / NUANCED POINTS

  • The exact PIE root is not firmly established: *math-/*moth- is reconstructed mainly from Germanic (and possibly Baltic) evidence, so it must be treated as probable rather than certain.
  • The relation between Middle English maddok (Norse-derived) and magot/maggot is not fully settled; the OED treats the maggot forms as an alteration (probably by suffix substitution) of maddok, but independent formation cannot be excluded.
