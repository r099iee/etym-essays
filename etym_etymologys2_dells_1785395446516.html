The word is a native English formation; its chain runs from the reconstructed Indo-European root through the Germanic branch to the plural. The plural is formed with the ordinary English plural suffix. Chain — "dell" (plural "dells"): - PIE *dʰel- (also reconstructed *dʰol-) "to curve, vault, arch; a hollow" — a traditional Indo-European root associated with arched, hollow, or sunken shapes (compare Greek thólos "a round building, a dome"); the reconstruction follows Pokorny and is conventional, though the exact grade and laryngeal details are debated → Proto-Germanic *dalą "a valley, a dale, low-lying ground" (reconstructed; attested through its descendants Old English dæl, Old Norse dalr, Gothic dal, Old High German tal) → Proto-Germanic *daljō (a jō-stem derivative, literally "the little dale," a diminutive feminine formation meaning "a small valley, a dell") → Old English dell "dell, a small dale or hollow in the ground" (attested from the Old English period, c. 10th century) → Middle English del, delle "dell, a small valley or hollow" (12th–15th centuries) → Modern English dell "a small, secluded, wooded valley or hollow" (16th century onward) → Modern English dells (the plural, formed with the Germanic plural suffix -s) = DELLS. Chain — the plural suffix -s: - PIE *-es, *-os (the nominative plural endings) → Proto-Germanic *-ōz / *-az (the plural endings of the a-stem declension) → Old English -as (the masculine a-stem nominative and accusative plural ending) → Middle English -es → Modern English -s, the ordinary plural suffix, giving dells. Note on debate: the intermediate Proto-Germanic form *daljō and its exact relationship to *dalą are reconstructed rather than directly attested; some scholars prefer to derive Old English dell directly from the base *dalą by i- or j-umlaut within English. The connection of the whole Germanic family to the PIE root *dʰel- "to arch, to be hollow" is plausible and traditional but not universally accepted; a minority view treats the Germanic *dal- words as an isolated set of uncertain deeper ancestry. The plural dells is a perfectly regular formation and involves no historical uncertainty.
