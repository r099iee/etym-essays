No secure PIE root is reconstructible; the word belongs to the Germanic sound-symbolic sn- cluster. Chain: Middle Dutch snoffen "to sniff; to crop, trim, snip off" (attested by the 15th c.; deeper origin unknown — an expressive Germanic formation in the sn- cluster, cf. Middle Low German snuffen / snoppen "to sniff, to crop," Middle High German snupfen "to sniff"; no reconstructible PIE etymon, likely imitative of nose and pinch sounds) → Middle English snoffen / snuffen "to trim or crop the burnt wick of a candle" (late 14th c.); noun snuff "the charred part of a candle wick" (mid-15th c.) → Modern English snuff "to extinguish (a candle), to put out by pinching or smothering" (the sense of full extinguishing from the 17th c.; "snuff out" as a metaphor for killing or suppressing from the 18th–19th c.) → SNUFFED "extinguished, pinched out, abruptly put an end to" (past tense and past participle). Parallel but distinct strand: Dutch snuffen "to sniff" → Dutch snuf(tabak) "snuff (tobacco)" → English snuff "powdered tobacco inhaled through the nose" (late 17th c.); the verb "to snuff" tobacco is separate from the wick verb. Note: the wick sense and the nasal/tobacco sense are different words that happen to share the same expressive root cluster; their histories should not be merged.
