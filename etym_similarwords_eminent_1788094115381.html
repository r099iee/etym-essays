• Distinguished: Similar because both denote publicly recognised excellence and high standing; differs because distinguished is more neutral about the breadth of fame and slightly less lofty — an eminent person is pre-eminent among the distinguished.
  • Prominent: Similar because both mean “standing out, conspicuous” (etymological twins, from Latin prominēre “to jut forward” vs ēminēre “to stand out”); differs because prominence can be purely visual or positional without any merit (“a prominent nose”, “a prominent politician”), while eminent always carries positive evaluation of rank or achievement.
  • Pre-eminent: Similar because it is the superlative strengthening of the same idea — surpassing all others of the kind; differs because it claims undisputed first rank, not merely a place among the excellent.
  • Notable: Similar because both mean “worthy of notice”; differs because notable is far weaker — it marks remarkability without implying superiority, rank, or acclaim.
  • Renowned: Similar because both imply wide fame built on achievement; differs because renowned stresses the spread of reputation (being “named again and again”) rather than the inherent quality of rising above others.
  • Celebrated: Similar because both imply much-praised public standing; differs because celebrated emphasises active public acclamation and festivity and applies readily to events, places, and works, not chiefly to persons of rank.
  • Illustrious: Similar because both mean distinguished and glorious; differs because illustrious is more archaic and poetic, suggesting radiant or inherited glory rather than earned professional standing.
  • Famous: Similar because an eminent person is usually famous; differs because famous denotes mere wide knowledge without any connotation of merit — one can be famous for being infamous.
  • Esteemed: Similar because both mean highly regarded; differs because esteemed is quieter and more personal, rooted in affection and respect rather than in public elevation above one’s peers.
  • Acclaimed: Similar because both imply public praise; differs because acclaimed focuses on the act of praise itself (applause, reviews, ovations), typically of artistic or sporting performance, not on standing and rank.
  • Outstanding: Similar because both mean conspicuously above the ordinary; differs because outstanding is broader and less formal, applying to performance, quality, and debt (outstanding balance) rather than to social elevation.
  • Conspicuous: Similar because both share the core image of standing out visually; differs because conspicuous is value-neutral or even negative (“conspicuous consumption”) and implies visibility rather than merit.
  • Exalted: Similar because both mean raised to a high position; differs because exalted suggests elevation conferred by office, rank, or divinity rather than earned reputation.
  • Reputable: Similar because both mean held in good regard; differs because reputable denotes mere probity and reliability — a reputable firm is honest, but not necessarily eminent.
  • Foremost: Similar because both imply first rank among one’s class; differs because foremost is positional and institutional (the foremost authority, the foremost city) and can apply to things without the personal honour of eminent.
  • Noteworthy: Similar because both mean deserving attention; differs because noteworthy is about interest and memorability, with no implication of superiority or rank.
