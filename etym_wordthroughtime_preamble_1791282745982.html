- Ceremonial forerunner — Late Latin and medieval sense of one who walks before; perceived as dignified, processional, heraldic preparation.
- Legal solemnity — 14th–18th century chancery and constitutional use as the venerable opening of laws; connotation of authority, legitimacy and founding purpose.
- Oratorical courtesy — 18th–19th century extension to polite speaker's exordium; seen as proper manners, securing goodwill before difficult news.
- Tedious delay — late 19th–20th century shift to impatient idiom (without further preamble); perception of fuss, waffle and pomp to be cut through.
- Modern dual use — contemporary split between respected constitutional preamble (mined for intent) and comic everyday preamble (mocked as throat-clearing), as in Wodehouse's mock-heroic brawl.
