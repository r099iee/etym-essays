• jokes: Similar because both refer to humorous remarks, stories, or tricks intended to provoke laughter and amusement; differs because joke (from Latin iocus, "sport, joke") is the universal, modern, everyday standard term for any comedic punchline, whereas jest belongs to a more traditional, literary, or slightly archaic register and often implies teasing mockery or sportive banter.
  • quips: Similar because both are quick, witty verbal retorts; differs because a quip is specifically a brief, clever, and often sarcastic or sharp-tongued one-liner made on the spur of the moment, whereas a jest can be a broader playful story, prolonged banter, or an action/prank.
  • witticisms: Similar because both involve intellectual humor and clever linguistic delivery; differs because a witticism strictly emphasizes refined verbal ingenuity, sophisticated irony, and intellectual sharpness (often of an elite literary nature), whereas jests frequently embrace earthy, rustic, slapstick, or simple good-humored tavern fun.
  • banter: Similar because both involve lighthearted, playful, and affectionate teasing between companions; differs because banter is an ongoing, reciprocal dialogue or volley of verbal sparring between two or more speakers, whereas a jest is an individual remark, story, or specific comic utterance.
  • pranks: Similar because both can describe playful physical tricks or mischievous deceptions played upon someone for amusement; differs because a prank is exclusively an active, mischievous deed or practical joke (often involving physical props or elaborate setups), whereas jests are predominantly verbal quips and anecdotes.
  • wisecracks: Similar because both are humorous, comic remarks aimed at an audience; differs because a wisecrack is informal modern slang carrying a strong connotation of flippancy, cheeky insolence, and streetwise cynicism, contrasting sharply with the warm, historic literary tone of jest.
  • repartees: Similar because both denote verbal cleverness and humorous conversation; differs because a repartee is specifically a swift, brilliant, and fencing-like comeback in conversation, demanding immediate conversational reaction, whereas a jest can be a self-contained joke or narrative.
  • ribaldry: Similar because both can encompass earthy, bawdy humor shared in taverns or festive gatherings; differs because ribaldry explicitly denotes coarse, vulgar, and sexually suggestive or irreverent humor, whereas jests can be completely innocent, simple, and clean (as with the simple jests of Tolkien's hobbits).
  • drolleries: Similar because both evoke quaint, amusing, and whimsical humor; differs because drollery emphasizes an eccentric, dry, peculiar, or oddly comical mannerism or visual curiosity, whereas jests emphasize active verbal humor and laughter.
  • pleasantries: Similar because both are light, good-natured remarks spoken in polite social settings; differs because a pleasantry is a mild, courteous, and conventional conversational remark designed to maintain polite decorum (often lacking any real comedic punch), whereas a jest actively aims to elicit laughter.
