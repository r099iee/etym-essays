• Meriones: Similar because he is Idomeneus's constant companion and fellow Cretan slayer; differs because he is squire and second, not king.
  • Nestor: Similar because he is another aged Achaean king-counsellor; differs because Nestor advises more than he kills, while Idomeneus still kills.
  • Diomedes / Tydides: Similar because Ruskin pairs them as twenty-to-one slayers and hard-working kings; differs because Diomedes is young Argive, Idomeneus old Cretan.
  • Agamemnon: Similar because he is supreme Achaean king and fellow slayer; differs because he commands all, while Idomeneus commands only Cretans.
  • Menelaus: Similar because he is another steadfast Achaean king at Troy; differs because his quarrel causes the war, while Idomeneus comes by oath.
  • Odysseus: Similar because he is a mature Ithacan king famed for labour; differs because his labour is cunning, while Idomeneus's is spear-work.
  • Deucalion (son of Minos): Similar because he is Idomeneus's father in myth; differs because he is genealogical background, not the Trojan actor.
  • Sarpedon: Similar because he is another island-associated leader (Lycian) and worthy foe; differs because he fights for Troy, Idomeneus for Achaea.
  • King of Crete: Similar because it is Idomeneus's title; differs because it is a description (Minos also held it), not the personal name.
  • Idomeneo (opera hero): Similar because it is the same figure in Mozart; differs because operatic Idomeneo is a father in moral torment, while Homeric Idomeneus is a battlefield slayer.
