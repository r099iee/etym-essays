• Trellis: Similar because both consist of an open framework of crossed wooden or metal strips; differs because a trellis is specifically designed as a garden structure or horticultural support for climbing vegetation, whereas latticework is a broader architectural, engineering, and metaphorical concept.
  • Grille (or Grating): Similar because both form an open barrier or screen of intersecting bars allowing the passage of air and light; differs because a grille is typically heavier, forged from metal, and intended for security, protection, or ventilation intake, whereas latticework emphasizes an interlaced pattern of thinner strips.
  • Fretwork: Similar because both are decorative openwork patterns created in timber, metal, or stone; differs because fretwork is specifically carved, pierced, or scrolled with a fretsaw into intricate ornamental motifs, whereas latticework is constructed by crossing and overlapping continuous strips or bars.
  • Tracery: Similar because both refer to intersecting architectural openwork; differs because tracery specifically designates the carved stonework ribbing in Gothic windows, screens, and vaults, whereas latticework is typically constructed from assembled strips of wood, metal, or modern synthetics.
  • Mesh (or Reticulation): Similar because both consist of a network of interwoven or interconnected strands forming regular open spaces; differs because mesh often denotes flexible, finely woven wire, plastic, or fabric, whereas latticework denotes a rigid, self-supporting structural or ornamental framework.
  • Treillage: Similar because both refer to architectural frameworks of crossed laths; differs because treillage is a specialized French architectural term for elaborate, formal garden trellis-work, often incorporating architectural trompe-l'œil, arches, and pilasters in classical landscape design.
  • Espalier: Similar because both are used in horticulture to support and train plants along a flat plane; differs because an espalier refers directly to the horticultural framework or the pruned, trained tree itself, whereas latticework refers strictly to the material lattice construction.
  • Balustrade: Similar because both delineate spatial boundaries and provide architectural enclosure; differs because a balustrade consists of a row of upright balusters topped by a railing or coping, lacking the crossed, diamond, or orthogonal mesh pattern characteristic of latticework.
