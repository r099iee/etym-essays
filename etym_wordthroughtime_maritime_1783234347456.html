- Of the sea in early English - From the 16th century (Latin maritimus) maritime has meant relating to the sea, shipping, or navigation, a learned adjective of seas and coasts.
- Geopolitical power - In the 17th-19th centuries, in the age of empire, maritime becomes institutional: a maritime power, maritime supremacy, a nation whose whole strength is built on the sea and trade (Macaulay's 'maritime power').
- Modern legal and technical sense - Today maritime is indispensable in international law and geopolitics (maritime law, maritime boundary), a formal, institutional adjective where ordinary speech would say coastal or seafaring.
