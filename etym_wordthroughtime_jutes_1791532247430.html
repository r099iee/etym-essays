- 6th–8th century: a small northern people named by Bede - the name is a learned, clerical label attached to Kent, the Isle of Wight and the Meon valley.
- 9th–12th century: Old English Ēotas in chronicles and translations - the Jutes become a fixed item in the origin story, remote and half-legendary.
- 13th–17th century: an antiquarian term - "Jutes" survives in histories of the Conquest and in discussions of Kentish privileges and customs.
- 18th–19th century: racial and philological history - the Jutes are classified as one of the Germanic "stocks" and fitted into national origin narratives.
- 20th–21st century: a technical historical and archaeological label - the word now marks a debated migration group, and the Jutland peninsula keeps the name alive geographically.
