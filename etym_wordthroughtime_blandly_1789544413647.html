- Classical Latin - [Soothing Flattery & Sweetness] - *Blandus* meant smooth-tongued, flattering, charming, and soft.
- 15th–16th Century - [Gentle & Pleasing Manner] - Borrowed into Middle/Early Modern English as *bland*, meaning mild, soothing, and affable.
- 17th–18th Century - [Culinary Insipidity & Medical Salves] - Expanded to describe non-irritating, gentle food and soothing ointments for invalids.
- 19th Century - [Victorian Ironic Politeness] - Standardized in fiction (Conan Doyle, Thackeray) for the inscrutable, polite mask worn by detectives and diplomats.
- 20th–21st Century - [Subtle Rhetorical & Psychological Tactic] - Used globally for calculated, calm, and non-threatening communication during tense confrontations.
