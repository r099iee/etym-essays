• Parsimonious: Similar because both denote extreme reluctance to spend or give; differs because parsimonious is a scholarly Latinate term focusing on excessive frugality and financial calculation, whereas niggardly carries sharper moral contempt and applies readily to intangibles like praise.
  • Miserly: Similar because both describe hoarding wealth; differs because miserly centers on the wretched psychology of the hoarder who lives in self-inflicted misery, while niggardly centers on the begrudging act of withholding from others.
  • Stingy: Similar because both mean ungenerous; differs because stingy is the common, conversational Anglo-Saxon workhorse, lacking the literary dignity and historical gravity of niggardly.
  • Penurious: Similar because both imply scarcity of giving; differs because penurious can also mean living in actual crushing poverty (destitution), whereas niggardly implies an unwillingness to give despite having means.
  • Closefisted / Tightfisted: Similar because both denote an unyielding refusal to open one’s hand; differs because these are vivid vernacular idioms, whereas niggardly is formal and literary.
  • Grudging: Similar because both involve giving only with reluctance and resentment; differs because grudging describes the bitter emotional state of yielding something under duress, while niggardly describes the character or tiny amount given.
  • Illiberal: Similar because both signify the opposite of generous and open-handed; differs because illiberal is an elevated philosophical term contrasting with classical liberality of mind, manners, and spirit.
  • Churlish: Similar because both describe mean, ungenerous behavior; differs because churlish emphasizes surly, boorish, and ill-bred rudeness rather than sheer financial tightfistedness.
  • Sparing: Similar because both describe using or giving minimal resources; differs because sparing is neutral or even laudable (showing restraint or mercy), whereas niggardly is consistently pejorative.
  • Avaricious: Similar because both relate to an unhealthy attachment to money; differs because avaricious focuses on the insatiable greed to acquire more wealth, whereas niggardly focuses on the refusal to let go of what one already possesses.
