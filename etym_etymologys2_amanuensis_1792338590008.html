Note on morphological formation: Latin āmanuēnsis is an unusual and fascinating compound formed directly from the prepositional syntagma ā manu ("at hand / from the hand / for handwriting") coupled with the adjectival suffix -ēnsis, which typically attached to geographical locations (e.g. Atheniensis "Athenian") or structural institutions (e.g. castrensis "of the military camp", forensis "of the forum"). By suffixing -ēnsis to the prepositional phrase, Roman administrative Latin created a substantive agent noun designating a person whose recognized domestic or bureaucratic post was defined specifically by "the hand" (i.e. manual writing and secretarial execution).
