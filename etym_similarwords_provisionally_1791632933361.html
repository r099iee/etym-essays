• tentatively: Similar because both adverbs describe decisions or steps taken subject to future revision; differs because tentatively emphasizes hesitation, cautious experimentation, or testing the waters (from Latin tentare, to try or touch), whereas provisionally denotes a formal, structured, or authorized interim arrangement awaiting definitive ratification.
  • temporarily: Similar because both indicate that a state of affairs is not permanent; differs because temporarily refers strictly to duration in time without inherently implying conditional terms or that a replacement is actively anticipated, whereas provisionally explicitly implies an interim mechanism designed to bridge a gap until permanent measures are finalized.
  • conditionally: Similar because both indicate dependence on future circumstances; differs because conditionally focuses squarely on prerequisites or stipulations that must be met for an agreement to remain valid, whereas provisionally emphasizes the operational status of an arrangement functioning for the time being.
  • pro tem: Similar because both denote serving for the time being; differs because pro tem (short for pro tempore) is an official Latinate postnominal or title modifier restricted to organizational offices (e.g., President pro tem), whereas provisionally functions broadly as an adverb across epistemological, legal, technical, and perceptual contexts.
  • ad interim: Similar because both describe an intervening temporary state; differs because ad interim is a formal bureaucratic phrase applied specifically to administrative appointments between regular incumbents, whereas provisionally can qualify actions, hypotheses, mechanical fixtures, or aesthetic judgments.
  • hypothetically: Similar because both denote propositions that are not established facts; differs because hypothetically resides purely in theoretical contemplation and abstract imagination, whereas provisionally implies that an idea, model, or protocol is actively being utilized in practice as an operational stopgap.
  • experimentally: Similar because both describe non-definitive measures; differs because experimentally highlights an active process of empirical testing to discover unknown results or evaluate efficacy, whereas provisionally highlights acceptance of an imperfect or incomplete solution out of practical necessity.
  • interimly: Similar because both convey an in-between temporal role; differs because interimly is an awkward, rarely attested adverb, whereas provisionally is the established, elegant, and scholarly standard.
