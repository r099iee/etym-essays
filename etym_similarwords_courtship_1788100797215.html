• Wooing: Similar because both name the active seeking of a person's love; differs because wooing is the action itself (archaic and tender), while courtship is the state or period of that action — one woos during a courtship.
  • Courting: Similar because both are the same root in noun and verb form; differs because courting is the everyday verbal noun, while courtship is the formal noun of the whole process — "courtship" sounds old-fashioned and grand by comparison.
  • Dating: Similar because both name the modern pre-marital relationship process; differs because dating is casual, contemporary, and open-ended (dating casually, dating apps), while courtship implies seriousness and intent to marry.
  • Romance (as a relationship): Similar because both belong to the early love of two people; differs because romance is the emotional and imaginative colour of love (or a literary genre), while courtship is the socially organised period of seeking a spouse.
  • Flirtation: Similar because both involve attraction between people; differs because flirtation is light, unserious, and brief play at attraction, while courtship is serious, protracted, and directed toward marriage.
  • Engagement: Similar because both precede marriage; differs because engagement is the formal betrothal entered into AFTER courtship has succeeded — with a ring and a date — while courtship is the preceding period of pursuit and testing.
  • Betrothal: Similar because both are stages of the path to marriage; differs because betrothal is the formal promise itself (legal or ceremonial), while courtship is the wooing that leads up to it.
  • Paying one's addresses (phrase): Similar because both name the formal wooing of a lady with intent to marry; differs because this is the dated 18th–19th-century phrase for the gentleman's formal approach, while courtship covers the whole period and both parties.
  • Keeping company / Walking out (historical phrases): Similar because both name the recognised Victorian period of courtship; differs because these are the period's everyday euphemisms for the courting couple's paid visits and walks, while courtship is the general and formal word.
  • Making love / Love-making (older senses): Similar because both named wooing in earlier centuries ("to make love" to someone meant to court them down to the 19th century); differs because those phrases have shifted their modern meaning to physical intimacy, while courtship has never changed its romantic sense.
  • Suit (to press one's suit): Similar because both describe courting with a purpose; differs because suit is the archaic noun for the wooing petition itself and survives in set phrases, while courtship is the living general word.
  • Courtship display / Mating ritual (zoology): Similar because both name animal behaviour that secures a mate; differs because these are the technical ethological terms for the specific signal-and-response performances, while courtship in biology is the general word covering the whole process.
