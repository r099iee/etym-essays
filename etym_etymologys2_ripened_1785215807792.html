PIE *rep- / *reip- 'to pluck, strip, tear off' (traditional Pokorny account; modern dictionaries note the connection is debated — *rīpiz may be an independent West Germanic formation; see note) → Proto-Germanic *rīpiz 'reapable, mature, ready for harvest' (adjective) alongside verb *rīpaną 'to cut, harvest' → West Germanic *rīpijaz 'ripe, mature' → Old English rīpe 'ripe, mature, ready for reaping; also sound, ready' (attested from the 9th c.) alongside Old English rīpan 'to reap, cut grain' → Middle English ripe (unchanged form) → ripenen / ripen 'to become ripe' (c. 1325, intransitive; transitive 'to make ripe' from the 16th c.; verbal suffix -en from Old English -nian 'to make/become') → English ripened 'became mature, fully developed' (past participle) → Modern English ripened. Note: the *rīpiz family is chiefly West Germanic (Old Saxon ripi, Middle Dutch and Dutch rijp, Old High German rifi, Middle High German rīfe, Modern German reif); the Scandinavian languages use a different word (e.g. Danish/Norwegian moden, Icelandic þroskaður), and there is no Sanskrit, Hindi, or Odia branch for this adjective. The traditional link of *rīpiz to PIE *rep-/*reip- 'to pluck, tear' (with Greek ereptesthai 'to feed on, tear down' and Baltic forms) is plausible but not certain.
