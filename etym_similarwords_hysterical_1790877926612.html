• Frenzied: Similar because both involve wild, agitated behavior; differs because frenzied emphasizes frantic physical speed, violent activity, and chaotic urgency, whereas hysterical centers on the collapse of emotional stability into irrational tears, screams, or laughs.
  • Frantic: Similar because both denote extreme emotional agitation; differs because frantic is driven by acute desperation, time pressure, or anxiety (e.g., a frantic search for a lost child), lacking the convulsive or unhinged nature of hysterical.
  • Hilarious: Similar because both describe extreme comedy colloquially; differs because hilarious is specifically dedicated to joyful, loud amusement and laughter, whereas hysterical is a hyperbolic transfer from a state of psychological breakdown.
  • Overwrought: Similar because both describe over-emotional states; differs because overwrought suggests excessive nervousness, over-stimulation, and strained theatricality without necessarily crossing into full wild pandemonium.
  • Panicked: Similar because panic is a major component of hysterical episodes; differs because panicked specifically highlights fear and flight responses, whereas hysterical can manifest across multiple conflicting emotions simultaneously (rage, grief, laughter).
  • Distraught: Similar because both describe individuals overwhelmed by grief or crisis; differs because distraught conveys deep, agonizing sorrow, confusion, and grief without the noisy, wild convulsive outward frenzy of hysterical.
