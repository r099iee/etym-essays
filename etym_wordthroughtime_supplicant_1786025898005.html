- Classical Antiquity (c. 500 BCE–400 CE) - [Sacred Bodily Posture] - Conceived in Greco-Roman culture as a sacred, ritualized status where a petitioner physically knelt, folded their knees, and touched the chin or knees of the petitioned under divine protection.
- Late Middle English (c. 1400s) - [Formal Religious & Ecclesiastical Plea] - Adopted in Christian theology and liturgy to describe the posture of sinful humanity approaching the throne of God in humble prayer.
- Early Modern English (1500s–1600s) - [Royal Courts & Legal Petitions] - Applied to political subjects and nobles presenting formal grievances or appeals for pardon before Tudor and Stuart monarchs.
- 1800s–Present - [Literary & General Interpersonal Metaphor] - Used in modern literature and elevated discourse to characterize anyone forced into an asymmetric, pleading posture before an unyielding authority or loved one.
