PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*(s)pen- / *pend-
"to draw, stretch, spin; to hang, cause to hang down, weigh"
Note: Prolific PIE root that also yielded Latin pendere ('to hang'), English span, spin, and spider.
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE)
pendere (transitive/intransitive verb)
"to suspend, hang down; to weigh on scales, pay out money"
↓
Classical Latin (Frequentative / Intensive) (c. 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE)
pensāre (supine stem pens- + frequentative -are)
"to weigh carefully, counterbalance, consider, ponder mentally"
↓
Late / Vulgar Latin (c. 4th–7th c. CE)
*pensivus
"weighing in mind, thoughtful, deliberating"
↓
Old French (c. 12th–13th c.)
penser (verb) / pensif (masc. adj., fem. pensive)
"thoughtful, sad, melancholy, heavy with care, brooding"
↓
Middle English (c. late 14th c., c. 1380–1400)
pensif / pensyf → pensive
"sorrowfully thoughtful, anxious, deeply meditative"
↓
