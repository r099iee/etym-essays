• Fringe: Similar because both are hanging threads used for decoration; differs because a fringe is a band of threads along an edge, continuous and attached along its whole length, whereas a tassel is a gathered, knotted bunch hanging from a single point or from the end of a cord.
  • Pompon: Similar because both are round ornaments made of threads; differs because a pompon is a solid, fluffy ball of threads without the streaming strands that give a tassel its trailing, swaying motion.
  • Tuft: Similar because both are small clumps of hair or fibre; differs because a tuft names a natural clump of hair, feathers, or grass, without the deliberate knot and ornamental purpose of a tassel.
  • Epaulette: Similar because both are decorative shoulder ornaments; differs because an epaulette is a structured shoulder-piece that may end in fringed cords or small tassels, whereas the tassel is the free-hanging element itself, not the structure.
  • Torsade: Similar because both are cord- or thread-based ornaments; differs because a torsade is a twisted or plaited cord (as a curtain tie-back) that often terminates in tassels, while the tassel is the knotted hanging bunch at the end.
  • Frog: Similar because both are decorative fastenings of cloth or cord; differs because a frog is a loop-and-button fastening sewn to a garment, structural and paired, while a tassel is a hanging ornament that may but need not fasten anything.
  • Tass: Similar because both are hanging ends of cord or cloth; differs because a tass is the older word for the hanging end of a mantle or cloak, close in sense but archaic, while tassel is the modern general word.
  • Knot: Similar because both are made by tying cords or threads; differs because a knot is the tying of a cord to itself or another (a sailor's knot), functional and without strands, while a tassel is an ornament of gathered hanging strands.
