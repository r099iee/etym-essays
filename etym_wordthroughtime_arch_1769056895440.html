- Builder's curve — The noun settles into English in the 13th century as the stone curve that carries weight across an opening — the voussoired brow of Roman and Gothic building, a working mason's word.
- The curve as shape — By the early modern period 'arch' also names any bowed form — the arch of the sky, the foot, the eyebrow — keeping the curve while shedding the load-bearing force.
- Triumph and monument — The ceremonial arch enters public language — triumphal arch, the Arch of Titus, the Arc de Triomphe — making 'arch' the emblem of victory, passage, and commemoration.
- Contemporary sense — Modern English keeps the noun architectural and geometric, yet its figurative life is strong (the 'arch of history'), while the unrelated prefix arch- 'chief' in archbishop and archangel stays a separate word that must never be merged with the curve.
