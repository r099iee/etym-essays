• Teem: Similar because both mean to be present in large quantities or overflowing numbers; differs because teem (from Old English tēman, to bring forth offspring) specifically evokes active, swarming, biological movement or visible agitation (teeming with insects, a teeming metropolis), whereas abound is broader, dignified, and readily applied to abstract phenomena, resources, or examples.
  • Overflow: Similar because both fundamentally carry the hydraulic metaphor of liquid spilling over containers; differs because overflow is a concrete physical action where limits are visibly breached and contents spill outwards, whereas abound describes the general state of rich profusion without requiring physical spillage.
  • Proliferate: Similar because both describe great abundance; differs because proliferate emphasizes a rapid, aggressive rate of growth, reproduction, or multiplication over time (e.g., nuclear weapons proliferating, cancer cells proliferating), whereas abound describes existing plentiful status.
  • Brim: Similar because both denote fullness to the very limit; differs because brim emphasizes the upper edge of a container being filled to the absolute maximum capacity (eyes brimming with tears, a cup brimming), while abound implies wealth, copious extent, or widespread presence across a domain.
  • Flourish: Similar because both describe thriving, plentiful states; differs because flourish emphasizes robust health, vigorous growth, prosperity, or artistic blooming, whereas abound emphasizes mere quantity or copiousness of existence.
  • Superabound: Similar because both denote great abundance; differs because superabound carries the prefix super- to indicate excessive, redundant, or surplus profusion beyond what is needed or desirable.
  • Swarms: Similar because both describe large numbers occupying a space; differs because swarm conveys the dense, chaotic, buzzing motion of a multitude of creatures (bees, tourists, locusts), whereas abound has an elevated, harmonious, or neutral literary register.
  • Prevail: Similar because both can describe something widely present in an environment; differs because prevail emphasizes dominance, supremacy, enduring victory, or widespread acceptance over rivals (e.g., "reason prevailed"), rather than simple numerical profusion.
  • Multiply: Similar because both relate to large quantities; differs because multiply is an active mathematical or reproductive process of increasing numbers, whereas abound is an intransitive state of existing plentifully.
  • Redound: Similar because both are etymological cousins from Latin unda (wave); differs decisively because redound means to contribute greatly to an effect, credit, honor, or consequence (e.g., "actions that redound to his reputation"), rather than to exist in abundance.
