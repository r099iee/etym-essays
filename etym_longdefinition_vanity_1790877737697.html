Vanity (/ˈvæn.ə.ti/) is a noun with two primary, deeply interconnected semantic axes: first, excessive pride in, or narcissistic preoccupation with, one's own physical appearance, qualities, intellect, or social standing; and second, the fundamental quality of being empty, futile, ephemeral, worthless, or devoid of real substance or enduring value.

The rich polysemy of vanity spans ethical, theological, psychological, material, and commercial domains:

  • Egotistical Self-Admiration & Narcissism (Primary Modern Sense): An inflated sense of self-importance or an obsessive concern with how one appears to others. In theatrical performance and everyday psychology, an actor or individual possessed by vanity seeks constantly to draw attention, showcase glamour, or flatter their own ego rather than serving the truth of the moment.
  • Futility, Emptiness, and Transience (Theological & Existential Sense): The inherent fruitlessness and impermanence of worldly pursuits. Rooted in the famous biblical maxim of Ecclesiastes (Vanitas vanitatum et omnia vanitas — "Vanity of vanities; all is vanity"), this sense emphasizes that wealth, fame, beauty, and mortal ambitions inevitably dissolve before time and death.
  • Vanitas in the Visual Arts (Art History Genre): A specific genre of 16th- and 17th-century Dutch and Flemish still-life painting that deliberately incorporates symbolic objects—such as skulls, hourglasses, burnt-out candles, rotting fruit, and soap bubbles—to remind viewers of the brevity of human life and the worthlessness of worldly pleasures.
  • Dressing Table or Bathroom Fixture (Material Furniture Sense): A dressing table equipped with mirrors and drawers for cosmetics and grooming; by extension, in modern interior design and plumbing, a bathroom unit consisting of a washbasin set into a cabinet or countertop. This usage directly derives from the vanity of personal grooming.
  • Vanity Press, Vanity Sizing, and Commercial Jargon: In publishing, a vanity press is a service where authors pay to have their own books published without competitive editorial curation. In fashion, vanity sizing refers to the practice of assigning smaller size numbers to larger garments to flatter customers' egos. In automotive accessories, a vanity plate is a custom personalized license plate chosen to display one’s nickname or status.

Semantic Reasoning & Historical Bridges: The semantic journey of vanity begins with the Latin vanus, meaning "empty, hollow, void." In classical Antiquity and early Christian theology, vanitas meant literal emptiness or illusory unreality. A person who pursued mortal glory was chasing "emptiness." Over centuries, as the psychological focus shifted from the cosmic futility of the object to the character flaw of the person chasing it, vanity came to identify the specific moral defect of overvaluing one's own ephemeral charms. The transition from "empty illusion" to "conceited self-love" represents an internalizing psychological bridge: pride in that which is fundamentally hollow.
