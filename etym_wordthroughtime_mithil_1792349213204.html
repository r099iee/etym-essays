- [Late Vedic Era, c. 900–600 BCE] - designated the bustling, cultured frontier capital of Videha, celebrated in the Brāhmaṇas and Upaniṣads as the premier royal sanctuary for daring metaphysical debates on the nature of Brahman and the Self - [perception: viewed by orthodox western Vedic elites as an avant-garde eastern sanctuary of supreme philosophical inquiry and lavish royal patronage]
- [Epic & Classical Sanskrit Period, c. 500 BCE–500 CE] - immortalized in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa as the idyllic, sanctified realm of King Janaka and the natal home of Sītā, synonymous with virtue, fertile abundance, and the legendary trial of Śiva's bow - [perception: revered as the ultimate archetypal city of feminine purity, ideal domestic harmony, and righteous kingship]
- [Medieval & Early Modern Scholastic Era, c. 12th–17th c. CE] - re-emerged as the undisputed capital of Sanskrit logic through the Navya-Nyāya revolution founded by Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya, alongside the flowering of vernacular devotional love poetry composed by Vidyāpati - [perception: respected as an uncompromising citadel of academic rigor, dialectical brilliance, and lyric poetic elegance]
- [Colonial & Modern Era, 20th–21st c.] - expanded from an ancient literary toponym into a proud ethno-linguistic and artistic signifier, celebrated globally for vibrant Madhubani wall and canvas painting and cross-border Maithili regional identity between India and Nepal - [perception: recognized internationally as an extraordinary living civilization embodying ancient folk aesthetics, linguistic heritage, and feminist creative expression]
