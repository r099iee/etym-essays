Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE)
*laiþaz
‘hateful, hostile, grievous’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
lāð
‘hateful, hostile, odious’
↓
Old English (verb) (9th c.)
lāðian
‘to hate, be hateful’
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
lōth, lath
‘unwilling, reluctant’
↓
Modern English (current)
loath
‘unwilling, reluctant’

Note: cognates include Old Saxon lēð and German Leid (‘sorrow, harm’); any deeper PIE origin beyond Germanic is uncertain, and handbooks leave *laiþaz without an agreed PIE root rather than guessing one.
