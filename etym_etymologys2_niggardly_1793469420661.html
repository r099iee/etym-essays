Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE)
*khnawwuz / *hnuwwjaz
‘pinched, stingy, narrow’
↓
Old Norse (c. 9th–11th c.)
hnøggur
‘stingy, sparing, close-fisted’
↓
Early Scandinavian Dialects (c. 11th–13th c.)
*nygg- / nyggia
‘to rub, pinch, be stingy’
↓
Middle English (c. 1300–1350)
nygard / nigard
‘miser, covetous person’ (+ French -ard)
↓
Middle English (c. 1380–1400)
nygardly / nygardliche
‘miserly, stingy; in a miserly way’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
niggardly
‘parsimonious, grudging of praise/wealth’
↓
Modern English (current)
niggardly
‘stingy, grudging, parsimoniously meager’

Note: Etymologically, niggardly derives entirely from North Germanic / Old Norse roots (*hnøggur*, *nyggia*, meaning "to pinch, scrape, be stingy"), joined with the pejorative agent suffix *-ard* of Old French / Germanic origin. It bears zero historical, morphological, or etymological connection to the phonetically similar racial slur, which descends from Latin *niger* ("black") via Spanish/Portuguese.
