Part 1 — WATCH
PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*weg-
"to be strong, be lively,
be awake"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*wakaną
"to wake, be awake,
rouse oneself"
↓
Proto-Germanic (causative)
*wakjaną
"to keep awake, wake,
be watchful"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
wæccan
"to stay awake, keep
watch, be vigilant"
↓
Old English (derived noun)
wæcce
"a watching, vigil,
guard, sentry-duty"
↓
Middle English (13th–15th c.)
wacche
"watch, vigil, period
of guard duty"
↓
Modern English (16th c.)
watch
"the act of keeping vigil;
a guard, sentinel, lookout"

Part 2 — POST
Latin (2nd c. BCE)
ponere
"to place, put, set,
fix, station"
↓
Latin (past participle)
positus
"placed, put, set down,
stationed"
↓
Italian (13th–14th c.)
posta
"a place, position,
station, relay-post"
↓
Middle French (14th–15th c.)
poste
"a place, position,
station of duty"
↓
English (16th c.)
post
"a place of duty,
station, position"
↓
English (compound: watch + post, 19th c.)
watch + post
"a station where a
watch or guard is kept"
↓
watch-post
"a place where a sentinel
or lookout is posted"

Note: Latin ponere is built as po- + sinō "to let, put," reaching back to an Indo-European root of uncertain shape sometimes reconstructed as *seh₁- "to let, leave, put"; because the pre-Latin stages are debated, this chain begins at the attested Latin verb. Note also that post "a wooden stake" (Old English post < Latin postis "doorpost") and post "mail" (from the relay-post sense) are separate words.
