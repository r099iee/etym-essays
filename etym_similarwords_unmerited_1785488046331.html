• undeserved: Similar because both mean not earned or merited; differs because undeserved is the plain, everyday word lacking unmerited’s measured, quasi-legal dignity.
  • unwarranted: Similar because both lack a proper basis; differs because unwarranted concerns justification or grounds and suits accusations, conclusions or intrusions rather than desert.
  • unearned: Similar because both come without the corresponding labour; differs because unearned has a faintly economic cast, emphasizing reward or payment rather than justice.
  • gratuitous: Similar because both are uncalled-for; differs because gratuitous carries a distinctly negative charge (a gratuitous insult) and means given freely without provocation, not weighed against merit.
  • unjustified: Similar because both lack a sufficient reason; differs because unjustified stresses the absence of a logically or morally adequate defence rather than the absence of desert.
  • groundless: Similar because both lack foundation; differs because groundless is about the absence of factual support (groundless rumour) rather than the absence of earned right.
  • baseless: Similar because both are without foundation; differs because baseless applies to claims and allegations, while unmerited applies to treatment, praise, punishment or repose.
  • uncalled-for: Similar because both are inappropriate; differs because uncalled-for is conversational and mild, while unmerited is formal and sets the matter before a scale of justice.
  • undue: Similar because both exceed what is proper; differs because undue means more than is owed (undue influence) — excess rather than absence of desert.
  • inequitable: Similar because both involve a failure of justice; differs because inequitable describes an unfair distribution between parties, while unmerited judges a single claim against desert.
