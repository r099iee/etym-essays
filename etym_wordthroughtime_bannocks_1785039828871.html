- Old English and Celtic antiquity - 'bannock' (Old English 'bannuc,' from Celtic; Gaelic 'bannag') is among the oldest bread-words of the British Isles: a flat, round cake of coarse meal baked on a griddle or hearthstone, the daily bread of northern and Celtic households.
- Medieval-early modern - bannocks remain the staple of the Scottish Highlands and Northern English countryside, baked over the open fire for every household; the word is plain, homely, and regionally specific.
- 18th-19th century - as oven-baked loaves spread, bannocks become the bread of tradition rather than necessity — carried by shepherds, soldiers, and travellers as durable rations, and celebrated in folk song and county lore.
- Contemporary - 'bannocks' is archaic and regional, deliberately chosen to evoke hearth, Highland, and Celtic antiquity; it names a specific griddle-baked loaf and carries a faint, comfortable scent of folklore and welcome.
