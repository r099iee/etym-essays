Middle English (c. 1400)
touselen
‘to handle roughly, rumple’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
tousle, touzle
‘to pull about, dishevel’
↓
Early Modern English (frequentative) (1620s)
tussle
‘a small struggle, scuffle’
frequentative of tousle
↓
Modern English (current)
tussle
‘a scuffle; a brisk contest’

Note: the deeper origin of tousle itself is uncertain and debated. Older dictionaries guessed at a Scandinavian source akin to Old Norse tosa, to shred, or at Proto-Germanic *tūs-, to tear, but no chain is securely attested, so the sober derivation stops at Middle English touselen rather than inventing a root.
