STOWAWAYS is a compound of the verb stow and the adverb away. The two chains run separately before joining in the nineteenth century. Chain — stow 1. Proto-Indo-European *steh₂- "to stand, set in place" (the root of Latin stāre, Sanskrit tiṣṭhati, English stand; zero grade *stǝ-). 2. Proto-Germanic *stōwō "a place, a spot" (derived from the zero grade with a suffix; attested only in West Germanic). 3. Old English stōw "a place" (8th–10th c.; fossilized in placenames such as Chepstow, Felixstowe). 4. Old English stōwian "to place, keep, store, put away" (denominal verb, c. 1000). 5. Middle English stowen "to pack, put in place, store" (13th–15th c.). 6. Modern English stow "to pack or store away compactly", used especially of cargo; joined with away in the phrasal verb to stow away. Chain — away 1. Proto-Indo-European *weǵʰ- "to convey, carry, move, go" (also the root of Latin vehere "to carry", English wagon, way). 2. Proto-Germanic *wegaz "a way, road, course". 3. Old English weg "a way, road" (the exact source of English way). 4. Old English on weg / a weg "on (the) way, hence, from this place" — a prepositional phrase adverbialized as aweg, contracted to away (9th c. onward). 5. Modern English away "at a distance, from this place, off". Joining: the nautical phrasal verb to stow away, "to stow goods away for storage", is attested from the 16th century; from it the noun stowaway, "a person who hides aboard a vessel to obtain free passage", is recorded from the 1850s (1856); the plural STOWAWAYS follows immediately and becomes the standard legal and nautical term. Debated / nuanced note: the noun is almost always parsed as stow + away, and there is no separate etymology. The PIE ancestry of stow (via *steh₂-) is standard, but the intermediate *stōwō is attested only in West Germanic, so the precise suffixal detail is reconstructed rather than directly attested. Some handbooks, following the OED, treat the Germanic noun *stōwō as a formation without an obvious cognate outside West Germanic and note the possibility of an independent development.
