• Languid: Similar because both describe a lack of physical energy, speed, or enthusiasm; differs because languid carries connotations of graceful, relaxed, sensual, or pleasantly sluggish slowness (such as a languid summer breeze or a languid wave of a fan), whereas listless implies a hollow, joyless, or depressed absence of vital desire and spirit.
  • Lethargic: Similar because both denote extreme sluggishness and inactivity; differs because lethargic emphasizes severe physical or pathological drowsiness, sleepiness, and slow metabolic functioning (as in clinical stupor), whereas listless primarily highlights psychological apathy and lack of interest.
  • Apathetic: Similar because both convey indifference and total lack of enthusiasm; differs because apathetic is purely psychological and emotional (denoting an utter lack of care, feeling, or concern about an issue or person), whereas listless manifests clearly in physical demeanor, slumped posture, and flat vocal tone.
  • Enervated: Similar because both describe a state of depleted vitality; differs because enervated is the result of an active, debilitating drain on one's strength or moral fiber (e.g., enervated by the extreme desert heat or long illness), whereas listless describes the resulting mood and outward disposition.
  • Torpid: Similar because both describe inactive, unresponsive states; differs because torpid implies profound physical dormancy, numbness, or temporary suspended animation (like an animal in winter hibernation), whereas a listless person is awake but utterly uninterested in exertion.
  • Lackadaisical: Similar because both denote a lack of energetic drive and purpose; differs because lackadaisical emphasizes careless, half-hearted, frivolous, or lazy execution of duties, whereas listless implies genuine psychological depletion, melancholy, or physical exhaustion.
  • Indolent: Similar because both involve avoiding exertion; differs because indolent implies a habitual, deliberate love of ease and constitutional laziness (an active preference for doing nothing), whereas listlessness is an involuntary emotional or physical state of depleted desire.
  • Phlegmatic: Similar because both describe an unemotional, unexcitable temperament; differs because phlegmatic is a permanent, stable personality type characterized by calm, rational, unflappable composure, whereas listless is an apathetic, weary state of deficit.
  • Blasé: Similar because both describe a bored, indifferent reaction to events; differs because blasé arises from sophisticated over-indulgence and world-weary cosmopolitan satiety (having seen and experienced everything), whereas listless can affect anyone suffering from fatigue, depression, or fever.
  • Somnolent: Similar because both involve low energy; differs because somnolent specifically means sleepy, drowsy, or causing sleep, whereas listless individuals may be completely unable to sleep despite their total lack of energy.
  • Spiritless: Similar because both denote absence of vitality; differs because spiritless emphasizes a lack of courage, pride, defiance, or backbone, often after being crushed by defeat.
