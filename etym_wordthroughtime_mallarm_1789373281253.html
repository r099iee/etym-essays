- 12th to 14th Century - Originates as an Old French chivalric nickname (*mal-armé*), designating an ill-equipped warrior or poorly armed combatant, before developing into a hereditary family surname.
- 1860s to 1870s - Enters French literary circles under Stéphane Mallarmé as an eccentric Parnassian poet and provincial English teacher, composing notoriously hermetic and jewel-like verses such as *L'Après-midi d'un faune*.
- 1880s to 1890s - Reaches the apex of European cultural influence as the intellectual leader and high priest of the Symbolist movement; his Tuesday salons (*les Mardis de la rue de Rome*) mentor the brightest luminaries of the avant-garde.
- 1897 to Early 20th Century - Shatters typographical and poetic conventions with *Un Coup de dés jamais n'abolira le hasard*, inventing modern spatial poetry and anticipating Futurism, Dada, and concrete poetics.
- Mid-20th Century to Present - Canonized by Paul Valéry, Maurice Blanchot, and Jacques Derrida as the supreme prophet of modern textuality, pure poetry (*la poésie pure*), and semiotics, standing as the ultimate symbolist pole in world literature.
