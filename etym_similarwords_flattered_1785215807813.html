• Compliment: Similar because both involve expressing approval or admiration; differs because a compliment may be sincere and disinterested, while flattery is always self-serving and often insincere — flattery is compliment with a motive.
  • Praise: Similar because both involve expressing approval; differs because praise is the general word for approval or admiration, carrying no implication of falsehood or ulterior purpose, whereas to flatter is to praise in order to gratify vanity or win favour.
  • Adulate: Similar because both mean excessive, devoted praise; differs because adulate implies servile, worshipful, indiscriminate admiration, while flattery is lighter, more subtle, and more calculating — praise aimed at a specific vanity.
  • Toady / toady to: Similar because both mean fawning praise for advantage; differs because toadying is the blunt, abject, obvious fawning of the parasite, while flattering is the finer art of pleasing without being caught at it.
  • Fawn: Similar because both denote servile courting of favour; differs because fawning is primarily physical and behavioral (cringing, grovelling) as well as verbal, while flattering is specifically verbal praise.
  • Cajole: Similar because both mean persuading someone with pleasing words; differs because cajole aims at persuading someone to do something, by coaxing and wheedling, while flatter aims at gratifying vanity itself, with no necessary request attached.
  • Blandish: Similar because both mean coaxing with pleasant words; differs because blandish is a formal, literary word for soft, flattering coaxing (cajolery), while flatter is the everyday term.
  • Butter up: Similar because both mean praising someone to win favour; differs because butter up is informal and comic, emphasizing the transparent flattery of an ingratiating speaker, while flatter is the neutral-to-moral term.
  • Sycophancy / sycophant: Similar because both denote the self-serving pursuit of favour through praise; differs because sycophancy is the wholesale, abject practice of the flatterer considered as a class, while flattery is the specific act of insincere praise.
  • Soothe / gratify: Similar because both can mean pleasing or satisfying a person; differs because soothe calms an uneasy feeling and gratify satisfies a desire, while flatter specifically pleases the vanity through praise.
  • Sweet-talk: Similar because both mean persuading or pleasing with flattering speech; differs because sweet-talk is informal and stresses glib, beguiling talk aimed at getting one's way, while flatter is the standard word for insincere praise.
  • Puff: Similar because both mean praising excessively; differs because puff (as in "puffery") is the promotional, inflated praising of a product or person in public, whereas flattery is the private, interpersonal stroking of vanity.
