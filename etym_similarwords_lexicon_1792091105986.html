• Vocabulary: Similar because both refer to the collection of words known or used by a person or language; differs because vocabulary is the everyday, versatile Germanic-influenced term describing personal word knowledge, whereas lexicon carries scholarly, formal, computational, and cognitive theoretical depth.
  • Dictionary: Similar because both denote a comprehensive reference work compiling words and their definitions; differs because dictionary describes a physical or digital book primarily of living vernacular languages, whereas lexicon historically denoted dictionaries of ancient/classical tongues (Greek/Hebrew) and conceptually represents the abstract mental inventory.
  • Glossary: Similar because both compile lists of terms and meanings; differs because a glossary is a brief, alphabetical appendix placed at the end of a specific book or manual explaining specialized, obscure, or technical terms found within that single text.
  • Thesaurus: Similar because both are comprehensive linguistic reference resources; differs because a thesaurus organizes words conceptually by semantic affinity, grouping synonyms and antonyms together rather than providing definitions, etymologies, or grammatical subcategorizations.
  • Nomenclature: Similar because both involve systems of naming and terminology; differs because nomenclature refers to a standardized, formal system of names applied within a specific scientific classification scheme (such as Linnaean binomial nomenclature in biology or IUPAC chemical nomenclature).
  • Argot: Similar because both denote specialized vocabularies used by particular groups; differs because argot specifically refers to an exclusive, secretive slang or cant developed by subcultures, criminal underworlds, or street gangs to prevent outsiders from understanding their conversations.
