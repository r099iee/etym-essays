ROOT 1 — "head" - Proto-Indo-European *káput- "head, skull" (reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE; shape debated, see note) - Proto-Germanic *haubudą "head" (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE) - Old English hēafod "head; top, summit, extremity" (attested 8th c.) - Middle English heved/hed "head" (12th–15th c.) - Modern English head "top or front extremity" (16th c.–present) ROOT 2 — "land" - Proto-Indo-European *lendh- "open land, heath" (reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE; shape debated) - Proto-Germanic *landą "land, ground" (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE) - Old English land "land, earth, ground" (attested 8th c.) - Middle English land (12th–15th c.) - Modern English land (16th c.–present) COMPOUND STAGE - Old English hēafod-land "field-end turn-strip; coastal headland" (attested c. 900) - Middle English hevedland (attested 13th c.) - Modern English headland (16th c.–present) → headlands (plural, the target root word) Debate / nuance note: The PIE root for "head" is sometimes reconstructed *kauput- rather than *káput-, and the root for "land" (*lendh-/*lendʰ-) is debated (some derive it from a form meaning "heath"). Neither uncertainty affects the Germanic-to-English line, which is securely attested.
