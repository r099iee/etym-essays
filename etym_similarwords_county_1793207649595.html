• Shire: Similar because in England a shire and a county are the same territory (Yorkshire, Lancashire); differs because shire is the older Anglo-Saxon name, poetic and northern, while county is the Norman official term.
  • Province: Similar because both are large divisions of a country; differs because a province is usually bigger, more autonomous, and often historically a conquered territory, while a county is a smaller administrative tile.
  • District: Similar because both divide territory for administration; differs because district is vaguer and smaller (a school district, the Lake District), while a county is a fixed historic unit with courts and councils.
  • Borough: Similar because both are units of English local government; differs because a borough is a town with chartered privileges, while a county is the great rural division containing many boroughs.
  • Parish: Similar because in Louisiana the parish does exactly what a county does elsewhere; differs because parish elsewhere means the tiny ecclesiastical district, while county is far larger and secular.
  • Canton: Similar because Swiss cantons are the middle layer of government like counties; differs because a canton is nearly sovereign within its federation, while a county merely administers.
  • Department: Similar because French departments tile France the way counties tile England; differs because departments are rational Napoleonic creations numbered and uniform, while counties are lumpy historic growths.
  • Prefecture: Similar because both organise regional administration under a central state; differs because a prefecture answers to an appointed prefect, while a county typically answers to elected councillors or commissioners.
  • Riding: Similar because the Ridings were Yorkshire’s thirds, divisions of a great county; differs because riding names a part of a county, while county names the whole shire itself.
