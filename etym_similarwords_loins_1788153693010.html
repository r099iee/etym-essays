• Hips: Similar because both name the pelvic side regions and are the modern word for the same area in dress and anatomy; differs because hips are specifically the bony prominence and are neutral/technical, while loins in modern use is archaic-literary or scriptural.
  • Groin: Similar because both can denote the lower abdomen where the legs meet; differs because groin is clinical and localised to the crease, while loins is broader and (since the Victorians) euphemistic.
  • Waist: Similar because both mark the mid-body line where a belt goes (the girding point); differs because waist is the narrow circumference, feminine-dress oriented, with no strength or descent meaning.
  • Lumbar region / lower back: Similar anatomically — the strict denotation of loins; differs because it is clinical, singular, modern, and entirely without the biblical and poetic freight of loins.
  • Haunches: Similar because both name the hind quarters of body (often of animals); differs because haunches stresses the fleshy mass and animal posture, loins the strength-and-generation centre.
  • Flank: Similar as a side-of-body noun and a meat cut; differs because flank is the flat side wall only, and as meat is coarse and tough, unlike the prized loin.
  • Pelvis: Similar as the bony basin underlying the region; differs completely in register — scientific, singular, accusative, with no idiom attached.
  • Kidneys / reins (archaic): Similar because Biblical and Hebrew anatomy locates the deepest feelings in the same mid-body region (KJV 'reins'); differs because reins is confined to Biblical allusion, while loins survived into cookery and modern anatomy.
