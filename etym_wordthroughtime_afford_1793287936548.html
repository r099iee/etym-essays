- Old English — to put forth and accomplish: geforðian means to carry out, advance, and bring to pass, a verb of doing rather than paying.
- Middle English — to provide and bestow: afforden supplies, furnishes, and grants, as lords furnish aid and fields furnish crops.
- Late 14th–15th c. — to bear the cost: the giving sense turns inward, so that to supply a sum from one's own means becomes to be able to pay it without ruin.
- 17th–18th c. — the household verb: afford settles into budgets, rents, and servants, the sober accounting of who can keep what station.
- 19th c. — time joins money: speakers begin to afford hours, delays, and risks, treating patience and reputation as purses that can also run empty.
- Today — capacity in every currency: money first, then time, attention, and error, with the old literary sense of landscapes affording views kept alive by writers and walkers.
