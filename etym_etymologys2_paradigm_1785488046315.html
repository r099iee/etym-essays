Chain for PARADIGM — primary root (Greek deiknumi 'to show'):
1. PIE *deyk- 'to point out, show, indicate' — a securely attested root (Sanskrit disati 'points, shows', Avestan daes- 'to show, teach', Latin dicere 'to say, declare' and index, Greek deiknumi 'I show').
2. Greek deiknumi 'I point out, show, prove' (thematic nasal present built on *deyk-, with the -numi/-numi suffix).
3. Greek paradeiknumi 'to show side by side, set forth, point out as an example' (para 'beside, alongside' + deiknumi).
4. Greek paradeigma 'pattern, model, example; illustrative instance' — substantivized from the verbal stem; attested in Aristotle, Plato, and the rhetorical tradition (4th c. BCE).
5. Latin paradigma 'model, example, pattern' — a learned borrowing from Greek in the grammatical and rhetorical tradition (1st c. BCE).
6. Old French paradigme / Middle French paradigme 'model, example' (a learned loan from Latin; the word enters French in the late medieval period).
7. Middle English paradigme 'an example, pattern, model' — first recorded 1483 (in Caxton's printing circle), borrowed through French.
8. PARADIGM (modern English; spelling settled in the 16th–17th c., the final -e dropped).

Secondary chain — the para- prefix as a separate root element:
1. PIE *per- / *pr- 'forward, through, beyond, across' — a widely attested prepositional root (Sanskrit para 'beyond', Greek para, Latin per, English for).
2. Greek para 'beside, alongside, beyond' (preposition; from *per- with Greek *-a vocalism).
3. Greek para- (prefix) 'beside, alongside, contrary to' — the first element of paradeigma, meaning literally 'shown beside.'

Note on the debate: the route from PIE *deyk- to Greek deiknumi is standard and secure; the only nuance is that the prefix para- contributes 'beside' rather than 'before', so the word's original image is of a model set alongside its copies for comparison. No letter labels A–F are used; every stage is named by its real attested form.
