PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*gʰlewbʰ- / *gʰley-
"to shine, gleam, look bright; to play, be cheerful"
Note: Ancient Indo-European base associating visual brightness and radiant shining with emotional joy and musical performance.
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–1st c. CE)
*glīwą
"joy, mirth, musical entertainment, play"
↓
Old English (c. 700–1000 CE)
glēo / glīw
"mirth, joy, entertainment, music, minstrelsy"
Note: Base of glēoman ('minstrel, singer') and glēodrēam ('sound of joy, music').
↓
Middle English (c. 1150–1450)
gle / glee
"merriment, amusement, pleasure; vocal or instrumental song"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500–1700)
glee
"exultant joy, lively delight, sportive mirth"
↓
18th Century English (Musical Specialization) (c. 1750–1787)
glee (musical genre)
