• Deformed: Similar because both describe an abnormal, misshapen physical or structural appearance; differs because deformed often implies a congenital, structural, or developmental flaw in bone or growth, whereas disfigured strongly implies that a previously intact, beautiful, or normal form was subsequently marred by external trauma or injury.
  • Defaced: Similar because both involve spoiling the surface appearance of something; differs because defaced specifically highlights damage done to the "face" or surface of an object, inscription, coin, or monument (often through deliberate human vandalism), while disfigured encompasses deeper structural mutilation and systemic distortion.
  • Mutilated: Similar because both involve severe physical damage and trauma; differs because mutilated emphasizes the violent violent destruction, hacking away, or deprivation of essential limbs or organs, whereas disfigured focuses primarily on the resulting loss of outward beauty, symmetry, and aesthetic form.
  • Marred: Similar because both signify that perfection or beauty has been spoiled; differs because marred is a milder, more general term that can refer to minor surface blemishes, scratches, or ruined occasions (e.g. "the celebration was marred by rain"), whereas disfigured conveys profound, often permanent aesthetic ruin.
  • Distorted: Similar because both denote an alteration away from true shape or truth; differs because distorted highlights twisting, warping, or stretching out of natural proportion (such as an optical distortion or a distorted argument), whereas disfigured highlights unsightly, repulsive scarring and degradation.
  • Blemished: Similar because both involve flaws on a surface; differs because blemished refers to small, superficial spots, stains, or moral shortcomings, lacking the tragic, severe physical or intellectual severity of being disfigured.
  • Mangled: Similar because both involve violent physical damage; differs because mangled evokes the mechanical tearing, crushing, and shredding of flesh or metal into an unrecognizable heap, whereas disfigured focuses on the surviving countenance being rendered grotesque.
  • Corrupted: Similar because both describe the degradation of an original pure state; differs because corrupted emphasizes internal rot, bribery, or linguistic degradation from within, whereas disfigured emphasizes the grotesque visual or structural outward result.
  • Scarred: Similar because both describe lasting marks left by trauma; differs because scarred can refer to a single healed incision or psychological wound without necessarily destroying overall aesthetic beauty, whereas disfigured explicitly asserts that beauty and form have been spoiled.
  • Vandalized: Similar because both describe damage inflicted on works or monuments; differs because vandalized stresses the criminal, malicious intent of the perpetrator, whereas disfigured focuses on the objective physical state of ruin, regardless of whether caused by malice, accident, or time.
