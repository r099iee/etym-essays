• tarried: Similar in denoting remaining in a place longer than planned; differs because tarried is an older, more formal or biblical term that often suggests an explicit delay or postponement in travel rather than an emotional, poetic reluctance to leave.
  • loitered: Similar in remaining in an area; differs because loitered carries a negative, suspicious, or purposeless connotation (often associated with vagrancy, wasting time, or idling in public spaces without lawful business).
  • dawdled: Similar in moving slowly and taking extra time; differs because dawdled emphasizes childish sluggishness, procrastination, or lack of discipline rather than atmospheric persistence or romantic attachment.
  • persisted: Similar in continuing to exist over time; differs because persisted conveys stubborn tenacity, willful determination, or an unwelcome endurance against obstacles, lacking the gentle, soft melancholy of lingered.
  • remained: Similar as a general term for staying in a location or condition; differs because remained is entirely neutral and objective, devoid of the affective hesitation, tenderness, or gradual fading implied by lingered.
  • dallied: Similar in delaying departure; differs because dallied emphasizes playful, frivolous, or amorous flirtation and time-wasting.
  • hovered: Similar in staying close to a spot; differs because hovered involves physical suspension in the air or anxious, nervous physical proximity.
  • lagged: Similar in falling behind in time or space; differs because lagged denotes physical fatigue, slowness, or inferior pace rather than voluntary hesitation.
  • waited: Similar in staying in place; differs because waited is goal-oriented, anticipating a specific person, event, or signal to occur.
  • protracted: Similar in extending duration; differs because protracted is transitive and formal, usually describing disputes, negotiations, or lawsuits dragged out excessively.
