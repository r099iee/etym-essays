- Old English (c. 700–1100) - Laboring & Striving: Originated as the verbal noun *teolung* / *tilling*, meaning striving, laboring, procuring, or cultivating the land.
- Middle English (c. 1200–1450) - Agrarian Specialization: Narrowed from general striving to the specific, foundational act of working, plowing, and preparing soil for agricultural crops (*tillynge*).
- Early Modern English (c. 1500–1700) - Scriptural & Classical Standard: Cemented in the Authorized King James Bible and agrarian treatises as the definitive literary term for agricultural cultivation.
- 18th to 19th Century - Agricultural Revolution: Became a cornerstone of agrarian science, describing mechanized plow technologies and soil fertility techniques.
- 20th to 21st Century - Environmental Agronomy: Standardized in ecological and conservation agronomy (e.g., intensive tilling vs. conservation tilling and no-till farming).
