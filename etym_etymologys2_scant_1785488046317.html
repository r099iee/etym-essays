Chain for SCANT (a Scandinavian borrowing; no letter labels A–F):
1. Proto-Germanic *skammaz 'short, brief' — the reconstructed West/North Germanic adjective; no secure PIE ancestor has been established (see note below).
2. Old Norse skammr 'short' (of space, time, or measure) — the North Germanic continuation; its neuter form skamt 'short of measure, a short while' is the direct source of the English adjective.
3. Middle English scant / scante 'short in measure, deficient, not full' — first recorded in the 14th century, adopted from the Scandinavian contact speech of northern and eastern England (the word shows the northern Norse influence that also gave English skin, sky, egg, and scant itself).
4. Modern English scant (adjective; also adverb 'scarcely' and verb 'to stint, limit' from the 16th–17th c.).
5. SCANT (the exact form on this card).

Note on the debated PIE stage: the Proto-Germanic form *skamma- is secure for North and West Germanic (Old Norse skammr and a small cognate set), but no convincing Indo-European etymon has been agreed upon. Older dictionaries suggested connections to roots meaning 'to cut, shorten' or to Latin and Greek words for 'bare, clipped', but these are speculative and are not accepted by modern scholarship (de Vaan and the OED treat the PIE ancestry as unknown). The attested record is therefore: PGmc *skamma- to ON skammr to ON neuter skamt to ME scant to ModE scant to SCANT. No stage is invented; the only uncertain point is the root's deeper pre-Germanic ancestry, which is stated here explicitly rather than guessed.
