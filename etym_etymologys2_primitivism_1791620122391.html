Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*per- / *pr̥h₂-
"forward, through, in front, early"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*pri-smo-
"foremost, first"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
prīmus
"first, earliest, foremost"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. CE)
prīmītīvus
"first-born, original, earliest"
↓
Old French (c. 13th c.)
primitif
"original, ancestral, primary"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.)
primitive
"earliest; original church"
↓
Modern English (c. 19th c.)
primitive + -ism
"advocacy of early forms"
↓
Modern English (c. 1900s)
primitivism
"idealization of archaic life or art"

Note: Latin prīmītīvus was formed from prīmus ('first') with the suffix -tīvus. In the 19th century, the suffix -ism (Greek -ismos) was appended in English and French art criticism to denote the intentional ideological or aesthetic movement celebrating primeval simplicity.
