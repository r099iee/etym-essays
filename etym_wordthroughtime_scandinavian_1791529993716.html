- 1st century CE: Latin Scandinavia names a northern island or region, probably Scania; the word is a learned geographical label, not a self-designation.
- Middle Ages: the name fades in ordinary use, while “Danes,” “Norwegians,” and “Swedes” dominate; Latin scholarship keeps Scandinavia alive.
- 18th century: “Scandinavian” enters English as a learned adjective for the northern kingdoms and their peoples.
- 19th century: Romantic nationalism and the Scandinavism movement give the word political and cultural charge; racial anthropology misuses it for a supposed “Scandinavian type.”
- 20th century: “Scandinavian” becomes a neutral regional and linguistic term, and “Scandinavian design” turns it into a global style label.
- 21st century: the word is a cultural brand (design, crime fiction, welfare models), while “Nordic” increasingly displaces it in official usage.
