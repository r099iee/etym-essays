Root word SLINKING, one linear derivation chain (Germanic; no certain cognates outside Germanic):
1. PIE *slenk- "to creep, crawl, wind" (prehistoric reconstruction; a movement-root of the kind used for serpents and vermin; secure only within Germanic).
2. Proto-Germanic *slinkaną "to creep, crawl" (reconstructed from Old English and Old High German).
3. Old English slincan "to creep, crawl, glide" (attested; used of creeping things).
4. Middle English slinken "to creep, move stealthily" (attested 14th c.).
5. English slink "to move stealthily or guiltily" (modern verb; the "guilt" nuance developed in the 16th–17th centuries).
6. English SLINKING (present participle, used as adjective/verbal noun) "moving stealthily, quietly, or guiltily; creeping."

Note on the debate: the Old High German slinkan, "to creep, crawl" (attested), confirms the Germanic root; no verifiable Indo-Aryan or Romance cognates are attested, so none are claimed. The rural specialization "slink calf" (a calf born prematurely) dates from the 17th century and rests on the root sense of something that slips away before its time.
