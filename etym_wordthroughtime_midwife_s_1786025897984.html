- Proto-Germanic *midi (with) & *wīβą (woman) - Ancient Germanic roots for female companions.
- Old English *mid (preposition "with") & *wīf (noun "woman").
- Middle English *midwif / *medewife (14th c.) - Compound for female childbirth attendant ("woman with the mother").
- 16th–17th Century - Centrality of midwives in community medicine; emergence of phrases like "midwife's tale".
- 18th–19th Century - Institutionalization of medicine, creating tension with traditional "midwife's tales".
- Modern / Contemporary usage - Revalorization of midwifery alongside metaphorical usage for intellectual catalysts.
