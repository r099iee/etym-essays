Origin not fully secure: the word family begins in Old French, and no Proto-Indo-European root can be demonstrated; the deeper ancestry of Old French trufle is debated.
→ Old French trufle / trufe (mockery, jest, trickery, deceit; also the truffle mushroom; 12th–13th c.) — itself of uncertain origin, possibly an expressive or dialectal formation, and possibly connected (the link is debated) with Latin tuber "a swelling, a truffle"
→ Middle English trufle / trifle (a false tale, a trick, a piece of mockery; later a thing of small value; 13th c.)
→ English trifle (n.) "a small matter, a thing of little value"; trifle (v.) "to mock, to deal idly, to play with" (14th–16th c.)
→ English TRIFLING (present participle and adjective, "unimportant, frivolous, mocking"; first recorded 16th c.) ◄ TARGET

Note on debate: Some etymologists connect trufle with Latin tuber "swelling, truffle," since the mushroom name is certain; but whether the mockery-word and the mushroom-word are the same word, or two distinct words that happened to converge in form, is genuinely uncertain. The English trifle dessert (17th c.) is a later, separate application of the "small matter" sense. The origin is therefore stated as uncertain rather than fabricated.
