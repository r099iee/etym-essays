• Princess: Similar because both name a royal daughter or prince's consort of fairy-tale rank; differs because princess is the generic Western title, while tsarevna is specifically Russian and always carries a Slavic folkloric aura.
  • Tsarina: Similar because both are feminine titles of the Russian imperial house; differs because a tsarina is the tsar's wife, a reigning or consort empress, while a tsarevna is his daughter, junior in power and precedence.
  • Tsarevich: Similar because both are children of the tsar sharing the same blood and fairy-tale world; differs because tsarevich is the male heir, the questing hero of the tales, while tsarevna is the daughter, so often the prize or the imperilled beauty.
  • Korolevna: Similar because both mean a king's or tsar's daughter in Slavic languages, built on the same -evna daughter-suffix; differs because korolevna derives from korol, king, a lesser crown, and lacks the imperial and English literary currency of tsarevna.
  • Infanta: Similar because both are daughters of an absolute monarch with marriage-diplomacy value; differs because infanta belongs to Spain and Portugal and to Catholic Habsburg politics, while tsarevna belongs to Orthodox Russia and its wonder-tales.
  • Grand duchess: Similar because after Peter the Great Russian tsarevnas were restyled grand duchesses at court; differs because grand duchess is the Westernised official style, while tsarevna is the older native word kept alive by folklore and English translation.
  • Maharani's daughter / Rajkumari: Similar because both name a royal daughter of a non-Western empire with fairy-tale resonance in English; differs because the Indian terms belong to a wholly different courtly world, while tsarevna is fixed to snow, steppe, and Slavdom.
  • Vasilisa: Similar because Vasilisa the Beautiful is the archetypal tsarevna-figure of Russian tales; differs because Vasilisa is a personal name, one famous bearer of the type, while tsarevna is the title and type itself.
