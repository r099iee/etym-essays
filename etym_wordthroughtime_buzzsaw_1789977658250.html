- 1830s–1840s mill tool — neutral technical nickname among lumbermen for the new screaming circular saw; admired for speed, feared for accidents.
- Late 19th c. workshop slang — affectionate working-class word; buzzsaw versus handsaw marks modern power versus muscle; connotation of progress and danger.
- Early–mid 20th c. comic simile — to snore like a buzzsaw becomes a stock humid joke; connotation shifts from tool to unbearable human noise, masculine and funny.
- Late 20th c. sports / political metaphor — to hit a buzzsaw means to be shredded by a superior opponent; connotation of ruthless efficiency, fear and awe.
- Contemporary horror / DIY — buzzsaw evokes both weekend workshop and slasher-film menace; simultaneously handy and terrifying, handy in store, terrifying without a guard.
