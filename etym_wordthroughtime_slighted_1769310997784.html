- Smooth and level (14th c.) - Borrowed from Old Norse into Middle English meaning smooth, flat, level, plain - a physical word about surfaces and even ground.
- Plain, of little account (14th-16th c.) - Widens from 'plain, ordinary' to 'small, slender, thin, insignificant': a slight figure is a slender one, a slight reason a weak one.
- The noun and verb of neglect (16th c.) - A slight becomes an act of neglect or disdain, and to slight is to treat with studied indifference - the metaphor of size turns social.
- Slighted: rendered small (16th c. onward) - The past participle names the wound of being treated as beneath notice; in Austen's Regency world, where attention was the currency of worth, to be slighted was to be publicly diminished.
- Modern usage - Literary and reflective; the quieter insult of omission rather than commission, the injury of being weighed and found small.
