- Old English roots — the parts wait: hand names the body-part and cræt names a wain, but no one has joined them yet.
- c. 1200 — cart settles in: Middle English cart, reinforced by Norse kartr, becomes the everyday word for two-wheeled vehicles.
- 17th c. — the compound is born: hand-cart appears for the human-powered sort, distinguishing porters' wheels from horse-drawn ones.
- 18th–19th c. — streets of handcarts: hawkers, dustmen, and railway porters make the handcart the signature vehicle of working towns.
- 20th c. — engines crowd it: vans and forklifts take the heavy work, but markets, stations, and golf courses keep the handcart alive.
- Today — the helper's wheels: handcarts of parcels, luggage, and fête chairs still block lanes before parties, exactly as at Bag End.
