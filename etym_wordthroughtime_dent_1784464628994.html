Old English Blow-Sense - Dent begins as dint, from the Old English dynt, "a blow, strike, stroke" — in early use the word names the violent action itself, the blow of a weapon, fist, or fate.
Middle English Force-Sense - Middle English dynt/dint still means the blow and its force; the fossil phrase "by dint of" ("by force of") preserves this older sense into modern English.
Early Modern Meaning-Slide - In the early modern period the variant spelling dent gains ground, and the meaning slides from the blow to the visible consequence of the blow — the pushed-in hollow left on metal, wood, or flesh.
Contemporary Sense - Today dent is the ordinary noun (and verb) for an impact-hollow — the scar of a blow — while dint survives only in the idiom "by dint of."
