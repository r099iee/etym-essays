Single linear chain for VEXING
Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European root *weǵh- (also *wegh-), "to carry, transport, move in a vehicle," c. 4500–3000 BCE. Reflexes: Sanskrit váhati "he drives, carries"; Greek ókhos "carriage"; Proto-Germanic *wegaz "road" (English way), *wegan "to carry, move" (English weigh).
Stage 2 — Latin vehō, vehere, vēxī, vectum, "to carry, convey, transport," attested 3rd–2nd c. BCE (Plautus); from PIE *weǵh-.
Stage 3 — Latin vexō, vexāre, vexāvī, vexātum, "to shake, jolt, toss; to harass, torment, annoy," attested 1st c. BCE (Caesar, Cicero); the frequentative/intensive of vehere — to keep hauling back and forth.
Stage 4 — Old French vexer, "to vex, harass, torment," attested 12th c.; from Latin vexāre.
Stage 5 — Anglo-Norman / Middle English vexen, "to vex, harass, afflict, torment," attested 14th c. (first c. 1330).
Stage 6 — English vex, 15th c., with the modern spelling settling; the present participle and participial adjective vexing, "causing vexation," attested 16th c. — FINAL NODE, the exact form used on this card.
Debate note: The derivation vehere → vexāre is standard and undisputed: vexāre is the frequentative formation of vehere, the -x- in vēxī/vexāre reflecting the old perfective stem *weǵ-s-. The PIE reconstruction *weǵh- (with palatalized velar) is standard; under Grimm's Law it yields Germanic *weg- (way, wagon, weigh). No stage of the chain is in doubt.
