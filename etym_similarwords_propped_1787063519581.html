• Supported: Similar because both mean bearing the weight of something to keep it upright; differs because supported is a broad, neutral, and permanent term (e.g., steel columns supporting a bridge), whereas propped explicitly suggests a temporary, makeshift, leaning, or precarious arrangement (often using an external brace or leaning posture).
  • Sustained: Similar because both involve maintaining someone or something in existence; differs because sustained implies enduring nourishment, internal strength, or prolonged continuity over time, while propped refers to an immediate physical brace preventing physical or structural collapse.
  • Bolstered: Similar because both mean reinforcing a weakened entity; differs because bolstered is often applied to intangible concepts like morale, confidence, arguments, or defense lines by adding reinforcing strength, whereas propped typically involves a physical slant, lean, or emergency post (often with the particle 'up').
  • Buttressed: Similar because both denote placing an external structure against an object to reinforce it; differs because buttressed specifically implies heavy, permanent, architectural masonry designed to withstand lateral thrust (e.g., flying buttresses of a cathedral), whereas propped implies temporary wooden stakes, poles, or human shoulders.
  • Shore (Shored up): Similar because both describe temporary bracing during repairs or danger; differs because shored up is a technical maritime and construction term involving elaborate timber frameworks to support walls or trenches, while propped is ubiquitous in everyday bodily postures and personal care.
  • Braced: Similar because both involve providing structural rigidity; differs because braced emphasizes tightening, securing against sudden impact or shock, or stiffening, whereas propped focuses on supporting downward gravitational weight or leaning against a barrier.
