• crane: Similar because the sārasa is biologically a true crane belonging to the family Gruidae; differs because crane is a broad, generic global term encompassing fifteen distinct species across four continents, whereas sārasa refers specifically to the towering Indian Sarus crane (*Antigone antigone*) imbued with unique mythological and literary stature.
  • krauñca: Similar because both refer to legendary wetland birds in classical Sanskrit literature and are frequently treated as virtually synonymous in *Rāmāyaṇa* commentaries; differs because krauñca is an archaic Vedic onomatopoeic name (evoking the bird's harsh trumpeting cry) specifically tied to the mythical Krauñca mountain and the original slaughter episode, whereas sārasa is a descriptive lacustrine name (*from saras*).
  • stork: Similar because both are large, long-legged wading birds frequenting marshes and wet grasslands; differs because storks belong to a completely distinct biological family (Ciconiidae), fly with necks extended without trumpeting trachea, and lack the lifelong monogamous devotion and rich poetic symbolism associated with the sārasa.
  • heron: Similar because both inhabit wetland margins, stalking shallow water for prey; differs because herons belong to the family Ardeidae, are generally smaller, fly with their long necks retracted in an S-curve, and lead solitary hunting lives rather than pairing in devoted lifelong duets.
  • egret: Similar because both are conspicuous, elegant wetland birds; differs because egrets are small-to-medium white herons (family Ardeidae) famous for delicate nuptial breeding plumes (*aigrettes*), lacking the towering six-foot height and slate-and-crimson plumage of the sārasa.
  • flamingo: Similar because both are tall, long-necked wetland birds with striking pink-red coloration; differs because flamingos belong to the order Phoenicopteriformes, possess specialized filter-feeding down-curved bills for straining brine, and live in massive colonial flocks rather than tight-knit monogamous pairs.
  • brolga: Similar because the brolga (*Antigone rubicunda*) is the closest biological relative of the Sarus crane, sharing the same genus, courtship dances, and grey-and-red plumage; differs because the brolga is native exclusively to Australia and New Guinea, entirely lacking the Sanskrit literary pedigree and sacred epic history of the Indian sārasa.
  • ibis: Similar because both are revered in ancient religious traditions and inhabit wetlands; differs because the ibis (family Threskiornithidae) has a distinct downward-curving sickle bill, is much smaller, and was sacred to ancient Egyptian Thoth, rather than serving as the muse of Sanskrit poetic meter.
  • swan / cygnus (haṃsa): Similar because both are celebrated in Sanskrit literature as noble aquatic birds symbolizing purity and spiritual grace; differs because the haṃsa (swan/goose) is renowned for intellectual discrimination (separating milk from water, *nīra-kṣīra-viveka*), whereas the sārasa is celebrated specifically for romantic fidelity and tragic grief.
