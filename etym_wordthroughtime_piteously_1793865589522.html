- c. 14th c. [Compassionately / Devoutly / Mercifully] - Initially used in Middle English to describe acting out of tenderhearted Christian charity, filial piety, or sacred duty; doing something 'pitously' meant performing an act of holy mercy or religious reverence.
- c. 14th–16th c. [Wretchedly / Mournfully / In Agony] - Underwent a major perspectival shift from the dispenser of mercy to the victim of misfortune; came to describe crying, begging, or suffering in a state of utter affliction that violently wrenches the sympathies of onlookers.
- c. 17th–18th c. [Pathetically / Tragically] - Standardized within Shakespearean and Jacobean drama as an evocative aesthetic marker of pure Aristotelian pathos, describing the heart-rending cries of abandoned lovers, doomed monarchs, and tragic heroines.
- 19th c.–Present [Desperately / Helplessly / Poignantly] - Retained its high literary and dramatic register; today it describes expressions of deep, vulnerable agony (human or animal) with zero sarcasm, distinguishing itself cleanly from the often pejorative modern sense of 'pathetically'.
