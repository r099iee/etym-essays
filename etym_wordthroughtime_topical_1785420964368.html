- Late 16th century (rhetoric, from Greek topos 'place') - of or dealing with 'topics' or commonplaces: general, often superficial treatment of a subject, as well as 'of a place'.
- 17th–18th centuries (medicine, geography) - the 'local' sense solidifies: applied to a particular place or part of the body (topical remedies).
- 19th century (journalism, literature) - a new sense rises: relating to the topics of the day; 'topical allusion' means timely public reference, a modish, news-aware register.
- 20th century (Empson's age) - the news-value sense dominates: topical = currently fashionable in public debate, sometimes faintly dismissive of the ephemeral.
- Present day - chiefly 'of immediate current interest' (topical jokes, topical debates); the older local and medical senses survive only as specialist terms.
