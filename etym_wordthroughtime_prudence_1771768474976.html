- [14th century the cardinal virtue] - [Enters as one of the cardinal virtues — practical wisdom that weighs consequences and chooses the safest, most honourable course; connotation of moral excellence, the reason that governs conduct]
- [17th-18th century cold arithmetic] - [Christian moral theology makes prudence direct all other virtues, but the age also narrows it toward cautious self-interest; Gibbon's 'prudence and propriety' governing his broken engagement is the cool arithmetic of the marriage market]
- [19th-20th century thrift and care] - [Settles toward caution, economy, and risk-avoidance — prudent investment, prudent management; connotation safe and careful]
- [20th-21st century everyday caution] - [In ordinary use prudence means careful avoidance of risk; the full classical weight survives mostly in moral philosophy and in irony, where the calculating heart is quietly judged]
