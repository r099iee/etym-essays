• Bow: Similar because a bow is also a bodily sign of respect or submission; differs because a bow is a shallow, reversible inclination exchanged between near-equals, while a kotow is a full forehead-to-ground prostration before a superior.
  • Grovel: Similar in conveying abject self-abasement before authority; differs because grovel is purely figurative and emotional—begging, crawling, humbling oneself—while kotow still carries the image of the literal ritual.
  • Fawn: Similar in submissive, ingratiating behaviour toward superiors; differs because fawning is verbal flattery and wagging obsequiousness, whereas kowtowing stresses physical prostration and surrender of dignity.
  • Prostrate: Similar in the flat, ground-level posture of submission; differs because prostrate describes the physical position itself (or utter defeat), while kotow is the specific ritual act of knocking the forehead in homage.
  • Obeisance: Similar as a general word for a reverent gesture of submission; differs because obeisance is a bookish umbrella term for any homage, while kotow names the specific Chinese prostration.
  • Curtsy: Similar as a bodily gesture of deference; differs because a curtsy is a feminine bending of the knees in Western etiquette, while a kotow is the full Chinese forehead-to-floor prostration.
  • Genuflect: Similar as a bending of the body in submission or reverence; differs because genuflection touches only one knee to the floor, a liturgical gesture of European churches, far lighter than the kotow.
  • Toady: Similar in sycophantic self-abasement before the powerful; differs because toadying is active, self-interested flattery in search of advantage, while kotow stresses the ritual surrender of dignity.
