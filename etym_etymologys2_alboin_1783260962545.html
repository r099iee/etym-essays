ALBOIN is a two-element Germanic compound; the two elements have separate chains from separate Indo-European roots. CHAIN 1 — THE FIRST ELEMENT (Alb-, "elf"): Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *albho- "white, bright" (3rd millennium BCE; also the root of Latin albus "white" and, via Latin, English albino, album, and the name Albion). Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *albiz / *albaz "elf" (reconstructed; literally "the white, shining being" — the elves of Germanic paganism were luminous spirits, not Victorian fairies). Stage 3 — Langobardic (Lombardic) alb- "elf" (attested as the name-element of the Lombard king himself, Langobardic Albwin; also the general Germanic name-element in Ælfwine, Alboin, etc.). CHAIN 2 — THE SECOND ELEMENT (-oin, from -win, "friend"): Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *wenh₁- "to desire, strive for, win, love" (3rd millennium BCE; also the root of Latin Venus, venia, and Old Irish fine "kindred"). Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *winiz "friend, beloved" (reconstructed; cf. Old English wine "friend, lord, companion of the comitatus"). Stage 3 — Langobardic (Lombardic) -win "friend" (name-element attested in Lombardic royal and noble names). COMPOUND PATH: Stage 1 — Langobardic Albwin "elf-friend" (the historical king's own form of the name; 6th century CE). Stage 2 — Latinized Alboinus / Albuin (the form in Latin sources, chiefly Paul the Deacon's Historia Langobardorum, 8th century CE). Stage 3 — English ALBOIN (modern English historical spelling, transmitted through medieval chronicle and 18th–19th-century historiography). FINAL NODE. NOTE: The Old English name Ælfwine "elf-friend" is the exact structural equivalent, showing the same two elements built independently in another Germanic language — a Wessex man could bear the same name as the Lombard king.
