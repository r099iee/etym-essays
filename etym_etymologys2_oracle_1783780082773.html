PIE *ōs- (Proto-Indo-European, "mouth"; reconstructed on the reflexes Hittite āiš "mouth" [attested in 2nd-millennium BCE cuneiform], Sanskrit ās- "mouth, face," Avestan āh- "mouth," Latin ōs, ōris "mouth"; the root is also written *h₁eh₃-s- in laryngeal notation, with the variant stem *ōss-; the older card's *ōr- is NOT the root — ōr- is merely the Latin stem of ōs/ōris) → Latin ōs, ōris ("mouth"; classical) → Latin ōrāre ("to speak with the mouth, plead, pray"; developed from ōs by adding the denominative suffix; the sense shifted from plain speaking toward formal pleading and prayer) → Latin ōrāculum ("a divine utterance, an oracle, the shrine where a god is consulted"; formed with the instrumental suffix -culum from the stem ōrā-; classical, 1st c. BCE–CE) → Old French oracle (12th–13th c.) → Middle English oracle (c. 1400; religious texts, Chaucer and his contemporaries) → Modern English Oracle ◄ TARGET NOTE: the development "mouth → speak → pray → divine utterance → shrine → medium → authoritative expert" is fully attested within Latin itself; the root is the "mouth" root *ōs-, not a root meaning "speak." No stages are conjectural beyond the standard reconstruction of the PIE root.
