- [16th century: to cover with a crust] - 'Encrust' enters English meaning to cover a surface with a hard outer layer, first of mineral or stony crusts; connotation is geological and material.
- [17th-19th century: the crust of gems] - Applied to jewellery and regalia, 'jewel-encrusted' describes objects whose gems form a continuous, hardening layer over the base; connotation becomes princely and sacred - the decoration of royal crowns, reliquaries, and treasuries.
- [Contemporary sense] - A compound adjective for anything densely covered with jewels; connotation is opulent and hyperbolic - a surface so thickly set with gems that the base material all but disappears, the vocabulary of the treasure-house and of its parody.
