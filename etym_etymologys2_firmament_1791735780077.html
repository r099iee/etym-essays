Note: The conceptual evolution of 'firmament' represents a fascinating cross-linguistic translation chain. In the Hebrew Masoretic text of Genesis 1:6, the word is 'rāqīaʿ' (רָקִיעַ), derived from the verbal root 'rāqaʿ' ('to beat out, stamp flat, hammer out thin sheets of metal', as in hammering gold into plates). When Jewish scholars translated the Torah into Greek for the Septuagint (3rd c. BCE), they chose 'steréōma' (στερέωμα, 'solid foundation, firm structure'), reflecting Hellenistic cosmological models of solid crystalline spheres. Saint Jerome closely rendered 'steréōma' into the Latin Vulgate as 'firmāmentum' (from 'firmus' solid + suffix '-mentum'), permanently baking the concept of solidity into European translations.
