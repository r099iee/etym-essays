- Gothic and Ostrogothic tradition - Theodoric, ruler of the people, was the Ostrogothic king who from Ravenna ruled Italy (493-526) as the heir of Rome, preserving Roman law and culture, and so his name stood from the start for the fusion of Germanic power with Roman civilization.
- Medieval German legend - the same man was transformed into Dietrich of Bern, the dragon-slayer of the Nibelungenlied, so the historical ruler's reputation shaded into heroic folklore.
- 18th-19th-century historiography - Gibbon and Macaulay made Theodoric the centrepiece of the catalogue of Teutonic dynasty-founders, a zealous Christian (albeit an Arian) whose enlightened kingship proved the civilizing power of the Roman inheritance.
- Contemporary sense - Theodoric remains a byword for the enlightened barbarian king, valued for the Roman senate, roads, aqueducts, and mosaics he preserved, and for the paradox that the ruler of the people outlived his empire in art and legend.
