MINCE-PIES is a compound of two independent root words, MINCE and PIE(S); each has its own derivation chain. CHAIN 1 — MINCE (the “chopped small” strand): 1. Proto-Indo-European *mei- (1) “small, to lessen, to diminish” (reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE; the root behind Latin minus “less” and minor “smaller”; Pokorny's *mei- “small”). 2. Latin minuere “to lessen, reduce” and minutus “small, made small, reduced to tiny pieces” (attested from the 2nd–1st c. BCE). 3. Late/Vulgar Latin *minutiare “to chop into small pieces” (reconstructed from Romance outcomes; the denominative verb built on minutus). 4. Old French mincier / menuzer “to cut small, to chop” (9th–11th c. attested), whence Anglo-French and Middle English forms. 5. Middle English mincen “to chop fine, to cut into small bits” (attested late 14th c.; Chaucer's minceth / y-minced “finely cut”). 6. Modern English mince “to cut into very small pieces” (verb), also noun and participial adjective minced. 7. English mince in the fixed compounds mincemeat and the card's MINCE-PIES (the dish-name “mync’d pies” attested from the late 16th c., modern mince-pies). CHAIN 2 — PIE(S) (the “mixed filling under crust” strand): 1. Latin pīca “magpie” (attested from the 1st c. BCE), the pied, black-and-white bird known for gathering odds and ends; the bird-name’s own deeper origin is debated (possibly imitative of its chatter, or from an adjective “pied, painted”; not securely traced to PIE). 2. (Late/Old French) Old French pie “magpie” (9th–11th c.). 3. Middle English pie “baked pastry with a mixed filling” (attested c. 1300); the standard etymology is a nickname: the pie's miscellaneous contents were likened to a magpie's gathered hoard (the bird also itself called a pie in medieval English). The link is traditional but not absolutely certain; some scholars derive the pastry sense from a word for “pasty, folded”, though the magpie route is the usual scholarly answer. 4. Modern English pie, plural pies; the compound mince-pie built on it. 5. English pies in the card's MINCE-PIES (final node of this chain).
