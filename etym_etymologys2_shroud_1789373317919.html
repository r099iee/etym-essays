PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*(s)ker- / *(s)kreu-
"to cut, sever, cleave, snip off"
Note: Base root *(s)ker- with dental extension *(s)kreud- ('piece cut off'). Cognate with English shear, share, shred, and short.
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE)
*skrudą
"something cut off; piece of cloth, fabric, garment, dress"
↓
Old English (c. 8th–11th c.)
scrūd
"garment, clothing, dress, vesture; protective covering"
Note: Pronounced with initial /skr-/ and long /uː/. Regular palatalization of initial /sk-/ to /ʃ/ occurred during the late Old English to Early Middle English period.
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
shroud / schroud / shroude
"garment, clothing; winding-sheet for a corpse; rope stay for a ship's mast"
Note: During Middle English, the meaning specialized from general clothing to funerary winding-sheets, while nautical usage developed for mast-supporting rigging.
↓
Early Modern English (15th–17th c.)
shroud
"burial sheet, mortuary cloth; nautical rigging; (verb) to envelop, veil, conceal"
Note: Great Vowel Shift transformed Middle English long /uː/ into diphthong /aʊ/. Everyday clothing sense became obsolete.
↓
