• opiates: Similar because they are stupor-inducing poppy drugs; differs because opiates specifically derive from opium, while narcotics is the wider legal class.
  • opioids: Similar because they are the modern medical term for narcotic painkillers; differs because opioids includes synthetics like fentanyl, while narcotics carries legal/moral overtones.
  • drugs: Similar because it covers psychoactive substances generally; differs because drugs is neutral and broad, narcotics implies stupor or illegality.
  • sedatives: Similar because they calm and induce sleep; differs because sedatives (benzodiazepines) relieve anxiety more than pain.
  • tranquillizers / tranquillisers: Similar because they dull agitation; differs because they suggest minor calmatives, not strong stupor.
  • depressants: Similar because they slow the nervous system; differs because depressants is the technical opposite of stimulants, while narcotics is legally stretched.
  • analgesics / painkillers: Similar because narcotics relieve pain; differs because many analgesics (paracetamol) cause no stupor.
  • anaesthetics: Similar because they produce numbness; differs because anaesthetics abolish sensation entirely for surgery, narcotics cloud it.
  • soporifics / hypnotics: Similar because they induce sleep; differs because they emphasise sleep, not pain relief or euphoria.
  • intoxicants: Similar because they inebriate and impair; differs because intoxicants centres on alcohol-like drunkenness, narcotics on stupor.
  • stupefacients: Similar because they literally stupefy, including fish poisons; differs because stupefacient is rare and technical, narcotics is common.
  • controlled substances: Similar because it is the legal synonym for regulated drugs; differs because it is bureaucratic and neutral, narcotics is emotive.
  • dope: Similar because slang for illicit drugs; differs because dope is informal and can mean fool or information.
