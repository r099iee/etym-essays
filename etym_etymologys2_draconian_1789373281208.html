Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*derḱ-
"to see, look, glance, flash with the eyes"
(source of Sanskrit darśana "vision, philosophy", Old English torht "bright, clear")
↓
Ancient Greek Verb (archaic/classical period)
δέρκομαι (dérkomai)
"to see clearly, gaze, cast a piercing glance"
↓
Ancient Greek Substantive Noun (archaic period)
δράκων (drákōn)
"serpent, dragon; literally 'the keen-sighted one, piercing gazer'"
↓
Ancient Greek Historical Personal Name (c. 621 BCE)
Δράκων (Drákōn)
"Draco, Athenian archon and lawgiver"
↓
Latin Historical Borrowing (classical period)
Draco / Dracōnem
"Draco, ancient Athenian legislator"
↓
Latin Adjectival Suffix (classical/post-classical)
-iānus
"pertaining to, adhering to"

Late Modern English (19th c.–present)
DRACONIAN
