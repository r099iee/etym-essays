• strict: Similar because both describe rules enforced exactly, without indulgence; differs because strict is the plain everyday word and stresses obedience, while stringent is formal and stresses the tightness of the requirement itself.
  • rigorous: Similar because both imply exacting standards and no loopholes; differs because rigorous is chiefly a term of praise for thorough method or logic, whereas stringent often sounds burdensome and is applied to regulations and budgets.
  • severe: Similar because both can describe harsh conditions; differs because severe measures the weight of suffering or punishment, while stringent measures how little latitude a rule allows.
  • harsh: Similar because they overlap in describing unpleasant demands; differs because harsh is openly emotional and condemning, while stringent can be a neutral technical description of regulatory tightness.
  • tight: Similar because both are built on the image of something drawn close; differs because tight is informal and physical (tight rules, tight budget), whereas stringent belongs to formal prose.
  • exacting: Similar because both describe demands that permit no error; differs because exacting focuses on the effort and precision required of a person, while stringent focuses on the binding force of the rule.
  • rigid: Similar because both suggest inflexibility; differs because rigid implies inability to adapt and is often a criticism, while stringent implies deliberately tight design and can be a defence.
  • draconian: Similar because both describe severe measures; differs because draconian is strongly pejorative, evoking excessive punishment, and carries no suggestion of precision.
  • astringent: Similar because it is the etymological twin, from the same Latin verb, and originally meant contracting or binding; differs because in modern use it is literal (a substance that puckers tissue) or metaphorical for sharp, biting criticism.
  • inflexible: Similar because neither allows exceptions; differs because inflexible describes a disposition or a refusal to change, while stringent describes the content and force of a requirement.
  • demanding: Similar because both imply high requirements; differs because demanding is neutral-to-positive and people-focused, while stringent is impersonal and institutional.
