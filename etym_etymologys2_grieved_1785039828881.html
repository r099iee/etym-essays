PIE *gʷreh₂- (older reconstruction *gwer-) 'heavy, weighty' - Latin gravis 'heavy, grave, burdensome' (classical Latin; the adjective of weight in every sense — physical, moral, and social) - Latin gravāre 'to burden, weigh down' (denominative verb built on gravis) - Old French grever 'to afflict, burden, oppress' (from Latin gravāre; current in French from the 12th century, feeding English from c. 1300) - Old French grief 'burden, injury, wrong' (noun from grever) — borrowed into Middle English as grief - Middle English greven 'to burden, sadden; to feel sorrow' (c. 1300, from Old French grever) - Modern English grieved (past tense and past participle of grieve; the emotional sense — 'to feel sorrow' — firmly established in Early Modern English)
