- Medieval sack - bagge means a small sack or pack; perception workaday-peasant, seed, tools, and game carried.
- 1743 loose clothing - baggy means hanging like a bag; connotation of ill-fit and comfort, borrowed clothes and ageing bodies.
- 1920s Oxford bags - baggy trousers as fashion, wide plus-fours and flannels; from shame to swagger, students flaunting excess cloth.
- Mid-20th century critical transfer - baggy novels, baggy narratives; looseness becomes aesthetic fault, editors urging cuts.
- Contemporary lexical diagnosis - predator baggy with misuse; from trousers to terms, retaining stretched shapelessness through overuse, as in words worn sloppy.
