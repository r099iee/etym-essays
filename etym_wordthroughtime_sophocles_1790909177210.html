- 5th Century BCE (Classical Athens) - Celebrated as Athens' most successful tragic playwright, winning roughly 24 victories at the Dionysia, honored as statesman and general.
- 4th Century BCE (Aristotelian Era) - Established by Aristotle in the *Poetics* as the golden standard and supreme master of tragic plot construction, peripeteia, and anagnorisis.
- Hellenistic & Byzantine Eras (3rd c. BCE – 14th c. CE) - Preserved in the classical canon of the "Seven Tragedies", studied continuously as a pinnacle of Attic Greek eloquence.
- Renaissance & Enlightenment (16th–18th c.) - Rediscovered and translated, establishing the foundational principles of modern Western dramatic theory.
- 19th–20th Century - Reinterpreted through psychological, philosophical, and existential lenses (Hegel on *Antigone*, Freud on *Oedipus Rex*, Kierkegaard, Camus).
- Contemporary Era - Universal cultural touchstone for high tragic drama, ethical dilemmas, and deep psychological storytelling.
