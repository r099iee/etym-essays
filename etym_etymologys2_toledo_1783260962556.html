CHAIN — THE PRE-ROMAN CELTIBERIAN TOPONYM: Stage 1 — Pre-Roman Celtiberian (Carpetanian) base *tolet- / *tul- — a fortified or raised place on the hill above the Tagus; the original language and precise meaning are UNCERTAIN, though a Celtic base meaning "hill, rising ground" (cf. Old Irish tulach "hillock, mound", Welsh twl "hill") is the traditional explanation. Stage 2 — Latin Toletum "Toledo" (attested from the 2nd–1st century BC — in Livy and later Pliny; the town of the Carpetani on the Tagus, later a Roman municipium). Stage 3 — Andalusian Arabic Ṭulayṭulah (طليطلة) "Toledo" (attested from the 8th century under Islamic rule until 1085; the Arabic rendering preserves the Latin base with a characteristic vocalic pattern). Stage 4 — Old Spanish Toledo (after the Christian reconquest of 1085; the form with Spanish loss of the old ending, alongside the adjective Toletano "Toledan"). Stage 5 — Modern Spanish Toledo and English Toledo (proper name; in English from the 16th century — and in the same period a byname for a fine sword, "a Toledo"). DEBATE NOTE: the Celtiberian etymology is not certain. The *tul- "hill" derivation is traditional and plausible for a town on a steep promontory, but the original pre-Roman form and meaning are lost; no Indo-European root can be securely demonstrated, and one must say so rather than invent a stage. The Latin-to-Arabic-to-Spanish-to-English chain is historically secure.
