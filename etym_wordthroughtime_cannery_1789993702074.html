- Old container word — canne, a cup or vessel for drink and grain; domestic and small-scale for centuries.
- Early 1800s — the tin can invented (Appert's method, Durand's 1810 patent): can reborn as an airtight food technology; verb to can and noun canning coined.
- 1860s–1880s — industrial canning spreads (oysters, salmon, fruit); on the model of bakery/brewery, American English coins cannery for the factory itself.
- Early–mid 20th century — the cannery as labour icon: salmon canneries of Alaska, fruit canneries of California, sardine canneries of Monterey; seasonal, migrant, union-organising; Steinbeck's Cannery Row (1945) fixed the word in literature.
- Late 20th century to present — automation and offshoring shrank the old giants; the word turned nostalgic-industrial, then artisanal again with craft canneries and conservas culture.
