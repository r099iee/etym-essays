• Neck: Similar because it names the same region of the body behind the head; differs because neck covers the whole cervical region as the neutral anatomical default, whereas nape selects only the back of the neck and adds a note of vulnerability and intimacy.
  • Scruff: Similar because it denotes the back of the neck; differs because scruff is the informal word used chiefly of animals — grabbing a dog by the scruff — and carries a graceless, disciplinary charge that no ornate or romantic prose would tolerate.
  • Nucha: Similar because it is the anatomical term for the back of the neck; differs because nucha is the technical medical term from medieval Latin, confined to scientific registers, while nape is the everyday, literary word.
  • Occiput: Similar because it names the back of the head where the nape begins; differs because occiput is the back of the skull itself, not the neck, and would be anatomically wrong in a context describing the neck.
  • Back of the neck: Similar because it denotes the identical anatomical site; differs because it is a plain descriptive phrase — serviceable but flat — lacking the single precise noun that a translator or poet needs for rhythm and for the word's reserve of tenderness.
  • Cervix: Similar because it is an anatomical term for the neck region; differs because cervix is wholly technical and, in modern English, hopelessly ambiguous with the uterine cervix, so it cannot serve the literary use of nape.
