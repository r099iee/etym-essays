Note on historical debate: Etymologists (including the Oxford English Dictionary and Walther von Wartburg's Französisches Etymologisches Wörterbuch) note that while Anglo-Norman havok is an alteration of Old French havot ("pillage, plunder"), the ultimate source is debated. The predominant philological consensus traces havot to a Germanic borrowing via Old Frankish *haf ("hook, grab, handle"), ultimately from PIE *kap- ("to grasp, seize"), cognate with Old English hæft and Modern English have. A minority alternate view proposed a derivation from Old French have ("pale, gaunt, sickly"), but this semantic connection lacks documentary evidence compared to the direct martial association with grasping booty.
