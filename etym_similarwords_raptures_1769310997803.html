• Ecstasy: Similar because both denote overwhelming delight that transports the person; differs because ecstasy (from Greek ek-stasis, "standing outside oneself") implies a trance-like, out-of-body absorption, while raptures emphasises the active expression of delighted enthusiasm and is more likely to be theatrical or pluralised ("in raptures").
  • Bliss: Similar because both name intense happiness; differs because bliss is serene, peaceful, heavenly joy (often religious or domestic contentment), while raptures are passionate, noisy, and overwhelming — bliss is calm, rapture is volcanic.
  • Delight: Similar because both denote great pleasure; differs because delight is a broad, moderate, everyday word for enjoyment, while raptures is an intense, literary, hyperbolic extreme — one is delighted by a cake, but in raptures over it.
  • Exultation: Similar because both mean triumphant, expressive joy; differs because exultation (from Latin ex-saltare, "to leap up") is joy grounded in victory or success and is publicly triumphant, while raptures is ecstatic transport before beauty, art, or love and need not involve triumph.
  • Euphoria: Similar because both mean intense well-being and delight; differs because euphoria is a clinical/modern term for a drug- or illness-induced state of exaggerated well-being, whereas raptures is a literary, aesthetic, pre-clinical word for joyful transport.
  • Joy: Similar because both name positive emotion; differs because joy is the broad, basic noun for happiness in general, while raptures is a specialised, pluralised, literary peak of joy that overwhelms and "carries away" the subject.
  • Transport: Similar because both describe being carried away by emotion; differs because transport is a rarer, more archaic literary term (used in the plural "transports of joy") and functions almost as a synonym in fixed phrases, while raptures is the everyday-but-literary word for the same state.
  • Enthusiasm: Similar because both can mean keen, eager pleasure; differs because enthusiasm is a neutral, moderate, action-oriented word (inspired eagerness for an activity), while raptures is an intense, emotional, passive-experiential extreme.
