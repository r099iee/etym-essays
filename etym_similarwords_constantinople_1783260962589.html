• Byzantium: Similar because it names the same geographic site before Constantine's refoundation; differs because Byzantium is the ancient Greek colony (founded c. 657 BCE) that preceded the imperial city, and in loose scholarly usage it also labels the Eastern Empire itself.
  • Istanbul: Similar because it is the modern name of the same living city on the same site; differs because Istanbul is the Turkish name in use since the Ottoman conquest (from the Greek phrase "eis ten polin," "to the City"), officially adopted in the 20th century, while Constantinople designates the Byzantine imperial era.
  • New Rome: Similar because Constantinople was officially styled "New Rome" (Nova Roma) at its foundation; differs because New Rome is an honorific title echoing the old capital, whereas Constantinople is the substantive toponym built from the founder's name.
  • Rome: Similar because both were the two great imperial capitals of the Roman world and Constantinople was modelled on Rome; differs because Rome remained the ancient Western capital and the seat of the Latin papacy, while Constantinople was the Christian Eastern capital for over a millennium.
  • Tsargrad: Similar because it is the Slavic name for Constantinople, meaning "Caesar's city"; differs because Tsargrad is the Old Slavonic/Byzantine diplomatic name used by the Slavs and Russians, not the Greek or English toponym.
  • Konstantiniyye: Similar because it is the Ottoman Turkish name for the city used from 1453 into the early 20th century; differs because Konstantiniyye is the Ottoman/Islamic official form, whereas Constantinople is the Western and Byzantine form of the same Greek name.
  • Capital: Similar because Constantinople was the archetypal imperial capital; differs because capital is the generic administrative term applying to any seat of government, while Constantinople names the specific millennium-spanning Byzantine capital.
  • Metropolis: Similar because Constantinople was the model great city, the mother-city of commerce and empire; differs because metropolis is a general term for a leading city, whereas Constantinople is the concrete historical Queen of Cities.
  • The City: Similar because "the City" (he Polis) was Constantinople's own name par excellence; differs because as a common phrase "the City" can mean any dominant city (notably the City of London's financial district), while in Byzantine Greek it meant Constantinople alone.
  • Hagia Sophia: Similar because the great church is the surviving symbol of Constantinople and the Byzantine millennium; differs because Hagia Sophia is the name of the cathedral-mosque-museum, a building, not the toponym of the city itself.
