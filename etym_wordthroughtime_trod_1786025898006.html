- Old English (treodan / træd / troden) - A fundamental Germanic strong verb denoting literal walking, stepping upon earth, or trampling under heavy boots, carrying strong agricultural and martial connotations.
- Middle English (treden / trad / troden) - Expanded into Biblical and theological registers to express divine wrath, the pressing of grapes in winepresses, and moral paths of righteousness or sin.
- Early Modern English (16th–17th c.) - Developed theatrical idioms such as 'trod the boards' (acting on stage) and poetic imagery of human sorrow, tyranny, and mortality.
- 19th Century (Romantic & Victorian Literature) - Strongly associated with solitary journeying, rural desolation, industrial toil, and philosophical contemplation of untrodden ways.
- Modern & Contemporary English - Retained chiefly in literary, poetic, and idiomatic contexts ('a well-trodden path', 'untrodden wilderness'), carrying a nostalgic, serious, or reverent tone.
