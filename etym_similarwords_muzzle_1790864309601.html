• Snout: Similar because both refer to the projecting nose and mouth of an animal; differs because snout often connotes a broader, rougher, or upturned nose (such as that of a pig, crocodile, or tapir) and can carry an uncomplimentary human connotation, whereas muzzle is the standard dignified anatomical term for horses, dogs, and bears and also denotes the protective restraining guard.
  • Gag: Similar because both mean to silence someone and prevent vocalization; differs because a gag is a cloth or object stuffed inside or tied across the mouth, whereas a muzzle is an external cage or harness fitted over the outside of the snout, and gag emphasizes choking or acute physical restraint.
  • Censor: Similar because both denote suppressing free expression and communication; differs because censor is an official, institutional, or bureaucratic act of redacting or banning specific content, whereas muzzle is a vivid, forceful metaphor evoking physical silencing and subjugation.
  • Beak / Bill: Similar because both are the projecting mouthparts of animals; differs because a beak or bill is the rigid, toothless, keratinous jaw structure belonging exclusively to birds, turtles, and monotremes, whereas a muzzle belongs to furred, fleshy-lipped mammals.
  • Silence: Similar because to muzzle someone is to silence them; differs because silence is a neutral, broad verb meaning to stop sound or speech by any means, whereas muzzle carries a strong negative connotation of repressive, forcible suppression.
  • Proboscis: Similar because both are prominent facial projections; differs because a proboscis is an elongated, flexible tube or trunk-like snout (such as an elephant's trunk or an insect's feeding organ), whereas a muzzle is the solid bony facial jaw structure.
