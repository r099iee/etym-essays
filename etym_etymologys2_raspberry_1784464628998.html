RASPBERRY is a compound whose two elements have separate histories; a complete linear chain is given for each root word in isolation. Chain for the second element, -berry: PIE ancestry uncertain and debated (a connection to a root meaning 'bright, shining' has been proposed but is not accepted) → Proto-Germanic *basją 'berry' (also *bazją) → Old English berie 'berry' (8th–11th c.) → Middle English bery(e) 'berry' (12th–15th c.) → Modern English berry, the second element of RASPBERRY. Note on the debate: *basją is a solid Germanic reconstruction with cognates in all the major branches (Old Norse ber, Old High German beri), but its deeper Indo-European connections remain uncertain; some scholars relate it to words for 'bright/glossy,' others leave the ultimate root open. Chain for the first element, rasp-: Medieval Latin raspecia/raspis (12th c. and later) 'a sweet reddish wine; also the reddish berry,' of uncertain origin (perhaps connected to Latin/Romance raspa 'bunch of grapes,' itself of obscure source) → Middle English raspis 'raspberry; a raspberry-coloured wine' (15th c.) → sixteenth-century English raspis-berry (the native berry added to the foreign form) → Modern English RASPBERRY (fused compound established by the early 17th c., with the internal -s- of raspis lost). Note on the debate and the folk etymology: the common claim that the name comes from the verb rasp (Old French rasper, of Frankish origin, 'to scrape') because the berry's surface is rough is a folk-motivated association; the documentary spine runs wine-name → fruit-name → compound, and the rasp-tool connection, while plausible as an influence, is not the attested source.
