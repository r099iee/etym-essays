PIE (reconstructed)
*h₂er-
"to fit together, join, assemble"
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*armo-
"joints, fitted equipment, weapons"
↓
Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
arma (neuter plural)
"tools, implements of war, weapons"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
armāre
"to furnish with weapons, equip with armor"
↓
Latin (reversive prefix compound)
dis- + armāre
"to strip of weapons, un-arm"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
desarmer
"to strip of armor or weapons"
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
desarmen / disarmen
"to strip of armor, deprive of weapons"
↓
Modern English (participial adjective, 19th c.)
disarming
"allaying hostility through charm or frankness"
Note: The base root *h₂er- ("to fit together") also produced harmony, art, and articulate. The Latin prefix dis- acts as a privative reversive, undoing the state of being equipped with weapons.
