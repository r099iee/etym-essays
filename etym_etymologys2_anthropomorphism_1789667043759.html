Chain 1 — anthropo-

Ancient Greek (8th c. BCE)
anthropos
"human being, person"
↓
Hellenistic Greek (c. 300 BCE–300 CE)
anthropomorphos
"of human form"
↓
French (18th c.)
anthropomorphisme
"human-shaped deity doctrine"
↓
ANTHROPOMORPHISM
"humanising non-humans"

Chain 2 — -morphism

Ancient Greek (8th c. BCE)
morphe
"form, shape, appearance"
↓
Hellenistic Greek (c. 300 BCE–300 CE)
-morphos, -morphia
"-shaped, -formation"
↓
Modern Greek / French (17th–18th c.)
-morphisme
"condition of having form"
↓
Modern English (1753–present)
ANTHROPOMORPHISM
"attributing human form"

Note: deeper PIE origins of both Greek bases are insecure and therefore omitted beyond Greek. Beekes treats anthropos and morphe as possibly Pre-Greek substrate with no secure PIE root; the traditional aner plus ops face derivation for anthropos is folk-etymological rather than proven. English first records anthropomorphism in 1753. The Greek-to-French-to-English path is secure; anything before Greek is debated.
