Linear derivation chain for FAGGOTS (a single line of descent, earliest attested form to present): 1) Greek phákelos (φάκελος) "bundle, faggot of wood" — attested from the 5th c. BCE; classified by Beekes as a Pre-Greek substrate word, since its shape (labial + velar cluster, -el-os ending) does not look Indo-European. 2) Vulgar Latin *facus "bundle" — reconstructed, unattested; the hypothetical base from which the Romance forms descend. The link to Greek phákelos is plausible but unproven, and some scholars doubt it. 3) Italian fagotto "bundle of sticks" — medieval Italian, a diminutive in -otto ("small bundle"); the form that travelled north to France (and separately named the bassoon). 4) Old French fagot "bundle of sticks" — attested from the 13th c. 5) Middle English fagot / fagald / faggald "bundle of twigs, faggot of firewood" — late 13th c., borrowed from Old French. 6) Early Modern English faggott "bundle of brushwood" — 16th c., with the doubled -gg- spelling consolidating in this period. 7) Modern English faggot "bundle of twigs or sticks for fuel" — plural FAGGOTS. Debated note: a rival "fascis theory" traces the word instead to Latin fascis "bundle, faggot of wood" (source of fasces, the rods-and-axe insignia of Roman magistrates). Both Greek phákelos and Latin fascis are plausible sources for a Vulgar Latin *facus, and scholarship is divided; the OED derives English faggot from Old French without committing on the deeper ancestry. The -gg- spelling is secondary (16th c.) and purely graphic.
