• Phantom: Similar because both refer to an immaterial, ghostly apparition or an illusory mental image; differs because phantom emphasizes insubstantiality, fleeting illusion, and dreamlike unreality, whereas specter carries a heavier connotation of menace, dread, and haunting foreboding.
  • Wraith: Similar because both describe visual apparitions of the dead; differs because wraith specifically connotes an ethereal, wispy apparition seen just before or shortly after a person's death, or an emaciated, shadow-like person, lacking the broad political and metaphorical application of specter.
  • Apparition: Similar because both denote a visible supernatural appearance; differs because apparition is a more formal, neutral term that can include divine or holy visions (such as Marian apparitions) rather than exclusively ominous, frightening spirits.
  • Ghost: Similar because both represent the disembodied spirit of a deceased person; differs because ghost is the universal, Germanic everyday term, whereas specter possesses higher literary register and readily extends into figurative dreads and political anxieties.
  • Bogie / Bogeyman: Similar because both evoke dread and irrational terror; differs because a bogeyman is a folkloric creature used specifically to frighten children into obedience, lacking the solemn, imposing gravity and historical weight of a specter.
