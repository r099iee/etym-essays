• history: Similar because it names the study of the past and the written record of it; differs because history is primarily the events and their investigation, while historiography is the writing about them and the study of that writing.
  • historiology: Similar as a near-synonym for the science of history; differs because it is exceedingly rare, sounds antiquated, and denotes the theoretical study of history rather than the corpus of historical writing.
  • metahistory: Similar because both examine how history is written at a level above the events; differs because metahistory specifically analyses the narrative and rhetorical deep structure of historical accounts, a project associated with Hayden White, and is a narrower theoretical enterprise.
  • historical method: Similar because both concern the discipline's procedures; differs because historical method is the practical technique of source criticism and evidence, not the study of historians' interpretations or the literature they produce.
  • historical scholarship: Similar because it denotes the accumulated academic work on the past; differs because it is a broader, less technical phrase and does not imply reflection on method or ideology.
  • chronicle: Similar because it is a written record of past events; differs because a chronicle is an annalistic, year-by-year compilation, often medieval, with little analysis, whereas historiography is a critical and interpretive practice.
  • annals: Similar as a record of events arranged by year; differs because annals are bare records, and the word is now mainly used of periodicals or of the formal records of an institution.
  • hagiography: Similar because it is writing about past persons; differs because it idealises its subject and suspends criticism, whereas historiography is defined by critical method—hence the insult “hagiographical” for uncritically admiring history.
  • the literature: Similar in the loosest academic sense of “what has been written on a topic”; differs because it is informal shorthand with no methodological content, whereas historiography implies analysis of schools, sources and assumptions.
