The word is a Germanic compound of "army" and "protect"; each element has its own chain. Chain for har- (the army element): PIE *koro- "war, army, host" (reconstructed, debated; cf. Greek koiranos "commander," Lithuanian kãras "war") → Proto-Germanic *harjaz "army, host" (1st millennium BCE–CE; cf. Old English here "army, host," Old Norse herr, Old High German heri, German Heer "army"). Chain for -bor- (the protect element): PIE *bhergh- "to hide, protect, preserve" (reconstructed; cf. Old English beorgan "to protect, preserve," Old Norse bjarga "to save," Old High German bergan "to save, hide," German bergen "to recover, salvage") → Proto-Germanic *bergan "to protect, shelter, keep safe." Chain for the compound: Proto-Germanic *harja-bergō "shelter for an army; lodgings for a host" (reconstructed; the sense is "a place that takes in a host of people") → Old English herebeorg "lodging, shelter, inn" (attested OE; also herebeorga); Old Norse herbergi "inn, room, lodging" (cf. modern Scandinavian: Swedish härbärge, Danish/Norwegian herberge "hostel, shelter"; German Herberge "inn, hostel"; Dutch herberg "inn") → Middle English herberwe / herberwe / herberge / harborwe "shelter, lodging, inn, refuge" (from the 12th c.); verb herberwen / herberwe "to lodge, to give shelter to" (13th c.) → Modern English harbor: the noun specializes to "sheltered place for ships" (by c. 1500); the verb broadens to sheltering persons and, figuratively, thoughts and feelings → HARBOR (American spelling; British harbour). Debated points: (1) PIE *koro- is reconstructed from a limited set of cognates and is not accepted by all scholars, though the Germanic *harjaz "army" is secure from its attested daughters. (2) The exact Proto-Germanic form of the compound (*harja-bergō vs. *harja-bergija-) is not directly attested and is reconstructed from the daughters. (3) The reduction of Middle English herberwe to harbor (loss of the second syllable and medial consonant) is a typical English shortening, but its precise date and path are only approximately documented.
