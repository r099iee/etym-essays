ROOT 1: SLEEPING
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*slēb- / *sleb-
"to be limp, languid, slack"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*slēpaną
"to sleep, be inactive, become limp"
↓
Old English (c. 850 CE)
slǣpan / slēpan
"to slumber, rest in sleep, repose"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200 CE)
slepen (participle: slepynge)
"slumbering, dormant, resting"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550 CE)
sleeping
"causing or inducing slumber; resting"

ROOT 2: DRAUGHT
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*dʰregʰ-
"to draw, drag along the ground"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*drahtuz / *draganą
"a drawing, pulling, hauling"
↓
Old English (c. 900 CE)
*dreaht / dragan
"act of drawing or pulling"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200 CE)
draht / drought
"act of drawing; a single gulp or drink"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500 CE)
draught
"a liquid potion swallowed in one continuous drink"
↓
Modern English (Compound c. 1600 CE)
SLEEPING-DRAUGHT
"a medicinal liquid potion taken to induce sleep"
