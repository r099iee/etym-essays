Note on historical debate and Dravidian substrate hypothesis: Classical Sanskrit scholiasts analyze Kiṣkindhā as a compound of kiṣku ("span, cleft, fissure, cave-opening") and the root dhā ("to hold, bear"), yielding the meaning "that which contains or holds rocky clefts/caverns," which perfectly matches Vālmīki's explicit description of the city as a guha (cavern fortress). However, modern comparative linguists (including Suniti Kumar Chatterji and Dravidianist scholars) propose an alternate substrate hypothesis: because Kiṣkindhā is situated deep in the Kannada-speaking Deccan plateau, the name likely represents a Sanskritization of an ancient Proto-Dravidian topographical toponym, potentially related to Proto-Dravidian *kiṟu- ("small, narrow") + *kaṇḍa ("cleft, gap, mountain defile") or *kiccu ("rocky knoll"). Both models reflect the identical physical reality: a kingdom defined by narrow granite clefts and cavernous mountain portals.
