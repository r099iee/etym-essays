• Tin: Similar because both denote metal storage vessels utilized for protecting food and dry goods; differs because tin is frequently disposable, hermetically crimped for long-term wet food preservation (canning), or refers to the raw element itself, whereas canister implies a durable, reusable container with a removable lid, or an engineered pyrotechnic/military casing.
  • Caddy: Similar because both are specialized containers for preserving dry staples, particularly tea; differs because caddy (originating from the Malay dry measure kati) is restricted almost exclusively to tea storage and often denotes an ornate, decorative table container kept in the parlor, whereas canister spans broad industrial, military, culinary, and pyrotechnic functions.
  • Receptacle: Similar because both refer to vessels engineered to receive and contain physical matter; differs because receptacle is an abstract, formal umbrella category with no specified shape, material, or sealing mechanism, whereas canister exhibits a distinct rigid, cylindrical or prismatic form.
  • Cartridge: Similar because both serve as enclosed casings containing energetic propellants, filtration media, or chemical compounds; differs because cartridge typically denotes a complete, unified round of firearm ammunition or a compact, single-use mechanical insert designed to click into a host device, whereas canister is broader and often functions as an independent storage vessel.
  • Cylinder: Similar because both frequently share identical geometric dimensions and rotational symmetry; differs because cylinder is a pure geometric abstraction or mechanical machine component (like an engine block cylinder), devoid of inherent containment or storage identity.
  • Cask: Similar because both are sturdy containers built to store and transport valuable goods; differs because cask denotes a heavy, convex wooden barrel bound with iron hoops designed primarily for aging and transporting liquids like wine and spirits, whereas canister is smaller, typically metal or ceramic, and focused on dry contents.
