Note on historical derivation: Sanskrit krauñca is a vṛddhi derivative of the base noun kruñca or verbal root kruñc- ("to cry out, crook, bend"), which is fundamentally onomatopoeic in origin, echoing the harsh, bugling or trumpeting call of the crane. Philologists connect it to the Proto-Indo-European acoustic root family *gerh₂- ("to make a harsh sound, cry hoarsely; crane"), which generated cognate names for the crane throughout Indo-European: Ancient Greek geranos (γέρανος), Latin grus, Proto-Germanic *kranô (Old English cran, Modern English crane), and Welsh garan. Alternatively, Manfred Mayrhofer and others connect it with Sanskrit krośati ("cries aloud", PIE *klewk- / *krew-).
