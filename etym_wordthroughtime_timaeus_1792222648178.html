- c. 360 BCE (Classical Greece) — Authored by Plato in Athens as his masterwork of natural philosophy and cosmology, named after the Pythagorean astronomer Timaeus of Locri.
- 1st c. BCE to 2nd c. CE (Hellenistic & Roman Eras) — Translated into Latin by Cicero (c. 45 BCE); extensively debated by Middle Platonists and Neoplatonists (Plotinus, Proclus) as the sacred core of Platonic metaphysics.
- 4th c. to 12th c. CE (Late Antiquity & Middle Ages) — Translated and commented upon by Calcidius (c. 4th c.), becoming virtually the only Platonic text accessible to Latin Western Europe for eight centuries; profoundly inspired the 12th-century cathedral School of Chartres to harmonize Genesis with Platonic physics.
- 15th–16th c. (Italian Renaissance) — Re-translated and popularized by Marsilio Ficino; musical and geometric ratios from the dialogue revolutionized Renaissance painting, linear perspective, and architecture (Alberti, Brunelleschi, Palladio).
- 17th c. to Present — Celebrated by Johannes Kepler in Harmonices Mundi; analyzed by modern historians of philosophy, physics, and literature as a primary source of Western scientific cosmology and the Atlantis legend.
