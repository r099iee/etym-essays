• Elation: Similar because both name intense, joyful high spirits; differs because elation is calm and interior — a silent, private buoyancy — while exultation bursts outward in shouts, cries, or leaping.
  • Jubilation: Similar because both name fervent, triumphant rejoicing; differs because jubilation is communal and carnivalesque, a festival of many voices, whereas exultation is the cry of a single triumphant being.
  • Triumph: Similar because both are tied to victory and elation in winning; differs because triumph names the victory and its public procession itself — the parade, not the emotional eruption that celebrates it.
  • Joy: Similar because exultation is a species of joy; differs because joy is the broad, general emotion of gladness at any good, while exultation is specifically the loud, leaping joy of triumph.
  • Delight: Similar because both name lively pleasure; differs because delight is light, immediate, and easily satisfied, with none of exultation’s ceremonial violence of self-assertion.
  • Glee: Similar because both are bright, exuberant joy; differs because glee is playful and personal, even mischievous, while exultation is triumphant, public, and formal.
  • Ecstasy: Similar because both are overwhelming joy; differs because ecstasy is passive and trance-like, a losing of the self, while exultation is active, leaping, and self-assertive.
  • Rapture: Similar because both describe being seized by intense joy; differs because rapture carries you out of yourself (rapere, “to seize”), while exultation is joy expressed outward in cries and leaps.
  • Transport: Similar because both name being carried away by strong feeling; differs because transport implies involuntary overpowering, whereas exultation is an active, vocal self-assertion.
  • Rejoicing: Similar because both name the expression of joy; differs because rejoicing is general and can be quiet or devout, while exultation is specifically the loud, triumphant celebration of victory.
  • Celebration: Similar because both accompany great success; differs because celebration is a social performance with rites and rituals, while exultation is the spontaneous emotional outburst itself.
  • Euphoria: Similar because both are intense positive feeling; differs because euphoria is a diffuse, drug-like well-being, while exultation is pointed, triumphant, and directed at a victory.
  • Bliss: Similar because both are supreme happiness; differs because bliss is serene and complete, even heavenly, with no cry and no leap — the opposite of exultation’s clamour.
  • Mirth: Similar because both are lively gladness; differs because mirth is laughter and light-hearted amusement, whereas exultation is the solemn, loud joy of victory.
  • Exultancy: Similar because it is the exact noun for the state of exulting; differs only in being rarer and more abstract — the disposition of joy rather than its eruption in a cry.
