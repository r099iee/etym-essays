Note: In Roman onomastics, the cognomen 'Pīlātus' has two leading etymological derivations in Latin philology. The prevailing view derives it from 'pīlum' (the iconic Roman infantry javelin), rendering 'pīlātus' as 'armed with the javelin' or 'spear-bearer'—a fitting military cognomen for a Roman equestrian family. A secondary competing theory derives it from 'pileātus' ('wearing the pileus'), from 'pileus' (the brimless felt cap of freedom presented to emancipated Roman slaves), which would suggest descent from an honorable freedman ancestor. His nomen 'Pontius' denotes belonging to the ancient Samnite plebeian gens Pontia (possibly related to the Oscan numeral for 'fifth', cognate with Greek 'pente').
