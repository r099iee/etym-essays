• Crooked: Similar because both involve a bent, non-linear angular shape; differs because crooked is a generic adjective applying to deformed, bent, or dishonest objects, whereas akimbo is a specialized adverbial posture term for elbows flared from the hips.
  • Bowed: Similar because both trace etymologically to roots denoting curvature or bending; differs because bowed describes a continuous arc, curve, or submissive inclination (such as a bowed head), whereas akimbo represents a sharp, rigid, and defiant outward angle.
  • Defiant: Similar because both convey an attitude of bold resistance and challenge; differs because defiant describes the internal psychological disposition or general demeanor, whereas akimbo refers explicitly to the physical body posture that communicates that defiance.
  • Strutting: Similar because both represent physical displays of assertive dominance and physical posturing; differs because strutting emphasizes an arrogant, rhythmic walking movement, while standing akimbo is typically a stationary, planted stance.
  • Posturing: Similar because both involve deliberate bodily arrangements intended to project authority or intimidate an onlooker; differs because posturing is an abstract concept that can involve rhetorical or theatrical affectation, whereas akimbo is a precise physical placement of the hands and elbows.
