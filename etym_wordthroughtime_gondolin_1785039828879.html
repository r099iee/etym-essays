- First Age (fictional antiquity) - 'Gondolin' (Sindarin, the hidden rock) is a working name for a real, inhabited place: Turgon's secret city of white stone and fountains within the Encircling Mountains, famed for its craftsmanship but kept deliberately unknown to the wider world.
- After the Fall - once betrayed and destroyed 'many ages ago' by dragons and goblins, the name ceases to name a living city and becomes a relic-name: it now means a lost golden age, a memory carried in old songs, heirlooms, and the kin of its refugees.
- In Tolkien's work (1917 onward) - the name moves from the earliest private legends of The Book of Lost Tales to the published world of The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, gradually crystallizing as the supreme byword for Noldorin smithcraft and doomed beauty.
- Contemporary - in the wider legendarium, 'Gondolin' is the standard emblem of the Elder Days: supreme elven craft, hidden glory, tragic fall; it stands opposed to Nargothrond and Khazad-dum as the city of white stone, its mere mention consecrating swords like Glamdring and Orcrist as blades of legend.
