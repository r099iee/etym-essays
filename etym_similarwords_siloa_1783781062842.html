• Siloam: Similar because it names the identical pool and conduit of Jerusalem and shares every geographical, biblical, and typological reference; differs because Siloam is the standard English Bible form (Tyndale, KJV, from Greek Silōám), while Siloa is the shorter, Vulgate-derived, Miltonic poetic variant.
  • Shiloah / Šīlōaḥ: Similar because it is the Hebrew original of the name, denoting the same conduit and its waters; differs because Shiloah is the scholarly transliteration of the Hebrew (Isaiah 8:6), precise and archaeological in register, while Siloa is the Latinized English poetic form.
  • Gihon: Similar because the spring of Gihon is the very source whose water the conduit "sent" into the pool; differs because Gihon names the spring outside the wall in the Kidron Valley, while Siloam names the receiving channel and pool inside the city.
  • Kedron / Kidron: Similar because the brook Kidron runs in the same valley east of Jerusalem, fast by the Temple Mount, just as Siloa's brook flows beside it; differs because Kidron is the valley torrent, often dry and used in biblical symbolism for refuse and pollution, while Siloa is the clear living water "sent" from the spring.
  • Bethesda: Similar because the Pool of Bethesda in John 5 is another Jerusalem healing pool, also associated with stirred, healing water; differs because Bethesda heals by the troubling of the water by an angel and has no "sending" etymology, while Siloam heals through a commanded washing and is literally "the Sending."
  • En-rogel: Similar because En-rogel (Job's Well) is another spring in the same Kidron/Tyropoeon area of Jerusalem, a boundary and water source of the ancient city; differs because En-rogel never acquired the healing, messianic, and typological freight of Siloam and remains a minor geographical marker.
  • Jordan: Similar because the Jordan is the great sacred river of washing, crossing, and baptism in Scripture, and Christian tradition reads Siloam baptismally; differs because the Jordan is a national river whose crossing and baptismal symbolism dominate, while Siloa is a small urban pool tied to one named act of healing and one word, "Sent."
  • Bethel: Similar because Bethel ("house of God") is a sacred spring-site-like holy place whose very name encodes theology, as Siloa's name encodes sending; differs because Bethel is a hilltop sanctuary far north near Luz, while Siloa is the water site of the southern city.
