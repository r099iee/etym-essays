• Overturned: Similar because both denote the loss of an upright posture; differs because overturned emphasizes an inverted state or flipping upside down (such as a vehicle or a legal verdict), whereas toppled specifically emphasizes a top-heavy structure losing balance and crashing downward.
  • Overthrown: Similar because both describe the forceful removal of a government, ruler, or established power; differs because overthrown is predominantly socio-political and intentional, whereas toppled preserves vivid physical, gravitational imagery and can occur purely through internal instability.
  • Collapsed: Similar because both involve structural failure and downward descent; differs because collapsed implies an inward caving, buckling, or disintegration of internal support, whereas toppled describes swaying and falling over from the top as a relatively intact mass.
  • Tumbled: Similar because both denote an uncontrolled, unsteady descent; differs because tumbled evokes continuous rolling, bouncing, or clumsy movement along a surface, whereas toppled centers specifically on the critical initial moment of overbalancing from a height.
  • Unseated: Similar because both refer to dislodging someone from an elevated status or position; differs because unseated is milder, more formal, and often procedural (such as in parliamentary elections or equestrian riding), lacking the dramatic, cascading visual impact of toppled.
  • Felled: Similar because both signify bringing down an upright entity; differs because felled requires an external cutting, striking, or deliberate forceful blow (like cutting down a tree or knocking out a fighter), whereas toppled can be self-induced by inherent top-heaviness.
  • Subverted: Similar because both lead to the downfall of an established system; differs because subverted involves covert, gradual undermining of principles or institutions from within, whereas toppled captures the overt, climactic crash.
