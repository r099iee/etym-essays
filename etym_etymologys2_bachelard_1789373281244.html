Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
vacca

↓
Late / Medieval Latin (c. 6th–9th c.)
vaccāria / baccalāria
"dairy farm, cattle pasture, grazing land"
Note: Vulgar Latin betacism shifted initial [w]/[v] to [b]; baccalaria denoted an agricultural grazing plot held by a rustic tenant.
↓
Medieval Latin (c. 10th–11th c.)
baccalārius
"tenant holder of a grazing plot; young squire, novice knight"
Note: Historical philology also records a competing hypothesis deriving baccalarius from a Celtic base *bacc- ('peg, hook, small staff').
↓
Old French (c. 11th–12th c.)
bacheler
"young man, aspiring knight, youthful bachelor"
↓
Middle French (c. 13th–14th c.)
bacheler + -ard
"stout young fellow; spirited youth (surname Bachelard)"
Note: Formed with Germanic augmentative suffix -ard (*hard- 'bold, hardy'), originating as a regional family surname in eastern France.
↓
Modern French (1884–1962)
