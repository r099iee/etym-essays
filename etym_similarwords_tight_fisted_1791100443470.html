• stingy: Similar because both withhold money; differs because stingy is the plain everyday term, tight-fisted adds the vivid fist image.
  • miserly: Similar because both hoard wealth; differs because miserly implies pathological hoarding, tight-fisted is milder and more comic.
  • niggardly: Similar because both give grudgingly; differs because niggardly is formal-literary and easily confused with a slur, tight-fisted is safe colloquial.
  • parsimonious: Similar because both are frugal to excess; differs because parsimonious is Latinate and formal, tight-fisted is Anglo-Saxon and blunt.
  • penurious: Similar because both suggest poverty of giving; differs because penurious can mean actually poor, tight-fisted means rich but mean.
  • cheap: Similar because both avoid spending (AmE); differs because cheap also means low quality, tight-fisted only means ungenerous.
  • close-fisted: Similar because near-identical image and meaning; differs only in tight vs close — interchangeable.
  • mean: Similar because British mean means stingy; differs because mean also means unkind or average, tight-fisted is specific.
  • greedy: Similar because both grasp; differs because greedy wants more, tight-fisted won't let go of what it has.
  • avaricious: Similar because both love money; differs because avaricious stresses acquisition, tight-fisted stresses retention.
  • grasping: Similar because Dickens pairs them and both clutch; differs because grasping is aggressive taking, tight-fisted is refusing to release.
  • thrifty: Similar because both save; differs because thrifty is virtuous prudence, tight-fisted is vicious excess.
  • tight: Similar because short form with same sense; differs because tight alone is slang and also means drunk or close, tight-fisted is unambiguous.
