galloping:
- 'To run well' of horses (13th c.) - borrowed from Old French galoper, naming the horse's four-beat gait at full extension, all four feet momentarily clear of the ground
- Runaway figuratives (19th c.) - galloping consumption (tuberculosis) transfers the unstoppable speed to disease and anything accelerating beyond control
- Contemporary - still the fastest equine gait and the standard image of wild, hard-to-stop momentum

hooves:
- Older plural hoofs (Old English onward) - the regular -s plural of hoof
- Regularized hooves (17th-18th c.) - formed by analogy with wolf/wolves, the voiced form becomes the standard plural
- Contemporary - almost always plural in narrative, since a horse is heard as four hooves: the drumming that announced power and speed before engines did

lunging:
- A fencing attack (18th c.) - borrowed from French allonge, 'an extension': the swordsman drives the whole body forward to its full length
- From sword to body (19th-20th c.) - any creature lunges - a tiger, a boxer, a horse - throwing its whole frame forward in one explosive horizontal thrust
- Contemporary - the participle keeps the fencer's edge of violence and precision, as in 'lunging break-neck'
