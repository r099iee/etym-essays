- Early 17th c. – Pedagogical instruction & classical education - Originally neutral and positive; denoted educating, tutoring, or instructing a student in sciences, arts, or Christian virtues.
- 18th to 19th c. – Institutional training & religious instruction - Maintained a formal educational meaning of grounding pupils in religious catechism, institutional bylaws, or scientific principles.
- Early 20th c. – Totalitarian propaganda & youth mobilization - Acquired an ominous political coloring with the rise of Fascist and Soviet state apparatuses aggressively molding youth into party shock-troops.
- Mid-20th c. (Cold War) – Brainwashing & ideological coercion - Cemented as a heavily pejorative term associated with Orwellian "thought control," Marxist-Leninist agitprop, and psychological subversion.
- 21st c. to Present – Algorithmic radicalization & curricular culture wars - Pervasively applied in debates over school curricula, political echo chambers, religious cults, and corporate cultural compliance.
