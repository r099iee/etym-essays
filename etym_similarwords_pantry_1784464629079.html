• Larder: Similar because both are household rooms for storing food; differs because the larder (from the word for bacon, bacon/lard) specializes in cool storage of meat, fish, and perishables, whereas the pantry is the bread-and-dry-goods room — though the terms overlap in modern speech.
  • Cupboard: Similar because both store food and household goods; differs because a cupboard may be a single piece of furniture, whereas a pantry is classically a room, and the cupboard's original sense was a board or table for cups and dishes.
  • Storeroom: Similar because both are rooms for keeping provisions; differs because a storeroom is a general, neutral term for any stored goods (including tools, linen, stock) and lacks the pantry's domestic food identity.
  • Cellar: Similar because both store household supplies; differs because a cellar is underground, usually for wine, beer, coal, or bulk stores, while the pantry is above ground and food-focused.
  • Buttery: Similar because it is a medieval service-room companion of the pantry, storing drink (from butt, a barrel); differs because it held liquids and barrels, not bread and dry provisions, and is now archaic except in historic houses and university colleges.
  • Spence: Similar because it is another old household store-room (from Old French despense 'store, expense'); differs because the spence was the general store-room or serving-parlour and the word is now archaic or regional.
  • Stillroom: Similar because it was a service room of the great house alongside the pantry; differs because it was for distilling, preserves, cordials, and medicines, not daily food storage.
  • Scullery: Similar because both are service rooms adjoining the kitchen; differs because the scullery was for washing dishes, pots, and rough kitchen tasks, not for storing provisions.
  • Store / storehouse: Similar because both hold goods; differs because a store is usually larger, commercial, or general-purpose, whereas the pantry is the small domestic food-room.
  • Food closet / walk-in pantry: Similar because these are the modern American terms for a shelved pantry; differs because they name the fitted cupboard or closet form of the same function.
  • Kitchen: Similar because both are food-centred rooms; differs because the kitchen is where food is cooked, while the pantry is where it is stored before cooking or serving.
  • Dairy: Similar because both are household rooms for foodstuffs; differs because the dairy was specifically for milk, butter, and cheese.
