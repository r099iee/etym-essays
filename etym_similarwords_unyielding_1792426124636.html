• intransigent: Similar because both denote an absolute refusal to compromise or change a stance; differs because intransigent is a political and ideological term derived from Spanish and Latin (*in-* + *transigere*, "not coming to an agreement"), emphasizing stubborn political refusal to negotiate, whereas unyielding applies equally to moral courage and literal physical rigidity.
  • inflexible: Similar because both describe inability to bend or be altered; differs because inflexible tends toward a dry, bureaucratic, or mechanical tone denoting rigid adherence to rules, regulations, or schedules without room for nuance, whereas unyielding carries dynamic emotional power, epic weight, and heroic fortitude.
  • adamantine: Similar because both evoke unbreakable strength; differs because adamantine is an elevated, mythic, and literary adjective derived from the Greek diamond or mythical metal *adamas*, emphasizing diamond-hard physical impenetrability or unbreakable celestial armor.
  • resolute: Similar because both describe firm, unwavering determination; differs because resolute focuses on a positive, settled state of purposeful decision-making and inner confidence, whereas unyielding focuses on the active resistance against external pressure seeking to break that resolve.
  • tenacious: Similar because both convey persistent endurance; differs because tenacious (from Latin *tenere*, "to hold") highlights holding fast, gripping tightly, and refusing to let go of an object, goal, or life itself, whereas unyielding highlights resisting deformation and refusing to step back.
  • inexorable: Similar because both describe relentless, unstoppable forces; differs because inexorable (from Latin *in-* + *exorare*, "cannot be moved by prayer") means incapable of being persuaded, moved, or halted by pleas or tears, often describing fate, death, or the passage of time.
  • obstinate: Similar because both describe refusing to give way; differs because obstinate is almost always pejorative, implying irrational, pig-headed, and unreasonable stubbornness in the face of obvious facts or wise counsel.
  • unbending: Similar because both use the physical metaphor of refusing to curve or bow; differs because unbending frequently characterizes severe, formal social manners, stiff dignity, or unyielding austerity in personal conduct.
  • implacable: Similar because both denote an inability to be softened; differs because implacable applies specifically to anger, hostility, enmity, or vengeance that cannot be pacified or appeased.
  • steadfast: Similar because both indicate unwavering firmness; differs because steadfast emphasizes loyal, faithful, and affectionate constancy (as in a steadfast friend), whereas unyielding stresses tough, uncompromising resistance.
  • recalcitrant: Similar because both describe resistance to control; differs because recalcitrant (literally "kicking back with heels") denotes active, unruly defiance and rebellious disobedience toward authority.
  • dogged: Similar because both express stubborn persistence; differs because dogged implies grim, plodding, low-glamour endurance against exhausting fatigue and hardship.
