Note on Sanskrit grammatical mechanics and genealogical nuance: Under Pāṇinian morphological rules (Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī 4.1.92–178, tasyāpatyam), secondary patronymic derivatives (taddhita affixes, specifically aṇ or yañ) systematically require vṛddhi (strengthening) of the initial syllable vowel: the short vowel u in Pulastya lengthens and diphthongizes into au, producing Paulastya, literally "he who belongs to or issues from Pulastya." In epic genealogy, the sage Pulastya fathered the sage Viśravas. Viśravas took two wives: Iḍaviḍā (who bore Kubera, the god of wealth) and the rākṣasa princess Kēkasī (who bore Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Vibhīṣaṇa, and Śūrpaṇakhā). Consequently, both Kubera and Rāvaṇa are technically Paulastyas, but in the martial context of the Rāmāyaṇa, the compound Paulastya-vadha ("the slaying of Paulastya") functions as the standard epic title and metonym for the climactic defeat and death of Rāvaṇa.
