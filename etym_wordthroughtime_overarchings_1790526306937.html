- 1400s–1650s: Physical masonry and architectural arching - Emerged in Late Middle English and Early Modern English to describe stone vaults, bridges, and ceilings built to arch over an expanse.
- 1700s–1850s: Romantic natural landscape imagery - Adopted by nature poets and essayists to describe tree boughs intertwining over forest paths, atmospheric halos, and mountain ridges.
- 1880s–1950s: Semantic transition to abstract dominance - The participial adjective "overarching" shifted from physical architecture to abstract systems, signifying supreme principles or governing themes.
- 1960s–Present: Plural nominalization in critical theory - Formalized as a plural noun in literary criticism, sociology, and philosophy to analyze the multiple overarching ideological frameworks and meta-structures governing modern culture.
