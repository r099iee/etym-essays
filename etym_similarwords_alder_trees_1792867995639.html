• Alders: Similar because it is the same tree in simplex form; differs because alders alone can mean scrub, carr, or timber, while alder-trees insists on standing individuals.
  • Willows: Similar because willows share the alder's waterside station and leaning habit; differs because willows flaunt narrow silvery leaves and pliant withies, while alders bear round dark leaves and cones.
  • Osiers: Similar because osiers grow in the same wet beds and are coppiced for basketry; differs because osier names basket-willows grown for withies, while alder-trees names full riverside trees.
  • Birches: Similar because birches and alders are close botanical cousins with catkins and pioneer habits; differs because birches gleam white and love drier heaths, while alders darken beside running water.
  • Poplars: Similar because poplars also line waterways in leaning rows; differs because poplars are tall, shimmering, and wind-blown, while alders are squat, dark, and brooding over still pools.
  • Carrs: Similar because alder carr is the waterlogged woodland the trees dominate; differs because carr names the whole boggy habitat, while alder-trees names the trees within it.
  • Sallows: Similar because sallows haunt the same marshy fringes with catkins in spring; differs because sallow is a bushy goat-willow of hedges and fens, while the alder is a true trunked tree.
  • Hazels: Similar because hazel shares the catkins, the coppice stool, and the streamside; differs because hazel is a multi-stemmed shrub of nuts and spars, while alder is a single-trunked timber tree of piles and sluices.
