Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*h₂n̥bʰi- + *h₂eǵ-
"around, on both sides"
+ "to drive, lead, act"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*ambi- + *agō
"around, both sides"
+ "to drive, move"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
ambigere
"to wander around, go about,"
"to dispute, hesitate, doubt"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
ambiguus
"moving in two directions, wavering,"
"uncertain, doubtful, obscure"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1520s)
ambiguous
"open to multiple interpretations,"
"doubtful, uncertain in meaning"

Note: Latin ambiguus is formed from the prefix ambi- ('on both sides, around') and agere ('to drive, lead'); the physical image is of driving cattle or wandering in two directions at once, hence hesitating or wavering between paths.
