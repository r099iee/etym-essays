- Old Norse / Early Medieval Scandinavian (álfr) - Referred to a mythological supernatural entity, elf, or hidden being of nature.
- Middle English (alfe / auf / aufe, c. 1400–1600) - Shifted in folklore to denote a fairy changeling—a deformed, sickly, or simple-minded child left behind by elves in exchange for a human baby.
- Early Modern English (17th Century) - Transitioned from literal supernatural belief to a secularized metaphor for an idiot, half-wit, or mentally deficient person.
- 18th to 19th Century (Spelling shift to 'oaf') - Acquired the physical connotation of an awkward, clumsy, lumbering, and unrefined boor.
- 20th to 21st Century - Cemented as a common colloquial and literary mild-to-moderate insult for a clumsy, dim-witted, or socially graceless man.
