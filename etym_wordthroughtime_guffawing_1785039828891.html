- 18th century (c. 1720) - guffaw enters as an imitative word for a sudden, loud, coarse burst of laughter, at home in taverns and farces.
- 19th century - novelists use guffaw to mark the unrefined, open-mouthed laugher — hearty but without restraint or refinement.
- 20th-21st centuries - guffawing still denotes belly-bursting, echoing laughter; in prose it paints the laugher as loud, coarse, and indifferent to others.
- Contemporary - the laugh of no self-restraint: suddenness, loudness, and coarseness combined in a single verb.
