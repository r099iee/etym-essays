- 14th Century (Late Middle English) - Both homographs borrowed from Old French; *reparer* signifies restoring physical objects or making amends for spiritual wrongs, while *repairier* signifies returning to one's homeland or seeking refuge in a dwelling.
- 15th to 16th Century (Tudor Era) - The noun *repair* becomes entrenched in English property leases and common law ("to keep in tenantable repair"); the verb broadens to repairing breaches in statecraft and religious schisms.
- 17th to 18th Century (Augustan Era) - Neoclassical literature frequently employs "repair to" as an elegant locative verb for retiring to parlors or pleasure gardens; "in good/bad repair" becomes standard for road and estate maintenance.
- 19th Century (Victorian Era) - The Industrial Revolution elevates mechanical and civil repair into vast engineering disciplines; social reformers use "in bad repair" to condemn squalid urban tenements.
- 20th Century - Formalized in automotive engineering, aerospace maintenance schedules, and molecular genetics ("DNA repair mechanisms").
- 21st Century - Extends into digital data recovery ("repair disk permissions") and fuels the global "Right to Repair" legislative movement defending consumer repair autonomy against planned obsolescence.
