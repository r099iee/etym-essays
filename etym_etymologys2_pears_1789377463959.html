Pre-Indo-European / Mediterranean Substrate (c. 3000–1500 BCE)
*pira- / *pera-
"pear fruit, pear tree"
Note: Ernout-Meillet and de Vaan classify the root as an ancient non-Indo-European Mediterranean agricultural loanword.
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE–1st c. CE)
pirum (singular) / pira (plural)
"pear fruit"
↓
Late / Vulgar Latin (c. 3rd–6th c. CE)
pira (reanalyzed feminine singular)
"pear, pears"
Note: The Latin neuter plural pira was reinterpreted in spoken Vulgar Latin as a feminine singular collective.
↓
Proto-West Germanic (c. 4th–5th c. CE)
*pera / *piru
"pear fruit"
Note: Early agricultural loanword borrowed along the Rhine frontier during Roman provincial expansion.
↓
Old English (c. 700–1000 CE)
pere / peru (plural peran)
"pear, fruit of the pear tree"
↓
Middle English (c. 1150–1450)
pere / peere (plural peres / peeres)
"pear, pears"
Note: The weak Old English plural -an was replaced by the general strong masculine plural suffix -es.
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
peare / pear (plural peares / pears)
"cultivated pome fruit of the genus Pyrus"
↓
Modern English (present)
PEARS
