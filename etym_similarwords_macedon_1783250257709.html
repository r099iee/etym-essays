• Macedonia: Similar because it names the same ancient land and people; differs because it is the ordinary, modern term for the region and state, while Macedon is the archaizing, dignified name for the kingdom itself.
  • Macedonian: Similar because it belongs to the same people and dynasty; differs because it is the adjective for the language, army, and identity, whereas Macedon names the political entity.
  • Hellas / Greece: Similar because Macedon was part of the wider Greek world and its story; differs because Hellas is the general Greek self-name for the whole Greek land, while Macedon singles out the northern kingdom.
  • Alexander's empire: Similar because it names the power that Macedon became under Alexander; differs because it is a personal label for the conquests, not the name of the homeland and state.
  • Argead kingdom: Similar because it is the scholarly name for the same Macedonian royal state; differs because it specifies the dynasty rather than the land, losing the literary dignity of “Macedon.”
  • Epirus: Similar because it was a neighbouring ancient kingdom of northwestern Greece; differs because Epirus was smaller and never achieved Macedon's imperial reach or fame.
