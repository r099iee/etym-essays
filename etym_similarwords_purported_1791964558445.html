• alleged: Similar because it marks a claim as unverified and keeps the speaker at arm’s length; differs because alleged is the default legal and journalistic term for accusations of wrongdoing (“alleged fraud”) and is more pointedly adversarial, whereas purported suits claims of identity, meaning or status and sounds more bookish.
  • supposed: Similar because both attribute a belief to others; differs because supposed is conversational and often ironic (“his supposed friends”) and can imply the speaker’s outright disbelief, while purported is formal and merely declines to confirm.
  • reputed: Similar because it reports a general belief or reputation (“the reputed author”); differs because reputed leans on widespread reputation rather than a specific claim, and is neutral to positive (“a reputed scholar”) where purported is sceptical.
  • ostensible: Similar because it describes what something appears or is declared to be; differs because ostensible focuses on the outward appearance presented (“his ostensible reason”) and often hints at a hidden real motive, while purported focuses on who made the claim.
  • professed: Similar because it concerns a declared identity or belief; differs because professed implies a voluntary, self-declared commitment (“a professed atheist”) and can even carry sincerity, whereas purported reports someone else’s claim and usually doubts it.
  • avowed: Similar in denoting an openly declared position; differs in being frank and often approving (“an avowed aim of the policy”), with no insinuation that the claim is false — the opposite connotation from purported.
  • so-called: Similar because both distance the speaker from a label; differs because so-called is informal, attributive and openly sneering (“his so-called reforms”), while purported is neutral in tone and can appear predicatively.
  • apparent: Similar in signalling that something is not established; differs because apparent describes how things look to an observer (“the apparent cause”) rather than a claim made by someone, and it can also mean “obvious”.
  • nominal: Similar because it marks a title or role as existing in name only; differs because nominal asserts that the reality falls short (“the nominal leader”), whereas purported only reports that the status is claimed.
  • feigned: Similar in casting doubt on a presented identity; differs because feigned asserts deliberate deception (“feigned illness”), a much stronger and more hostile judgement than the agnostic hedge of purported.
  • would-be: Similar in qualifying an aspirant identity (“a would-be novelist”); differs because would-be is informal, faintly mocking and describes aspiration, not a claim that others repeat.
