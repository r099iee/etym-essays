- 18th c. — work in the open field: farmers, soldiers, and engineers do field work as two words, labour outdoors as opposed to shop or garrison.
- Mid 19th c. — naturalists claim it: geologists and botanists return with maps and specimens, and field work becomes the honourable opposite of cabinet study.
- 1914–1922 — Malinowski remakes it: long Trobriand residence and Argonauts of the Western Pacific turn fieldwork into one word and a moral ideal, learn the language, live the life.
- Mid 20th c. — every discipline goes out: sociologists to streets and factories, psychologists to villages, students graded on their fieldwork diaries.
- 1970s–1990s — reckoning: decolonising critiques, ethics boards, and reflexive writing ask who the field belongs to and who speaks for it.
- Today — beloved and contested: still the badge of real research, stretched from rainforests to online communities, and mocked whenever a flying visit borrows its name.
