• Writhe: Similar because it means to twist the body; differs because writhing is slower and larger and usually implies pain or violent struggle, while wriggling is quick, small, and often playful or purposeful.
  • Squirm: Similar because it means to twist with small movements; differs because squirming adds discomfort or embarrassment — one squirms from unease, but wriggles to escape or to slip through.
  • Wiggle: Similar because it means small quick side-to-side movements; differs because wiggling is lighter and more casual and often of a single part, while wriggling is a whole-body, effortful contortion.
  • Twist: Similar because it means to turn and bend; differs because twist is the general verb of rotation and wrenching, without the repeated, squirming, escape-seeking quality of wriggle.
  • Worm (one's way): Similar because it means to make progress by slow sideways effort; differs because worming is slow, stealthy, and ingratiating, while wriggling is quick and squirming.
  • Slither: Similar because it means smooth writhing movement; differs because slithering is the gliding motion of a snake across a surface, continuous and graceful, not the struggling twist of a body freeing itself.
  • Snake (one's way): Similar because it means to move in a winding course; differs because snaking carries you over distance in curves, while wriggling is a rapid squirm in place or through a gap.
  • Wangle (out of): Similar in the figurative sense of escaping an obligation; differs because wangling implies crafty persuasion or scheming, while wriggling out suggests evasion or slippage rather than clever talk.
