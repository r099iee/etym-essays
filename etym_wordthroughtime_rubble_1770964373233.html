- 14th–16th c. 'Rough fragments of broken stone' — a builder's term for undressed, irregular stones used to fill or face walls, entirely practical and technical with no emotional charge.
- 17th–19th c. 'Broken masonry from collapsed structures' — extends to the heap of brick and stone left after a building falls, gaining a tone of disorder and ruin.
- 20th–21st c. 'Debris of destruction' — now the standard image of devastation in war reports and disaster coverage (earthquakes, bombings, fires), carrying a heavy register of catastrophe, loss, and grim aftermath rather than mere building refuse.
