• Identity: Similar because both answer "who one is" as a distinct self; differs because identity names membership in a group or category and implies no inner richness or self-expression, whereas individuality is the distinctive inner selfhood one holds and expresses even against pressure.
  • Personality: Similar because both describe what distinguishes a person; differs because personality is the observable psychological make-up others perceive and is morally neutral, while individuality concerns one's own distinct selfhood and the right to express it.
  • Character: Similar because both concern a person's inner constitution; differs because character names moral and temperamental make-up, judged as good or bad, while individuality stresses distinctness and self-expression rather than moral evaluation.
  • Uniqueness: Similar because both mean being unlike others; differs because uniqueness merely asserts one-of-a-kindness (usable of objects, statistics, and events) and carries no ethical weight, while individuality is reserved for persons and implies a core self that deserves to flourish.
  • Selfhood: Similar because both name the essential being of a person; differs because selfhood is the philosophical fact of being a self at all, while individuality is the distinctive quality and expression of that self.
  • Autonomy: Similar because both involve living by one's own lights; differs because autonomy is the capacity for self-rule and independent decision — a power — while individuality is the distinctive character itself plus its expression.
  • Independence: Similar because both oppose external control; differs because independence is a condition of not being governed or supplied by others, while individuality is a positive, substantive set of inner traits.
  • Originality: Similar because both imply being one's own rather than a copy; differs because originality stresses creative novelty of work, thought, or style, while individuality covers the whole distinctive being of a person, creative or not.
  • Eccentricity: Similar because both mark deviation from the common; differs because eccentricity implies oddity, often cultivated or comical, while individuality is the neutral-to-admirable fact of distinct selfhood.
  • Idiosyncrasy: Similar because both denote a personal distinguishing trait; differs because idiosyncrasy is a single peculiar habit or quirk, while individuality is the whole integrated distinctness of a person.
  • Personhood: Similar because both concern being a person; differs because personhood is the legal and ethical status of being a rights-bearing subject, while individuality is the distinctive character within that person.
  • Self-expression: Similar because both involve making one's distinct self manifest; differs because self-expression is an act or mode of communication, while individuality is the underlying quality expressed.
