PIE ancestry uncertain — the second element is Germanic; a PIE link to *bhrus- 'to swell, sprout' is proposed but not established - Old English on 'on, at, in' — reduced to the prefix a- in Middle English (as in afoot, ashore, ablaze, asleep) - Old English brēost 'breast, chest' (from Proto-Germanic *brusts 'breast'; no certain cognates outside Germanic — a PIE root *bhrus-, 'to swell, sprout,' has been proposed because a breast is a swelling, but the connection is conjectural, per the OED) - Middle English a-brest / abrest 'breast-by-breast, side by side, chests level in a line' (attested c. 1350–1470) - Modern English abreast 'side by side and facing the same way; keeping up' — figurative sense 'informed, up to date' from the mid-17th century; nautical sense 'opposite a ship's beam' from the late 17th century
