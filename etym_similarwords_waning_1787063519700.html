• declining: Similar because both describe a gradual decrease or downward course; differs because declining is broader, covering politely refusing, tilting down, and falling in numbers, while waning keeps the sense of fading from fullness to emptiness.
  • diminishing: Similar because both mean growing less; differs because diminishing is more objective and measurable (e.g., diminishing returns), while waning retains a natural, cyclical, often lunar flavor.
  • decreasing: Similar because both name a process of reduction; differs because decreasing is neutral, statistical, and technical, whereas waning is more literary and implies a phase or season drawing to an end.
  • ebbing: Similar because both describe a receding or weakening (of tides, strength, life); differs because ebbing is tied to the tide image and is more dramatic, while waning covers size and quantity as well as force.
  • fading: Similar because both mean losing intensity over time; differs because fading applies especially to light, color, sound, and memory, while waning more often concerns size, power, or phase.
  • dwindling: Similar because both describe shrinking gradually to a small or vanishing amount; differs because dwindling emphasizes running out (dwindling supplies), while waning emphasizes the retreat from fullness (waning moon).
  • subsiding: Similar because both mean growing less intense; differs because subsiding usually describes a force settling down (a storm, a fever, an argument), whereas waning describes a stage of decline.
  • atrophying: Similar because both describe a decline in size or strength; differs because atrophying is medical and implies wasting from disuse, while waning is general and natural.
  • tapering off: Similar because both describe a gradual lessening; differs because tapering off is colloquial and implies a controlled or expected reduction, while waning is more organic and literary.
  • wasting: Similar because both mean gradually becoming smaller or weaker; differs because wasting has a strongly negative, morbid sense of consumption or decay, while waning is neutral and natural.
