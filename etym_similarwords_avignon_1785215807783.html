• Avennio: Similar because both name the same city; differs because Avennio is the Gallo-Roman toponym of antiquity (the town of the Cavares, recorded by Pliny), while Avignon is the modern French name that carries the later papal and literary history.
  • Comtat Venaissin: Similar because both name the papal territory of which Avignon was the capital; differs because the Comtat is the surrounding papal county, while Avignon is the city proper on the Rhône, the seat of the palace and the papacy.
  • Vaucluse: Similar because both name the region in which the city stands; differs because Vaucluse is the modern French department created at the Revolution, while Avignon is the city that serves as its capital.
  • Palais des Papes: Similar because both evoke the city's identity; differs because the Palais is the Gothic papal palace within the city — a single landmark, not the city itself, which layers Roman, papal, and Millian history.
  • Provence: Similar because both name the region of southern France to which the city belongs; differs because Provence is the broad cultural region, while Avignon is one specific city within it (historically attached to the Holy See rather than to Provence).
  • Pont d'Avignon / Pont Saint-Bénézet: Similar because both evoke the famous broken bridge of the city; differs because the bridge is a single monument (itself the subject of the folk song "Sur le pont d'Avignon"), while Avignon is the whole city famous for palace, papacy, and Mill.
  • The Avignon Papacy / Babylonian captivity: Similar because both name the city's great historical epoch; differs because these are names of the historical period (1309–1377) of the papal residence, not of the city itself.
  • Mill's retirement: Similar because both refer to the city's literary identity; differs because this is the biographical episode (1858–1873) of Mill's last decade, while Avignon is the place where the episode unfolded.
  • Arles: Similar because both are historic Roman towns of the lower Rhône in Provence; differs because Arles is a separate city (once capital of Roman Gallia Narbonensis) with its own amphitheatre, while Avignon's fame is papal and modern.
  • Avignonet / Avenas / Avon: Similar because all share the same Celtic river-word root; differs because these are distinct places — a small town in Languedoc, a village in the Ardèche, and the many English rivers Avon — not the papal city.
