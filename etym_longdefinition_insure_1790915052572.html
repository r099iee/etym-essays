Insure (/ɪnˈʃʊə/ or /ɪnˈʃɔː/) is a transitive and intransitive verb meaning to provide, secure, or contract for financial compensation and indemnification against potential loss, damage, illness, or death through a formal policy with an underwriter; or, particularly in American English and historical/general usage, to make sure, certain, secure, or guaranteed (functioning as a variant of ensure).

The verb operates across distinct financial, legal, risk management, and stylistic domains:

  • Financial Indemnification and Commercial Underwriting (Primary Strict Sense): In commerce, law, and everyday finance, to insure means to enter into a legally binding contract (an insurance policy) wherein one party (the insurer or underwriter), in exchange for the payment of regular premiums, guarantees to reimburse another party (the insured or policyholder) for specified financial losses resulting from unforeseen perils, accidents, property damage, natural disasters, liability claims, illness, disability, or death.
  • Guaranteeing Certainty or Securing an Outcome (Variant / General Sense): In broader general usage—especially prominent in American English and historically common throughout British literature—insure is frequently used to mean to make certain, secure, or inevitable (e.g., 'careful preparation will insure success' or 'talented stylists won't insure a great photograph'). While modern British style guides and strict editorial prescriptive standards often recommend reserving ensure for making certain and insure for financial indemnity, insure has a centuries-long pedigree in both senses.
  • Risk Management and Structural Safeguarding (Protective Sense): In corporate strategy, engineering, and cybersecurity, to insure against a contingency means to take proactive structural measures, install redundant systems, or implement contingency protocols to protect an organization from catastrophic failure or systemic vulnerability.
  • Grammatical and Semantic Distinction (Assure vs. Ensure vs. Insure): The tripartite family of assure, ensure, and insure presents one of the most closely examined stylistic triads in the English language: assure is directed toward people to remove doubt or instill confidence ('I assure you'); ensure is directed toward outcomes to guarantee their realization ('ensure compliance'); and insure is directed toward commercial contracts of financial risk mitigation or general security.
  • Historical and Marine Underwriting Traditions (Maritime Sense): Historically, the modern practice of insuring developed in Renaissance maritime commerce (such as Lloyd’s of London at Edward Lloyd's coffee house in 1688), where merchants and shipowners banded together with underwriters who wrote their names under the risk calculations of sea voyages, insuring ships and cargoes against piracy, shipwreck, and storms.

The semantic core of insure rests on the Latin sēcūrus ('free from care, safe'), representing humanity’s institutional and psychological quest to establish certainty and security against the unpredictable hazards of existence. It provides a formal bulwark against existential and financial chaos in human society across commerce, law, and daily life.
