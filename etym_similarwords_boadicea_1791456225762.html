• Boudica: similar because it is the modern scholarly spelling of the same historical queen; differs because it reflects the classical form and is the preferred name in academic and museum usage.
  • Boudicca: similar because it is Tacitus’s variant with a double c, referring to the same woman; differs because of its Latin spelling and its slightly more antiquarian feel.
  • Buddug: similar because it is the Welsh form of the name, used in Wales for the same queen; differs because of its living Welsh-language context and pronunciation.
  • Bonduca: similar because it is the Renaissance dramatic form used by Fletcher and Purcell; differs because it belongs to Stuart theatre rather than to history.
  • Voadicia: similar because it is a Tudor and Elizabethan spelling of the name; differs because of its Renaissance antiquarian context and its now obsolete form.
  • Britannia: similar because it is the female personification of Britain, often depicted with a spear and shield; differs because it is an allegory rather than a historical person.
  • warrior queen: similar because it is a descriptive phrase for a ruling woman who leads troops; differs because it is a general category rather than a name.
  • Amazon: similar because it is a member of the mythical race of female warriors; differs because it is mythological and carries a stronger exotic charge.
  • virago: similar because it is a fierce, domineering or manly woman; differs because it is a common noun with a derogatory edge, not a historical reference.
  • termagant: similar because it is a violent, quarrelsome woman; differs because of its emphasis on noise and scolding rather than on military leadership.
  • harridan: similar because it is a strict, scolding old woman; differs because of its focus on age and domestic tyranny.
  • battle-axe: similar because it is a formidable, aggressive woman; differs because it is colloquial and often comic rather than heroic.
  • Cartimandua: similar because it is another first-century British queen, of the Brigantes; differs because it is a historical contemporary who allied with Rome instead of joining the revolt.
  • Zenobia: similar because it is a third-century queen of Palmyra who rebelled against Rome; differs because of her Syrian context and later date, though she is a close typological parallel.
