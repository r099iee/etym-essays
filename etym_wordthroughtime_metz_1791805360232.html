- 1st c. BCE — the site is the capital of a Gallic tribe recorded by Caesar, then a Gaulish stronghold rather than a Roman city.
- 4th c. — the city now takes its name from the tribe that had held it, the old tribal designation reduced to a mere city name.
- 6th–9th c. — Metz becomes a Merovingian and Austrasian residence, and the name is fixed in medieval usage.
- 12th–16th c. — the free imperial city, styling itself a republic, gives the name a republican and episcopal double identity.
- 1552–1870 — as a French fortress, Metz becomes a byword for siege and frontier defence.
- 1870–1945 — capitulation, German annexation, return, annexation and liberation turn the name into a symbol of Franco-German conflict.
- Modern — a French city of art and stained glass, its name unchanged in English.
