- [Roman image of Fortuna, goddess of chance] - [she turns her wheel and human fate appears capricious — personified, respected and feared]
- [Middle English "fortune" = chance or fate in general] - [colourless and philosophical; religious writers half-christianised her as Providence]
- [16th–18th c. "one’s lot or condition in life"] - [plural fortunes gathers the whole drift of a life — health, rank, income — shaped by luck]
- [18th–19th c. "wealth, property"] - ["to make a fortune" replaces older "to make one’s fortune"; material success becomes the leading sense in a commercial age]
- [20th–21st c. "the fortunes of X" = the fluctuating career of an institution or idea] - [used of companies, clubs and nations; neutral and almost statistical, no longer about luck but about measurable ups and downs]
