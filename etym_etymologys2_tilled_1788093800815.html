Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*tilaz
"fitting, suitable, good"

↓

Proto-Germanic (c. 200–500 CE)
*tilōjaną
"to strive after, aim at, attain"

↓

Old English (8th–11th c.)
tilian
"to strive, labour; to cultivate land"

↓

Middle English (c. 1200–1500)
tilen
"to cultivate, plough; to obtain"

↓

Modern English (16th c. – present)
TILLED
"cultivated, ploughed and prepared (of land)"

Note: The deeper origin of *tilaz is debated: some scholars connect the family to PIE *dʰeh₁- "to put, set" (a thing set as a goal or mark, cf. German Ziel "goal"), but no PIE etymon is securely agreed. Unrelated to till "until" (from Old Norse til) and to till "cash drawer".
