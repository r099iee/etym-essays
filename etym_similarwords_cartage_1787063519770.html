• haulage: Similar because both name the transport of goods by road vehicle and the fee for it; differs because haulage is the modern motor-age word covering trucks and long-haul freight, while cartage evokes horse-drawn carts and short-distance hauling, and is older and narrower.
  • carriage: Similar because both are "-age" nouns for transporting goods and the charge for it; differs because carriage also names a passenger vehicle, a part of a machine, and a way of bearing oneself, a much wider range.
  • drayage: Similar because both denote short-distance, terminal-to-terminal goods hauling (often docks and railheads); differs because drayage specifically means hauling by dray, a low flatbed cart, and is the more technical modern successor term.
  • freight: Similar because both belong to the transport of goods and its costs; differs because freight refers primarily to the goods (cargo) themselves or the overall shipping charge, is also a verb, and covers rail, sea, and air, while cartage is strictly the carting leg and its fee.
  • transport: Similar because both mean moving things from one place to another; differs because transport is a general verb/noun covering every mode and person (including passengers), and carries no built-in "charge" sense.
  • portage: Similar because both are "-age" nouns of carrying and of the fee for carrying; differs because portage is carrying boats/goods overland between waterways (or the toll for it), a water-travel term, not a road-carting one.
  • towage: Similar because both are "-age" service-fee words for moving things; differs because towage is the charge for towing vessels, a nautical usage with no cart or wagon in it.
  • lighterage: Similar because both are commercial "-age" charges for transferring cargo; differs because lighterage is loading/transport by lighter (a flat-bottomed barge) on the water.
  • carting: Similar because it is the plain verbal noun of the same act (carting = conveyance by cart); differs because carting never names the money charge, only the activity, whereas cartage carries both the act and the fee.
  • conveyance: Similar because both are general nouns for moving goods; differs because conveyance is far broader (any means of carriage, and often passengers or legal transfer of property), whereas cartage is fixed to carts and their charges.
