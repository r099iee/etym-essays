- Late Latin / early use of remit: to send back, release, forgive - legal and theological register; remission as mercy
- 16th–17th century remit in English: to pardon, to slacken, to abate - doubled sense of forgiving a debt and lessening an effort
- 17th–18th century unremitting coined: not pardoned, not slackened - stern moral and religious tone; unremitting sin, unremitting diligence
- 19th century: ceaseless labour and suffering - Victorian admiration for tireless duty alongside dread of unrelieved toil
- 20th century to present: unremitting pressure, attention, abstraction - formal journalistic and academic favourite; connotation of exhausting constancy that begs for relief
