PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*bʰel- (swelling, round stem) + *werǵ- (to work, make)
"tree trunk, log" + "to work, fashion, construct"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE)
*bulō- + *werką
"tree trunk, log, plank" + "work, construction, fortification"
↓
Middle Dutch (c. 13th–14th c.)
bolwerc
"bastion, timber rampart, defensive earthwork faced with tree trunks"
Note: Cognate with Middle High German bolewerc (modern German Bollwerk) and English bulwark.
↓
Middle French (c. 1400–1450)
boulevart / boulevert
"artillery bastion, earthen rampart, timber fortification"
Note: Borrowed from Middle Dutch military engineering during the Hundred Years' War and Franco-Flemish conflicts.
↓
Classical French (c. 1670–1700)
boulevard
"broad, tree-lined public promenade laid out on demolished city ramparts"
Note: Decisive semantic shift following Louis XIV's decree of 1670 razing the defensive walls of Paris.
↓
Modern French / English Borrowing (mid-18th c. – 19th c.)
boulevard
"broad, grand, tree-lined thoroughfare or avenue"
↓
Modern English (present)
BOULEVARD
