• ’Tis: Similar as the same proclitic contraction of “it is” in the same archaic-poetic register; differs in tense (present) and in having a slightly more living afterlife in proverbs (“’tis ever thus”) than ’twas.
  • ’Twere: Similar as the sister contraction of “it were” (subjunctive/past hypothetical); differs in being rarer and reserved for conditionals and contrary-to-fact clauses.
  • ’Twill / ’twould: Similar as contractions of “it will / it would” in the same family; differ in having retreated even further from ordinary use than ’twas.
  • ’Twasn’t: Similar as the negative of ’twas; differs by being doubly contracted (it was not) and almost exclusively dialectal or humorous.
  • “It was”: Similar as the uncontracted equivalent with identical meaning; differs in register and rhythm — two full syllables, no archaism, the default of ordinary speech and prose.
  • “There was” / ‘twas existential: Similar because ’twas often serves the same scene-setting function as “there was”; differs grammatically — ’twas keeps “it” as subject while “there was” uses the existential there.
  • Once upon a time: Similar as a fixed story-opening that sets a past scene; differs as a phrase, not a verb, and as a fairy-tale marker rather than a poetic archaism.
  • Yore / of yore: Similar in signalling deliberately old-fashioned narrative retrospect; differs as an adverb-noun of “long ago” rather than a verb form.
