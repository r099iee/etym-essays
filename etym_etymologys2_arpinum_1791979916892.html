Latin (4th c. BCE)
Arpinum
"Volscian town on the Liris"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
Arpinum
"municipium; Cicero's birthplace"
↓
English (16th c.–present)
Arpinum
"the ancient town"

Note: the toponym is pre-Latin and probably Volscian, but no Volscian form is attested and no etymology is securely established; ancient founder stories are legend. English Arpinum is a humanist borrowing of the Latin name, while Italian Arpino is the town's continuous vernacular reflex.
