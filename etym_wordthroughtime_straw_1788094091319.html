- (700s–1000s) - strēaw: dried grain stalks, literally "that which is strewn" — the universal floor-covering and bedding of hall and byre; the name describes the use, not the plant.
- (1100s–1400s) - The medieval all-rounder: mattress of rich and poor, stable litter, roof thatch, brick-clay — "to be in the straw" is a received idiom for lying in childbed.
- (1500s–1600s) - The age of proverbs: "not worth a straw", "man of straw" (a sham); Bible English fixes straw as trivial matter (the straw withheld in Exodus; the straw and stubble burned at judgment).
- (1700s–1800s) - "Straw in the wind", "draw straws", "the last straw that breaks the camel's back"; in America the "straw man" and the "straw poll" are born.
- (1800s–1900s) - The drinking straw is industrialized (waxed paper 1888, plastic mid-1900s); straw hats become a world fashion; "straw boss" appears in US labour slang.
- (2000s–today) - Plastic-straw bans make the humble tube a global environmental symbol; "strawman" is standard in argumentation theory; the material itself returns as eco-insulation and building block — 1,300 years from strewn bedding to rhetoric and ecology.
