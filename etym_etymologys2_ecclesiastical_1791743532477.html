Note: Greek ekklēsía initially denoted the sovereign democratic assembly of male Athenian citizens summoned forth by a herald. In the Hellenistic Septuagint, Jewish translators adopted ekklēsía to render Hebrew qāhāl ("the gathered congregation of Israel"). Early Christian writers subsequently appropriated the term to designate the local and universal community of believers "called out" from pagan society, permanently shifting the word from civic politics to sacred institutional theology.
