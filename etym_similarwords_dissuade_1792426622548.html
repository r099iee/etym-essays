• deter: Similar because both verbs seek to prevent someone from undertaking a particular course of action or dangerous endeavor; differs because deter operates primarily through fear of consequences, threat of punishment, physical barriers, or intimidation (as in armed deterrence), whereas dissuade relies strictly on rational argument, verbal persuasion, and appeals to intellect or conscience.
  • discourage: Similar because both involve turning someone aside from an intended purpose; differs because discourage focuses on dampening enthusiasm, undermining confidence, or portraying an endeavor as hopelessly difficult and bleak, whereas dissuade proceeds through structured argumentation, rational evidence, and formal advice.
  • persuade: Similar because both denote convincing an agent through verbal argumentation and rhetorical appeals addressed to their reasoning faculty; differs because persuade is positively directed toward urging an individual into action or belief (persuade to act), whereas dissuade is privative and preventative, steering an individual away from a contemplated deed (dissuade from acting).
  • disadvise: Similar because both convey advising an interlocutor against an unwise undertaking; differs because disadvise is an archaic, pedantic, and rare formation that merely denotes giving contrary advice, whereas dissuade carries strong connotations of successful persuasion and psychological turning.
  • exhort: Similar because both involve passionate, earnest verbal appeals delivered by an advisor or friend; differs because exhort typically means to urgently encourage, incite, or spur someone onward toward a noble duty, whereas dissuade is specifically oriented toward stopping or withholding an action.
  • deprecate: Similar because both express strong disapproval of a contemplated action and attempt to ward it off; differs because deprecate historically means to pray against or express vehement moral protest against a measure, whereas dissuade operates as direct dialogue aimed at changing another person's personal choice.
  • caution: Similar because both warn an agent about hidden dangers, pitfalls, or adverse consequences; differs because caution merely provides notice of risk without necessarily insisting that the entire project be abandoned, whereas dissuade seeks the complete renunciation of the intended endeavor.
