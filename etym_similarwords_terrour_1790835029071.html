• Dread: Similar because both represent profound, heavy fear; differs because dread emphasizes a lingering, anticipatory, and brooding apprehension of an approaching future evil, whereas terrour is an acute, sharp, and overwhelming shock of immediate peril.
  • Horror: Similar because both involve extreme emotional shock; differs because horror incorporates feelings of revulsion, disgust, moral shock, and dismay at witnessing something grotesque or gruesome, whereas terrour is rooted strictly in personal peril and panic.
  • Panic: Similar because both involve intense, sudden fright; differs because panic denotes sudden, unreasoning, and infectious mass hysteria or frantic, disorganized flight, whereas terrour can be frozen, solitary, or awe-inspired.
  • Fear: Similar because both denote the emotional response to danger; differs because fear is the broad, generic baseline emotion covering all degrees from mild caution to intense alarm, whereas terrour represents the ultimate, extreme zenith of fear.
  • Trepidation: Similar because both trace etymologically to trembling; differs because trepidation refers to nervous, hesitant, and agitated anxiety or trembling uncertainty, lacking the overpowering magnitude of terrour.
  • Consternation: Similar because both involve emotional shock; differs because consternation emphasizes sudden amazement, perplexity, and paralyzing dismay that renders someone unable to decide what to do.
  • Awe: Similar because both involve being overwhelmed by vast power; differs because awe blends deep reverence, wonder, and veneration with fear, whereas terrour is dominated by acute distress and danger.
