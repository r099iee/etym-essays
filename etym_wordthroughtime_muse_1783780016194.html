Ancient Greek Deities - Muse names one of the nine sister goddesses of poetry, music, dance, and the arts, daughters of Zeus and Mnemosyne; in classical antiquity the word denotes a real cult-object invoked at the start of epic poems, with a sacred, ritual aura.
Latin & Renaissance Art - Through Latin and the Renaissance, Muse becomes the standing figure of the epic invocation — the divine source a poet calls upon for inspiration — while remaining a mythological personage, elevated and learned.
17th-18th Century Secularization - Poets from Milton onward absorb the Muse into their own imagination, so the word shades from an actual goddess into the inner or heavenly spirit of creative power, already half-personified and half-metaphor.
Contemporary Sense - In modern usage the lower-case muse means any person or thing that inspires creative work (an artist's muse), a fully secular and affectionate term, while the capitalized Muse survives only in classical or archaic literary contexts.
