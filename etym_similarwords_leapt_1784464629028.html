• Jumped: Similar because both mean to leave the ground suddenly; differs because jumped is the general, everyday, neutral word, while leapt adds suddenness, force, and often upward reach — a more vivid and literary bound.
  • Sprang: Similar because both are strong pasts meaning to rise or move suddenly; differs because sprang stresses elasticity or a source of springy energy (a coiled spring), while leapt stresses the single forceful bound itself.
  • Bounded: Similar because both mean to spring vigorously; differs because bounding is a series of springy steps or loping movement, while leapt is one sudden, forceful bound — especially upward.
  • Vaulted: Similar because both mean to jump, especially over something; differs because vaulting usually clears a specific obstacle and may use hands, a pole, or a horse, while leaping is the general sudden bound.
  • Flared: Similar because both can describe a sudden increase in flame or light; differs because flaring stresses spreading, brightening, and heat expansion, while a flame that leapt stresses the sudden upward surge of the fire.
  • Shot up: Similar because both mean to rise suddenly and fast; differs because shot up is colloquial and emphasizes raw speed, while leapt is the formal, slightly literary word for the same sudden rise.
  • Soared: Similar because both describe rising rapidly; differs because soaring is a smooth, sustained, often majestic flight or climb, while leaping is a single abrupt bound — explosive, not gliding.
  • Darted: Similar because both describe sudden quick movement; differs because darting is lateral or erratic motion at speed, while leaping is the specific airborne bound, especially upward.
  • Leaped: Similar because it is the same verb’s regular past form; differs only in form and register — leaped (pronounced /liːpt/) is the regularized spelling, common in American English, while leapt (pronounced /lɛpt/) is the strong, chiefly British spelling used in this card.
  • Vault / spring over: Similar because both are leaping motions over or across obstacles; differs because these stress clearing a barrier, while leapt can be a pure upward surge with no obstacle at all.
