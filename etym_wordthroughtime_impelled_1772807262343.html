- [Physical driving] - "Impel" enters English (from the Latin root for driving against) meaning to push or drive something forward, literally and mechanically.
- [Moral and psychological force] - It shifts to figurative force on the will: to drive or urge a person to action by duty, conscience, or feeling, without external command.
- [Modern formal usage] - Today "impelled" keeps a formal, Latinate register, used when an inner pressure — not law or threat — propels someone to act; lighter than "compelled," stronger than "prompted."
