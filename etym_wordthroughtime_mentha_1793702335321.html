- Ancient Greek (μίνθη, *minthē*) - Associated with mythology (nymph Minthe) and recognized for its aromatic and medicinal properties. Used in culinary, ritualistic, and medicinal contexts.
- Classical Latin (*mentha*) - Direct adoption from Greek, maintaining its association with the plant. Widely cultivated and used in Roman cuisine, perfumery, and medicine.
- Medieval Latin (*mentha*) - Continued use in botanical and medical texts throughout the Middle Ages, often appearing in herbals and pharmacopoeias. Its practical applications remained consistent.
- Early Modern Botanical Nomenclature (*Mentha*) - Formalized as the genus name by Carl Linnaeus in the 18th century, establishing it as the scientific classification for all mint species. This marked a shift to a strictly scientific and taxonomic usage.
- Contemporary Scientific Usage (*Mentha*) - Remains the universally accepted botanical genus name. Its connotation is purely scientific, referring to the biological classification of mint plants, distinct from the common English word 'mint' which derives from it.
