Old English (c. 9th–11th c.)
dreflian
"to slaver, drivel, snivel"
↓
Middle English (13th–15th c.)
drulen
"to dribble, slaver"
↓
English dialect (18th c.)
drivel / drool
"to let saliva run from the mouth"
↓
English (1802, first printed record)
drool
"to slaver; talk nonsense"
↓
drooling
"letting saliva flow; drivelling"

Note: the origin is uncertain. Drool is first recorded in print in 1802 and is generally taken to be a dialectal variant of drivel, whose Old English ancestor dreflian is attested. The Middle English drulen is a reconstructed or dialectal intermediate rather than a securely documented standard form, and the deeper Germanic or Indo-European root of the family has not been established with confidence. No PIE root is therefore asserted here.
