- Late 13th to 14th Century: Literal spatial and physical resting place - Denoted physically resting or positioned upon a tangible object, altar, or tract of land ("there-upon").
- 15th to 16th Century: Sequential narrative connective and temporal immediacy - Flourished in late Middle English and early Tudor chronicles to mean "straightway, at once, or immediately following that event."
- 17th to 18th Century: Causal consequence, theological deduction, and philosophical reasoning - Emphasized direct moral or logical outcome (as in Milton's "loss thereupon of Paradise"), denoting that which flows inevitably from a premise.
- 19th Century: Institutionalization in statutory drafting, judicial decrees, and parliamentary records - Connoted formal authority, exact procedural sequence, and official administrative consequence.
- 20th to 21st Century: Archaic literary and specialized appellate legal register - Largely absent from colloquial conversation, but cherished in epic storytelling, historical prose, and judicial opinions for its solemnity and precision.
