• Dwarf: similar because it denotes a small humanoid being of folklore, often bearded and associated with mining or craftsmanship; differs because dwarfs are usually sturdier, more warlike, and more fully social, while gnomes are earth-elementals and often more secretive.
  • Elf: similar because both are diminutive supernatural beings; differs because elves are traditionally associated with air, light, beauty, and archery, not with underground treasure.
  • Goblin: similar because both are small folkloric creatures; differs because goblins are usually malicious, grotesque, and surface- or cave-dwelling raiders, whereas gnomes are more often neutral guardians.
  • Sprite: similar because it names a small nature spirit; differs because a sprite is airy, elusive, and often water- or woodland-bound, without the gnome’s mining and treasure associations.
  • Pixie: similar because it is a tiny legendary being; differs because pixies are specifically southwestern English, mischievous, and often winged or dancing, not underground miners.
  • Brownie: similar because it is a small household helper of British folklore; differs because brownies work at night in houses and farms and are not earth-elementals or treasure-guardians.
  • Kobold: similar because the German kobold is a mine spirit and a close folkloric cousin; differs because kobolds are often malevolent tricksters and the word is not naturalized in English as a general term.
  • Leprechaun: similar because it is a small bearded figure associated with hidden treasure; differs because leprechauns are specifically Irish, shoemaking, and associated with a pot of gold at the rainbow’s end.
  • Troll: similar because both live underground or in remote places; differs because trolls are large, strong, and threatening, the opposite of the gnome’s diminutive scale.
  • Fairy: similar because both belong to the broad family of supernatural nature beings; differs because fairies are usually associated with enchantment, wings, and the fairy realm rather than with mines and ore.
  • Imp: similar because it names a small supernatural creature; differs because an imp is typically a minor demon or malicious sprite, with a stronger diabolic connotation.
  • Hobgoblin: similar because it is a small household or cave spirit; differs because it is specifically a mischievous or frightening goblin, not a treasure-guarding earth elemental.
  • Tomte / Nisse: similar because these Scandinavian farm spirits are the closest regional relatives of the gnome; differs because they are tied to households and barns, and the words remain foreign loanwords in English.
  • Garden gnome: similar because it is the ornamental descendant of the creature; differs because it denotes a mass-produced statue, not a being believed in.
