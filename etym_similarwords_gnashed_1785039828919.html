• Gritted: Similar because both involve clenching the teeth; differs because gritting is silent, controlled endurance under strain, while gnashing is loud, involuntary, explosive fury.
  • Clashed: Similar because both mean hard objects striking together; differs because clashing is external — blades or shields meeting — whereas gnashing is internal, the body's own teeth grinding.
  • Ground: Similar because both involve rubbing the teeth together; differs because grinding is slow, sustained pressure with no snapping impact, while gnashing is convulsive and audible.
  • Snapped: Similar because both are sharp jaw actions; differs because a snap is a single quick bite, while gnashing is repeated and grinding.
  • Raged: Similar because both express violent anger; differs because raged is a general verb of fury, while gnashed is a specific physical gesture of the teeth.
  • Stamped: Similar because both are percussive gestures of fury; differs because stamping is the feet striking the ground, while gnashing is the teeth colliding — and in the passage the two are paired as parts of one drumbeat.
  • Crunched: Similar because both are grinding, grating actions; differs because crunching is usually of food or brittle matter under the teeth, while gnashing is the bare teeth against themselves.
  • Grated: Similar because both are rasping contacts; differs because grating describes rough surfaces rubbing, while gnashing is specifically the teeth striking together.
  • Snarled: Similar because both express animal rage; differs because snarling is a facial and vocal baring of the teeth, while gnashing is the audible grinding of them.
  • Clenched: Similar because both are involuntary expressions of fury; differs because clenching is a tight, motionless grip of jaw or fist, while gnashing is a repeated percussive collision.
