Classical Latin (1st c. BCE)
recēdere

↓
Old French (12th c.)
receder
‘to retreat, withdraw’
↓
Middle English (late 15th c.)
receden
‘to retire, go backward’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
recede
‘to move back, withdraw’
↓
Modern English (current)
recedes
‘moves back, withdraws, diminishes’

Note: The Latin base cēdere is compounded with the directional prefix re- (‘back, again, away’). In the transition to Early Modern English, the third-person singular present inflection shifted from the older Southern/Midland suffix -eth (producing recedeth) to the modern standard northern inflectional -s (recedes).
