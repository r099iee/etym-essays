• Interlude: Similar because both denote an intervening period of time between two distinct events; differs because an interlude is typically a brief, pleasant, recreational, or theatrical pause between acts, whereas an interregnum is a serious, often tense or volatile vacancy of governance or sovereign authority.
  • Hiatus: Similar because both refer to an interruption or gap in continuity; differs because a hiatus is a generic pause, break in work, or missing anatomical/textual gap without any inherent connection to political leadership, sovereign rule, or power dynamics.
  • Intermission: Similar because both denote a temporary cessation of activity; differs because an intermission is a planned, structured recess during a performance, sporting match, or formal conference designed for rest and refreshment.
  • Lacuna: Similar because both represent an unfilled gap or void; differs because a lacuna specifically signifies a missing textual passage, lost manuscript section, or blind spot in a legal statute or scientific dataset, rather than a temporal suspension of leadership.
  • Transition: Similar because both describe the phase bridging an old state of affairs to a new one; differs because a transition emphasizes the active, orderly process of moving forward and adapting, while an interregnum emphasizes the vacant void, suspension, and potential instability between regimes.
  • Power vacuum: Similar because both convey a dangerous absence of centralized authority; differs because power vacuum is a modern political science idiom describing the geopolitical void left by a fallen regime that invites rival forces to rush in, while interregnum is the formal historical and temporal term for the intervening duration.
  • Lull: Similar because both denote a temporary cessation; differs because a lull is a brief, soothing, or deceptive calming of storm, battle, or activity, whereas an interregnum can be turbulent, chaotic, and prolonged.
  • Interposition: Similar because both stem from Latin roots denoting action in between; differs because interposition refers to the physical or diplomatic act of stepping between conflicting parties to mediate or obstruct, rather than a temporal succession gap.
  • Vacancy: Similar because both describe an unoccupied post; differs because a vacancy is an administrative state indicating an open job, seat, or office waiting to be filled, lacking the grand historical, dynastic, and sociopolitical gravity of an interregnum.
