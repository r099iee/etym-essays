- Legal harmlessness - 15th-17th century promise to keep a party without loss, pardon for technical illegality, protective and reassuring
- Commercial cover - 18th-19th century insurance and contract clauses spreading risk, respected as prudent business practice
- Imperial exaction - late 19th-early 20th century great-power indemnities imposed on China, France and others, turning protection word into tribute, viewed as humiliation by payers and justice by exactors
- Modern double use - contemporary insurance indemnity as neutral protection versus historical war indemnities as condemned extortion, with reparations debates reviving moral scrutiny
