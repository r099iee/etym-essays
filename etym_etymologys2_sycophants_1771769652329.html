LINEAR CHAIN (SYCOPHANTS, plural of SYCOPHANT) — a Greek compound; each element has its own history. ELEMENT 1 — Greek σῦκον (sŷkon) "fig": 1. Greek σῦκον (sŷkon, "fig," the fruit or the tree) — attested in ancient Greek; beside it συκῆ (sykē, "fig tree"). 2. No secure PIE reconstruction: sŷkon is widely treated as a Pre-Greek / Mediterranean substrate word or as of uncertain origin; it does not belong to the well-attested Indo-European vocabulary, and any deeper chain would be speculation. The element survives in compounds such as συκοφάντης (sykophántēs). ELEMENT 2 — Greek φαίνειν (phaínein) "to show, bring to light": 1. PIE *bʰeh₂- (to shine, appear; by extension to make appear, show, speak) — reconstructed root (5th–4th millennium BCE); the source of a large family of "appearing/showing" words in Greek. 2. Greek φαίνειν (phaínein, "to show, make visible, reveal, bring to light") — attested from the Homeric period onward; thematic present of the "shine/appear" family. 3. Greek compound element -φάντης (-phántēs, "one who shows," in agent compounds) — as in συκοφάντης. COMPOUND PATH TO ENGLISH: 4. Greek συκοφάντης (sykophántēs, literally "fig-shower"; classical sense: informer, false accuser, malicious denouncer in the law-courts) — attested in Greek from the 5th century BCE. 5. Latin sȳcophanta (informer; later also trickster, parasite, self-seeking flatterer) — attested from the 1st century BCE onward. 6. French sycophante (same senses) — Old/Middle French. 7. English sycophant (16th century: "slanderer, false accuser"; later: "servile flatterer") — borrowed via Latin/French. 8. English sycophants (the plural; beside the derivatives sycophantic, sycophancy) — the modern idiomatic form. NOTE ON DEBATE: The literal meaning "fig-shower" is notoriously obscure and has been debated since antiquity. Traditional explanations: denouncing those who illegally exported figs from Attica; "showing the fig" as a gesture of insult or accusation; or a marketplace origin for the petty informer. No single explanation is secure. The PIE root *bʰeh₂- for phainein is standard, but the reconstruction of sŷkon as a Pre-Greek substrate word is itself a theory, not an established fact.
