- [Old English: c. 800–1100] - filmen - A thin skin, anatomical membrane, foreskin, or caul covering animal tissues - [perception: medical, anatomical, and pastoral terminology for animal hides and membranes]
- [Middle English: c. 1150–1450] - filme - Expanded to denote plant pellicles, skins of seeds, the lining of eggshells, and early references to cloudiness in the eyes - [perception: domestic culinary, botanical, and herbalist observation]
- [Early Modern English: c. 1500–1750] - film - Widely used in literature (Shakespeare, Milton, Pope) for the dimming veil over the eyes of the dying or fainting, gossamer webs, and surface scum on water - [perception: poetic, melancholic symbol of failing senses and fragile beauty]
- [Industrial & 19th Century: c. 1800–1895] - film - Applied in chemistry to surface coatings, and in 1888 adopted by George Eastman for photographic celluloid strips coated with light-sensitive emulsion - [perception: technical precision, chemical innovation, and popular photography]
- [20th–21st Century] - film - Universal worldwide term for the cinema, movies, filmmaking industry, and advanced materials science (semiconductor thin films) - [perception: premier 20th-century artistic medium; cutting-edge nanotechnology]
