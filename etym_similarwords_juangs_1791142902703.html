• Santals: Similar because they are also a large North Munda Adivasi people; differs because they are far more numerous, live mainly in Jharkhand-West Bengal-Odisha plains, and speak Santali not Juang.
  • Mundas: Similar because they are fellow Munda-speaking tribes of the Chota Nagpur plateau; differs because Mundas are centred in Jharkhand with distinct Mundari language and history.
  • Kharias: Similar because they are the closest linguistic relatives of the Juangs within Munda; differs because Kharias form a separate tribe with Kharia language and different districts.
  • Hos: Similar because they are another Munda tribal group of Odisha-Jharkhand; differs because Hos have their own Ho language and Kolhan identity.
  • Bhils: Similar because they are also a major Scheduled Tribe of India; differs because Bhils live in western-central India and speak Indo-Aryan Bhil languages, not Austroasiatic.
  • Gonds: Similar because they are a large central Indian Adivasi group; differs because Gonds speak Dravidian Gondi and have a different kingdom history.
  • Adivasis: Similar because Juangs are included under this umbrella for Indigenous peoples; differs because Adivasi is a generic category, not a specific community.
  • Scheduled Tribe: Similar because Juangs hold this constitutional status; differs because it is a legal-administrative label covering hundreds of groups.
  • Aborigines: Similar because older English used it for tribal first peoples; differs because it is generic, now often Australia-specific, and considered dated for India.
  • tribespeople: Similar because it denotes members of a tribe; differs because it is a common noun without the Juangs’ specific language, place, and history.
  • clan: Similar because Juangs are organized in exogamous clans (boksa); differs because clan is a subunit of kinship, not the whole ethnic group.
  • villagers: Similar because most Juangs live in small hill villages; differs because villager describes residence only and erases ethnicity and language.
