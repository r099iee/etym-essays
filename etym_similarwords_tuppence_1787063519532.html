• Two cents: Similar because both represent a tiny monetary sum used to denote an unsolicited opinion (e.g., "my two cents"); differs because two cents is American English, whereas tuppence is distinctly British and Commonwealth.
  • A fig: Similar because both are used in negative idioms expressing complete indifference (e.g., "not care a fig"); differs because a fig has an older, Mediterranean botanical and gestural origin (the obscene thumb gesture fico), while tuppence is monetary.
  • A damn: Similar because both emphasize total disregard (e.g., "not give a damn"); differs because a damn is a profanity expressing angry or dismissive contempt, whereas tuppence is genteel, polite, and whimsical.
  • A straw: Similar in archaic expressions of worthlessness (e.g., "not care a straw"); differs because a straw draws upon worthless agricultural chaff rather than currency.
  • A whit: Similar because it means the smallest conceivable amount; differs because a whit is formal, literary, and archaic (e.g., "not a whit better"), lacking the conversational, everyday flavor of tuppence.
  • Peanuts: Similar because both denote an insignificant sum of money; differs because peanuts specifically describes meager payment or wages (e.g., "working for peanuts") rather than an attitude of indifference.
  • Trifle: Similar because both denote something of little value or importance; differs because trifle is an abstract noun of French origin describing an unimportant matter, object, or dessert.
  • Pittance: Similar because both refer to tiny financial amounts; differs because a pittance carries a strong sense of injustice and pathos, denoting an inadequate wage that barely sustains life.
  • A hoot: Similar in expressions of indifference (e.g., "don't give a hoot"); differs because a hoot is an informal American colloquialism referring to an owl's call or a shout of derision.
  • Dime: Similar as a low-value coin used in idioms (e.g., "a dime a dozen"); differs because a dime is ten American cents and emphasizes common abundance rather than indifference.
