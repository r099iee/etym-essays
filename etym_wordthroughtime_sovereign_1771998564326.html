- [13th-14th century supreme ruler] - ['Sovereign' first names a monarch with ultimate power, above all others and answerable to none]
- [17th century absolute authority] - [Extends to power, right, and will: sovereign authority is final and undivided, the property of a supreme state]
- [17th-18th century nation-state concept] - [Becomes the keystone of modern political thought: a sovereign state is one with full independence and no superior]
- [19th-20th century legal and technical] - [In international law 'sovereignty' is technical and precise, while 'sovereign' also drifts into the decorative (sovereign remedy, sovereign contempt) and the monetary (a gold sovereign)]
- [Present double life] - [Still fuses supreme power with the aura of reigning majesty, whether of a monarch, a state, or an authority]
