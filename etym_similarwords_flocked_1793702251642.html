• Gather: Similar because it implies coming together; differs because 'gather' can be for any number of people/things, while 'flock' specifically suggests a large, often spontaneous, and somewhat unorganized group movement.
  • Congregate: Similar because it means to assemble in a crowd; differs because 'congregate' often implies a more formal or intentional assembly, whereas 'flock' can be more instinctive or driven by a common attraction.
  • Converge: Similar because it means to come together from different directions; differs because 'converge' focuses on the meeting point, while 'flock' emphasizes the collective movement towards that point.
  • Stream: Similar because it suggests continuous movement in a large number; differs because 'stream' often implies a more orderly or flowing movement, while 'flock' can be more chaotic or dense.
  • Surge: Similar because it implies a sudden, powerful movement of a large crowd; differs because 'surge' often suggests a more forceful or overwhelming push, while 'flock' is generally a more sustained, albeit large, movement.
  • Throng: Similar because it refers to a large, densely packed crowd, and can also be a verb meaning to crowd into or around; differs because 'throng' often emphasizes the density and press of the crowd, while 'flock' focuses more on the act of moving together.
  • Crowd: Similar because it refers to a large number of people, and can be a verb meaning to fill a space; differs because 'crowd' can imply a more static mass, whereas 'flock' specifically denotes movement towards a common point.
  • Horde: Similar because it refers to a large, often unruly, group; differs because 'horde' often carries a more negative connotation of invasiveness or destructiveness, which 'flock' generally does not.
