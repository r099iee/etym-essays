- Old English — the word asks “in what way”; it also introduces indirect questions and, paired with the demonstrative, forms the formula “how that” (“how is it that”).
- Middle English — the conjunctive use (“he said how that…”) flourishes, and the phrase “how be it” hardens into the single word howbeit.
- 14th–16th c. — however and howsoever are coined; exclamatory how becomes a staple of poetry and pulpit rhetoric (“How art thou fallen”).
- 17th c. — how do you do? and how now? fix the word in polite and dramatic formulas; the interrogative of health generalises.
- 19th c. — colloquial and technical compounds multiply: know-how (1838), how come (by 1848), and how!; the Western-film greeting How! spreads as a pseudo-Indian cliché.
- 20th c. — how-to becomes an attributive adjective for practical guides; how’s that? is fixed as a cricket appeal.
- 21st c. — the word dominates instructional and digital registers (how-to videos, explainers) and survives in discourse formulas (“how good is that?”), while its literary exclamatory use, as in the source fragment, now reads as deliberate archaism.
