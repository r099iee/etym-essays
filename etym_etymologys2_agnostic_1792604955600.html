Note on historical debate: The historical coining of agnostic is among the most thoroughly documented in the history of English lexicography. Thomas Henry Huxley specifically recorded in his 1889 retrospective essay *Agnosticism* that he invented the word in 1869 to describe his own philosophical position among the eminent thinkers of the London Metaphysical Society. While some contemporary classical purists objected that Greek *agnōstikós* did not exist in classical Attic prose—and argued that etymologically an adjective formed from *a-* + *gnōstikos* would strictly mean "incapable of knowing" or "unintelligent"—Huxley defended his neoclassical synthesis. He explained that he intended it as a direct, evocative antithesis to the early Christian *Gnostics*, who claimed esoteric, unprovable illumination into divine mysteries. Huxley's coinage succeeded brilliantly, filling a vital intellectual void in modern philosophy and language.
