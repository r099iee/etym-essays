- Ancient Greek Mythology - Primarily known as the divine keeper of the winds, a powerful but subordinate figure in the Olympian pantheon, often invoked in epic poetry (Homer, Virgil). His name was associated with the swift, changeable nature of wind.
- Classical and Renaissance Literature - Continued to be a prominent literary allusion, symbolizing the unpredictable forces of nature or divine intervention, often invoked to explain storms or sudden changes in fortune. Connotation remained largely consistent with his mythological role.
- 18th-19th Century Romanticism & Science - The figure of Aeolus inspired the invention of the Aeolian harp, linking his name to musical and artistic expression. Simultaneously, his name was adopted into scientific terminology (Aeolian processes, Aeolian tone) to describe wind-related phenomena, shifting his perception from purely mythological to a conceptual anchor for natural forces.
- Modern Usage - Primarily retains its mythological and literary significance, serving as a direct reference to the keeper of the winds. The derived adjective 'Aeolian' is widely used in geology, acoustics, and fluid dynamics, making the concept of 'wind-related' the most common contemporary application of his name, often without direct reference to the mythological figure himself.
