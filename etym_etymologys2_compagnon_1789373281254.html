PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*ḱom ("with, together, beside") + *peh₂- ("to protect, feed, pasture")
"to feed together; sharing food and nourishment"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*kom- + *pānis
"together with bread, shared nourishment"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
cum + pānis
"with bread" (conceptual calque basis)
↓
Late Latin / Vulgar Latin (c. 5th–7th c.)
compāniō (nom.) / compāniōnem (acc.)
"one who eats bread with another; messmate, comrade"
Note: An early Romance innovation, likely calqued on Germanic *gahlaibo ('messmate', literally 'with-loaf').
↓
Old French (c. 10th–12th c.)
compain (nom.) / compaignon (obl.)
"fellow, comrade, partner, fellow traveler"
Note: Preserved two-case Old French declension; the oblique form compaignon survived into modern French.
↓
Middle French (c. 14th–16th c.)
compagnon
"journeyman artisan in a trade guild; intimate associate; hereditary surname"
↓
Modern French & International Academia (20th c.–present)
Antoine Compagnon (1950–present)
"celebrated literary historian, Proustian critic, and Academician"
