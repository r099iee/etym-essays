• Advice: Similar because counsel is advice; differs because “advice” is the plain everyday word, while “counsel” implies formal, weighty, considered guidance.
  • Guidance: Similar because both mean direction given to someone; differs because “guidance” suggests ongoing support rather than a single piece of advice, and has no legal sense.
  • Recommendation: Similar because both are suggestions of a course of action; differs because a recommendation is specific and advisory in a lighter sense, with no deliberative or legal weight.
  • Suggestion: Similar because both propose a course; differs because “suggestion” is tentative and weak, the opposite of counsel's gravity.
  • Consultation / deliberation: Similar because “to take counsel” is to consult or deliberate; differs because these name the process, not the advice produced.
  • Wisdom: Similar because good counsel embodies wisdom; differs because wisdom is a quality, while counsel is the advice that expresses it.
  • Admonition: Similar because both are advice with moral weight; differs because admonition reproves or warns, while counsel may be purely practical.
  • Exhortation: Similar because both urge a course of action; differs because exhortation is an emotional, stirring appeal, while counsel is calm and reasoned.
  • Lawyer / attorney / barrister / solicitor: Similar because “counsel” in legal use denotes these professionals; differs because these are specific role names, while “counsel” is the collective, court-room term for the advocate or advocates.
  • Adviser / consultant: Similar because a counsellor is one who advises; differs because “adviser” is generic (financial adviser, political adviser), while “counsellor” in modern use suggests professional, often therapeutic guidance.
  • Council: Similar because it is the identically pronounced word for an assembly that deliberates and gives counsel; differs because a council is a body of people, not the advice itself, and its Latin source (concilium) is a different word.
  • Rede: Similar because the native English word “rede” meant exactly counsel; differs because “rede” is now archaic and poetic, while “counsel” (a French loan) became the standard word.
  • Plan / design: Similar because archaic “counsel” could mean an intention or design; differs because these are modern, neutral words with no advisory or legal senses.
