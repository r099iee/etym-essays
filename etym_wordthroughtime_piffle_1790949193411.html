- Mid-19th Century - Coined in British regional dialects as an expressive/onomatopoeic formation blending piff (a slight puff of air) with the frequentative -le.
- Late 19th Century - Adopted into British public school slang, university banter, and London literary journalism to describe harmless drivel.
- Early 20th Century - Popularized worldwide through Edwardian comic fiction, satirical magazines (such as Punch), and the humorous novels of P.G. Wodehouse.
- Mid to Late 20th Century - Retained in educated British, Commonwealth, and literary English as an affectionate, genteel dismissal of silly ideas.
- 21st Century - Continues as a whimsical, non-offensive synonym for nonsensical or trivial discourse in political commentary and popular culture.
