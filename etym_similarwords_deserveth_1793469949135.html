• Meriteth: Similar because both denote being worthy of reward or consequence under moral law; differs because meriteth (from Latin meritum, ‘earned wage’) has an even stronger philosophical and theological association with formal entitlement and spiritual justification, whereas deserveth traces back to personal service.
  • Warranteth: Similar because both justify a specific reaction or treatment; differs because warranteth focuses on authorization, justification, and legitimate sanction (e.g., ‘the evidence warranteth suspicion’), whereas deserveth focuses on the inherent moral claim of the subject.
  • Earneth: Similar because both involve acquiring a claim through effort; differs because earneth is transactional, economic, and tied directly to physical labor or discrete outputs, while deserveth encompasses intrinsic moral worth and character.
  • Entitleth: Similar because both establish a rightful claim; differs because entitleth focuses on legal, formal, or contractual rights bestowed by law or status (e.g., ‘a ticket entitleth the bearer’), whereas deserveth operates within the domain of moral justice.
  • Justifieth: Similar because both demonstrate fitness for an outcome; differs because justifieth emphasizes vindication, defending an action as right or reasonable, while deserveth asserts positive or negative worthiness.
  • Claimeth: Similar because both assert an interest in a reward; differs because claimeth is an active, vocal demand put forward by a claimant, whereas deserveth is an objective state of fitness, regardless of whether any claim is made.
  • Befitteth: Similar because both involve harmony between person and treatment; differs because befitteth emphasizes decorum, aesthetic suitability, and customary propriety (e.g., ‘conduct that befitteth a king’), while deserveth focuses on moral justice and earned desert.
  • Incurreth: Similar because both involve bringing consequences upon oneself; differs because incurreth applies almost exclusively to negative outcomes (liabilities, debts, wrath, penalties) through exposure, whereas deserveth applies equally to noble glory and severe censure.
