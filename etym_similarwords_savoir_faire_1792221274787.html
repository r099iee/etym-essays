• Tact: Similar because both involve avoiding social friction and displaying sensitivity to others' feelings; differs because tact is focused specifically on considerate speech and avoiding causing offense, whereas savoir-faire is a broader, more worldly mastery of general conduct, poise, and practical action.
  • Finesse: Similar because both denote subtle, refined skill in handling complex or delicate situations; differs because finesse emphasizes delicate manipulation, technical artistry, or strategic craft, while savoir-faire emphasizes innate social competence and unflappable self-assurance.
  • Poise: Similar because both convey calm dignity and composed self-presentation in social settings; differs because poise is primarily an internal state of balance, grace, and physical or mental composure, whereas savoir-faire is active and transactional, requiring knowing how to navigate relationships and execute deeds.
  • Adroitness: Similar because both indicate dexterity, quickness, and skill in dealing with difficult problems; differs because adroitness carries a stronger mechanical, physical, or tactical connotation of mental nimbleness, whereas savoir-faire is inherently linked to social sophistication and cosmopolitan manners.
  • Worldliness: Similar because both reflect extensive experience with human nature, travel, and social conventions; differs because worldliness can sometimes carry negative overtones of cynicism, materialism, or loss of innocence, while savoir-faire remains an admiring designation for practical social brilliance.
  • Savvy: Similar because both denote practical understanding, shrewdness, and informal know-how; differs because savvy belongs to an informal, street-level or commercial register ("tech-savvy", "business-savvy"), whereas savoir-faire maintains a distinctly sophisticated, continental, and elevated register.
