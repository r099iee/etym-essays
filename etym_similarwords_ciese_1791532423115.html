• Cheese: The direct modern descendant of the same word; differs because it is the standard modern spelling and pronunciation, while ciese is an Old English Anglian form with a pronounced final vowel.
  • Casein: The milk protein that forms the basis of cheese; differs because it is a technical scientific term for a substance, not the food itself, though both derive from Latin cāseus.
  • Curd: The solid part of coagulated milk; differs because curd is an intermediate product in cheesemaking, not the finished, aged food.
  • Whey: The watery liquid left after curds form; differs because it is the by-product, not the cheese, and is almost the opposite part of the process.
  • Dairy: Milk and milk products collectively; differs because it is a category term covering butter, cream, yoghurt and cheese, not a specific food.
  • Fromage: The French word for cheese; differs because it is a foreign borrowing used in English for French cheeses and menus, not the native term.
  • Käse: The German cognate of the same Latin loan; differs because it is German, showing the shared origin of the word across the Germanic languages.
  • Queso: The Spanish descendant of Latin cāseus; differs because it is Spanish and often names Mexican or Spanish cheeses in English usage.
  • Cheddar: A specific hard English cheese; differs because it is a hyponym, one variety rather than the general word.
  • Cheesy: The adjective meaning cheap or sentimental; differs because it is a derived evaluative term, not the food, and has drifted far from the literal sense.
