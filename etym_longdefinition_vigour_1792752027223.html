vigour (noun; American English spelling: vigor) — Active, robust physical strength, blooming bodily health, and high biological vitality; energetic forcefulness of mind, intellect, or artistic expression; and the operational efficacy, validity, or enforcement strength of legal, statutory, or institutional systems. The word encapsulates a profound state of flourishing wherein internal energy is abundantly generated, actively sustained, and forcefully manifested in outward action.

Primary Lexical Senses and Specialized Registers:

  • Physiological Vitality and Physical Robustness: The foundational sense of the word refers to an organism's bodily health, stamina, and muscular power. Unlike mere absence of sickness, vigour denotes exuberant health—the spring in an individual's step, the firmness of their flesh, and the capacity to undertake strenuous manual labor or withstand environmental rigors without exhaustion. In gerontology and longevity studies, "prolonged vigour" describes individuals who retain their faculties, mobility, and energetic zest well into advanced old age.
  • Intellectual and Creative Forcefulness: When applied to the mind, philosophy, or artistic creation, vigour characterizes sharp, trenchant, and lucid thinking. A vigorous debate, a treatise argued with intellectual vigour, or a painting executed with artistic vigour possesses vivid boldness, clarity of purpose, and relentless logical or aesthetic drive, standing in sharp contrast to feeble, hesitant, or derivative works.
  • Biological and Agricultural Science (Heterosis / Hybrid Vigour): In genetics, botany, and animal breeding, the technical term hybrid vigour (synonymous with heterosis) describes the biological phenomenon whereby the crossbred offspring of two genetically distinct inbred parental lines exhibits superior traits—such as faster growth rates, larger biomass, higher disease resistance, and greater grain yield—compared to either parent. Seed vigour similarly measures the biochemical potential of seeds to germinate rapidly and uniformly across a wide range of suboptimal soil conditions.
  • Legal, Juridical, and Institutional Validity: In constitutional law, jurisprudence, and treaty interpretation, the phrase "in full vigour" signifies that a statute, decree, treaty, or executive order remains legally active, operative, and enforceable with full binding legal effect. A law that has not lapsed through desuetude or legislative repeal retains its statutory vigour.
  • Administrative and Political Determination: In civic governance and law enforcement, the noun denotes resolute determination, uncompromising urgency, and thoroughness in executing policy or investigating wrongdoing. A prosecutor who pursues a public corruption inquiry "with unflagging vigour" acts with speed, thoroughness, and uncompromising rigor.

Semantic Bridge and Cognitive Imagery:

The etymological core of vigour is the Proto-Indo-European verbal root *weǵ-, signifying "to be lively, awake, fresh, and alert." The conceptual trajectory moves from the awakeness and freshness of the senses to the blossoming, thriving growth of living plants, and subsequently to the muscular and constitutional strength of animals and human beings. From this physical baseline of bodily vitality, the mind naturally abstracts the concept into intangible domains: a mind that is wide awake and active possesses intellectual vigour; a law that is awake, watchful, and enforced possesses legal vigour; and a hybrid plant that springs from the soil with exceptional speed displays hybrid vigour.

In J.R.R. Tolkien's The Fellowship of the Ring, from which the card's context sentence originates ("And if that was not enough for fame, there was also his prolonged vigour to marvel at"), the word is applied to Bilbo Baggins as he approaches his eleventy-first (111th) birthday. To the Shire hobbits, Bilbo's refusal to look old or decrepit—appearing essentially as an agile, sprightly hobbit of fifty—was an unnatural mystery. Tolkien's use of "prolonged vigour" carries a subtle dual resonance: on the surface, it reflects the envious admiration of rustic neighbors at Bilbo's youthful longevity; beneath the surface, it is the sinister, unnatural consequence of possessing the One Ring, which preserves physical tissue by stretching it thin without granting true biological rejuvenation.
