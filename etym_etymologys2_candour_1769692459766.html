Linear derivation chain (real attested forms with dates; 6 stages): 1. PIE root *kand- (also *kend-) "to shine, glow, be white-hot" (reconstructed, c. 4000-2500 BCE; Sanskrit candra "shining, moon"). 2. Latin candere "to shine, glow, be dazzling white, be white-hot" (attested Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE). 3. Latin candor "brilliant whiteness, radiance; purity, freedom from malice" (attested Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE-1st c. CE; Cicero). 4. Old French candeur "whiteness, purity, openness" (attested c. 12th-13th c.). 5. Anglo-Norman / Middle English candour "purity, openness, fairness of judgement" (attested 14th-16th c.; in English spellings candour / candor). 6. Modern English CANDOUR "frankness, openness, generous fairness of judgement" (16th c. onwards; the classic generous sense in Jane Austen, Pride and Prejudice, 1813; the modern blunt-honesty sense develops from the 19th c.). Note: the British spelling candour reflects French / Anglo-Norman -our; the US spelling candor restores the Latin -or. The semantic shift from "whiteness" to "honesty" is documented within Latin itself (candor "whiteness" then "impartiality, sincerity"). The PIE root is reconstructed; the Latin and later stages are fully attested.
