• friendly: Similar because both denote goodwill and an absence of hostility; differs because friendly can express real warmth and affection between people, while amicable is cooler, more formal, and typically describes arrangements rather than feelings.
  • amiable: Similar because both descend from Latin amicus and concern pleasantness; differs because amiable describes a person's likeable disposition, whereas amicable describes relations, agreements, or outcomes free of conflict.
  • cordial: Similar because both suggest courteous goodwill; differs because cordial implies warmth and sometimes effusiveness of manner, whereas amicable implies cooperative peacefulness without emotional display.
  • harmonious: Similar because both describe conflict-free relations; differs because harmonious stresses agreement and pleasing compatibility, while amicable merely stresses the absence of hostility and the presence of goodwill.
  • peaceable: Similar because both concern the avoidance of strife; differs because peaceable often describes a disposition or a person who avoids violence, while amicable is relational and transactional.
  • conciliatory: Similar because both aim at ending or preventing conflict; differs because conciliatory describes an action intended to placate an opponent, while amicable describes the settled, non-hostile quality of the relationship itself.
  • civil: Similar because both describe polite, non-aggressive conduct; differs because civil is the bare minimum of courtesy and can be cold or grudging, whereas amicable implies genuine goodwill.
  • neighbourly: Similar because both suggest helpful friendliness between parties who must coexist; differs because neighbourly is warmer and more domestic, evoking local community rather than legal or diplomatic settlement.
  • non-adversarial: Similar because both describe proceedings conducted without combat; differs because non-adversarial is a technical, procedural label and says nothing about goodwill, while amicable positively asserts friendliness.
