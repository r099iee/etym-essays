English Legal & Common Usage (16th–19th c.)
bona fides

Etymological Note: Directly borrowed from Latin bona fidēs (nominative singular: "good faith", from the feminine adjective bona + fifth-declension noun fidēs). In legal usage, the ablative phrase bonā fidē ("in good faith", with macrons over the terminal vowels) is used adverbially/adjectivally (e.g. bona fide), while bona fides functions in English as the substantive noun for credentials or good faith.
