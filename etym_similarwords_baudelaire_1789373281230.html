• Baudelairean: Similar because both refer directly to the poetics, persona, and themes of Charles Baudelaire; differs because Baudelaire is the proper noun denoting the historical man himself, whereas Baudelairean is the derived relational adjective describing artistic moods, styles, or urban atmospheres characterized by macabre elegance, decadent beauty, and existential spleen.
  • Rimbaud: Similar because both are iconic 19th-century French poetic revolutionaries who shattered bourgeois literary conventions and pioneered Symbolist techniques; differs because Baudelaire maintained strict classical metrics, formal verse discipline, and a mature, brooding dandyism, whereas Arthur Rimbaud was a fiery, adolescent visionary who radically disrupted syntax, invented free verse and prose poetry, and abandoned literature entirely at age twenty.
  • Mallarmé: Similar because both are foundational pillars of French Symbolist aesthetics who believed poetry should evoke ideal metaphysical realities; differs because Stéphane Mallarmé pursued extreme hermetic obscurity, typographical experimentation, and abstract linguistic purity that dissolved physical reality, whereas Baudelaire remained intensely anchored in the visceral, physical squalor and olfactory sensations of urban life.
  • Poe: Similar because both masters explored the dark recesses of human psychology, morbid obsession, gothic decay, and the absolute primacy of aesthetic beauty over moral didacticism; differs because Edgar Allan Poe worked primarily in the American Gothic short story and critical essay, whereas Baudelaire was primarily a lyric poet of European urban modernity who translated and popularized Poe throughout Continental Europe.
  • Decadent: Similar because both embrace themes of decay, morbid sensory stimulation, artificiality, and world-weary aesthetic refinement; differs because Decadent is a broad cultural and literary movement that flourished across late 19th-century Europe (Huysmans, Wilde), whereas Baudelaire is the singular pioneer whose intense spiritual torment and classical rigor transcended mere decorative decadence.
  • Symbolist: Similar because both reject literal realism and didacticism in favor of suggestive, musical, and metaphysical imagery; differs because Symbolist denotes an organized historical school of poets and painters who followed in Baudelaire's wake, whereas Baudelaire himself remained an unclassifiable solitary figure straddling late Romanticism, Parnassian formalism, and nascent modernism.
  • Dandy: Similar because both cultivate uncompromising elegance, aristocratic emotional detachment, and disdain for vulgar commercial society; differs because a dandy is a social archetype often focused primarily on sartorial fashion and haughty social manners, whereas Baudelaire transformed dandyism into a rigorous, tragic spiritual discipline of artistic resistance.
