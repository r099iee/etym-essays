15th-Century Legal-Historical Import - Depredation enters English in the 15th century from Middle French, from Latin depraedatio ("a plundering"), a formal, learned word for the act of seizing spoil and laying waste in war.
Early Modern Chronicle Register - Through the 16th-18th centuries depredation keeps its legal, historical, and ecclesiastical seriousness — the depredations of armies, pirates, and bandits are matters of record, catalogued in the plural.
19th-Century Natural Extension - The word extends from human marauders to nature: the "depredations of insects," of weather, of dragons — any systematic destructive predatory activity, keeping its formal tone.
Contemporary Sense - Today depredation (often plural) is the grave, almost legal-historical word for plundering and laying waste, used for chronic ruin rather than petty theft, and for predatory damage in natural history.
