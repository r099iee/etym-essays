• Pardon: Similar because both mean formal release from punishment or offense; differs because pardon is an executive or political act commuting or remitting a legal penalty (a presidential pardon), while absolution is sacramental or spiritual cleansing, concerned with sin and guilt.
  • Acquittal: Similar because both mean formal legal release; differs because acquittal is a jury's verdict that a defendant is not guilty, wiping away the charge, whereas absolution assumes guilt existed and has been washed away or released.
  • Forgiveness: Similar because both involve letting go of offense; differs because forgiveness is a personal, emotional state of releasing resentment, which can be private and informal, while absolution is a formal ritual or legal act of release performed by an authorized person.
  • Remission: Similar because both mean the cancellation or release of a penalty, debt, or sin; differs because remission emphasizes the cancellation or reduction itself (remission of sins, remission of a fine, remission of a disease's symptoms), while absolution emphasizes the act of releasing the person.
  • Amnesty: Similar because both grant freedom from punishment; differs because amnesty is a collective, state-level pardon for a class of offences (amnesty for draft dodgers), while absolution is individual and concerns sin or a single guilt.
  • Exoneration: Similar because both free a person from blame; differs because exoneration clears a person of blame or responsibility by proving they were not at fault, while absolution releases from guilt that may be fully deserved.
  • Redemption: Similar because both concern release from sin; differs because redemption is the act of being bought back or saved - the whole work of salvation - while absolution is the specific declaration of forgiveness within that larger drama.
