• Plump: Similar because both describe a heavy, blunt fall onto a surface; differs because plump implies a heavier, bulkier, and more resonant impact with greater bodily momentum, while plop emphasizes softness, damping, or liquid entry.
  • Splash: Similar because both involve objects entering a body of liquid; differs because splash highlights the dramatic, noisy scattering of droplets in all directions, whereas plop focuses on the single muffled sound of entry with minimal spray.
  • Drop: Similar because both denote downward motion under gravity; differs because drop is a generic, neutral verb of descent without specific auditory or kinetic character, whereas plop specifically evokes an unceremonious, muffled landing.
  • Thud: Similar because both convey a dull impact sound; differs because a thud is a heavy, solid impact against a hard, unyielding surface, while plop is soft, wet, or cushioned.
  • Flop: Similar because both describe an ungraceful, relaxed physical collapse; differs because flop emphasizes loose, sprawling, awkward bodily motion (e.g., flopping on a bed), while plop emphasizes the sudden deadweight descent and its acoustic sound.
  • Slump: Similar because both depict a collapse under tiredness or weakness; differs because slump describes a gradual, sagging, or progressive loss of posture rather than a sudden downward fall.
