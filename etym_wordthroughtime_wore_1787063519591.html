- Old English (c. 700–1100) - Clothing & Harboring: Originated as the weak verb *werian* meaning to clothe oneself, put on armor, or harbor/defend.
- Middle English (c. 1200–1450) - Transition to Strong Conjugation: Under the analogical influence of verbs like *bear/bore/born* and *tear/tore/torn*, the weak past tense *werede* shifted to the strong ablaut past tense *wore*.
- 16th to 17th Century - Frictional Erosion & Temporal Extension: Semantic broadening occurred to include physical friction wearing down objects, and time "wearing on" or "wearing to night" like cloth rubbing against stone.
- 18th to 19th Century - Nautical Jargon & Psychological Characterization: Adopted into naval terminology for tacking ships downwind, and deployed in Victorian literature for displaying subtle emotional countenances and enduring grueling fatigue.
- 20th to 21st Century - Universal Idiomatic Currency: Maintained across colloquial and literary English for fashion, fatigue, material durability, and the inexorable march of hours.
