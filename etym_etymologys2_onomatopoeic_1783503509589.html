Derivation chain for ONOMATOPOEIC (two root words; chains given separately). Chain for ONOMA (name): 1. Proto-Indo-European *nō-mn- "name" (reconstructed; c. 4th millennium BCE; cognates Latin nōmen, English name, Sanskrit nā́man). 2. Ancient Greek ónoma "name" (Attic; ónyma in Aeolic/Doric, which supplies the combining form -onym). 3. Greek onomato- "name-" — the combining stem of ónoma used in compound words. 4. Greek onomatopoiía (ὀνοματοποιΐα) "the making of names; word-formation by sound imitation" — onomato- + poieîn "to make" (attested from classical rhetoric, 4th c. BCE). 5. Latin onomatopoeia "onomatopoeia" — learned borrowing from Greek (attested in late Latin rhetorical writers). 6. English onomatopoeic — formed in the 19th c. on Greek onomatopoiía with the English adjectival suffix -ic (variant spellings onomatopoetic, onomatopœic). 7. English onomatopoeic — modern standard form. Chain for POIEIN (make): 1. Proto-Indo-European *kʷey- "to heap up, pile up, build, make" (reconstructed root per Beekes; c. 4th millennium BCE; also given as *kʷei- or *kʷeyh₁-, with the exact laryngeal debated). 2. Ancient Greek poieîn "to make, do, compose" (attested from Homer, 8th c. BCE); the agent noun poiētḗs "maker, poet" is the source of English poet (via Latin poēta). 3. Greek -poiíā / -poia "a making" — the nominal combining form of poieîn, as in onomatopoiía "name-making" (4th c. BCE). 4. Latin onomatopoeia "onomatopoeia" — borrowing of the Greek compound (late Latin). 5. English onomatopoeic — 19th-century English adjective on the Greek compound + -ic. Note: The first element *nō-mn- "name" is among the most securely reconstructed PIE roots. The second element, the root of poieîn, is standardly reconstructed as *kʷey- "to heap up, make" (Beekes), though older handbooks vary in the laryngeal detail; the Greek verb poieîn and its compounds are, in contrast, directly attested.
