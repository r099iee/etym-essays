• Chores: Similar because both are small routine duties of household life; differs because chores are jobs done mostly at home (washing, tidying), while errands require going out on short trips.
  • Tasks: Similar because both are pieces of work to be completed; differs because tasks is the neutral general word for any job, while errands specifically involve travel to fetch, deliver, or arrange something.
  • Missions: Similar because an errand was once literally a mission entrusted to a messenger; differs because missions today sound grand, solemn, or military, while errands are humble and everyday.
  • Commissions: Similar because both can mean a task entrusted to someone by another; differs because a commission is formal and often paid or artistic, while an errand is informal and menial.
  • Assignments: Similar because both are jobs given to someone to complete; differs because assignments belong to school, work, or journalism, while errands belong to household running about.
  • Jobs: Similar because both name pieces of work; differs because jobs covers employment itself and any odd job, while errands names only the short trip-and-task pattern.
  • Duties: Similar because both imply obligation to get something done; differs because duties stress moral or role-based obligation, while errands stress the actual going-and-fetching.
  • Messages (archaic sense): Similar because the oldest errands literally were messages carried for another; differs because messages today means the communication itself, while errands means the journey and task.
