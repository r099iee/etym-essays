• stranded: Similar because both denote helpless immobility; differs because stranded stresses being left ashore or without resources, becalmed specifically windless at sea.
  • marooned: Similar because abandoned motionless; differs because marooned implies deliberately abandoned on island/coast, becalmed environmentally stalled.
  • stalled: Similar because progress halted; differs because stalled is general mechanical or procedural, becalmed nautical and atmospheric.
  • stagnant: Similar because lacking flow or advance; differs because stagnant suggests foul stillness of water or ideas, becalmed temporary windlessness.
  • motionless: Similar because without movement; differs because motionless neutral description, becalmed explains cause (no wind).
  • immobilized: Similar because rendered unable to move; differs because immobilized often physical restraint or injury, becalmed lack of impetus.
  • stuck: Similar because unable to proceed; differs because stuck colloquial and general, becalmed literary-nautical.
  • adrift: Similar because helpless at sea; differs because adrift implies drifting without control, becalmed implies not even drifting, glassy stillness.
  • static: Similar because unchanging state; differs because static technical-neutral, becalmed evocative-frustrated.
  • paralyzed: Similar because unable to act; differs because paralyzed suggests fear or neurological failure, becalmed external deprivation of force.
  • delayed: Similar because held back; differs because delayed temporary lateness, becalmed indefinite helpless waiting.
  • arrested: Similar because development halted, as in arrested development; differs because arrested formal/medical, becalmed metaphorical-nautical.
  • calmed: Similar because made calm; differs because calmed is positively soothed, becalmed negatively deprived of wind.
