- [Vedic & Epic Era (c. 1000 BCE–300 CE)] - established in Vedic texts and the epics (Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa) as the southern boundary of Āryāvarta, renowned for its perilous forests (Vindhyāṭavī), wild elephant herds, and the myth of Sage Agastya humbling the proud mountain - [perception: viewed with reverence and awe as a formidable geographic barrier and the wild frontier of civilization]
- [Classical Sanskrit Period (c. 4th–8th c. CE)] - celebrated in courtly poetry and drama (Kālidāsa's Meghadūta and Raghuvaṃśa, Bāṇabhaṭṭa's Kādambarī) as an exotic, romantic wilderness filled with fragrant trees, wild elephants, and indigenous forest dwellers - [perception: idealized in high literature as a realm of untamed natural beauty, ascetic retreat, and royal hunting grounds]
- [Medieval Shakta Era (c. 8th–16th c. CE)] - consolidated as one of India's premier sacred landscapes through the worship of the goddess Vindhyavāsinī at the shrine of Vindhyachal, attracting pilgrims across the subcontinent - [perception: revered as the sacred mountain abode and cosmic bastion of the Great Mother Goddess]
- [Colonial British Era (18th–20th c.)] - surveyed systematically by the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India and mapped by geologists as the ancient Proterozoic "Vindhyan Supergroup" - [perception: transformed into an object of imperial topography, geological classification, and timber resource management]
- [21st c. Modern Era] - celebrated in the Indian National Anthem as an eternal geographic axis of the nation, housing world-heritage rock-art sites (Bhimbetka) and critical tiger reserves (Bandhavgarh, Panna) - [perception: cherished as a premier sanctuary of prehistoric human heritage, biodiversity, and national identity]
