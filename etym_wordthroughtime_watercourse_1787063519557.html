- [c. 1430–1550: Late Middle English 'watercours'] - Coined by combining Germanic 'water' and Anglo-Norman 'course' to describe channels for flowing water in agrarian and milling records.
- [c. 1600–1750: Early Modern English & Common Law] - Formally codified in English riparian and property law to define legal rights-of-way for flowing water.
- [c. 1800–1900: 19th Century Geomorphology] - Adopted into earth sciences and physical geography to classify both active streams and dry erosional riverbeds (wadis/arroyos).
- [c. 1900–Present: Modern English] - Standard statutory term in environmental regulation, civil drainage engineering, and landscape description.
