- 1850s to 1860s – Intrepid field explorer & co-originator of natural selection - Achieved international scientific renown following the historic joint presentation with Darwin at the Linnean Society in 1858 and his groundbreaking expeditions in the Amazon and Malay Archipelago.
- 1870s to 1890s – Father of evolutionary biogeography & controversial public intellectual - Celebrated worldwide for *The Malay Archipelago* (1869) and *The Geographical Distribution of Animals* (1876); aroused skepticism among Victorian peers for his ardent advocacy of spiritualism, socialism, and land nationalization.
- Early to Mid-20th c. – Eclipsed co-discoverer in Darwin's shadow - Often treated as a historical footnote—the unheralded correspondent whose letter spurred Darwin to finish *Origin of Species*—though he remained revered by field biologists and taxonomists.
- Late 20th c. to Present – Rediscovered ecological pioneer & humanitarian visionary - Re-evaluated as an independent intellectual titan: co-founder of modern evolutionary biology, pioneer of island biogeography, early defender of indigenous equality, and prophetic champion of global environmental conservation.
