• Tevere: the Italian name of the same river; identical in reference, but Italian in register and used in modern maps, travel writing and hydrology rather than in English literary or religious idiom.
  • Tiberis: the Latin name; similar as the historical designation, but it belongs to classical texts and inscriptions and carries the river's pagan and imperial associations more strongly than the English form.
  • Tiberine: the adjective meaning "of the Tiber"; similar in referring to the same river, but it is a derivative describing things and people rather than a name for the river itself.
  • Rubicon: a small Italian river famous as a boundary and a metaphor for an irreversible decision; similar as a Roman river charged with symbolic meaning, but its force is about crossing a point of no return, whereas the Tiber's is about Rome, the papacy and religious allegiance.
  • Po: Italy's longest river, flowing east through the northern plain; similar as a major Italian waterway, but it is associated with the north and with agriculture and industry, not with Rome.
  • Arno: the river of Florence and Pisa; similar as a Tuscan river of art-historical fame, but it belongs to Dante's and the Renaissance city rather than to the imperial and papal capital.
  • Aniene: the Tiber's chief tributary at Rome; similar because it joins the Tiber within the city, but it is a smaller local river and lacks the symbolic load of the main stream.
  • Danube: the great river of central and eastern Europe; similar as a Roman frontier river, but it marked the empire's northern limit rather than its sacred centre.
  • Thames: the river of London; similar as a capital river used metonymically for the city and its institutions, but its associations are parliamentary and commercial rather than papal.
  • Vatican: the papal state and its institutions; similar because "crossing the Tiber" is really about crossing to the Vatican, but the Vatican names the institution, while the Tiber names the physical and symbolic boundary.
  • Rome: the city on the Tiber; similar as a metonym for the same cultural and religious complex, but it is the city, not the river, and lacks the specific image of crossing or boundary.
  • Tiberias: a city on the Sea of Galilee named after the emperor Tiberius; similar in sound and in imperial origin, but it is a different place entirely and is not connected to the river Tiber.
