• ruffles: similar because both are gathered fabric edgings sewn to garments; differs because a ruffle is a broader, looser gathered flounce with visible open waves, while a frill is a tighter, stiffer strip of pleats or crimps that stands out in a firm edge; ‘ruffle’ also means to disturb order (ruffled feathers), a sense frills lacks.
  • flounces: similar because both are decorative bands of fabric attached at one edge; differs because a flounce is wide and dramatic, typically at a hem, and drapes loosely, whereas frills are narrow, repetitive, and can trim collars and cuffs.
  • furbelows: similar because ‘frills and furbelows’ pairs them as synonyms for showy trimming; differs because furbelow (from Spanish/Italian ‘flounce’, with a folk-etymological ‘fur’) now sounds archaic or ironic and denotes any fussy flounced material, while frills remains live in the ‘needless extra’ idiom.
  • laces: similar because ‘laces and frills’ treats them as twin ornaments of civilization; differs because laces are cords, braids, or openwork thread-web, while frills are gathered fabric edges; in the modern commercial idiom only frills (no-frills) became the name for dispensable extras.
  • pleats / tucks: similar because both are ornamental manipulations of cloth creating parallel folds; differs because pleats and tucks are structural folds pressed or stitched into the garment itself, while a frill is an attached strip standing out from it.
  • trimmings: similar because both are decorative additions sewn on; differs because ‘trimmings’ is the generic word for any edging or garnish (fur, braid, ribbon), whereas frills are a specific gathered-edging type, and ‘trimmings’ has also settled into the roasted-turkey feast (‘turkey and all the trimmings’).
  • embellishments: similar because both name ornaments that can be detached from the essential; differs because embellishment is abstract and medium-neutral — one embellishes a story or a room — while frills keeps its fabric pedigree and lightly pejorative tone.
  • flourish: similar because both mean a decorative, non-essential extra; differs because a flourish is a sweeping gesture of style (a signature flourish, fanfare), whereas frills are tangible material or procedural extras; flourish is always positive, frills is usually dismissive.
  • extras / amenities / perks: similar because in the ‘no-frills’ commercial register all name non-essential additions bundled with a product or service; differs because amenities and perks are neutral or positive benefits (hotel amenities, job perks), while frills brands them as unnecessary dispensables to be cut.
  • garnish: similar because both are trimmings making something more attractive without changing its substance; differs because garnish is culinary and fleeting, frills are durable textiles or permanent extras; nobody says a ‘no-garnish airline’.
  • bells and whistles: similar because the idiom names superfluous attractive features beyond the basic function; differs in register and metaphor — bells and whistles is informal, functional-technical (devices, software, cars), while frills is general and mildly old-fashioned.
  • finery: similar because both name ornamental dress; differs because finery is the whole brilliant outfit (jewels, silks), appreciative in tone, while frills are small attachable ornaments and, figuratively, trivial extras.
  • fancies / niceties: similar because both denote refinements beyond necessity; differs because niceties are delicate social graces or fine distinctions (points of etiquette), whereas frills are material or superfluous ornament; niceties can be intellectual, frills cannot.
