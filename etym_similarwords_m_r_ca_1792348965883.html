  • Rāvaṇa: Similar because both are formidable demonic figures in the Rāmāyaṇa who scheme against Prince Rāma and cause the crisis of Sītā's abduction; differs because Rāvaṇa is the arrogant, all-powerful ten-headed King of Laṅkā driven by hubris and lust, whereas Mārīca is his reluctant subordinate who desperately attempts to dissuade Rāvaṇa from confronting Rāma before being coerced under threat of immediate death.
  • Tāṭakā: Similar because she is the biological mother and monstrous progenitor of Mārīca, sharing his dark sorcery and blood-soaked war against Vedic ashrams; differs because Tāṭakā was a ferocious Yakṣiṇī turned cannibalistic demoness who met her death at Rāma's hands in the early forest encounters, whereas Mārīca lived to orchestrate the grand illusion of the golden deer.
  • Vibhīṣaṇa: Similar because both are rākṣasas who recognize Rāma's supreme righteousness and plead with Rāvaṇa to abandon his disastrous path of conflict; differs because Vibhīṣaṇa successfully defects to Rāma's camp and is crowned King of Laṅkā, whereas Mārīca remains trapped under Rāvaṇa's tyranny and chooses death by Rāma's arrow.
  • Proteus: Similar because both are ancient mythological figures possessing the extraordinary magical ability to alter their physical shape at will to deceive or evade onlookers; differs because Proteus is a classical Greek sea-god of prophecy who shifts shapes to avoid revealing the future, while Mārīca is a Hindu rākṣasa who transforms into an exquisite animal to carry out a lethal decoy mission.
  • Loki: Similar because both are cunning, shapeshifting trickster figures whose magical metamorphoses precipitate catastrophic cosmic conflicts in their respective mythologies; differs because Loki is a Norse deity driven by cynical malice, chaotic envy, and personal trickery, whereas Mārīca acts out of terror and existential resignation under tyrannical coercion.
  • Mephistopheles: Similar because both employ dazzling, alluring illusions to tempt and entrap noble mortals; differs because Mephistopheles is a Christian demonic spirit who bargains for Faust's soul through intellectual and worldly pleasures, whereas Mārīca assumes the form of a physical golden deer to visually enchant Sītā.
  • Kabandha: Similar because both are cursed demonic entities in the Rāmāyaṇa who encounter Rāma and are liberated from their monstrous existences upon their deaths; differs because Kabandha was a celestial Gandharva cursed into a headless monster who advises Rāma to ally with Sugrīva, while Mārīca was born a demon and served as Rāvaṇa's reluctant accomplice.
