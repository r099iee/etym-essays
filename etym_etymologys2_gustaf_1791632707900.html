PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*gʰew- / *gʰewd- + *stebʰ-
"to pour, libate" (tribal Geatas) + "post, pillar, staff"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*Gautaz + *stabaz
"a Geat / Goth" + "staff, support, pillar"
↓
Old Norse (c. 9th–10th c.)
Gautstafr
"staff or pillar of the Geats"
↓
Old Swedish (c. 13th–14th c.)
Gøtstaf
"masculine personal name"
↓
Early Modern Swedish / German (16th c.)
Gustaf / Gustav
"royal name; pillar of the Goths"
↓
English / International (modern)
Gustaf
"masculine proper name; Swedish royal name; theorist Gustaf Britsch"

Note: While the standard Germanic etymology derives the name from *Gautstafr* ("staff of the Geats"), an alternative 19th-century Slavic hypothesis suggested borrowing from *Gostislav* ("glorious guest"), though Scandinavian runic and historical records overwhelmingly support the North Germanic *Gaut-* ("Geat") + *-stafr* ("staff/pillar") compound.
