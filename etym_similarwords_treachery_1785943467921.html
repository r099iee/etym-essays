• betrayal: Similar because both denote the breaking of trust or faith; differs because betrayal is the general term for any violation of confidence, while treachery specifically implies covert plotting and a feigned show of loyalty.
  • treason: Similar because both describe betrayal of allegiance; differs because treason is a precise legal crime against a sovereign or state, whereas treachery is a broader moral concept applicable to friends and allies as well.
  • perfidy: Similar because both denote deliberate breach of a solemn pledge or oath; differs because perfidy is a formal, often diplomatic or military term for oath-breaking, while treachery covers all covert betrayal and carries a more visceral moral condemnation.
  • duplicity: Similar because both involve deception and double-dealing; differs because duplicity names the habit of playing two sides or dissembling, while treachery names the destructive act of betrayal that may follow from it.
  • disloyalty: Similar because both denote failure to remain faithful; differs because disloyalty is a broad, often passive failure of allegiance, while treachery is an active, planned, and harmful act.
  • faithlessness: Similar because both denote breaking faith or trust; differs because faithlessness is a general, often colorless word for lack of fidelity, while treachery emphasizes cunning and moral enormity.
  • backstabbing: Similar because both describe covert harm done to someone who trusted you; differs because backstabbing is informal and refers to career or social settings, while treachery is a grand, historic word for betrayal of oaths and nations.
  • double-dealing: Similar because both involve deceiving someone while pretending to act in their interest; differs because double-dealing stresses the dishonest conduct itself, whereas treachery stresses the violated relationship and its grave consequences.
  • insurrection / sedition: Similar because both threaten established authority; differs because insurrection and sedition are acts against the state, while treachery may be purely personal and need not involve government at all.
  • deceit: Similar because both involve misleading others; differs because deceit is any kind of fraud or misrepresentation, while treachery is specifically the deceit of a trusted ally that results in betrayal.
