• bubbling: Similar in the liquid, repetitive noise of air churning through water; differs because bubbling is neutral or merry and lacks the bodily, throaty, potentially repulsive charge of gurgling.
  • burble: Similar in a gentle, bubbling, watery sound; differs because burbling is pleasantly musical and mild — the opposite pole of feeling — with none of the choked, viscous horror of gurgling.
  • gargling: Similar in a liquid, throaty noise; differs because gargling is a deliberate, controlled rinsing of the throat, whereas gurgling is an involuntary bubbling of choked weeping or water.
  • glugging: Similar in a liquid sound of irregular flow; differs because glugging is the heavier, slower sound of a viscous liquid glugging from a bottle or vessel, without the fine-bubbled, continuous quality of gurgling.
  • croaking: Similar in a throaty sound; differs because croaking is dry, harsh, and rasping, while gurgling is wet and deep, with liquid lodged in the throat.
  • gulping: Similar in a throaty, liquid sound; differs because gulping is the noise of swallowing, a single convulsive action, not a continuous bubbling of weeping or water.
  • purling: Similar in a gentle, murmuring flow of water; differs because purling is soft, clear, and musical, describing a smooth stream, with none of gurgling’s wet, throaty, or disturbing undertones.
  • rippling: Similar in a light, watery motion and sound; differs because rippling is a smooth, surface movement of waves or ridges, not the breaking of bubbles in a throat or a narrow channel.
  • chuckling: Similar in a low, bubbling sound; differs because a chuckle is a quiet laugh of a person, purely human and amused, whereas gurgling is a physical sound of liquid or of the throat in distress or joy.
  • gushing: Similar in a strong flow of liquid; differs because gushing is a forceful, copious outpouring, while gurgling is a broken, bubbling emission.
  • cooing: Similar in a soft, tender, throaty sound of contentment; differs because cooing is a smooth, dove-like murmur with no wetness or bubbling, lacking gurgling’s liquid physics.
  • wheezing: Similar in a breathy, abnormal breathing sound; differs because wheezing is a dry, whistling obstruction of the airway, where gurgling is wet and bubbling with liquid.
