• art prose: Similar because it is the literal English translation; differs because it is a calque without institutional prestige, while Kunstprosa invokes a German scholarly tradition.
  • literary prose: Similar because it denotes non-verse writing with aesthetic ambition; differs because it is broad and neutral, while Kunstprosa stresses deliberate craftsmanship and rhythm.
  • poetic prose: Similar because it means prose with lyrical density; differs because it suggests verse-like effusion, while Kunstprosa includes sober periodic argument as well as lyricism.
  • belles lettres: Similar as an older prestige category for fine writing; differs because it is French, genteel and now archaic, while Kunstprosa is German, philological and still technical.
  • purple prose: Similar because it denotes highly ornamented prose; differs because it is always pejorative for excess, while Kunstprosa is normally honorific.
  • fine writing: Similar in schoolroom praise; differs because it is vague and moralistic, while Kunstprosa implies analyzable technique (clausulae, isocolon, hypotaxis).
  • rhetorical prose: Similar because it stresses persuasive figures; differs because rhetoric suggests oratory and argument, while Kunstprosa includes narrative and essayistic beauty.
  • Gebrauchsprosa: Similar as its German antonym pair; differs because it means utility prose for information, while Kunstprosa means art prose — the contrast itself is the point.
  • Prosakunst: Similar because it is the reversed compound with nearly identical meaning; differs only in emphasis — Prosakunst stresses art made out of prose, Kunstprosa stresses prose raised to art.
  • essay: Similar because the essay is the home genre of Kunstprosa; differs because essay denotes a genre, while Kunstprosa denotes a stylistic quality across genres.
  • euphuism / Ciceronianism: Similar as historical styles of wrought prose; differs because they name period manners, while Kunstprosa names the general category.
