- 1769 to Mid-19th Century: Coined by William Cullen to denote physical, non-febrile disorders of the bodily nervous system without lesions.
- 1890s–1950s: Radically redefined by Freud and psychoanalysis as the unconscious psychological conflict between instinctual desire and cultural repression.
- 1980s: Removed from formal psychiatric diagnostic manuals (DSM-III) in favor of descriptive symptom-based anxiety and mood disorders.
- Late 20th–21st Century: Persists in personality psychology (as the trait of neuroticism) and thrives in cultural and literary criticism as the hallmark of anxious, authentic subjectivity.
