• sat: Similar because it is the same verb in the same tense — satst is literally "thou sat"; differs because sat is the modern person-neutral form used for all persons, while satst is the archaic second-person singular with the -st ending and a reverential or archaic register.
  • satest: Similar because it is a variant of the same second-person singular past form ("thou satest"); differs because satest preserves the fuller syllabic ending -est (common in verse for metrical ease), while satst is the contracted, non-syllabic form preferred in prose and in the Authorized Version.
  • sittest: Similar because it is the same verb's second-person singular present tense ("thou sittest"); differs because sittest is present tense while satst is past tense, and the two show the strong-verb ablaut pair i vs a (sit/sat).
  • didst sit: Similar because it is the periphrastic early modern past of the same verb ("thou didst sit"); differs because it uses the auxiliary do plus the bare infinitive, adding emphasis or formality, whereas satst is the simple inflected past without an auxiliary.
  • sat down: Similar because both express the past action of sitting; differs because sat down emphasizes the change of posture into the seated position, while satst emphasizes the state or posture of being seated, and it is archaic.
  • was seated: Similar because both express a seated condition in the past; differs because was seated is a passive or stative construction that treats seating as a state imposed by another, while satst is an active, intransitive verb form.
  • brooded: Similar because Milton's Spirit "satst brooding," where the participle captures the same hovering, watchful incubation; differs because brooded names the mental state of anxious or watchful meditation and is not a posture verb, while satst names the posture itself.
