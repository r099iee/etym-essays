• Colt: Similar because both refer to young horses; differs because 'colt' specifically denotes a young uncastrated male horse up to four years old, whereas 'yearling' is strictly age-bound (one to two years old) and applies to males, females, cattle, and sheep.
  • Filly: Similar because both can describe young equines; differs because 'filly' is exclusively female (under four years old), whereas 'yearling' specifies exact chronological age between 12 and 24 months regardless of sex.
  • Juvenile: Similar because both refer to sexually immature or growing organisms; differs because 'juvenile' is a broad biological and legal classification encompassing any stage between infancy and adulthood.
  • Hogget: Similar in agricultural livestock age classification; differs because 'hogget' is specifically a British/Australasian term for a young sheep between its first and second year (after weaning, before becoming adult mutton).
  • Teg: Similar in livestock terminology; differs because 'teg' is a specialized British dialect word specifically for a two-year-old sheep.
  • Adolescent: Similar in developmental stage; differs because 'adolescent' is primarily used for human psychological and physical pubertal development.
  • Shearling: Similar in pastoral age ranking; differs because 'shearling' denotes a sheep that has been shorn once, roughly corresponding to its second year of life.
