• creeping: Similar because both mean slow, close-to-the-ground motion; differs because creeping is neutral — slow, cautious, or insidious — with no necessary shame or menace, whereas slinking carries an air of guilt, fear, or furtive self-concealment.
  • sneaking: Similar because both mean moving so as not to be observed; differs because sneaking implies deliberate human deception and slyness (he sneaked a look, a sneak thief), while slinking suggests shame, fear, or animal wariness rather than craft.
  • prowling: Similar because both describe restless, roving movement; differs because prowling is the hunter's quartering of ground in search of prey — purposeful, even predatory — where slinking is shrinking and self-effacing, seeking to go unnoticed rather than to hunt.
  • skulking: Similar because both mean lurking or moving in concealment; differs because skulking emphasizes hiding behind cover with an intent to remain unseen or ambush, while slinking emphasizes the guilty, low, sinuous quality of the movement itself.
  • stealing (old sense): Similar because both mean moving silently and secretly; differs because stealing (as in "stole away") is a gentle, literary word for slipping off unnoticed, lacking slinking's connotation of shame or crouching.
  • sidling: Similar because both mean moving obliquely and cautiously; differs because sidling means moving edgewise or sideways, often with flattery or shy approach, where slinking is low and furtive withdrawal.
  • lurking: Similar because both suggest staying hidden from view; differs because lurking is remaining in concealment, often with hostile intent, while slinking is the act of moving furtively rather than staying still.
