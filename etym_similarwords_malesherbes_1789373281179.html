• Haussmann: Similar because both refer to monumental Second Empire Parisian urban thoroughfares and planning; differs because Haussmann designates the mastermind prefect and his ubiquitous architectural style, whereas Malesherbes designates a specific residential boulevard named for an Enlightenment jurist.
  • Saint-Germain: Similar because both denote affluent, historically significant residential quarters of the Parisian elite; differs because Saint-Germain represents ancient aristocratic faubourg lineage and Left-Bank intellectualism, whereas Malesherbes represents nineteenth-century grand-bourgeois financial wealth and wide Right-Bank boulevards.
  • Combray: Similar because both serve as primary geographic anchors in the biography and literature of Marcel Proust; differs because Malesherbes represents urban enclosure, illness, and winter domesticity, whereas Combray embodies pastoral liberation, ancestral memory, and summer holidays.
  • Turgot: Similar because both were reform-minded Enlightenment statesmen and ministers who served Louis XVI; differs because Turgot focused primarily on physiocratic economic reforms and taxation, whereas Malesherbes focused on legal administration, publishing liberty, and judicial defense.
  • Beaubourg: Similar because both are Parisian toponyms derived historically from descriptive place-names that evolved into major cultural symbols; differs because Beaubourg denotes a medieval 'beautiful village' turned modern art center, whereas Malesherbes evolved from 'bad weeds' into an aristocratic title and bourgeois boulevard.
