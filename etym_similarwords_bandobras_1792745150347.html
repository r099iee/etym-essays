• Bullroarer: Similar because it is the exact historical cognomen and legendary epithet borne by Bandobras Took himself; differs because Bullroarer is an informal, loud, rustic English moniker (referencing both his booming voice/furious charge and the ancient whirled acoustic instrument), whereas Bandobras is his formal, genealogically recorded baptismal name echoing medieval chivalric romance.
  • folk hero: Similar because Bandobras functions within Shire culture as an iconic, larger-than-life figure whose deeds are sung and remembered across generations; differs because folk hero is a broad sociological category encompassing historical or mythical figures across all world cultures, whereas Bandobras is a specific individual literary protagonist within Tolkien's legendarium.
  • champion: Similar because both refer to a warrior who fights on behalf of his people or leads their vanguard into battle against an oppressor; differs because champion is a general military and chivalric title, whereas Bandobras represents an idiosyncratic, club-wielding hobbit whose heroic battle is intertwined with comic pastoral folklore (such as the invention of golf).
  • paladin: Similar because both evoke the chivalric, high-medieval martial tradition associated with Carolingian romances (from which the suffix -bras derives); differs because a paladin is an exalted, courtly knight of kingly retinue bound by strict chivalric vows and religious devotion, whereas Bandobras is an agrarian Shire gentleman-farmer who fought out of emergency necessity.
  • behemoth: Similar because both emphasize astonishing, anomalous physical size that towers far above the surrounding norm; differs because behemoth carries a biblical, monstrous, or grotesque connotation of overwhelming bulk, whereas Bandobras's height (four foot five) is gigantic only in comparison to three-foot hobbits and is remembered with affectionate civic pride.
  • paragon: Similar because both denote an unrivaled model of excellence or supreme physical capability; differs because a paragon represents an idealized, flawless exemplar of a virtue (e.g., a paragon of virtue or martial skill), whereas Bandobras was an eccentric, rough-and-tumble frontier squire remembered for a single ferocious battle.
  • chieftain: Similar because Bandobras was of high dynastic birth (the son of the Thain) and led his people's armed men in crisis; differs because chieftain implies formal, autocratic political command over a clan or tribe, whereas Bandobras was the second son and never actually inherited the office of Thain (which passed to his elder brother Fortinbras I).
