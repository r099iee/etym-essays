- 1st c. BCE Latin benevolens wishing well - civic goodwill winning friends and votes - political-rhetorical, respectable, active
- 4th-13th c. Christian benevolentia charity - goodwill to poor and strangers as holy duty - pious-humble, almsgiving
- c.1470 English benevolent kindly prince - aristocratic favour, kind disposition of lords and stars - courtly-literary, lofty
- 1500s Tudor benevolences forced loans - so-called voluntary gifts extorted by kings - ironic-coercive, resented
- 1720s-1770s Enlightenment benevolence virtue - natural sympathy grounding morals in Hutcheson, Hume, Smith - philosophical-optimistic, universal
- Victorian benevolent societies and gentlemen - organised charity funds and Dickensian patrons - respectable-paternal, institutional
- Modern benevolent ruler, donor, AI, universe - kind power personal or cosmic - warm-praising, but questioned when patronising
