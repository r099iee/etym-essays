• Tail-end: Similar because both denote the final, terminal part or conclusion of something; differs because tail-end is a neutral, non-judgmental anatomical metaphor (the rear end of a line or sequence), whereas fag-end carries strong connotations of wear, fraying, cheapness, and exhausted dregs.
  • Remnant: Similar because both describe a remaining fragment or scrap left behind after the main body is gone; differs because remnant is formal and dignifying, frequently used for ancient survivors, noble relics, or unsold retail fabric, lacking the ragged, colloquial scruffiness of fag-end.
  • Dregs: Similar because both refer to the lowest, least valuable, or discarded leftovers of something; differs because dregs originates in the sediment settled at the bottom of a wine vat, emphasizing liquid murk, bitterness, and moral degradation, whereas fag-end evokes a frayed physical edge or temporal exhaustion.
  • Tag-end: Similar because both name a loose, trailing, or hanging extremity of an object or period; differs because tag-end suggests an attached label or fluttering pendant, lacking the specific British tobacco and nautical frayed-rope heritage of fag-end.
  • Stub: Similar because both can describe the short, spent remainder of a cigarette, pencil, or ticket; differs because stub emphasizes a rigid, blunt, truncated physical piece that has been chopped or worn down, whereas fag-end emphasizes unraveling, raggedness, and temporal conclusion.
  • Butt: Similar because both designate the discarded end of a smoked cigar or cigarette; differs because butt is the standard North American term, devoid of the rich textile, nautical, and temporal literary associations that fag-end maintains in British prose.
  • Leavings: Similar because both denote what is abandoned or rejected after the desirable parts have been consumed; differs because leavings is plural and food-centric (table scraps, animal leavings), lacking the singular focus on an expiring boundary found in fag-end.
  • Residue: Similar because both refer to that which remains after a process or consumption is complete; differs because residue is an objective, technical, chemical, or legal term, entirely devoid of colloquial atmosphere, emotional weariness, or poetic deflation.
  • Scraps: Similar because both describe small, detached, inferior fragments; differs because scraps suggests fragmented, broken bits of food, metal, or paper scattered about, whereas fag-end is a singular, continuous terminal extremity.
  • Vestige: Similar because both represent the last trace of something that once was vast and potent; differs because vestige is elevated, scholarly, and archaeological (a footprint or monument of the past), whereas fag-end is down-to-earth, worn out, and slightly comic.
  • Scrag-end: Similar because both are gritty British terms for the inferior leftover portion of something; differs because scrag-end specifically derives from the bony, gristly neck of mutton used in cheap broths, carrying visceral butcher-shop connotations.
