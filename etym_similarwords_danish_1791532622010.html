• Dane: The person from Denmark; similar as the demonym, but it is a count noun for an individual, whereas "Danish" is the adjective and the language name.
  • Denmark: The country itself; similar in referring to the same nation, but it names the territory, not the people, language or products.
  • Dansk: The Danish word for "Danish"; similar in meaning, but it is the endonym and is used when speaking or writing Danish.
  • Norse: The medieval North Germanic language and culture; similar as an ancestral label, but it refers to the older common language, not the modern national one.
  • Old Norse: The earlier stage of the Scandinavian languages; similar in being the ancestor of Danish, but it is a historical and scholarly term.
  • Nordic: Relating to the Nordic countries, including Finland and Iceland; similar as a regional adjective, but broader and not specifically Danish.
  • Scandinavian: Relating to Scandinavia, usually Denmark, Norway and Sweden; similar in overlapping with Danish, but it covers three nations and a wider cultural sphere.
  • Viking: A raider or trader of the Norse world; similar in evoking the Danish past, but it names an activity and a period, not a modern nationality.
  • Jutish: Relating to Jutland, the Danish mainland; similar as a regional adjective within Denmark, but narrower and mostly historical or dialectal.
  • Faroese: The language and people of the Faroe Islands; similar as a North Germanic neighbour, but a separate language and nationality.
  • Norwegian: The language and people of Norway; similar as a close North Germanic relative, but a different nation and standard language.
  • Dutch: Relating to the Netherlands; a false friend, historically related through the Germanic word for "people", but not Danish.
  • Deutsch: The German endonym for "German"; another false friend, sharing the ancient *theudō "people" root with Dutch but not with Danish.
