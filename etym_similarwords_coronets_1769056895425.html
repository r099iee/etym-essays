• crown: Similar because both are gold head-ornaments worn to signal rule; differs because the crown is the supreme symbol of sovereignty worn by monarchs and unmarked for rank, while a coronet is deliberately smaller and coded to a specific noble station below the sovereign.
  • diadem: Similar because it is a crown-like badge of authority; differs because the diadem is historically a light textile fillet tied around the brow, associated with Eastern rulers and personified "power," carrying no legal-heraldic code of leaves and pearls.
  • tiara: Similar because it is an elaborate jewelled head-ornament; differs because the tiara belongs to popes or, in modern dress, to brides and formal ceremony — fashion rather than a codified peerage, and worn by women rather than ranked peers.
  • circlet: Similar because it is a small band worn around the head; differs because the circlet is plain or minimally ornamented with no heraldic significance, whereas the coronet's whole point is its strict metal-and-pearl rank code.
  • chaplet: Similar because it is a small wreath or garland worn on the head; differs because the chaplet is of leaves, flowers, or beads and carries devotional or floral meaning, not the metallic rank-coded insignia of a peer's coronet.
  • wreath / garland: Similar because both trace to the same "curved, circular head-ornament" ancestry (Greek κορώνη, Latin corōna); differs because a wreath or garland is of woven leaves or flowers and marks celebration, mourning, or victory, not an inherited rank in a heraldic system.
  • fillet / bandeau: Similar because both are simple bands worn about the head; differs because the fillet or bandeau is a plain strip of cloth, ribbon, or metal with no sovereign or peerage code attached, worn ornamentally rather than heraldically.
  • regalia: Similar because it refers to the ceremonial insignia of royalty and nobility, among which coronets count; differs because regalia is the collective noun for all such objects (crowns, orbs, sceptres, robes) rather than the specific small crown of a peer.
  • halo / corona: Similar because both descend from Latin corōna "crown" and picture a ring around a head or body; differs because a halo or corona is a ring of light (and, in science, the sun's outer atmosphere) rather than an object worn by a noble.
