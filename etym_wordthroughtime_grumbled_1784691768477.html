- 1580s - adopted as an echoic word of sound, "grumble" first named the noise itself: thunder grumbles, the belly grumbles, a cart grumbles over stones — a low, continuous, rumbling rumble.
- 17th-18th century - the word's centre of gravity shifted from noise to mood, from "to rumble low" to "to express discontent in a low voice"; the grumbler, the Grumbletonians of Queen Anne's Parliament, and the grump family (grumpish, grumpy) all grew from the same stock.
- Modern English - "grumble" still holds both ends: a grumble remains at once a sound and a state of mind, the deep, round, repetitive low-voiced form of protest — the noise of a small storm passing through a mood.
