• Scapegoat: Similar because both refer to an innocent entity targeted to bear the blame, guilt, or punishment for the misdeeds of others; differs because a scapegoat is often selected impulsively or ritually to deflect collective anxiety, whereas Dreyfus specifically designates an individual systematically framed and legally railroaded by state machinery and ideological prejudice.
  • Martyr: Similar because both denote figures who endure profound suffering, public degradation, or imprisonment under hostile authorities; differs because a martyr typically chooses to suffer or die willingly in defense of a religious, moral, or political principle, whereas Dreyfus was an involuntary victim of systemic bigotry whose sole objective was the restoration of truth and military honour.
  • Patsy: Similar because both describe someone manipulated, deceived, or framed into taking the legal fall for a crime orchestrated by others; differs because 'patsy' is colloquial or criminal register suggesting gullibility or low social stature, whereas Dreyfus carries heavy historical, constitutional, and civil-rights gravitas.
  • Judicial victim: Similar because both denote an individual wrongfully convicted through dishonest testimony, fabricated evidence, or legal corruption; differs because a judicial victim is a generic legal category, whereas Dreyfus evokes a specific civilizational turning point involving national hysteria, antisemitism, and mass intellectual rebellion.
  • Pariah: Similar because both refer to persons subjected to radical social ostracism, contempt, and expulsion from respectable society; differs because a pariah describes the ongoing state of social exclusion without necessarily implying formal legal framing or subsequent worldwide political exoneration.
