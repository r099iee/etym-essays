• flinging: Similar because both mean throwing; differs because fling implies violent, careless force, whereas toss is light and casual.
  • hurling: Similar as throwing; differs in far greater force and speed — a hurl is violent and aimed, a toss is easy and loose.
  • throwing: Similar as the generic verb of projection; differs as the neutral general term, while toss adds lightness and randomness.
  • pitching (of a ship): Similar as violent up-and-down motion; differs because pitching is the whole vessel's fore-and-aft movement, while tossing implies shorter, faster agitation.
  • thrashing: Similar as violent movement in bed; differs in being more desperate and less rhythmic than tossing.
  • turning (in bed): Similar as restless movement; differs as calmer and more deliberate — the two words join in the fixed pair 'tossing and turning'.
  • heaving: Similar as lifting agitation of the body or sea; differs in laboured heaviness and its nausea register.
  • jiggling / jouncing: Similar as small agitated movement; differs in being lighter, smaller, and more mechanical.
  • shuffling: Similar as casual handling; differs as sliding without any lift or throw.
  • tumbling: Similar as falling and rolling motion; differs in loss of control and downward direction, the opposite of a toss's upward flick.
