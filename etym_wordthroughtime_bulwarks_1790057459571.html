- 13th-14th c. Low Countries bolwerc plank-rampart - timber-faced earth bastions of dyke-towns and sieges - military-engineering, workmanlike
- 15th c. Middle English bulwerk - borrowed outwork before gates and castles; cannon-proof earth-and-timber - martial, modernising
- 1500s-1600s generalised rampart plus ship's side - town bulwarks stormed in Civil War; sailors lean on oak bulwarks - martial at once ashore and afloat
- 1700s Burke's constitutional bulwark - liberty, faith, and property as built defences - lofty-parliamentary, principled
- 1800s voyage-prose coastal bulwarks - basalt, coral, and ice cliffs read as fortresses against the sea; your sentence's register - romantic-scenic, sublime
- Modern bulwarks - steel ships keep literal sides; editorials keep figurative walls against tyranny, inflation, despair - split: technical-nautical and grand-political
