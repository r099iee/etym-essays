- c. 1380–1550: Abstract noun meaning political governance, royal dominion, sovereign authority, or medical rule of health - Formal, elevated Latinate political term.
- late 1500s–1700s: Specialized into a permanent body of soldiers under a colonel's command - Associated with professional standing armies, martial honor, and royal regiments.
- 1800s–1900s: Emergence of the transitive verb 'to regiment' and noun 'regimentation' - Connoted industrial efficiency, Taylorism, but also bureaucratic rigidity and loss of individuality.
- Present: Dual usage - high prestige and historical pride in military contexts, while mildly pejorative/critical when describing overly rigid civilian institutions.
