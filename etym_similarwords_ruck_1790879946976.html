• Crowd: Similar because both describe a large gathering of people; differs because crowd is a neutral term for any physical assembly, whereas the ruck implies an undistinguished, mediocre, or trailing mass.
  • Hoi polloi: Similar because both refer to the common multitude; differs because hoi polloi is a Greek loanword specifically emphasizing the social masses in contrast to the elite aristocracy, while ruck focuses on the undistinguished pack in a competition or profession.
  • Rabble: Similar because both describe a mass of common people; differs because rabble carries strong negative connotations of disorder, lawlessness, and unruly vulgarity.
  • Rank and file: Similar because both describe the general body of an organization; differs because rank and file is a military metaphor denoting regular enlisted members or obedient workers, whereas ruck implies lack of distinction.
  • Scrum: Similar because both are rugby formations and chaotic clusters; differs because a scrum is an ordered, referee-supervised set piece, whereas a ruck occurs dynamically around a tackled player on the ground.
  • Crease: Similar because both refer to lines or ridges in fabric; differs because a crease is a sharp, deliberate pressed line, while a ruck is an irregular, unintentional gather or wrinkle.
