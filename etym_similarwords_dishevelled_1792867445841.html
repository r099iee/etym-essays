• Unkempt: Similar because both describe neglected hair and appearance; differs because unkempt stresses long-uncombed straggle from the roots up, while dishevelled pictures present disorder, often sudden, with sympathy.
  • Rumpled: Similar because both name crumpled untidiness of hair or clothes; differs because rumpled is homely and small-scale, bedlinen and jackets, while dishevelled is grander and can engulf whole houses and lives.
  • Disordered: Similar because both mean the proper arrangement is lost; differs because disordered is clinical and general, ranks or papers, while dishevelled keeps the streaming-hair image and its pathos.
  • Untidy: Similar because both note the absence of neatness; differs because untidy is the plain everyday scold for desks and bedrooms, while dishevelled is literary and tender toward the mess.
  • Messy: Similar because both cover tousled people and chaotic rooms; differs because messy is blunt, modern, and faintly cheerful, while dishevelled is elegiac, mourning the order that was.
  • Disarrayed: Similar because both picture dress or ranks fallen from array; differs because disarrayed stresses the broken formation, military or sartorial, while dishevelled stresses the flowing, lock-like quality of the ruin.
  • Bedraggled: Similar because both show a figure the worse for weather or travel; differs because bedraggled adds wetness and draggle, mud on the hem, while dishevelled need not be damp at all.
  • Scruffy: Similar because both offend the tidy eye; differs because scruffy judges and looks down, scruffy shoes and scruffy pubs, while dishevelled commiserates and half-admires.
  • Derelict: Similar because a dishevelled house verges on the derelict; differs because derelict means abandoned and ruinous, beyond pity, while dishevelled suggests neglect that love could still repair.
  • Wild-eyed: Similar because dishevelled mourners and visionaries stare wildly; differs because wild-eyed names only the staring eyes of frenzy, while dishevelled clothes the whole figure in disorder.
