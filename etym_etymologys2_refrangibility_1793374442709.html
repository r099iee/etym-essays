Note on Etymology: Compounded from the Latin prefix re- ("back, again") and the verb frangere ("to break", cognate with English break, fracture, and fragile). In Classical Latin, vowel weakening produced refringere, but Early Modern scientific Latin coinages analogically rebuilt the stem upon the simplex frangere to yield refrangibilis, emphasizing the physical analogy of breaking the path of a ray.
