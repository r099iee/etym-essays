• Barrel: Similar because both denote staved cylindrical wooden containers bound by hoops; differs because cask is the overarching general term for containers of any dimension, whereas barrel is technically a precise statutory unit of capacity (e.g., 36 imperial gallons for British beer, or 42 US gallons for petroleum).
  • Keg: Similar because both store beer and other beverages under containment; differs because a modern keg is typically a smaller, pressurized stainless-steel or aluminum container with an internal extraction tube (spear) for pressurized gas dispensing, whereas a cask relies on secondary yeast fermentation and gravity/suction dispensing.
  • Hogshead: Similar because both are traditional wooden coopered vessels; differs because a hogshead is a specific large standardized size of cask holding approximately 54 imperial gallons (or 63 US gallons), often used for aging Scotch whisky.
  • Tun: Similar because both store wine and fermenting liquids; differs because a tun is a massive stationary cellar cask or brewing vat holding up to 252 imperial gallons.
  • Firkin: Similar because both are wooden liquid vessels; differs because a firkin is a small cask holding precisely one-quarter of a beer barrel (9 imperial gallons).
  • Vat: Similar because both are large vessels designed to hold liquids; differs because a vat is typically an enormous, stationary, open-topped or enclosed industrial tank used for active fermentation, mashing, or chemical dyeing rather than transportable storage.
  • Butt: Similar because both are wooden storage casks; differs because a butt (or pipe) is a specific massive wine cask holding 108 to 126 gallons (such as a butt of Malmsey or Sherry).
  • Drum: Similar because both are cylindrical bulk shipping containers; differs because a drum is a modern industrial cylinder manufactured from pressed steel, heavy plastic, or fiberboard for transporting hazardous chemicals, oil, or powders.
