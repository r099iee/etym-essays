• Greek: the everyday English adjective and noun for the people, language and country; nearly synonymous with Hellenic, but it derives from the Latin exonym Graecus and is neutral and colloquial, whereas Hellenic is formal and culture-laden.
  • Hellenistic: belonging to the period after Alexander (323-31 BCE) or to the Greek culture diffused across the Near East; similar in referring to Greek civilisation, but it names a specific post-classical era, not the classical or modern nation.
  • Hellenism: the study or adoption of Greek culture, or the ancient Greek cultural movement; similar as a derivative of Hellas, but it is a noun for a process or movement, not an adjective for a people or language.
  • Hellenist: a scholar of Greek culture, or an ancient Greek-speaking non-Greek; similar in belonging to the same word family, but it is personal and often technical.
  • Hellenization: the process of becoming Greek in language or culture; similar in scope, but it names a process rather than a quality or affiliation.
  • Hellas: the Greek name for Greece; similar as the source of Hellenic, but it is a proper place-name, poetic and patriotic in English, not an adjective.
  • Grecian: an older English adjective for Greek, especially in architecture and art; similar in meaning, but archaic and largely confined to fixed phrases such as "Grecian urn".
  • Attic: of Athens or Attica, or the classical Athenian dialect; similar as a marker of Greek classical culture, but narrower and specifically Athenian.
  • Panhellenic: involving all Greeks, as in the Panhellenic games; similar in referring to the whole Greek world, but it emphasises unity across cities rather than Greekness as such.
  • Achaian / Achaean: a Homeric name for the Greeks; similar as an ancient collective ethnonym, but it is literary and archaic and does not denote the modern nation or language family.
  • Byzantine: of the Greek-speaking Christian empire centred on Constantinople; similar in denoting a phase of Greek culture, but it is a later, Christian and eastern Roman category, not classical Hellas.
  • Roman: of Rome; similar only as a contrast term in the classical Mediterranean, and entirely different in language and institutions.
