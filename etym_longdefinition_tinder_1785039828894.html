TINDER (noun) denotes dry, highly flammable material specially prepared to catch the tiny spark struck from flint-and-steel (or later, from a match or lighter) and to nurse that spark into a flame. Historically it was scorched or charred linen, charred cotton or cloth, dried tree-fungus (amadou or touchwood), punk or rotten wood, wood shavings, or bark fiber, kept in a dry, tight-lidded tinder-box together with the flint and steel needed to strike the spark. Its defining property is a very low ignition threshold: the material is so desiccated and porous that a single hot particle falling on its surface is enough to make it glow, after which it is coaxed, blown, or transferred to the kindling and fuel of a fire. Tinder is therefore the passive, patient link in the fire-making chain — the substance that receives the spark and holds its heat while it is fanned into life.

In the passage from J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit, Chapter 4 ("Over Hill and Under Hill"), the phrase "before you could say tinder and flint" is used as a proverb of instantaneous speed: the goblins seize the entire party of dwarves (six goblins per dwarf, two even for the hobbit) and carry them through a crack in the mountain wall so quickly that a man could not even utter the two words of the fire-making formula. The collocation "tinder and flint" had become a fixed pair in English because tinder and flint were the inseparable two halves of the old fire-starter's kit — you could not say the one without thinking of the other. Tolkien uses it for comic-horrific effect: the violence is absurdly, mechanically fast, and the homely fireside word-pair makes the ambush feel almost like a sleight of hand.

  • Polysemy 1 — fire-making substance (primary, concrete sense): dry material that catches a spark; the oldest and literal sense, going back to Old English tynder.
  • Polysemy 2 — figurative tinder (anything that inflames): since the 1590s tinder (especially tinder-box) is applied to any person, situation, or volatile mixture that is ready to burst into anger, violence, passion, or conflict; a "tinderbox" region is one where war or revolution may break out at any moment. The semantic bridge is the low ignition threshold: just as tinder catches a single spark, such situations flare up at the smallest provocation.
  • Polysemy 3 — the dating app Tinder (2012, proper noun): the mobile dating application is named after the word, and the marketing logic is explicit — matches are "kindled" quickly from a spark of mutual attraction; the historical bridge is the same instant-ignition metaphor used in Polysemy 2, transposed from fire to romance.
  • Polysemy 4 — adjective in compounds: tinder-dry means "so dry as to be perfectly ready to burn" (applied to grass, forests, timber in drought); tinder fungus is the bracket fungus (Fomes fomentarius) used to make amadou; tinder-box is both the historical container and, figuratively, a place or situation on the verge of explosion.
  • Polysemy 5 — technical/zoological extension: in entomology and herpetology, "tinder" has occasionally been used by analogy for dry nesting or pupating material; and tinder in some dialects simply means "fuel generally," showing the word drifting toward the wider notion of "anything burnable."

Why the secondary meanings developed: the chain of senses is driven by the single core idea of minimal ignition. A physical material that catches fire at the lightest touch becomes, by analogy, a person or crowd on the edge of fury; a volatile political region becomes a "tinderbox"; and a dating app that pairs strangers on the basis of a quick, mutual "spark" extends the metaphor to human attraction. The word thus moves from the hearth to the battlefield, the newspaper, and the smartphone without ever losing its original image of a dry, waiting combustible.

Distinctions from near-synonyms: kindling is small dry sticks and wood arranged to feed an already-laid fire, coarser and larger than tinder; fuel is the bulk material that sustains burning once the fire is going; touchwood (or amadou) is the soft charred fungus specifically carried in tinder-boxes; punk is dry rotten wood kept for smouldering; char-cloth is modern prepared linen tinder; spunk (Scottish and dialectal) is the same idea of touchwood or tinder; and fomes — the Latin word for tinder — survives in the medical term fomite, "an object capable of carrying infection," a literal echo of the fire-carrying idea. What distinguishes TINDER from all of these is its position at the very beginning of the fire: it is the flashpoint that converts a spark into a flame, before kindling and fuel take over.
