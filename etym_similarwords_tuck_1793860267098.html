• Insert: Similar because both involve putting an object inside a receptacle, boundary, or narrow opening; differs because insert is a neutral, clinical, and generic term denoting the mere act of placement within an aperture, whereas tuck emphasizes snugness, compactness, neatness, and the folding or securing of loose edges.
  • Fold: Similar because both involve bending flexible material back upon itself to reduce its surface area or adjust its dimensions; differs because fold specifically denotes creating a sharp or soft crease along a plane of cloth or paper, whereas tuck entails pushing the folded edges into an enclosure, crevice, or stitched seam to hold them securely.
  • Stash: Similar because both describe stowing or concealing an item in a small, out-of-the-way space; differs because stash carries an informal, secretive, or clandestine connotation often associated with contraband or emergency provisions, whereas tuck implies domestic tidiness, snugness, or gentle concealment.
  • Nestle: Similar because both convey settling comfortably and snugly into a sheltered, cozy resting place; differs because nestle emphasizes warmth, physical affection, emotional tenderness, and quiet repose (e.g., a child nestling against a parent), whereas tuck focuses on the physical, deliberate action of arranging coverings or margins.
  • Enfold: Similar because both describe surrounding, covering, and enclosing something snugly; differs because enfold is an elevated, poetic term suggesting total enveloping, embracing, or wrapping (e.g., darkness enfolding the valley), whereas tuck is pragmatic, manual, and precise.
  • Gather: Similar because both are technical sewing operations that manipulate cloth into clustered folds; differs because gather draws fabric together along a running thread to create soft, billowing fullness or ruffles, whereas tuck creates flat, parallel, stitched ridges of uniform depth.
  • Hem: Similar because both denote finishing or altering edges in tailoring; differs because a hem folds under the raw perimeter of a fabric piece to prevent unraveling and secure a border, whereas a tuck is a decorative or sizing fold taken anywhere within the body or perimeter of a garment.
  • Stuff: Similar because both describe pushing material into an interior space; differs because stuff implies crude, forceful, indiscriminate cramming to full capacity, whereas tuck conveys careful, orderly, and tidy placement.
