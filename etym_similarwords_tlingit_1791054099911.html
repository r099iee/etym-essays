• Haida: Similar because they are neighbouring Northwest Coast canoe-potlatch people; differs because Haida speak a language isolate and live largely on Haida Gwaii, not Tlingit territory.
  • Tsimshian: Similar because they share formline art, salmon culture, and moieties; differs because Tsimshian speak a Penutian-affiliated language and live south of the Tlingit.
  • Kwakwaka'wakw (Kwakiutl): Similar because they are northern Northwest Coast potlatch societies; differs because their language is Wakashan and their winter dances differ.
  • Athabaskan (Dene): Similar because they are linguistically related Na-Dene relatives in the Interior; differs because Athabaskans are largely interior caribou hunters, not coastal salmon-seal people.
  • Eyak: Similar because Eyak is the closest linguistic relative to Tlingit-Athabaskan; differs because Eyak is a distinct, now-dormant Copper River people.
  • Aleut (Unangan): Similar because they are Alaska Native maritime hunters; differs because Aleut culture centres on Aleutian kayaks and Russian-Orthodox history.
  • Inuit / Iñupiat / Yupik: Similar because they are northern Indigenous maritime peoples; differs because Inuit-Yupik languages and whaling culture are unrelated to Na-Dene.
  • Chinook: Similar because Chinook are another famed salmon-trading coastal people; differs because Chinook live on the lower Columbia far south and spoke Chinookan languages.
  • Coast Salish: Similar because they share cedar, salmon, and potlatch traits; differs because Salish languages and social organisation are distinct and centred around Puget Sound.
  • Heiltsuk / Nuxalk: Similar because they are central-coast neighbours with similar art; differs because their languages (Wakashan, Salishan) and histories differ.
