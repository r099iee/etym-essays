*steh₂- / *steur-

*stōraz

stórr
"large, big, tall, stout, proud"
↓
Early Middle English (Danelaw dialect) (c. 12th–13th c.)
stor / stour
"strong, sturdy, bold, formidable"
↓
Late Middle English & Northern Dialectal English (c. 14th–16th c.)
stoor
"stout, thick-set, massive, hardy"
↓
Modern English (Literary / Tolkien's Legendarium) (1954)
Stoors
"broad, river-dwelling breed of Hobbits"

Note on linguistic nuance: In J.R.R. Tolkien's translation schema (detailed in Appendix F of The Lord of the Rings), the Common Speech (Westron) of Middle-earth is translated into English. Words of rustic, archaic, or dialectal flavor in Westron were systematically rendered into archaic or dialectal Northern English and Scots equivalents. Tolkien selected the dialectal Northern English adjective stoor (derived directly from Old Norse stórr) because its dual connotations of stout, thick-set physical bodily mass and unpolished, stubborn resilience perfectly mirrored the characteristics of this river-loving Hobbit breed.
