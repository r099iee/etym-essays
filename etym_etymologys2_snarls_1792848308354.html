Middle Dutch and Middle Low German (13th–14th c.)
snarren
‘to growl, grumble, rattle’
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
snar
‘to growl, snap at’
↓
Early Modern English (1590s)
snarl
‘to growl with bared teeth’
↓
Modern English (plural noun)
snarls
‘repeated growls, hostile utterances’

Note: growl-snarl is a frequentative of Middle English snar built with the iterative -le suffix, rooted in echoic Germanic vocabulary shared with Dutch snarren and German schnarren. It has no classical pedigree and is wholly distinct from tangle-snarl, which descends from snare.
