- [Middle English flewe] - 'mouthpiece of a hunting horn; a pipe, a tube' (∼1400): the word names a narrow, hollow passage, purely functional.
- [Chimney smoke-duct (from 1580)] - the word enters the record as the smoke-passage of a chimney, born of the great age of the enclosed English fireplace; register domestic and architectural.
- [Soot-era associations (18th–19th c.)] - through chimney sweeps and Victorian townhouses the word gains a grim, sooty resonance — the dark, unvisited conduit of the mechanical sublime.
- [Modern technical term] - today flue is clinical and engineering-grade — boiler flue, flue gas, flue damper — extended from hearths to furnaces and power stations; no longer colloquial, purely functional.
