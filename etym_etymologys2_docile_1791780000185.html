Note: Latin docilis is built from docere ("to teach") and the suffix -ilis (expressing passive capability, "able to be taught"). It belongs to the same prolific Indo-European root (*deḱ- "to accept") that produced doctor ("teacher"), doctrine ("that which is taught"), document ("instructive proof"), and Greek dogma ("opinion, belief"). Over centuries in English, the active intellectual sense of "quick to learn" decayed into the passive behavioral sense of "easy to control".
