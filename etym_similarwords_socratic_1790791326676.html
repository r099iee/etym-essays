• Dialectical: Similar because both involve cooperative, logical discourse and the pursuit of truth through reasoned argument and counter-argument; differs because dialectical is a broader philosophical framework (encompassing Hegelian and Marxist dialectics of historical materialism) that does not necessarily employ Socratic irony or question-driven pedagogy.
  • Maieutic: Similar because it refers specifically to the Socratic method of drawing out latent ideas from a student; differs because maieutic is a technical metaphor derived from Greek midwifery (intellectual 'midwifery'), focusing exclusively on birthing latent wisdom rather than encompassing Socrates's broader ethical and ironic philosophy.
  • Inquisitorial: Similar because both involve intense, persistent questioning; differs because inquisitorial carries a hostile, coercive, and accusatory tone (reminiscent of the Inquisition or an authoritarian interrogation), whereas Socratic inquiry aims at philosophical enlightenment and exposing logical truth.
  • Heuristic: Similar because both encourage active self-discovery and learning through exploration rather than direct instruction; differs because heuristic refers to practical problem-solving rules of thumb and discovery techniques, without the structured question-and-answer dialectic of the Socratic method.
  • Catechetical: Similar because both utilize a question-and-answer format in teaching; differs because catechetical instruction involves reciting fixed, pre-formulated doctrinal answers (as in a religious catechism), the exact opposite of open-ended, critical Socratic inquiry.
