• Vasari: Similar because both were Italian artists who authored indispensable primary source texts that defined the historiography and practices of Renaissance art; differs because Giorgio Vasari wrote biographical narratives (*Le Vite*) celebrating artistic progress and heroic individual genius, whereas Cennino wrote a technical workshop recipe book detailing manual material craft.
  • Alberti: Similar because both composed seminal Italian Renaissance treatises establishing the rules and dignity of painting; differs because Leon Battista Alberti approached painting as a humanist intellectual discipline grounded in Euclidean geometry and narrative rhetoric (*istoria*), whereas Cennino approached it as a devotional guild craft grounded in pigments, brushes, and apprentice labor.
  • Lomazzo: Similar because both were Italian painter-theorists who documented artistic methods for posterity; differs because Gian Paolo Lomazzo wrote in the late Mannerist era codifying academic systems and astrological temperaments, whereas Cennino documented late Trecento workshop practice.
  • Theophilus: Similar because both wrote procedural medieval handbooks detailing the materials, formulas, and artisanal techniques of ecclesiastical art; differs because Theophilus (Roger of Helmarshausen) was a 12th-century northern European Benedictine monk writing in Latin for monastic craftsmen, whereas Cennino was a lay Florentine guild painter writing in vernacular Tuscan.
  • Ghiberti: Similar because Lorenzo Ghiberti was an early Quattrocento Florentine master who documented artistic theory and workshop lineage in his *Commentarii*; differs because Ghiberti blended art history with autobiographical memoirs and optics, whereas Cennino focused on an instructional, step-by-step studio manual for apprentice painters.
  • Vitruvius: Similar because both authored classic instructional treatises establishing the foundational material practices of their respective disciplines; differs because Vitruvius wrote on classical Roman architecture, military machinery, and civil engineering in Latin, whereas Cennino focused specifically on painting, drawing, and gilding in Renaissance Italy.
