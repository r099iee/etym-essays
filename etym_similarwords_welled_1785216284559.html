• gush: Similar because both describe liquid rising or pouring out abundantly; differs because gushing is a violent, spurting outflow under pressure, while welling is a steady, gentle upwelling with no force.
  • surge: Similar because both name a rise from below; differs because a surge is a great wave-like heave of force, whereas welling rises softly of its own accord.
  • flood: Similar because both name abundant outpouring; differs because flooding overwhelms and swamps its container, while welling fills generously without destructive excess.
  • brim: Similar because both involve liquid approaching the top of a container; differs because brimming only reaches the edge and stays level, whereas welling actively rises from below and can overflow.
  • seep: Similar because both describe liquid moving through a surface; differs because seeping is thin, slow passage through porous matter, lacking welling's abundance and fullness.
  • trickle: Similar because both describe gentle flowing; differs because a trickle is feeble and meagre, whereas welling is steady and full-bodied.
  • emerge: Similar because both describe something coming up into view or existence; differs because emergence is neutral and intellectual, with none of welling's liquid, involuntary, elemental pressure.
  • overflow: Similar because both involve rising liquid; differs because overflowing specifically names spilling over the rim, a consequence of welling rather than the rising itself.
