PIE *peig- / *peik- "to pierce, prick, stick, mark by punching" (reconstructed root, c. 4000 BCE) → Vulgar Latin *piccāre "to prick, pierce with a sharp point" (Late Latin / early medieval period, c. 5th–7th c. CE; origin debated — possibly a PIE descendant, possibly imitative) → Old French piquer "to prick, sting; to spur, excite" (9th–12th c. CE; also source of piquant, piquet, picket) → French pique (noun) "a prick; a sting of resentment, wounded pride, a slight" (c. 16th c. CE) → English pique (verb) "to wound, irritate, or stimulate by pricking" (borrowed mid-17th c. CE) → English piqued (past participle / participial adjective) "stimulated, irritated, wounded in pride" ◄ TARGET (present-day rootWord) Debate / nuance note: The deep ancestry of *piccāre is disputed. Many dictionaries link it, uncertainly, to PIE *peig- / *peik- "to stick, prick" (compare Greek pikros "sharp, bitter"; the Germanic branch gave pick, peck, pike). Other authorities treat *piccāre as a sound-symbolic or imitative formation with no secure PIE source. The Romance forms (Italian piccare, Spanish picar, Portuguese picar, French piquer) are fully certain; the ultimate prehistoric origin is not.
