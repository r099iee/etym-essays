• Reluctant: Similar because both mean unwilling and slow to act; differs because reluctant is the neutral everyday word for hesitation, while loath is stronger, more formal, and suggests active dislike behind the hesitation.
  • Unwilling: Similar because both describe a person who does not want to do something; differs because unwilling is the plain general term for refusal of consent, while loath adds an emotional colour of aversion or distaste.
  • Averse: Similar because both express a settled dislike that blocks action (averse to risk); differs because averse names a standing disposition toward a thing, while loath names the felt reluctance toward a particular act.
  • Disinclined: Similar because both mean lacking inclination toward an action; differs because disinclined is cool and mild, a mere absence of desire, while loath is warm with repugnance, a presence of dislike.
  • Hesitant: Similar because both describe holding back before acting; differs because hesitant stresses wavering uncertainty about what to do, while loath stresses certainty about not wanting to do it.
  • Resistant: Similar because both describe pushing back against something proposed; differs because resistant suggests active opposition or immunity to pressure, while loath suggests inward shrinking rather than outward fighting.
  • Opposed: Similar because both involve being against a course of action; differs because opposed is a firm principled stance openly declared, while loath is a feeling of repugnance that may never become a declared position.
  • Slow: Similar because slow to anger and loath to anger both praise restraint; differs because slow describes the delay itself, while loath describes the dislike that causes the delay.
  • Backward: Similar because backward in coming forward resembles reluctance; differs because backward suggests shyness or retarded development, while loath suggests a conscious, often honourable shrinking from the act.
