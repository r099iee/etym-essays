- 14th Century: Entered Middle English from Old French and Ecclesiastical Latin, referring exclusively to formal ecclesiastical divisions and splits in the Catholic Church (such as the Great Western Schism of 1378–1417).
- 16th to 17th Century: Expanded during the Protestant Reformation to describe the major institutional fractures between Roman Catholicism and Protestant denominations, as well as splits among Protestant sects.
- 18th to 19th Century: Broadened through secular metaphor to describe deep political splits, parliamentary cleavages, and fractures within philosophical movements.
- 20th to 21st Century: Widely applied to secular organizations, ideological factions in political parties, disputes in scientific paradigms, and rival factions in artistic and theatrical methodologies (e.g., post-Stanislavski acting schools).
