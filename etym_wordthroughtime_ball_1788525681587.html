- [12th-13th c. / Old French] - baller / bal - [courtly dancing, song-dance, and joyous round dances in Provençal and Old French aristocratic courts]
- [1630s / Early Modern English] - ball - [borrowed into English from French bal to designate grand aristocratic dancing assemblies held at royal courts and stately residences]
- [18th-19th c. / Georgian & Victorian Eras] - ball - [the supreme institutional centerpiece of high-society courtship, fashion, and aristocratic matchmaking; strictly regulated by chaperones, dance cards, white tie, and waltzes]
- [20th c. / Inter-war & Post-war Era] - ball - [broadened to civic charity galas, debutante presentations, and university balls; the colloquial American phrase "have a ball" emerged in 1920s jazz slang]
- [Late 20th-21st c. / Modern Era] - ball - [preserved in high diplomacy and state functions (e.g., Presidential Inaugural Balls); transformed into a revolutionary artistic performance subculture via Harlem LGBTQ+ Ballroom culture]
