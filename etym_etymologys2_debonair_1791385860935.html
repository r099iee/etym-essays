Note: The base phrase combines Romance preposition de (from Latin dē 'from, of'), adjective bon (Latin bonus), and noun aire. The origin of Old French aire ('lineage, extraction, breeding') is traditionally traced to Latin ager ('field, land, native territory', PIE *h₂éǵ-ro- 'field'), though some philologists suggest influence from or fusion with Latin area ('open threshing floor, courtyard') or a specialized sense of aerie (eyrie, 'nest of a bird of prey', from Medieval Latin aeria). The derivation of the full idiom from aristocratic falconry (describing a bird of fine disposition) is well attested in medieval Anglo-Norman romance.
