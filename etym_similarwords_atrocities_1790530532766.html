• Massacres: Similar in denoting horrific acts of violence with widespread loss of life; differs because massacres (from Middle French macacre, "slaughterhouse/butchery") specifically requires the indiscriminate slaughter of many helpless people, whereas atrocities can encompass individual heinous tortures, systemic barbarities, or cultural desecrations beyond sheer body count.
  • Infamies: Similar in denoting shameful and abhorrent deeds; differs because infamies focuses primarily on public disgrace, scandalous wickedness, and the destruction of moral reputation, whereas atrocities centers on ferocious, savage cruelty and physical devastation.
  • Barbarities: Similar in describing cruel, uncivilized, and inhuman actions; differs because barbarities (from Greek barbaros, "foreign/uncivilized") emphasizes a departure from civilization and cultured norms into crude savagery, whereas atrocities denotes the shocking enormity and malevolent horror of the acts themselves.
  • Enormities: Similar in describing acts of monstrous evil; differs because enormities (from Latin enormitas, "out of the norm/monstrous magnitude") emphasizes the boundless, extreme scale and deviation from moral law, though in modern popular usage it is often mistakenly confused with sheer physical size.
  • Carnages: Similar in describing bloody scenes of destruction; differs because carnage (from Latin carnaticum, "flesh/meat") is typically uncountable and emphasizes heaps of butchered physical flesh and corpses on a battlefield, while atrocities denotes deliberate, morally culpable acts of wickedness.
  • Depredations: Similar in involving violent destruction and suffering; differs because depredations (from Latin praeda, "prey/plunder") stresses predatory plundering, looting, and widespread material laying-waste during war.
  • Cruelties: Similar in causing intentional suffering; differs because cruelties is a broad, everyday term for any unfeeling infliction of pain, whereas atrocities is reserved for the most extreme, horrifying, and shocking manifestations of inhuman malice.
  • Outrages: Similar in denoting acts that violate moral decency; differs because outrages emphasizes the transgression of established boundaries and the indignant emotional reaction provoked, whereas atrocities conveys visceral, physical horror.
