BUSKINS
↓ Old French (16th c.; of uncertain origin)
bouzeguin
"a buskin"
variant brosequin
↓ English (1570s)
buskin
"a high boot reaching
to the calf; the boot
of tragedy"
↓ English (plural)
buskins
"high boots reaching
to the calf"

Debate: origin uncertain. Possibly from an Italian or Catalan word (cf. Italian borzacchino, Spanish borceguí); a derivation from Medieval Latin borsa "a purse, bag" has been proposed but is unproven. The association with tragedy comes from the high boot (cothurnus) worn by actors in ancient tragedy.
