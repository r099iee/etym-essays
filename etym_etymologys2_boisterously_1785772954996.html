Modern English BOISTEROUSLY (adverb, "in a noisy, energetic, unruly manner", in regular use from the 16th century onward) ← Early Modern English boisterously (adverb formed from boisterous + -ly) ← Early Modern English boisterous "rough, violent, stormy" (form stabilised in the 16th century, reshaped by analogy with adjectives in -erous/-ous) ← Middle English boistous, boystous, bustuous "rough, rude, coarse, fierce, violent, stiff, stout" (14th–15th centuries) ← Anglo-Norman / Old French boistous, boisteus "rough, rude, clumsy, lame, fierce" (12th–13th centuries). Note on the deeper origin (debated / uncertain): No secure PIE etymon is attested for this word, and the Anglo-Norman/Old French forms are themselves of disputed further origin. One frequently cited hypothesis connects boisteus to Old French boiste "box" (from Late Latin buxida, Latin pyxis, Greek πύξις pyxis "box"), as though "lame, awkward" meant "boxed-in, constrained" and later shaded toward "rough, clumsy"; but the semantic leap from "box" to "violent, stormy" is far from secure, and many etymologists remain unconvinced. Other proposals invoke Low German or expressive coinage. The chain is therefore securely attested only from the Anglo-Norman/Middle English contact zone forward; any deeper reconstruction must be treated as speculative.
