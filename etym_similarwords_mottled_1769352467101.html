• Dappled: Similar because both mean marked with patches of different color or tone; differs because dappled specifically describes patches of light and shadow (dappled sunlight through leaves) or the spots of deer and horses, and sounds gentler and more poetic.
  • Speckled: Similar because both mean covered with small marks of another color; differs because speckled refers to many tiny, distinct dots, whereas mottled refers to larger, irregular blotches.
  • Spotted: Similar because both mean marked with colored spots; differs because spotted implies more regular, round, discrete spots and is the more general, everyday word.
  • Blotchy: Similar because both mean marked with uneven patches; differs because blotchy carries a negative, often medical or cosmetic tone (blotchy skin from rash or redness) and describes an unattractive unevenness.
  • Variegated: Similar because both mean multi-colored and patterned; differs because variegated is the elegant term used of deliberately patterned foliage and fabric, suggesting beauty rather than irregular wear.
  • Stippled: Similar because both mean covered with many small marks; differs because stippled specifically describes fine dots applied deliberately by an artist (stippling technique), implying craftsmanship rather than natural irregularity.
  • Marbled: Similar because both mean streaked with different colors; differs because marbled specifically describes wavy veins like those of marble, as in marbled meat, paper, or stone.
  • Flecked: Similar because both mean marked with small patches of color; differs because flecks are tiny, scattered light marks, smaller and sparser than mottling.
  • Brindled: Similar because both describe patchy animal coloring; differs because brindled is used of gray-or-tan streaking (cattle, dogs) with a streaky rather than blotchy pattern.
  • Pied / parti-colored: Similar because both describe patches of contrasting color; differs because pied specifies two-tone, blocky patches (magpie-like) and is a more literary, old-fashioned word.
  • Splotchy: Similar because both mean covered in irregular patches; differs because splotchy is informal and emphasizes large, messy smears.
