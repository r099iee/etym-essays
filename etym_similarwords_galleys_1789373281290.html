• Proofs: Similar because both refer to trial impressions of printed matter produced for inspection and correction before final publication; differs because proofs is an overarching generic category encompassing all stages of pre-press verification (including bluelines, page proofs, and digital press proofs), whereas galleys specifically denotes the early, unpaged, continuous-column strips before page imposition.
  • Advance Reader Copies (ARCs): Similar because both represent preliminary, pre-publication versions of a book circulated for editorial and promotional review; differs because ARCs are fully formatted, bound paperback copies distributed to critics and booksellers for marketing purposes, whereas galleys are raw, working editorial texts focused strictly on mechanical corrections.
  • Page Proofs: Similar because both are intermediary printing documents used to identify textual defects; differs because page proofs already reflect the final page dimensions, running headers, footnotes, illustrations, and index pagination, whereas galleys present text as an unbroken, continuous river devoid of pagination.
  • Placards: Similar because placard is the direct historical French printing counterpart to galley proofs, famously used by Proust; differs because in English placard predominantly denotes an affixed public poster or notice, whereas galleys retains its specific Anglo-American identity as a tray and proof slip.
  • Triremes: Similar because both describe ancient Mediterranean warships propelled by oarsmen; differs because triremes refers exclusively to classical Greek and Roman warships equipped with three stacked banks of oars and a bronze ram, whereas galleys is a broad, cross-epochal class spanning classical, medieval, and Renaissance oared vessels.
  • Cabooses: Similar because both have historical nautical associations with shipboard cooking spaces; differs because caboose evolved in North American railroading into the crew car at the end of a freight train, whereas galley remains the standard international maritime and aeronautical term for a vehicle's culinary compartment.
