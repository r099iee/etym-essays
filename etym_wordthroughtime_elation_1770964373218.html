- Latin "elatio" (a lifting up, carrying away) - Entered English as a bodily and mental metaphor of being raised or borne aloft, applicable to both pride and joy.
- 17th century usage - Ambivalent: "elation" of spirits could mean either exalted joy or inflated pride/conceit, with the negative nuance of being puffed up often dominant in moral writing.
- 18th-19th century shift - The sense narrows toward the positive: a swelling, buoyant joy rising from relief, triumph, or good fortune, leaving the prideful meaning behind.
- Contemporary sense (exhilarating, soaring happiness) - Purely positive in register, a formal and slightly romantic term for intense, uplifting happiness that feels like a psychological ascent above ordinary concerns.
