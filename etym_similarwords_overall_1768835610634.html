• uniform: Similar because both are full garments that dress a whole body for a role; differs because the uniform displays institutional rank and membership outward, while the overall conceals and protects privately.
  • apron: Similar because both are protective layers worn over ordinary clothes; differs because the apron guards only the front of the body, leaving the wearer's own clothes visible, while the overall engulfs the whole figure.
  • smock: Similar because both are loose work coveralls; differs because the smock belongs to the studio and carries a bohemian, aesthetic flavour, while the overall is the dust-grey, utilitarian livery of the custodian.
  • dungarees: Similar because both are heavy protective workwear; differs because dungarees are bib-and-brace trousers of farm and industry, while the overall here is the totalising one-piece suit — and, in Peake, the instrument of transformation into the ritual officer.
  • boiler suit: Similar because both are one-piece protective working garments; differs because the boiler suit stresses industrial, engine-room labour, while the overall belongs to the museum and the servant's ritual of dust.
  • coveralls: Similar because coveralls is the standard American one-piece protective suit, essentially the same garment as the British overall; differs because coveralls is strictly plural and strictly industrial, while overall also carries its adjective and adverb senses of totality.
  • frock: Similar because both are loose outer garments; differs because the frock is a dress or a loose smock-like garment without a protective function, and carries no connotation of guarding one's clothes from dirt or of work.
