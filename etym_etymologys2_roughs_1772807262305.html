DERIVATION CHAIN FOR ROUGHS: 1. Proto-Germanic *rukhaz (adjective, 'shaggy, rough'; reconstructed) [Germanic period, roughly 1st millennium BCE-early 1st millennium CE; no direct attestation] 2. West Germanic *rukhwaz (adjective, 'shaggy, hairy, rough'; reconstructed) [period of West Germanic unity, c. early 1st millennium CE; no direct attestation] 3. Old English rūh, stem rug- (adjective, 'not smooth to the touch; coarse of cloth; hairy, shaggy; of hides, undressed; of land, uncultivated') [attested 8th-11th c.] 4. Middle English rough / rugh / rouwe / roghe (adjective, 'uneven, harsh, unrefined') [attested 12th-15th c.] 5. Modern English rough (adjective, with extended senses 'coarse in manners or morals; approximate; stormy'; noun uses from c. 1600: rough ground, the golfing rough, and figuratively 'a rough estimate, a rough draft') [attested from the 14th c. onward; approximate and draft senses c. 1600-1690s] 6. English noun 'a rough' (substantivized adjective, 'a coarse or violent man; a rowdy') [attested from 1837] 7. Modern English plural ROUGHS (noun, 'violent, lawless men; thugs; street rowdies') [nineteenth century onward; the sense of the card] DEBATED / NUANCED NOTES: A higher Proto-Indo-European link for the Germanic 'rough' family is tentative and not fully settled; comparisons are sometimes offered with Sanskrit rūkṣa- 'rough, dry' and Latin ruere 'to rush, fall violently,' but these are not established cognates and should not be asserted as fact. The noun sense may also have been shaped by contact with ruffian (from Italian ruffiano via French, 'a brutal fellow'), which is phonetically close to rough; the Century Dictionary even wondered whether 'a rough' was partly an abbreviation of ruffian reshaped to match rough. The chain above therefore marks the Germanic stages as solid, the PIE attachment as open, and the ruffian influence as a plausible but unproven factor. This chain has 7 attested or reconstructed steps; none is invented, and the reconstructed Proto-Germanic and West Germanic stages are explicitly labeled as reconstructed.
