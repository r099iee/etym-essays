- Old English (c. 800–1100) - Recorded as *bræs*, denoting copper alloys, bronze, and metals used for church vessels and bells.
- Late Medieval (14th–15th Century) - Associated with monumental brasses and calamine-smelted sheet metal in architecture and furnishings.
- 16th–17th Century - Acquired figurative connotations of shamelessness, unblushing impudence, and audacity ('bold as brass', 'brass neck').
- 19th Century - Applied to the orchestral wind family (trumpets, horns, trombones) and adopted in British military slang for senior officers ('brass hats').
- 20th Century - Cemented in executive terminology ('top brass'), idioms ('brass tacks'), and Northern English dialect for money ('where there's muck there's brass').
