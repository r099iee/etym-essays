Root 1 — LUNGED:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*dlongʰ-
"long"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
longus
"long"
↓
French (17th c.)
allonger
"to lengthen; to thrust"
(fencing)
↓
English (18th c.)
lunge
"a sudden forward thrust
(as in fencing)"
↓
English (past tense)
lunged
"made a sudden forward movement"

Note: lunge entered English as a fencing term, from French allonger "to lengthen" (earlier spelled longe) — the thrust is an "extended" arm.

Root 2 — FENDED:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*gʷʰen-
"to strike, kill"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
fendere
"to strike"
(only in compounds)
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
dēfendere
"to ward off, defend"
(dē- "away" + fendere "to strike")
↓
Old French (11th c.)
defendre
"to defend, ward off"
↓
English (13th c.)
defend
"to ward off, protect"
↓
English (aphesis, 14th c.)
fend
"to ward off, keep off"
↓
English (past tense)
fended
"blocked or pushed away
an attack"

Note: Latin fendere is not attested on its own — only in compounds such as dēfendere and offendere. English fend is an aphetic (shortened) form of defend.
