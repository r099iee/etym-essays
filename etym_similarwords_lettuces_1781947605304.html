• Greens: Similar because both denote leafy vegetables eaten fresh; differs because greens is a broad hypernym covering kale, spinach, chard, and more, and often implies cooked or generic leafy matter, whereas lettuces names the specific genus and its individual garden plants.
  • Salad: Similar because lettuce is the archetypal salad ingredient; differs because salad is the prepared dish, not the living or harvested plant, and a salad may contain no lettuce at all.
  • Salad greens: Similar because both denote lettuce-family and lettuce-like leaves sold for salads; differs because salad greens is a culinary and marketing category that includes arugula, mesclun, and baby leaves alongside true lettuce.
  • Cabbage: Similar because both are dense-leafed garden vegetables; differs because cabbage is a Brassica with tightly packed, tougher leaves usually cooked, whereas lettuce is a Lactuca with loose, soft, milky-sapped leaves eaten raw.
  • Spinach: Similar because both are leafy greens used in salads; differs because spinach is a strongly flavoured, mineral-rich Amaranthaceae leaf, cooked or wilted more often than lettuce, and belongs to a different plant family.
  • Endive: Similar because both are salad plants of the daisy family; differs because endive is bitter, frilly, curly-leaved Cichorium endivia, whereas lettuce is mild and non-bitter.
  • Chicory: Similar because both are Asteraceae salad plants; differs because chicory refers to Cichorium intybus, a bitter relative used for roots, radicchio, and witloof, not the mild leaves of Lactuca.
  • Romaine / Cos: Similar because romaine is a variety of lettuce; differs because romaine/cos names one tall, crisp-headed type (the "Roman" lettuce of the Mediterranean), a subtype rather than the genus as a whole.
  • Iceberg: Similar because iceberg is a lettuce variety; differs because iceberg denotes the dense, pale, round crisphead that dominates supermarkets, not lettuce in general and not the garden rows of Potter.
  • Arugula / Rocket: Similar because both are salad leaves; differs because arugula is a peppery, pungent Brassicaceae leaf of a different family and flavour profile than the mild lettuce.
  • Leaves: Similar because lettuce is eaten leaf by leaf; differs because leaves is anatomical and partial, and Potter needs the whole cultivated plant as a unit of theft and appetite.
  • Vegetables: Similar because lettuce is a vegetable; differs because vegetables is a hypernym that erases the specific milky-sap plant and is too abstract for a child to picture in a garden bed.
  • Crop: Similar because lettuces are a garden crop; differs because crop is an agricultural term for a harvestable yield, losing the individual, countable heads Peter gobbles.
