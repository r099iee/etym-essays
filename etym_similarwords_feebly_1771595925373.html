• Weakly: Similar because it is the plainest synonym for "with little strength, lacking force," the neutral default for both physical and figurative feebleness; differs because weakly is uncoloured and matter-of-fact, whereas feebly carries a faint pathos and fragility, an echo of its Latin ancestor "lamentable, pitiable."
  • Faintly: Similar because it describes something barely present, done or perceived at the edge of perceptibility; differs because faintly points to the dimness of a stimulus (faint light, faint sound) and to the threshold of perception, while feebly points to the deficient force of an action or effort.
  • Limply: Similar because it describes action without force; differs because limply suggests flaccidity — the absence of tension or resistance, like a limp handshake — while feebly can describe an effort that is weak yet earnestly, even solemnly, intended.
  • Powerlessly: Similar because it names action without strength; differs because powerlessly names the absence of power or means, a matter of capability and helplessness, whereas feebly describes the quality of the effort itself, which may still be sincerely offered.
  • Ineffectually: Similar because it describes an effort that lacks force or result; differs because ineffectually is a result-oriented, clinical judgment (the effort fails to achieve its end), while feebly describes the weakness of the effort as such, regardless of its outcome.
  • Inadequately: Similar because it denotes insufficiency; differs because inadequately measures the effort against a required standard and is impersonal and administrative, while feebly is personal, humane, and faintly pitying.
  • Haltingly: Similar because it describes unsteady, faltering action; differs because haltingly stresses interruption and hesitation — starting and stopping — while feebly stresses sheer deficiency of force.
  • Falteringly: Similar because it describes action that wavers and loses strength; differs because falteringly emphasises the wavering or the losing of momentum mid-course, while feebly can describe a steady but weak effort.
  • Languidly: Similar because it describes slow, unenergetic action; differs because languidly suggests relaxed, dreamy, or indolent slowness — often attractive — while feebly suggests powerless frailty.
  • Frailly: Similar because it means "in a fragile, delicate manner," close in form and meaning to feebly; differs because frailly is rarer and applies chiefly to fragile bodies or things, while feebly ranges over efforts, arguments, light, and sound.
  • Fecklessly: Similar because it denotes a lack of effective power or responsibility; differs because feckless carries a moral note of irresponsibility or uselessness, whereas feebly is pitiable rather than blameworthy.
  • Indistinctly: Similar because it describes something perceived without clarity; differs because indistinctly is about the lack of definition in perception or utterance, not about the lack of strength in an effort.
