• Round: Similar because both mean circular or spherical in shape; differs because round is the general, all-purpose word for any circular form, while rotund specifically suggests a full, plump roundness, usually of a person's body, with a dignified or amused tone.
  • Plump: Similar because both describe a pleasantly full, round body; differs because plump is the warm, informal word for agreeable fullness (a plump cheek, a plump cushion), while rotund is more formal and literary, with a suggestion of dignity or humor.
  • Portly: Similar because both describe a large, round body with dignity; differs because portly emphasizes the stateliness and heavy build of age and office (a portly gentleman), while rotund emphasizes the roundness of outline itself and can be playful.
  • Stout: Similar because both describe a heavy, full-bodied person; differs because stout stresses solidness and strength of build rather than roundness, and can even be admiring, whereas rotund stresses the round contour and is more neutral-to-amused.
  • Chubby: Similar because both mean round and full; differs because chubby is informal and applies especially to children or cheeks, with a cute connotation, while rotund is formal and literary, applied to adults with dignity.
  • Corpulent: Similar because both describe a fat body; differs because corpulent is a formal, often clinical or disapproving word for bodily bulk, while rotund is affectionate and pictures a neat roundness rather than gross bulk.
  • Obese: Similar because both concern excess body weight; differs because obese is a medical term for severe overweight with health implications, while rotund is a mild, humorous, purely descriptive adjective for plumpness.
  • Bulbous: Similar because both describe a round, swelling shape; differs because bulbous applies to things (noses, roots, domes) and suggests an odd or grotesque swelling, while rotund is used of persons with dignity and a neat full roundness.
  • Spherical: Similar because both describe a round, ball-like form; differs because spherical is a precise geometric term for a perfect ball shape, with no application to persons, while rotund is human and descriptive.
  • Grandiloquent (for the speech sense): Similar because both can describe full, impressive speech; differs because grandiloquent means pompously high-flown language, while rotund speech is merely full-bodied and rounded, not necessarily pompous.
