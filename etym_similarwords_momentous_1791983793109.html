• important: Similar because it is the plain, general word for having consequence; differs because it is neutral in register and admits every scale, whereas momentous insists on gravity and large consequences.
  • significant: Similar because both point to consequences that matter; differs because significant can be technical and measurable (“a statistically significant difference”) and often means “meaningful” rather than “world-changing”.
  • consequential: Similar because it stresses results flowing from an event; differs in being cooler, more analytic and often legal or political, without the emotional weight of momentous.
  • weighty: Similar because it preserves the same metaphor of heaviness and importance; differs because weighty is more often applied to arguments, reasons and tomes than to events, and can mean simply “bulky” or “solemn”.
  • critical: Similar because a critical moment is a decisive one; differs because critical implies a point of danger or urgency at which something must be decided, whereas momentous may describe an event whose importance is only recognised afterwards.
  • crucial: Similar because both denote decisive importance; differs because crucial is about the hinge on which an outcome turns, is more colloquial, and is often used of small practical details.
  • pivotal: Similar because it describes the point on which everything turns; differs in being a spatial metaphor of a pivot, typically applied to a person, role or moment within a process rather than to the grandeur of the event.
  • historic: Similar because both are used of great public events; differs because historic asserts that something will be recorded in history, an act of commemoration, while momentous asserts that it has great effects.
  • epoch-making: Similar because it marks a change of era; differs in being more rhetorical and much rarer, almost always applied to discoveries, works or inventions rather than to occasions.
  • fateful: Similar because it suggests an event heavy with consequence; differs in carrying an ominous, often tragic colouring and the idea of destiny, which momentous does not.
  • portentous: Similar because it can mean “of great import”; differs because it primarily means “serving as an omen” and now usually implies pompous self-importance.
  • grave: Similar because both convey seriousness; differs because grave concerns danger, solemnity or moral weight, not historical scale.
