Note: Coined in 1516 by Sir Thomas More as the title of his sociopolitical dialogue Libellus vere aureus, nec minus salutaris quam festivus, de optimo reipublicae statu deque nova insula Utopia. More constructed the name from Ancient Greek οὐ (ou, ‘not, no’) and τόπος (topos, ‘place’, of uncertain Pre-Greek or Indo-European origin), appending the Latin country-naming suffix -ia to yield literally ‘Nowhere’ or ‘No-Place’. At the same time, More designed the name as a brilliant homophonic pun with Ancient Greek εὖ (eu, ‘well, good’), forming Eutopia (‘Good-Place’), demonstrating that this ideal society was simultaneously the best place and a place that exists nowhere in fallen human geography.
