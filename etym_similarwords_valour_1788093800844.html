• courage: Similar because the core meaning is identical — the ability to face danger; differs because valour is elevated, literary and strongly martial, while courage is the everyday neutral term covering all kinds of fear-facing.
  • bravery: Similar because both denote fearlessness in danger; differs because bravery is less formal and equally at home for civilians, whereas valour adds chivalric and military grandeur.
  • heroism: Similar because both denote exceptional courage; differs because heroism implies great deeds and self-sacrifice celebrated by others, while valour is the personal quality behind such deeds.
  • gallantry: Similar because both denote conspicuous courage, especially in battle and in honours language; differs because gallantry also means courtly attentiveness to women, a social sense valour never has.
  • prowess: Similar because both denote outstanding martial quality; differs because prowess emphasises skill and ability in arms, whereas valour emphasises moral courage.
  • intrepidity: Similar because both denote fearless boldness; differs because intrepidity is more intellectual and literary and stresses the absence of fear, while valour stresses strength of will.
  • fortitude: Similar because both denote courage; differs because fortitude is courage in enduring hardship and pain passively, while valour is active risk-taking in danger or battle.
  • mettle: Similar because both denote a person's innate courage or spirit (“show your mettle”); differs because mettle is capacity that may be tested, while valour is courage actually displayed.
  • doughtiness: Similar because both denote sturdy bravery; differs because doughtiness is archaic with a rustic flavour, whereas valour is courtly and refined.
  • pluck: Similar because both denote spirited courage; differs because pluck is informal, cheerful and small-scale, while valour is grand and formal.
  • nerve: Similar because both denote steadiness under danger; differs because nerve is colloquial and often appears negatively (“losing your nerve”), without the heroic dignity of valour.
  • daring: Similar because both denote bold risk-taking; differs because daring may be reckless or spectacular, whereas valour implies principled, honourable courage.
  • fearlessness: Similar because both denote courage; differs because fearlessness is descriptive and psychological — the absence of fear — with none of the honour-ethics that valour carries.
  • valiancy: Similar because it is formed from the same root and means the same thing; differs because valiancy is rare and nearly obsolete, while the common adjective is valiant and the noun is valour.
