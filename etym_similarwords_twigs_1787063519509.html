• Branches: Similar because both are woody appendages extending outward from a tree or shrub to support foliage; differs because *branches* are larger, structural divisions supporting multiple secondary shoots, whereas *twigs* are strictly the smallest, thinnest terminal extremities.
  • Boughs: Similar because both belong to the woody skeletal framework of an arboreal canopy; differs because *boughs* are massive, load-bearing primary limbs emanating directly from the tree trunk, representing the highest order of branch thickness, whereas *twigs* represent the lowest.
  • Sprigs: Similar because both denote tiny, slender offshoots of vegetation; differs because a *sprig* usually includes its attached leaves, herbs, or flowers and is typically gathered or plucked for decorative or culinary purposes (e.g., a sprig of rosemary), whereas *twig* emphasizes the bare, woody stem itself.
  • Sticks: Similar because both refer to slender pieces of wood; differs because a *stick* is usually a detached, dead, or fallen piece of wood used as a tool, cane, or fuel, whereas *twig* refers to a small shoot in its living botanical context on the branch or newly fallen.
  • Shoots: Similar because both represent small, nascent stems of a plant; differs because a *shoot* is a young, fresh, newly sprouted green growth produced during a single growing season, whereas a *twig* is a lignified, woody terminal branchlet.
  • Switch: Similar because both are slender, flexible woody stems from a bush or tree; differs because a *switch* is specifically cut and used as a flexible whip or rod for corporal discipline or driving livestock.
  • Offshoots: Similar because both describe lateral developments branching off a parent stem; differs because *offshoot* is commonly used in an abstract, institutional, or conceptual sense (e.g., an offshoot of a political party), whereas *twig* is predominantly physical and botanical.
