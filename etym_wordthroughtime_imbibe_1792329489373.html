- Late 14th to 15th Century — Alchemical & Medicinal Drenching: Entered English via medieval Latin and French alchemical treatises meaning to soak, steep, moisten, or saturate solid substances or chemical powders with liquid distillates.
- 16th to 17th Century — Literal Human Drinking & Moral Ingestion: Expanded in Early Modern English to human physical drinking of beverages (wine, ale) while simultaneously developing a vibrant metaphorical sense of drinking in moral principles, spiritual doctrine, or emotional passions.
- 18th to 19th Century — Polished Literary & Scientific Osmosis: Cemented in Enlightenment and Victorian prose as a distinguished term for intellectual enlightenment (imbibing knowledge, wisdom, classical culture) and botanical absorption of water by plant seeds and roots.
- 20th to 21st Century — Bifurcated High-Register & Social Euphemism: Functions today in dual distinct modes: an elevated literary verb for absorbing cultural or sensory input, and a playful, polite social euphemism for consuming alcoholic beverages at social gatherings.
