Note on philological debate regarding first element: Historical linguists (including Pierre Chantraine, Hjalmar Frisk, and Robert Beekes) have debated the precise origin of the initial element blas-. While traditional lexicography connected it directly to bláptō ("to damage, harm", hence "speech that causes harm"), modern comparative Indo-European philology favors a connection to Greek bláx ("sluggish, stupid, silly"), tracing to PIE *bʰleh₁- ("to babble, speak foolishly"). Under this prevailing view, blásphēmos originally signified "one who utters foolish, ill-omened babble" before shifting to malicious slander, and finally to divine sacrilege.
