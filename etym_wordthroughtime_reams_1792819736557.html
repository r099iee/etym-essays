- Arabic — the bundle: rizma means a bale or bundle, especially of paper — the warehouse word of the paper trade.
- Medieval France — the importer’s word: Old French rayme, raime names the paper bundle arriving from Arab papermakers through Mediterranean trade.
- 14th c. — England counts paper: Middle English reme enters with the paper trade itself, a wholesaler’s unit of twenty quires.
- Print era — the office standard: Caxton onward, printers buy by the ream, and the 480-sheet and 500-sheet standards settle the trade.
- 19th–20th c. — metaphor floods in: reams escapes the stationer’s and means any deluge of words — evidence, regulations, and Dora’s good advice.
- Today — paper fades, metaphor stays: offices buy less paper but write more words, so reams survives as the universal measure of too much text.
