CHAIN 1 — THE ELEMENT priest (a Greek and Latin word of the Christian church): Stage 1 — PIE *per- (also reconstructed as *pres-) “before, forward, at the head” (Pokorny *per- “hinaus, vorwärts, durch”; the root of Latin per, Greek pará and pró, English fore and first) — reconstructed, 4th–3rd millennium BCE. Stage 2 — PIE compound *pres-gʷu- “one who walks before the cattle; the leading elder, the senior” — *pres- “before” + *gʷou- “ox, cow, cattle” (Sanskrit gáuḥ, Greek boûs, Latin bōs, English cow). TRADITIONAL DERIVATION of Greek πρέσβυς (présbys); DEBATED — Beekes treats πρέσβυς as possibly Pre-Greek, so this stage is the traditional, not the certain, etymology. Stage 3 — Ancient Greek πρέσβυς (présbys) “old man, elder; also ambassador” (the elder as the one who goes first, at the head of the community; attested from Homer, 8th c. BCE). Stage 4 — Ancient Greek comparative πρεσβύτερος (presbýteros) “older, elder”; in Christian Greek the title of the appointed leaders of each church, “the elders” (New Testament: Acts 14:23; Titus 1:5; 1st c. CE). Stage 5 — Late Latin presbyter “elder, priest” (ecclesiastical loanword of the early Church, 2nd–3rd c. CE). Stage 6 — Vulgar Latin *prester (syncopated form of presbyter, late antique and early medieval). Stage 7 — Old English prēost “priest” (an early, pre-Norman borrowing of the syncopated form, 7th–8th c. CE; the native sācerd from Latin sacerdos was displaced by it). Stage 8 — Middle English preest → Modern English priest (12th–16th c.). FINAL NODE (with the suffix -hood) — PRIESTHOOD (priest + -hood, Middle English, 14th–15th c.). CHAIN 2 — THE SUFFIX -hood (a native Germanic abstract-forming element): Stage 1 — PIE *skei- “to cut, divide, separate” (PROPOSED root of the -hood suffix; DEBATED — the deeper PIE connection is uncertain, some deriving the suffix instead from a root for “kind, manner, way”; the Germanic stage below is secure, the PIE stage only probable). Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *haiduz “state, condition, manner, rank, degree” (reconstructed; cognate with Old Norse heiðr “honour” and German -heit as in Menschheit). Stage 3 — Old English hād “state, condition, rank, order, office, character” (attested from the 8th c. CE; used of the three orders of medieval society and of holy orders). Stage 4 — Middle English -hod(e) / -hed(e) “state, condition” (the abstract suffix, as in godhed “godhead”). Stage 5 — Modern English -hood, the abstract suffix of PRIESTHOOD. DEBATE NOTE: the traditional reconstruction *pres-gʷu- for Greek πρέσβυς is questioned by some modern scholars (Beekes), who suspect a Pre-Greek substrate origin; and the PIE stage of the -hood suffix rests on a proposed *skei- that is probable but not securely fixed. Both uncertainties are stated rather than hidden.
