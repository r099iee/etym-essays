• Drink: Similar because both mean taking liquid in; differs because drink is the neutral, general verb, with no implication of volume, relish, or ceremony.
  • Gulp: Similar because both involve large swallows; differs because a gulp is a single hurried, often involuntary swallow, frequently of nervousness, without the deliberate heartiness of a quaff.
  • Swig: Similar because both mean taking a large drink; differs because swig is quick, slangy, and utilitarian, often from a bottle, while quaff is slower, more ceremonial, and filled with relish.
  • Guzzle: Similar because both mean drinking large quantities; differs because guzzling adds careless excess and disapproval, while a quaffer stays hearty, joyful, and celebratory.
  • Swill: Similar because both mean drinking a lot; differs because swill is contemptuous, of animals or drunkards, sloppy and disgusting — the opposite of quaff's gusto.
  • Sip: Similar because both are ways of drinking; differs because sipping is delicate, small, and measured, the precise opposite of quaff's copious, noisy swallows.
  • Imbibe: Similar because both mean to drink; differs because imbibe is formal or humorous and can also mean absorbing ideas, while quaff is boisterous, physical, and purely about drinking deep.
  • Drain: Similar because both mean to empty a vessel; differs because drain stresses finishing the container to the last drop, with no necessary pleasure or sense of volume.
  • Down: Similar because both mean drinking quickly and fully; differs because down is slangy and abrupt, lacking quaff's old-fashioned heartiness.
  • Toast: Similar because both are festive drinking acts; differs because toasting is the formal act of drinking to someone's health, whereas quaffing is the drinking itself.
