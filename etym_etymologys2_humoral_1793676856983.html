Etymological Note on Orthography: The initial h- in Latin humor and English humoral is an unetymological, hypercorrect graphic addition that developed in Late and Medieval Latin under the influence of folk-etymological association with humus ("ground, earth") and humilis ("lowly"). Philologically, the classical Latin form is strictly ūmor, which descends directly from the zero-grade of PIE *wegʷ- ("wet, moist").
