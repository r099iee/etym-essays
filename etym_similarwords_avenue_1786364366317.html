• Boulevard: Similar because both denote broad, grand urban thoroughfares; differs because boulevard specifically originated from military ramparts (*bulwark*) and typically features wide median strips or landscaped walkways, whereas avenue began as an approach way to estates.
  • Thoroughfare: Similar because both refer to open passages or roads; differs because thoroughfare emphasizes unobstructed passage from one end to another without obstruction, lacking the grand aesthetic or metaphorical "method/channel" nuance of avenue.
  • Conduit: Similar because both can describe a metaphorical channel or pipeline through which things (trade, information) flow; differs because conduit emphasizes the physical or structural transmission channel (like a pipe or cable) rather than a pathway or method of approach.
  • Channel: Similar because both denote a means of access, communication, or commercial passage; differs because channel evokes fluid flow or formal bureaucratic routes ("diplomatic channels"), whereas avenue emphasizes an open direction of pursuit.
  • Course: Similar because both signify a path or route of progress toward an objective; differs because course emphasizes duration, sequence, or trajectory over time rather than a physical or metaphorical approach road.
  • Artery: Similar because both describe major transportation routes critical for commerce; differs because artery employs a biological metaphor emphasizing vital circulating flow and high traffic density.
  • Pathway: Similar because both denote a route leading toward a destination or outcome; differs because pathway carries connotations of a smaller, more intimate, or biochemical route rather than a broad, formal avenue.
