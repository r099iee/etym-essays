- 12th-13th c. - do on as two words - neutral everyday phrase for putting anything on, no special prestige
- 14th-15th c. - fused don - common verb for putting on clothes and armour, practical and unmarked
- 16th-18th c. - put on takes over ordinary speech - don drifts upward toward formal, military and ceremonial use, connotation of duty
- 19th c. - literary and academic flavour - Oxford, church, and antiquarian writers favour don for robes and dignities, slightly bookish
- Modern - technical plus comic-formal - safety manuals use don for PPE and uniforms as precise term; novelists use commenced to don for mock grandeur when describing ordinary dressing
