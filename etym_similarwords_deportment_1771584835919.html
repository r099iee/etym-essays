• Bearing: Similar because both mean the way the body is carried; differs because bearing is more static — a posture one has ("she has a dignified bearing") — while deportment is active and behavioural, a manner one maintains through deliberate conduct and long training.
  • Demeanor: Similar because both mean outward behaviour and self-presentation; differs because demeanor is broader, covering attitude, facial expression, and mood as well as carriage ("a calm demeanor"), whereas deportment focuses on physical conduct, posture, and trained carriage.
  • Comportment: Similar because both mean the manner of conducting oneself; differs because comportment, from Latin comportāre "to carry together," leans toward the moral and behavioural regulation of oneself and appears often in institutional or disciplinary contexts, whereas deportment is more conspicuously about the trained body.
  • Posture: Similar because both concern how the body is held; differs because posture is purely physical — the alignment of the spine and limbs — and lacks the social, moral, and trained dimensions that deportment carries.
  • Mien: Similar because both name the whole air of a person; differs because mien is a literary word for the total aspect — chiefly countenance and expression — while deportment is more active and behavioural, about carriage, walk, and conduct.
  • Carriage: Similar because both mean the way one carries the body; differs because carriage is almost entirely physical — how the body is held in motion — while deportment adds behaviour, manners, and the idea of deliberate training.
  • Conduct: Similar because both mean behaviour; differs because conduct is general behaviour and management of oneself in any sphere, moral or practical, without the specific physical, bodily focus of deportment.
  • Presence: Similar because both denote the impression a person makes; differs because presence emphasises the effect on others — charisma and force of personality — while deportment is the observable, trainable manner of carrying oneself.
