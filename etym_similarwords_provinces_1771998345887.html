• Regions: Similar because both name areas of a country or empire; differs because a region is a broader, looser geographic area without the Roman administrative or legal specificity of a province.
  • Territories: Similar because both are governed areas; differs because a territory is vaguer and often implies a land not yet fully incorporated, lacking the technical sense of a province as a magistrate's charge.
  • Colonies: Similar because both are lands under an empire; differs because a colony implies settlement by the metropolis's people, while a province was often a governed native people rather than a settler colony.
  • Dependencies: Similar because both are subordinate lands; differs because a dependency stresses subordination but loses the Roman office-holding sense of the prōvincia as a charge.
  • Dominions: Similar because both can name territories of an empire; differs because dominion is a later imperial term (often British) and blurs the classical administrative precision Gibbon inherits.
  • States: Similar because both are political divisions; differs because state imports modern constitutional sovereignty and federalism, whereas a province is subordinate to a central or imperial authority.
  • Departments: Similar because both are administrative divisions; differs because department suggests a modern, bureaucratic subdivision (as in France) without the Roman historical charge.
  • Counties: Similar because both are territorial units of government; differs because a county is a smaller local unit, typically English or American, not a great imperial province.
  • Prefectures: Similar because both are governed subdivisions; differs because a prefecture is defined by its governor (prefect) and is often a smaller or more centralized unit, without the broad imperial-civilizational sense.
  • Satrapies: Similar because both are administrative divisions of an ancient empire; differs because satrapy refers specifically to the Persian (Achaemenid) system of governors, not the Roman or modern province.
  • Districts: Similar because both are bounded administrative areas; differs because a district is usually smaller and more generic, lacking the constitutional or imperial dignity of a province.
  • Marches: Similar because both are border territories; differs because a march is a frontier defense zone, while provinces extend across the whole empire, settled and frontier alike.
  • Principalities: Similar because both are territories under rule; differs because a principality is a state or territory ruled by a prince, often sovereign, whereas a province is subordinate to a larger authority.
