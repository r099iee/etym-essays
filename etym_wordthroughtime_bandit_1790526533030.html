- 1590s–1650s: An officially banished person or proclaimed outlaw in Italian and Mediterranean contexts - perceived primarily through the lens of legal expulsion and judicial decree rather than ordinary criminality.
- late 17th–18th c.: An armed highwayman or brigand preying upon travelers in mountainous, lawless, or frontier regions - evocative of romantic danger, peril of travel, and romanticized rogue lore.
- 19th c.: A dangerous guerrilla fighter, insurgent, or member of an organized marauding band - widely used in military reports and colonial administration to delegitimize political rebels.
- 20th c. (Mid): Shift into colloquial, playful, and specialized idioms - such as a 'one-armed bandit' for slot machines or military/aviation tactical brevity code for confirmed enemy aircraft.
- 21st c.: Metaphorical application to predatory corporations, rogue financial actors, or unfair competitors ('corporate bandits'), alongside persistent core usage in crime fiction and geopolitical conflicts.
