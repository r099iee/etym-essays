• Vasari: Similar because both were sixteenth-century Italian painter-theorists who established foundational literature on Renaissance art; differs because Giorgio Vasari organized his work as biographical chronicles celebrating Tuscan-Florentine supremacy, whereas Lomazzo formulated an encyclopedic, academic system of aesthetic rules, astrological correspondences, and emotional expression (*moti*) championing Lombard and North Italian art.
  • Zuccari: Similar because Federico Zuccari was another premier late Mannerist painter-theorist who founded the Accademia di San Luca in Rome and wrote *L'Idea de' scultori, pittori e architetti* (1607); differs because Zuccari developed a Scholastic metaphysical theory of internal versus external design (*disegno interno* vs. *disegno esterno*), whereas Lomazzo created a cosmological, astrological system linking artistic temperament to planetary deities.
  • Cennini: Similar because both were Italian artists who documented artistic methods for subsequent generations; differs because Cennino Cennini wrote an early fifteenth-century craft handbook of practical workshop recipes (*Il Libro dell'Arte*), whereas Lomazzo wrote an encyclopedic, philosophical, and academic system of late Mannerist aesthetic rules and psychological temperaments.
  • Alberti: Similar because both formulated monumental theoretical treatises on painting, perspective, and proportion; differs because Leon Battista Alberti approached art through early fifteenth-century Euclidean geometry, optics, and humanist rhetoric, whereas Lomazzo codified late sixteenth-century Mannerist decorum, emotional expression (*moti*), and Neoplatonic planetary mysticism.
  • Bellori: Similar because Giovanni Pietro Bellori was another monumental Italian art theorist who codified idealist academic theory (*Le vite de' pittori, scultori et architetti moderni*, 1672); differs because Bellori wrote in the seventeenth-century Baroque era championing classicism against both Mannerism and Caravaggism, whereas Lomazzo was the quintessential voice of late Mannerist intellectualism.
  • Leonardo: Similar because Leonardo da Vinci was Lomazzo's primary Milanese inspiration whose anatomical and emotional theories of bodily gesture (*i moti mentali*) deeply influenced him; differs because Leonardo's insights were empirical, observational notebook fragments, whereas Lomazzo systematized Leonardo's observations into a dogmatic, academic curriculum.
