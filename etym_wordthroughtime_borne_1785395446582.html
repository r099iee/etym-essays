- [Old English beran] - The verb carries the full range from the start: to carry, to endure, to give birth — weight lifted and moved, suffering sustained, a child brought forth, all in one participle boren.
- [Middle English divergence: born vs borne] - The participle splits; born is restricted to birth ("he was born"), while borne keeps the whole remaining range — carried, transported, supported, endured.
- [Expansion of borne's constructions] - Borne settles into its full modern grammar: the perfect with have ("has borne the weight"), the passive with by ("borne by the wind"), and pregnancy ("has borne a child").
- [Modern register] - Borne is the elevated, faintly archaic participle — borne aloft, borne away by death — dignifying the carrying while emphasising the carrier's mastery; its tone is solemn, passive, final, the pure object of someone else's motion.
