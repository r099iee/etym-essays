• Tribesmen: Similar because the Typees were organized into a traditional kinship-based tribal society (*mata'eina'a*); differs because tribesmen is an abstract, universal sociological category applicable to kinship groups worldwide, whereas the Typees designates a specific, historically defined Polynesian tribal group native to Nuku Hiva.
  • Marquesans: Similar because the Typees are ethnically, culturally, and geographically Marquesan islanders; differs because Marquesans encompasses the entirety of the indigenous population across all twelve islands of the archipelago, whereas the Typees (Taipi) constituted a single specific valley clan and tribal confederacy residing in eastern Nuku Hiva.
  • Islanders: Similar because the Typees inhabited an isolated oceanic island in the South Pacific; differs because islanders is a generic geographical descriptor that conveys nothing regarding social structure, language, material culture, or historical reputation.
  • Hapa'a (Happars): Similar because the Hapa'a were an adjacent indigenous tribe on Nuku Hiva contemporaneous with the Typees, sharing language, customs, and material culture; differs because the Hapa'a inhabited a neighboring valley and were traditional military rivals of the Typees, historically perceived by early Western whalers as more accommodating than the warlike Taipi.
  • Polynesians: Similar because the Typees belong to the broader Polynesian ethnolinguistic family; differs because Polynesians encompasses millions of people across thousands of miles from Hawaii to New Zealand (Maori), representing an expansive macro-cultural family rather than an individual valley clan.
  • Indigenous people: Similar because both designate original inhabitants occupying ancestral territory prior to Western colonization; differs because indigenous people is a contemporary international human rights and legal collective term, whereas Typees is a specific historical-literary ethnonym.
