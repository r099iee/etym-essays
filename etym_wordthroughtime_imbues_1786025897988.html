- Classical Latin (1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE) - [Moistening & Dyeing Fabrics] - *Imbuere* meant literally to wet, soak, steep, or dye cloth, and secondarily to train or accustom a youth to a discipline.
- Late Middle English to Early Modern (15th–16th c.) - [Moral & Spiritual Inculcation] - Adopted directly from Latin to describe filling young minds with principles, religious doctrines, or virtues; retained a strong educational and theological flavor.
- 17th–18th Century (Enlightenment & Romanticism) - [Aesthetic & Emotional Saturation] - Shifted into literary and aesthetic philosophy, describing how poetry, landscape, or divine providence saturates the human soul with wonder, melancholy, or virtue.
- 19th–20th Century (Victorian to Modern Era) - [Pervasive Artistic & Cultural Infusion] - Broadened to describe political ideology, artistic tone, cinematic mood, or cultural traditions thoroughly permeating an institution or society.
- 21st Century (Contemporary) - [Abstract Characterization & High-Register Rhetoric] - Retained as an elevated verb for describing how technology, design, narrative, or leadership deeply colors the identity and spirit of an entity.
