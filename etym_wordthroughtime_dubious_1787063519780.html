- 1540s: borrowed from Latin "dubiosus" as a learned word meaning "doubtful, uncertain" — a neutral, objective register used of uncertain claims and outcomes.
- 17th century: extends to describe a person's attitude — "inclined to doubt, hesitating" — the word becomes more personal and psychological.
- 18th–19th centuries: acquires a moral shading — "of questionable character, suspect, untrustworthy" (dubious dealings, dubious reputation); neutral doubt hardens into distrust.
- 19th century: ironic and light-hearted uses bloom — the "dubious honour" or "dubious distinction" of being singled out for something awkward.
- 20th–21st centuries: everyday scepticism — "I'm dubious about that"; media language of "dubious claims," "dubious provenance," and "dubious statistics" makes it the standard flag for suspicious information.
