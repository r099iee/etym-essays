• shuffling: Similar in the dragging, sliding steps that never fully lift the feet; differs because shuffling is slow, weary, and neutral, without shambling’s loose-jointed, crooked awkwardness.
  • lumbering: Similar in heavy, ungainly motion; differs because lumbering implies the rolling weight of large beasts and bulk without crookedness, where shambling suggests ill-hung joints.
  • lurching: Similar in unsteady movement; differs because lurching is a rhythm of sudden sideways lapses and staggers, not a continuous shuffling gait.
  • scuttling: Similar in an odd, animal-like locomotion; differs because scuttling is fast, skittering, and crab-like with the body held low, where shambling drags loose-jointed and slovenly.
  • hobbling: Similar in an awkward walk; differs because hobbling is the uneven limp of an injured person, halting and painful, without shambling’s loose, crooked, forward energy.
  • slouching: Similar in a lax, drooping posture in motion; differs because slouching is a human posture of laziness or defiance that keeps the body upright, whereas shambling is a whole crooked, dragging gait.
  • staggering: Similar in unsteady, ungainly movement; differs because staggering is a reeling, lurching loss of balance, as from a blow or drink, not a continuous shuffling walk.
  • tottering: Similar in weak, unsteady walking; differs because tottering suggests feebleness and near-falling, where shambling is loose-jointed and slovenly but sustained.
  • waddling: Similar in an awkward, rolling gait; differs because waddling is the side-to-side rocking of ducks or heavy bodies, without the crooked, dragging shuffle of shambling.
  • clumping: Similar in heavy, ungainly steps; differs because clumping is the loud, weighty stamping of heavy feet, without the loose-jointed, crooked quality of shambling.
  • trudging: Similar in slow, weary walking; differs because trudging is a plodding, labouring progress, purposeful and tired, while shambling is crooked, slovenly, and faintly degenerate.
  • trundling: Similar in a clumsy, rolling progress; differs because trundling belongs to wheeled objects or heavy people moving heavily, not to crooked, shuffling limbs.
