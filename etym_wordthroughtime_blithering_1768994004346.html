- 18th c. — blither hardens into a distinct variant of blather (Middle English blathren); the participle blithering at first simply means 'babbling, driveling' — foolish talk, the sound of a mouth full of noise without thought.
- — Late 19th–early 20th c. — The decisive leap: in the collocation 'blithering idiot,' popularized by comic writing, the word stops describing talk and becomes a pure intensive — 'utter, complete, hopeless.' The babbling sense collapses into a single blast of contempt.
- — Contemporary: a contemptuous intensifier meaning 'complete, senseless' that can attach to anything idiotic — including, as in Maspic's usage, a sandstorm — grading the noun upward in extremity and downward in dignity.
