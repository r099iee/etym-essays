- Pre-1500s local names - island known by many riverine and coastal polities; outsiders say Brunei, Waruni, Klemantan, not one island name
- 1500s–1600s Portuguese Borneo - Europeans fix Borneo/Bornéu on maps for the whole island after encounters with Brunei, with exotic-spice connotations
- 1700s–1800s colonial Borneo - Dutch and British partition the name into spheres (Dutch Borneo, British North Borneo, Sarawak), shifting perception to possession and profit
- Early 1900s ethnographic Borneo - “natives of Borneo” become textbook primitives with digging sticks, longhouses, and headhunting in evolutionist writing
- 1940s–1960s wartime and nationalist Borneo - Sandakan marches, Confrontation, and decolonization recast Borneo as battlefield and new states (Sabah, Sarawak, Kalimantan)
- 1980s–present conservation Borneo - Heart of Borneo, orangutan, palm-oil, and haze debates make Borneo shorthand for threatened paradise and climate stakes
