• Joycean: Similar because both Andrei Biely and James Joyce revolutionized 20th-century literature through radical stylistic experimentation, mythic parallelism, rhythmic prose, and the apotheosis of the modern city (*Petersburg* and *Ulysses*); differs because Joycean prose is grounded in somatic earthiness, encyclopedic humanism, and secular linguistic parody, whereas Biely's prose is steeped in apocalyptic mysticism, anthroposophy, and Russian messianism.
  • Proustian: Similar because both Biely and Marcel Proust constructed monumental modernist masterpieces that dissected aristocratic and high-bourgeois society through complex psychological and temporal structures; differs because Proustian narrative focuses on involuntary memory, psychological introspection, and the delicate sensory retrieval of lost time, whereas Biely is propulsive, rhythmic, hallucinatory, and preoccupied with violent historical apocalypse.
  • Kafkaesque: Similar because both Biely and Franz Kafka portrayed nightmarish bureaucratic labyrinths, authoritarian patriarchs, and grotesque existential dread; differs because Kafkaesque evokes spare, unadorned, parable-like clarity in the face of illogical horror, whereas Biely deploys a dazzling, kaleidoscopic linguistic pyrotechnic display overflowing with color, meter, and sound.
  • Symbolist: Similar because Biely was the foremost theoretician and practitioner of the Russian Symbolist movement; differs because Symbolist is a broad artistic category encompassing European poets, painters, and playwrights (e.g., Mallarmé, Verlaine, Maeterlinck) who privileged suggestion over direct statement, whereas Biely represents a specific, highly individual Russian synthesis of mathematics, apocalyptic politics, and rhythmic prose fiction.
  • Modernist: Similar because Biely is classified among the supreme titans of the modernist movement; differs because modernist is a vast, multifaceted 20th-century umbrella term covering cubism, futurism, imagism, and stream-of-consciousness, whereas Biely denotes the concrete authorial voice, historical individual, and distinct rhythmic prose architecture of Boris Bugaev.
  • Gogolian: Similar because Biely considered Nikolai Gogol his supreme literary master, inheriting Gogol's grotesque humor, hallucinatory St. Petersburg topography, and frenzied syntactic rhythms; differs because Gogol operated within 19th-century romantic realism and Christian moral anguish, whereas Biely fused Gogolian stylistic exuberance with 20th-century avant-garde poetics, anthroposophy, and revolutionary upheaval.
