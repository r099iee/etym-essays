• Hostility: Similar in denoting active opposition or ill-will; differs because hostility can be momentary or one-sided and is often used of actions, while enmity is the mutual, durable state of being enemies.
  • Animosity: Similar in denoting strong ill-feeling; differs because animosity is passionate, energetic, and personal, hotter but less institutional than enmity.
  • Antagonism: Similar in meaning opposition; differs because antagonism is intellectual and structural — opposition in principle — closer to rivalry than to the declared, mutual enmity of enemies.
  • Antipathy: Similar in being a dislike or aversion; differs because antipathy is instinctive and temperamental, not the declared conflict enmity implies.
  • Hatred: Similar in being a feeling of intense ill-will; differs because hatred is the strong emotion, while enmity is the state of being enemies that may contain or institutionalize that emotion.
  • Rancor: Similar in denoting deep-seated ill-will; differs because rancor is bitter, brooding, long-nourished resentment — an emotional quality — while enmity is a settled state of mutual opposition.
  • Resentment: Similar in being a hostile feeling; differs because resentment is a personal feeling of grievance about an injury, while enmity is the mutual state of being enemies.
  • Feud: Similar in being a state of hostility; differs because a feud is the concrete, ongoing quarrel — a sequence of vengeful acts — while enmity is the underlying state a feud expresses.
  • Bad blood: Similar in denoting long-standing ill-feeling; differs because bad blood is informal and suggests accumulated grievance, while enmity is formal and denotes the state of being enemies.
  • Ill will: Similar in meaning hostile feeling; differs because ill will is a milder, more passive disposition of unfriendliness, while enmity implies active, declared opposition.
  • Opposition: Similar in meaning being against someone or something; differs because opposition is neutral and can be peaceful or principled, while enmity names hostility between enemies.
  • Conflict: Similar in denoting a clash of interests or forces; differs because conflict is the active struggle itself, while enmity is the state of hostile relations that may or may not erupt into conflict.
