- [Vedic & Pre-Epic Period (c. 1000–500 BCE)] — functioned primarily as a descriptive Sanskrit compound adjective (su- + tīkṣṇa) meaning "exceedingly sharp, pungent, highly acute, or severely burning", applied to physical weaponry, fire, and medicinal herbs - [perception: descriptive, physiological, denoting piercing sharpness or burning intensity]
- [Epic Period: Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇyakāṇḍa (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)] — canonized as the personal name of the venerable forest anchorite and disciple of Agastya dwelling in the Daṇḍaka wilderness, greeting Rāma with radiant humility and total surrender of ascetic merit - [perception: revered as the quintessential forest saint, harmonizing severe meditative discipline with serene devotion]
- [Classical Sanskrit & Metaphysical Canon: Yoga Vāsiṣṭha (c. 6th–11th c. CE)] — elevated into the primary philosophical interlocutor who receives the profound non-dual teachings of sage Vasiṣṭha via Agastya, symbolizing the awakened seeker - [perception: celebrated as an earnest philosopher and ideal recipient of Vedāntic illumination]
- [Medieval Bhakti Period: Tulsīdās's Rāmcaritmānas (16th c. CE)] — transformed into the supreme paradigm of ecstatic, trance-like surrender (*prema-bhakti*), running barefoot through the forest in blissful disarray upon Rāma's approach - [perception: iconic archetype of selfless devotional ecstasy melting ascetic severity]
- [Modern Contemporary & Ayurvedic Usage (20th–21st c.)] — permanently preserved in Indian cultural memory as the devoted sage of the epic, while in Ayurvedic and botanical terminology continuing to denote penetrating, highly acute medicinal potency - [perception: sacred epic identity paired with technical pharmacological terminology]
