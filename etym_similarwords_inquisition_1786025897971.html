• Interrogation: Similar because both involve rigorous questioning; differs because interrogation is typically conducted by law enforcement as a neutral procedure, while inquisition carries dramatic, ideological, and intrusive overtones.
  • Inquest: Similar because both are formal official inquiries; differs because an inquest is a specific legal investigation into a death, whereas an inquisition suggests relentless interrogation or a broad inquiry.
  • Investigation: Similar because both seek facts; differs because investigation is neutral, systematic, and unmarked, while inquisition implies aggressive or prejudged questioning.
  • Cross-examination: Similar because both involve challenging questioning; differs because cross-examination is a specific, rule-bound courtroom procedure, while inquisition extends to any domain and carries no procedural guarantee of fairness.
  • Grilling: Similar because both mean intense questioning; differs because grilling is informal, everyday slang, whereas inquisition is formal, literary, or dramatic in tone.
  • Third degree: Similar because both describe harsh, coercive questioning; differs because third degree is a slang term for police brutality, while inquisition has historical and religious resonance.
  • Probing: Similar because both involve deep questioning; differs because probing is a gentle, exploratory, and neutral word, whereas inquisition is confrontational and judgmental.
  • Witch-hunt: Similar because both describe a zealous search for offenders; differs because a witch-hunt emphasizes the persecution of innocent targets, while an inquisition emphasizes the severity of the questioning itself.
  • Examination: Similar because both involve questioning or inspection; differs because examination is a plain, neutral term for any test or inspection, while inquisition is loaded with connotations of hostility and abuse of power.
