- Late Latin mortificare - to make dead, to deaden; patristic and monastic, theological
- 14th c. surgical mortification - dying tissue, gangrene; bedside and battlefield, feared and often fatal
- 16th–17th c. to mortify pride - to deaden arrogance by rebuke or discipline; moral and evangelical
- 18th–19th c. mortifying as shame (Ruskin's era) - social humiliation as the common sense alongside gangrene; a crushed leg mortifies and a schoolboy is mortified
- Modern split - medicine keeps gangrenous/necrotic, everyday speech keeps mortifying = humiliating; the corpse-word lives on as a blush
