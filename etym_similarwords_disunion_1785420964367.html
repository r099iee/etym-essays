• Division: Similar because both name the state of being split into parts; differs because "division" is neutral and can describe any split, even an intentional and orderly one (division of labour), whereas "disunion" implies the rupture of something previously one.
  • Schism: Similar because both name a rupture within a formerly unified body; differs because "schism" is a formal, often doctrinal split within a church or organized body with ecclesiastical overtones, while "disunion" is the general state of internal division.
  • Separation: Similar because both denote a parting of what was joined; differs because "separation" is a concrete, sometimes amicable parting, while "disunion" carries the sense of discord and of a damaged whole.
  • Alienation: Similar because both involve separation from a former whole; differs because "alienation" stresses the subjective feeling of estrangement, while "disunion" emphasizes the objective fracture of a previously unified entity.
  • Discord: Similar because both point to conflict and disagreement; differs because "discord" describes clashing voices or opinions in active opposition, while "disunion" names the condition of being severed or divided.
  • Dissolution: Similar because both describe a structure coming apart; differs because "dissolution" implies the complete breaking of a structure into its components, whereas "disunion" keeps the sense of a unity damaged but still recognizable.
  • Disunity: Similar as the near-twin formed on "unity"; differs because "disunity" is the plainer, more abstract noun for lack of unity, while "disunion" retains the historical and political weight of an undone Union.
  • Rupture: Similar because both name a decisive breaking; differs because "rupture" is the tearing event itself, often sudden and dramatic, whereas "disunion" is the resulting state or condition.
  • Fragmentation: Similar because both denote the breaking of a whole into pieces; differs because "fragmentation" suggests many scattered pieces and a shattering, while "disunion" typically pictures parts that have fallen out of relation.
  • Estrangement: Similar because both involve loss of a former bond; differs because "estrangement" stresses the emotional and relational distance, while "disunion" stresses the structural fact of having come apart.
  • Split: Similar because both name a division into parts; differs because "split" is casual and often refers to a single action or opinion-divide, while "disunion" is formal and describes an enduring state.
  • Secession: Similar because both name the withdrawal of a part from a larger whole; differs because "secession" is the specific political act of a state leaving a federation, while "disunion" is the broader condition of which secession is one instance.
