• Consecrated: Similar because [both describe being dedicated or set apart as holy]; differs because [consecrated broadly covers the dedication of places and objects (a church, a chalice) as well as persons, while anointed specifically implies the ritual smearing of oil and personal investiture of authority].
  • Ordained: Similar because [both describe being formally invested with sacred office]; differs because [ordained stresses official institutional decree and holy orders conferred by a church hierarchy, whereas anointed evokes a mystical, divine, or royal election that may bypass administrative procedure].
  • Sanctified: Similar because [both describe being made holy]; differs because [sanctified centres on moral purification and being rendered free from sin, while anointed centres on functional empowerment and endowment with authority or blessing].
  • Blessed: Similar because [both describe the conferral of divine favour]; differs because [blessed is a general word for any divine favour or happiness, while anointed names the specific ritual act of consecration by oil].
  • Hallowed: Similar because [both describe something held sacred]; differs because [hallowed describes the state of being revered as holy (hallowed ground, hallowed name), while anointed describes the act of marking a person or object with oil for office].
  • Designated: Similar because [both describe being officially chosen for a role]; differs because [designated is a neutral administrative term entirely without sacred solemnity, while anointed carries religious or dynastic prestige].
  • Christened: Similar because [both involve ritual naming and initiation, and Christened is etymologically "anointed with chrism"]; differs because [christened specifically means baptized and given a name in Christian ceremony, while anointed means consecrated with holy oil for office or healing].
  • Chrismated: Similar because [both involve the application of consecrated oil (chrism)]; differs because [chrismated is the technical liturgical term, chiefly in Eastern Christianity, for the sacrament of confirmation, whereas anointed is the general word covering coronations, ordinations, healing, and figurative designation].
  • Dedicated: Similar because [both describe being set apart for a purpose]; differs because [dedicated can be entirely secular (a dedicated team, a dedicated machine) and implies commitment rather than sacred investiture, while anointed implies consecration by ritual].
  • Invested: Similar because [both describe being formally endowed with authority or office]; differs because [invested is the institutional, ceremonial term for installing someone in an office (investiture), while anointed specifically involves the unction of oil and divine or dynastic sanction].
  • Empowered: Similar because [both describe being given the authority to act]; differs because [empowered is a secular term for granting capacity or rights, while anointed carries sacred or quasi-sacred selection for a mission].
  • Appointed: Similar because [both describe being selected for a position]; differs because [appointed is the ordinary, bureaucratic word for filling a post, entirely lacking the divine or ritual character of anointed].
  • Set apart: Similar because [both describe being separated out for a special role]; differs because [the idiom set apart is neutral and can be used of anything isolated, whereas anointed implies consecration and visible marking with oil].
  • Selected: Similar because [both describe being chosen from among candidates]; differs because [selected is a purely neutral, evaluative word for any choice, while anointed implies a pre-ordained, favoured, or ritually consecrated choice].
