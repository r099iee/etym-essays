Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*dis- + *lewh₁- / *lewH-
"apart, asunder" + "to loosen, untie"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*se-luwō
"to loosen, release, untie"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
solvere
"to untie, loosen, dissolve, pay"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
dissolvere (pp. dissolūtus)
"to loosen apart, unloose,"
"melt, break up, destroy"
↓
Old French (c. 12th c.)
dissoudre (pp. dissout)
"to dissolve, liquefy, melt"
↓
Middle English (c. 1380)
dissolven (pp. dissolved)
"to melt, loosen, break up"
↓
Modern English (c. 16th c.)
dissolved
"liquefied, disintegrated,"
"terminated, disbanded"

Note: Latin dissolvere compounds distributive prefix dis- ('apart') with solvere ('to loosen', source of solve, solution). English regularized the weak past participle dissolved, while Latin past participle dissolutus was borrowed separately as dissolute.
