- 1630s–1700s: Literal architectural, naval, and engineering descriptor for any building or framework raised above the ground, foundations, or deck of a vessel.
- 18th–19th c. (Early Metaphor): Extended into political philosophy and legal theory to describe social systems, governments, and legal codes built on human nature or custom.
- 1859–20th c. (Marxist Historical Materialism): Karl Marx (Überbau translated into English as superstructure) popularized the term as the ideological, legal, religious, and political apparatus determined by the material base (Basis).
- mid-20th c. (Western Marxism & Cultural Theory): Antonio Gramsci, Louis Althusser, and Raymond Williams redefined superstructures as sites of ideological struggle, cultural hegemony, and relative autonomy.
- 21st c.: Used across structural engineering (bridges, ships, high-rises), critical theory, digital architecture (meta-frameworks built atop base protocols), and literary analysis.
