- [Early Vedic Period, c. 1500–1000 BCE] — Identified in the *Ṛgveda* as the primary seer (*draṣṭā*) and clan-founder of the sixth Maṇḍala, composed of 76 sublime hymns invoking Agni, Indra, and the Maruts — [perception: revered as an inspired primordial prophet-singer possessing direct visionary communion with the gods]
- [Late Vedic & Brāhmaṇa Era, c. 1000–600 BCE] — Celebrated in the *Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa* as the insatiable student of sacred wisdom who spent three full human lifespans studying the Vedas, whereupon Indra granted him a fourth lifespan, teaching that knowledge is infinite like mountains — [perception: held up as the supreme exemplar of tireless, lifelong intellectual and spiritual learning]
- [Epic Period, c. 500 BCE–200 CE] — Centralized in the *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa* as the grand anchorite of Prayāga who graciously guides Rāma to Mount Citrakūṭa and later stages a miraculous, paradisiacal feast for Prince Bharata and the army of Ayodhyā; and in the *Mahābhārata* as the father of Droṇācārya — [perception: venerated as a benevolent sage-host of boundless spiritual power and father of legendary warriors]
- [Classical & Purāṇic Era, c. 300–1200 CE] — Standardized as one of the eternal Saptarṣis who preserve cosmic balance across world-ages (*manvantaras*), and established as the eponymous ancestor of the prestigious *Bhāradvāja Gotra*, one of the most widespread Brahmanical lineages in India — [perception: venerated as a foundational patriarchal ancestor and spiritual patron of countless priestly families]
- [Modern Era, 19th–21st c.] — Revered in Hindu culture, pilgrimage traditions at Prayagraj (Allahabad), and Indological scholarship as one of the greatest civilizational architects of Vedic poetry and ethical hospitality — [perception: celebrated as an eternal symbol of Sanskrit scholarly heritage and majestic spiritual hospitality]
