• Gruff: Similar because it describes a rough, blunt, or irritable manner; differs because gruff is purely about a hoarse, curt, or brusque way of speaking, with no baked-crust image and no implied tender interior.
  • Irritable: Similar because both name easily provoked annoyance; differs because irritable is a general, clinical disposition of quick temper, while crusty is warmer and implies an affectionate, shell-like gruffness.
  • Surly: Similar because both describe a rude, unfriendly manner; differs because surly is more hostile and gloomy, with no hint of hidden kindness, whereas crusty usually preserves a lovable core.
  • Cantankerous: Similar because both name an ill-tempered, quarrelsome person; differs because cantankerous is actively argumentative and peevish, while crusty is more about brusque surface manner than active quarrelsomeness.
  • Curmudgeonly: Similar because both describe a grumpy, cross old person; differs because curmudgeonly implies a habitual, miserly, or genuinely sour disposition, whereas crusty often carries affectionate, good-humoured warmth beneath the shell.
  • Testy: Similar because both describe easy irritation; differs because testy suggests petulant, touchy impatience in the moment, while crusty suggests a settled, dignified gruffness.
  • Cross: Similar because both mean bad-tempered; differs because cross is a mild, everyday peevishness, especially of children, lacking the aged, weathered flavour of crusty.
  • Grouchy: Similar because both describe a complaining, irritable temper; differs because grouchy is informal and mood-based (a bad day), while crusty is more of a fixed, characterful trait.
  • Prickly: Similar because both describe a person who is easily offended or sharp in manner; differs because prickly suggests touchiness and defensiveness that readily turns to irritation, while crusty suggests a tougher, dryer shell of gruffness.
  • Abrasive: Similar because both describe an irritatingly rough manner; differs because abrasive names the grating effect on others and carries clearer criticism, while crusty is far more tolerant and often affectionate.
  • Brusque: Similar because both describe abrupt, curt behaviour; differs because brusque is purely about blunt efficiency of speech, with no age, shell, or hidden warmth implied.
  • Encrusted: Similar because both describe a hard outer layer; differs because encrusted means covered over with a deposit (dirt, jewels, barnacles), while crusty names the resulting hard, crisp surface itself.
  • Hardened: Similar because both can mean made hard on the outside; differs because hardened often implies a person made callous or insensitive by experience, whereas crusty keeps the image of a mere surface.
  • Rough-edged: Similar because both describe an unpolished, blunt outer manner; differs because rough-edged is about lack of refinement generally, while crusty specifically evokes a hard shell over softness.
