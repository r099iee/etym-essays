• Bactria: Similar because both refer to the exact same ancient geographical realm situated between the Hindu Kush and the Oxus River; differs because Bactria is the classical Greco-Roman name (from Greek Baktrianḗ), whereas Bāhlīka is the ancient Indo-Aryan Sanskrit name carrying specific epic, dynastic, and Purāṇic literary resonances.
  • Balkh: Similar because both denote the ancient oasis metropolis that served as the heart of the region; differs because Balkh is the modern Persian/Islamic name of the city in northern Afghanistan, whereas Bāhlīka designates the entire ancient kingdom and its associated epic civilization.
  • Kamboja: Similar because both were celebrated northwestern kingdoms famous for elite cavalry warhorses in Sanskrit epics; differs because Kamboja was located slightly further south and east in the Pamir-Badakhshan-Hindu Kush mountain passes, whereas Bāhlīka was situated in the rich agricultural river plains of Bactria proper across the Oxus.
  • Gandhara: Similar because both were prominent northwestern realms on the Uttarāpatha trade highway; differs because Gandhara was centered in the Peshawar Valley and Taxila on the upper Indus River within the Indian cultural sphere, whereas Bāhlīka was a Trans-Hindukush Iranian/Central Asian territory.
  • Transoxiana: Similar because both describe regions north of the Hindu Kush in Central Asia; differs because Transoxiana is a broad regional geographic term meaning "the land beyond the Oxus River" (incorporating ancient Sogdiana and Uzbekistan), whereas Bāhlīka was centered specifically around the southern Oxus basin and Balkh.
  • Sogdiana: Similar because both were neighboring Iranian civilizations along the Silk Road; differs because Sogdiana lay to the north between the Oxus and Jaxartes rivers (centered on Samarkand and Bukhara), whereas Bāhlīka (Bactria) lay directly to its south.
  • Tokharistan: Similar because both designate the Bactrian territory; differs because Tokharistan is the later medieval name for the region (arising after the migration of the Tokharian / Yuezhi nomads who founded the Kushan Empire), whereas Bāhlīka is the ancient Vedic and Epic designation.
  • Uttarapatha: Similar because both are intimately linked to northern Indian geography; differs because Uttarapatha designates the vast transcontinental "Northern Highway" and northern trade zone spanning from Bengal to Central Asia, whereas Bāhlīka was a specific destination kingdom along that route.
  • Parthia: Similar because both were eastern Iranian powers famous for equestrian warfare and archery; differs because Parthia lay to the west in northeastern Iran (Khorasan), whereas Bāhlīka was situated further east in Bactria.
  • steppes: Similar because both provided horse-breeding environments; differs because the steppes refer to the boundless, arid grasslands of Eurasia inhabited by nomadic pastoralists, whereas Bāhlīka was an intensely cultivated, urbanized oasis civilization fed by river irrigation.
  • Khorasan: Similar because Balkh became a primary capital of historic Khorasan; differs because Khorasan is an Islamic medieval geographical designation encompassing northeastern Iran, northern Afghanistan, and southern Turkmenistan, whereas Bāhlīka is the ancient pre-Islamic classical name.
  • Aria: Similar because both were eastern satrapies of ancient Persian and Hellenistic empires; differs because Aria was centered around the Hari River valley (modern Herat in western Afghanistan), whereas Bāhlīka was centered further north at Balkh.
