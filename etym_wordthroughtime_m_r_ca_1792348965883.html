- [Epic Sanskrit Period: Valmiki Ramayana, c. 500 BCE–200 CE] — introduced as a formidable rakshasa sorcerer, son of Sunda and Tataka, who attacks Vishvamitra's ashram, is hurled across the ocean by Rama's blunt Manavastra arrow, and later acts as the reluctant golden deer (maya-mriga) under Ravana's death threats - [perception: a cunning demon who experiences traumatized awe of Rama, serving as a philosophical voice warning against unrighteousness]
- [Classical Sanskrit Kāvya & Drama, c. 300–800 CE: Kalidasa, Bhavabhuti] — elevated in classical literature (such as Kalidasa's Raghuvamsha) as the supreme aesthetic symbol of seductive, fatal sensory deception - [perception: the poetic archetype of the golden snare whose glittering exterior hides mortal danger]
- [Medieval Bhakti Traditions, c. 1100–1600 CE: Kamban, Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas] — reinterpreted through the lens of devotional bhakti theology: Maricha is portrayed as inwardly enlightened, consciously choosing to die by Rama's blessed arrow so as to achieve immediate spiritual liberation (moksha) - [perception: a tragic, noble devotee trapped in demonic karma who attains salvation at the hands of God]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era] — celebrated in Ramlila performances, folklore, and Indian literature as a proverbial figure representing deceptive illusions, tragic entrapment between two evils, or the fateful lure of glittering mirages - [perception: an enduring cultural symbol of the danger of material covetousness and the power of divine destiny]
