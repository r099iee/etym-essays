PRECIPICE

PIE (prehistoric)
*kaput
"head"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
caput
"head"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
praeceps
"headlong, falling headfirst; steep"
(prae- "before, forth" + caput "head")
↓
Latin (Late Latin)
praecipitium
"a steep place, precipice"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
precipice
"a steep cliff, precipice"
↓
English (16th c.)
precipice
"a very steep rock face or cliff"
Note: praeceps literally means "falling headfirst"; the sense "steep place" develops in Late Latin praecipitium.

SHEERLY

Old English (uncertain)
scīr
"bright, clear, pure"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
schere
"clear, sheer, pure"
↓
English (17th c.)
sheer
"steep, vertical; complete, absolute"
↓
English
sheerly
"in a complete, absolute,
or vertical manner"
Note: the origin of sheer is debated — it may come from OE scīr "bright, clear" or from an Old Norse word; the "steep, vertical" sense is a later English development.
