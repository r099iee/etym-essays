• Gratification: Similar because both refer to the satisfaction of desires and pleasure; differs because gratification names the psychological feeling of being satisfied, while indulgence names the act of yielding and relaxing discipline before the satisfaction.
  • Leniency: Similar because both involve soft, tolerant treatment of faults or wishes; differs because leniency specifically implies mercy or mildness in judgment (a judge shows leniency), while indulgence implies actively granting favors or pleasures.
  • Pampering: Similar because both describe treating someone or oneself with special care and luxury; differs because pampering is colloquial and physical (baths, massages), while indulgence carries theological, historical, and ethical weight.
  • Coddling: Similar because both describe over-protecting and yielding to someone; differs because coddling carries a strongly negative connotation of making someone weak and fragile through excessive care, whereas indulgence can also describe a harmless treat.
  • Hedonism: Similar because both involve the pursuit of pleasure; differs because hedonism is a systematic philosophical doctrine that pleasure is the highest good, while indulgence refers to individual acts of yielding to desire.
  • Extravagance: Similar because both involve going beyond reasonable limits, especially in spending; differs because extravagance highlights excess beyond financial or decorative bounds, while indulgence highlights the satisfying of emotional or sensory appetites.
  • Dispensation: Similar because both are official exemptions or remissions granted by authority; differs because dispensation is a formal administrative or canonical release from an obligation, while indulgence in its theological sense remits the punishment of an already-forgiven sin.
  • Forbearance: Similar because both describe patient tolerance; differs because forbearance means refraining from enforcing a right or punishment (holding back), while indulgence means actively granting a pleasure or exemption.
  • Permissiveness: Similar because both describe allowing freedom beyond strict rules; differs because permissiveness is a neutral sociological term for lax rules, while indulgence is personal, emotional, and often directed at specific desires.
  • Self-gratification: Similar because both describe pleasing oneself without restraint; differs because self-gratification is clinical and impersonal, while indulgence is warmer and can also mean indulging others, not only oneself.
  • Treat: Similar because both can name something pleasurable enjoyed beyond strict need; differs because treat is the plain, casual word for a special pleasure, while indulgence keeps a hint of moral or sensory excess.
  • Luxury: Similar because both describe expensive, sensual pleasures; differs because luxury is an economic and material category, while indulgence describes the psychological act of yielding to that luxury.
  • Pardon: Similar because both can mean official forgiveness or remission; differs because pardon forgives the guilt of an offense itself, while an indulgence remits only the temporal punishment after guilt is already forgiven.
