• ketchup: Similar because it denotes the exact same tomato-based, vinegar-sweetened table condiment; differs because 'ketchup' is the globally dominant commercial and lexical form (standardized primarily by Heinz), whereas 'catsup' is increasingly seen as an archaic, regional, or brand-specific variant.
  • catchup: Similar because it is an early phonetic variant of the same Asian loanword used for savory table sauces; differs because 'catchup' is now virtually obsolete in contemporary English, preserved only in historical literature and archaic culinary texts.
  • relish: Similar because both are cooked, seasoned table condiments applied to meats and sandwiches to add acidity and brightness; differs because relish possesses a chunky, coarse texture made primarily of minced pickled cucumbers or vegetables rather than a smooth, puréed liquid emulsion.
  • condiment: Similar because catsup belongs directly to this broad category of meal accompaniments; differs because 'condiment' is a broad hypernym encompassing mustard, mayonnaise, hot sauce, and salt, lacking catsup's specific sweet-tangy flavor profile.
  • chutney: Similar because both condiments combine cooked fruits or vegetables with vinegar, sugar, and warming spices to create a sweet, savory, and acidic relish; differs because chutney originated in the Indian subcontinent, typically features visible fruit pieces and complex spice profiles (like ginger, cumin, and chili), and retains a chunkier, jam-like consistency.
  • barbecue sauce: Similar because both are thick, sweet, tomato-based sauces seasoned with vinegar, brown sugar, and spices; differs because barbecue sauce incorporates molasses, liquid smoke, garlic, and Worcestershire sauce, expressly formulated for basting and smoking meats rather than serving as a universal dipping sauce.
  • Worcestershire sauce: Similar because both descended from colonial trade attempts to replicate fermented, umami-rich Asian fish or mushroom seasoning liquors; differs because Worcestershire remains a thin, dark, highly fermented liquid containing anchovies, tamarind, and malt vinegar, functioning as a cooking condiment rather than a thick table spread.
  • sauce: Similar because catsup is fundamentally a prepared liquid or semi-solid accompaniment for food; differs because 'sauce' is an overarching culinary classification that includes hot culinary reductions (such as béchamel, hollandaise, or velouté) served hot as integral components of a dish rather than a shelf-stable, ambient table condiment.
