Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*lewbʰ-
"leaf, peel, foliage, bark"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*laubijō-
"shelter made of leaves, leafy bower"
↓
Frankish (c. 6th c. CE)
*laubja
"arbor, bower, lodge"
↓
Old French (c. 12th c.)
loge
"arbor, hut, cabin, shelter"
↓
Old French (c. 13th c.)
logier
"to lodge, dwell, quarter troops"
↓
Old / Middle French (c. 14th c.)
deslogier (des- + logier)
"to decamp, expel from quarters"
↓
Middle English (c. 15th c.)
dislogen
"to drive from a camp or shelter"
↓
Modern English (c. 1600s)
dislodges
"forces out of place or shelter"

Note: The Germanic root *laub- ('leaf') entered Romance through Frankish *laubja ('leafy shelter'), giving Old French loge. The privative prefix des- (Latin dis-) formed deslogier, which Middle English adopted as dislogen before modern verbal inflection produced the third-person form dislodges.
