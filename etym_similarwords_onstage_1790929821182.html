• Offstage: Similar because both are theatrical directional terms defining an actor's location relative to the stage; differs because 'offstage' means out of sight of the audience (in the wings or backstage), whereas 'onstage' means directly visible in the performance area.
  • Backstage: Similar because both relate to theater architecture; differs because 'backstage' refers to the private behind-the-scenes support area (dressing rooms, green room, rigging), whereas 'onstage' is the public performance zone.
  • In the spotlight / Front stage: Similar because both convey high visibility and public attention; differs because 'in the spotlight' is an idiom focusing on intense scrutiny, whereas 'onstage' is a concrete spatial and behavioral descriptor.
  • Publicly: Similar because both involve acting before an audience; differs because 'publicly' is a general adverb denoting openness to society, whereas 'onstage' explicitly invokes performance dynamics and theatrical presence.
  • Live: Similar because both characterize real-time artistic presentation; differs because 'live' contrasts recorded or pre-taped media with real-time broadcasting, whereas 'onstage' specifies physical presence upon a stage.
