- [Discernment sense] - Early English use of "discretion" meant the power of discerning or judging — the intellectual faculty of telling things apart, a morally neutral capacity of reason.
- [Prudence & restraint sense] - The word shifted toward practical wisdom: careful, cautious behaviour that avoids offense, error, or unwanted attention, prized as a social and moral virtue.
- [Confidentiality sense] - In modern use "discretion" became strongly tied to tact and keeping private matters secret — "acting with discretion" now implies quiet, protective self-restraint.
- [Freedom-of-choice sense] - In legal and administrative contexts "discretion" also denotes the authority to decide "at one's own discretion," a neutral delegated power rather than a personal quality.
