- 13th–16th century: High Medieval Fortress & Feudal Toponym - Originated in the Holy Roman Empire as a proud toponymic compound (*ēre* + *fels*), denoting a 'cliff of honor' or 'rock of dignity', most famously embodied in Burg Ehrenfels, a stone customs castle erected c. 1211 by the Archbishops of Mainz on the Rhine gorge.
- 17th–18th century: Austrian Aristocratic Nobility - Bestowed as a noble territorial title upon prominent Austrian civil servants and military figures, creating the baronial lineage of Freiherr von Ehrenfels with estates in Lower Austria.
- 1890: The Philosophical Breakthrough in Vienna & Graz - Propelled into the international lexicon of philosophy and psychology by Christian Freiherr von Ehrenfels with his epochal treatise 'Über Gestaltqualitäten', proving that perceptual forms possess qualities irreducible to elemental sensory atoms.
- 1910s–1930s: Catalyst of the Berlin Gestalt Revolution - Celebrated throughout European universities as the direct intellectual forefather of Max Wertheimer, Wolfgang Köhler, and Kurt Koffka, who transformed Ehrenfels's philosophical form-qualities into an empirical revolution.
- Mid-20th century to Present: Enduring Monument in Cognitive Science - Revered in the history of ideas, philosophy of mind, and cognitive science as the foundational name that gave Gestalt theory its name and structural conceptualization.
