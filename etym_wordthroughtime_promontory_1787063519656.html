- High rock jutting into the sea - purely a descriptive geographical term, borrowed into English from Latin (promontorium) and used in learned writing, maps, and natural history.
- In the age of sail (16th–19th c.) - gained navigational weight as the name for the great landmarks that guided ships and marked national frontiers, often crowned with lighthouses.
- In the 19th-century growth of anatomy and geology - extended metaphorically to bony and rocky projections, entering technical medical and scientific vocabulary.
- Modern usage - remains a formal, literary, and technical term; the average speaker says "headland" or "cliff", while "promontory" evokes epic or scholarly writing, such as Tolkien's description of the rock that shelters Lake-town.
