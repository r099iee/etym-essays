• Underway: Similar because both mean an undertaking is in progress or beginning; differs because underway is neutral and factual, while afoot is vague, secretive, and often ominous.
  • In progress: Similar because both describe something happening now; differs because in progress is a plain official statement of activity, while afoot implies hidden or early motion without a named agent.
  • Astir: Similar because both mean stirring, in motion, and active and share the on/a- prefix pattern; differs because astir is livelier and more physical (a household astir at dawn), while afoot carries plot and foreboding.
  • Brewing: Similar because both mean something is developing invisibly and ominously; differs because brewing is metaphorical (a storm, trouble, a plot) and emphasizes gradual gathering, while afoot emphasizes motion already begun.
  • In the works: Similar because both mean a plan is being prepared; differs because in the works is conversational and matter-of-fact, while afoot is literary and suggests concealed purpose.
  • In motion: Similar because both mean activity has started; differs because in motion is physical and general (trains, processes), while afoot is reserved for plans and mysterious events.
  • Stirring: Similar because both mean beginning to move or happen; differs because stirring can be literal (people, animals waking) and less secretive, while afoot always implies an unseen agenda.
  • Pending: Similar because both concern things not yet fully realized; differs because pending is a waiting for an expected outcome, without the sense of hidden active movement that afoot carries.
  • Impending: Similar because both point to something about to happen; differs because impending is about an approaching threat, while afoot is about secret activity already under way.
  • On foot: Similar because it is the literal ancestor of afoot and shares the physical sense; differs because on foot denotes walking travel only, while afoot is now almost wholly figurative.
