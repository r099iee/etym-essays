• Palau: Similar because both refer to the identical archipelago and sovereign nation in western Micronesia; differs because Palau is the modern, universally recognized official English and international name, whereas Pelew is an archaic eighteenth- and nineteenth-century British colonial toponym.
  • Belau: Similar because both denote the same island homeland and cultural nation; differs because Belau is the native indigenous Palauan autonym and constitutional name, whereas Pelew represents an early English phonetic approximation of that native word.
  • Palaos: Similar because both are Western colonial designations for the archipelago; differs because Palaos (specifically Islas Palaos) is the historical Spanish name, coined by early Iberian navigators who laid nominal claim to the region prior to British contact.
  • Micronesia: Similar because both pertain to the same geographic region; differs because Micronesia is a vast geographical and cultural subregion of Oceania encompassing thousands of islands across four major archipelagos, whereas Pelew specifies only one discrete western archipelago.
  • Caroline Islands: Similar because the Pelew group constitutes the westernmost chain of this island group; differs because the Caroline Islands is an expansive regional designation spanning over two thousand miles, of which the Pelew Islands form merely one cultural and geographical component.
  • Polynesia: Similar because both represent major cultural subregions of the Pacific Ocean; differs because Polynesia is located in the central and eastern Pacific (encompassing Hawaii, New Zealand, and Tahiti), with distinct linguistic, social, and political systems entirely separate from Micronesian Pelew.
  • Melanesia: Similar because both are Pacific island regions; differs because Melanesia ("black islands") lies to the south of Micronesia (including New Guinea, Fiji, and the Solomon Islands), exhibiting distinct Papuan and Austronesian social organizations and cultural histories.
  • South Seas: Similar because both evoke historic European maritime exploration in the Pacific; differs because the South Seas is a romantic, vaguely defined historical moniker applied broadly to all of Oceania, whereas Pelew is a precise island group.
