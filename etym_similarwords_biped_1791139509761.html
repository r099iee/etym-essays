• quadruped: Similar because it also names an animal by number of locomotor limbs; differs because it means four-footed rather than two-footed and is the direct antonym in gait classification.
  • tetrapod: Similar because it denotes four-limbed vertebrates including bipeds; differs because it is a much broader clade term and does not specify habitual two-legged walking.
  • bipedal creature: Similar because it is a near-exact paraphrase of biped; differs because it is a phrase and uses the modern preferred adjective bipedal.
  • two-footer: Similar because it colloquially means a two-footed being; differs because it is informal, rare, and often jocular.
  • upright walker: Similar because it describes habitual erect locomotion; differs because it emphasizes posture rather than limb count and need not be strictly two-legged in machines.
  • hominin: Similar because all hominins are habitual bipeds; differs because it is a taxonomic term for the human lineage specifically, not any two-legged animal.
  • human: Similar because humans are the prototypical bipeds in most discourse; differs because human adds rationality, culture, and species membership beyond locomotion.
  • pedestrian: Similar because it evokes one who goes on foot; differs because it means a walker in traffic contrasted with a driver, not a two-legged species.
  • bird: Similar because all birds are anatomically bipeds; differs because bird specifies feathers, wings, and avian lineage, while biped specifies only leg number.
  • humanoid: Similar because humanoids are typically imagined as bipeds; differs because humanoid stresses human-like shape including head, torso, arms, not just two legs.
  • hexapod: Similar because it also classifies animals/robots by leg number; differs because it means six-legged and belongs to insects and multi-legged robots.
  • centipede: Similar because it appears alongside biped in the source as a many-legged contrast; differs because it names a specific myriapod with dozens of legs, not a two-legged animal.
