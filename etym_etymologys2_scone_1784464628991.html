Origin uncertain — proposed source (standard account): Middle Dutch schoonbroot "fine bread" (schoon "fine, beautiful, bright" + broot "bread"; 15th c.) or Middle Low German schōnbrot → Middle Scots scon / skone "scone (bread)" (attested c. 1513) → Scots scone (16th c.) → English scone, general adoption as a tea-bread (18th–19th c.) → Modern English SCONE Note on the debated stages: (1) the Low-Countries compound is the leading etymology (favoured by the OED and supported by Scotland's late-medieval trade with the Low Countries), but it is not proven; (2) older proposals connecting the word to the place-name Scone, or to Scottish Gaelic sgonn "lump, mouthful", are now generally rejected or doubted; (3) no Indo-European root-chain can be given for the word itself, because the earliest attested form is a 16th-century Scots borrowing and no older native ancestor exists. If the Low-Countries etymology is right, the ultimate Germanic components are the adjective *skauniz "beautiful, bright" (cf. Modern German schön) and the noun *braudą "bread"; if it is wrong, the word's ancestry is simply unknown.
