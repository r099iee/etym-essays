• Antigone: Similar because both are iconic Greek tragic heroines created by Sophocles who defy state/royal authority out of unwavering family devotion; differs because Antigone defies King Creon to bury her brother out of divine religious duty, whereas Electra seeks active retributive vengeance (matricide) for her father's murder.
  • Medea: Similar because both are fierce, traumatized women driven to extreme acts of vengeance in Greek tragedy; differs because Medea murders her own children to punish an unfaithful husband, whereas Electra acts with her brother to avenge a slain parent.
  • Iphigenia: Similar because both are daughters of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra; differs because Iphigenia is the sacrificial victim offered up by Agamemnon at Aulis, whereas Electra survives to punish Clytemnestra for Agamemnon's subsequent murder.
  • Cassandra: Similar because both are grieving women entangled in the curse of the House of Atreus; differs because Cassandra is a Trojan prophetess cursed never to be believed, while Electra is an active conspirator in familial retribution.
  • Clytemnestra: Similar because both are powerful, vengeful royal women in the Oresteia myth; differs because Clytemnestra is the murderous mother who killed Agamemnon, while Electra is the daughter who orchestrates Clytemnestra's death.
