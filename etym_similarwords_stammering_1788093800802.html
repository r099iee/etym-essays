• Stuttering: Similar because both name the same disorder of speech fluency (repetitions, blocks, prolongations). Differs because “stutter” is the dominant term in American English, while “stammer” is the usual British term; older textbooks sometimes reserved “stutter” for rapid repetition (clonic) and “stammer” for the tense block (tonic), a distinction modern usage has largely abandoned.
  • Faltering: Similar because both describe speech (or movement) that hesitates and breaks. Differs because “falter” is gentler, often moral (faltering courage), and never clinical; “stammering” names the specific involuntary speech pattern.
  • Hesitating: Similar because both involve pauses before or within speech. Differs because “hesitate” is about choosing or fearing — a normal, willed pause — while “stammering” is involuntary, repetitive and beyond the speaker’s control.
  • Halting: Similar because both mean “broken, uneven, stopping and starting”. Differs because “halting” describes the effect (any halting speech) while “stammering” names the cause and the clinical phenomenon.
  • Spluttering / Sputtering: Similar because both can describe choked, broken speech bursts. Differs because “splutter” emphasizes spitting, choking and indignation; “stammering” is a steady fluency problem, not an outburst.
  • Mumbling: Similar because both can make speech unclear. Differs because “mumble” is quiet, low and lazy, while “stammering” can be clearly articulated yet broken in fluency.
  • Stumbling: Similar because both convey tripping over words. Differs because “stumble” is metaphorical and momentary (a stumble over a hard name), while “stammering” is recurrent and involuntary.
  • Garbling: Similar because both produce distorted speech. Differs because “garble” implies confused jumbling of words, while “stammering” is repetition and blocking, not confusion.
  • Floundering: Similar because both suggest struggling and getting lost in mid-utterance. Differs because “flounder” is about losing one’s way or composure, while “stammer” is specifically the mechanical interruption of fluency.
