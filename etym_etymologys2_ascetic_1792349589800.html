Note on radical semantic inversion: The historical evolution of ascetic exhibits one of the most remarkable semantic reversals in European lexicography. In Archaic and Classical Greece, an askētḗs (ἀσκητής) was a vigorously trained professional athlete, gymnast, or pugilist competing in the Panhellenic Games, celebrated for robust physical strength, muscle mass, and bodily vitality. During late antiquity, Hellenistic philosophers (such as Epictetus) and early Christian Desert Fathers (such as Anthony the Great) appropriated the athletic metaphor of bodily training (áskēsis), reinterpreting the "contest" (agōn) not as a struggle for an earthly laurel wreath against human rivals, but as a spiritual combat against demonic temptation and bodily appetite. Through this metaphoric transformation, the "athlete of God" became an emaciated, fasting hermit who starved the very body that the classical athlete had cultivated.
