Traversez (functioning as a French loan-imperative and literary signifier in comparative literature and cultural criticism) is the second-person plural (and formal polite singular) present imperative and indicative form of the French verb traverser, meaning "cross!", "traverse!", "pass over!", or "cross ye!". Originating in the Classical Latin compound trānsversus ("turned across, placed athwart"), the word combines spatial movement across an expanse with dynamic transition across a boundary, threshold, or perilous divide.

In literary criticism and cultural essayism—specifically within the highlighted context analyzing Marcel Proust's modernism and the urban semiotics of Paris—the specific phrase traversez en deux temps ("cross in two stages") functions as a celebrated philosophical trope. The flashing red Parisian pedestrian sign directing walkers to cross the multi-lane traffic of the boulevard by pausing at a central median haven before venturing to the opposite side becomes an allegory for navigating human temporality. In the critic's reading, traversez is not merely a bureaucratic traffic command, but an existential injunction: to cross over the dangerous chasms of memory, grief, and time requires broken, multi-staged navigation rather than a single, effortless leap.

The verb form demonstrates rich polysemy across regulatory, architectural, theatrical, psychological, and metaphorical domains:

  • Civic and Traffic Regulatory Imperative (Iconic Urban Sense): In modern French civic infrastructure, traversez is the standard regulatory command displayed on illuminated electronic pedestrian crossing signs and painted street markings. The directive traversez or traversez en deux temps instructs pedestrians when and how to cross busy boulevards, emphasizing safety, regulated timing, and attentiveness to vehicular traffic.
  • Literary, Philosophical, and Semiotic Allegory: In Proustian studies and continental literary theory, traversez is elevated into an existential metaphor. It embodies the human challenge of crossing across fragmented temporal intervals, negotiating the abyss between the forgotten past and the remembered present. The imperative addresses the reader directly, urging them to venture across the dangerous thoroughfares of life and textuality.
  • Physical Spatial Navigation (Across Obstacles and Frontiers): In general usage, the imperative traversez! commands or invites individuals to pass through, cross over, or journey across a wide physical expanse, barrier, or territory—such as crossing a river (traversez le fleuve), traversing a mountain range, passing through a dark forest, or crossing an international border. It emphasizes moving from one distinct margin or frontier completely to the other side.
  • Theatrical and Choreographic Direction: In French stage directing and classical ballet choreography, traversez serves as a technical imperative directing an actor, dancer, or chorus to move across the stage (to make a "traverse"), transitioning from stage left to stage right across the visual field of the audience.
  • Existential Endurance and Psychological Ordeal (La traversée du désert): Figuratively, the verbal stem evokes enduring a prolonged trial or severe spiritual testing. In French political and literary idiom, the phrase la traversée du désert ("the crossing of the desert") describes a period of political exile, creative isolation, or personal tribulation that one must endure and cross before regaining prominence or fulfillment.
  • Physical Penetration and Piercing (Archaic / Weaponry Sense): Historically in early modern French fencing and military literature, traverser could mean to run through, pierce, or impale with a blade (traverser le corps d'une épée, "to run a sword through the body"), emphasizing forceful transversal penetration.

The historical semantic bridge linking ancient Latin transversal orientation to modern Parisian traffic signals is the concept of passing athwart a flowing current: whether moving diagonally against a river current, navigating across a line of marching soldiers, or stepping across lanes of speeding motor vehicles, traversez commands the traveler to negotiate and overcome a perpendicular hazard to reach safety on the opposing shore.
