• Attached: Similar because both mean joined or fixed to something; differs because attached is the neutral general word (can be loose or firm), while fastened implies firm, deliberate securing.
  • Secured: Similar because both mean made firm and safe; differs because secured emphasizes safety against loss or escape, while fastened emphasizes the physical act of fixing.
  • Fixed: Similar because both mean made immovable; differs because fixed can mean repaired, arranged, or set in place permanently, while fastened implies a mechanical joining that could be undone.
  • Tied: Similar because both mean bound with something; differs because tied specifically implies a knot, cord, or string, while fastened covers buckles, clasps, hooks, nails, and glue.
  • Bound: Similar because both mean held together; differs because bound is formal and literary and suggests wrapping, restraints, or obligation (bound by duty), while fastened is physical and mechanical.
  • Locked: Similar because both mean closed securely; differs because locked specifically implies a lock and key (or mechanism) preventing opening, while fastened covers any closure method.
  • Clasped: Similar because both mean gripped or held firmly; differs because clasped implies holding with hands or a clasp device and is often about embrace, while fastened is about attachment.
  • Hitched: Similar because both mean attached by a loop or hook; differs because hitched implies a temporary looped attachment (hitching a trailer), while fastened is more permanent and general.
  • Moored: Similar because both mean secured against movement; differs because moored is nautical-specific (a boat tied to a dock), while fastened is general.
  • Anchored: Similar because both mean fixed firmly; differs because anchored is figurative or nautical (fixed to a point), while fastened is mechanical and physical.
  • Gripped: Similar because both can mean holding onto something firmly; differs because gripped implies holding with a hand or claws, while fastened implies attachment by a device or adhesion.
  • Stuck: Similar because both mean attached and unable to move; differs because stuck is informal and suggests adhesion or jamming, while fastened implies deliberate securing that can be undone.
