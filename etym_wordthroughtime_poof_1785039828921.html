- 18th-19th centuries - Appears as an onomatopoeic interjection for a soft, explosive puff of air or smoke - the sound of a flame blown out, a conjurer's trick, a sudden disappearance.
- 20th century - The playful, theatrical sense hardens: 'poof!' becomes the standard word for vanishing in a puff of smoke in stories and stage magic, always light, sudden, and slightly comic.
- Contemporary sense - Poof still names the soft pop of instant disappearance; a separate slang noun of c. 1850, meaning an effeminate man and probably derived from 'puff', survives as an unrelated and rightly avoided slur.
