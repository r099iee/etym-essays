- Ancient Vedic to Epic Era (c. 800–300 BCE) — Narrative & Mythic Origin Framing: Functioned within oral epic composition as the foundational frame story (as seen in the Bālakāṇḍa of the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa) establishing how divine inspiration catalyzed poetic meter and how the song was passed to reciting bards.
- Classical Sūtra & Śāstra Era (c. 300 BCE–500 CE) — Epistemological Necessity: Codified in logical systems (like the Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā) as a precise analytical category where an introductory topic or incidental problem must be addressed because it is indispensable to completing the main investigation.
- Medieval Scholastic Period (c. 600–1600 CE) — Hermeneutic Commentary Standard: Employed by major Sanskrit commentators (such as Śaṅkara, Mallinātha, and Abhinavagupta) as the formal introductory essay justifying the fourfold preliminary criteria (anubandha-catuṣṭaya) before explicating canonical verses.
- Modern Academic Indology & Global Literary Studies (19th–21st c.) — Theoretical Category: Utilized by philologists, comparative mythologists, and literary theorists to analyze recursive framing, oral lineage transmission, and meta-narrative devices in South Asian literature.
