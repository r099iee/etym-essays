- Courtly origin (14th-16th c.) - Belonging to the royal court: courteous denotes the polished, refined manners and chivalric breeding expected of courtiers.
- Diffusion to polite society (17th-19th c.) - The courtesy of the court spreads through genteel society; it becomes a mark of considerate, obliging behavior toward all, though still freighted with status-consciousness, as in Austen's Sir William Lucas.
- Contemporary sense - Democratic and egalitarian: courteous simply means polite, respectful, and considerate in everyday manner, stripped of its aristocratic associations.
