• Hallooing: Similar because both are loud, archaic cries of pursuit or alarm and appear together in the source sentence; differs because hallooing is a hunting or hailing shout used to rouse or signal, while hooting is a hollow, animal-like call, often of mockery.
  • Whooping: Similar because both are loud, voiced cries; differs because whooping is a high, triumphant, almost whistling cry, while hooting is deep and hollow.
  • Shouting: Similar because both mean raising the voice loudly; differs because shouting is neutral and articulate, while hooting is wordless, hollow, and often jeering.
  • Jeering: Similar because both express derision; differs because jeering is specifically insulting mockery with words or gestures, while hooting is a wordless cry of scorn.
  • Booing: Similar because both are crowd sounds of disapproval; differs because booing is a long low "boo", while hooting is a repeated hollow cry, also used of owls.
  • Hollering: Similar because both mean calling out loudly; differs because hollering is informal, chiefly American, and human, while hooting is hollow and animal.
  • Shrieking: Similar because both are loud, piercing cries; differs because shrieking is high-pitched and panicked, while hooting is deep, resonant, and hollow.
  • Screeching: Similar because both name harsh cries; differs because screeching is grating and high (as of the screech owl), while hooting is the deep, round call of the true owl.
  • Ululating: Similar because both are long, wavering cries; differs because ululation is a ritual, trembling vocalization, while hooting is a short, hollow, percussive blast of sound.
  • Crying: Similar because both are voiced sounds from the throat; differs because crying is broad and usually emotional, while hooting names a specific hollow, carrying call.
