- 13th-14th centuries - 'Toggen' enters Middle English as a plain verb for hauling or towing with a sharp, straining effort, close kin to 'tow'; a tug is a strong pull on rope or rein, effort against resistance.
- 16th-19th centuries - The word gains a nautical second life: the steam tugboat that hauls larger ships appears in the 19th century, and 'tug-of-war' moves from a literal rope-pulling trial of strength into a sporting and political metaphor.
- Contemporary sense - Tugging keeps its straining, repeated pull - the hand tugging a sleeve, a heart-tugging emotion - and in every use it signals visible effort against resistance rather than smooth, easy motion.
