PIE (reconstructed)
*deh₃-
"to give, grant, bestow"
(ancestor of Sanskrit dā-, Greek didonai, Latin dare)
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*didō / *dare
"to give"
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE)
reddere
"to give back, return, restore, deliver, yield, translate"
(compound of re- "back, again" + dare "to give")
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 4th–6th c.)
*rendere
"to give back, yield, surrender"
(altered from reddere on analogy of prehendere / prendere "to seize")
↓
Old French (10th–12th c.)
rendre
"to give back, yield, surrender, produce"
↓
Middle French (16th c.; c. 1530s)
rendition
"the act of surrendering or yielding up (a fortress or garrison)"
(noun of action formed from rendre + suffix -ition)
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1590s–1600; Shakespeare, Bacon)
rendition
"surrender, yielding up of property; translation"
↓
Modern English (19th c.–present)
rendition
"artistic performance/interpretation; translation; legal/extradition transfer"
