• Hinder: similar because both mean to delay or obstruct progress; differs because "hinder" is the native English word, everyday and flexible, often used of people and their actions ("he hindered the rescue"), whereas "impede" is Latinate, formal, and typically takes abstract or collective objects.
  • Obstruct: similar because both mean to block passage or progress; differs because "obstruct" emphasizes a barrier — physical or institutional — placed in the way (obstructed roads, obstruction of justice), while "impede" stresses slowing and encumbrance rather than outright closure.
  • Hamper: similar because both mean to hinder free action or movement; differs because "hamper" (from hamper "to fetter, entangle") is more concrete and usually describes conditions rather than agents ("snow hampered deliveries"), and it can suggest tangling up equipment or limbs, exactly the picture Latin impedire was built on.
  • Thwart: similar because both denote frustrating an effort; differs because "thwart" (Old Norse þvert "across") implies active cross-purposes and the outright frustration of a plan — stronger, more adversarial, and more personal than the impersonal obstacle of "impede."
  • Retard: similar because both mean to slow the progress of something ("retard the flow"); differs because "retard" is now avoided outside narrow technical contexts (fire retardant, developmental research) after its offensive use as a slur, while "impede" carries no such stigma.
  • Encumber: similar because both mean to load down and impede; differs because "encumber" implies burdens, debt, or weight pressing upon someone or something (an encumbered estate), whereas "impede" implies an obstacle in the path that slows forward motion.
  • Block: similar because both mean to prevent movement or passage; differs because "block" is concrete and complete — a blocked road is impassable — while "impede" is partial and durative: progress continues, but slowly.
  • Frustrate: similar because both appear when achievement is prevented; differs because "frustrate" (Latin frustra "in vain") implies a person's hopes or efforts are defeated and carries emotional weight, while "impede" stays clinical about the obstruction itself.
  • Delay: similar because both involve making something late or slower; differs because "delay" is purely temporal and may be innocent (a late train), while "impede" always implies a positive obstacle in the way of progress or motion.
  • Curb / check: similar because both mean to hold back; differs because "curb" and "check" restrain an activity's growth or excess (curb spending, check inflation), whereas "impede" obstructs motion toward a goal rather than limiting excess.
