• Euphoria: Similar because both are states of intense happiness; differs because euphoria often implies a clinical, drug- or illness-induced bliss divorced from real cause, while elation is a natural, healthy joy.
  • Exhilaration: Similar because both are high-energy joy; differs because exhilaration is tied to physical stimulus and adrenaline (cold wind, speed, exertion), while elation is emotional and spiritual uplift.
  • Jubilation: Similar because both are great happiness; differs because jubilation is public, ceremonial, and often collective rejoicing, while elation is an inner, personal feeling.
  • Exultation: Similar because both imply triumphant joy; differs because exultation emphasises open, even boastful triumph, while elation is quieter and more inward.
  • Rapture: Similar because both are ecstatic happiness; differs because rapture suggests being carried out of oneself, often religious or overwhelming, while elation keeps the self intact and conscious.
  • Bliss: Similar because both are profound happiness; differs because bliss is serene, perfect contentment, while elation is active, soaring, and expansive.
  • Glee: Similar because both are merry happiness; differs because glee is playful, mischievous, and lighter, while elation is elevated and dignified.
  • High spirits: Similar because both describe a buoyant, upbeat mood; differs because high spirits is informal and names a mood, while elation is a formal noun for intense joy.
  • Joy: Similar because both are happiness; differs because joy is a general, broader term, while elation specifies the exhilarating, uplifted quality of the happiness.
  • Delight: Similar because both are pleasure; differs because delight is caused by a specific pleasing object or event, while elation is a general state of uplift.
  • Transport: Similar in the shared idea of being carried away by emotion; differs because transport is archaic and implies loss of self-control, while elation is a controlled elevation of spirits.
  • Joyfulness / Cheerfulness: Similar because both describe a happy disposition; differs because these name a steady temperament, while elation is a heightened, episodic surge of joy.
