- 14th-15th centuries - Clink enters Middle English as a direct imitation of the sharp, thin ringing of metal on metal - a coin, a chain-link, a glass meeting another glass.
- 16th-19th centuries - English uses reduplicated echo-forms (clink-clink, tick-tock) to stretch and rhythmically repeat such sounds; clink-clink becomes the natural notation for the repeated striking of light iron, as of fetters in motion.
- Contemporary sense - Clink-clink still names a light, metallic ringing repeated in sequence; it reads as delicate, comic, and rhythmic - the sound of keys, coins, or light chains keeping time with a hurried run.
