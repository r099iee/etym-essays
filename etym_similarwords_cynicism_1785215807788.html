• skepticism: Similar because both denote a refusal to accept claims at face value; differs because skepticism is doubt about knowledge, evidence, and truth (the skeptic waits for proof), while cynicism is doubt about human motives and assumes the worst from the start, carrying contempt rather than suspended judgment.
  • pessimism: Similar because both expect the negative; differs because pessimism is a general expectation that things will go badly and may be benign or even self-deprecating, whereas cynicism is specifically about human self-interest and carries a flavour of scorn.
  • distrust: Similar because both denote a withholding of confidence; differs because distrust is a neutral, particular lack of trust (distrust of a specific person or claim), while cynicism is a generalised, bitter, principled disbelief in sincerity itself.
  • misanthropy: Similar because both involve a negative view of humanity; differs because misanthropy is hatred or dislike of people as such, while cynicism is a distrust of their motives — a misanthrope avoids people, a cynic sneers at their professions.
  • irony: Similar because both can involve saying or perceiving the opposite of the apparent; differs because irony is a neutral rhetorical device (saying one thing while meaning another), available to the kind and cruel alike, while cynicism is an attitude of distrust and contempt.
  • sarcasm: Similar because both are often joined in mocking speech; differs because sarcasm is a verbal mannerism (the cutting remark), while cynicism is the underlying attitude the sarcasm expresses — one may be sarcastic without being cynical.
  • jadedness / world-weariness: Similar because both denote tired disillusionment; differs because world-weariness is passive fatigue and loss of enthusiasm, whereas cynicism is active, accusing contempt directed at human motives.
  • disillusionment: Similar because both follow the collapse of naive faith; differs because disillusionment is the neutral state of having lost illusions (and can be the first step to wisdom), while cynicism hardens that loss into systematic distrust.
  • nihilism: Similar because both deny value in what others profess; differs because nihilism is a radical philosophical denial of value and meaning altogether, while cynicism still cares enough to mock — it assumes selfishness, not meaninglessness.
  • realism / hard-headedness: Similar because both refuse sentimental illusion; differs because realism is a neutral, often admirable facing of facts, while cynicism adds the gratuitous assumption that every noble profession hides a base motive.
  • opportunism: Similar because both are associated with the marketplace view of self-interest; differs because opportunism is a practice of seizing advantage, while cynicism is an attitude of believing everyone does so.
  • asceticism (historical link): Similar because the ancient Cynic school practised rigorous self-denial and contempt for luxury; differs because asceticism is the religious or philosophical discipline of austerity itself, while cynicism in its modern sense has nothing to do with self-denial and denotes distrust of others' motives.
