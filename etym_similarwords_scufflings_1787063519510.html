• Rustlings: Similar because both refer to light, soft, continuous friction sounds produced by movement in foliage or debris; differs because rustlings specifically emphasize the dry, papery whisper of leaves, fabric, or paper rubbing together, whereas scufflings imply agitated scraping, scratching feet, or bodily friction.
  • Scramblings: Similar because both capture chaotic, hurried physical motions or the accompanying clamor of limbs in contact with terrain; differs because scramblings emphasize clumsy climbing, struggling to maintain footing, or competitive haste rather than scraping friction sounds or close-quarters tussles.
  • Skirmishes: Similar because both can describe minor, disorganized physical or verbal clashes between opposing parties; differs because skirmishes belong to a formal or military register describing brief, localized engagements with weapons or tactical intent, whereas scufflings denote petty, disorderly, unarmed physical grappling or minor frictional noise.
  • Shufflings: Similar because both describe dragging foot movements that scrape across a floor or ground; differs because shufflings typically suggest slow, weary, reluctant, or stealthy sliding steps without lifting the feet, whereas scufflings convey agitation, frantic scraping, or multi-party friction.
  • Scratches: Similar because both involve abrasive contact against a hard or textured surface; differs because scratches denote sharp, linear incisive marks or high-pitched abrasive sounds caused by claws or points, whereas scufflings describe broader, chaotic agitation or minor tussles.
  • Fracases: Similar because both denote noisy, disorderly disturbances or physical altercations; differs because a fracas is an overt, loud, rowdy brawl or public uproar of significant volume and chaos, whereas scufflings are smaller-scale, often muffled, petty physical scuffles or frictional sounds.
  • Tussles: Similar because both refer to rough, disorderly physical struggles or competitive contests; differs because tussle focuses almost purely on vigorous physical or metaphorical grappling for possession or dominance, lacking the auditory nuance of frictional rustling on forest floors.
  • Patterings: Similar because both describe the rapid acoustic presence of small creature movements; differs because patterings describe distinct, rhythmic, light tapping impacts of paws or raindrops on a surface, rather than dragging, scraping frictional turbulence.
