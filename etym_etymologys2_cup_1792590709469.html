Note on Germanic semantic split: When Late Latin cuppa was borrowed into early West Germanic, it sparked a remarkable divergent semantic trajectory across European languages. In English, Old English cuppe remained faithfully tethered to the concave drinking vessel, eventually extending outward into botany (the acorn's cupule), sports trophies, and kitchen volume. In High German, however, the borrowed cognate kopf ("cup, bowl") underwent a radical anatomical metaphor: medieval German speakers began referring to the human skull or cranium as a "bone-cup" (hirnkopf, "brain-cup"), until Kopf completely replaced the ancient Germanic word for head (Haupt) as the standard German noun for the human head.
