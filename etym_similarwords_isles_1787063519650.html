• islands: Similar because both mean landmasses surrounded by water; differs because islands is the plain, neutral, everyday plural while isles is literary and archaic.
  • islets: Similar because both denote small islands in a group; differs because islets are specifically tiny isles and the word is neutral, not poetic.
  • archipelago: Similar because both name a cluster of islands; differs because an archipelago is a named geographic chain considered as one unit, whereas isles is simply multiple islands without that collective structure.
  • atolls: Similar because both are island landforms; differs because an atoll is a specific coral-ringed lagoon formation, a technical term with no poetic register.
  • keys: Similar because both denote small low islands; differs because a key is a sand or coral cay specific to the Caribbean and Florida region.
  • cays: Similar because both are low islands of sand or shell; differs because a cay is a specific geological term, not a literary plural.
  • skerries: Similar because both are rocky islets; differs because a skerry is specifically a bare rock reef and the word is Scandinavian-derived and rarer.
  • holms: Similar because both can be river or near-shore islets; differs because a holm is a dialectal, Scandinavian-inherited word now rare outside place names.
  • iles: Similar because it is the same word; differs because it is the older spelling without the Latin-influenced silent -s-, used in some place names.
