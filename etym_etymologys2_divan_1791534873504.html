Note: Traditional folk etymologies derived the word from Persian dēv ("demon, devil"), alleging that royal scribes performed bureaucratic calculations with demonic speed and cunning. Modern comparative philology refutes this, demonstrating direct borrowing from Mesopotamian administrative vocabulary through Old Persian *dipi- ("writing"), cognate with Elamite and Sanskrit lipi ("script, alphabet").
