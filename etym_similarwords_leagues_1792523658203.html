• miles: Similar because both are traditional units of linear terrestrial and nautical distance; differs because a mile is a shorter standard unit (5,280 feet), whereas a league historically equals three miles (an hour's march).
  • yojanas: Similar because both represent large units of distance used in epic literature to describe city dimensions and royal marches; differs because yojana is an ancient Indian Vedic/Puranic measure (typically 8 to 9 miles, or about 2 to 3 leagues).
  • parasangs: Similar because both are traditional historical measures representing roughly an hour's walking travel; differs because parasang is of ancient Iranian/Persian origin (about 3.5 to 4 miles), whereas league is rooted in Celtic and Gallo-Roman heritage.
  • stadia: Similar because both are classical units measuring geographic distance in ancient historical literature; differs because a stadion is much shorter (about 185 meters or one-eighth of a Roman mile), used for footraces and city perimeters.
  • versts: Similar because both are obsolete imperial distance measures used in regional literature; differs because a verst is a Russian imperial unit equal to about 1.07 kilometers (roughly two-thirds of a mile), far shorter than a three-mile league.
  • kilometers: Similar because both measure terrestrial distance along roads and territories; differs because kilometer is a modern decimal metric unit (1,000 meters), completely standardized and devoid of romantic or archaic literary overtones.
  • furlongs: Similar because both are traditional Anglo-European units of length; differs because a furlong is an agricultural furrow-length (one-eighth of a mile / 220 yards), whereas a league represents a multi-mile trek.
  • alliances: Similar because homonymic leagues denotes formal coalitions and pacts between groups; differs because alliance focuses on political diplomacy and mutual support rather than spatial linear distance.
  • confederations: Similar because the secondary meaning of leagues encompasses unions of autonomous bodies; differs because confederation highlights permanent institutional and political governance structures.
  • coalitions: Similar because both describe organized combinations of factions or teams; differs because coalition usually denotes a temporary, issue-specific tactical alignment rather than a permanent measure or formal body.
