(source of Sanskrit deha, English dough/lady, Latin fingere/figura)

Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1200–800 BCE)
deha (देह)
"the physical body, formed bodily vessel"
↓
Late Vedic Sanskrit (c. 800–600 BCE; Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa)
Videgha / Videha (विदेघ / विदेह)
"the king Māthava Videgha; the eastern realm across the Sadānīrā"
(prefix vi- "without, apart" + deha "body" -> "bodiless")
↓
Epic & Classical Sanskrit (c. 500 BCE–500 CE; Upaniṣads, Rāmāyaṇa, Vedānta)
Videha (विदेह)
"the kingdom of Janaka; also bodiless, disembodied (videha-mukti)"
↓
Modern Indo-Aryan / English (loanword via Indological scholarship)
Videha
"ancient kingdom of northern Bihar/Nepal; realm of Janaka; unembodied state"

Note on historical debate: Philologists (such as Michael Witzel and Arthur Anthony Macdonell) emphasize the phonetic shift between Vedic Videgha (as in the tribal chieftain Māthava Videgha in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa) and Classical Sanskrit Videha. The older tribal name likely featured an intervocalic voiced aspirate velar -gh-, which underwent regular eastern Indo-Aryan spirantization and weakening into the glottal fricative -h-. While the Purāṇic tradition provides a popular folk etymology deriving the name from vi- ("without") + deha ("body") based on King Nimi's discarnate spirit, historical linguistics suggests Videha was originally an ethnonym designating a distinct eastern Indo-Aryan clan whose name later became assimilated to the theological concept of bodiless liberation.
