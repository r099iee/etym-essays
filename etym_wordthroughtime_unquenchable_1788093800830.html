- [Late 16th century: "that cannot be extinguished"] - Coined on the model of quenchable, first of literal fire and of divine wrath; the Bible's "unquenchable fire" (Matthew 3:12, KJV 1611) fixes a stern, apocalyptic register.
- [17th century: thirst and appetite] - Extends to thirst and bodily appetites; still tied to the physical imagery of dousing flame or slaking drought.
- [18th–19th centuries: the Romantic leap] - Passions, ambitions, curiosity and genius are "unquenchable"; the word shifts from divine punishment toward sublime human longing — awe replaces dread.
- [20th century: the heroic virtue] - Fully psychological and marketable: "unquenchable enthusiasm", "unquenchable optimism", "unquenchable curiosity" are admired qualities; biographers, advertisers and motivational writers adopt it wholesale.
- [Contemporary: double register] - It still carries biblical weight applied to fire or hell, but in daily use it means "insatiable" with a warm, admiring colour ("an unquenchable love of life").
