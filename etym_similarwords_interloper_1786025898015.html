• Intruder: Similar because both refer to an uninvited person entering a space; differs because intruder often implies physical entry or immediate threat of burglary/trespass into a building, whereas interloper extends powerfully into social spheres, professional trades, family affairs, and markets where one lacks legitimate entitlement.
  • Trespasser: Similar because both enter property or domains without permission; differs because trespasser is a strictly physical and legal property-law violation, while interloper involves meddling in relationships, negotiations, exclusive monopolies, or closed social circles.
  • Encroacher: Similar because both advance gradually into territory belonging to others; differs because encroacher suggests a slow, stealthy, incremental nibbling away at boundaries or rights, whereas an interloper is an unauthorized participant boldly stepping in.
  • Meddler: Similar because both insert themselves into other people's business; differs because a meddler or busybody interferes with advice, gossip, or unwanted assistance, while an interloper is an illegitimate outsider seeking a foothold, profit, or role where they have no right.
  • Gatecrasher: Similar because both attend events without an invitation; differs because gatecrasher is informal and specific to parties, banquets, and social events, whereas interloper carries historical gravity and broad institutional applicability.
  • Usurper: Similar because both claim a position without legitimate authority; differs because a usurper explicitly seizes and holds a throne, title, or sovereign office by force, whereas an interloper intrudes, poaches, or meddles without necessarily overthrowing the sovereign power.
