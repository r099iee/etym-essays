• Warren: Similar because both name rabbit tunnel-systems; differs because a warren is the whole network of linked burrows (or its human keep), while a burrow can be one single tunnel or the act of digging it.
  • Den: Similar because both are snug animal retreats; differs because a den is any lair or hollow refuge (often natural), while a burrow must be excavated by digging.
  • Sett: Similar because both are dug mammal homes; differs because sett names specifically the badger's extensive ancestral earth, while burrow covers rabbits, foxes, hobbits, and all diggers.
  • Earth: Similar because a fox's earth is exactly its burrow; differs because earth in this sense is huntsman's jargon for the fox-hole alone, while burrow is the general naturalist's word.
  • Tunnel: Similar because both are underground passages; differs because a tunnel is usually human-engineered for transit, while a burrow is dug for dwelling, smaller, snugger, and animal-made.
  • Dig: Similar because burrowing is digging for a home; differs because dig is the plain general action (gardens, graves, foundations), while burrow adds dwelling, hiding, and snuggling to the excavation.
  • Excavate: Similar because both remove earth to make a hollow; differs because excavate is formal and archaeological (sites, foundations), while burrow is animal, domestic, and affectionate.
  • Nestle: Similar because both press snugly into soft shelter; differs because nestle stresses cuddling against warmth, while burrow stresses pushing into and disappearing within.
  • Hole up: Similar because both hide away from the world; differs because hole up is slangy and often temporary or fugitive, while burrow suggests a proper dug home lovingly kept.
