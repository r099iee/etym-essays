- 17th c. Neo-Latin ontologia coined by Lorhard (1606) and Göckel (1613) to name the science of being — perception scholastic and taxonomic, a new label for old Aristotelian metaphysics
- 18th c. Wolffian system: ontologia as first part of metaphysics, yielding adjective ontologicus / ontological (1720s–60s) — connotation rationalist and architectonic, the foundation-stone of philosophy
- 19th c. decline and suspicion: positivists dismiss ontology as empty verbiage — perception pejorative, ontological becomes a term of abuse for untestable speculation
- 20th c. revival: Husserl, Heidegger (Being and Time 1927), Sartre, Quine restore ontology — connotation profound and urgent; Laing (1960) coins ontological insecurity, Giddens (1990s) ontological security, shifting the adjective from seminar to clinic and sociology
- Contemporary: dual track — analytic ontological commitment (austere, technical) plus information-science ontological engineering (practical, computational) plus everyday academic intensifier for deep (ontological anxiety) — perception learned but now flexible, from being itself to data models of what counts as being
