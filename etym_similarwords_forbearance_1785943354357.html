• Patience: Similar because both mean calm endurance of difficulty or delay; differs because patience is general uncomplaining endurance, while forbearance specifically means actively holding back one's own power, anger, or legal action against someone.
  • Restraint: Similar because both mean holding oneself back; differs because restraint is the broad word for checking any impulse or movement, while forbearance is merciful, patient refraining — usually from punishment or enforcement.
  • Tolerance: Similar because both mean putting up with what one dislikes; differs because tolerance is enduring opinions or behaviors one finds objectionable, while forbearance is refraining from retaliation when wronged.
  • Self-control: Similar because both involve governing one's impulses; differs because self-control is the general psychological capacity for regulating desires, while forbearance is specifically restraint toward others, often merciful.
  • Long-suffering: Similar because both denote patient endurance of wrong; differs because long-suffering is strongly biblical and emphasizes enduring suffering over a long time, while forbearance is cooler, more practical, and also legal.
  • Clemency: Similar because both mean leniency toward those who could be punished; differs because clemency is official mercy granted by a judge or ruler (commuting a sentence, pardoning), while forbearance is personal or contractual restraint.
  • Indulgence: Similar because both allow lenient treatment; differs because indulgence suggests spoiling, pampering, or granting pleasure, while forbearance is dignified self-denial.
  • Endurance: Similar because both involve standing firm under difficulty; differs because endurance emphasizes withstanding hardship or pain, while forbearance emphasizes voluntary non-action and restraint of power.
  • Temperance: Similar because both mean moderation and self-mastery; differs because temperance is chiefly about controlling appetites (drink, food, pleasure), while forbearance is about controlling reactions to others.
  • Stoicism: Similar because both suggest calm endurance without complaint; differs because stoicism is a philosophy of suppressing emotion altogether, while forbearance is a moral restraint that may still involve feeling — it is about action, not feelinglessness.
