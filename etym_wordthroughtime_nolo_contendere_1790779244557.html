- Classical Antiquity: Classical Latin phrase *nōlō contendere* ("I do not wish to strive or litigate") used in Roman forensic rhetoric and procedural dialogue.
- 14th–16th centuries: Formalized in English royal criminal courts as an implied confession (*non vult contendere cum domino rege*), allowing petty offenders to pay a fine to the Crown without formal felony conviction.
- 19th–20th centuries: Codified across Anglo-American statutory law (e.g. U.S. Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 11) as a major defense instrument in corporate, antitrust, and white-collar prosecutions to avoid civil estoppel.
- Late 20th–21st centuries: Expanded into literary criticism and educated prose as a metaphor for yielding ground or declining to debate while refusing to confess intellectual error.
