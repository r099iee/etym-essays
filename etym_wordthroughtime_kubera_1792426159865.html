- [Late Vedic Era (c. 1000–800 BCE)] - first recorded in the Atharvaveda and Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa as Kuvera, a mysterious chthonic spirit and chief of shadowy subterranean beings and evil spirits - [perception: feared as an uncanny, powerful lord of hidden earth-spirits]
- [Epic & Early Classical Period (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)] - transformed in the Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata into the noble son of Viśravas, granted immortality by Brahmā and elevated to Regent of the North (Lokapāla) and Lord of Wealth - [perception: revered as the divine archetype of boundless charity, royal treasure, and righteousness]
- [Gupta Classical Age (c. 4th–6th c. CE)] - immortalized by Kālidāsa in the Meghadūta as the ruler of the romantic celestial city Alakā on Mount Kailāsa, friend of Lord Śiva - [perception: celebrated in high Sanskrit poetry as the patron of beauty, arts, opulence, and sensory joy]
- [Pan-Asian Buddhist Diffusion (c. 3rd–12th c. CE)] - transmitted across the Silk Road and East Asia as Vaiśravaṇa (Japanese Bishamonten, Tibetan Jambhara), guardian of the northern direction and protector of the Dharma - [perception: venerated across China, Tibet, and Japan as a martial, armor-clad protector of temples and grantor of prosperity]
- [Modern Contemporary Era] - universally recognized across South Asia as the ultimate idiom for astronomical fortune (Kuber kā khazānā), worshipped alongside Goddess Lakshmi on Dhanteras - [perception: embraced as the supreme symbol of financial abundance and benevolent economic blessing]
