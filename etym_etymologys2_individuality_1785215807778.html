INDIVIDUALITY is formed within English from individual + the abstract suffix -ity; individual descends through Latin from two Indo-European roots that meet in the verb dividere. Two converging chains: Chain 1 — the element dis- "apart, in two": - PIE *dwis- "two, in two, apart" (reconstructed; c. 4500–3500 BCE; cognates include Sanskrit dvā́ "two", Greek dí- "twice") → Latin dis- "apart, asunder, in two" (attested from the earliest Latin, e.g. Plautus, 3rd–2nd c. BCE) → used as the first element of dividere "to force apart" (see Chain 2). Chain 2 — the verbal base weidh- "to separate": - PIE *weidh- "to separate, divide, part" (reconstructed; c. 4500–3500 BCE; the root of Latin vidua "widow" and English widow via Germanic) → Latin viduus "bereft, widowed; separated" (attested 1st c. BCE; feminine vidua "widow", literally "the separated woman") → Latin dividere "to force apart, separate, divide" [dis- "apart" + *-videre "to separate"] (attested Plautus, c. 200 BCE) → Latin dividuus "divisible" (attested in technical Latin, 1st c. CE) → Latin individuus "indivisible, not separable" [in- "not" + dividuus] (attested Cicero and Lucretius, 1st c. BCE) → Late Latin individuum "an indivisible thing; a single unit, an individual" (substantivized; used to render Greek á-tomos "uncut, indivisible"; Boethius, 5th–6th c.) → English individual (adjective "indivisible, single", c. 1450; noun "a single human being", c. 1600) → English individuality "the state of being a distinct, self-determining person" [individual + -ity, from Latin -itas] (first recorded early 17th c.; the moral-political sense fixed in the 18th–19th c., especially through J. S. Mill) → Modern English INDIVIDUALITY. Note on debate: dividere is traditionally analyzed as dis- + a base from *weidh-, with dis- linked to *dwis- "two" (hence "split in two"), but the exact analysis of dis- is debated and some scholars treat it as an independent Latin prefix of uncertain ancestry; the link to viduus/vidua is secure, and the philosophical lineage through Greek á-tomos and Scholastic individuum is well attested.
