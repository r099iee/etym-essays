• Maria: Similar because it represents the exact same Biblical onomastic root (*Miryam*); differs because Maria is the ecclesiastical, Latinate, and standard modern Russian official passport spelling, whereas Marya is the historic East Slavic secular and literary form.
  • Mary: Similar because it is derived from the same Hebrew ancestor (*Miryam*); differs because Mary is the standard Anglicized vernacular equivalent dominant in the English-speaking world rather than the Slavic linguistic sphere.
  • Miriam: Similar because it is the original Biblical Hebrew source name; differs because Miriam retains its pristine Semitic phonology and Old Testament associations, whereas Marya reflects Greek, Church Slavonic, and East Slavic historical phonetic adaptations.
  • Masha: Similar because it designates the exact same Russian personal name; differs because Masha is the informal, everyday diminutive/hypocoristic used among intimates, whereas Marya is the full formal given name.
  • Marusya: Similar because it is an affectionate Russian hypocoristic of Marya/Maria; differs because Marusya carries a distinctively lyrical, folkloric, pastoral, and musical emotional register.
  • Marie: Similar because it shares the same Semitic origin; differs because Marie is the French realization, which was heavily utilized by the 19th-century Russian aristocracy in Francophone letters and court conversation.
  • Manya: Similar because it is an intimate Russian diminutive of Marya; differs because Manya carries a softer, more domestic, provincial, and cozy familial tone compared to the universal diminutive Masha.
