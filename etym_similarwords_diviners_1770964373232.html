• prophets: Similar because both reveal hidden or future truths and deal with the divine; differs because a prophet delivers unsolicited messages directly from a deity with moral urgency and a calling, while a diviner actively uses rituals, tools, and methods to seek answers to specific questions on behalf of clients.
  • soothsayers: Similar because both foretell events by interpreting signs; differs because soothsayer (literally "truth-sayer") emphasizes speaking the truth aloud in public and is now archaic and faintly comic, while diviner stresses systematic ritual interpretation and retains a scholarly, respectful seriousness.
  • seers: Similar because both are people who perceive what is hidden or to come; differs because a seer is one who sees visions in a passive, inspired way, whereas a diviner works actively through external techniques such as casting lots or reading omens.
  • augurs: Similar because both are ancient interpreters of divine signs, and the augur is a specific kind of diviner; differs because an augur is strictly the Roman state priest who read the flight and cries of birds to authorize public acts, while a diviner is the general term covering all such specialists.
  • oracles: Similar because both are channels of hidden divine knowledge; differs because an oracle is either the sanctified person (like the Delphic priestess) or the pronouncement itself, delivered under possession, whereas a diviner is a working practitioner who interprets signs rather than being possessed.
  • astrologers: Similar because both use signs to obtain hidden knowledge of the future; differs because astrologers specialize exclusively in the positions and motions of celestial bodies, forming a narrow, codified subset of the broader practice of divination.
  • haruspices: Similar because both are ancient Roman ritual specialists reading divine will; differs because a haruspex read specifically the entrails of sacrificed animals, a single technical method, while a diviner covers every method from birds to lots to dreams.
  • fortune-tellers: Similar because both are people who claim to predict the future; differs because a fortune-teller is the modern, commercial, often informal practitioner (palms, tarot, crystal balls) selling readings for money, while a diviner carries the older, institutional, ritually grounded weight of the word.
  • psychics: Similar because both claim access to knowledge beyond ordinary perception; differs because a psychic relies on claimed inner mental or spiritual sensitivity without traditional ritual tools, while a diviner works through external, methodical signs and ceremonies.
  • clairvoyants: Similar because both access hidden or future knowledge; differs because a clairvoyant (French for "clear-seeing") depends on claimed spontaneous inner sight, whereas a diviner uses external rituals, tools, and interpretive systems.
  • mediums: Similar because both mediate between ordinary people and the hidden world; differs because a medium communicates specifically with the spirits of the dead, whereas a diviner reads signs and omens and is not necessarily in contact with the departed.
  • dowsers: Similar because a dowser is the modern water- or mineral-diviner, locating hidden underground things with rods; differs because dowsing is the specialized, quasi-technical trade of finding water and ores, while diviner is the general term for all practitioners of divination, including future-reading.
