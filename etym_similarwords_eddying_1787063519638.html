• Swirling: Similar because both describe circular, rotating fluid or particulate motion; differs because swirling is a broader, generic descriptor for any spinning movement, whereas eddying specifically emphasizes the countercurrent, turbulent reversal against a main flow, or localized hydrodynamic vortex.
  • Whirling: Similar because both involve rapid rotational movement; differs because whirling emphasizes extreme speed, dizziness, and intense centrifugal force, whereas eddying can be gentle, meandering, backflowing, and complexly layered.
  • Vortexing: Similar because both describe helical fluid motion; differs because vortexing is a formal, technical hydrodynamic term focusing on low-pressure cores and suction, lacking the literary, evocative imagery of eddying.
  • Rippling: Similar because both describe surface disturbances in water; differs because rippling consists of small undulating linear waves spreading outwards, whereas eddying consists of circular rotating currents and backflows.
  • Churning: Similar because both describe violent or agitated liquid; differs because churning emphasizes violent, frothing, and opaque vertical agitation (like a butter churn or stormy sea), while eddying focuses on horizontal or spatial rotational loops.
  • Meandering: Similar because both convey indirect, non-linear fluid travel; differs because meandering describes the broad, slow, snaking bends of an entire river channel across a valley, rather than localized spinning countercurrents.
  • Spiraling: Similar because both involve curved rotational trajectory; differs because spiraling strictly implies expanding outward or contracting inward in a geometric spiral, whereas eddying includes irregular, shifting, and turbulent circular flow.
  • Billowing: Similar because both can describe smoke or vapor clouds; differs because billowing emphasizes swelling outward in large, puffing masses (like sails filled with wind) rather than spinning in tight reverse loops.
