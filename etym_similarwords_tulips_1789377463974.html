• Daffodils: Similar because both are popular spring-flowering geophytes cultivated from underground bulbs that bloom in early spring; differs because daffodils belong to the amaryllis family (Narcissus) and feature a prominent trumpet-shaped central corona surrounded by six petals, whereas tulips belong to the lily family (Tulipa) and bear cup-shaped blossoms without a central trumpet.
  • Crocuses: Similar because both are cup-shaped vernal flowers that emerge from underground storage organs; differs because crocuses grow from solid corms rather than tunicate bulbs, belong to the iris family (Iridaceae) with only three stamens, and bloom much lower to the ground weeks before tulips.
  • Hyacinths: Similar because both are fragrant, bulbous spring ornamentals cultivated extensively in the Dutch floral trade; differs because hyacinths (Hyacinthus) produce a dense, cylindrical raceme crowded with dozens of intensely fragrant, star-shaped florets, whereas a tulip typically produces a single, large, scentless or mildly scented terminal cup.
  • Lilies: Similar because both belong to the family Liliaceae and feature six showy tepals and stamens; differs because true lilies (genus Lilium) grow on tall, leafy stems from scaly bulbs that bloom in mid-to-late summer, possessing flared, outward-facing trumpets with prominent protruding anthers, unlike the compact spring cups of tulips.
  • Anemones: Similar because both produce brilliant, saucer- or cup-shaped blossoms in saturated reds, purples, and yellows; differs because anemones belong to the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae), grow from knobby tubers, and have dark, button-like central pistil clusters completely lacking the bulbous morphology of tulips.
  • Poppies: Similar because both display vibrant, delicate, tissue-thin petals forming cupped blossoms of blazing scarlet and yellow; differs because poppies belong to the Papaveraceae family, are annuals or herbaceous perennials grown from tiny seed grains rather than underground bulbs, and yield distinctive capsule seed pods containing milky latex.
