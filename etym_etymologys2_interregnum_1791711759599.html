Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*h₁en-ter-
"between, among"
+
*h₃rḗǵ-
"to rule, lead straight, king"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*enter + *rēg-no-
"between" + "kingdom, rule"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 6th–5th c. BCE)
inter + rēgnum
"between" + "royal rule, reign"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 5th c. BCE)
interrēgnum
"interval between two reigns"
↓
Renaissance Latin / French (c. 15th–16th c.)
interregnum / interrègne
"period between sovereign reigns"
↓
Modern English (c. 1570)
interregnum
"gap between reigns; hiatus"

Note: Latin interrēgnum is a compound of the preposition inter ('between') and the second-declension neuter noun rēgnum ('royal authority, realm, reign', derived from rēx, 'king', from PIE *h₃rḗǵ-). English borrowed the Latin word directly in the late 16th century, retaining its classical Latin nominative singular form.
