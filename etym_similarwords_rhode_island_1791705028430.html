• Providence Plantations: Similar because it formed the second half of the state's official constitutional name from 1663 until 2020; differs because Providence Plantations specifically designated the mainland continental settlements founded by Roger Williams, whereas Rhode Island originally designated the island of Aquidneck in the bay.
  • Aquidneck Island: Similar because it is the exact physical landmass within Narragansett Bay that was officially renamed "Rhode Island" by colonial magistrates in 1644; differs because Aquidneck is the preserved native Narragansett Indian name used locally today to distinguish the physical island from the entire surrounding state.
  • The Ocean State: Similar because both refer directly to the state of Rhode Island; differs because The Ocean State is the modern official promotional nickname adopted on license plates and signage to celebrate its 400 miles of tidal shoreline, whereas Rhode Island is the formal geographic and constitutional name.
  • Little Rhody: Similar because both identify the state of Rhode Island; differs because Little Rhody is an affectionate, colloquial, and folksy moniker highlighting the state's diminutive geographic size as the nation's smallest state.
  • Connecticut: Similar because both are founding New England colonies sharing a long land border and maritime heritage; differs because Connecticut was established under orthodox Puritan covenant theology (the Fundamental Orders) and possesses four times the land area, whereas Rhode Island was created as an open sanctuary for radical religious outcasts.
  • Massachusetts: Similar because both are adjacent New England coastal states central to early American history; differs because Massachusetts was the Puritan theocracy whose magistrates banished Roger Williams and Anne Hutchinson, representing the rigid civil-religious orthodoxy that Rhode Island was explicitly founded to resist.
  • Block Island: Similar because it is an island territory within the legal borders of the State of Rhode Island; differs because Block Island lies twelve miles south in the open Atlantic Ocean (named after Dutch navigator Adriaen Block), distinct from Aquidneck Island / Rhode Island proper within Narragansett Bay.
