• Hustled: Similar because both mean hurried energetically through a crowd or task; differs because hustled is pushier and more physical (hustled off the stage), while bustled is fussier and friendlier — hospitality rather than force.
  • Scurried: Similar because both describe quick short-legged darting (Maggot-like in-and-out motion); differs because scurried suggests smallness and timidity (mice scurry), while bustled suggests cheerful self-importance.
  • Hurried: Similar because both denote fast movement under time pressure; differs because hurried is plain and neutral (hurried to the station), while bustled adds noise, fuss, and visible flurry.
  • Fussed: Similar because both involve anxious attention to small details; differs because fussed stresses worry and complaint (fussed over nothing), while bustled stresses energetic motion — fuss with its shoes on.
  • Fluttered: Similar because both picture light agitated movement around a centre; differs because fluttered is delicate and nervous (fluttered around the patient), while bustled is sturdier and more purposeful.
  • Fidgeted: Similar because both are restless small movements; differs because fidgeted stays in one seat (fidgeted through the sermon), while bustled travels — in and out, up and down, room to room.
  • Dashed: Similar because both are sudden quick movements; differs because dashed is one straight fast line (dashed for the train), while bustled is many short trips stitched into a flurry.
  • Scuttled: Similar because both are brisk low-to-the-ground hurries; differs because scuttled hints at fleeing or guilt (scuttled away, crabs scuttle), while bustled is open, upright, and socially cheerful.
  • Bestirred itself: Similar because both describe a place or person springing into activity; differs because bestirred is literary and marks the start of motion, while bustled paints the noisy middle of it.
