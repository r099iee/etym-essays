Note on multiple roots and phonology: As an ancient compound noun, Ṛśyaśṛn̄ga preserves two distinct archaic Indo-Iranian roots in perfect isolation. Root 1, ṛ́śya, is an Indo-Iranian zoological term for the native horned game of the Asian steppes and northern subcontinent (attested in the Ṛgveda and cognate with Pashto rōsh and Khotanese rrūsa-). Root 2, śṛṅga, derives from the exceptionally widespread PIE root for head-horns (*ḱer-), exhibiting a characteristic Indo-Aryan nasalized zero-grade extension (*ḱr̥-n-g-) parallel to Latin cornū and English horn. The spelling variant with the velar nasal with macron (n̄ / ṅ) reflects standard 19th-century Indological transliteration of the Sanskrit conjunct ṅga (ङ्ग).
