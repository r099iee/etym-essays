Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed; debated)
*ger-
"to turn, wind, bend"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE; reconstructed)
*kringaną
"to turn, bend, give way, fall"
↓
Old English (9th–11th c.)
cringan
"to yield, give way, fall, become bent"
↓
Middle English (early 13th c.)
crengen
"to bend low, cower, stoop"
↓
Modern English (present)
cringing
Note on the debate: The link to PIE *ger- is speculative and not universally accepted; some scholars treat *kringaną as an isolated Germanic formation with no secure Indo-European cognates. The Middle English sense may also have been reinforced by Old Norse krengja "to cause to bend, twist". No stage before Old English is directly attested, so treat the Proto-Germanic root as the earliest secure point.
