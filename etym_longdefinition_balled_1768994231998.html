In the sentence "his hands balled in the cotton pockets of his cheap coat," the past participle describes the boy's hands clenched into tight spheres inside his pockets — fists made, knuckles hidden. The word is physical and almost anatomical: to ball a hand is to gather it into a round, hard shape, the fingers folded over the palm like the tightening of a muscle. Here the gesture is read as tension made visible — a small body of anger, fear, or readiness held in the fists while the rest of him walks on through the southern night. The tone is restrained and cinematic, the narrator registering the boy's inner state through a single bodily detail, and "balled" gives that detail a hard, rounded, almost weapon-like solidity: the pocket hides the fist, but the reader feels its shape.

Primary sense: balled is the past tense and past participle of the verb to ball — to form into a ball, to make round and compact. Of a hand or fist it means clenched into a sphere; of dough or snow it means rolled into a round mass.

Polysemy and related senses:

  • Hands/fists: "balled fists," "hands balled in pockets" — clenched, rounded, tight; the core sense of the card.
  • Substance: to ball dough, to ball cotton, to ball snow — to gather a pliable substance into a ball; a horse "balls" a hoof with packed snow.
  • Phrasal verb "ball up": (1) to clench or gather into a ball ("his hands balled up"); (2) figuratively, in American slang, to confuse or tangle ("I got all balled up") — the image of a thing knotted into a tangled sphere.
  • Body parts: "the ball of the foot," "the ball of the thumb" — the rounded, padded parts of the body, the same roundness named by the noun.
  • Noun "ball" (the underlying word): a round object — a sphere for play (football, tennis ball); a globular mass; and the homophone "a formal dance" (a DIFFERENT word from Greek ballizein "to dance," via Latin and French) — must not be conflated in etymology.

Synonym boundaries:

  • Clenched: stresses the hard, closed grip of the fist — the most common word for an angry hand; balled adds the image of roundness, the hand become a sphere.
  • Fisted: blunter, naming the fist without the image of curvature.
  • Curled: a gentler, less aggressive gathering; balled is hard, compact, almost weapon-like.
  • Knotted: tense, painful bunches of muscle; balled is a clean sphere — readiness rather than spasm.
  • Folded: passive and weak — the arms crossed or hands loosely joined, the opposite of the tight, stored energy of balled.

Register: plain, concrete, and slightly colloquial, at home in narrative prose of the mid-twentieth century and after. "Balled" in cotton pockets also plays on the softness of the fabric against the hardness of the fist, an opposition the other synonyms do not set up.
