- [Early Vedic Period, c. 1500–1200 BCE] - revered as the inspired master-poet of Mandala 7 of the Ṛgveda, personal priest of King Sudās in the Battle of the Ten Kings, and author of the Mahāmṛtyuñjaya Mantra - [perception: celebrated as the supreme mediator of divine favor and the master of sacred invocations to Varuṇa and Mitra]
- [Late Vedic & Brāhmaṇa Era, c. 1000–600 BCE] - codified as the archetypal Brahmin ritualist and royal counselor, author of the Vasiṣṭha Dharmasūtra, establishing rules for sacred law and royal conduct - [perception: respected as the foundational pillar of priestly orthodoxy, ritual purity, and societal law]
- [Epic & Purāṇic Period, c. 500 BCE–300 CE] - immortalized in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa as the patient royal guru of Ayodhyā who defeated Viśvāmitra's military might through pure ascetic forbearance - [perception: revered as the ultimate embodiment of spiritual supremacy (brahmatēja) and patient forgiveness over brute physical aggression]
- [Medieval Advaitic Renaissance, c. 10th–16th c. CE] - re-envisioned as the supreme enlightened master of non-dual consciousness and cosmic illusion in the monumental treatise Yoga Vāsiṣṭha - [perception: celebrated as an unparalleled spiritual philosopher who revealed that the entire cosmos is pure consciousness]
- [Modern Era, 19th–21st c.] - preserved in celestial astronomy as the star Mizar (orbiting with his wife Arundhatī as Alcor) and remembered in wedding rituals as the eternal symbol of marital harmony and spiritual wisdom - [perception: venerated across the Hindu diaspora as one of the seven eternal spiritual patriarchs of Indian civilization]
