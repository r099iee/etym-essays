thine is the archaic second-person singular possessive pronoun corresponding to modern yours, and, as a possessive determiner before vowels and silent h, corresponding to modern your. It belongs to the obsolete thou / thee / thy / thine paradigm that was once the ordinary singular familiar form in English, preserved today chiefly in the King James Bible, Shakespeare, liturgy, hymns, poetry, and deliberate archaism.

In its core grammatical use, thine has two closely related functions that mirror the modern mine / my distinction. As a possessive pronoun standing alone, it means that which belongs to thee: This house is thine means This house is yours. As a prenominal determiner before a vowel or silent h, it means thy: thine eyes, thine honour, thine inheritance. The consonant rule was systematic in Early Modern English: thy before consonants (thy sword), thine before vowels (thine army), exactly parallel to a / an and my / mine in the same period, where mine eyes later became my eyes.

Beyond grammar, thine carries a powerful stylistic and affective charge. Because it vanished from ordinary speech by the eighteenth century, its appearance instantly signals solemnity, intimacy, reverence, antiquity, or poetic elevation. Thou and thine could express tender love, prayerful closeness to God, noble dignity, or, in some contexts, condescension to an inferior. Quakers famously retained thou / thee / thine long after others abandoned it precisely to refuse honorific social distinction and to assert spiritual equality and inward intimacy.

  • Possessive pronoun = yours (absolute use): What is mine is thine. I am thine, save me. Here thine stands alone as subject complement or object, needing no following noun, exactly as yours does today. The semantic bridge is direct possession by the singular thou-addressee.
  • Possessive determiner before vowels / h = your: Thine eyes have seen; thine heart; thine honour. Before consonants Early Modern English preferred thy, but thine appears regularly before vowels for euphony, avoiding hiatus. Modern editions often regularize, but original texts preserve the distribution.
  • Vocative and devotional use: In prayer, hymns, and scripture, thine expresses consecration and belonging: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory. Here possession broadens into sovereignty, stewardship, and divine right, not merely ownership.
  • Literary and romantic address: In Shakespeare and later poetry, thine marks erotic, friendly, or rhetorical intimacy: Shall I compare thee to a summer's day? Thou art more lovely. Thine eyes. The archaism creates closeness and elevation at once, distinguishing a singular beloved thou from plural or formal you.
  • Ethical and philosophical use: Phrases such as to thine own self be true use thine reflexively to mean your own essential self, property extended inward to identity, conscience, and integrity. The possessive becomes a moral claim about authenticity.
  • Fixed idioms and survivals: Thine own, thine ever, ever thine in letters; For thine is in the Lord's Prayer; Thine be the glory in hymns. These frozen forms survive as quotations even among speakers who never use thou spontaneously.

The reason thine developed both determiner and pronoun senses is phonological and analogical. Old English thin could already be used attributively and absolutely. Middle English preserved mine / my and thine / thy as doublets conditioned by following sound. When final -n was lost before consonants but retained before vowels and in isolation for stress, the pair split functionally. Later, when you / your / yours wholly displaced thou in standard speech, thine was stranded as a marked archaic variant, and its meaning narrowed socially to signal pastness and solemnity rather than ordinary possession.

For the learner, treat thine as a direct code-switch: if you can say yours, you can say thine absolutely; if you can say your before a vowel in a biblical line, you can say thine. Do not use it in modern prose except for deliberate effect, quotation, prayer, or verse, where its resonance is precisely its distance from everyday yours.
