- c.900-1200 Old Norse bolr trunk - everyday word for stem of tree, mast, and pillar - practical-seafaring, neutral
- mid-14th c. Middle English bole tree trunk - borrowed into northern English and Scots forestry - woodman's term, massive-timber sense
- 15th-17th c. literary bole - Chaucerian and Elizabethan poets use bole for aged oaks and pines - venerable-picturesque, shading into cathedral imagery
- 18th-19th c. scientific forestry bole - bole length, bole volume, clear bole become mensuration terms - technical-commercial, empire timber trade
- Modern ecological-literary boles - Tolkien, fantasy, and conservation writing revive giant boles as ancient beings; foresters still cruise boles - split between wonder (old growth) and commodity (board-feet)
- Note: red bole pigment runs parallel as separate word from Greek-Latin, never merging in meaning despite identical spelling
