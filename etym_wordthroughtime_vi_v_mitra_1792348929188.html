- [Rigvedic Period, c. 1500–1000 BCE] — historical Vedic rishi, chief poet of the Bharata clan, author of Rigveda Mandala 3 and composer of the sacred Gayatri Mantra (RV 3.62.10) - [perception: supreme spiritual seer, celestial conduit of divine revelation, and revered progenitor of the Vishvamitra gotra]
- [Late Vedic & Brahmana Period, c. 1000–600 BCE] — featured in liturgical narratives (such as the Aitareya Brahmana story of Shunahshepa) challenging established sacrificial conventions - [perception: an uncompromising reformer of ritual and compassionate protector of the oppressed]
- [Epic Sanskrit Era: Ramayana & Mahabharata, c. 500 BCE–300 CE] — fully mythologized as King Kaushika, the Kshatriya monarch whose bitter rivalry with Sage Vashistha drove him to achieve Brahmarshi status through unfathomable tapas; mentor of Rama and creator of Trishanku's southern heaven - [perception: an indomitable titan of spiritual willpower, defying celestial hierarchy, capable of terrifying curses and divine mentorship]
- [Classical Puranic Era, c. 300–1200 CE] — celebrated in classical kavya, drama, and puranas as the exemplar of spiritual ascendance overcoming the temptations of apsaras (Menaka, Rambha) - [perception: the absolute symbol of mortal perseverance transcending caste and cosmic limitations]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era] — immortalized across Indian literature, drama, television, and spiritual discourse as a cultural icon of egalitarian spiritual achievement and righteous self-determination - [perception: the visionary archetype of divine courage whose tapas revolutionized the Vedic worldview]
