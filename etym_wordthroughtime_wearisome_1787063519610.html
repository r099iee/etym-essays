- Late 14th to 15th Century: [Physical Labor & Toil] - Erupted into late Middle English literature (*werisom*) denoting that which causes bodily fatigue, physical toil, or severe somatic distress; associated with arduous medieval pilgrimages, chivalric campaigns, and ascetic monastic vigils.
- 16th to 17th Century: [Spiritual & Existential Weight] - Flourished across Early Modern English prose and drama (including Shakespeare and the King James Bible) to express the heavy burden of mortal life, spiritual melancholy (*tedium vitae*), and the futility of worldly vanity.
- 18th to 19th Century: [Social Etiquette, Pedagogy & Victorian Monotony] - Shifted into polite social commentary and literary criticism to describe dull company, long-winded pedagogical treatises, formal societal rituals, and Victorian industrial routines.
- 20th Century to Present: [Bureaucratic Red Tape & Everyday Exasperation] - Maintained high literary resonance while being widely applied to corporate meetings, bureaucratic compliance, legal gridlock, and mentally depleting modern routines.
