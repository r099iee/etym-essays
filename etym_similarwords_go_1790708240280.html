• Proceed: Similar because both denote moving forward along a path or course of action; differs because proceed belongs to a formal, Latinate register, often implying resumption after an interruption or adherence to formal procedural steps, whereas go is fundamental, Germanic, and universally applicable.
  • Travel: Similar because both involve physical spatial transit; differs because travel emphasizes journeying over significant distances or through unfamiliar territories for recreation or business, whereas go can denote the simplest micro-movement or abstract state transition.
  • Extend: Similar because both can describe the physical or abstract reach of something up to a boundary (as in 'how far something goes'); differs because extend focuses on elongation, outward stretching, or granting of duration/privileges, lacking the dynamic kinetic baseline of go.
  • Depart: Similar because both involve moving away from an origin; differs because depart is a formal verb focusing specifically on the moment of leaving a station, schedule, or life, whereas go encompasses both departure, ongoing transit, and ultimate destination.
  • Suffice: Similar because in contexts of financial or resource limits ('money only goes so far'), both convey adequacy; differs because suffice is a static evaluative verb meaning 'to be enough', whereas go conveys the active expenditure or reach across a span.
  • Wend: Similar because both historically denote motion; differs because wend is an archaic, poetic verb meaning to direct one's way slowly or meanderingly, though it famously donated its past tense form (went) to go.
