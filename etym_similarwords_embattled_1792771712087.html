• Beleaguered: Similar because both mean beset and under siege; differs because beleaguered stresses prolonged encirclement and exhaustion (a beleaguered city), while embattled stresses active fighting and defiance.
  • Besieged: Similar because both describe being surrounded by hostile forces; differs because besieged is literal siege-warfare (ringed about, cut off), while embattled is broader — any opposition, from armies to auditors.
  • Beset: Similar because both mean attacked by troubles on all sides; differs because beset is plain and general (‘beset by doubts’), while embattled adds martial nobility — a fighter holding ground.
  • Harassed: Similar because both describe being worn down by persistent attacks; differs because harassed stresses wearying, repeated pinpricks (often stressed mothers and managers), while embattled suggests grander, more public conflict.
  • Arrayed: Similar because Tolkien's ‘embattled army’ is literally an arrayed army; differs because arrayed is neutral ordering (troops, data), while embattled adds the imminence of combat.
  • Fortified: Similar because both describe preparation for attack (walls, positions); differs because fortified is about strengthened defences (concrete, wine, milk!), while embattled is about being under attack or crowned with battlements.
  • Crenellated: Similar because both describe notched castle parapets; differs because crenellated is the precise architectural term for the gap-toothed pattern, while embattled is the older poetic equivalent.
  • Castellated: Similar because both mean built like a castle with battlements; differs because castellated describes mock-castle ornament (Victorian mansions, radiator grilles), while embattled can mean genuinely war-ready.
  • Defiant: Similar because the embattled figure typically refuses to yield; differs because defiant names the attitude of resistance itself, while embattled names the situation of being attacked.
