- 16th century - champ, probably imitative, means to chew noisily and vigorously; horses champ their fodder or their bit.
- 17th-19th centuries - 'champing at the bit' becomes an idiom for restless eagerness: the horse biting and worrying its bit, impatient to be off.
- 19th-20th centuries - the American variant chomp emerges, heavier and more casual; champing keeps its eager, gripping energy in British English.
- Contemporary - champing is the word of eager, satisfied feeding and of impatient gripping, the sound of a horse gulping its oats.
