• Shattered: Similar because both mean broken violently into pieces; differs because shattering produces many irregular fragments of all shapes, while splintering produces long, thin, sharp slivers.
  • Smashed: Similar because both describe destructive impact; differs because smashed stresses the loud, crushing blow itself, while splintered names the resulting sharp fragments.
  • Split: Similar because both belong to the same Germanic family of dividing; differs because split names a clean division along a line or seam, while splintered is a messy fragmentation into many pieces.
  • Cracked: Similar because both describe damage to a solid; differs because cracked means fissured without full separation, while splintered is complete, final breakage.
  • Fractured: Similar because both name a break in a solid body; differs because fractured is the clinical or geological term for a clean break, while splintered implies sharp, multiple fragments.
  • Crumbled: Similar because both describe falling apart; differs because crumbling is gradual disintegration into small soft particles, while splintering is a sharp, sudden fragmentation.
  • Broken: Similar because both mean damaged into pieces; differs because broken is the broad, neutral term, while splintered specifies thin, sharp, needle-like fragments.
  • Sundered: Similar because both mean divided; differs because sundered is poetic and abstract — partings, ties — while splintered is physical and concrete.
  • Rended / Torn: Similar because both mean pulled apart violently; differs because rending and tearing are ragged and without a blade or grain, while splintering follows the natural lines of wood or bone.
  • Fragmenting: Similar because both mean breaking into parts; differs because fragmenting is the neutral technical term for division into fragments, while splintered specifies the long, sharp shape of the pieces.
