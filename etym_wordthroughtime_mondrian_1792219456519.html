- 17th–19th c. — Existed in the Netherlands as a regional Dutch family surname (*Mondriaan*, with earlier 17th-century parish records showing *Monderyan*).
- 1911–1912 — Pieter Cornelis Mondriaan moves from Amsterdam to Paris and deliberately drops the second "a" from his surname, adopting **Piet Mondrian** to signal his Parisian integration and avant-garde international identity.
- 1917–1920s — Co-founds De Stijl; the name *Mondrian* becomes the ideological lightning rod across Europe for radical geometric abstraction, fiercely attacked by academic traditionalists and venerated by the avant-garde.
- 1930s–1940s — Welcomed in London and New York; exhibited at the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), solidifying *Mondrian* in American culture as a foundational pioneer of twentieth-century modernism.
- 1960s — Leaps spectacularly from fine art into global mass culture, inspiring Yves Saint Laurent's iconic 1965 "Mondrian Collection" of color-block cocktail dresses and mid-century modern interior design.
- 21st c. — Universally recognized worldwide as an iconic synonym for orthogonal grid architecture, primary color theory, and pure geometric visual harmony.
