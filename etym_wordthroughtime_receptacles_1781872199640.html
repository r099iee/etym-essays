- Container in early English - Since the 14th-15th centuries receptacles (from Latin receptaculum) have named any vessel that receives and holds things, a plain Latinate word for a container.
- Botanical and electrical senses - By the 18th century it gains the botanical sense of the flower's receptacle; by the 19th-20th centuries, the household and electrical sense of a socket that receives a plug.
- Aesthetic and philosophical metaphor - In late-Victorian criticism (Pater) the plural becomes a charged metaphor — works of art as 'receptacles of power or virtue', vessels charged with effect — so the word splits between mundane household use and elevated metaphysical storage.
