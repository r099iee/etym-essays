• Pitter-patter: Similar because it is the full reduplicative pair of which pitter is the lighter half; differs because the pair names the whole double-beat rhythm (rain, feet, heart), while pitter alone isolates the smaller, quicker element.
  • Patter: Similar because both name light rapid tapping sounds and are rhyming twins; differs because patter has a fuller, heavier vowel and names the whole rapid succession, while pitter, with its short closed vowel, sounds smaller, faster, and more delicate.
  • Pit-a-pat (pitapat): Similar because both imitate the same quick trembling beat of the heart; differs because pit-a-pat is the older adverbial formula (“my heart went pit-a-pat”), while pitter is chiefly a noun or the half of a reduplicative.
  • Tick: Similar because both are tiny, quick, regular sounds; differs because tick is mechanical and regular (a clock, a timer), whereas pitter is organic, tremulous, and uneven.
  • Tap: Similar because both name light striking sounds; differs because tap is a single discrete impact, while pitter is the small, repeated, fluttering variant.
  • Flutter: Similar because both suggest a light, rapid, tremulous motion or sound; differs because flutter is chiefly motion (wings, eyelids) and can be silent, while pitter is a sound made by tiny repeated impacts.
  • Throb: Similar because both describe a beating pulse; differs because throb is a deep, full, regular pulse felt in the body, while pitter is a light, rapid, almost audible heartbeat — small where throb is heavy.
  • Palpitate: Similar because both describe a rapid, fluttering heartbeat; differs because palpitate is the formal, medical verb for an abnormally racing pulse, while pitter is the homely onomatopoeic noun.
  • Rustle: Similar because both are soft, light, quick sounds; differs because rustle is the dry rubbing of surfaces (leaves, paper), while pitter is percussive — tiny impacts rather than friction.
  • Rat-a-tat: Similar because both name rapid repeated light sounds; differs because rat-a-tat is louder and more percussive, suggesting a drum or knock, while pitter is tiny and intimate.
  • Purr: Similar because both are soft rhythmic sounds of pleasure or contentment; differs because purr is a continuous low vibration, while pitter is a rapid series of separate light taps.
  • Whisper: Similar because both are extremely soft; differs because whisper is breathy and continuous, of speech or wind, while pitter is percussive and rhythmic.
