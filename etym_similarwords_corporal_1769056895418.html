• sergeant: Similar in being a non-commissioned officer of the ranks; differs because a sergeant holds a higher NCO rank, commands a section, and enforces discipline, while a corporal commands a smaller squad and is the lowest NCO.
  • private: Similar in belonging to the same chain of command; differs because a private is the base rank with no authority at all, while the corporal is the first step above it — the first man with a little authority over others.
  • lance corporal: Similar in being a junior non-commissioned rank leading a small body of soldiers; differs because the lance corporal is an acting or provisional step below the full rank, while the corporal proper is the established lowest non-commissioned rank.
  • lieutenant: Similar in being a military rank that leads men; differs because a lieutenant is a commissioned officer, holding authority by commission and standing apart from the ranks, while a corporal is promoted from the ranks and remains close to the men he leads.
  • captain: Similar in being a rank of command over soldiers; differs because a captain is a commissioned officer commanding a company, far above the corporal, who leads only a squad.
  • petty officer: Similar in being a non-commissioned rank of authority; differs because the petty officer is the naval equivalent, belonging to a different service culture, while the corporal is the army's junior NCO.
  • non-commissioned officer: Similar as the category to which the corporal belongs; differs because NCO is the general term for the whole class of ranks promoted from the ranks, while corporal names the specific lowest member of that class.
  • squad leader: Similar in describing one who commands a small body of soldiers; differs because squad leader is a functional role that may be held by any rank in a fire team, while corporal is the specific, institutional rank.
  • corporal (adjective): Similar in being the same English spelling; differs because the adjective "of the body" (Latin corporalis, from corpus) is a different word entirely — the homograph whose spelling remodelled the rank noun, which truly means "head".
  • corps: Similar in coming from the same corpus "body" family and meaning a body of troops; differs because a corps is a large, permanent military formation, while a corporal is the lowest individual rank who heads a tiny body of men.
