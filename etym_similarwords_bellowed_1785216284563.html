• roared: Similar because both name a loud, deep, powerful cry; differs because roared is more generic — used of fierce animals, rage, or a crowd — and lacks bellowed's specifically bovine resonance.
  • shouted: Similar because both name loud vocal volume; differs because shouted is merely loud speech, lacking the depth of register and animal timbre of a bellow.
  • yelled: Similar because both name a loud raised voice; differs because yelled suggests a higher, thinner, more panicked pitch rather than the deep chest-shaking boom of a bellow.
  • hollered: Similar because both name shouting loudly; differs because hollered is informal and rustic, without bellow's depth of register and command.
  • bawled: Similar because both name loud, unrestrained vocalizing; differs because bawled implies weeping or harsh, uncontrolled crying, while bellowed is deliberate, powerful, and commanding.
  • called: Similar because both mean speaking to someone; differs because called is neutral and untroubled, at the opposite end of the volume scale from a bellow.
