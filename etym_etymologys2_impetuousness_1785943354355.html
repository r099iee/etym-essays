*pet- (to rush, fly, spread the wings; Proto-Indo-European, reconstructed — one of the most securely attested roots of the family; reflexes include Sanskrit pátati "flies, falls," Greek pétomai "to fly," Latin petere "to seek, rush at")
→ Latin petere (to seek, aim at, attack, rush at; attested from early Latin, 3rd c. BCE)
→ Latin impetus (a rushing upon, an onset, an attack; violent impulse; formed from in- "into, upon" + petere; classical, 1st c. BCE)
→ Late Latin impetuōsus (violent, rushing, impetuous; derived adjective on impetus)
→ Old French impetueux (impetuous, violent; 12th c.)
→ Middle English impetuous (violent, raging, impetuous; 14th c.)
→ English IMPETUOUSNESS (impetuous + -ness; first recorded 16th c.) ◄ TARGET

Note: *pet- also yields Sanskrit pátati "flies," Greek pétomai "to fly" (whence pterón "wing," and English helicopter, pterodactyl), and Latin petere, whose descendants include petition, compete, appetite, and repeat. The chain above is the direct Latin line into English; the Greek and Indic branches are sisters, not ancestors.
