Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE)
*krak-
‘to make a sharp cracking noise’
(imitative stem)
↓
Old English (9th c.)
cracian
‘to crack, resound, boast’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
craken
‘to break with a snap’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
crack + -ed
‘snapped’ + participle ending
‘having a crack’
↓
Modern English (current)
cracked
‘split; mad; solved’

Note: crack is universally treated as imitative (sound-symbolic) Germanic vocabulary, cognate with Middle Dutch kraken and Old High German krahhon; handbook attempts to attach the Germanic stem to a deeper PIE root are speculative and not generally accepted, so the chain properly begins with the Germanic imitative stem.
