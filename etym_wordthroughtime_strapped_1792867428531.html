- Antiquity — the twisted band: Greek strophos and Latin stroppus name cords and thongs for harness, ships, and sandals.
- Old English — the workaday thong: strop binds books, harnesses horses, and sharpens blades in an age of leather.
- Middle English — strap with an a: northern dialect voices the vowel, and strappe spreads as the everyday band for packs and girths.
- 17th–18th c. — the verb takes hold: to strap is to bind, lash, and also to beat, the schoolroom strap becoming an instrument of discipline.
- 19th–20th c. — buckles and webbing: rucksacks, car seats, parachutes, and stretchers all get strapped, safety through tightness.
- Today — from rucksacks to slang: walkers strap packs while the broke are strapped for cash and the wary hear strapped as armed.
