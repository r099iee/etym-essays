Root 1 — RAMPARTS:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*perh₁-
"to provide, procure"
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE)
parare
"to prepare"
↓
Old French (10th c.)
parer
"to defend, prepare"
↓
Old French (11th c.)
emparer
"to fortify, take hold of"
(en- + parer)
↓
Old French (12th c.)
remparer
"to fortify"
(re- + emparer)
↓
Old French (12th c.)
rempart
"a rampart"
↓
English (16th c.)
rampart
"a defensive wall"
↓
ramparts
"defensive walls"

Root 2 — HOVELS:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*kew-
"to bend, arch"
(uncertain)
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*hofą
"a court, yard"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
hof
"a court, dwelling"
↓
Middle English (15th c.)
hovel
"a shed, hovel"
(possibly a diminutive of hof)
↓
English (16th c.)
hovel
"a small, wretched dwelling"
↓
hovels
"small, wretched dwellings"

Debate: the origin of hovel is uncertain — it may be a diminutive of Old English hof "court, dwelling", but this is not proven; the PIE root *kew- is likewise speculative.

Root 3 — LIMPETS:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*leb-
"to lick"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
lambere
"to lick"
↓
Latin (late Latin)
lampreda
"a lamprey, limpet"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
lempedu
"a limpet"
↓
English (13th c.)
limpet
"a small sea snail
that clings to rocks"

Debate: the same Latin word lampreda also yields lamprey. Its origin is uncertain — it is often explained as "rock-licker" (lambere "to lick" + petra "rock"), the shellfish clinging to rocks, but this is not securely established; the PIE root *leb- "to lick" is a supporting inference.
