- Medieval calendars - a "dismal day" was one of the unlucky, ill-omened dates, the "evil days" marked as inauspicious; the word carried doom and superstition from the very start.
- Early modern period - "dismal" widened from unlucky dates to any calamitous, dreary, or sinister gloom, and the adverb "dismally" took on its manner sense: gloomily, depressingly, wretchedly.
- Modern English - "dismally" still keeps that faint flavour of fated bad luck, so one fails "dismally" with a sense of inescapable doom and something "dismally real" feels not merely unpleasant but fated.
