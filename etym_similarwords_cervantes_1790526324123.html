• Shakespeare: Similar as a supreme early modern literary giant who fundamentally reshaped Western world literature and explored the depths of human nature (both dying within days of each other in April 1616); differs because William Shakespeare operated primarily in dramatic verse, theatrical staging, and poetic tragedy/comedy, whereas Cervantes pioneered the expansive prose architecture, psychological interiority, and structural polyphony of the modern novel.
  • Rabelais: Similar in deploying exuberant vernacular satire, bawdy humor, and carnivalesque parody to subvert medieval dogma and rigid scholastic traditions; differs because François Rabelais relies heavily on grotesque hyperbole, bodily farce, and encyclopedic humanist comedy in Gargantua and Pantagruel, whereas Cervantes tempers his comedic mockery with delicate psychological pathos, multi-layered philosophical irony, and poignant character development.
  • Boccaccio: Similar as a transformative Romance-language prose innovator who moved European narrative away from dry medieval allegories toward secular humanism; differs because Giovanni Boccaccio's Decameron consists of a framed anthology of discrete, self-contained novellas, whereas Cervantes sustained an integrated, evolving, character-driven journey where characters dynamically transform across hundreds of pages.
  • Sterne: Similar in deploying radical metafictional disruptions, self-conscious narrator commentary, and affectionate parody of conventional literary forms (directly inheriting Cervantic techniques in Tristram Shandy); differs because Laurence Sterne operates within 18th-century British sentimentalism, typographical games, and philosophical associations of ideas, whereas Cervantes established the foundational realism and ironical counterpoint of 17th-century Counter-Reformation Spain.
  • Borges: Similar as a Spanish-language literary master fascinated by labyrinthine metafiction, self-referential narratives, and fictional literary commentaries (famously exemplified in "Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote"); differs because Jorge Luis Borges expressed his genius through ultra-compressed, philosophical short fictions, essays, and poetic miniatures rather than sweeping, episodic, full-length socio-realistic novels.
  • Fielding: Similar as an early modern novelist who consciously modeled his comic epic prose style upon Cervantic principles (explicitly stating on the title page of Joseph Andrews that it was written "in imitation of the manner of Cervantes"); differs because Henry Fielding constructed tightly controlled, moralizing, omnisciently orchestrated plots reflecting 18th-century Enlightenment social satire rather than Spanish existential ambiguity.
