Virādha
"Virādha; the Daṇḍaka forest rākṣasa slain by Rāma"

Note on morphological analysis: The proper noun Virādha is a primary nominal derivative (kṛdanta) from the verbal root rādh- ("to succeed, be propitious, satisfy, accomplish"), combined with the oppositive/privative prefix vi- ("apart, opposing, contrary, adverse"). While rādh- signifies harmony, prosperity, and success (the base of names like Rādhā, "prosperity, adoration"), the prefix vi- completely reverses the verbal polarity into "hindrance, injury, opposition, alienation." Hence, virādha literally designates one who opposes prosperity, an implacable adversary, or an insurmountable obstacle. In Pāṇinian morpho-syntax, the noun is formed with the agentive suffix -a (ghañ / ac), yielding virādha as "the obstructer."
