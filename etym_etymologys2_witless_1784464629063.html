WITLESS is a native English compound of two elements, each with its own deep and well-attested history; the linear chains follow in isolation. Chain 1 — the base wit: PIE *weid- 'to see' (reconstructed; attested through cognates: Sanskrit véda 'I know', Greek oîda 'I know', Latin vidēre 'to see', Old Irish fiss 'knowledge') → PIE perfect stem *woid-/*wid- 'I have seen, hence I know' (the perfect-of-perception becomes a verb of knowing) → Proto-Germanic *witaną 'to know' (preterite-present verb; cf. Gothic wait 'I know', Old Norse vita) and noun *witją 'understanding, intelligence' → Old English witan 'to know' and wit 'mind, understanding, intellect, sense' (attested in Old English, e.g. Beowulf) → Middle English wit 'mind, sense, intelligence' (13th c.; from the 16th–17th c. also 'cleverness, verbal humour') → English wit → base element of the compound. Chain 2 — the privative suffix -less: PIE *lewH- 'to loosen, release, set free' (reconstructed; cf. Greek lū́ein 'to loosen', Latin solvere 'to set free') → Proto-Germanic *lausaz 'loose, free, devoid of' (attested through reflexes: Old Norse lauss, Gothic laus, German los) → Old English lēas 'loose; false; devoid of' → Old English suffix -lēas '-less, without' (productive, as in hūslēas 'shelterless') → Middle English -les → modern English -less → suffix element of the compound. Fusion of the two elements: Old English wītlēas 'witless, foolish, senseless' (attested) → Middle English witles 'lacking sense, foolish' (13th c.) → English witless 'lacking intelligence or sense; dazed, stupefied' (sense history: from 'foolish' to 'emptied of mind'; the intensifier use 'scared witless' is modern) → WITLESS (card form). Note on the nuance: the semantic career of the base wit — shifting from 'the mind's faculties' (Old English) to 'clever verbal humour' (17th c.) — did not pull witless with it; the compound stayed anchored to the older 'mental faculties' sense, which is why witless means 'dazed/foolish' rather than simply 'unfunny', though the humorous reading is live today.
