• Despite: Similar because both govern a noun of obstacle overridden (despite the rain); differs because despite is the plain everyday preposition, while notwithstanding is formal, heavier, and alone can stand after its noun.
  • In spite of: Similar because both mean defiance of circumstance with identical grammar; differs because in spite of is conversational and slightly personal (spite once meant grudge), while notwithstanding is impersonal and judicial.
  • Although: Similar because both concede an obstacle before asserting the main clause; differs because although is a pure conjunction joining clauses, while notwithstanding is primarily a preposition governing nouns, turning conjunction only with that.
  • Though: Similar because both grant the counter-case (though it rained); differs because though is short, flexible, and colloquial (even as a sentence-ending afterthought), while notwithstanding is long, stiff, and documentary.
  • Albeit: Similar because both are formal concessive connectives of literary register; differs because albeit (all-be-it) concedes with a shrug and takes adjectives or clauses, while notwithstanding confronts a named obstacle and overrides it.
  • Regardless of: Similar because both dismiss obstacles wholesale (regardless of cost); differs because regardless of is blunter and more modern-managerial, while notwithstanding is older, legal, and carries the echo of the courtroom.
  • Nevertheless: Similar because both assert persistence against opposition (the rain nevertheless); differs because nevertheless is purely adverbial and sentence-level, while notwithstanding attaches to a specific noun it overrides.
  • Nonetheless: Similar because both concede and proceed with formal gravity; differs because nonetheless is the slightly lighter modern twin of nevertheless, while notwithstanding alone can govern the obstacle itself as a preposition.
  • However: Similar because both pivot against what was just said; differs because however is the all-purpose contrastive adverb of every register, while notwithstanding reserves itself for weighty, often legal or polemical contradiction.
  • Even so: Similar because both mean the action proceeds despite everything; differs because even so is conversational and warm, the speaker's shrug, while notwithstanding is the judge's seal — cold, final, and Latinate.
