Note on phonosemantics and onomatopoeia: In Sanskrit historical phonology, dundubhi is universally classified as an expressive onomatopoeic formation (anukaraṇa-śabda). The reduplicated base dun-dubh- mirrors the acoustic physics of a large, heavy drumhead: the initial voiced dental stop d- and nasal -n- represent the solid, heavy impact of the mallet upon taut rawhide, while the voiced aspirate -bh- evokes the sustained, low-frequency reverberation that decays inside a cavernous wooden chamber. Compare similar acoustic formations in Indo-Aryan such as ḍamaru (the rattling hourglass drum), ḍhaḍḍhura, and Greek tumpanon.
