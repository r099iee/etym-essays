• poke: Similar because both mean to push with a pointed object; differs because poke is a neutral physical push, while prod adds a sharper, more goading intent and a strong figurative "urge" sense.
  • jab: Similar because both are sharp, quick thrusts with a point; differs because a jab is usually a punch or a quick finger-poke, while a prod is more deliberate and is often done with a stick or goad.
  • nudge: Similar because both mean a light touch to get attention or action; differs because a nudge is gentle, friendly, and often given with an elbow, while a prod is sharper, more insistent, and implies resistance to be overcome.
  • goad: Similar because both are pointed instruments for driving cattle and verbs meaning to urge sharply; differs because goad is the older, harsher word (a cattle-spike, and a fierce incitement), while prod is blunter and more everyday.
  • spur: Similar because both mean to urge someone on to action; differs because spur implies motivating, often positively (the spur of ambition), while prod implies pressing someone reluctant, with mild irritation.
  • urge: Similar because both mean strongly encourage action; differs because urge is a direct verbal or psychological push and is neutral-to-positive, while prod can be physical and implies the person needed external goading.
  • prompt: Similar because both mean to move someone to act; differs because prompt is gentle, timely, and often polite (a prompt reply, a reminder), while prod is more forceful and insistent.
  • push: Similar because both mean to apply pressure to make something happen; differs because push is broad (physical or abstract, often strong), while prod specifically evokes a pointed, repeated, goading contact.
  • egg on: Similar because both mean to incite someone to act, usually more boldly or rashly; differs because egg on is strictly figurative verbal incitement, while prod keeps the physical poke image and a neutral-to-nagging tone.
  • stab: Similar because both are thrusts with a sharp instrument; differs because a stab aims to wound seriously, while a prod is a light, goading, or testing touch.
  • dig: Similar because both can be a sharp poke with a finger or elbow; differs because a dig is often secretive or a snide remark, while a prod is an open, forward poke or prompt.
