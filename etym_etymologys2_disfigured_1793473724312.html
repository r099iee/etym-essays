Note: The Indo-European root *dʰeyǵʰ- ("to knead, form") is exceptionally prolific, also giving rise to Sanskrit dih- ("to smear, plaster"), Avestan pairi-daēza- ("walled enclosure", source of English paradise), Gothic deigan ("to knead"), and Old English dāg ("dough"). In Latin, fingere generated both fictio (fiction, something fashioned) and figūra. The prefixation of Latin dis- ("apart, away, reversing") created the compound verb indicating the undoing, ruining, or corrupting of an established form.
