PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*tkei-
‘to settle, dwell, be at home’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*haimaz
‘home, village, settlement’
↓
Old English (7th c.)
ham + tun
‘homestead’ + ‘enclosure’
‘home farm, settlement’
↓
Old English (10th c.)
Hamtunscir
‘district of the Hamtun’
↓
Domesday Latin (1086)
Hantescire
‘the shire of Hamtun’
↓
Middle English (13th–14th c.)
Hamptonshire
‘the county of Hampton’
↓
Modern English (current)
Hampshire
‘the county of Winchester’
‘district and Southampton’

Note: tun is widely held to be an early loan from Celtic *dunos, fort, rather than a native Germanic formation, and the Hampton versus Southampton spellings divided only in the later Middle Ages; the chain above follows the standard handbook account.
