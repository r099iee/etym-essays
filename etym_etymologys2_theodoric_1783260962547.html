THEODORIC — compound of two Germanic name-elements, each with its own chain. CHAIN 1 — first element 'people': PIE *tewtéh₂- 'the people as a community, the tribe' (reconstructed; cf. Old Irish túath 'people, tribe', Lithuanian tauta 'people, nation', probably Latin tōtus 'all') -> Proto-Germanic *þeudō 'people, nation' (reconstructed) -> Gothic þiuda 'people' (4th c., Wulfila's Bible) -> Gothic compound *Þiudareiks 'ruler of the people' (name-element, 5th c.) -> Latinized Theodericus / Theodoricus 'Theodoric' (6th c., Cassiodorus, Jordanes, Procopius) -> English Theodoric (as in Macaulay, 19th c.). CHAIN 2 — second element 'ruler': PIE *h₃rēǵ- 'to rule, set straight, direct' (reconstructed; attested as Sanskrit rāj- 'king', Latin rēx/rēg- 'king', Gaulish rīx 'king') -> Celtic *rīg- 'king' (attested Gaulish -rix in names like Vercingetorix, 1st c. BCE) -> Proto-Germanic *rīks 'ruler, king' (loanword from Celtic into Germanic, 1st millennium CE; cf. Gothic reiks 'ruler', used by Wulfila, 4th c.) -> Gothic *-reiks 'ruler' in the compound *Þiudareiks -> Latinized Theodericus -> English Theodoric. NOTE ON THE CHAIN: The first element is a native Germanic inheritance from PIE; the second element, *rīks, is a well-attested CELTIC LOANWORD into Germanic (the Germanic peoples had no inherited word for a territorial king). The reconstruction of the PIE root for 'all/people' and the exact dialectal route of the Celtic loan remain points of scholarly discussion, but the compound *Þiudareiks -> Theodericus -> Theodoric is securely attested.
