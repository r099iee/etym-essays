• Gleam: Similar because both mean a flash or beam of light; differs because a gleam is usually steady, soft and gentle, while a glint is quick, sharp and brief.
  • Glimmer: Similar because both denote a faint, unsteady light; differs because a glimmer is weak, wavering and usually continuous, whereas a glint is a single sharp flash.
  • Glitter: Similar because both involve reflected sparkles of light; differs because glitter is plural, scattered and brilliant, while glint is singular and momentary.
  • Sparkle: Similar because both name small flashes of light; differs because sparkle suggests many tiny, lively points of light and a cheerful quality, while glint is sharper and can be menacing.
  • Flash: Similar because both denote a sudden burst of light; differs because a flash is longer, brighter and can be glaring or artificial, while a glint is small, reflected and subtle.
  • Shimmer: Similar because both are reflected-light effects on surfaces; differs because shimmer is a continuous trembling sheen (as on heat-haze or silk), while glint is a single brief catch of light.
  • Glow: Similar because both concern light; differs because glow is steady, warm and emitted, whereas glint is brief, cool and reflected.
  • Glance: Similar because both derive from the same Germanic root and share the idea of a quick catch of light; differs because glance is now chiefly about looking, while glint is about light itself.
