• Fiddling: Similar because both are small purposeless hand-motions (fiddling with keys, fidgeting with coins); differs because fiddling suggests idle tinkering or meddling with an object, while fidgeting stresses the restless bodily state behind the motion.
  • Squirming: Similar because both betray discomfort through restless movement; differs because squirming moves the whole body (twisting in a chair with embarrassment), while fidgeting stays small, hands and feet only.
  • Twitching: Similar because both are quick involuntary-looking movements; differs because a twitch is sudden and spasmodic (an eye, a cheek), while fidgeting is continuous, rhythmic, and semi-voluntary.
  • Jiggling: Similar because jiggling feet and fidgeting feet are the same classroom sight; differs because jiggling names the bouncing motion itself (knees, keys, jelly), while fidgeting names the restless condition driving it.
  • Fussing: Similar because both describe agitated small activity under stress; differs because fussing is busy and vocal (arranging, complaining, hovering), while fidgeting is quiet and contained, hands moving while the mouth stays shut.
  • Fretting: Similar because fretting and fidgeting both spring from worry; differs because fretting is the inner gnawing of anxiety itself, while fidgeting is its outward hand-signal.
  • Tinkering: Similar because both involve turning small things over in the hands; differs because tinkering aims at fixing or improving (engines, settings), while fidgeting aims at nothing, motion as its own relief.
  • Shifting: Similar because restless people shift and fidget alike; differs because shifting moves weight from foot to foot or hip to hip, while fidgeting works the fingers and small extremities.
  • Puttering: Similar because both are small aimless activities filling nervous time; differs because puttering wanders about a room touching tasks, while fidgeting stays seated, motion without locomotion.
