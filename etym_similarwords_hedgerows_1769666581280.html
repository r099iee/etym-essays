• Hedges: Similar because both name rows of shrubs used as boundaries; differs because hedge is the generic, often trimmed single planting, while hedgerow is a wilder, mixed, semi-natural linear strip of trees and shrubs between fields.
  • Fence: Similar because both define and enclose property boundaries; differs because fence is a man-made structure of wood, wire, or stone, whereas a hedgerow is living vegetation.
  • Thicket: Similar because both are dense clusters of bushes and small trees; differs because a thicket is an unorganized, roughly circular wild clump, while a hedgerow is linear and managed along a boundary.
  • Boundary: Similar because hedgerows serve to mark boundaries between fields; differs because boundary is an abstract legal or geographical line, not a physical planting.
  • Windbreak: Similar because both are linear plantings that shelter land from wind; differs because a windbreak is planted chiefly for shelter, while a hedgerow is primarily a boundary that also gives shelter.
  • Shelterbelt: Similar because both are strips of trees and bushes for protection; differs because a shelterbelt is typically wider, of taller trees, and planted around farmsteads rather than used as a field division.
  • Quickset: Similar because both refer to living hedge plants (traditionally hawthorn) set into the ground; differs because quickset names the young plants or the act of planting, not the mature boundary line.
  • Copse: Similar because both are dense woody growth; differs because a copse is a small grove or area of trees, not a linear row.
  • Spinney: Similar because both are small patches of bushy or tree growth; differs because a spinney is a small thicket or wood, not a boundary line.
  • Bocage: Similar because in Normandy the dense hedgerow banks are called bocage; differs because bocage specifically denotes the whole hedged landscape of NW France, not a single row.
  • Shrubbery: Similar because both involve planted shrubs; differs because a shrubbery is a garden ornamental planting, not an agricultural boundary.
  • Border: Similar because both mark the outer edge of an area; differs because border is generic and may be any edge, while hedgerow specifically describes a living plant boundary.
  • Row: Similar because a hedgerow is literally a row of plants; differs because row is a purely linear arrangement of anything, lacking the plant-and-boundary meaning.
