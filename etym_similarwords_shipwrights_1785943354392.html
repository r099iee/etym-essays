• Shipbuilder: Similar because both denote a person who constructs ships; differs because shipbuilder is the broad, modern, industrial term covering steel and composite vessels, while shipwright specifically denotes the traditional wooden-ship craftsman.
  • Boatbuilder: Similar because both are builders of vessels from wood; differs because boatbuilder works on smaller craft (boats), while shipwright works on larger seagoing vessels (ships).
  • Naval architect: Similar because both are involved in the design and construction of ships; differs because the naval architect is a theoretical engineer working with mathematics, fluid dynamics and blueprints, while the shipwright is the practical artisan who shapes and assembles the hull.
  • Carpenter: Similar because both are woodworkers who cut, shape and join timber; differs because the carpenter works on buildings, furniture and general construction, while the shipwright is the specialized sea-oriented branch of the craft.
  • Caulker: Similar because both are essential to making a wooden ship sound; differs because the caulker only seals the seams between planks with oakum and pitch, while the shipwright builds the entire hull.
  • Rigger: Similar because both are shipyard specialists serving the construction of a vessel; differs because the rigger attends to masts, ropes and sails above deck, while the shipwright builds the hull itself.
  • Joiner: Similar because both are skilled woodworkers found in shipyards; differs because the joiner fits furniture, paneling and fittings, while the shipwright forms the structural skeleton and planking.
  • Wright (generic): Similar because shipwright contains the word wright, both meaning a skilled maker or builder; differs because wright alone is now archaic except in compounds, while shipwright names the specific craft of ship construction.
  • Shipwright's mate / apprentice: Similar because both belong to the same trade community; differs because the mate or apprentice is a junior helper, whereas shipwright denotes the qualified craftsman.
  • Loftsman: Similar because both work in the shipyard from the lines of the vessel; differs because the loftsman draws the full-size lines on the mould-loft floor, while the shipwright cuts and assembles the actual timber.
