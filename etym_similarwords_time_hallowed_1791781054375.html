• time-honoured: Similar because it is the direct twin compound, formed the same way and meaning “respected through long use”; differs because honoured implies social esteem, while hallowed implies sanctity, making time-hallowed more religious, more archaic and rarer.
  • age-old: Similar because both point to great antiquity; differs because age-old merely measures age and carries no suggestion of reverence or consecration.
  • long-standing: Similar because both describe something established over time; differs because long-standing is neutral and bureaucratic, used of arrangements and disputes, with no aura of sanctity.
  • venerable: Similar because both attribute dignity and respect; differs because venerable can be earned by character or office and does not require age, whereas time-hallowed requires long duration by definition.
  • hallowed: Similar because it is the second element and carries the same religious charge; differs because hallowed alone can be holy by divine or ecclesiastical act, while the compound specifies time as the agent.
  • traditional: Similar because both describe inherited practice; differs because traditional is descriptive and neutral, whereas time-hallowed approves what it describes.
  • established: Similar because both imply stability and acceptance; differs because established can be purely institutional or legal, with no suggestion of age or reverence.
  • immemorial: Similar because both invoke a past beyond living memory; differs because immemorial is a legal and historical formula (“time immemorial”) and says nothing about holiness.
  • sacrosanct: Similar because both make something immune to criticism; differs because sacrosanct is absolute and often ironic, while time-hallowed bases its claim on mere duration.
