• thronged: Similar because it describes a dense crowd filling a space; differs because thronged stresses crowding and pressure, while milled stresses restless, undirected movement with room to drift.
  • swarmed: Similar because both suggest many bodies moving at once; differs because swarmed implies purposeful, often agitated mass movement toward or around something, whereas milled implies aimlessness.
  • clustered: Similar because people gather in groups; differs because clustered suggests settled, stable grouping, while milled suggests groups forming and dissolving in continuous motion.
  • congregated: Similar because a number of people come together; differs because congregated is formal and implies a gathering with a purpose or occasion, not a loose, hovering drift.
  • eddied: Similar because both describe circular, swirling movement in a confined space; differs because eddied is literary and fluid, evoking water or smoke, while milled is the ordinary word for a crowd.
  • churned: Similar because both suggest agitated, repetitive motion; differs because churned is stronger and more violent, implying turbulence and confusion, while milled is milder and often patient.
  • drifted: Similar because the movement has no clear direction; differs because drifted describes slow, smooth, individual motion, while milled requires a plurality and a certain busy restlessness.
  • loitered: Similar because both describe lingering without leaving; differs because loitered implies idleness and often suspicion, with little movement, while milled is full of motion.
  • jostled: Similar because both occur in crowds; differs because jostled means being pushed and elbowed, a matter of physical contact, whereas milled need involve no collision at all.
  • circulated: Similar because people move among one another; differs because circulated suggests a social purpose (working a room) and often a single person, while milled describes the crowd as a body.
  • ground: Similar because it is the standard synonym of milled in the grinding sense; differs because it cannot be used of a crowd and lacks the technical precision of milled particle size.
  • stamped: Similar because it describes a coin struck by a die; differs because stamped says nothing about the machinery or the edge, whereas milled specifically contrasts with hand-hammered coinage.
