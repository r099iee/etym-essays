- 14th–16th Century: Regarded strictly as formal logic and disputation within the medieval academic Trivium.
- 17th–18th Century: Often viewed with Enlightenment skepticism as pedantic scholastic hair-splitting, until revived by Kant as the critique of metaphysical illusion.
- 19th Century: Dramatically elevated by Hegel and Marx into a grand metaphysical, historical, and economic law of contradictory evolutionary progress.
- 20th–21st Century: Broadened beyond Marxism into literary criticism, psychoanalysis, and systems biology to describe open, dynamic tensions, reciprocal interactions, and non-reconciled polarities.
