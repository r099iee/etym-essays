• Dejected: Similar because both denote low spirits and a state of being cast down after disappointment; differs because dejected descends from Latin jacere “to throw” and stresses the throwing-down of the spirits after a specific setback, while downcast is more immediately visual, English-native in feel, and pictures the eyes and face lowered toward the ground.
  • Despondent: Similar because both name a state of dejection; differs because despondent emphasizes the loss of hope and a bleak orientation toward the future, whereas downcast may describe a temporary low mood or a passing expression without necessarily implying hopelessness.
  • Crestfallen: Similar because both picture a person sagging under disappointment; differs because crestfallen evokes the theatrical deflation of a crest (an animal’s comb or a heraldic plume) after pride has been punctured, a more dramatic, self-dramatizing collapse than plain downcast.
  • Depressed: Similar because both name low mood; differs because depressed is the clinical and modern-psychological term with an enormous range (depressed economy, depressed lever, depressed area), whereas downcast is narrower, more literary, and keeps the concrete image of casting downward.
  • Disheartened: Similar because both denote a loss of spirit or courage; differs because disheartened focuses on lost heart or courage as a resource for action, while downcast describes the visible and felt state of low spirits itself.
  • Dispirited: Similar because both mean having one’s spirits taken away; differs because dispirited stresses the draining of energy and morale (being without spirit), while downcast adds the specific downward physical image.
  • Glum: Similar because both denote a gloomy, low-spirited look; differs because glum suggests a sullen, silent, often irritable manner, whereas downcast emphasizes sorrowful lowering rather than grumpiness.
  • Low, down, blue: Similar as informal labels for sad mood; differs because these are plain colloquial adjectives without the etymological and visual force of downcast, which names the very act of being thrown downward.
  • Melancholy: Similar because both can describe sadness; differs because melancholy is a temperament or a brooding aesthetic mood, often chronic and poetic, while downcast is an acute, present-tense state of dejection.
  • Dismayed: Similar because both follow a blow to hope; differs because dismayed stresses alarm and discouragement at a sudden shock, whereas downcast pictures the settled lowering of spirits and gaze after the blow.
