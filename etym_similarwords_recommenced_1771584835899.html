• Resumed: Similar because it means began again after a pause; differs because resume stresses continuity — picking up an action mid-course — whereas recommence emphasises a fresh beginning from the initial point.
  • Restarted: Similar because it means began again; differs because restart is modern and mechanical — at home with engines, computers and programmes — and would deflate the ceremonious context where recommence belongs.
  • Renewed: Similar because it means took up again; differs because renewed adds a gust of fresh energy or emotion (renewed interest, renewed attack), overstating the steady, patient quality that recommence conveys.
  • Reopened: Similar because it means began again after a stop; differs because reopen is reserved for cases, inquiries, negotiations and wounds — things reopened rather than begun — while recommence applies to actions undertaken afresh.
  • Began again: Similar because it means started afresh; differs because it is the plain spoken alternative that surrenders the formality the context wants, whereas recommence keeps the Latinate dignity of an undertaking.
  • Took up again: Similar because it means resumed something; differs because it is a phrasal verb of casual, spoken register, whereas recommence is formal and ceremonious, the language of an undertaking.
