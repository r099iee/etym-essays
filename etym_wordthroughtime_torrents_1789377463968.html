- Late 16th Century (Renaissance English) - Borrowed via Middle French and Latin into English travel accounts and classical translations to describe fierce Mediterranean mountain rivers and seasonal flash floods.
- 17th Century (Miltonic & Stuart Era) - John Milton and contemporary poets expanded the term into cosmic, biblical, and hellish deluges, describing torrential rains, cascading waters of the Genesis flood, and burning torrents of liquid brimstone in *Paradise Lost*.
- 18th Century (Augustan & Enlightenment Era) - Established the permanent rhetorical sense: an unstoppable, overwhelming cascade of speech, vitriol, applause, or passion ("a torrent of eloquence").
- 19th Century (Romantic & Victorian Eras) - Ubiquitous in romantic nature writing, gothic tales, and tragic melodrama (such as Oscar Wilde's *The Devoted Friend*) as a dramatic emblem of nature's lethal fury overwhelming vulnerable human travelers.
- Late 20th to 21st Century (Digital Era) - Revolutionized by computer science with the creation of the BitTorrent peer-to-peer protocol (2001), permanently establishing "torrent" in global computational vocabulary for distributed file-sharing networks.
