• Rushed: Similar because both mean moving with haste; differs because rushing implies hurry, pressure, and possible disorder, while speeding is smooth, swift, forward motion without the connotation of panic.
  • Hurried: Similar because both mean rapid movement; differs because hurrying is purposeful and slightly anxious (making haste for a deadline), whereas sped has a cleaner, more physical swiftness.
  • Dashed: Similar because both mean moving very quickly; differs because dashing is sudden, headlong, and often short-range (dash to the door), while speeding suggests sustained travel along a route.
  • Raced: Similar because both mean moving at great speed; differs because racing implies competition, contest, or maximum motor effort, whereas sped is simply fast travel without contest.
  • Flew: Similar because both describe extremely fast motion; differs because flew literally describes flight through the air or very rapid passage, while sped is more general and less winged in imagery.
  • Zoomed: Similar because both mean moving fast; differs because zooming is colloquial, buzzy, and implies a swooping or whirring motion, lacking sped's neutral literary dignity.
  • Hurtled: Similar because both mean rapid, forceful motion; differs because hurtling adds reckless, out-of-control momentum (hurtling over a cliff), while speeding can be smooth and controlled.
  • Bolt / bolted: Similar because both mean sudden swift departure; differs because bolting is abrupt and startled (a horse bolts), while sped is a deliberate, steady rush.
