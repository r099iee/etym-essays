• editions: Similar because both refer to specific published or codified presentations of a text; differs because an edition typically refers to a commercial, printed publication produced at a specific time by a publisher or editor (e.g., a modern paperback edition), whereas a recension represents a major historical family or ancestral manuscript lineage of an ancient work identified through critical stemmatic analysis.
  • versions: Similar because both denote divergent forms or renderings of a common literary source; differs because version is a broad, non-technical term that frequently implies translations into other languages (e.g., the Syriac version of the Gospels) or loose adaptations, whereas a recension is a precise philological classification within the original language tradition.
  • redactions: Similar because both describe forms of a text that have undergone conscious editorial alteration, restructuring, or revision; differs because a redaction emphasizes the deliberate ideological, theological, or stylistic rewriting performed by a specific redactor or school, whereas recensions encompass organic regional traditions that accumulated variant readings and interpolations over generations of scribal copying.
  • readings (variae lectiones): Similar because variant readings are the individual textual discrepancies that reveal the existence of recensions; differs because a reading is a micro-level variation of an individual word, phrase, or line, whereas a recension is the macro-level textual tradition or entire branch of the manuscript tree that shares those characteristic readings.
  • archetypes: Similar because both are foundational concepts in Lachmannian stemmatics; differs because an archetype is the single, common ancestral manuscript or reconstructible nodal text from which all surviving witnesses descend, whereas recensions are the divergent branchings or filial families that descended from that archetype.
  • witnesses: Similar because both are central components of textual criticism; differs because a witness is an individual physical manuscript, papyrus fragment, lectionary, or ancient quotation that testifies to a text, whereas a recension is the broader genealogical grouping or family into which multiple related witnesses are categorized.
  • stemmata (stemmata codicum): Similar because stemmata are the genealogical family trees used by textual critics to map recensions; differs because a stemma is the visual or conceptual diagram charting ancestral relations, while recensions are the actual textual families situated along the branches of that diagram.
  • textual variants: Similar because both terms deal with divergences across textual transmission; differs because textual variants are the specific, localized points of divergence themselves, whereas recensions are the comprehensive textual recasting or systemic traditions resulting from those variations.
