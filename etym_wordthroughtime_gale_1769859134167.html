- Early 16th-century sense: a gentle or moderate breeze, a pleasant wind — in nautical speech, a steady wind without violence. - By the 17th–18th centuries strengthened to mean a strong, sustained wind, especially in sailing contexts, though not yet necessarily destructive. - In the 19th–20th centuries standardized by the Beaufort scale as winds of force 7–10 (32–63 knots) — a properly violent, hazardous storm wind. - Contemporary sense: a powerful sustained wind, and figuratively a loud outburst ("gales of laughter"), carrying an energetic, sometimes violent register.
