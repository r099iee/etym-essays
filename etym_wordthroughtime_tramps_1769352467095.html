- 1600s-1800s - "Tramp" first names the heavy, rhythmic walk (to tramp the roads); as a noun, a long walking journey — neutral and purely physical.
- 1800s (noun for a person) - Comes to denote a wanderer who lives by tramping: a vagrant or homeless drifter; sympathy and contempt mingle as public perception ties the figure to poverty and shiftlessness.
- 1900s - Broadens and darkens: "tramp steamer" (a cargo ship with no fixed route) and a derogatory slang sense for a promiscuous woman; the sympathetic migrant-worker sense survives mainly in literary and historical writing.
- Contemporary - Now dated or insulting when applied to people; in Steinbeck's prose it reads as a sympathetic, heavy-footed marker of Depression-era itinerant labor.
