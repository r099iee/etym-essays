• Lacking: Similar because both express absence of something needed or expected; differs because "lacking" is an adjective/participle that implies deficiency against a norm, whereas "without" is a neutral preposition carrying no judgement of insufficiency.
  • Minus: Similar because both can colloquially mean "not having" ("coffee minus the sugar"); differs because "minus" is primarily a mathematics word for subtraction and is informal in the privative role.
  • Sans: Similar because both mean "without" and are interchangeable in jocular, fashionable, or mock-formal usage; differs because "sans" is a French-derived literary borrowing, not part of ordinary neutral speech.
  • Devoid of: Similar because both denote complete absence; differs because "devoid of" is a formal, emphatic adjective construction that stresses total emptiness, whereas "without" is the unmarked everyday word.
  • Bereft of: Similar because both mean "deprived of"; differs because "bereft" is literary and implies the painful losing of something once possessed, carrying a grief that "without" does not.
  • Excluding / barring / excepting: Similar because all can mean "not including"; differs because these are participial prepositions used for membership, enumeration, and exceptions rather than for general lack.
  • Free from / free of: Similar because both mean "not affected by" something; differs because these stress deliverance or deliverance-like relief (free from fear), whereas "without" is neutral absence.
  • In the absence of: Similar because both mean "when X is not present"; differs because this is a formal, circumstantial phrase favoured in legal and academic prose.
  • Wanting / short of: Similar because both indicate deficiency; differs because these imply falling short of a required amount and therefore carry a judgement of inadequacy.
  • Outside: Similar because both are spatial and once overlapped; differs because "outside" has taken over the purely spatial role that "without" used to hold and is never privative.
