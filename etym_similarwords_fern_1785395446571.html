• Bracken: Similar because it is the most common coarse fern, with the same feathery fronds and spore reproduction; differs because bracken is not a synonym but one species (Pteridium aquilinum), the aggressive invasive fern of heaths and open woodland, taller and coarser than the typical woodland fern.
  • Moss: Similar because both are low, flowerless, non-woody plants of damp ground; differs because moss is tiny, cushion-like, and non-vascular, while a fern can stand waist-high or taller, with true roots, stems, and a water-conducting system.
  • Undergrowth: Similar because both name the ground layer of a forest; differs because undergrowth is an ecological term for the whole ground layer of a wood, of which ferns are only one part.
  • Shrub: Similar because both are low plants of the forest floor; differs because a shrub has a woody stem or trunk, while a fern has none at all — its fronds rise directly from a creeping rhizome.
  • Herb: Similar because both name non-trees of the ground layer; differs because a herb is a non-woody flowering plant reproducing by seed, while a fern is flowerless and reproduces by spores.
  • Frond: Similar because both belong to the same plant; differs because frond names the leaf-like organ of a fern or palm, while fern names the whole plant that bears the fronds.
  • Bush: Similar because both are low, spreading vegetation; differs because a bush is woody and branched, while a fern has no woody stem and bears no branches in the ordinary sense.
