- [15th century properness] - [Enters meaning the quality of being proper — fitness, rightness, correctness; connotation of things being as they ought]
- [17th century two strands] - [Alongside the social sense, the word also means individual property and ownership; the older sense later fades while correct conduct gains the field]
- [18th-19th century the code of the respectable] - [Becomes the standard word for conformity to polite social convention — the etiquette, decorum, and moral expectations of the upper classes; connotation of social pressure, the code that quietly trumps personal happiness]
- [20th-21st century dated formality] - [Retains the sense of correct conventional behaviour but sounds dated and formal in ordinary speech; used with irony or in legal, formal, and period contexts, its pressure felt most strongly in 'the proprieties' of a society]
