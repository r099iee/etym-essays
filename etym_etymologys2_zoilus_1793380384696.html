Note: The Greek masculine personal name Zōílos (Ζώϊλος) is formed directly from the root of zōós (ζωός, "alive, living") or zōē (ζωή, "life")—both descended from PIE *gʷeyh₃- ("to live")—combined with the affectionate hypocoristic diminutive suffix -ilos (-ιλος), yielding an original etymological sense of "lively little fellow" or "full of vitality." It was a common, well-attested Macedonian and northern Greek personal name. Through the notorious career of Zoilus of Amphipolis, however, this joyful, life-affirming onomastic origin was completely eclipsed and ironically inverted in European literary memory, becoming synonymous with sterile pedantry, joyless fault-finding, and toxic critical envy.
