- [Old and Middle English: the roof's overhanging rim] - 'Eaves' names the projecting lower edge of a roof that sheds rainwater clear of the walls; connotation is plain, practical, and architectural - the building's sheltering margin.
- [16th-17th century: the eaves as a listening place] - The word spawns 'eavesdropping' - standing under the eaves to overhear what is said indoors - and so the noun acquires a clandestine resonance: the eaves as the marginal, half-hidden zone where outside meets inside.
- [Contemporary sense] - The standard plural-only architectural term for the overhanging roof edge; connotation is neutral and technical, though the old sense of a threshold - a place of partial, screening shelter between the domestic world and the weather - still colours literary use.
