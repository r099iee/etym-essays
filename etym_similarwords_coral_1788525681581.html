• carnelian: Similar because both are semi-precious ornamental stones prized for their rich, warm reddish-orange coloration; differs because carnelian is an inorganic microcrystalline chalcedony quartz formed through mineral sedimentation, whereas coral is an organic biogenic skeleton produced by marine polyps.
  • ruby: Similar because both represent the historical pinnacle of rich, royal red gemstones used in fine jewelry and literary blazons; differs because ruby is a precious, transparent, crystalline corundum mineral of extreme crystalline hardness (Mohs 9) and deep crimson fire, whereas coral is an opaque, relatively soft (Mohs 3.5) organic marine material.
  • vermilion: Similar because both describe an intense, warm, slightly orange-tinted red color; differs because vermilion is an artificial or natural inorganic pigment made from mercuric sulfide (cinnabar), characterized by opaque painterly density, whereas coral is a biological material and a softer, more pinkish-salmon hue.
  • salmon: Similar because both name warm, pinkish-orange hues derived from maritime organisms; differs because salmon leans distinctly more toward soft pink and orange-flesh tones, whereas gemological coral encompasses deep, blood-red crimsons and vermilions.
  • gorgonian: Similar because both belong to the marine order of anthozoan corals that build branching, fan-shaped colonies in ocean caverns (sea fans); differs because gorgonian is a precise biological taxon characterized by a flexible, horny internal protein skeleton (gorgonin), whereas true stony coral possesses a rigid, mineralized calcium carbonate exoskeleton.
  • polyp: Similar because the coral organism and its colonies are formed by these cnidarian creatures; differs because polyp refers strictly to the soft, individual soft-bodied tentacled invertebrate animal itself, whereas coral typically designates the hard, communal stony skeleton left behind.
  • reef: Similar because biogenic marine corals are the primary building blocks that compose modern tropical ocean reefs; differs because a reef is a massive geographical, navigational, and topographic submerged ridge or shoal composed of aggregated rock, sand, and skeletal debris, of which coral is the biological origin.
