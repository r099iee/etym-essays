SINGLE ROOT WORD (tower) — a Latin/Mediterranean architectural loan with no securely reconstructible PIE ancestry. Pre-Indo-European / Mediterranean substrate building term (hypothesized; no PIE root securely reconstructible) → Latin turris 'tower, high structure, siege tower' (attested from the 2nd c. BCE) → Vulgar Latin *torre 'tower' (reconstructed) → Old French tor / tour 'tower' (attested 9th–10th c.) → Anglo-Norman tour → Middle English tour / tourre 'tower' (attested 12th–13th c.) → English tower 'tall defensive or ornamental structure' (13th c. onward) → English towering (present participle of the verb to tower 'to rise to a great height', and participial adjective 'rising high; overwhelming in degree'; in use from the 16th c.) → TOWERING (as in 'a towering rage'). Debate note: Older popular etymologies forced Latin turris under the PIE root *steh₂- 'to stand' (a 'that which stands upright' folk etymology). Standard handbooks (OED, de Vaan tradition) treat turris as of uncertain, likely non-Indo-European Mediterranean origin. Greek týrsis / týrrhis 'tower, fortified height' and Latin turris are best understood as cognate or parallel loans from a shared east-Mediterranean substrate, not as descendants of a transparent PIE 'stand' root. English towering is therefore the participial growth of a Romance/Latin architectural loan, not a native Germanic 'height' verb.
