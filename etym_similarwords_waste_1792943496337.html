• Wasteland: Similar because both name barren untilled ground; differs because wasteland is the full compound for devastated or empty country, while waste alone is older, shorter, and broader.
  • Moor: Similar because the source pairs them as open wild country; differs because a moor is specifically peaty upland heath, while waste is any untilled emptiness, wet or dry, high or low.
  • Heath: Similar because both are uncultivated scrub country; differs because heath names sandy shrub-covered land precisely, while waste judges its emptiness of use.
  • Desert: Similar because both are barren tracts travellers hurry through; differs because desert is arid by climate, while waste may be bog, rock, or merely untilled.
  • Squander: Similar because both condemn reckless spending; differs because squander stresses lavish scattering of wealth, while waste covers any fruitless use of anything.
  • Refuse: Similar because both name what is thrown away; differs because refuse is household rubbish politely termed, while waste spans industrial, nuclear, and moral scales.
  • Desolation: Similar because both evoke emptied ruined country; differs because desolation stresses grief and ruin after calamity, while waste may be merely natural and untilled.
  • Devastation: Similar because laying waste is devastating; differs because devastation names violent destruction done, while waste can simply lie empty, harmed by no one.
