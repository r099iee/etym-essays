Note on morphological doublet and Indo-European cognates: The adjective valorous forms an etymological doublet with valiant. Both trace directly back to the Latin verb valēre, but arrived in English through distinct morphological pathways: valiant was borrowed earlier (c. 1300) from the Old French present participle vaillant (from Latin valentem, literally "being strong"), while valorous was derived from the Late Latin abstract noun valor combined with the adjective-forming suffix -ous (Latin -ōsus, "full of"). Through the Proto-Indo-European verbal root *h₂welh₁- ("to be strong, prevail"), valorous is an ancient cousin to native Germanic words such as English wield (from Old English wealdan, "to rule, have power over"), German walten ("to rule"), and the royal name Walter (literally "ruler of the army").
