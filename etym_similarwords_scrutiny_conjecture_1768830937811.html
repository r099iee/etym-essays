• Examination: Similar in close attention to a thing; differs because examination may be neutral or academic, while scrutiny is suspicious, item-by-item sifting.
  • Inspection: Similar in careful looking-over; differs because inspection is often routine or procedural, while scrutiny is intense and distrustful.
  • Surveillance: Similar in careful watching; differs because surveillance adds watchful suspicion over time, whereas scrutiny is concentrated analytical inspection.
  • Audit: Similar in detailed verification; differs because audit checks financial or official records against standards, while scrutiny applies to any critical observation.
  • Analysis: Similar in breaking a thing into parts; differs because analysis is systematic and scientific, while scrutiny is a searching gaze that may be purely visual.
  • Perusal: Similar in close reading; differs because perusal applies to texts, whereas scrutiny extends to persons and objects.
  • Investigation: Similar in systematic probing; differs because investigation is an active, extended inquiry, while scrutiny is the intense watching that feeds it.
  • Review: Similar in critical re-examination; differs because review is a formal re-assessment, while scrutiny implies suspicion and detail beyond review.
  • Observation: Similar in watching; differs because observation is neutral recording, while scrutiny is a searching, distrustful look.
  • Prying: Similar in intrusive looking; differs because prying is impertinent curiosity, whereas scrutiny is legitimate if severe examination.
  • Speculation: Similar in forming conclusions on incomplete information; differs because speculation is more freely imaginative and often financial, while conjecture is a reasoned guess respecting the evidence.
  • Guess: Similar in an answer without proof; differs because guess may be pure chance, while conjecture implies inference from hints and signs.
  • Hypothesis: Similar in a provisional explanation; differs because a hypothesis is a formal, testable proposal in science, while conjecture is an informal reasoned guess.
  • Supposition: Similar in something assumed; differs because supposition is an assumption taken as a starting point, while conjecture is a conclusion drawn from incomplete evidence.
  • Surmise: Similar in a guess based on slender evidence; differs because surmise is often a wary, fleeting suspicion, while conjecture is more deliberately reasoned.
  • Inference: Similar in reasoning from evidence; differs because inference claims a logical step, while conjecture admits the evidence is incomplete.
  • Theory: Similar in a broad explanatory idea; differs because theory in science is a well-supported framework, while conjecture is unproven.
  • Assumption: Similar in a belief taken for granted; differs because assumption is accepted without argument, while conjecture is proposed as a guess.
  • Reckoning: Similar in a calculated estimate; differs because reckoning emphasises arithmetic estimation, while conjecture emphasises the reading of signs.
