• food: Similar because both name what is eaten for nourishment; differs because food is the general, neutral hypernym of unlimited register, while victuals specifically means food regarded as provisions or stores, in a homely, old-fashioned plural form.
  • provisions: Similar because both mean supplies of food laid in for a journey, army, or household; differs because provisions is broader (can include non-food supplies) and more formal-modern, while victuals is narrower (food proper) and more old-fashioned and homely.
  • supplies: Similar because both name stores set aside for future need; differs because supplies covers food, fuel, tools, and ammunition alike, whereas victuals is restricted to food and drink.
  • rations: Similar because both name food allocated for use, especially in military contexts; differs because rations are fixed, measured, portioned allowances issued to individuals, while victuals are general stores not necessarily portioned.
  • fare: Similar because both name food considered in relation to eating; differs because fare means the food offered or served on a table ("simple fare"), while victuals means the food laid in or carried as stores.
  • sustenance: Similar because both mean that which maintains life and strength; differs because sustenance is abstract, biological, and formal, while victuals is concrete, practical, and plural — actual stored food.
  • nourishment: Similar because both name food as sustainer; differs because nourishment stresses the life-giving and nutritional value abstractly, while victuals stresses the physical stock of food for consumption.
  • grub / chow / eats: Similar because all are informal words for food; differs because these are modern slang, casual and jokey, while victuals is old-fashioned and literary yet shares the hearty, homely spirit.
  • viands: Similar because both are archaic-plural words for food; differs because viands means choice or delicate dishes — elegant and refined — while victuals are plain, sturdy provisions.
  • provender: Similar because both name stored food for consumption; differs because provender is chiefly dry fodder for livestock (extended humorously to people), while victuals is ordinary human food.
  • comestibles: Similar because both are formal plural words for edible things; differs because comestibles is technical-grocery jargon ("edibles"), cold and commercial, while victuals is warm, homely, and old-fashioned.
  • vittles: Similar because this is simply the phonetic spelling of the same word; differs only in spelling and register — vittles is the folksy, dialectal, informal form, while victuals is the standard spelling with the silent c.
