• Topsoil: Similar because both name the dark fertile surface layer growers prize; differs because topsoil is positional (the upper horizon, whatever its texture), while loam is compositional (the ideal mixture, wherever it lies).
  • Humus: Similar because both mean dark rich organic matter in soil; differs because humus is specifically the rotted organic fraction, while loam is the whole mineral mixture that humus enriches.
  • Clay: Similar because both are heavy workable earths that hold water and nutrients; differs because clay is the pure fine particle (sticky when wet, brick-hard when dry), while loam is clay tamed by sand and silt into friability.
  • Silt: Similar because both are smooth fertile soil fractions that hold moisture; differs because silt is the single mid-sized particle (floury, slumping), while loam is the balanced blend of all three particles.
  • Marl: Similar because both are prized historical soil mixtures of clay with lime or sand; differs because marl is specifically calcareous clay dug to sweeten acid fields, while loam is the general ideal texture.
  • Mould (soil mould): Similar because both name dark rich vegetable earth for growing; differs because mould (leaf-mould) is rotted vegetation itself, while loam is mineral soil made fertile partly by such mould.
  • Earth: Similar because both mean the ground under cultivation in its kindliest sense; differs because earth is the universal word (planet, ground, soil), while loam names only its best, most workable kind.
  • Compost: Similar because both are dark crumbly fertility-builders gardeners dig in; differs because compost is manufactured rotted matter added to soil, while loam is the soil itself at its natural best.
  • Tilth: Similar because both belong to the husbandman's praise of workable ground; differs because tilth names the condition of soil brought by tillage (fine, level, ready), while loam names the soil's inherent composition.
  • Dirt: Similar because both are plain words for soil; differs because dirt is casual and often contemptuous (filth, squalor), while loam is respectful — dirt with a reputation.
