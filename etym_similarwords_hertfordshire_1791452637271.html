• Hertford: similar because it is the county town from which the county takes its name; differs because Hertford is a single town and administrative centre, not the whole county.
  • Herts: similar because it is the standard abbreviation of Hertfordshire; differs because it is informal and shortened, used mainly in addresses, sports, and local speech.
  • Herefordshire: similar because it is another English county with a similar-looking name; differs because it lies on the Welsh border and has a completely different history and landscape.
  • Bedfordshire: similar because it is a neighbouring Home County to the north-west; differs because it has its own county town, boundaries, and identity.
  • Buckinghamshire: similar because it borders Hertfordshire to the west and shares the commuter-belt character; differs because its county town is Aylesbury and its Chiltern landscape is more prominent.
  • Cambridgeshire: similar because it borders Hertfordshire to the north-east; differs because it is associated with the university city of Cambridge and the Fens.
  • Essex: similar because it borders Hertfordshire to the east and is another Home County; differs because it has a long Thames estuary and North Sea coastline.
  • Middlesex: similar because it is a historic county immediately south of Hertfordshire and now largely absorbed into Greater London; differs because it no longer exists as an administrative county.
  • Greater London: similar because it borders Hertfordshire and shapes its commuter economy; differs because it is a metropolitan region, not a shire county.
  • St Albans: similar because it is Hertfordshire’s historic cathedral city and a symbol of the county; differs because it is a city, not the county.
  • Watford: similar because it is Hertfordshire’s largest town; differs because it is a single urban settlement, not the whole county.
  • The Home Counties: similar because Hertfordshire is one of them; differs because the phrase covers a group of counties around London, not one named place.
  • The shires: similar because it evokes traditional rural England and county society; differs because it is a loose cultural label, not a legal or geographic unit.
