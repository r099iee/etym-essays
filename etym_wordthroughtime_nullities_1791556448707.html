- 16th Century (1560s–1590s): Canon law, marital annulment, and legal jurisprudence - Signified strict legal invalidity, describing contracts, royal decrees, or marriages that had no legal existence from their inception.
- 17th to 18th Century: Metaphysical void and political invalidation - Expanded into philosophical theology (the nothingness of creation before God) and constitutional clashes regarding unconstitutional decrees.
- 19th Century: Diplomatic and social contempt for nonentities - Applied with biting irony to politically impotent statesmen, powerless ministers, and socially negligible individuals (e.g. Henry Adams, British parliamentary debates).
- 20th to 21st Century: Mathematical formalization and contemporary legal theory - Institutionalized in linear algebra (Rank-Nullity Theorem) while retaining its classic legal bite for void administrative actions and literary depictions of hollow bureaucracy.
