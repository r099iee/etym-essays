DERIVATION CHAIN — STEW (with explicit statement of the unknown earliest stage): EARLIEST STAGE UNKNOWN / DEBATED: No PIE etymon can be confidently reconstructed for this word. The attested chain begins in the medieval Latin tradition of "heated rooms" and "steaming"; the deeper origin of that tradition is disputed (see note below). → (probable) Vulgar Latin *extūfare "to steam, heat with vapor" — NOT DIRECTLY ATTESTED; a reconstructed verb built on a root connected with Greek túphos "smoke, vapor" — DEBATED → Medieval Latin stupha "a heated sweating-room, a vapor bath" (also spelled stupha, stufa; attested in medieval Latin records) [c. 10th–12th c.] → Old French estuve "stove, heated room, sweat room, bath house" (Modern French étuve) [attested 11th–12th c.] → Middle English stue / stewe "a heated room, sweat-bath, bath-house" and, by extension, "a vessel for slow simmering; the dish cooked in it" [attested 14th c.] → Early Modern English stew (noun) "a dish of meat or vegetables slowly cooked in liquid" and (verb) "to cook slowly in a covered pot" [15th–16th c.] → Modern English stew (noun) "slow-cooked dish" / (verb) "to simmer slowly" / (figurative) "to fret, worry, be agitated" → STEW NOTE ON THE DEBATE / NUANCE: The ultimate origin is genuinely uncertain. The mainstream account derives the word from Medieval Latin stupha "vapor bath" through Old French estuve, but the source of stupha is disputed. Three main hypotheses are proposed: (1) from Vulgar Latin *extūfare "to steam," connected with Greek túphos "smoke, vapor"; (2) from a Germanic root *stub- (cf. Old High German stuba "heated room"), the same root that gives modern English stove; or (3) from Greek tūpheîon "smoke-room." The closeness of English stove and stew is not accidental — both look back to the same "heated room" vocabulary — but the precise line between them is debated. The semantic bridge runs: "heated room / vapor bath" → "place where food is slowly cooked over heat" → "the slow-cooked dish itself" → "to cook slowly" → "to fret, as if simmering in internal heat."
