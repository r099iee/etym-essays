PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*pau- / *peu-
"to strike, cut, trim, cleanse"
Note: Base of Latin putāre ('to prune, trim, cleanse; to settle accounts, reckon, think').
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
dēputāre
"to prune away, cut off; to reckon, assign, allot, esteem"
Note: Formed from dē- ('away, from') + putāre ('to trim, reckon').
↓
Late / Imperial Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
dēputātiō (genitive dēputātiōnis)
"assignment, allocation of duty, appointment of a proxy or representative"
↓
Old French (c. 13th–14th c.)
députation
"act of appointing a representative; group of appointed delegates"
↓
Late Middle English (c. 1380–1400)
deputacioun / deputacion
"appointment of a substitute or agent; authority conferred on a proxy"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th–17th c.)
deputation
"formal delegation of persons sent to deliver a petition or negotiate"
