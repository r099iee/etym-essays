Root 1 — battering:
Latin (1st c. BCE)
battuere
"to beat, strike"
↓
Vulgar Latin (late antiquity)
*battere
"to beat"
↓
Old French (9th-11th c. CE)
batre
"to beat, strike"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
bateren
"to strike repeatedly, batter"
↓
English (present participle)
battering
"striking again and again"

Root 2 — ram:
Proto-Germanic (early 1st millennium CE)
*rammaz
"a male sheep"
↓
Old English (before 10th c. CE)
ramm
"a male sheep; a ram (siege engine)"
↓
Middle English (12th-14th c.)
ram
"a male sheep; a battering-ram"
↓
English (plural)
rams

Compound: battering + rams
↓
battering-rams

Debate / nuance: the ultimate origin of Proto-Germanic *rammaz is uncertain — it may connect to a verb of butting or to a root meaning 'strong' — and Latin battuere was already regarded by Roman writers as a foreign (probably Celtic or Gaulish) word, likely imitative of the sound of blows; neither origin can be pinned down beyond that.
