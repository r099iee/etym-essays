• Panted: Similar because both involve rapid, labored breathing from exertion; differs because panted describes a continuous, rhythmic series of shallow breaths (like a dog running), whereas gasped is a sharp, sudden, single catching of breath or desperate gulp for air.
  • Wheezed: Similar because both denote difficult breathing; differs because wheezed specifically features a high-pitched, whistling or rasping sound produced by constricted bronchial airways (as in asthma).
  • Puffed: Similar because both convey breathlessness from physical effort; differs because puffed emphasizes the active, forceful blowing out of air through rounded lips (e.g., puffing and blowing), whereas gasped emphasizes the desperate drawing in of air.
  • Choked: Similar because both involve obstructed breathing; differs because choked implies that the airway is physically blocked by an object, liquid, or overwhelming emotional grief.
  • Gulped: Similar because both involve swallowing or taking in air sharply; differs because gulped emphasizes the swallowing motion in the throat, often associated with fear, nervous hesitation, or drinking.
  • Heaved: Similar because both describe the violent movement of the chest; differs because heaved emphasizes the heavy, upward physical lifting and falling of the ribcage or stomach.
  • Sighed: Similar as an involuntary respiratory expression of emotion; differs because sighed is a long, slow, audible exhalation expressing relief, sadness, longing, or weariness, rather than a sharp inhalation.
  • Exclaimed: Similar when used as a speech tag; differs because exclaimed highlights loud, articulate, emotional shouting without any physiological breathlessness.
  • Spluttered: Similar because both occur during sudden respiratory shock; differs because spluttered involves spitting out short, explosive sounds and saliva in confusion, rage, or coughing.
  • Stifled: Similar in contexts of suppressed breath; differs because stifled means actively repressing or smothering a laugh, sob, or yawn.
