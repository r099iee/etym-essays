- early 18th century borrowing - enters English from French (verbiage, from verbe, "word") as a critical term for an excess of words; the word is born dismissive, a French dressing for padded prose.
- 18th-19th century literary censure - applied in criticism to writers who bury meaning under surplus language; the tone is learned disdain, the accusation that too many words carry too little meaning.
- 19th-20th century official prose - becomes the standard term of complaint about bureaucratic, legal, academic, and corporate writing, the enemy of the plain-style movement.
- Contemporary use - still pejorative and everyday; "cut the verbiage" asks for brevity, and the word names not how something is said but how much is said.
