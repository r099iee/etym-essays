• Hormesis: Similar because both name a beneficial response to mild stress, the low-dose stimulation that produces an adaptive gain; differs because hormesis describes an organism's response to its OWN stressors, while xenohormesis specifically concerns stress signals produced by ANOTHER species and perceived across the species boundary.
  • Mitohormesis: Similar because both are specialist terms for adaptive, low-dose stress responses linked to longevity pathways; differs because mitohormesis isolates mitochondrial stress signaling within the same organism, whereas xenohormesis is an inter-species, dietary signal-detection phenomenon.
  • Caloric restriction: Similar because it is the flagship longevity intervention whose benefits xenohormesis is invoked to explain (via sirtuin and AMPK activation); differs because caloric restriction is an actual dietary regime imposed on the organism itself, while xenohormesis is a hypothesis about perceiving other species' stress compounds.
  • Resveratrol: Similar because this plant polyphenol is the paradigmatic xenohormetic molecule cited in the theory; differs because resveratrol is a specific chemical compound found in grape skins and red wine, not the general biological hypothesis itself.
  • Xenobiotic: Similar because both begin with the Greek combining form xeno- ("foreign"); differs because a xenobiotic is a foreign chemical an organism must detoxify, with no implication of beneficial stress signaling.
  • Adaptive stress response: Similar because it denotes the organism-level response that xenohormesis predicts; differs because it is a general physiological phenomenon within one organism, lacking the cross-species "signal eavesdropping" component.
  • Symbiosis: Similar because both concern interactions between different species; differs because symbiosis is a broad category of mutually (or one-sidedly) beneficial living arrangements between organisms, while xenohormesis is a specific chemical-signal detection across kingdoms with no implied co-habitation or mutualism.
