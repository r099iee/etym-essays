• Soaked: Similar because both describe saturation with liquid; differs because soaked is a general term for wetness, while waterlogged implies so much water that the object is rendered heavy and inert.
  • Saturated: Similar because both mean holding as much water as possible; differs because saturated is the scientific, neutral term for maximum absorption, while waterlogged emphasises the harmful, disabling result.
  • Sodden: Similar because both describe being thoroughly soaked and heavy; differs because sodden applies broadly to cloth, ground, or even a sodden spirit (drunk), while waterlogged suggests the object acts like a water-filled log.
  • Drenched: Similar because both describe soaking; differs because drenched describes the act or state of being wetted from outside, while waterlogged describes water held within the substance itself.
  • Soggy: Similar because both describe water-saturated softness; differs because soggy refers to soft, mushy consistency (food, paper, ground), while waterlogged can apply equally to hard objects like ships or timbers.
  • Swamped: Similar because both describe being flooded or overwhelmed by water; differs because swamped is more active and frequently figurative (swamped with work), while waterlogged is a physical condition of saturation.
  • Flooded: Similar because both describe water covering or filling something; differs because flooded implies a body of water moving in from outside, while waterlogged implies absorption of water into the material itself.
  • Bogged: Similar because both describe being stuck in wet ground; differs because bogged implies sinking into mire and being immobilised, while waterlogged describes the saturated condition itself.
  • Logy / Loggy: Similar because both derive from "log" and describe heaviness and sluggishness; differs because logy (chiefly American, "sluggish, dull") refers to mental or physical dullness, not to water content.
  • Inundated: Similar because both describe being overwhelmed with water; differs because inundated is formal and about flooding by a body of water, while waterlogged is about internal saturation and its disabling effect.
  • Water-soaked: Similar because both describe absorption of water; differs because water-soaked merely states the fact of wetness, while waterlogged adds the crucial idea of heaviness, inertness, and dysfunction.
