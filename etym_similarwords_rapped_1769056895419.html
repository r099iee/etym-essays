• tap: Similar in being a short, striking contact; differs because a tap is lighter, gentler, and often repeated — a delicate touch — while a rap is harder, sharper, and more decisive, a single crisp blow that makes an audible sound.
  • knock: Similar in striking a surface with a blow; differs because a knock is the standard, usually repeated and moderate force used on doors, while a rap can be one sharp, quick blow and always emphasises the sound of the contact.
  • thump: Similar in being a blow that makes a sound; differs because a thump is heavy and dull — the sound of a soft-mass impact — while a rap is sharp, dry, and brief, the report of a firm object meeting a surface.
  • smack: Similar in being a sharp, audible striking contact; differs because a smack is loud and flat — typically the open-hand blow — while a rap is the crisp, quick sound of a hard object (knuckles, a stick, a shield) striking a surface.
  • strike: Similar in being a general verb of impact; differs because strike carries no information about force, speed, or sound, while rap specifies a quick, sharp, audible blow and is almost always light.
  • hit: Similar in being a verb of impact; differs because hit is entirely neutral and general — any blow in any direction — while rap is the specific short, percussive, sound-making knock of a hard thing on a hard surface.
  • thwack: Similar in being a percussive, sound-making blow; differs because thwack is heavier and more swinging, the sound of a flat broad contact, while a rap is short, staccato, and dry.
  • whack: Similar in being a forceful blow; differs because a whack is a heavy, swinging stroke, while a rap is light, quick, and sharp, producing a crisp sound rather than a heavy impact.
  • crack: Similar in being a sharp, sudden, percussive sound; differs because crack suggests something snapping or splitting — a whip, a bone, ice — while rap is the clean contact-sound of hard objects that neither bends nor breaks.
  • bang: Similar in being a sudden, audible impact sound; differs because a bang is loud, heavy, and explosive — a slammed door, a gun — while a rap is quick, light, and dry, almost polite by comparison.
  • patter: Similar in being a rapid series of light striking sounds; differs because pattering is the continuous, gentle patter of rain or footsteps, while a rap is a single (or few) sharp, decisive blows that make themselves heard.
  • pound: Similar in being repeated striking against a surface; differs because pounding is heavy, forceful, and often angry or insistent, while rapping is quick, light, and crisp — the sound of control rather than of force.
