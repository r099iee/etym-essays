• Wealth: Similar because both denote riches; differs because wealth is the general, neutral term for all economic resources, with no implication of display or visible fullness.
  • Riches: Similar because both are concrete wealth; differs because riches is the plural, down-to-earth word for possessions, while opulence adds abundance plus visible, material quality.
  • Affluence: Similar because both name prosperity; differs because affluence stresses the steady inflow of money and comfortable means, without necessarily implying sumptuousness.
  • Prosperity: Similar because both describe thriving; differs because prosperity is the social and economic condition of a person or nation, not the visible splendour of the wealth itself.
  • Abundance / Plenty: Similar because both mean a large quantity; differs because they are neutral about whether the plenty is of money, and carry none of opulence's sumptuous display.
  • Luxury: Similar because both suggest lavish wealth; differs because luxury names the indulgent comforts that wealth buys, while opulence is the wealth itself as a visible, material quality.
  • Lavishness: Similar because both imply great abundance; differs because lavishness focuses on generous, even wasteful spending, while opulence names the state of being rich and full.
  • Sumptuousness: Similar because both denote costly splendour; differs because sumptuousness describes the expense of a particular thing (a sumptuous feast), while opulence is the general abundance from which such expense flows.
  • Grandeur: Similar because both suggest impressiveness; differs because grandeur stresses majesty and scale (of buildings, occasions), while opulence stresses wealth and fullness.
  • Magnificence: Similar because both denote splendid richness; differs because magnificence is the quality of being splendid and impressive, while opulence is specifically material wealth in bulk.
  • Profusion: Similar because both mean a large abundant amount; differs because profusion is quantity without the money-connotation, while opulence is riches themselves on show.
