• Thrifty: Similar because thrift is the same careful husbandry of resources; differs because thrifty stresses flourishing through care (thrift thrives), while frugal stresses restraint and simplicity.
  • Economical: Similar because both avoid waste and manage means well; differs because economical is cool and managerial (an economical car), while frugal is personal and moral (a frugal life).
  • Sparing: Similar because both use little and give carefully; differs because sparing stresses holding back (sparing with praise), while frugal stresses principled simplicity.
  • Austere: Similar because both embrace plainness and self-denial; differs because austere is severe and forbidding, while frugal is cheerful and sufficient.
  • Abstemious: Similar because both practise restraint at table and beyond; differs because abstemious targets appetite itself (drink, food), while frugal targets spending and waste.
  • Provident: Similar because both provide wisely for the future; differs because provident looks forward (saving for winter), while frugal acts now (wasting nothing today).
  • Prudent: Similar because prudence likewise manages resources with care; differs because prudent is broad caution in all affairs, while frugal is specifically restraint in consumption.
  • Miserly: Similar because misers also spend little; differs because miserly hoards from fear and meanness and is always condemnatory, while frugal restrains from principle and is praise.
