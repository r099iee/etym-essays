• Order: Similar in being an authoritative demand to act; differs because an order is blunt and peremptory with no need for relationship, while an injunction carries a solemn, conscience-binding or legal weight.
  • Directive: Similar in being an official instruction; differs because a directive is bureaucratic and procedural - the language of offices and memos - where an injunction has moral or judicial authority.
  • Edict: Similar in being an authoritative proclamation; differs because an edict is the public decree of a ruler or state, while an injunction is targeted at a specific person or action.
  • Mandate: Similar in conveying a binding instruction or commission; differs because a mandate is a broad authorization or electoral instruction, while an injunction is a specific order to do or refrain from doing a particular thing.
  • Command: Similar in demanding obedience from a subordinate; differs because a command carries military or sovereign force expecting instant compliance, while an injunction may be a gentler, conscience-binding or judicial charge.
  • Instruction: Similar in telling someone what to do; differs because an instruction is neutral, practical, and often procedural, while an injunction is authoritative, solemn, and morally or legally binding.
  • Admonition: Similar in being a warning or gentle reproof from authority; differs because an admonition is chiefly corrective and cautionary, while an injunction is a positive order or charge.
  • Commandment: Similar in being a binding rule imposed on conduct; differs because a commandment is religious or quasi-scriptural (the Ten Commandments), while an injunction is secular, ranging from law to household custom.
