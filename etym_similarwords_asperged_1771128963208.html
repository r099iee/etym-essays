• Sprinkled: Similar because both mean scattered in fine drops or particles; differs because sprinkled is the ordinary, neutral everyday word, while asperged is formal and liturgical, implying a deliberate, often holy sprinkling.
  • Scattered: Similar because both mean dispersed over an area; differs because scattered is broad and can apply to solid objects, crowds, or random distribution, while asperged specifically means sprinkled with a liquid, gently and deliberately.
  • Besprinkled: Similar because both mean sprinkled over or upon; differs because besprinkled is a mild, neutral literary verb, while asperged carries the ceremonial, holy-water resonance of the liturgy.
  • Sprayed: Similar because both involve a liquid dispersed in droplets; differs because sprayed is mechanical, forceful, and everyday (hoses, sprays), while asperged implies fine, gentle, deliberate scattering, often sacred.
  • Showered: Similar because both describe liquid falling or being directed onto something; differs because showered suggests a heavier, freer fall or grant, while asperged suggests a fine, controlled sprinkling.
  • Splashed: Similar because both involve liquid striking a surface; differs because splashed is abrupt, messy, and abundant, while asperged is gentle, fine, and deliberate.
  • Moistened: Similar because both mean made slightly wet; differs because moistened is a plain functional term for slight wetness, while asperged implies scattering of drops rather than mere dampening.
  • Bedewed: Similar because both describe light moisture deposited on a surface; differs because bedewed is purely about dew-like dampness, while asperged is about the act of sprinkling, often holy water.
  • Drizzled: Similar because both mean falling or applied in fine drops; differs because drizzled is mainly about rain or thin liquid poured in a stream, while asperged is about deliberate scattering, especially as a rite.
  • Dusted: Similar because both mean covered lightly with fine matter; differs because dusted applies to dry powders, while asperged specifically involves liquid.
  • Spattered: Similar because both involve drops of liquid being scattered; differs because spattered implies irregular, accidental, often dirty splashing, while asperged implies deliberate, clean, ceremonial sprinkling.
  • Consecrated: Similar because both can involve the application of holy water to bless something; differs because consecrated means formally dedicated or made sacred, while asperged means merely sprinkled — often as one step in such a rite.
