Note: The word rein descends from Classical Latin retinēre ("to hold back, restrain", formed from re- "back" + tenēre "to hold"). In the colloquial speech of late imperial cavalrymen (Vulgar Latin), the verb generated the feminine verbal noun *retina ("that which holds back"). Through Gallo-Romance syncopation of the unstressed post-tonic vowel, *retina contracted into *ret'na, followed by the lenition and vocalization of the dental stop before the nasal, yielding Old French resne (Modern French rêne). Anglo-Norman scribes brought the form into post-Conquest England as reine and reyne. A notorious homophonic trap exists in Modern English between rein ("bridle strap"), reign ("sovereign rule", from Latin regnare), and rain ("precipitation", from Germanic *regna-), leading to widespread contemporary folk misspellings such as *'free reign'* instead of the correct free rein.
