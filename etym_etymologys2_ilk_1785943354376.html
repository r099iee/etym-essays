PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*līg-
"form, shape; similar"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*līkaz
"form, shape, appearance"
↓
Old English (c. 9th–10th c.)
ilca
"the same, selfsame"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
ilke
"the same; of the same kind"
↓
Modern English
ilk
"kind, sort, class"

Note: Old English ilca compounds the demonstrative stem *i- "this" with *līkaz "form". The noun "kind, class" grew out of the Scottish phrase "of that ilk" (16th c.) — "of the same place-name" — and generalized to "of the same kind" by the 18th century.
