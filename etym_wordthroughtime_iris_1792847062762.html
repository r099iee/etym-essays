- Homeric Greece — rainbow and messenger: îris is both the rainbow arc and the swift goddess who runs it as a road between gods and mortals.
- Classical Greece — the flower takes the name: physicians and naturalists call the many-hued flag îris, since no bloom shows the rainbow's palette more proudly.
- Rome — borrowing complete: Latin īris carries rainbow, plant, and bright-haloed gem together into the empire's handbooks.
- 14th–16th c. England — flower and sky: herbalists grow iris in physic gardens while poets keep the rainbow sense alive in verse.
- 16th–18th c. — the eye claims it: anatomists name the coloured ring of the eye the iris, and the plural irides enters medical Latin.
- 19th c. — instruments borrow it: opticians build the iris diaphragm for microscopes and cameras, the mechanical pupil.
- Today — marsh, eye, and lens at once: yellow flags still gild the Gladden-like rivers, photographers still stop down the iris, and every brown-eyed child carries the rainbow goddess in miniature.
