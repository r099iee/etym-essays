PIE (c. 3500 BCE)
*upo + *yeh₁-
"under + to throw, cast"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*sub-jakiō
"to throw under, place below"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
subicere (subiicere)
"to throw under, make subject"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
subiectus
"placed under, subordinate"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th c. CE)
subiectiō
"submission, subjection, obedience"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
subjection / soujection
"fealty, subjection, submission"
↓
Middle English (c. 1380)
subjeccioun / subjection
"subjection, state of obedience"
↓
Modern English
subjection
"subjection, subjugation, submission"

Note: Latin subicere was formed from prefix sub- ("under") and verb iaciō ("to throw"), showing regular apophony of the medial root vowel [a] to [i]. Late Latin nominalized the participial stem subiect- with suffix -tiō to create subiectiō, directly translating Greek hypotagē in ecclesiastical texts.
