MULE
Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
mūlus
"a mule"
↓
Old French (c. 11th c.)
mule
"a mule"
↓
English (13th c.)
mule
"the hybrid offspring of
a horse and a donkey"

Note: origin of Latin mūlus is uncertain — possibly a loanword from a Mediterranean substrate; a PIE *mus- "a mule" has been proposed but is not securely established. Old English mūl (from Latin) is a separate parallel borrowing.

HARROW
Old Norse (c. 10th c.)
herfi
"a harrow"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
harwe
"a toothed tool for
breaking up soil"
↓
English (13th c.)
harrow
"a farm tool for
breaking up soil"

Note: origin uncertain — a Proto-Germanic *harwō has been reconstructed, possibly related to *harwa- "harrowing, painful", but no secure PIE root is known. The verb "to harrow" (to torment) derives from the tool's teeth.
