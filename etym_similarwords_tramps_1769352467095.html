• Vagrants: Similar because both name people who wander without a settled home; differs because vagrant is the legal and administrative term emphasising lack of settled residence, while tramps emphasises the foot-travelling itself and carries a heavier, more human, more sympathetic colouring.
  • Vagabonds: Similar because both name wandering, unsettled people; differs because vagabond has a romantic, roguish, or literary flavour, while tramp is plainer and names the weary heavy-footed walker.
  • Hobos: Similar because both name itinerant travellers of the railroad era; differs because hobo specifically names the rail-riding migrant worker who travels to find work, while tramp names the foot-walker, who may or may not seek work.
  • Drifters: Similar because both name people who move without a fixed purpose or home; differs because drifter suggests passive, aimless movement, while tramp suggests deliberate, heavy walking of the roads.
  • Wanderers: Similar because both name people who travel from place to place; differs because wanderer is broad and can be positive or neutral (a wanderer in the hills), while tramp in the person-sense is tied to homelessness and rough living.
  • Transients: Similar because both name people passing through a place without settling; differs because transient is a modern, somewhat bureaucratic word for anyone who stays only briefly, while tramp is the older, harsher word for the foot-travelling poor.
  • Itinerants: Similar because both name people who travel from place to place; differs because itinerant is neutral and often professional (itinerant preacher, itinerant worker), while tramp names the wanderer who lives rough.
  • Bums: Similar because both are slang for homeless men who live by the road; differs because bum implies a stationary, idle, often urban vagrant who does not work, while tramp implies the active, weary walker of the roads.
  • Beggars: Similar because both can name the poor who live on the road; differs because beggar names a person defined by asking for alms, while tramp names a person defined by walking, who may or may not beg.
  • Wayfarers: Similar because both name people who travel by foot; differs because wayfarer is archaic and literary, with a dignified, medieval romance (Chaucer's wayfarers), while tramp is modern, rough, and tied to the working poor.
  • Nomads: Similar because both name people without a fixed home who move about; differs because nomad names a people or person who moves with a culture of movement (herding, seasonal), while tramp is an individual vagrant of industrial society.
  • Rovers: Similar because both name people who wander; differs because rover is light, adventurous, and often admiring, while tramp is heavy, weary, and rooted in poverty.
