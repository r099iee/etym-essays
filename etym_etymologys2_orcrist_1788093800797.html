NOTE: Orcrist is a constructed (artificial) word coined by J. R. R. Tolkien for The Hobbit (1937). The chains below follow his own invented philology (root system recorded in "The Etymologies", late 1930s); they are his design, not a historical language record.

Element 1 — the "orc" element
Elvish root (constructed) (Tolkien, late 1930s)
ŌROK
"demon, orc"
↓
Quenya (constructed)
urko / orco
"orc, goblin"
↓
Sindarin (constructed)
orch
"orc, goblin"

Element 2 — the "cleaver" element
Elvish root (constructed) (Tolkien, late 1930s)
KIRIS
"to cut, cleave"
↓
Sindarin (constructed)
crist
"cleaver, sword"

Compound
Sindarin (constructed) (The Hobbit, 1937)
ORCRIST
"orc-cleaver, goblin-cleaver"

Note: Tolkien himself glosses the name "Goblin-cleaver" in the narrative (Elrond names the blade at Rivendell); the goblins of Goblin-town call it "Biter". The compound analysis (orch + crist) is standard in Tolkien scholarship; the reconstruction of the primitive roots is Tolkien's own invention, and is noted as invented, not attested. Tolkien derived the modern English word "orc" partly by choosing the Old English orc "demon" (Beowulf, LotR Appendix F) to translate the Elvish word.
