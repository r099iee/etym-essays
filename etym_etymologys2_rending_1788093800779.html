Note (stage gap, no guesswork): no PIE root, Proto-Germanic form, or foreign ancestor can be securely reconstructed for this verb: Old English rendan has no certain cognates outside English (a Middle Dutch renden 'to tear' is sometimes adduced, but the connection is not established), and the ultimate origin is unknown — the chain therefore begins at the first attested form instead of an invented root. All stages after Old English are the ordinary historical development of the English verb system (infinitive -en, participial -ende > -ing); note that the strong-style past participle rent (ME rente) coexists with regular rended from the Middle English period and survives in literary use.
