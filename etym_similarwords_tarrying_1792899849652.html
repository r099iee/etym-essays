• Lingering: Similar because both mean remaining beyond the moment of leaving; differs because lingering is the neutral everyday word, while tarrying is literary and graver, suited to exiles rather than shoppers.
  • Loitering: Similar because both describe hanging about instead of moving on; differs because loitering implies suspicious idleness and invites the police, while tarrying implies wistful delay and invites sympathy.
  • Dallying: Similar because both mean delaying with a pleasure in the delay; differs because dallying hints at flirtation or trifling, while tarrying is innocent of mischief.
  • Dawdling: Similar because both mean being slow to get going; differs because dawdling is a scold’s word for children and idlers, while tarrying dignifies the pause.
  • Delaying: Similar because both name postponed departure or action; differs because delaying is plain and often transitive (delaying a flight), while tarrying is intransitive, personal, and poetic.
  • Waiting: Similar because both involve staying until something happens; differs because waiting fixes on the expected event, while tarrying fixes on the lingering itself, event or none.
  • Sojourning: Similar because both describe a temporary, conscious stay, as in Tolkien’s sentence; differs because sojourning stresses residence (a sojourn of months), while tarrying stresses the hovering before departure.
  • Abiding: Similar because both carry the biblical perfume of remaining faithfully; differs because abiding promises permanence (abide with me), while tarrying promises eventual leaving.
  • Procrastinating: Similar because both postpone what should be done; differs because procrastinating is clinical and blaming, a failure of will, while tarrying may be loving, necessary, or fated.
