• Durability: Similar because both denote the capacity to resist decay, wear, or destruction over time; differs because durability emphasizes physical toughness, mechanical strength, and resilience against external damage or heavy usage, whereas permanence emphasizes perpetual, unchanging temporal continuation or ontological eternity.
  • Perpetuity: Similar because both designate unending or indefinite duration; differs because perpetuity carries a formal, legal, or mathematical finality (frequently denoting an estate, annuity, or trust established to last forever by law), whereas permanence can describe physical material stability, psychological states, or philosophical substances.
  • Constancy: Similar because both convey steadfastness and the absence of wavering; differs because constancy emphasizes emotional, moral, or behavioral fidelity (such as constant affection, steadfast loyalty, or unwavering purpose), whereas permanence denotes the existential fact of enduring survival across time.
  • Stability: Similar because both describe the condition of resisting collapse, upheaval, or disruption; differs because stability highlights equilibrium, balance, and the capacity to return to a steady state after being shaken, whereas permanence highlights enduring survival throughout the whole expanse of time.
  • Immutability: Similar because both characterize entities that do not alter; differs because immutability denotes the absolute inability to change or be modified in any aspect (frequently reserved for divine attributes or mathematical truths), whereas permanence simply means remaining in existence without being destroyed, even if superficial modifications occur.
  • Endurance: Similar because both denote lasting through time; differs because endurance highlights the active struggle of bearing hardship, pain, or grueling stress (surviving through suffering), whereas permanence is the serene or neutral condition of lasting continuously.
  • Transience: Similar because both are direct antonymic counter-poles that define the boundaries of temporal existence; differs because transience denotes the fleeting, ephemeral brevity of passing phenomena (like morning dew or passing clouds), whereas permanence denotes unyielding survival that defies the flow of time.
