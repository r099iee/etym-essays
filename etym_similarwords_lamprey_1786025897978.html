• Eel: Similar because both are long, slender, snake-like aquatic animals with eel-like bodies; differs because true eels are jawed bony fish (Anguilliformes) with fins and gill covers, whereas lampreys are jawless cyclostomes with cartilaginous skeletons and a suctorial mouth.
  • Hagfish: Similar because both are primitive, jawless, eel-shaped cyclostomes that live in water; differs because hagfish are deep-sea scavengers that produce copious slime and lack true vertebrae, while lampreys have cartilaginous vertebral elements and often parasitize live fish.
  • Leech: Similar because both attach to host bodies by suction and feed on blood; differs because leeches are segmented annelid worms (invertebrates), whereas lampreys are vertebrate fish.
  • Parasite: Similar because both feed off a host organism for sustenance; differs because parasite is a broad biological and social category applying to countless organisms and people, while lamprey names one specific vertebrate species and its feeding method.
  • Bloodsucker: Similar because both drain the bodily fluids of others and are used metaphorically for exploiters; differs because bloodsucker is an informal, emotionally charged insult that can refer to fleas, vampires, or greedy people, while lamprey is the precise name of a fish.
  • Sucker: Similar because both describe organisms that attach by suction, and both are applied to gullible people; differs because sucker is a general word for anything that sucks or any dupe, while lamprey denotes the specific fish and its specific parasitic habit.
  • Remora: Similar because both are fish that attach to larger sea animals by a suction device; differs because the remora is a jawed bony fish with a dorsal suction disc that hitches a ride (commensalism), whereas the lamprey is jawless and feeds parasitically by boring in.
  • Sycophant: Similar because both metaphorically attach to powerful people for gain; differs because a sycophant uses flattery and subservience, while the lamprey image emphasizes physical attachment and draining of resources.
  • Toady: Similar because both name a flattering hanger-on in the social world; differs because toady is a purely human term for a servile flatterer, while lamprey as an insult keeps the visceral image of a blood-draining animal.
  • Conger eel: Similar because both are large, edible, marine eel-like creatures; differs because the conger is a true jawed bony eel caught for food today, while the lamprey is jawless and its culinary fame is largely historical.
  • Lancelet: Similar because both are primitive chordates used to study vertebrate origins; differs because the lancelet (amphioxus) is a tiny, burrowing, fishless chordate that is not a fish and never parasitizes, while the lamprey is a true (if jawless) vertebrate fish.
  • Vampire: Similar because both drink blood and are metaphors for draining exploiters; differs because vampire is a supernatural humanoid of folklore and fiction, while the lamprey is a real, observable fish whose biology inspired no mythology of its own.
