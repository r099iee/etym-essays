PIE *steu- "to push, strike, knock" (conjectural; c. 4500–3000 BCE) → Proto-Germanic *stubbaz "a stump — the projecting remnant of a cut tree" (c. 500 BCE–200 CE) → Old English stybb "a stump, stub" (attested c. 1000 CE in glosses) → Middle English stubbe "a stump; any short blunt remnant" (12th–15th c.) → Modern English stub (n.) "a short blunt remnant" (pencil stub, ticket stub) → stub (v.) "to strike (one's toe) against a fixed projection" (19th c.) → Modern English stubbed (past tense; "struck a toe or foot against a hard object") Note: the PIE attribution is conjectural — the geminate -bb- of the Germanic base suggests a sound-symbolic or expressive formation, possibly substrate in origin, and scholars caution that *steu- is probable but unproven. False friends: stubble descends from Latin stipula via Old French, not from stybb; stubborn is of obscure origin, though a derivation from stybb ("as stiff as a stub") has often been suggested but is not established.
