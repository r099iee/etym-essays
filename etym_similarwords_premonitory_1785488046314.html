• Prophetic: Similar in foreseeing or revealing the future; differs because prophetic implies supernatural or divinely inspired revelation, while premonitory is satisfied by an ordinary sign or symptom that gives notice in advance.
  • Ominous: Similar in giving a warning of what is to come; differs because ominous is wholly negative — it always threatens evil or disaster — whereas premonitory can be neutral or even positive in its older sense.
  • Portentous: Similar in foreshadowing a significant event; differs because portentous adds a pompous, self-important, or solemn tone and usually signals something momentous or dreadful, while premonitory is plain and unassuming.
  • Foreboding: Similar in pointing toward future misfortune; differs because foreboding is an inner feeling of dread rather than an outward sign — one feels a foreboding, one reads a premonitory symptom.
  • Prognostic: Similar in predicting a future state from present signs; differs because prognostic is chiefly medical and clinical — a doctor's forecast of the course of a disease — while premonitory ranges across weather, behaviour, and abstract signals.
  • Presaging: Similar in foretelling what is to come; differs because presaging stresses the act of indication by a portent, whereas premonitory keeps the sense of an advance warning given to a perceiver.
  • Augural: Similar in relating to signs of the future; differs because augural belongs specifically to formal augury and bird-reading in classical religion, whereas premonitory is used of any ordinary signal.
  • Oracular: Similar in disclosing what is to come; differs because oracular implies the speech of an oracle — mysterious, authoritative, ambiguous — while premonitory implies plain, readable warning signs.
  • Anticipatory: Similar in relating to what comes before an event; differs because anticipatory means simply 'taking place or acting in advance' with no warning content, while the core of premonitory is the giving of notice.
  • Precursory: Similar in preceding or announcing something; differs because precursory stresses mere priority or a preliminary stage, often without the 'warning' element that premonitory always carries.
