• muleteer: Similar in driving mules on the road; differs because muleteer names the driver of mules specifically, with an older, more international, borrowed sound (French muletier, Spanish mulero), while teamster names the man who manages the whole yoked team.
  • wagoner: Similar in being a driver of a wagon; differs because wagoner is relatively neutral and old-fashioned, naming the vehicle, whereas teamster names the harnessed team — the animal unit yoked and pulling together — and the man who manages it.
  • carter: Similar in being an occupational hauling driver; differs because the carter hauls with a cart, carrying a low, workaday register and a shorter range, while the teamster belongs to the long freight roads.
  • drayman: Similar in being an occupational hauling driver; differs because the drayman drives the dray, the heavy brewery cart, and belongs to urban commerce, while the teamster belongs to the long freight roads and the frontier.
  • driver: Similar as the generic word for one who operates a vehicle or team; differs because driver has no trade color, while teamster names the specific animal-unit freight trade and its later union history.
  • carman: Similar in being a driver of a vehicle; differs because carman drives a tram or cart and is chiefly British, while teamster is American freight vocabulary.
  • trucker: Similar as the modern successor who hauls freight; differs because the trucker drives a motor vehicle, and the word today often means a union member who never drove an animal at all — the teamster's animal origins live only in the name.
  • haulier / hauler: Similar in conveying goods; differs because haulier is a general modern logistics term (British), while teamster preserves the historical animal-team sense and the American union meaning.
  • mule skinner: Similar in being a frontier driver of mules; differs because "mule skinner" is specifically the American colloquial name for the man of the mule train, while teamster is the broader occupational class covering horses and mules alike.
