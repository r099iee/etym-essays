• Habituation: Similar because both describe behavioural patterns established through repeated experience or exposure over time; differs because habituation implies becoming psychologically accustomed, desensitized, or passive to a stimulus, whereas frequentation emphasizes the active, deliberate practice of visiting or associating with a place, person, or text.
  • Familiarity: Similar because both result from repeated interaction and close acquaintance; differs because familiarity describes the resulting psychological state of knowledge, ease, or intimate understanding, whereas frequentation is the ongoing behavioral act, visit, or process that produces that familiarity.
  • Attendance: Similar because both involve being physically or socially present at a place or event; differs because attendance is a formal, functional term denoting presence at meetings, classes, or ceremonies (often measured quantitatively for duty or record-keeping), whereas frequentation suggests an intimate, voluntary, and culturally rich relationship with a venue or circle.
  • Association: Similar because both denote regular social contact, companionship, or connection; differs because association is a broad term encompassing formal partnerships, commercial alliances, and general connection, whereas frequentation specifically highlights the repetitive, physical or intellectual act of keeping regular company.
  • Haunting: Similar because both convey the idea of constantly visiting or spending time in a specific haunt or location; differs because haunting often carries eerie, spectral, or obsessive psychological connotations, whereas frequentation is a dignified, literary, or sociologically grounded practice.
  • Patronage: Similar because both describe the repeated visiting and support of establishments like shops, theaters, or literary salons; differs because patronage emphasizes the commercial, financial, or protective power exerted by the client or benefactor, whereas frequentation focuses purely on physical or social presence and regular habit.
  • Communion: Similar because both describe deep, intimate spiritual or intellectual sharing; differs because communion denotes a profound mystical, emotional, or sacramental union, whereas frequentation describes the iterative, worldly, or reading practice through which such understanding is cultivated.
