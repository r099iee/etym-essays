↓ PIE (reconstructed)
*h₂elh₂-
"to wander"
(uncertain)

↓ Latin (1st c. BCE)
ambulare
"to walk"

↓ Vulgar Latin (reconstructed)
*alare
"to go"

↓ Old French (9th c.)
aler
"to go"

↓ Old French (12th c.)
alee
"a going,
a passage"

↓ Middle English (14th c.)
aley
"a passage,
a walk"

↓ English (15th c.)
alley
"narrow passage
between buildings"

↓ English (modern)
alleys
"narrow passageways"

Note: alee is the feminine past
participle of aler, used as a noun.
Old French aler is usually linked
to Latin ambulare via a reconstructed
Vulgar Latin *alare, but the steps
are unattested and the PIE root
*h₂elh₂- 'to wander' is debated.
