Quadrupeds is the plural noun for animals that walk on four feet, most typically mammals such as dogs, cats, horses, cows, lions, and bears. The singular is quadruped: a quadruped is one four-footed animal, quadrupeds are several. The term is classificatory rather than taxonomic today: it describes a mode of locomotion and body plan, not a single evolutionary family.

In zoology and everyday speech, quadruped contrasts with biped (humans, birds, kangaroos when hopping), tripod (rare), centipede and millipede (many legs), legless snakes and worms, and aquatic swimmers and fliers. A horse is an obligate quadruped; a chimpanzee is a facultative quadruped that can knuckle-walk or stand; a human infant crawling is temporarily quadrupedal. Robotics borrows the term directly: a quadruped robot has four legs for stability over rough ground.

  • Core zoological sense: four-footed mammals and reptiles. Example: African quadrupeds include elephants, rhinos, and antelopes. Implies weight borne on four limbs, spine horizontal.
  • Locomotion sense: quadrupedal gait - walk, trot, pace, gallop, bound. Example: cheetahs as quadrupeds can reach 100 km/h by flexing the spine between front and hind legs.
  • Paleontological sense: early tetrapods and dinosaurs: sauropods were giant quadrupeds, theropods were bipeds. Describes posture inferred from fossils and trackways.
  • Literary-pejorative sense: as in the prompt, chattering quadrupeds reduces talkative beasts - monkeys, dogs - to noisy inferiors, or ironically elevates them as future masters who will devour man. Tone is satirical and misanthropic.

Polysemy is limited because quadrupeds is technical and transparent: quadru- always signals four, -ped always signals foot. Secondary extensions are metaphorical rather than lexicalized. To go down on all fours is to become quadrupedal, implying regression, drunkenness, infancy, or bestial submission. Insults using quadruped suggest brutish lack of reason. In science fiction, quadrupeds can denote alien life-forms with four limbs, even if not feet.

  • Mammals: the default referent. Dogs, wolves, foxes, cats, horses, cattle, deer, pigs, elephants. Birds are never quadrupeds despite having four limbs (wings count separately).
  • Reptiles and amphibians: lizards, crocodiles, salamanders, frogs (as adults) are quadrupeds functionally, though herpetologists more often say tetrapods or quadrupedal.
  • Robots: quadruped platforms like Spot, ANYmal. Engineers discuss quadruped balance, trotting gait, and payload.
  • Human development: crawling babies, injured adults, or climbers are described as moving quadrupedally, a temporary posture.
  • Myth and heraldry: sphinxes, griffins, centaurs (in part) depicted as quadruped bodies with human or eagle fronts.

Grammar is simple: countable noun, regular plural with -s, singular quadruped. Collocates with small, large, domestic, wild, African, grazing, chattering, galloping quadrupeds. Opposite pole is biped, with human as the prototypical marauding biped in the prompt's apocalyptic vision. Register is neutral-formal, at home in textbooks, documentaries, and literary prose, slightly pompous in casual speech where four-legged animal or four-legged friend would be warmer.

To master quadrupeds is to see feet as worldview: how many points touch the earth decides speed, stability, height of eyes, and even moral symbolism - erect man versus prone beast. The prompt exploits exactly this symbolism to invert hierarchy: the despised four-footed chatterers will one day devour their two-footed exploiter.
