• Mārutātmaja: Similar because both literally mean "son of the wind god" and serve as exalted epic epithets for Hanumān and Bhīma; differs because Mārutātmaja is formed from the storm-god name Māruta and the metaphysical compound ātmaja ("born from the self/soul"), whereas Vāyusunu utilizes the ancient Indo-European kinship noun sūnu ("son") and the simplex name of the wind god Vāyu ("the blower").
  • Māruti: Similar because both are patronymics celebrating Hanumān's descent from the wind deity; differs because Māruti is a single derivative word formed with the secondary patronymic suffix -i (an apatya suffix), whereas Vāyusunu is an explicit two-word compound pairing the deity's name with the noun for son.
  • Vāyuputra: Similar because both directly pair the god's name Vāyu with a kinship term for son; differs because Vāyuputra uses putra (the standard, widespread Sanskrit noun for son), whereas Vāyusunu uses the archaic, highly poetic Indo-European noun sūnu (cognate to English son), carrying distinct epic cadence and antiquity.
  • Pavanasuta: Similar because both designate Hanumān through his wind paternity; differs because Pavanasuta uses Pavana ("the pure, cleansing breeze") and suta ("offspring"), a formation common in medieval devotional vernaculars (such as the *Hanumān Chālīsā*), whereas Vāyusunu is high classical epic Sanskrit.
  • Vātātmaja: Similar because both denote the wind's offspring; differs because Vātātmaja derives from Vāta (the physical, blowing wind, cognate to Latin ventus), whereas Vāyu represents the personified Vedic deity who drives a thousand horses and drinks the sacrificial Soma.
  • Hanumān: Similar because both denote the paramount heroic monkey champion of Rāma; differs because Hanumān is his primary proper name (derived from his injured jaw, hanu), whereas Vāyusunu is a descriptive patronymic kenning emphasizing his celestial origin.
  • Bhīmasena: Similar because Bhīma is also celebrated in the Mahābhārata as a Vāyusunu (the spiritual son of Vāyu begotten upon Queen Kuntī); differs because Bhīmasena is a human Kuru warrior prince renowned for his mace-combat and voracious appetite, whereas Hanumān is a divine immortal vānara.
  • Añjanīsuta: Similar because both identify Hanumān through his divine parental lineage; differs because Añjanīsuta is a matronymic honoring his biological mother, the vānara queen Añjanā, whereas Vāyusunu is a patronymic honoring his atmospheric father.
  • Kesarinandana: Similar because both celebrate Hanumān's lineage; differs because Kesarinandana designates Hanumān as the beloved son of King Kesari, his earthly foster father, whereas Vāyusunu asserts his true divine biological origin from the wind god.
  • Pavanatanaya: Similar because both mean "son of the wind"; differs because Pavanatanaya uses tanaya (literally "the son who extends the family thread"), carrying a distinct metrical shape in classical verse.
  • Bajraṅgabālī: Similar because both are venerated titles of Hanumān; differs because Bajraṅgabālī focuses on his adamantine, diamond-like physical body (*vajrāṅga*) and physical strength (*balī*), whereas Vāyusunu highlights his atmospheric, divine parentage.
  • Gandhavahatanaya: Similar because both mean "son of the wind"; differs because Gandhavaha is a poetic circumlocution for the wind meaning "the bearer of fragrances", making Gandhavahatanaya an elaborate double-kenning ("son of the bearer of scents"), whereas Vāyusunu is a direct, classical patronymic.
