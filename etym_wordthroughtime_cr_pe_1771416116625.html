- [18th-century French import] - Adopted as the name of the crimped, crinkled mourning fabric - black crêpe becomes the badge of deep grief in Victorian dress.
- [19th-century texture vocabulary] - Expands across dressmaking: silk crêpe, crêpe de chine, the matte pebbled surface prized as the elegant opposite of glossy satin.
- [20th-century split across domains] - The same word now also names the thin French pancake (crêpe), crêpe paper, and crêpe rubber soles - a single form fanning out over fabric, food and industry.
- [Contemporary register] - In fashion, crêpe still signals refined, matte, structured elegance; in the kitchen and workshop, the word is plain and functional.
