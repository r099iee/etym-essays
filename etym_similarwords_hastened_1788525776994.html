• hurried: Similar because both denote rapid physical motion or swift action undertaken with urgency; differs because hurried is the common, neutral everyday term that often implies fluster, disorganization, or loss of composure, whereas hastened carries a more elevated, deliberate, and literary register.
  • rushed: Similar for fast, urgent movement; differs because rushed emphasizes reckless speed, sudden violent impetuosity, or careless precipitation, whereas hastened preserves intentionality and purposeful dignity.
  • quickened: Similar for increasing the rate of movement or process; differs because quickened focuses specifically on the acceleration of an already existing pace (or the stirring of life/pulse), whereas hastened emphasizes the immediate motive of prompt dispatch toward a goal.
  • accelerated: Similar in the transitive sense of making a process occur faster; differs because accelerated is a technical, mechanical, and scientific term used for velocity changes, chemical reactions, or economic rates, lacking the personal narrative warmth of hastened.
  • precipitated: Similar for bringing about an event prematurely; differs because precipitated carries a strong connotation of sudden, violent, or disastrous causation (e.g., precipitating a crisis or downfall), whereas hastened simply denotes making something happen sooner.
  • expedited: Similar in the sense of speeding up the progress of a task or transaction; differs because expedited belongs to administrative, bureaucratic, and commercial registers (e.g., expedited shipping or processing), whereas hastened is primarily narrative and literary.
  • speeded: Similar for moving or causing to move fast; differs because speeded (or sped) emphasizes raw physical velocity or exceeding speed limits rather than purposeful urgent intent.
  • scurried: Similar for fast movement across an open space; differs because scurried evokes the short, rapid, agitated scampering of small animals or anxious individuals, contrasting with the purposeful stride of hastened.
  • dashed: Similar for sudden fast running; differs because dashed implies an explosive, short burst of violent running across a short distance, whereas hastened denotes sustained purposeful dispatch.
