assiduously: In a constant, attentive, and unremitting manner — with persistent, conscientious effort applied steadily to a task, as though one had made a seat of the work and would not rise until it was finished. The word is the adverbial form of assiduous, and the whole family descends from Latin assiduus, "constant, unwearied," built from the verb assidēre, "to sit down at, to sit close beside" (ad-, "at, toward," + sedēre, "to sit"). That seated image is the key to the word's meaning and to its special literary force: the person who works assiduously does not merely perform a duty from time to time but attends it with the permanence of someone who has settled in for the duration. Assiduity is the drip of water that wears the stone, not the flash of lightning; it is constancy, watchfulness, and a loyalty to the task that can border on obsession. The primary sense. In its central and governing meaning, assiduously means "with great care, persistence, and diligent effort." One works assiduously on a manuscript, studies assiduously for an examination, or tends assiduously to a sick friend. The emphasis falls on the constancy of application — on the continuity and unbrokenness of the effort — rather than on the intensity of any single burst. A task done assiduously is a task that is never abandoned halfway, never done by fits and starts, never left to the last moment; it is the steady, patient habit of the one who stays. Secondary and extended senses. From this core the word radiates into several connected meanings, each carrying a share of the seated image:
  • Regular, habitual, repeated care: assiduously can describe anything done steadily and frequently — a garden "assiduously maintained," a correspondent "assiduously in touch." Here the sense is of habit, of never letting the attention lapse.
  • Watchful, anxious vigilance: because constancy of attendance can shade into fussiness, the word often suggests a slightly obsessive, almost anxious care — the care of a keeper who treats every speck of dust as an invader. The passage exploits this exact shade: the dust is "so assiduously kept from the carvings" that it has "no alternative resting place" and collects, deep and ash-like, in the four corners of the hall. The cleaning is not mere tidiness but harrying — the dust is hounded off every carved surface into exile.
  • Social attentiveness: assiduously also describes punctilious attendance on a person — the assiduous courtier, the host who is assiduously attentive to every guest — where constancy signals devotion, respect, or sometimes servility.
  • Professional and scholarly register: in academic and professional prose, assiduous, assiduously, and the noun assiduity name the patient, systematic application that produces reliable work — the virtue of the researcher and the administrator, as opposed to brilliance or inspiration.
Grammar and register. Assiduously is an adverb and typically modifies verbs of work, study, care, and maintenance: to work, study, attend, clean, cultivate, and tend. It is not used of physical speed or force; it governs effort and attention rather than motion. The family includes the adjective assiduous ("constant in application"), the noun assiduity (the older and more formal abstract noun, "constant perseverance"), and the rarer assiduousness. The word sits at a formal, literary register — more bookish than "hard" or "busily," more precise than "constantly," and preferred in prose that wants to suggest a quiet, unbroken devotion to the task in hand. Comparison with near-synonyms. The word is set apart by the constancy-and-attendance nuance. diligently stresses careful, loving application to duty (from diligere, "to value highly"); sedulously implies sincere, guileless devotion; industriously stresses energetic, productive busyness; painstakingly accepts trouble and slow care; meticulously and scrupulously stress exactness born of fear or conscience. Only assiduously preserves the sense of constant attendance — of sitting by a thing until the work is done — which is exactly the obsessive loyalty the passage dramatizes and which the sentence confirms by showing where the displaced dust must go.
