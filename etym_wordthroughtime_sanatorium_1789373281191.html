- Mid-19th c. (1840s–1880s) - Curative Haven for the Consumptive: Coined in medical Latin and popularized in Germany and Britain as a revolutionary alpine or coastal retreat offering open-air cures for pulmonary tuberculosis.
- Turn of the 20th c. (1890s–1920s) - Elite Neurological and Literary Sanctuary: Expanded into luxurious private clinics treating upper-class neurasthenia and depression (such as Proust's stay); immortalized in European literature as a philosophical microcosm in Thomas Mann's 'The Magic Mountain'.
- Mid-20th c. (1930s–1950s) - Soviet Workers' Wellness Resort: Reimagined across the Soviet Union as a monumental, state-subsidized palace of preventive medicine, balneotherapy, and collective health for industrial labourers.
- Post-WWII Era (1950s–1980s) - Medical Obsolescence: The discovery of streptomycin and rifampicin rendered tuberculosis sanatoria obsolete, leading to their widespread closure, abandonment, or conversion into general hospitals.
- Contemporary Era - Architectural Relic and Historical Symbol: Evokes nostalgic, eerie, or romantic images of pre-modern medicine, alpine convalescence, and modernist literature.
