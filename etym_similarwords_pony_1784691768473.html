• Horse: Similar because the pony is a horse, sharing anatomy, nature, and husbandry; differs because a horse conventionally stands over about 14 hands, while pony names a small breed type — the difference is breed and stature, not age or kind.
  • Foal: Similar because both are small equines; differs because a foal is a young horse of any breed and any eventual height, while a pony may be old or young — its smallness is a breed trait, not an age.
  • Colt / Filly: Similar because both are young equines; differs because these name a young male / female horse respectively, again by age and sex, while pony names a small breed regardless of age or sex.
  • Nag: Similar because both denote a horse in everyday speech; differs because nag is colloquial and faintly disparaging of an old, worn, or inferior horse, while pony is a neutral, affectionate term for a small but useful one.
  • Steed: Similar because both denote a ridden mount; differs because steed is archaic-high style for a grand, spirited, noble horse, while pony is the humble, workaday pack-animal of practical travel.
  • Mount: Similar because both can denote a horse one rides; differs because mount is a general, functional word for any ridden animal or conveyance, with no suggestion of smallness, while pony specifies the small breed.
  • Palfrey: Similar because both are types of riding horse, typically docile and smooth-gaited; differs because palfrey is an archaic word for an elegant saddle-horse, while pony is the modern, humble small breed.
  • Hack: Similar because both denote a serviceable riding horse; differs because hack is a horse for ordinary riding or hire, of any size, while pony is specifically the small hardy breed.
  • Bronco / Mustang: Similar because both are smallish, hardy Western horses of the American range; differs because these are semi-feral North American horses, while pony is the general term for a small breed of horse.
