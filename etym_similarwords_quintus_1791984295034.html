• Quintus Tullius Cicero: Similar because it is the full, unambiguous form of the name in the source; differs because the nomen and cognomen identify the individual and distinguish him from every other Quintus in Roman history.
  • Marcus Tullius Cicero: Similar because he is the brother named in the same sentence and shares the family names; differs because Marcus is the elder, the orator and the subject of the biography, while Quintus is the companion.
  • Quinctus: Similar because it is the older Latin spelling of the same praenomen, with the nasal still pronounced; differs because it belongs to archaic inscriptions and the calendar (Quinctilis), not to the classical name of Cicero's brother.
  • quīntus: Similar because it is the Latin ordinal “fifth” from which the name derives; differs because it is a common adjective that can qualify any noun, while Quintus is a fixed personal name.
  • quint: Similar because it descends from the same ordinal and denotes a set of five, especially five cards in sequence in piquet; differs because it is an English common noun of the card table, not a name.
  • quintet: Similar because it also means a group of five and comes from the same Latin root; differs because it is a musical collective noun borrowed through Italian, with no personal reference.
  • Quinta: Similar because it is the feminine counterpart of the praenomen; differs in gender and in modern use, where it is a given name rather than a Roman clan designation.
  • Quentin: Similar because it derives from Quintinus, a diminutive of Quintus, and preserves the “fifth” root; differs because it is a saint's name transmitted through French, with a different form and a Christian rather than Republican history.
  • Sextus: Similar because it is another ordinal praenomen of the same archaic group; differs because it means “sixth” and was far commoner in a few noble families than in the population at large.
  • Decimus: Similar because it belongs to the same numerical series and was borne by prominent late-Republican senators; differs because it means “tenth” and is even rarer as a modern name.
