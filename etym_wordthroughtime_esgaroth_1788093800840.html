- 1937 – first published in The Hobbit as the Elvish name of the Lake-town on the Long Lake; the name sounds archaic and magical beside the plain English “Lake-town”.
- 1937–1966 – within the book's own story the name passes from thriving trading town to dragon-burnt ruin to rebuilt prosperity; the emotional register moves from mercantile comfort to catastrophe to renewal.
- 1977 – the Rankin–Bass animated film gives Esgaroth its first mass visual form, and the name enters general Tolkien fandom.
- 2012–2014 – Peter Jackson's film trilogy rebuilds Esgaroth as a vast, crowded stilt-metropolis of bridges, cranes and chimneys; popular perception inflates the town from village scale to city scale.
- Today – Esgaroth is shorthand in fandom for the Lake-town episode of The Hobbit and for the archetype of the fantasy lake-city built on piles.
