PIE *bʰel- "to sound, roar" (expressive/imitative root; c. 4500–3000 BCE) — a voiced breathy bilabial bʰ + a lateral liquid l capture a deep, resonant, booming blast → Proto-Germanic *bellaną "to roar, bark, sound" (c. 500 BCE–200 CE) → Old English bellan "to roar, cry out" (strong verb, 8th c. CE); beside it bylgan / bylgian "to bellow, roar" (weak -j- formation) → Middle English belwen / belgen "to roar" (12th–14th c.) → English bellow (v.) "to roar like a bull" (early 14th c.) → Modern English bellowed (weak past tense in -ed; of animals at first, of humans since c. 1600) Notes: the root also produced Old English belle "a bell" (the sounding instrument, named for the noise it makes), Old Norse belja "to roar" and bjalla "bell", Middle High German bellen "to bark" (modern German bellen), and Dutch blaffen "to bark". The parallel Germanic formation *belganą "to swell, be angry" (Old English belgan "to be angry") colours the word with fury. In the Baltic branch, Lithuanian balsas and Latvian balss, "voice", preserve the root's oldest meaning, "sonorous sound". No Slavic cognate is securely established, and no Indo-Aryan reflex reached Sanskrit, Hindi, or Odia; the root is expressive/imitative, so a precise PIE reconstruction is approximate.
