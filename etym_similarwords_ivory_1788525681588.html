• alabaster: Similar because both denote fine, smooth, translucent white materials renowned for exquisite decorative carving and poetic skin metaphors; differs because alabaster is a mineral (a fine-grained form of gypsum or calcite) that is cool, fragile, and non-organic, lacking the dentine Schreger grain of ivory.
  • porcelain: Similar because both present a refined, lustrous, off-white surface associated with luxury decorative arts; differs because porcelain is a vitrified ceramic produced by high-temperature kiln firing of kaolin clay.
  • marble: Similar because both are classical white sculpting media representing monumental permanence and artistic beauty; differs because marble is a cold, heavy metamorphic limestone rock with crystalline grain and natural veining.
  • pearl: Similar because both are precious organic luxury substances derived from animals exhibiting lustrous creamy whiteness; differs because pearl is formed from nacre inside mollusc shells and displays distinctive iridescent optical sheen.
  • bone: Similar because both are vertebrate skeletal tissues composed of calcium phosphate and collagen; differs because bone is far more porous, brittle, coarse-grained, prone to discoloration, and devoid of ivory's dense microstructural uniformity and luxury prestige.
  • chalk: Similar because both are white substances; differs because chalk is an inexpensive, soft, powdery sedimentary rock composed of microscopic calcium carbonate shells.
  • cream: Similar because both describe a soft, warm off-white colour with yellowish undertones; differs because cream is a daily dairy substance and informal colour descriptor lacking any precious material or luxury connotation.
  • ivory-towered: Similar because it stems directly from the metaphorical root; differs by functioning strictly as an adjectival critique of academic detachment and impractical intellectual seclusion.
