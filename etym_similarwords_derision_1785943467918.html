• Ridicule: Similar because both denote mockery of someone or something; differs because ridicule is the general act of making fun (often by parody or satire), while derision emphasizes bitter, contemptuous, hostile laughter intended to demean.
  • Mockery: Similar because both denote treating someone as an object of fun or contempt; differs because mockery typically works through imitation, mimicry, or absurd representation, while derision focuses on the scornful, descending laughter itself.
  • Scorn: Similar because both denote strong contempt; differs because scorn is a general attitude of disdain that may be silent or internal, while derision is that contempt made visible and audible in mocking laughter or jeering.
  • Contempt: Similar because both denote a feeling of superiority over someone or something unworthy; differs because contempt is the static emotion, while derision is the active, vocal, mocking expression of it.
  • Satire: Similar because both can expose faults and humiliate their targets; differs because satire is a considered literary or artistic genre using irony and exaggeration for (often constructive) social criticism, while derision is direct, destructive, personal mockery.
  • Jeering: Similar because both denote loud, contemptuous taunting of a target; differs because jeering is the specific noisy crowd behavior of shouted taunts, while derision is the broader attitude of contemptuous laughter that jeering expresses.
  • Sneering: Similar because both denote contemptuous belittling; differs because sneering is quiet, facial, and underhanded, while derision is open, audible laughter.
  • Disdain: Similar because both denote a feeling that something is beneath one's notice or respect; differs because disdain is a cool, haughty dismissiveness, while derision is heated, mocking, and publicly performative.
  • Taunting: Similar because both involve provoking or humiliating a target with words; differs because taunting is targeted provocation to anger or fear, while derision is mocking laughter expressing contempt.
  • Risible mockery / Lampoon: Similar because both belong to the mockery family and involve making someone look foolish; differs because a lampoon is a written or graphic attack (a genre), while derision is the immediate vocal contemptuous response.
