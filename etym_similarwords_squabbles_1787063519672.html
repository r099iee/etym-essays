• Quarrels: Similar because both describe angry disputes; differs because quarrels is broader and can be serious and long-lasting, while squabbles are petty, trivial, and noisy.
  • Arguments: Similar because both describe verbal disagreements; differs because arguments may involve reasoned debate over substantial matters, while squabbles are noisy, emotional, and inconsequential.
  • Disputes: Similar because both describe disagreements; differs because dispute is formal and can concern weighty legal or commercial matters, while squabble is informal and petty.
  • Wrangles: Similar because both describe noisy disputes; differs because wrangle suggests prolonged, contentious argument with some substance, while squabble is childish and trivial.
  • Spats: Similar because both describe brief petty quarrels; differs because spat is shorter, often American, and suggests a one-off flare-up, while squabble suggests repeated bickering.
  • Tiffs: Similar because both describe minor quarrels; differs because tiff is even slighter and usually between friends or lovers, while squabbles can be public and recurring.
  • Bickerings: Similar because both describe petty, repeated arguing; differs because bickering names the continuous process of small sniping, while squabbles are discrete, noisy episodes.
  • Feuds: Similar because both describe conflict; differs because feuds are long-lasting, serious, often violent vendettas, while squabbles are short and trivial.
  • Altercations: Similar because both describe noisy disputes; differs because altercation is a formal word and may involve physical confrontation, while squabble is informal and purely verbal.
  • Clashes: Similar because both describe conflict; differs because clash suggests force or a fundamental opposition of interests, while squabble concerns trivia and dignity.
  • Rows: Similar because both describe noisy quarrels; differs because row (British) is louder and more serious, an actual shouting-match, while squabble is more petty and faintly comic.
