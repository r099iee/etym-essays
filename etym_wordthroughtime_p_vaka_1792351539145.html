- Vedic Samhitā Era (c. 1500–1000 BCE) — Used fluidly as a radiant adjective ("cleansing, bright, clear") applied to Agni, Vāyu, the storm Maruts, and sacred cleansing waters in liturgical hymns.
- Brāhmaṇa & Aranyaka Ritualism (c. 1000–600 BCE) — Systematized into a distinct theological entity, categorized alongside Pāvamāna and Śuci as one of the three primary fires required for public Śrauta rituals.
- Epic & Classical Kāvya Period (c. 500 BCE–800 CE) — Mythologized in the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa as a son of Agni and Svāhā, while serving as a standard poetic metonym for fierce, purifying fire.
- Classical Sāstric & Medical Age (c. 200–1200 CE) — Adapted into technical Ayurvedic prose to denote internal metabolic heat (jāṭharāgni) and into astronomical chronograms as the numerical symbol for the number 3.
- Modern Philological & Religious Era (19th c.–Present) — Analyzed in comparative Indo-European studies as an exemplar of archaic laryngeal root reflexes, while remaining an active sacred name in contemporary Hindu domestic rituals.
