• Sniffling: Similar because both mean audibly drawing up nasal mucus while upset or ill; differs because sniffling is neutral and clinical, while snivelling adds tearful weakness and contempt.
  • Blubbering: Similar because both describe noisy tearful weeping; differs because blubbering is loud and uncontrolled, all sobs, while snivelling is thin, sniffly, and wheedling.
  • Whimpering: Similar because both are small weak cries of distress; differs because whimpering is the sound of a hurt puppy or frightened child, while snivelling stresses the wet nose and self-pity.
  • Whining: Similar because both complain in an irritating high tone; differs because whining is purely vocal and peevish, while snivelling adds tears, mucus, and abjectness.
  • Snuffling: Similar because both involve noisy nasal breathing; differs because snuffling can be cheerful, as of dogs or laughter, while snivelling is always miserable or contemptible.
  • Blubbing: Similar because both are British nursery words for crying; differs because blubbing is frank bawling, while snivelling is the sneakier drip-and-sniff that adults find harder to forgive.
  • Snarling: Similar because in the source sentence the two are twins of Gollum's confession; differs because snarling bares teeth in aggression, while snivelling sniffs back tears in submission.
  • Mewling: Similar because both are feeble pitiful cries, Shakespeare's mewling babe grown older; differs because mewling is kitten-weak and helpless, while snivelling carries a whiff of manipulative self-pity.
  • Canting: Similar because the snivelling hypocrite of satire is a canter; differs because canting is insincere pious talk itself, while snivelling is the tearful delivery that may or may not accompany it.
