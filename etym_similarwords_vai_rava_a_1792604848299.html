• Kubera: Similar because both refer to the identical god of wealth, king of the Yakṣas, and regent of the North; differs because Kubera is his primary mythological personal name (traditionally derived from roots meaning "deformed" or "slow-moving" due to his iconographic limp or corpulent body), whereas Vaiśravaṇa is his prestigious, honorable patronymic lineage title.
  • Dhanada: Similar because both denote the divine sovereign of riches; differs because Dhanada is a descriptive functional compound meaning literally "the giver or dispenser of wealth" (from dhana "wealth" + da "giving"), focusing on his munificent role toward devotees, while Vaiśravaṇa highlights his noble ancestry.
  • Yakṣarāja: Similar because both describe his sovereignty over the supernatural realm; differs because Yakṣarāja is an administrative title meaning "king of the Yakṣas," emphasizing his political dominion over subterranean spirits, whereas Vaiśravaṇa is his personal patronymic name.
  • Lokapāla: Similar because both denote his cosmic rank in governing the universe; differs because Lokapāla is a general functional office meaning "world-guardian" shared by the four or eight deities of the cardinal directions, whereas Vaiśravaṇa specifies the unique guardian of the northern quadrant.
  • Plutus: Similar because both are ancient classical deities presiding over vast material riches and prosperity; differs because Plutus is the Greek god of wealth (often depicted as a blind boy distributing riches randomly and foolishly), whereas Vaiśravaṇa is a dignified cosmic world-guardian and enlightened Buddhist protector.
  • Mammon: Similar because both are cultural personifications of boundless material wealth and gold; differs because Mammon carries a deeply pejorative, sinful connotation in Christian scripture of idolatrous greed and spiritual corruption, whereas Vaiśravaṇa is an auspicious, revered deity associated with righteous generosity.
  • Croesus: Similar because both serve in literature as the supreme hyperbolic benchmark of astronomical wealth; differs because Croesus was a real historical mortal king of Lydia whose vast gold reserves ended in tragic defeat and capture by Cyrus the Great, whereas Vaiśravaṇa is an immortal divinity.
  • Midas: Similar because both names are synonymous with the accumulation of massive gold reserves; differs because Midas represents the cautionary myth of tragic, ruinous greed through his "golden touch," while Vaiśravaṇa represents divine stewardship and righteous patronage.
  • magnate: Similar because both denote figures commanding colossal financial fortunes; differs because magnate is a modern corporate and industrial term for a wealthy mortal capitalist, entirely stripped of mythological, cosmological, and spiritual dimensions.
  • plutocrat: Similar because both characterize entities possessing overwhelming wealth; differs because plutocrat carries critical political connotations of oligarchic power and governance by the wealthy elite, whereas Vaiśravaṇa is a sacred divine figure.
  • nabob: Similar because both designate individuals of extravagant oriental opulence; differs because nabob (from Urdu/Arabic nawāb) was used pejoratively in 18th-century Britain for East India Company officials who returned with corrupt fortunes, whereas Vaiśravaṇa is a venerated epic deity.
  • Dhanapati: Similar because both celebrate his lordship over riches; differs because Dhanapati literally means "master or lord of wealth," serving as a descriptive compound, while Vaiśravaṇa establishes his paternal descent from sage Viśravas.
