Linear derivation chain for EMULATION (single root word): 1. PIE *h2eym- / *aim- "to copy, imitate" (reconstructed; the base also underlies Latin imago "image" and imitari "to imitate"). Stage debated: the direct Italic reflex is generally given as *aimelos, but some scholars prefer a link to a "going toward" root (*h2ey-), and no cognate outside Italic is certain. c. 4000-3000 BCE. 2. Proto-Italic *aimelos "rivaling, striving" (reconstructed intermediate; encodes the competitive-copy idea). 3. Latin aemulus "striving, rivaling; (as noun) a rival, competitor" (attested from the 2nd century BCE, e.g., in Plautus). 4. Latin aemulari "to rival, strive to excel, emulate" (deponent verb built on aemulus). 5. Latin aemulatio, accusative aemulationem "emulation, rivalry pursued by imitation" (noun of action; classical, 1st century BCE onward, e.g., Cicero). 6. Old French / Middle French emulation "emulation" (borrowed from Latin aemulatio in the medieval period). 7. Middle English emulacioun "emulation, rivalry" (first attested c. 1400, in the rivalry sense). 8. Modern English EMULATION "effort to equal or surpass by imitation" (the present root word; also the computing sense "one system reproducing another", attested from the mid-20th century). Debate note: the PIE root is the weakest link. The traditional OED/Etymonline chain connects aemulus to the same root that gives imago and imitari ("to copy"), but no secure IE cognate outside Italic survives; de Vaan and others treat *aim- as possible but not demonstrated. All stages from Latin aemulus onward are securely attested.
