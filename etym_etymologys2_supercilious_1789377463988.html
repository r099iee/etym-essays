PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*(s)kew- / *kel-
"to cover, conceal; covering, eyelid"
Note: Base root yielding Latin celāre ('to hide') and cilium ('eyelid, hair covering the eye').
↓
Classical Latin (Compound Formation) (c. 1st c. BCE)
supercilium
"eyebrow (literally: that which is above the eyelid); haughty look, pride"
Note: Formed from super ('above, over') + cilium ('lower eyelid/lash'). Metaphorized into arrogance because Romans raised eyebrows in disdain.
↓
Classical / Imperial Latin (c. 1st–2nd c. CE)
superciliōsus
"haughty, arrogant, disdainful, censorious, raising the eyebrows"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1520–1540)
supercilious
"haughtily contemptuous, arrogant, disdainful in demeanor"
↓
Modern English (present)
SUPERCILIOUS
