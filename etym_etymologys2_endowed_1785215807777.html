PIE *deh₃- "to give" (reconstructed) → Latin dō, dare "to give"; dōs (genitive dōtis) "dowry, marriage portion, gift" (Classical Latin) → Latin dōtāre "to provide with a dowry or portion, to endow" (Classical Latin) → Old French douer "to provide with a dowry or portion" (from Latin dōtāre) → Old French / Anglo-Norman endouer / endower (en- "in, into" + douer) "to endow, provide with a portion" (12th–13th century) → Middle English endowen "to give a portion or dowry to; to provide with a settled income" (14th century) → Modern English endow "to provide with a permanent fund or income; to furnish with a quality" → endowed (past participle, 15th century) Note on debate: English give is NOT from this root; it descends from PIE *gʰeh₁bʰ- "to give/take" (cf. Latin habeō "to have", Old Irish gaibim "I take"), so no stage of the present verb give belongs in this chain. The chain above is the Latin-Italic line that actually produced endow and endowed. Zero guesswork: all Romance-to-English stages are attested; only the PIE root is reconstructed and flagged as such.
