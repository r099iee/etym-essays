• Desk: Similar because both refer to a piece of furniture with a flat writing surface used for reading, writing, and administrative work; differs because a desk is the broad, generic term for any writing table, whereas a bureau historically refers to a specialized writing desk featuring a slant-top or roll-top lid concealing pigeonholes and drawers, or (in American English) a chest of drawers.
  • Escritoire: Similar because both designate elegant writing desks fitted with drawers and compartments; differs because an escritoire (or secretaire) is a light, upright, and decorative piece of furniture often with a drop-down hinged writing flap, whereas a bureau is generally a heavier, more substantial chest-like piece of cabinetry.
  • Agency: Similar because both refer to an administrative organization or governmental department executing specific duties; differs because agency emphasizes the delegated legal power to act on behalf of another (the principal), whereas bureau evokes an established institutional administrative office or division (e.g., the Federal Bureau of Investigation).
  • Department: Similar because both denote specialized operational branches of an executive government or business; differs because a department is usually a primary, high-level constitutional division (e.g., Department of State), within which individual specialized bureaus operate as subordinate directorates.
  • Dresser: Similar because in North American English, both refer to bedroom furniture for storing clothes; differs because a dresser (short for dressing table) frequently includes an attached mirror for grooming, whereas a bureau is a tall or wide chest of drawers.
  • Chiffonier: Similar because both are historic wooden storage cabinets fitted with drawers; differs because a chiffonier is a tall, narrow chest of drawers originally intended for storing feminine needlework scraps and odds and ends, lacking a writing flap.
