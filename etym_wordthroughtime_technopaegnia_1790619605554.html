- c.300 BCE Alexandrian invention as learned play - Simias and contemporaries admired for uniting craft and game in book-rolls; perception is elite delight in novelty
- Late antiquity preservation as marginal marvel - copied with Bucolics and Anthology as curiosities beside pastoral; connotation narrows to clever trifle, admired but sidelined
- Renaissance–Baroque imitation as devotion - humanists and poets like Fortunatus heirs, Herbert and Quarles revive altars and wings for sacred display; emotional register becomes pious wonder
- Modern recovery as media theory - from 19th-century philology to Apollinaire to digital concrete poetry, revalued as founding moment of visual poetics and book-conscious literature; now seen as serious evidence for ancient reading practices
