- Old English (8th–11th c.): Attested as *hulu* (noun meaning "husk, shell, pod, covering") from the Germanic root for concealing/covering; the Yorkshire river name *Hul* exists independently from an ancient Celtic hydronym.
- 13th–14th Century: Edward I charters *King's Town upon Hull* (1299); simultaneously, Middle English nautical speech transfers *hulle* to mean the main body or outer timber casing of a sailing vessel.
- 16th–17th Century: Military verb use develops ("to hull a ship" with cannon fire); Defoe establishes Hull as the departure port for Robinson Crusoe's ill-fated voyage in 1719.
- 19th–20th Century: Industrialization transforms Hull into Britain's third-largest port and premier deep-sea fishing haven, while *hull* solidifies in naval architecture and engineering.
- 21st Century: Kingston upon Hull is celebrated as UK City of Culture (2017), retaining its rich maritime heritage alongside standard global nautical and botanical usages.
