• Aphorisms: Similar because both represent compact, memorable literary formulations; differs because an aphorism seeks universal philosophical, ethical, or psychological truth without needing comedy, whereas an epigram prioritizes paradoxical wit, satirical bite, wordplay, and a sharp punchline.
  • Witticisms: Similar because both involve clever, humorous verbal remarks; differs because a witticism can be an offhand, spontaneous joke or clever observation lacking lasting literary structure, whereas an epigram is an exquisitely structured, polished work of verbal craftsmanship.
  • Quips: Similar because both are brief, clever remarks; differs because a quip is typically a brief, casual, and humorous comeback uttered on the spur of the moment, whereas an epigram has formal literary dignity and deliberate philosophical or satirical architecture.
  • Bon mots: Similar because both represent clever, sophisticated sayings; differs because a bon mot (French for "good word") is a witty, sparkling retort celebrated primarily in oral salon conversation, whereas an epigram has deep roots as a written literary or poetic form.
  • Repartees: Similar because both involve quick, razor-sharp wit; differs because repartee designates the rapid, back-and-forth exchange of clever retorts between conversationalists, whereas an epigram is a self-contained, standalone statement or couplet.
  • Maxims: Similar because both are concise statements; differs because a maxim is an earnest rule for practical or moral conduct ("better safe than sorry"), whereas an epigram frequently mocks conventional moralizing through satirical cynicism.
  • Pasquinades: Similar because both involve anonymous or satirical ridicule directed at public figures; differs because a pasquinade is a public lampoon or topical satire originally posted anonymously in public places (like the statue of Pasquino in Rome), whereas an epigram is a recognized classical literary genre.
