Stage 1 — PIE *treb- "to build, settle, dwell; a settlement or dwelling-place" (the base proposed for Latin tribus; cognates include Celtic Old Irish treb "building, farmstead", Welsh tref "town", Germanic *þurpa- "village" giving English thorp, and Baltic Lithuanian trobà "house, farmstead". DEBATED: an ancient folk-tradition, still repeated by Roman antiquarians, derived tribus from *tri- "three" + *bhu- "to be" as "one of the three parts" of Romulus' tribes; modern comparative scholarship prefers the settlement-district sense, though the deeper history is not entirely certain). Stage 2 — Latin tribus, tribūs "a tribe; one of the ancient territorial divisions of the Roman people; a district" (attested from the early Republic; the traditional threefold division of the Roman people). Stage 3 — Latin tribuere "to assign, allot, apportion, give" — originally to divide or allocate among the tribes (classical; Caesar, Cicero). Stage 4 — Latin retribuere "to give back, repay, recompense, requite" — re- "back" + tribuere (attested in classical Latin and prominent in Christian Latin). Stage 5 — Latin retribūtiō, retribūtiōnis "repayment, recompense, requital" — abstract noun in -tiō formed from the past participle retribūtus "given back" (Classical to Late Latin). Stage 6 — Old French retribution "repayment, requital" (13th century; also the verb retribuer). Stage 7 — Middle English retribucioun "recompense, repayment" (c. 1385, Chaucer) → Modern English retribution "deserved punishment". FINAL NODE: RETRIBUTION.
