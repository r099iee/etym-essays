- [Late Latin c. 400–600 CE] - [Late antique vessels: Evolved from 'flasco' (accusative 'flasconem'), a bottle encased in protective wickerwork for carrying wine.]
- [Old French c. 1100–1300] - [Feudal wine decanting: Adopted as 'flacon' and 'flascon', large wine bottles or carafes serving noble banquets.]
- [Middle English c. 1380–1450] - [Table pitcher: Borrowed as 'flagon' / 'flagoun', establishing itself as a substantial serving pitcher for taverns and festive tables.]
- [16th–18th c.] - [Ecclesiastical & pewter pinnacle: Reached widespread domestic and religious prominence with pewter tankard-lidded communion flagons mandated by church canons.]
- [19th–21st c.] - [Archaic & fantasy revelry: Retained primarily as an evocative literary and historical term for hearty medieval hospitality, tavern ale, and epic banquets.]
