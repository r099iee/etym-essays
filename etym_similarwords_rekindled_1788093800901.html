• Reignited: Similar because both mean “set alight again” literally and figuratively; differs because reignite (20th c., from ignite) suggests a sudden spark or explosion of energy, while rekindled implies slow, steady re-burning of embers.
  • Relit: Similar because both mean “lit again”; differs because relit is concrete and neutral (lamps, fires), rarely figurative, and carries no emotional warmth.
  • Revived: Similar because both mean “brought back to life or activity”; differs because revive covers persons, economies and fashions and is not tied to fire imagery.
  • Resurrected: Similar because both describe bringing something dead back; differs because resurrected is far stronger and religiously loaded — a dead thing, not a merely dormant one.
  • Reawakened: Similar because both describe stirring something dormant; differs because reawakened uses the sleep-and-consciousness metaphor, while rekindled uses the fire metaphor.
  • Reanimated: Similar because both mean “given life again”; differs because reanimate is clinical or fantastic (reanimated corpses), not used of feelings.
  • Rejuvenated: Similar because both suggest restored vitality; differs because rejuvenate implies making young again and applies to skin, economies and careers, while rekindle applies to fires, passions and quarrels.
  • Stoked / fanned: Similar because both are fire metaphors for intensifying feeling; differ because stoked and fanned mean “made stronger” (usually of something already burning), while rekindled means “lit again after going out”.
  • Renewed: Similar because both mean “made new again”; differs because renewed is far broader (leases, passports, vows) and lacks the warmth of the fire image.
  • Reopened: Similar because both can apply to disputes and debates; differs because reopen is procedural and door-like (reopened the case), with no emotional heat.
