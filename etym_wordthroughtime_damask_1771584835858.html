- [14th-century Oriental import] - Damask names the rich figured silk brought from Damascus - a luxury of princes, its patterns woven in, not printed.
- [16th-18th century expansion] - The name spreads to linen table damask, damask roses and Damascus steel, so that the word comes to denote a whole family of fine patterned things.
- [19th-century industrial democratization] - Weaving machinery makes damask affordable; the word now names any reversible figured fabric for table and home, elegant but no longer princely.
- [Contemporary register] - A textile term (often jacquard damask) with a literary halo of oriental luxury; also 'damask rose' in perfume and gardening.
