• Reverie: Similar because it is a state of mental absorption or daydreaming; differs because it is pleasant, passive, and entirely secular, while a trance is deeper, more involuntary, and can be uncanny or sacred.
  • Daydream: Similar because it is a state of fancy; differs because it is a conscious, wish-fulfilling fantasy that one directs, without the involuntary depth and detachment of a trance.
  • Meditation: Similar because it involves focused, absorbed awareness; differs because it is a deliberate, disciplined practice one performs, whereas a trance is undergone and involuntary.
  • Ecstasy: Similar because it is an intense, exalted mental state; differs because it emphasizes overwhelming joy or rapture, while a trance emphasizes absorption and detachment from the senses.
  • Swoon: Similar because in older English a trance could be a swoon or faint; differs because a swoon is a literal loss of consciousness from weakness, while a trance is a paradoxical state of deep wakefulness within absorption.
  • Stupor: Similar because both involve reduced responsiveness; differs because a stupor is pathological dullness and near-unconsciousness, lacking the vivid interior life of a trance.
  • Hypnosis: Similar because hypnotic trances are the modern artificial form of the state; differs because hypnosis names the induced condition and the technique, while trance names the experience itself and predates hypnotism by centuries.
  • Absorption: Similar because both mean deep engagement of the attention; differs because absorption is a milder, ordinary focus that does not suspend ordinary time the way a trance does.
