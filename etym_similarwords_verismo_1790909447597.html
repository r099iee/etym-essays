• Realism: Similar because both denote an artistic commitment to depicting life truthfully without romantic idealization; differs because 'realism' is a vast, international philosophical and artistic category spanning centuries, whereas 'verismo' specifically refers to the passionate, gritty Italian literary and operatic movement of the late 19th century.
  • Naturalism: Similar because both focus on the raw, deterministic struggles of lower-class life; differs because French and European 'naturalism' (e.g., Zola) emphasizes scientific detachment, biological determinism, and social heredity, whereas Italian 'verismo' is characterized by explosive emotional heat, Mediterranean passion, and tragic lyricism.
  • Neorealism: Similar because both are Italian artistic movements dedicated to the unvarnished portrayal of working-class struggle; differs because 'neorealism' specifically designates the post-World War II cinematic movement (Rossellini, De Sica, Visconti) utilizing non-professional actors and on-location shooting, whereas 'verismo' was rooted in 19th-century literature and opera.
  • Grittiness: Similar because both convey harsh, unvarnished reality; differs because 'grittiness' is a casual descriptive quality denoting dirt, violence, or roughness, lacking the formal aesthetic, musical, and literary traditions inherent to 'verismo'.
  • Romanticism (Antonym): Directly opposite because Romanticism idealizes the sublime, heroic individualism, supernatural folklore, and transcendent passion, whereas verismo insists on everyday reality and raw human flaws.
