Accreted is the past-participle and participial adjective of accrete, meaning grown together or built up bit by bit by external addition. In the source sentence it describes associations of narrowness and conservatism that have slowly crusted around the word parochial like limescale or coral — not part of its original meaning but deposited by decades of use. To strip them back is to reverse accretion, revealing the cleaner surface beneath. The metaphor is geological and biological at once, and it governs the word’s whole range.

Literally, accretion is growth by adhesion from without, as opposed to growth from within. Planets accrete from dust, pearls accrete nacre, stalactites accrete calcite, deltas accrete silt, glaciers accrete snow, cities accrete suburbs, savings accrete interest. Accreted is the state that results: accreted matter, accreted rock (in geology, terranes sutured to continents), accreted ice, accreted layers. The process is slow, incremental and largely impersonal; no single deposit matters, the mass does. Sciences use it precisely: astronomy for planetesimals, geology for continental growth and concretions, biology for shells and calculi, forestry for annual rings, finance for discount accretion where value builds to par.

  • Physical-natural: of particles, sediments, minerals or tissue fused into a larger mass; e.g. accreted dust, accreted silt, accreted bone. Neutral, technical.
  • Geological-tectonic: of terranes or strata added to a continent or formation; e.g. accreted terrane, accreted wedge. Specialist, mapping deep time.
  • Astronomical-cosmological: of matter gathered by gravity; e.g. accreted gas envelope, accreted disc. Modern, post-18th-century.
  • Figurative-cultural (the source sense): of meanings, customs, reputations, powers or debts built layer upon layer; e.g. accreted associations, accreted traditions, accreted authority, accreted prejudices. Often faintly negative — clutter to be stripped — but can be neutral or admiring (accreted wisdom).
  • Financial-legal: of value added over time, as accreted interest or accreted discount; parallel to accrued but with the flavour of organic piling.

Grammar fixes nuance. As verb form (“layers have accreted”), it stresses process; as adjective (“accreted meanings”), it stresses resultant crust. It differs from neighbours by mechanism: accumulated merely piled, amassed deliberately gathered, aggregated collected into a whole, accreted grown together from outside until inseparable. Register is formal and literary outside science; everyday speech prefers built up. Collocations favour mass nouns — matter, layers, deposits, growth, meanings, habits — and adverbs slowly, gradually, steadily. The noun accretion and adjective accretionary complete the family; the alternative verb accresce (1630s) is obsolete, and accrue is the French-routed cousin for money and benefits.
