- c. 1750 BCE — the name belongs to a reigning king and appears in the prologue and epilogue of his stele as a claim to justice.
- 12th c. BCE — the stele is carried to Susa as war booty; the name drops out of memory outside cuneiform scholarship.
- 1901–02 — the stele is excavated and translated; “Hammurabi” enters European languages as the type of the ancient lawgiver.
- Early 20th c. — biblical scholars debate the relation between the code and Mosaic law, and the name becomes shorthand for primitive retribution.
- Late 20th–21st c. — a fixture of legal history and world-history curricula, used attributively (“Hammurabi-like”) and in popular culture as an emblem of harsh justice.
