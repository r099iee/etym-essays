• Pico: Similar because both name the same philosopher; differs because Pico is the family name alone, while Mirandola adds the lordship and place-title that complete the formal style della Mirandola.
  • Renaissance humanist: Similar because it describes Pico's intellectual type; differs because it is a broad generic label, while naming Mirandola pinpoints one specific figure and his syncretic ambition.
  • Oration on the Dignity of Man: Similar because both are attached to Pico's fame; differs because it names his famous speech, not the person or the place-title itself.
  • Polymath: Similar because it denotes Pico's many-sided learning; differs because it is a general descriptor applicable to many figures, without the historical and dynastic specificity of the name.
  • Prodigy: Similar because it captures Pico's youthful, astonishing brilliance; differs because it emphasizes precocity of ability rather than the cultural institution of the name.
  • Ficino: Similar as another Florentine Platonist and Renaissance philosopher of Pater's world; differs because Marsilio Ficino is a distinct historical figure with a different program (Platonic theology), not a name-title of the same kind.
  • Mona Lisa: Similar as another Paterian icon of unique virtue in the same catalogue; differs because she is the artistic image, while Mirandola is the human-philosophical instance of the same principle.
  • Humanist: Similar in placing him within the humanist movement; differs because it lacks the toponymic, dynastic, and historical load of Mirandola.
