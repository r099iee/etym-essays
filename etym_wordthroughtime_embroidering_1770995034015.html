- Late Middle English 'Decorating cloth with needlework' — a costly, skilled craft of courts and fine households, where patterns were worked in silk and gold thread; an aristocratic, luxurious art with a prestige register.
- 16th–17th c. 'Figurative embellishment' — acquires a metaphorical sense of ornamenting speech or writing, giving rise to 'embroidering a story' — adding decorative, often fictitious detail to a narrative.
- 19th c. 'Domestic craft and commodity' — industrialization turns embroidery into a widespread, respectable women's craft and a marketable skill; the word becomes associated with patient, diligent, gentle household labor.
- Contemporary 'Delicate artistry and exaggeration' — still the physical sewing of decorative patterns, and metaphorically the embellishment or exaggeration of a tale; the physical sense retains a patient, meticulous register while the figurative one implies charming invention.
