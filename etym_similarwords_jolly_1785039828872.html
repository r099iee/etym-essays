• Merry: Similar because both mean high, festive spirits; differs because merry is an older native English word, more reserved and poetic, whereas jolly is robust and convivial.
  • Cheerful: Similar because both mean good humour; differs because cheerful is a steady, dispositional cheerfulness, while jolly is a festive, boisterous burst of merriment.
  • Happy: Similar because both mean a state of gladness; differs because happy is broad and general (and originally meant "lucky," from hap "chance"), lacking jolly's hearty, social, festive colour.
  • Jovial: Similar because both mean hearty good humour; differs because jovial suggests the genial good-nature supposedly born of the planet Jupiter, weightier and more dignified than jolly's light-hearted zest.
  • Gleeful: Similar because both mean gladness; differs because gleeful is a lighter, more childish delight, where jolly is large, smiling, and convivial.
  • Festive: Similar because both belong to celebration; differs because festive describes the occasion or atmosphere of a feast, while jolly describes the high-spirited mood itself.
  • Convivial: Similar because both mean sociable, hearty cheer; differs because convivial is a formal word focused on companionship and drink, while jolly is informal and purely about good spirits.
  • Exuberant / sprightly: Similar because both mean lively high spirits; differ because they stress energy and bounce, while jolly stresses warmth, mirth, and geniality.
