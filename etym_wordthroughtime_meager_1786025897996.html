- 14th–15th c. (Middle English) - Somatic emaciation & biological thinness: Denoted primarily humans, animals, or agricultural soil that was fleshless, gaunt, barren, or starved of vital nourishment.
- 16th–17th c. (Early Modern English) - Extension to material provisions & forces: Widened from bodily flesh to describe inadequate stipends, meager diets, and undersized military garrisons.
- 18th–19th c. (Enlightenment to Victorian Era) - Metaphorical deficiency in intellect & prose: Applied across literary and philosophical criticism to designate dry writing styles, weak arguments, and meager historical evidence.
- 20th–21st c. (Contemporary English) - General socio-economic deficit: Standard term for inadequate wages, underfunded social programs, meager pensions, and threadbare institutional safety nets.
