PIE *ḱom- "with, beside, near" (preposition/prefix) + root *mei-/*moi- "to change, exchange" (with nominal suffix *-no-) → [reconstructed] *ḱom-moini- "held in common, shared in exchange" (PIE stage, no date; a scholarly reconstruction) → Proto-Italic *komoinis "common, shared" (reconstructed, early 1st millennium BCE) → Latin commūnis "common, public, shared by all" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE; the standing opposite of privātus) → Latin commūnicāre "to make common, to share, to impart" (2nd c. BCE, from commūnis; gives communicate) → Latin commūniō, -ōnis "mutual participation, sharing, partnership, fellowship" (classical, 1st c. BCE; noun formed on the stem of commūnis) → Ecclesiastical Latin commūniō "fellowship in the faith; participation in the Eucharist" (2nd–4th c. AD; renders Greek koinōnia) → Old French communion / comunion "fellowship; the sacrament" (12th c.) → Middle English communioun "sharing; Holy Communion" (c. 1300) → Modern English COMMUNION (intimate sharing; spiritual fellowship; the Eucharist).

Debated / nuanced note: the PIE compound *ḱom-moini- is a reconstruction, not an attested form. The analysis of the second element as a derivative of the root *mei- "to exchange" is the standard scholarly view (cf. the OED s.v. common; de Vaan on commūnis), but older handbooks sometimes connected the element instead to a word for "one" or to mūnus "duty, service"; the Latin outcome commūnis and all later stages are certain. No letter labels are used here; every stage is a real attested or explicitly reconstructed form.
