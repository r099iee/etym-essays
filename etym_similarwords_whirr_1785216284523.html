• Buzz: Similar because both name a continuous low vibratory sound of rapid motion; differs because buzz is insect-like and tonal, often with a resonant quality, while whirr is the blur of rapid mechanical or wing movement.
  • Hum: Similar because both name a steady continuous low sound; differs because hum is more musical and regular (a machine idling, a tune under the breath), while whirr is quicker, more blurred, and tied to visible motion.
  • Drone: Similar because both are low continuous sounds; differs because drone is a single monotonous sustained note, often boring or menacing, whereas whirr has a rapid, busy, vibratory texture.
  • Rustle: Similar because both are soft sounds of movement; differs because rustle is the dry, intermittent sound of surfaces rubbing (leaves, paper, silk), while whirr is continuous and vibratory.
  • Thrum: Similar because both name a low, repeated, vibratory sound; differs because thrum suggests a plucked string or a slow continuous vibration, while whirr is rapid and blurred.
  • Purr: Similar because both are soft low continuous sounds; differs because purr is smooth, regular, and contented (a cat, an engine idling), while whirr is fast, busy, and mechanical.
  • Whistle: Similar because both are sounds made by air or rapid motion; differs because whistle is a sharp, high-pitched, piercing tone produced by air forced through a narrow opening, while whirr is a low, blurred buzz.
  • Whiz: Similar because both name the sound of something moving fast through the air; differs because whiz is a single brief high-pitched streak (a bullet, a ball), while whirr is a sustained continuous vibration.
  • Sizzle: Similar because both name a continuous rapid sound; differs because sizzle is specifically the hissing, spitting sound of hot fat or moisture on a hot surface, while whirr is dry mechanical vibration.
  • Murmur: Similar because both are soft continuous sounds; differs because murmur is a low, indistinct sound of voices or water, gentle and vocal, while whirr is percussive-mechanical and motion-driven.
  • Rattle: Similar because both name the sound of rapid repeated motion; differs because rattle is loud, dry, and discrete — a series of hard impacts — while whirr is a smooth continuous blur.
  • Clatter: Similar because both name sounds of rapid motion; differs because clatter is a noisy, jarring collision of hard objects, while whirr is soft, continuous, and blurred.
