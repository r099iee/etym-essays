Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*weyd-
"to see, perceive, know, view"
[Source of Sanskrit veda, Latin videre, English witness]
↓
Proto-Hellenic (c. 1500 BCE)
*wêdos
"visual shape, outward appearance, visible form"
[Nominal formation with digamma *w-]
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th–5th c. BCE)
εἶdos (eîdos)
"form, shape, figure, outward appearance, species"
[Core Greek philosophical term, central to Plato's Theory of Forms]
↓
Hellenistic Greek Diminutive (c. 3rd c. BCE)
εἰδύλλιον (eidúllion)
"little form, small picture, short descriptive pastoral poem"
[Applied in Alexandria to the bucolic verse of Theocritus of Syracuse]
↓
Classical Latin Borrowing (c. 1st c. BCE)
idyllium
"pastoral poem, short descriptive bucolic verse"
[Transliteration of Greek eidullion into Roman poetic terminology]
↓
French (c. 16th c.)
idylle
"short pastoral poem; tender, charming love romance"
[Adopted into English as idyll / idyl c. 1600]
↓
Late 18th c. English Derivation (c. 1790s)
idyll-ic
"pertaining to idylls; picturesque, charmingly pastoral"
[Formed in English via adjective suffix -ic, modelled on poetic terms]
↓
Modern English (present)
idyllic
"charmingly simple, serene, picturesque, peaceful"
