- 14th – 16th Century (Ming Dynasty): Established as a fortified naval base - Named 廈門 (Ē-mn̂g, "Gate of the Mansion") to defend the Fujian coast against wokou pirates.
- 17th – 18th Century: Base of Koxinga and entry of English tea trade - Served as the maritime stronghold of Ming loyalist Koxinga; English East India Company traders adopted the local Hokkien name Amoy.
- 19th Century (Victorian Era): One of the Five Treaty Ports of the British Empire - Highly famous in Victorian geography, tea trade, and fiction (e.g. Doyle's "Amoy River in Southern China").
- 20th – 21st Century: Modern Chinese metropolis of Xiamen - Recognized internationally under Pinyin as Xiamen, serving as a Special Economic Zone, while Amoy is preserved in historical, linguistic, and commercial brand contexts.
