- [14th c.] - [Middle English Anglo-Norman Influx] Borrowed into Middle English as clamour from Anglo-Norman, designating loud shouting, battlefield cries, and judicial outcries against grievances.
- [15th–16th c.] - [Acoustic & Mechanical Expansion] Broadened to describe the roaring din of crowds, musical instruments, church bells rung together, and industrial workshops.
- [17th–18th c.] - [Political Agitation & Public Opinion] Established in parliamentary and journalistic discourse to denote intense public agitation and collective demands for legal and political change ("public clamour").
- [19th–21st c.] - [Avian Flocks & Modern Polysemy] Applied extensively in natural history for bird colonies and wildlife cries, while retaining full force in modern social commentary and acoustic description.
