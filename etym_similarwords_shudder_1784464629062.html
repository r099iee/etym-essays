• Shiver: Similar because both name an involuntary trembling of the body from cold or fear; differs because a shiver is often slighter and more rapid and frequent with cold, while a shudder is a stronger, coarser, whole-body convulsion typically tied to horror or disgust — the dwarves shivered only after Thorin's shudder.
  • Tremble: Similar because both denote shaking of the body; differs because a tremble may be fine, sustained, and caused by weakness, age, or excitement, while a shudder is a short, violent, involuntary wave.
  • Quiver: Similar because both are small shaking movements; differs because a quiver is delicate and often slight or pleasurable, whereas a shudder is coarse and usually fearful or repellent.
  • Quake: Similar because both mean to shake violently; differs because a quake is stronger and more total, often of the ground or of great fear, while a shudder is a passing bodily convulsion.
  • Shake: Similar because both mean to move to and fro; differs because shake is the general word for any shaking, voluntary or involuntary, with none of shudder's involuntary dread.
  • Convulse: Similar because both denote violent involuntary bodily spasms; differs because convulse implies more violent and sometimes pathological muscle spasms, while a shudder is a single brief wave.
  • Spasm: Similar because both are sudden involuntary muscular contractions; differs because a spasm is local and may be painful (a muscle cramp), whereas a shudder runs through the whole body and is emotional in cause.
  • Twitch: Similar because both are sudden small movements; differs because a twitch is local (an eye, a lip) and near-mechanical, without shudder's whole-body dread.
  • Flinch: Similar because both are involuntary reactions to something frightening; differs because a flinch is a quick startle-recoil, usually from sudden threat or pain, shorter and more defensive than a full shudder.
  • Wince: Similar because both are involuntary reactions to unpleasantness; differs because a wince is a tightening of face or body in pain or embarrassment, with no whole-body shaking.
  • Frisson: Similar because both denote a passing thrill that runs through the body; differs because a frisson is often aesthetic or pleasurable (a frisson of delight), while a shudder is repellent or fearful.
  • Jolt: Similar because both denote a sudden abrupt movement; differs because a jolt is a single sharp jerk (often of a vehicle), while a shudder is a shaking wave, not one impact.
