- 1650s – “of true breed”: applied to horses and wine, meaning authentically characteristic of its stock; neutral, technical praise.
- Late 1600s – “full of flavour”: pungent, sharp, lively on the palate and, by extension, in prose; a compliment to vigour.
- 18th–19th c. – “racy of”: smacking of a place or period; learned and slightly archaic, used by essayists for local colour.
- Late 19th c. – euphemistic spice: “racy” begins to hint at impropriety while remaining deniable.
- 1901–mid 20th c. – sexual sense consolidates: racy postcards, racy novels, racy humour; mild titillation.
- Late 20th–21st c. – marketing label: “racy” promises adult content in reviews and blurbs, while the breed and flavour senses survive only in literary and wine writing.
