• Climbed: Similar because both mean moving upward over a surface; differs because climbing is neutral and may be easy or skilled, whereas clambering is always awkward, laborious, and improvised — climbing's unglamorous, four-limbed sibling.
  • Scrambled: Similar because both name rough, opportunistic upward movement; differs because scrambling is fast and animal-like over broken ground, while clambering is a heavier, slower, more deliberate labour using both hands and feet.
  • Scaled: Similar because both mean surmounting a steep surface; differs because scaling implies competence, purpose, and often equipment against a vertical face, with none of clambering's ungainly, scrabbling effort.
  • Ascended: Similar because both mean going upward; differs because ascending is formal, smooth, and dignified — the verb of thrones, stairs, and balloons — far removed from clambering's physical, slightly comic register.
  • Swarmed: Similar because both can describe many bodies moving up or over something at once; differs because swarming suggests a dense, insect-like mass moving fast and collectively, while clambering is individual, heavy, and slow.
  • Mounted: Similar because both mean getting up onto something; differs because mounting is a neat, deliberate action (onto a horse or a platform), while clambering is messy, effortful, and uncertain.
  • Clawed: Similar because both picture digging into a surface with the limbs; differs because clawing emphasises the hands and nails tearing at a hold, while clambering pictures the whole four-limbed body working its way up.
  • Hauled: Similar because both involve pulling one's own weight up with effort; differs because hauling emphasises the dragging, tugging force and can apply to objects, while clambering is specifically self-motion upward over obstacles.
  • Scrabbled: Similar because both are frantic, scrabbling movements over rough ground; differs because scrabbling is quicker, more panicked and horizontal (scratching at a surface), while clambering is the sustained vertical effort of the whole body.
  • Struggled: Similar because both name hard-won, effortful movement; differs because struggling is general — it covers any difficult exertion — while clambering names the specific act of hauling oneself up over rough surfaces.
