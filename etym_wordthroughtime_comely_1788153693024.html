- [Old English cymlic: "beautiful, splendid, lovely" of persons, things and virtues] - [Perception: a wholly admiring word for the radiant and the fine; no moral wrinkle yet]
- [Middle English: the sense divides into physical beauty (a comely lady) and seemly conduct (comely behaviour)] - [Perception: beauty and decency seen as companions — the comely is also the proper]
- [16th–18th c.: set phrase "comely and decent"; used of dress, manners, buildings, speech] - [Perception: the standard adjective for decency made attractive; a slightly conservative flavour]
- [19th c.: novelists (Austen, Scott, Eliot) use comely of wholesome, honest attractiveness, especially of country people] - [Perception: warm, homely virtue — beauty without vanity]
- [20th–21st c.: everyday speech largely drops comely; it survives in literary prose and formal phrases] - [Perception: pleasantly archaic; evokes a world in which looks and morals rhyme]
