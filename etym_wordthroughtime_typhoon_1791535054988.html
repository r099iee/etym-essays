- Late 16th c. – Exotic Eastern storm of the China Seas - Borrowed as *tuffon* or *touffon* from Portuguese navigators and Arab sailors to describe monstrous circular tempests encountered in the Indian and Pacific Oceans.
- 17th c. – Terrifying navigational hazard - Connoted catastrophic maritime destruction, shipwreck, and the terrifying cyclonic winds that shattered European trading armadas off the coast of Macau and Japan.
- Late 18th to Early 19th c. – Classical Greek orthographic reframing - Scribes and lexicographers respelled the term to *typhoon*, consciously evoking the mythic Greek storm monster Typhon and lending the word epic, sublime literary gravity.
- Mid-19th c. – Meteorological classification - Integrated into formal colonial meteorology (pioneered by Father Federico Faura in Manila) to classify Northwestern Pacific tropical cyclones systematically.
- 20th c. – Military and aeronautical namesake - Adopted for lethal high-performance war machinery (the Hawker Typhoon fighter, the Eurofighter Typhoon, the Soviet Typhoon-class nuclear submarine), symbolizing unstoppable destructive speed and power.
- Modern Era – Global climatological phenomenon - Evokes climate change urgency, rising sea surface temperatures, super-typhoon storm surges, and coastal resilience across East and Southeast Asia.
