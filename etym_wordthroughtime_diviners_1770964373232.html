- Middle English (14th c., from Latin "divinus", of the gods): "diviners" were those who discerned the will of the gods through signs and ritual — a sacred, culturally sanctioned and respected role in courts and communities.
- 16th–18th c.: the term widens to cover astrologers, soothsayers, dowsers, and lot-casters; its register becomes ambiguous — revered by the superstitious, condemned as fraud by the sceptical and the Church.
- 19th c.: Romantic and Victorian literature restores a mystical gravity to "diviners", and anthropology begins using the word respectfully of non-Western ritual specialists.
- Contemporary: formal, literary, or anthropological in tone; ordinary speech prefers "fortune-teller", "psychic", or "seer", leaving "diviner" to evoke ancient, ceremonial, and scholarly contexts.
