- 14th c. — the mason's tool: level names the plumb-and-bubble instrument, then the flatness it certifies.
- Medieval — roads and fields: level ground is workable, buildable ground; charters boast of level meadows as of good title.
- 16th c. — the verb rises: armies level walls and reformers would level ranks, flatness becoming action and then politics.
- 17th–18th c. — surveyors and seas: sea level fixes the great datum, and canals and turnpikes hunt the level line across counties.
- 19th c. — character and contest: level-headed men and level scores carry the word from ground into mind and game; Cobbett contrasts the level road with the prospecting hill.
- Today — tiers and screens: ground levels, sea levels, A-levels, and video-game levels stack the old flatness into storeys of life, while levelling up promises everyone a higher plane.
