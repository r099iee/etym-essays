- 14th to 18th Century - Originates as an established Dutch and Flemish patronymic in the Low Countries (*huysman*), designating a prosperous householder, estate steward, and celebrated lineage of Antwerp landscape painters.
- 1870s to Early 1880s - Enters Parisian literary history under Joris-Karl Huysmans as an unyielding practitioner of Zola's naturalist circle, depicting grim working-class malaise and bureaucratic drudgery.
- 1884 to Early 1890s - Achieves international fame and notoriety with *À rebours* (1884), hailed as the definitive "breviary of Decadence," fundamentally inspiring Oscar Wilde and establishing the supremacy of artifice over nature.
- 1890s to 1907 - Undergoes an astonishing public metamorphosis from the satanic investigation of *Là-bas* to devout, mystical Catholicism (*En route*, *La Cathédrale*), ending his life as a saintly Benedictine oblate.
- Contemporary Era - Revered across world literature as the master of sensory prose, a pioneering champion of Impressionist and Symbolist art, and the indispensable bridge linking naturalism, decadence, and modernism.
