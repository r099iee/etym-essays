- Vedic Ritual Era (c. 1500–900 BCE) — Originally denoted the physical surging, discharge, or streaming forth of sacrificial Soma juice, the galloping release of war steeds, or a flight of arrows shot from a bow.
- Upanisadic & Sāṃkhya Metaphysics (c. 800–300 BCE) — Conceptualized as the grand outward emission and evolutionary unfoldment of the material universe (prakṛti-sarga) from unmanifest potential.
- Epic Formalization (c. 300 BCE–200 CE) — Adopted in the Rāmāyaṇa as the universal title for its hundreds of narrative cantos, shifting the term into the premier vocabulary of epic architecture.
- Classical Mahākāvya Poetics (c. 400–1200 CE) — Codified in Sanskrit literary theory (Kāvyādarśa) as the definitive structural unit of court poetry, formalizing the rule that every sarga must conclude with a metrical change.
- Purāṇic Systematization (c. 400–1500 CE) — Enshrined as the first of the five essential pillars (pañcalakṣaṇa) of all Purāṇic literature, defining the cosmic Genesis of the universe.
- Modern Philology & World Literature (19th c.–Present) — Universally adopted in academic Indology and literary translation as the direct Sanskrit counterpart to the Homeric book and Dantean canto.
