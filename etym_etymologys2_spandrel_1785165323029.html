Chain for spandrel:

  • Proto-Indo-European *pet(h2)- "to spread, stretch out, open wide" (reconstructed; the root behind Greek petannymi "to unfold" and Latin pandere; the assignment of the English word to this root is traditional, not certain).
  • Latin pandere "to spread out, unfold, open" (attested from the 2nd century BCE).
  • Latin expandere "to spread out, extend" (ex- "out" + pandere; attested 1st century BCE–1st century CE).
  • Old French / Anglo-Norman espandre (later spandre) "to spread out, pour, scatter" (attested from the 12th century).
  • Anglo-Norman / Middle French espandreille, spandreille — a diminutive, "a little spreading piece" (14th century).
  • Middle English spandreil, spaundrel, spandrelle "the triangular space beside an arch" (first attested in English in the late 1400s).
  • Modern English spandrel (architecture, 16th century–present; the evolutionary-biology metaphor via Gould & Lewontin, 1979).

Note: The Oxford English Dictionary marks the origin as uncertain, deriving the word "perhaps" from Anglo-Norman espandre with a diminutive suffix; the PIE root shown here is the traditional scholarly account and should not be treated as certain.
