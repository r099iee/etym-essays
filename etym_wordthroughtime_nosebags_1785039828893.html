- 18th-19th centuries - nosebag, a plain compound of nose and bag, enters as the practical horse-keeper's term for a feed sack strapped over the muzzle.
- 19th-20th centuries - British informal English winks and extends the word to a human meal — 'time for the nosebag'.
- 20th-21st centuries - with the decline of working horses, the word becomes rural, nostalgic, and matter-of-fact; the comic human sense survives in British slang.
- Contemporary - nosebag anchors a homely picture of harnessed animals feeding, its very ordinariness reassuring after danger.
