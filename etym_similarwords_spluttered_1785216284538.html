• Stammered: Similar because both describe broken, non-fluent speech; differs because stammering is nervous hesitation and repetition in forming words, while spluttering is wet, explosive, spitting speech.
  • Stuttered: Similar because both describe disrupted speech; differs because stuttering is the mechanical repetition of initial sounds, whereas spluttering is a choked, spattering loss of vocal control.
  • Sputtered: Similar because "sputter" is the parent word and the two are near-synonyms for explosive, irregular noise; differs because "splutter" adds the wet "splash" quality (a blend of splash + sputter), making it messier and more informal than the drier "sputter".
  • Gabbled: Similar because both describe rapid, indistinct speech; differs because gabbling is fast, slurred chatter without the spitting or choking of a splutter.
  • Jabbered: Similar because both are noisy, confused speech; differs because jabbering is rapid, voluble nonsense talk, not the explosive spray of syllables a splutter is.
  • Choked: Similar because both describe speech blocked or broken by strong emotion; differs because choking is an obstruction of the voice, whereas spluttering is a loss of control that spits words out.
  • Gasped: Similar because both involve breath and strong emotion; differs because a gasp is a sharp intake of breath, while a splutter is an explosive outbreath with spittle.
  • Burbled: Similar because both are bubbling, fluid noises; differs because burbling is a gentle, continuous, voiced gurgle, while a splutter is sharp, explosive, and disordered.
  • Spat: Similar because both involve ejecting particles or spittle from the mouth; differs because "spat" is a single forceful ejection, while spluttering is a series of spitting fragments in broken speech.
  • Splashed: Similar because both are wet, scattering, explosive actions (and etymological kin); differs because splashing is the scattering of liquid, without the vocal or speech sense.
  • Spattered: Similar because both describe drops being flung about; differs because spattering is physical droplet-scattering, not a description of speech.
  • Faltered: Similar because both describe speech losing its flow; differs because faltering is hesitation and weakening, quiet and controlled, whereas spluttering is loud, wet, and explosive.
