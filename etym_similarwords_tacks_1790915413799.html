• Nails: Similar because both are pointed metal fasteners driven into materials to bind them; differs because tacks are much shorter with disproportionately large, flat heads designed for lightweight fabrics and leather rather than heavy structural timber.
  • Brads: Similar because both are small wire fasteners used in delicate carpentry; differs because brads have extremely small, unobtrusive heads designed to be countersunk and concealed, whereas tacks have broad heads to prevent tear-through.
  • Pins / Thumb tacks: Similar because both temporarily fasten paper or cloth to surfaces; differs because pins and thumb tacks are pushed in by hand for temporary holding, whereas traditional tacks are hammered in for permanent fastening.
  • Courses / Headings: Similar because both denote navigational directions of travel; differs because 'tacks' specifically implies zigzag maneuvers against contrary winds or indirect tactical pivots in strategy.
  • Fasteners: Similar because both describe hardware used for mechanical joining; differs because 'fastener' is a broad hypernym including bolts, screws, rivets, and buckles.
