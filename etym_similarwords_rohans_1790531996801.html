• Montmorencies: Similar because both represent the very highest tier of French feudal peerage and court nobility; differs because the Montmorencies were indigenous barons of the Île-de-France holding premier military crown offices (Constables of France), whereas the Rohans traced their descent to the sovereign Celtic Kings of Brittany and held the status of princes étrangers (foreign princes).
  • Bourbons: Similar because both were preeminent dynastic houses in France; differs because the Bourbons were the reigning royal Capetian dynasty holding the crown of France, whereas the Rohans were princely feudal nobility beneath the monarch.
  • Habsburgs: Similar because both were princely and sovereign dynasties of Europe; differs because the Habsburgs were an imperial and royal sovereign dynasty that ruled the Holy Roman Empire, Austria, and Spain, whereas the Rohans were a princely family within the French and Austrian realms.
  • Guéméné / Soubise: Similar because they refer to the same family; differs because Guéméné and Soubise are specific cadet branches (e.g., Rohan-Guéméné, Rohan-Soubise) of the overarching House of Rohan.
  • Dynasty: Similar because the Rohans constitute a dynasty; differs because dynasty is a generic term for any hereditary succession of rulers or prominent families, whereas the Rohans is the specific proper name of this Breton-French house.
  • Princely House: Similar because the Rohans held princely rank; differs because princely house is a general categorical descriptor, whereas Rohans specifies this particular historical family.
  • Aristocrats: Similar because all members of the House of Rohan were aristocrats; differs because aristocrats is a broad sociological class, whereas the Rohans occupied the supreme princely apex of that class.
  • Émigrés: Similar because many Rohans fled France during the Revolution; differs because émigré describes a political refugee status, whereas Rohans refers to their ancient dynastic identity.
