• shrine: A sacred place or container associated with a deity, saint or relic; similar as a focus of devotion, but it may be a building, a niche or a reliquary rather than a table for sacrifice.
  • sanctuary: The holiest part of a church, or a place of refuge; similar in denoting sacred space, but it names the whole area rather than the altar within it.
  • tabernacle: The receptacle in which the consecrated host is reserved, or the portable shrine of the Israelites; similar as a holy container, but it is not a place of sacrifice.
  • temple: A building dedicated to a god; similar as a place of worship, but it is the whole structure, not the altar.
  • chancel: The part of a church around the altar, reserved for the clergy; similar by proximity, but it is the space, not the table.
  • communion table: The table used for the Lord's Supper in Protestant churches; similar in function, but deliberately plainer and less sacerdotal in connotation than "altar".
  • ara: The Latin word for a Roman altar; similar as a sacrificial structure, but it belongs to classical antiquity and to pagan cult.
  • high place: A raised site of worship in the Hebrew Bible, often condemned by the prophets; similar in the idea of elevation, but it is a topographic site rather than a built table.
  • pedestal: A raised base supporting a statue or column; similar in form and elevation, but secular and not a place of offering.
  • hearth: The floor of a fireplace, traditionally the sacred centre of the home; similar as a focal point of ritual warmth, but domestic and not sacrificial.
  • mizbeaḥ: The Hebrew word for an altar; similar in denoting the biblical sacrificial structure, but it is a foreign-language term and not used in ordinary English.
