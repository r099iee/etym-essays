Derivation chain for lest (a fused phrase of two inherited roots): Component 1: the demonstrative / relative stem 1. PIE *to- / *te- 'this, that' (demonstrative and relative stem, attested in Sanskrit ta-, Greek to, Latin is-te, Germanic that). 2. Proto-Germanic *that 'that' and the instrumental form *thi (from the demonstrative stem), source of Old English thaet 'that' and its instrumental thy 'by that, therefore'. 3. Old English thy 'by that, by that which; therefore' (instrumental of thaet) - used in the formulaic comparative construction 'the less ... that'. Component 2: the comparative 'less' 1. PIE *leis- 'small, lesser' (reconstructed; the cognate set is thin and the root is debated, but the Germanic comparative formation is secure; related by some scholars to a root meaning 'to diminish'). 2. Proto-Germanic *laisiz- 'smaller' (comparative) - source of Old English laessa 'less, smaller'. 3. Old English laes 'less' (comparative adverb) - used in the fused phrase. The fused conjunction: 4. Old English formula thy laes the 'by that less that, so that not' (attested from the 9th-10th c.) - literally 'by the amount less that ...', i.e. 'in order that ... to a lesser degree'; a comparative construction meaning 'so that ... not'. 5. Middle English les te / leste / lest (12th-14th c.) 'for fear that; unless; so that ... not' - the three words fused into a single conjunction; at this period interchangeable with the sibling 'unless' (from on less than). 6. lest (Modern English, 16th c. onward) - the conjunction restricted to its fear and prevention senses and fixed with the subjunctive; unchanged in form since the Middle English fusion. Note on the debate: the internal chronology is only partially documented. The Old English formula thy laes the is well attested, but the intermediate reduction to a single word leste is not directly visible in the manuscript record (the fusion belongs to the period of late Old English / early Middle English spelling chaos, when thy was written le- and the unstressed final syllable -the was reduced to -t(e)). The proposed PIE root *leis- for 'less' is accepted by most authorities but rests on slender cognate evidence; the Germanic comparative *laisiz- is secure.
