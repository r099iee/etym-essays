• Everlasting: Similar because both mean "lasting forever"; differs because everlasting is the everyday word for endless duration (and a plant name), while imperishable stresses immunity to decay and carries a literary glow.
  • Eternal: Similar because both mean "without end, outside time"; differs because eternal is theological and timeless (eternal life, eternal verities), while imperishable is about endurance through time, not outside it.
  • Immortal: Similar because both mean "cannot die"; differs because immortal applies to living things and to fame that outlives death (immortal soul, immortal works), while imperishable extends to objects and materials that cannot rot or decay.
  • Undying: Similar because both mean "never dying, never fading"; differs because undying is emotional and personal (undying love, undying loyalty), while imperishable is broader, cooler and more impersonal.
  • Unfading: Similar because both mean "not losing colour or freshness"; differs because unfading is mostly poetic and visual (unfading beauty), while imperishable covers physical destruction of any kind.
  • Indestructible: Similar because both mean "cannot be destroyed"; differs because indestructible is physical and pragmatic (indestructible toys), with no idealistic halo, while imperishable usually points to fame, memory or the soul.
  • Enduring: Similar because both mean "lasting a long time"; differs because enduring is a gradient word (it endures = it lasts, for now), while imperishable is absolute — it cannot perish at all.
  • Abiding: Similar because both mean "continuing, lasting"; differs because abiding is quieter and more personal (abiding faith), usually of feelings, while imperishable is grander and more public.
  • Perpetual: Similar because both mean "never ending"; differs because perpetual describes ongoing activity (perpetual motion, perpetual care) rather than the indestructibility of a thing.
  • Deathless: Similar because both mean "not subject to death"; differs because deathless is poetic and almost exclusively figurative (deathless fame), while imperishable also works for materials and foodstuffs.
  • Sempiternal: Similar because both mean "everlasting" (a learned synonym); differs because sempiternal is a rare, high-flown literary word, while imperishable is in normal educated use.
  • Incorruptible: Similar because both mean "not subject to decay" (of bodies, matter, virtue); differs because incorruptible leads with moral purity and the biblical resurrection sense, while imperishable has no moral colour at all.
  • Nonperishable: Similar because both describe food that keeps without spoiling; differs because nonperishable is the plain practical word for canned and dried goods, while imperishable in that sense is rarer and more formal.
