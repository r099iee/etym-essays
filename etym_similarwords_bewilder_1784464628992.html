• Confuse: Similar because both mean to make someone unable to think clearly; differs because confuse is the broad, neutral word, while bewilder stresses disorientation and loss of bearings — the "lost in the wilds" image.
  • Perplex: Similar because both mean to trouble the mind with something hard to work out; differs because perplex stresses intricate, knotty complication that resists solution, while bewilder stresses dazed loss of orientation.
  • Puzzle: Similar because both mean to make someone unable to understand; differs because puzzle implies a problem or riddle that engages the mind, while bewilder implies the mind being overwhelmed and disorientated.
  • Baffle: Similar because both mean to defeat understanding; differs because baffle emphasizes frustratingly defeating all attempts to comprehend, while bewilder emphasizes the dazed, confused state that results.
  • Confound: Similar because both mean to throw into confusion; differs because confound is stronger and more formal, with an archaic sense of "to mix up, damn" (as in "confound it!"), while bewilder is milder and purely psychological.
  • Mystify: Similar because both mean to make hard to understand; differs because mystify highlights making something seem mysterious and inexplicable (sometimes deliberately), while bewilder highlights the confusion of the person affected.
  • Befuddle: Similar because both mean to make confused; differs because befuddle carries a jocular or alcoholic flavour (muddled, fuddled), while bewilder is the serious, disorientating confusion.
  • Bemuse: Similar because both mean to cause puzzlement; differs because bemuse suggests absorbed, quiet, dreamy puzzlement, while bewilder suggests active, overwhelming disorientation.
  • Daze: Similar because both describe a stunned, disoriented state; differs because daze suggests physical or emotional shock (a blow, bad news), while bewilder is cognitive loss of bearings.
  • Disorient: Similar because both involve losing one's sense of position or direction; differs because disorient is literal or figurative loss of spatial or contextual bearings, while bewilder is the mental confusion that often accompanies it.
  • Nonplus: Similar because both mean to render unable to proceed; differs because nonplus is a rarer, sharper word meaning to bring to a complete standstill of speech or action, while bewilder is the everyday, softer confusion.
  • Flummox: Similar because both mean to confuse; differs because flummox is informal and jocular, while bewilder is standard and neutral.
