- [Vedic & Epic Era (c. 1000–500 BCE)] - patronymic/matronymic ethnonym designating a royal woman from the north-western Kekaya kingdom; celebrated as a heroic, chariot-driving queen who saved King Daśaratha during the Asura wars - [perception: admired as a valiant, devoted royal consort possessing martial skill and high aristocratic status]
- [Classical Sanskrit Epic Tradition (c. 500 BCE–500 CE)] - codified in Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa as the catalyst of Rāma's fourteen-year exile (Ayodhyākāṇḍa), manipulated by her hunchbacked maid Mantharā into claiming two fateful boons - [perception: viewed with deep pathos as an emblem of sudden, tragic moral fallibility and maternal possessiveness]
- [Bhakti Medieval Devotional Traditions (c. 12th–17th c.)] - reinterpreted in Tulsīdās's Rāmcaritmānas and Kamban's Irāmāvatāram as an unwitting instrument of divine cosmic destiny (daiva / avatāra-kārya), divinely compelled to send Rāma into the forest so that Rāvaṇa could be destroyed - [perception: absolved of purely personal malice; understood as a tragic puppet of cosmic necessity]
- [Modern & Contemporary Re-readings (20th–21st c.)] - reappraised in Indian literature, feminist critique, and psychological drama as a complex, vulnerable political figure navigating patriarchal court polygamy and securing her biological son's security in an insecure dynastic succession - [perception: seen not as a two-dimensional villain, but as a multi-dimensional, fiercely protective mother and tragic heroine]
