• Nicaea: Similar because Nicene is simply the adjective of this noun, and both point to the same city; differs because Nicaea is the proper name of the ancient city (modern İznik, Turkey), while Nicene has become almost exclusively a theological adjective.
  • Nicaean: Similar because both are adjectives formed from Nicaea; differs because Nicaean is the regular Latinate form (Latin Nicaeanus) and usually refers to the city, its empire, or its school, whereas Nicene is the specialized English form fixed in "Nicene Creed" and "Nicene orthodoxy".
  • Arian: Similar because Arianism is the very heresy Nicaea condemned, and "Nicene" is always defined against it in church history; differs because Arian means "of Arius" and denotes the subordinationist doctrine rejected at the council, while Nicene denotes the orthodox position that won.
  • Trinitarian: Similar because Nicene faith is trinitarian doctrine, and the creed is a definition of the Trinity; differs because Trinitarian is the general term for belief in the Trinity and carries no reference to Nicaea or to any particular council.
  • Orthodox: Similar because Nicene doctrine is "right belief" (orthodoxia), and the Nicene position became orthodoxy; differs because orthodox is a generic term for correct or standard teaching, not tied to a specific council or creed.
  • Catholic: Similar because Nicene Christianity is the catholic faith and the creed claims to speak for the whole church; differs because Catholic denotes universality or the entire church, while Nicene names the specific doctrinal settlement.
  • Constantinopolitan: Similar because the Nicene Creed was completed at Constantinople in 381, and the technical name "Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed" joins the two; differs because the term refers to the second ecumenical council and its city rather than to Nicaea.
  • Homoousios / consubstantial: Similar because this Greek term ("of one substance") is the doctrinal heart of the Nicene definition; differs because it is a technical theological noun or adjective naming the specific relationship of Son to Father, whereas Nicene is the adjective naming the whole conciliar settlement.
  • Athanasian: Similar because it names another early creed and Athanasius, the great champion of Nicaea whose theology the word honours; differs because the Athanasian Creed is a later western confession, not a product of the council itself.
  • Ecumenical: Similar because Nicaea was the first ecumenical (empire-wide, worldwide) council; differs because ecumenical describes any general council or the principle of universality, not the Nicene doctrine specifically.
  • Creed: Similar because the Nicene Creed is the word's commonest home and the creed is itself a "Nicene" text; differs because creed is the generic noun for a confession of faith, while Nicene is the specific adjective of place and doctrine.
