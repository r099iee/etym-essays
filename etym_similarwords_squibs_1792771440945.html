• firecrackers: Similar because both are small pyrotechnic devices designed to ignite and explode noisily; differs because firecrackers are engineered primarily for a single, abrupt, deafening detonation without prolonged sparkling, whereas squibs traditionally fizz, hiss, and spit a stream of fiery sparks before terminating in a modest pop or crack.
  • crackers: Similar because both refer to festive explosive devices used in celebrations; differs because crackers can also refer to British holiday table novelties pulled apart by two people to reveal a paper crown and motto, or to louder, heavier ground bangers, whereas squibs specifically emphasizes hissing, scurrying pyrotechnics or literary lampoons.
  • sparklers: Similar because both are handheld or ground fireworks that emit a brilliant spray of incandescent sparks; differs because sparklers burn steadily, quietly, and slowly along a metal wire without exploding, whereas squibs burn violently and conclude with an explosive burst.
  • lampoons: Similar because squibs has a well-established secondary meaning as a biting satirical essay or caustic written squib mocking someone; differs because a lampoon is typically longer, more formally structured, and malicious in intent, whereas a literary squib is brief, pungent, epigrammatic, and explosive in wit.
  • pasquinades: Similar because both denote anonymous, sharp satirical squibs or parodies posted in public places; differs because pasquinades historically originated from the talking statues of Rome (Pasquino) as subversive political verses pasted on walls, whereas squibs can refer equally to physical gunpowder fireworks, theatrical detonators, or journalistic barbs.
  • sat двига / satires: Similar because both use humor, irony, and ridicule to expose folly; differs because satire is a broad literary genre and intellectual mode that can encompass entire novels, plays, or verse epics, while a squib is merely a miniature, two-line or short-paragraph squirt of ridicule.
  • petards: Similar because both are explosive devices used in warfare and metaphor; differs because a petard was a heavy, bell-shaped metal bomb filled with gunpowder used to breach fortress gates (as in "hoist with his own petard"), whereas a squib is a tiny, paper-wrapped firework or theatrical miniature.
  • duds: Similar because in the famous idiom "damp squib," a squib becomes synonymous with a failure; differs because a dud is completely inert and fails entirely to function from the start (such as an unexploded shell), whereas a damp squib often begins to fizz or smoke before pathetically sputtering out without its final explosion.
  • detonators (special effects): Similar because in modern film and television production, squibs are the technical term for miniature explosive charges; differs because detonator is the generic industrial/military explosive trigger, while a film squib is a specialized pyrotechnic device paired with a fake blood pouch (blood squib) or dust pack to simulate bullet impacts.
