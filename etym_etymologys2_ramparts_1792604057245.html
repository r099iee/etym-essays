Note on historical phonetics and suffixation: The phonetic evolution from Latin anteparare to French rempart involves well-documented Gallo-Romance sound changes. The initial Latin prefix ante- contracted and nasalized into *am- / *em- (yielding Old Provençal amparar / emparar, "to fortify, seize"). In Middle French, the intensive prefix re- was prefixed to create the verb remparer ("to re-fortify"). The substantive noun was then formed using the common Germanic-derived Gallo-Romance noun suffix -art / -ard (as seen in words like bastard, wizard, etendart), producing rempart. When borrowed into 16th-century Elizabethan English, the French vowel /ɑ̃/ was anglicized to /æm/, giving rampart.
