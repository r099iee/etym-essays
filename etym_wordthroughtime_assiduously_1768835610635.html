- 17th c. — First appearance in English (the adjective assiduous arrives around 1620; the adverb assiduously follows within decades): a formal, scholarly word meaning 'with constant, unremitting application,' still visibly carrying its Latin image of sitting close beside one's work — of a care that never rises from its post.
- 18th–19th c. — Settles into earnest, respectable use: faithful attendance to duty, steady service to a master or a task. It connotes watchful constancy rather than mere busyness — the man who remains seated when others rise.
- 20th–21st c. — The physical image of the seat fades from common awareness: assiduously now describes any diligent, unrelenting effort. What survives is the nuance of anxious, almost obsessive faithfulness — a labor so constant that it harries and hounds the thing it tends, never relaxing its watch.
