• Groan: Similar because both are low vocal sounds of strain and effort; differs because a groan is a prolonged, deep, often wailing sound of pain or despair, while a grunt is a short, sharp, guttural noise of effort without melody.
  • Snort: Similar because both are blunt animal-like noises expressing contempt or effort; differs because a snort forces air explosively out through the nose, while a grunt originates in the throat and chest.
  • Growl: Similar because both are low, guttural sounds of displeasure; differs because a growl is a continuous, rumbling, menacing threat-sound (of a dog or angry person), while a grunt is a single short sound of effort or acknowledgment.
  • Mutter: Similar because both are low human vocalizations of complaint or reluctance; differs because muttering involves mumbled, half-audible words, while a grunt is entirely wordless.
  • Murmur: Similar because both are low indistinct sounds; differs because a murmur is soft, continuous, and often soothing or reverent (also the sound of a stream), while a grunt is abrupt, guttural, and effortful.
  • Huff: Similar because both are breath-based sounds of strain or annoyance; differs because a huff is an audible forced exhalation, while a grunt is a vocalized throat sound with pitch.
  • Chuff: Similar because both are short breathy effort-sounds; differs because a chuff is a rhythmic puffing sound (of a steam engine or an animal), softer and more breathy than the vocalized grunt.
  • Bellow: Similar because both are animal sounds applied to humans; differs because a bellow is a loud, deep roar of rage or pain, while a grunt is short, low, and restrained.
