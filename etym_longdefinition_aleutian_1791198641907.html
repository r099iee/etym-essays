Aleutian is a proper adjective (and sometimes noun) meaning of or relating to the Aleut peoples — the Indigenous Unangan communities of the Aleutian Islands and western Alaska Peninsula — or to the Aleutian archipelago itself, its language, culture, wildlife, and climate. Capitalized always, it appears in phrases such as Aleutian women, Aleutian kayak, Aleutian basketry, and the Aleutian Islands.

In the source sentence, Aleutian women weaving a robe for a year evokes the extraordinary textile art of Unangan weavers, who split beach grass and spruce root into hair-fine warp and weft to make waterproof parkas, robes, mats, and hats. The adjective here is ethnic-cultural: made by, characteristic of Aleut tradition.

Principal uses today:

  • Ethnic-cultural: Aleutian heritage, Aleutian elders, Aleutian dance, Aleutian Orthodox Christianity — referring to Unangan people, now often called Unangan in community-preferred usage, and their Sugpiaq-Alutiiq neighbours loosely included by outsiders.
  • Linguistic: Aleutian language / dialect — the Eskimo-Aleut language Unangam Tunuu, with eastern and western dialects; Aleutian place-names like Unalaska, Attu, Atka.
  • Geographic: Aleutian Islands, Aleutian Range, Aleutian Arc — the 1,200-mile volcanic chain separating the Bering Sea from the Pacific, running from Attu to False Pass; Aleutian Low for the dominant winter low-pressure centre.
  • Technological: Aleutian kayak (baidarka), Aleutian bentwood hunting hat, Aleutian basket — world-famous adaptations to treeless maritime hunting: skin-on-frame kayaks, split-root weaving, and visored hats that shield eyes from spray while amplifying hearing.
  • Biological: Aleutian Canada goose, Aleutian tern, Aleutian cackling goose — endemic or characteristic fauna; Aleutian shield fern, Aleutian wormwood for flora.
  • Historical-military: Aleutian Campaign (1942–43) — the only WWII battle on U.S. soil, Japanese occupation of Attu and Kiska and American retaking; Aleutian internment for forced removal of villagers.

As a noun, an Aleutian means an inhabitant of the islands, and the Aleutians is shorthand for the island chain. The form is distinct from Aleut (the person or language without suffix): one says an Aleut elder but Aleutian tradition, parallel to Paris / Parisian.

  • Register and respect: formal geographic writing prefers Aleutian; anthropology increasingly specifies Unangan (self-name) for people and retains Aleutian for place. Using Aleutian for people is not insulting but can sound dated beside community preference.
  • Associations: remoteness, storminess, volcanic starkness, endurance, exquisite minute craftsmanship, Russian-Orthodox chapels amid tundra, fog-bound airstrips, and strategic military outposts.
  • Common confusions: not to be confused with Alutiiq (neighbouring Kodiak people) or Inuit broadly; Aleutian branch split from Eskimo languages ~4,000 years ago.

The semantic bridge is metonymy: people-name → place-name → everything belonging to that place. Because outsiders first met the people through Russian fur-trade labels, the Russian exonym stuck to the map, and the map then lent its name to winds, birds, and boats. To say Aleutian is thus to invoke a windswept Bering world where human skill — a year to weave one robe — answers extreme scarcity with extreme patience.
