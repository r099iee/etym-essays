Latin par, paris "equal; an equal, a match, a pair" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE onward; of uncertain deeper origin — see note below) → [Old French] per "equal, a peer" (10th–11th c.) and the compound nonper "not equal; odd (of a number); (noun) the third, deciding person" (non "not" + per "equal") → [Anglo-French] noumper "arbiter, umpire" (13th–14th c.) → [Middle English] numpere, noumpere "arbiter, the odd, not-equal third called in when two arbitrators fail to agree" (14th c.) → [REBRACKETING] a noumpere reanalysed as an oumpere → an umpire (the initial n- detached from the noun and reattached to the article, 15th–16th c.) → [Modern English] umpire "neutral official who enforces rules or settles a dispute" (the sports sense narrows from the 18th–19th c.) NOTE ON THE DEBATED STAGE: Latin par "equal" has NO secure Indo-European etymon. A proposed link to PIE *per- "to allot, grant, produce" and a connection with Latin parare "to make ready, arrange" (the theory that "to make equal" is "to arrange so as to match") are both speculative; authorities differ, and the root may even be of non-Indo-European origin. No deeper stage is therefore asserted.
