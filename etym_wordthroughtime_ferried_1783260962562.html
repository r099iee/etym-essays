- Old Norse inheritance - ferry came into English from Old Norse ferja, related to fare (to travel, go), and from the start meant transporting people or goods by boat across a river or narrow water.
- Mythic colouring - in classical and medieval tradition the verb acquired an underworld resonance, since Charon ferried the souls of the dead across the rivers of Hades, and Gibbon's spirits ... were ferried over exploits exactly that solemn, ominous colour.
- Contemporary sense - ferry still denotes a short, fixed crossing by boat, but has also widened metaphorically to any repeated shuttling of people or goods back and forth between two points, losing none of its water-bound root.
