- Old English body part - ban means tusk, tooth, and skeletal piece; perception everyday-bodily, bone as hard fate surviving fire and grave.
- Medieval residue and relic - bones as saints’ relics, dice as bones, bone of contention thrown to dogs; connotation of sacred permanence and quarrelsome remainder.
- Early modern plainness - to make no bones about, to feel in bones, bare bones; image of stripped truth, peasant bluntness against courtly flesh.
- 19th-century school slang intensifier - bone idle, bone lazy for incorrigibly work-shy boys; affectionate-exasperated masters’ hyperbole, idle to the marrow.
- Contemporary totality marker - bone dry, bone tired, bone weary; neutral-colloquial for utterly, while skeleton keeps anatomy and bone keeps idiom, as in talented though bone idle yet sated with books.
