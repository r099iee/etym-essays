gallop descends from a Frankish (West Germanic) compound; the two elements are traced in isolation, then joined. ELEMENT 1 — *wala "well": PIE *wel- / *welh₁- "to wish, choose, want; to be pleased" (reconstructed root, c. 4000 BCE; also source of Latin velle "to wish, will", English will) → Proto-Germanic *wela- / *wala- "well, good, as desired" (reconstructed; cf. Old English wel, Old Norse vel, Gothic waila) → Frankish *wala "well, fully, as it should be" (reconstructed West Germanic, c. 5th c. CE; the first element of the compound) ELEMENT 2 — *hlaupan "to leap": PIE *kleup- / *kleub- "to spring, leap, run, stumble" (reconstructed root, c. 4000 BCE; debated — see note) → Proto-Germanic *hlaupaną "to leap, spring, run, jump" (reconstructed; cf. Old English hlēapan, Old Norse hlaupa, Gothic hlaupan) → Frankish *hlaupan "to run, leap" (reconstructed West Germanic, c. 5th c. CE) COMPOUND STEP: Frankish *wala-hlaup "a good run, a full leap, a perfect bounding run" (reconstructed compound; *wala "well" + *hlaup "a leap, a run") → Old French galop (noun) / galoper (verb) "to gallop, to run at full speed" (attested c. 12th c. CE; Germanic initial *w- regularly becomes Romance g-/gu-, as in warden/guardian, war/guerre) → Anglo-Norman / Middle English galopen "to gallop" (attested 14th c. CE) → English gallop "to ride or move at the horse's fastest four-beat gait; that pace itself" ◄ TARGET (present-day rootWord) Debate / nuance note: Two points are genuinely debated. First, the deep ancestry of Germanic *hlaupaną: many authorities reconstruct PIE *kleup- / *kleub- "to spring, run", but others treat *hlaupaną as a Germanic formation of unclear or non-PIE origin — the ultimate PIE link is therefore uncertain. Second, the Frankish compound *wala-hlaup is itself a reconstruction: its two Germanic elements are securely attested across the family, and the Old French galop is securely attested, but the exact Frankish compound form is inferred from them. The initial sound change (Germanic w- → French g-) is regular and well attested (cf. *wardōn → garder "to guard", *werra → guerre "war"). The addition of the suffix -oper/-op in French is likewise regular verb formation.
