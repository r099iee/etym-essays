• Hedgerow: Similar because both name lines of shrubs marking field boundaries; differs because a hedgerow stresses the whole wild strip — bank, ditch, trees, and undergrowth — while a hedge is the planted shrub barrier itself.
  • Fence: Similar because both are boundaries that enclose land and exclude intruders; differs because a fence is built of dead materials (wood, wire, metal), while a hedge is alive and growing.
  • Thicket: Similar because both are dense tangles of woody growth sheltering wildlife; differs because a thicket is wild and unplanned, while a hedge is planted and maintained in a line.
  • Bush: Similar because hedges are literally made of bushes planted in a row; differs because a bush is one single shrub, while a hedge is the continuous living wall they form together.
  • Shrubbery: Similar because both are plantings of shrubs in gardens and grounds; differs because shrubbery is ornamental clumps for display, while a hedge is a functional linear barrier.
  • Safeguard: Similar because a hedge in its figurative sense is exactly a safeguard; differs because safeguard is abstract and general, while hedge keeps the flavour of a planted defensive barrier.
  • Barrier: Similar because both block movement or harm; differs because barrier is the broad abstract term (physical, legal, linguistic), while hedge implies a specifically planted or financial protection.
  • Evasion: Similar because to hedge verbally is to evade; differs because evasion names the act of dodging itself, while hedge adds the image of sheltering behind qualifications.
  • Fence-sitting: Similar because both describe refusing to commit to one side; differs because fence-sitting is purely about indecision, while hedge includes active protective measures like counter-bets.
