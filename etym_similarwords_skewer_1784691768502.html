• spit: Similar because both are pointed rods used in roasting meat over a fire; differs because a spit is long, heavy, and built to carry a whole carcass, while a skewer is short, light, and used for individual pieces, fixings, and trussing.
  • broach: Similar because both name a roasting rod for meat; differs because broach is an archaic or technical borrowing from French, now largely obsolete, while skewer is the everyday word and alone has the modern figurative sense of a piercing attack.
  • pin: Similar because both are slender, pointed objects used to pierce and hold things; differs because a pin is a general fastener (in sewing, hardware, explosives), while a skewer is specifically a cooking rod, and the skewer's verb has a special figurative sense of demolishing an argument.
  • needle: Similar because both are thin, pointed, piercing implements; differs because a needle is for sewing, knitting, or injection, while a skewer is for cooking meat, and "to skewer" (in criticism) is much more aggressive than "to needle" (to provoke gently).
  • spike: Similar because both can mean a sharp pointed rod and, as verbs, to pierce; differs because a spike is larger, heavier, and often part of a rail, wall, or weapon, whereas a skewer is a small kitchen or grill implement, and its figurative use is verbal rather than physical.
  • pierce: Similar because both denote penetrating with a sharp point; differs because pierce is a general verb for any penetration (ears, armour, silence), while skewer specifically means to thread onto a pin and, figuratively, to impale a target in argument or satire.
  • impale: Similar because both mean to transfix with a pointed object, and both have vivid figurative uses for exposing or destroying a victim; differs because impale suggests a much larger, often fatal weapon (stakes, lances), while skewer is kitchen-sized and its figurative sense is mockingly surgical rather than gruesome.
  • transfix: Similar because both mean to pierce through and hold fast; differs because transfix is formal and also means "to paralyse with emotion" (transfixed with wonder), a sense skewer entirely lacks.
  • shish kebab / kebab stick: Similar because both refer to the threaded meat-on-stick grill preparation; differs because these name the dish and its serving stick, while skewer is the general implement name and the base of a figurative verb.
  • wand / rod: Similar because both are long thin sticks; differs because rods and wands are general, often ceremonial or structural, while a skewer is a pointed cooking pin, and only "to skewer" has the critical sense.
