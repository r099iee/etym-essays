- [Channel for roof rainwater (14th–16th c.)] - A strictly utilitarian, neutral term for a trough or pipe carrying water off a building; no social stigma attached.

- [Open street drain (17th–19th c.)] - Extended to the paved channel running between sidewalk and street; still neutral and structural, but begins to accumulate connotations of dirt, waste, and the filth of urban streets.

- [Metaphor of social degradation (19th c. onward)] - "The gutter" becomes shorthand for the absolute bottom of society, the lowest, most degraded sphere of public life; compounds like "gutter press" and "gutter language" fix a strongly pejorative, class-loaded register.

- [Contemporary sense] - Still literal (a street/roof channel), while the figurative "in the gutter" keeps its contemptuous edge, signalling poverty, vulgarity, or moral and social debasement.
