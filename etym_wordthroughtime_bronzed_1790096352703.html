- Antiquity, bronze the alloy without the modern name; aes and chalkos; statues gleam, skin is merely sun-dark
- 13th to 14th c. Italian bronzo, foundry word for bell-metal; workshop-technical, Tuscan, prestigious
- 17th to 18th c. bronze and to bronze in English; coating plaster and iron bronze-colour; craft manuals, funerary art
- Romantic 19th c., bronzed skin of sailors, soldiers, and savage princes; poetic, imperial, statuesque, the living bronze
- 20th c. beach culture, the bronzed lifeguard and starlet as health and glamour; then dermatological irony, the admired glow as damage
- Modern, three stable senses side by side: coated statue, tanned body, hardened veteran; approving, golden-brown, enduring
