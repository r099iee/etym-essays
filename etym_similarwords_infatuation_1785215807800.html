• Love: Similar because both are powerful attachments to another person; differs because love involves knowledge, mutuality, and endurance, while infatuation is blind, idealizing, and short-lived, fed more by fantasy than by acquaintance.
  • Passion: Similar because both denote intense feeling; differs because passion is the broader word and may be constructive and sustained (a passion for justice, a passion for music), while infatuation names specifically the irrational romantic kind that clouds judgment.
  • Obsession: Similar because both dominate the mind; differs because obsession stresses compulsive mental occupation and is intrusive and pathological, whereas infatuation is primarily overpowering feeling that may or may not harden into obsession.
  • Crush: Similar because both name a consuming short-lived attraction; differs because crush is the light, informal, often adolescent word, while infatuation is more literary and solemn, carrying an implication of overmastering and unreasoning intensity.
  • Adoration: Similar because both involve fervent regard for another; differs because adoration implies reverence and worshipful devotion that may be sustained and selfless, while infatuation is fantasy-fed and expected to fade.
  • Fascination: Similar because both denote absorption in something or someone; differs because fascination may be purely intellectual and ethically neutral, while infatuation is emotional, disproportionate, and slightly foolish.
  • Limerence: Similar because both describe the intense, idealized early phase of romantic attraction; differs because limerence is a modern clinical coinage (1970s) for the obsessive craving for reciprocation, narrower and more technical than infatuation.
  • Mania: Similar because both imply a mind carried beyond reason; differs because mania is a clinical term for a disordered state, far more severe and pathological than any infatuation.
