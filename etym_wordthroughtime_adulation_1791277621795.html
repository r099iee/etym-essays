- Classical Latin adulatio - Fawning, flattery, also divine honours voted to emperors, contemptuous and political perception.
- Late Latin / Medieval adulation - Servile flattery in court and church, moralists condemn it as vice opposed to honest praise.
- 14th–16th c. English borrowing - Learned formal noun for sycophancy, used by Chaucer, Lydgate, and Tudor moralists, elevated literary register.
- 18th–19th c. public adulation - Extended to crowds and celebrity, democratic age makes multitude's cheers central, sceptical liberal perception of mass worship.
- Today - Pejorative term for celebrity, political, and fan-culture excess, signals critical distance and warns against uncritical worship.
