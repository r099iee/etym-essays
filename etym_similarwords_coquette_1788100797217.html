• Flirt: Similar because both denote playful amorous attention-seeking without commitment; differs because flirt is gender-neutral, everyday, and names the behaviour, while coquette is feminine, literary, and names a cultivated social type with a technique.
  • Tease: Similar because both involve attracting and then withholding; differs because tease implies deliberate cruelty or mockery and a game of refusal, and is far broader, covering non-amorous verbal baiting.
  • Siren: Similar because both denote an alluring woman; differs because the siren is mythically fatal — her lure destroys sailors — whereas the coquette's allure is playful and stops well short of ruin.
  • Femme fatale: Similar because both denote a woman who exercises power through attraction; differs because the femme fatale is deadly serious and systematically destructive, while the coquette plays and does not intend destruction.
  • Minx: Similar because both denote a pert, flirtatious girl; differs because a minx is impudent, mischievous and young, whereas the coquette implies sophistication, calculation and adulthood.
  • Heartbreaker: Similar because both win and discard admirers; differs because heartbreaker emphasises the result — broken hearts — rather than the performance, and is more colloquial and gender-neutral.
  • Vamp: Similar because both describe a flamboyantly flirtatious woman; differs because vamp is predatory, theatrical slang of the silent-film era (from vampire), with an openly dangerous edge.
  • Belle: Similar because both denote a popular, admired young woman; differs because a belle need not flirt at all — the word names beauty and social success, not the art of flirtation.
  • Charmer: Similar because both use personal charm to win others; differs because the charmer may pursue any end — persuasion, friendship, business — while the coquette's field is specifically romantic admiration.
  • Man-eater: Similar because both denote a woman who collects admirers; differs sharply in register: man-eater is aggressive modern slang implying conquest and consumption, where the coquette implies elegant refusal and play.
