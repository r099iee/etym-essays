- 6th century - a royal Lombard name borne by Rosamunda, wife of King Alboin; it carries associations of tragedy, captivity, and revenge.
- 12th century - the name reaches England with the Normans and becomes attached to Rosamund Clifford, mistress of Henry II.
- 13th–16th centuries - “Fair Rosamund” becomes a legend of hidden love, a labyrinth, and Queen Eleanor’s poison; the name acquires a romantic and tragic charge.
- 17th–18th centuries - the story is dramatised in poetry and opera; Rosamund becomes a recognised literary heroine of medieval romance.
- 19th century - Victorian medievalism and Romantic music revive the name; “Rosamunde” and “Fair Rosamund” appear in painting, theatre, and song.
- 20th–21st centuries - the name becomes rarer but remains a literary and cultural reference; Wodehouse uses it comically, and modern bearers such as Rosamund Pike keep it visible.
