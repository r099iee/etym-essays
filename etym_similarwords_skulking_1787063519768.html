• Lurking: Similar because both mean staying hidden, often to observe or attack; differs because lurking stresses lying in wait in one place without moving, while skulking stresses furtive movement or hiding with a guilty/cowardly motive.
  • Sneaking: Similar because both mean moving or behaving furtively to avoid detection; differs because sneaking is broader and can be neutral or even playful (sneaking a cookie), while skulking is always disreputable.
  • Slink: Similar because both mean to move in a stealthy, self-effacing way; differs because slink often describes an animal's or person's apologetic, hunched retreat (slink away in shame), while skulk implies active, suspicious hiding and concealment of motive.
  • Prowling: Similar because both describe stealthy movement with predatory intent; differs because prowling is especially associated with hunting and patrolling (a prowling lion, a prowler), while skulking emphasizes hiding and cowardly/guilty concealment rather than active stalking.
  • Creeping: Similar because both mean slow, quiet, stealthy movement; differs because creeping is neutral and physical (low, slow motion; also creeping plants), while skulking adds a disapproved motive to the stealth.
  • Stealing (archaic verb sense): Similar because both describe moving secretly and quietly; differs because this sense of “steal” (steal away) is now archaic or literary, while skulking is current and carries a guilty, lurking connotation.
  • Hiding: Similar because both involve staying out of sight; differs because hiding is neutral and can be innocent (hiding from danger), while skulking implies hiding for a disreputable or scheming purpose.
  • Furtive (adjective): Similar because both describe secret, sly, trying-not-to-be-seen behaviour; differs because furtive is an adjective describing manner (a furtive glance) and can be milder, while skulking is a verb/participle describing the whole act of hiding and sneaking.
  • Cowering: Similar because both describe shrinking out of sight; differs because cowering emphasizes fear and physical crouching, while skulking emphasizes secrecy and a guilty or scheming motive rather than mere fear.
  • Skulker (noun): the agent noun; similar in origin and meaning, but a noun naming the person who skulks, historically a soldier who shirks battle — while skulking names the action itself.
