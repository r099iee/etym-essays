• Pass: Similar in meaning go by of time; differs because pass is the neutral, all-purpose word, hugely ambiguous (pass a law, pass an exam, pass away), whereas elapse is unambiguous and specifically images time sliding smoothly away.
  • Go by: Similar in meaning pass of time; differs because go by is the informal, conversational twin of pass, which would deflate a stately rhythm, whereas elapse is formal and measured.
  • Expire: Similar in meaning come to an end of a period; differs because expire concentrates on the terminal moment when a term, deadline, or licence ends, whereas elapse pictures the whole interval flowing away.
  • Lapse: Similar in being a formal verb of time passing; differs because lapse suggests time passing so that a right or condition is lost (a contract lapses), whereas elapse is purely descriptive of duration, with no sense of forfeiture.
  • Glide (by): Similar in suggesting smooth, effortless motion of time; differs because glide is a poetic, image-bearing verb with physical overtones, whereas elapse is the neutral, technical word for duration.
  • Flow (by): Similar in suggesting continuous, even passage of time; differs because flow pictures time as a moving current, while elapse makes no such image and stays strictly functional.
  • Slip away: Similar in suggesting time passing unnoticed or escaping; differs because slip away is colloquial and faintly ominous, whereas elapse is dignified and entirely neutral.
  • Tick by / Tick away: Similar in meaning pass of measured time; differs because tick by stresses the mechanical marking of seconds (as on a clock) and is informal, while elapse ignores the machinery of time.
  • Intervene: Similar in describing an interval between events; differs because intervene names the gap that comes between two moments, whereas elapse describes the passing of any interval, not its position between events.
  • Roll by: Similar in meaning pass of time; differs because roll by is casual and pictures time as a series of units rolling past, without elapse's formal, legal-administrative dignity.
