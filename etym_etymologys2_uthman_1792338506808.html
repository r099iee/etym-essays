Note on onomastic philology: Classical Arab lexicographers (notably Ibn Manẓūr in Lisān al-‘Arab, al-Jawharī in al-Ṣiḥāḥ, and al-Zabīdī in Tāj al-‘Arūs) debate the primary etymology of the name ‘Uthmān. The predominant lexicographical tradition classifies it as a zoonym (an animal name), identifying al-‘uthmān as the chick or young offspring of the ḥubārā (the houbara bustard, Chlamydotis undulata), a large, majestic terrestrial bird celebrated in pre-Islamic Bedouin culture for its acute alertness, sharp vision, and solitary nobility. Alternatively, philologists connect the name to the verbal root ‘athama ("to mend or reset a broken bone without deformity"), suggesting a symbolic epithet for an individual who restores fractured relations, heals communal strife, or unites a broken people.
