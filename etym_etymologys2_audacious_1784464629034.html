PIE *h₂ewd- / *h₂ew- "to enjoy, be eager, desire" (reconstructed root; the exact shape is debated — often given as *h₂ewdʰ-, cognate with Latin avidus "eager, greedy"; see note) → Latin audeō, audēre "to dare, to venture, to be bold" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE, e.g. in Plautus; built on the older sense of eagerness/desire) → Latin audāx (genitive audācis) "bold, daring, reckless, rash" (classical Latin, 1st c. BCE) → Latin audācia "boldness, daring, impudence" (classical Latin, 1st c. BCE) → Medieval Latin audācia (preserved in learned writing) → Old French audace "boldness, daring" (12th c.) → French audacieux, audacieuse "bold, daring" (derived adjective) → English audacious "bold, daring, impudent" (borrowed from French, mid-16th c.) → Modern English audacious. Debated/nuanced stage: The PIE root is not securely fixed. Many handbooks reconstruct *h₂ewdʰ- from the evidence of Latin audeō and avidus, connecting "dare" to "eagerness/desire," but the full Indo-European cognate set is thin and the reconstruction is proposed rather than proven. Note also that audacious is etymologically unrelated to Latin audīre "to hear" (the source of audio, audience, audible); the shared aud- spelling is coincidental.
