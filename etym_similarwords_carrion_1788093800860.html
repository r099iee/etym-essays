• carcass / carcase: Similar because both concern a dead animal's body; differs because carcase names the whole dead body, while carrion names the flesh of it once it is rotting — carrion is what a carcass becomes, and may even be eaten or carried away piecemeal.
  • corpse: Similar because it is a dead body; differs because corpse is neutral, usually human, and can be fresh, embalmed, or intact — it implies nothing about decay or scavenging, and nothing is eaten of it.
  • remains: Similar because it denotes what death leaves behind; differs because it is a courteous, euphemistic word covering bones, ashes, and fragments, with no necrophagous or putrid charge at all.
  • offal: Similar because it is discarded flesh of animals; differs because it names the internal organs and entrails thrown out at slaughter, not the decaying body in the field.
  • flesh: Similar because carrion is flesh; differs because flesh is the neutral word for living tissue, meat, and the body — carrion restricts it to the dead, rotting, scavenged state.
  • putrefaction: Similar because it is the process carrion undergoes; differs because it names the process of decay itself (a scientific term), not the decaying substance
  • corruption: Similar because both describe decay, physical or moral; differs because corruption has been fully abstracted into moral, political, and informational senses, whereas carrion stays rooted in the rotting body.
  • dead meat: Similar because it is slang for carrion and also for a doomed person; differs because it is informal, modern, and metaphorical at both ends, lacking carrion's ancient biological exactness.
  • gore: Similar because it is the visceral stuff of death; differs because gore is chiefly spilled blood and torn tissue in slaughter or horror contexts, not flesh decaying after death.
  • carrion crow / vultures' meat: Similar because both are compounds built on the same idea; differs because they are creatures and phrases, not synonyms — they show how carrion organises language around scavengers.
