- [Pre-English / Scandinavian echo: Splash & wet noise] - Related northern forms describe splashing and soft wet mess; the sound-pattern encodes messy disturbance rather than high rhetoric.
- [c. 1600: English noisy wrangle] - Squabble appears in Early Modern English as tumultuous, chattering dispute; contemporaries hear it as low-style and vivid.
- [1700s: Petty quarrel label] - Settles as the ordinary word for trivial arguments among children, servants, or foolish equals; moralists and comic playwrights exploit the put-down.
- [1800s–1900s: Political & press diminisher] - Editorials ‘reduce’ parliamentary or party fights to squabbles, shaping public perception that the conflict is unworthy of serious attention.
- [Contemporary: Domestic & media staple] - Family squabble, Twitter squabble, coalition squabble—still codes noise + pettiness; rarely used when speakers wish to honor the stakes.
