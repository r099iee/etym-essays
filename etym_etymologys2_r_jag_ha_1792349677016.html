Note on historical debate: Ancient Sanskrit grammarians (such as Yāska and Pāṇini) analyzed gṛha as a primary derivative of the verbal root grah- ("to seize, hold, encompass"), defining it as gṛhyante 'tra padārthā iti gṛham ("that in which goods, inhabitants, and cattle are held and protected"). Modern Indo-European comparative linguists (Mayrhofer, Manfred EWAia; Lubotsky) acknowledge this ancient connection while also proposing cognacy with Proto-Indo-European *gʰerdʰ- ("to encircle, fence off, wall in"), placing Sanskrit gṛha within the same broad developmental lineage as Germanic *gardaz (source of English garden and yard) and Slavic *gordъ (source of Russian gorod and grad, "fortified town"). Both etymological paths converge on the essential ancient reality of an enclosed, fortified sanctuary.
