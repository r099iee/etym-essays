• abridgement: The exact English equivalent, naming a shorter version made by omission; it differs only in being native English and in extending metaphorically to the curtailing of rights ("an abridgement of freedom").
  • epitome: Similar as a condensed representation of a larger whole; it differs by emphasising the perfect embodiment of the subject's essentials and by being far more formal and rhetorical.
  • compendium: Similar as a compact collection of a subject's contents; it differs by suggesting a systematic handbook rather than a shortened narrative, and by being Latin rather than French in form.
  • summary: Similar in giving the substance in brief; it differs by being the neutral everyday word and by referring to the account rather than to a published work.
  • digest: Similar as an ordered, readable condensation, often of laws or history; it differs by implying rearrangement and classification rather than simple shortening.
  • abstract: Similar as a condensed statement; it differs by being a modern scholarly genre prefixed to an article and by lacking any suggestion of a book for general readers.
  • précis: Similar as a French-derived word for a compressed account; it differs by naming an exercise in exact summarising, with no connotation of a separate published abridgement.
  • synopsis: Similar in covering a whole at reduced scale; it differs by stressing the outline of a plot or argument rather than the reduction of a text.
  • conspectus: Similar as a comprehensive view in brief; it differs by being a learned Latinism for a tabular survey, much rarer than abrégé even in bibliographic prose.
  • outline: Similar in giving only the main lines; it differs by being a skeleton of headings rather than a continuous readable text.
