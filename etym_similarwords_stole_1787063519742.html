• pinched: Similar because it means to steal, especially small items; differs because it is slangy and informal, usually British, and never carries the literary "steal away" motion sense.
  • pilfered: Similar because it means to steal in small quantities; differs because it implies petty, sneaky theft of small items and never means to move stealthily.
  • swiped: Similar because it means to steal or grab quickly; differs because it is informal, suggests a quick snatch rather than secret removal, and has no motion sense.
  • filched: Similar because it means to steal slyly; differs because it is playful or literary and always implies smallness and cunning, with no "move quietly" sense.
  • purloined: Similar because it means to steal, especially formally; differs because it is literary and formal, implying theft by stealth in a high register, never casual motion.
  • crept: Similar because it describes quiet, careful movement (the intransitive motion sense of stole); differs because it only describes movement, carries no idea of taking or crime, and is the everyday verb for moving quietly.
  • sneaked / snuck: Similar because it means to move or take furtively; differs because it emphasises avoiding notice rather than the light, graceful silence of "stole", and is informal.
  • took: Similar because it can mean to remove or appropriate something; differs because it is completely neutral and general, lacking the secrecy and wrongfulness central to "stole".
  • lifted: Similar because it means to steal, colloquially; differs because it suggests shoplifting and is slang, with no motion sense.
  • robbed: Similar because it involves wrongful taking; differs because it typically describes taking by force or threat from a person or place, whereas "stole" implies secrecy and no confrontation.
