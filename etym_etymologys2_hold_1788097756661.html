Note: dictionaries give the nautical term as probably borrowed from Middle Dutch hol, with the native word “hole” (Old English hol) as a reinforcing doublet of the same root; some accounts derive the English form directly from “hole”. The -d spelling, first seen in the later 16th century, is generally agreed to be due to the verb “hold”. The verb itself (Old English healdan) is of disputed origin, possibly connected with a PIE root meaning “to drive, urge”, but this is not certain.
