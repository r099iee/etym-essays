Note on historical and linguistic substrate debate: The etymology of Sanskrit śāli (along with vrīhi) occupies a prominent position in South Asian historical linguistics and ethnobotany. Because rice (Oryza sativa) was domesticated in the humid river valleys of Asia long before the arrival of Indo-European pastoralists onto the Indian subcontinent, Sanskrit terms for rice lack cognates in European Indo-European branches (Latin, Greek, Germanic). Historical linguists such as Michael Witzel, Colin Masica, and Franklin Southworth hypothesize that śāli was borrowed into early Indo-Aryan from an indigenous Gangetic agricultural substrate—likely Austroasiatic (ancestral to the Munda languages) or an extinct Para-Munda dialect that pioneered intensive wetland paddy transplantation in the middle Ganges basin.
