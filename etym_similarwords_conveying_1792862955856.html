• Transporting: Similar because both mean moving goods or people from place to place; differs because transporting is the plain logistical term for freight and passengers, while conveying is more formal and extends further into communicating ideas.
  • Carrying: Similar because both describe bearing a load along; differs because carrying stresses the physical holding in arms or on the back, while conveying stresses the journey and delivery, often by vehicle or channel.
  • Hauling: Similar because both move heavy goods by cart or truck; differs because hauling stresses effortful dragging of weight, while conveying sounds smoother, more official, and covers messages as well as loads.
  • Ferrying: Similar because both shuttle loads back and forth along a route; differs because ferrying implies repeated trips across water or a gap, while conveying names any single steady transfer by any means.
  • Transmitting: Similar because both pass something along a channel; differs because transmitting belongs to signals, broadcasts, and disease, while conveying keeps one foot in carts and parcels and the other in meaning.
  • Communicating: Similar because both bridge minds with meaning; differs because communicating is the two-way exchange itself, while conveying pictures the one-way delivery of a particular sense or feeling.
  • Imparting: Similar because both hand over something immaterial such as knowledge or warmth; differs because imparting stresses generous giving from a store, while conveying stresses the travelling of the thing given.
  • Expressing: Similar because both put inner feeling into outward form; differs because expressing presses feeling outward from the self, while conveying carries it across to a receiver, with delivery guaranteed in the image.
  • Transferring: Similar because both shift possession from hand to hand; differs because transferring is neutral and bureaucratic, while conveying retains the road, the cart, and the legal parchment in its flavour.
  • Conducting: Similar because pipes and wires conduct what carts convey; differs because conducting is the engineer's word for guided flow plus the musician's word for leading, while conveying never leads an orchestra.
