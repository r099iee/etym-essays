Biblical Hebrew verbal root š-l-ḥ "to send, send out, let go, stretch forth" (attested from the earliest biblical strata, c. 9th–6th c. BCE; cognate with Akkadian šalāḫu and Ugaritic šlḥ). → Biblical Hebrew noun Šīlōaḥ (שִׁלֹחַ / שִׁילֹחַ) "conduit, aqueduct, channel; the sending [of water]" — the name of the waterwork and pool at Jerusalem (2 Kings 20:20; Isaiah 8:6; c. 8th–7th c. BCE). → Greek Σιλωάμ Silōám and the shorter variant Σιλωά Silōá (Septuagint transliteration, 3rd c. BCE; John 9:7 glosses the name "Sent"). → Latin Siloa (the Vulgate-derived form of the name in patristic and humanist Latin, 4th c. CE onward). → English SILOA (Milton, Paradise Lost I.11, 1667), the poetic Vulgate-derived form coexisting with the Bible-English form Siloam. Debated / nuanced: the derivation from š-l-ḥ "to send" is not seriously debated — it is stated in the New Testament itself (John 9:7) and is accepted by standard lexica. The genuine nuance is formal: which Greek form (Siloám or Siloá) underlies which English form (Siloam vs. Siloa), and whether Milton's Siloa comes directly from the Vulgate or through Latinized patristic and Renaissance usage; both routes end at the same attested English form.
