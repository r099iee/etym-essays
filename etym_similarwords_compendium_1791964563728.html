• compilation: Similar because both gather material from many sources into one work; differs because a compilation need not condense or organize — it can be a raw gathering — while a compendium promises a usable, abbreviated whole.
  • anthology: Similar in collecting selected texts; differs because an anthology presents extracts whole (poems, essays) and is primarily literary, whereas a compendium digests and systematizes knowledge.
  • digest: Similar in condensing a larger body of material into ordered form (“a digest of case law”); differs in being more strictly arranged for reference and less likely to be a physical miscellany of objects or games.
  • epitome: Similar in being a compact representation of something larger; differs because epitome stresses the perfect miniature (“the epitome of elegance”) and is often figurative, while compendium stays literal and bibliographic.
  • abridgment: Similar in shortening a text; differs because an abridgment reduces one specific work, while a compendium may draw on many and stands as a self-contained whole.
  • summary: Similar in brevity; differs because a summary follows the shape of the original and makes no claim to completeness or to standing as a work in its own right.
  • précis: Similar as a condensed statement; differs in being short, formal and usually confined to a single document or argument, with none of the “handbook” connotation.
  • handbook: Similar as a portable work of reference; differs because a handbook is organized for practical use and may be wholly original, whereas a compendium is defined by what it gathers and condenses.
  • miscellany: Similar in collecting diverse items; differs because a miscellany makes no claim to systematic completeness and is often deliberately random or belletristic.
  • florilegium: Similar as a gathering of choice passages; differs in being a learned term for a collection of literary extracts, with no pretence of summarizing a whole discipline.
  • encyclopedia: Similar in aspiring to comprehensive knowledge; differs in scale and arrangement — alphabetical, multi-volume and exhaustive, where a compendium is compact and selective.
  • vade mecum: Similar as a portable book carried for constant reference; differs in emphasizing habitual personal use rather than condensed completeness.
