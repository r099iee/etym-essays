• Ledge: Similar because it denotes a narrow horizontal shelf or projection; differs because a ledge need not belong to an opening and bears no structural duty, while a sill always bottoms a frame.
  • Threshold: Similar because it is the bottom strip of a doorway and a twin of the door sill; differs because threshold leans ceremonial and figurative (crossing thresholds), while sill stays workmanlike and literal.
  • Lintel: Similar because it is the sibling beam of the same opening, finishing its frame; differs because the lintel crowns the TOP of the opening and carries load downward, the exact mirror of the sill.
  • Jamb: Similar because it is another framing member of doors and windows; differs because jambs are the vertical sides, so the distinction is orientation: sides versus bottom.
  • Shelf: Similar because a window sill is used as a shelf for pots and elbows; differs because a shelf is for storage anywhere on a wall, while a sill’s shelving is incidental to its framing duty.
  • Rocker panel: Similar because it names the same automotive strip below the doors; differs because it is the American trade term, technical and replaceable, while sill is the older general word.
  • Foundation beam / sole plate: Similar because the house sill is literally the building’s foundation timber; differs because those terms name whole-structure engineering, a scale larger than any single opening.
