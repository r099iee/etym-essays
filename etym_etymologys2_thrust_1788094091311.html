THRUST

Note: the verb was borrowed from Old Norse into Middle English in the thirteenth century (first recorded c. 1300). Old Norse þrysta is a weak verb, and the identical past form (thrust/thrust) is the result of English levelling of its forms, not of any ancient strong pattern. The often-suggested connection to PIE *trewd- "to push, press" — the family of Latin trūdō "to thrust" and Old English þrēat "pressure, threat" — rests on an irregular sound correspondence and is debated; no certain PIE stage can be reconstructed, so this chain honestly begins at Proto-Germanic.
