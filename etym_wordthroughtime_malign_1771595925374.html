- [14th century: the adjective, 'evil, malignant'] - 'Malign' first enters English as an adjective meaning evil, harmful, baleful in disposition; connotation is moral and sinister from the start.
- [17th century: the verbal turn] - The word shifts to the verb: to malign is to speak evil of, to defame with deliberate spite; connotation becomes an act - intentional, malicious utterance aimed at injuring a reputation.
- [Contemporary sense] - 'To malign' means to speak about someone in a spitefully critical or injurious manner, spreading reports intended to damage a name; connotation is that of a moral indictment - not a mere error of taste but an act of malice committed in the organs of opinion.
