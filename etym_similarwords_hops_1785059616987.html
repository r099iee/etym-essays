• Gruit: Similar because both are the flavoring and preserving agents of beer, and gruit was the herbal alternative that preceded hops; differs because gruit is a variable blend of herbs (bog myrtle, yarrow, rosemary, juniper) often guarded as a local secret, while hops are a single, standardized plant.
  • Wort: Similar because both are essential to the same brewing process; differs because wort is the sweet sugar-rich liquid to which hops are added in the boil, while hops are the flower cones that flavor it.
  • Alpha acids: Similar because both name what makes beer bitter; differs because alpha acids (humulone and relatives) are the specific chemical compounds inside the hop flower, while hops are the whole dried flower clusters that contain them.
  • Hop vine / bine: Similar because both belong to the same plant; differs because the vine is the living climbing plant (technically a bine, twining clockwise) that bears the cones, while hops are the harvested female flower clusters.
  • Lupulin: Similar because both belong to the hop's bittering chemistry; differs because lupulin is the yellow resinous powder in the glands of the cones that holds the active compounds, while hops are the whole cones themselves.
  • Catkin / strobile: Similar because both are botanical terms for the hop's flower form; differs because a catkin or strobile is the general botanical type of flower cluster, of which the hop cone is one example, while hops is the brewing word for the crop itself.
  • Yeast: Similar because both are essential beer ingredients alongside malt and water; differs because yeast performs the fermentation that produces alcohol and carbon dioxide, while hops add bitterness, aroma, and preservation but no alcohol.
  • Herb: Similar because both are plant materials used to flavor and preserve drink; differs because an herb is any aromatic plant used in food or medicine, while hops are specifically the bittering cones of a single species used in brewing.
