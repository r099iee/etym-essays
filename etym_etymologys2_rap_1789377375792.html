PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*(s)krep- / *krep-
"to rattle, crack, make a sharp noise" (imitative / onomatopoeic)
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE)
*hrap- / *rappa-
"to strike, knock sharply, snatch, seize quickly"
Note: An echoic, sound-symbolic formation imitating the abrupt, crisp percussive crack of a sudden impact; cognate with Swedish rappa ("to strike, beat"), Danish rappe, Middle Dutch rapen, and Old Norse hrapa ("to rush, fall headlong").
↓
Middle English (c. 1300–1350)
rappen (noun: rap)
"to strike with a quick blow, knock sharply"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
rap
"a quick sharp blow; to utter sharply or suddenly (e.g. to rap out an oath)"
Note: In 18th-century Ireland, rap was applied to counterfeit copper coins, either from the idea of a worthless stamped scrap or strike.
↓
Early to Mid-20th Century American English (c. 1900–1960s)
rap
"criminal charge or sentence (slang); to talk, converse fluently, boast rhythmically (AAVE)"
↓
Late 20th Century Hip-Hop Culture (1970s–present)
rap
"musical genre and performance of rhythmic rhyming speech over beats"
