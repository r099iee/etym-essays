Benign is an adjective meaning gentle, kindly, and favorable in disposition, and — crucially in modern usage — not harmful in effect. It is a word of quietness and safety: what is benign does not threaten, does not attack, does not spread. The word has two great families of sense: a moral-personal sense (a kindly, gracious nature or influence) and a technical-medical sense (a growth or condition that is not malignant). In the card's context sentence — "Most of humanity lives possessing a benign humour that keeps them on an even path" — the word is used in the older humoral register: a "humour" (here meaning a temperament or disposition, in the vocabulary of the four humours) that is mild, kind, and steadying, preventing people from veering into extremes. The sense is thus "kindly and gentle", the moral sense, applied to a constitutional disposition that keeps human life on an even course.

Sense 1 — Kindly, gentle, gracious (the moral sense). The primary meaning in general language: a benign person, ruler, influence, or disposition is kind, warm, and well-disposed. "A benign smile", "a benign ruler", "a benign climate of opinion". This sense descends from Latin benignus, "well-born, kind, good-natured", which itself fuses bene ("well") with the root of gignere ("to beget") — literally "born well", hence "of good nature".

Sense 2 — Not harmful in effect; favorable, auspicious. The second core sense: conditions, forces, or influences that are favorable and harmless. A "benign climate" is mild and healthful; a "benign outcome" is a good one; a "benign presence" does no harm. Here the word shades into "favorable" and "auspicious" without their ceremonial weight — it simply guarantees safety and mildness.

Sense 3 — The medical/technical sense: not malignant. In medicine, a "benign" tumor or growth is one that is not cancerous: it does not invade surrounding tissue, does not metastasize, and is generally non-life-threatening. This is the most famous technical use of the word, coined in opposition to malignant, and it dominates modern scientific and lay vocabulary — "the biopsy showed the tumor was benign". In this sense the word means "structurally harmless", stripped of moral colour.

Sense 4 — In the humoral context (the card's sense). In the sentence about the "benign humour", the word belongs to the pre-modern medical-psychological system of the four humours (blood, phlegm, choler, melancholy), where a person's temperament depended on the balance of humours. A "benign humour" is a mild, kindly, well-balanced temperament — the disposition that keeps most people "on an even path" between the extremes of fury and despair. Here benign is a moral-psychological term meaning gentle, kindly, and steady.

Polysemy, jargon, and metaphoric shifts.

  • Moral sense: kindly, gracious, gentle — of persons, rulers, natures, and influences.
  • Favorable sense: mild, auspicious, harmless — of climates, outcomes, conditions, and forces.
  • Medical jargon: non-malignant, structurally harmless — of tumors and growths; the dominant modern technical sense.
  • Humoral-psychological sense: a mild, well-balanced temperament that steadies behavior, as in the card's context.
  • Metaphoric shifts: "benign neglect" (a policy of deliberate non-interference, from 20th-century politics), "benign environment", "benign influence" — the word extends from medicine and morals into policy and ecology to mean harmless, well-meaning, or favorable through inaction.
  • Register and negative edge: benign can carry a faint condescension — harmlessness that is faintly dismissive ("merely benign", "benign neglect") — since it promises the absence of harm rather than positive good.

Semantic boundaries. Against kind, benign is more constitutional and less active — it describes an inherent disposition rather than a chosen act. Against harmless, benign is more dignified and medical. Against favorable, benign adds the guarantee of safety and good nature. The word's double life — kindly in morals, non-cancerous in medicine — is bridged by the shared idea of a nature that is well-born and does no harm.
