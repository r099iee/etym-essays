• Stone's throw: Similar because both name a short, human-cast distance; differs because a stone's throw is brief, informal, and conversational, while a bowshot is a warlike, near-ceremonial measure implying scale and consequence.
  • Arrow's flight: Similar because both describe the range a projectile covers; differs because arrow's flight emphasizes the missile rather than the weapon and is rarer, while bowshot is the fixed, idiomatic measure.
  • Rifle shot: Similar because both are weapon-based measures of range; differs because rifle shot is modern, far longer, and gunpowder-powered, while bowshot belongs to premodern archery.
  • Spear's cast: Similar because both are archaic weapon-distance measures; differs because a spear's cast is shorter, more impromptu, and less ceremonial than a carefully reckoned bowshot.
  • Furlong: Similar because both express a distance on a human or agricultural scale; differs because a furlong is a standardized legal unit (220 yards) tied to surveying and racing, while a bowshot was never officially standardized.
  • Range: Similar because both denote the reach of a weapon or projectile; differs because range is abstract, technical, and neutral, while bowshot is concrete, archaic, and atmospheric.
  • Distance: Similar because both name the amount of ground between two points; differs because distance is the general, measurable term, while bowshot is a picturesque unit derived from a specific weapon's power.
  • Cast (as in "a stone's cast"): Similar because both are expressions of a thrown distance; differs because a cast concerns hand-thrown objects and is shorter, while a bowshot concerns the bow's greater, more deliberate and trained power.
