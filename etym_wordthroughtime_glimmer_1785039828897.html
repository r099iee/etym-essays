- 13th-14th centuries - Middle English glimeren: to shine faintly and unsteadily, a light trembling on the edge of vision.
- 16th-18th centuries - the noun glimmer names a faint unsteady light; 'a glimmer of hope or reason' marks the smallest possible spark of understanding.
- 19th-20th centuries - the figurative sense flourishes: the first glimmer of dawn, of an idea, of danger kindling in the dark.
- Contemporary - glimmer still joins faintness, unsteadiness, and distance — a light seen far off through darkness.
