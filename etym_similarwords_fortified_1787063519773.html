• strengthened: Similar because both mean made stronger; differs because strengthened is the general everyday verb for any increase in strength (physical, moral, positional), while fortified implies deliberate reinforcement and defence — walls, nutrients, reserves — and has more specific, often technical flavour.
  • reinforced: Similar because both mean adding strength or support; differs because reinforced emphasises extra material or troops added to something already there (reinforced concrete, a reinforced battalion), while fortified emphasises building up defences; only fortified has the nutritional sense.
  • enriched: Similar because both describe adding beneficial substances to food; differs because in food regulation enriched usually means restoring nutrients lost in processing, while fortified means adding nutrients beyond natural levels; enriched also means "made wealthier or richer" and has no military meaning.
  • defended: Similar because both relate to protection against attack; differs because defended is about actively protecting people or places against danger (and can be non-structural: legal defence, sports defence), while fortified is about making structures or persons strong and secure; you defend a city, you fortify its walls.
  • secured: Similar because both suggest making safe; differs because secured focuses on fastening, locking down, or obtaining safety (and can mean "obtained": "secured a loan"), not on building strength.
  • buttressed: Similar because both involve structural support and supporting an argument or institution; differs because buttressed is specifically the support of a buttress wall (and metaphorically a supporting argument), narrower than full defensive fortification.
  • guarded: Similar because both relate to security; differs because guarded means watched over by guards or cautious in expression, a defensive posture rather than a build-up of strength.
  • impregnable: Similar because both describe defence (an "impregnable fortress"); differs because impregnable is an adjective meaning "unable to be captured," describing the outcome of good fortification, not the act of strengthening, and it is not used of food or finance.
  • armoured / armored: Similar because both suggest protection against attack; differs because armoured means covered with armour plating (vehicles, ships), a surface protection, while fortified means structurally strengthened and has the food and figurative senses.
  • embattled: Similar because both belong to medieval defence vocabulary; differs because embattled means "prepared with battlements" or "beset by difficulties / under attack," whereas fortified means strengthened and defended and is not limited to a state of being attacked.
