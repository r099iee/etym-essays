↓ Latin (2nd c. BCE)
mora
"a delay, a lingering"
↓ Latin (2nd c. BCE)
morārī
"to delay, tarry, linger"
↓ Latin (1st c. BCE)
dēmorārī
"to remain, stay, linger"
↓ Vulgar Latin (c. 3rd–6th c.)
*dēmorāre
"to remain, stay"
↓ Old French (10th c.)
demorer
"to stay, remain, dwell"
↓ Old French (12th c.)
demouré
"settled, grave, serious"
↓ Middle English (late 14th c.)
demure
"calm, settled, grave, serious"
↓ English (sense shift 16th–18th c.)
DEMURE
"reserved, modest, prim in manner"
Debate: Latin mora has no secure Indo-European etymology
(de Vaan: origin uncertain). The shift from "staying, remaining" to
"settled, grave, serious" and finally "prim, coyly modest" is well
attested, but the exact pathway is debated.
