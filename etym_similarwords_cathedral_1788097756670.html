• church: Similar because a cathedral is a church, the species within the genus; differs because church covers any Christian place of worship and its congregation, while cathedral specifically requires a bishop's cathedra and diocesan rank.
  • minster: Similar because several English cathedrals are styled minsters (York, Ripon, Westminster) and the word denotes a large collegiate or cathedral church; differs because minster is the native English term for a church of a monastery or chapter and no longer implies episcopal status.
  • basilica: Similar because it names a large hall-like church of special liturgical rank in the Catholic Church; differs because basilica is an architectural form (a long aisled hall) and a papal honour, not necessarily the seat of a bishop.
  • abbey: Similar because abbeys were often cathedral-sized, and many English cathedrals began as abbeys; differs because an abbey is governed by an abbot and a monastic community, while a cathedral is governed by a bishop and a chapter.
  • chapel: Similar because it is a Christian place of worship; differs because a chapel is small, subordinate and without a cathedra — the polar opposite of the cathedral's scale and diocesan rank.
  • temple: Similar because it names a monumental sacred building, comparable in grandeur; differs because temple belongs to non-Christian religions (Greek, Roman, Hindu, Buddhist, Jewish) and carries no concept of a bishop's seat.
  • shrine: Similar because great cathedrals are often shrines, housing relics and pilgrimage sites; differs because a shrine is a holy place of any size, sometimes no building at all, while cathedral names the ranked church itself.
  • duomo: Similar because duomo is the Italian word for cathedral, used of the great churches of Florence, Milan and Siena; differs because duomo is the Italian term for the same institution, not a separate English architectural category.
