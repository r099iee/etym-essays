faber

fabricāre

Old French (10th-12th c.)
forgier
"to forge (metal); to make, build; to falsify"
(from Vulgar Latin fabricare; the medial cluster
-bric- is reshaped as -rg- in Gallo-Romance)
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
forgen
"to shape metal by fire and hammer;
to fashion; to invent, fabricate"
↓
Modern English (present)
FORGING
"the act of shaping metal; creating; counterfeiting"

Note: the phrase "forge ahead" (18th c.) belongs to a second, obscure English verb probably connected with "force"; it is not part of this chain. Because no secure PIE etymon for Latin faber is established, the chain begins at the first attested stage rather than inventing a root - a documented gap, stated honestly.
