• Residents: Similar because both mean people who live in a place; differs because "residents" is the modern administrative and legal word stressing formal domicile (as in "residents' association"), while "inhabitants" is the older, more neutral and demographic term for mere dwellers.
  • Citizens: Similar because both denote people belonging to a place; differs because "citizens" stresses legal status, rights, and civic membership, whereas "inhabitants" requires only residence — many inhabitants of the Roman empire were not full citizens.
  • Populace / population: Similar because both name the people of a place as a collective; differs because "populace" and "population" are statistical and impersonal masses, while "inhabitants" remains a countable plural of individual dwellers.
  • Natives: Similar because both denote people associated with a place; differs because "natives" stresses birthplace and can exclude settlers or incomers, while "inhabitants" includes anyone who lives there.
  • Dwellers: Similar because both mean people who live in a place; differs because "dwellers" is more archaic-poetic and thinner (cave-dwellers, city-dwellers), while "inhabitants" is the standard neutral formal term.
  • Occupants: Similar because both mean those present in a place; differs because "occupants" stresses physical presence or occupation (of a house, vehicle, seat) and can be temporary, while "inhabitants" implies habitual residence.
