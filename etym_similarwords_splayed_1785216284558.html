• spread: Similar in covering an area by extension; differs because spread is the broad, neutral term without any implication of flattening or pressing against a surface.
  • outstretched: Similar in limbs extended outward; differs because outstretched implies deliberate reaching toward something, with direction and intention, where splayed suggests wide, flat, animal positioning.
  • extended: Similar in being lengthened or spread out; differs because extended is the colourless general word for making something longer or broader, lacking splayed’s forced-wide, pressed-flat force.
  • fanned: Similar in spreading parts apart; differs because fanning implies an ordered radial arrangement, as of fingers or cards, not the flattened, sprawling pressure of splayed.
  • sprawled: Similar in a loose, spread-out posture; differs because sprawling is a carelessly flung, untidy extension of the whole body, while splayed emphasizes limbs forced wide and flat with deliberate or feral weight.
  • flattened: Similar in being pressed flat; differs because flattening is the making level of any surface, without splayed’s outward spreading and parting of the parts.
  • straddled: Similar in limbs positioned wide apart; differs because straddling is the standing astride of the legs, chiefly of the lower limbs, without the whole-body, ground-hugging spread of splayed.
  • separated: Similar in parts being moved apart; differs because separating is the neutral division of things formerly together, without the wide, flat, spread-wide posture of splayed.
  • opened out: Similar in unfolding from a closed state; differs because opening out is the general act of unfolding, without splayed’s specificity of limbs or parts forced outward and flattened.
  • spread-eagled: Similar in limbs spread wide and flat; differs because spread-eagled names the specific, symmetrical star-shape of all four limbs, while splayed covers any outward-flattened extension, often of a single hand or pair of hands.
  • widened: Similar in being made broader; differs because widening is the increase of breadth in any direction, especially architectural and abstract, without the flattened contact or limb-spread of splayed.
  • broadened: Similar in an increase of extent; differs because broadening is a general increase in width or scope, neutral and figurative, lacking splayed’s tactile, pressed-down spread.
