- Antiquity (c. 4th c. BCE – 1st c. CE) — Terror and conquest: Latin Gallī designated the formidable Celtic tribes of northern Italy and transalpine Europe who sacked Rome in 390 BCE and were eventually subjugated by Caesar.
- Medieval Era (c. 6th–14th c.) — Germanic overlay and linguistic redirection: Following Frankish dominance, the Germanic word *Walhaz merged with classical memory, emerging in Old French as Gaule to denote the historic pre-Frankish land.
- 16th–17th c. — Renaissance revival: Reintroduced into Early Modern English as Gauls through translations of classical Roman historians and French humanists exploring ancient national origins.
- 18th–19th c. — National myth-making and historiography: Adopted in Britain by historians like Hume to trace British migration, and embraced in post-revolutionary France as the foundational democratic ancestry (*Nos ancêtres les Gaulois*).
- 20th–21st c. — Cultural icon and modern metonym: Maintained in academic history as the precise term for continental Iron Age Celts, while celebrated worldwide in pop culture (e.g. Asterix) as a symbol of plucky French resistance.
