• Pantry: Similar because both name a room or cupboard for storing food; differs because historically a pantry (from Latin panis "bread") held bread, dry goods, and linens, while a larder (from Latin lardum "pork fat") held meat and cold provisions — though modern usage merges the two.
  • Cupboard: Similar because both can name a piece of furniture for storing food; differs because a cupboard is an item of furniture in any room for any goods, while a larder was traditionally a whole cool room for provisions.
  • Store-room / Storeroom: Similar because both name a room for keeping supplies; differs because a store-room is general (anything from tools to archives), while a larder is specifically about food, especially meat and cold provisions.
  • Storehouse: Similar because both name a place of stored supplies; differs because a storehouse is large and general, often commercial or institutional, while a larder is domestic and food-specific.
  • Cellar: Similar because both name a cool place for storing goods; differs because a cellar is specifically underground and holds wine, coal, or root vegetables, while a larder is usually above ground and holds daily provisions.
  • Root cellar: Similar because both are cool storage spaces for food; differs because a root cellar is underground and specialized for vegetables that keep through winter, while a larder holds everyday perishables like meat and dairy.
  • Buttery: Similar because both are domestic storerooms in old houses; differs because a buttery (from Anglo-Norman buterie, for casks) stored drink and bottled provisions, while a larder stored meat and solid food.
  • Spence: Similar because both are old household offices for storing provisions; differs because spence (from Latin dispensa, the "expenditure" room) was a general store-room, now archaic or dialectal, while larder survives in everyday use.
  • Scullery: Similar because both belong to the old domestic suite of service rooms; differs because a scullery was the washing-up and rough-work room, not a food store, while a larder is purely for provisions.
  • Cold store / Cold room: Similar because both name a cool place for keeping food; differs because a cold store is industrial and mechanical, while a larder is a domestic room that relied on position and ventilation, not machinery.
  • Meat safe: Similar because both store food needing coolness and air; differs because a meat safe is a small ventilated box or cupboard, while a larder is a whole room.
  • Fridge / Refrigerator: Similar because both keep food cold for storage; differs because a fridge is a modern mechanical appliance that made the old larder unnecessary, while a larder is the pre-refrigeration room or, today, a food cupboard.
