Interpolator is an agent noun denoting an individual who corrupts, alters, or falsifies a text, manuscript, legal record, or historical document by inserting unauthorized, alien, or spurious material between the existing lines or sections. In technical domains such as mathematics, data science, and digital signal processing, an interpolator is a mathematical algorithm, software module, or hardware circuit designed to calculate, estimate, or construct intermediate data points falling between known discrete values. In the source passage—Jonathan Swift's scathing 1735 Preface to Gulliver's Travels—Lemuel Gulliver denounces the unauthorized editorial meddling that ruined the original 1726 publication: ‘But you, or your interpolator, ought to have considered, that it was not my inclination, so was it not decent to praise any animal of our composition before my master Houyhnhnm...’ Here, Gulliver vents his fury at Benjamin Motte, the London publisher, who, terrified of government prosecution for treasonous satire, hired an anonymous hack or editor—an interpolator—to water down Swift's savage political attacks, remove offensive satire against the Whig prime minister Robert Walpole, and insert fawning, patriotic praise for the late Queen Anne.

Polysemy and Key Operational Domains: The word spans philological, scientific, and figurative domains, bound together by the underlying concept of introducing new elements into an existing continuum:

  • Classical Philology and Textual Criticism (The Primary Literary Sense): In the study of ancient, medieval, and early modern texts, an interpolator is an editor, copyist, scribe, or ideologue who inserts unauthentic words, explanatory glosses, or entirely fabricated passages into an author's original manuscript. Scribes frequently interpolated material either unintentionally (when a marginal gloss written by an earlier reader was mistakenly copied into the body text by a subsequent copyist) or deliberately (to introduce doctrinal orthodoxy, sanitize political subversion, or claim apostolic authority for later theological dogmas). Textual critics employ rigorous stylistic, metrical, and philological analysis to unmask the hand of the interpolator and purge the text back to its pure archetype.
  • Mathematics, Numerical Analysis, and Computing: In computational mathematics, an interpolator is a function, algorithm, or mathematical apparatus that constructs a continuous curve or surface passing through a discrete set of known data points, allowing one to estimate unknown intermediate values (e.g., linear interpolators, Lagrange polynomial interpolators, and cubic spline interpolators). The conceptual bridge connects text and mathematics: just as a scribal interpolator inserts words between lines of text, a mathematical interpolator inserts numerical coordinates between known data points.
  • Digital Signal Processing, Audio, and Video Engineering: In telecommunications, audio synthesis, and display technology, an interpolator is a digital filter or upsampling engine that increases the sampling rate of a signal by calculating intermediate signal values. In modern televisions and gaming monitors, motion estimation/motion compensation (MEMC) interpolators and AI neural networks generate intermediate video frames to convert 24fps or 60fps video into fluid 120fps display.
  • Figurative and Conversational Discourse: In conversational and social contexts, an interpolator refers to an intruder or meddler who interrupts an ongoing discussion, injecting uninvited comments, objections, or digressions into another person's speech or narrative.

Register and Connotative Value: In literary, political, and academic prose, interpolator carries a heavily derogatory, accusatory connotation. It implies cowardice, intellectual dishonesty, political sycophancy, or ideological vandalism. To accuse someone of being an interpolator is not merely to criticize their editing; it is to charge them with betraying the author's voice and desecrating historical truth. In scientific and engineering contexts, by contrast, the word is entirely neutral, technical, and complimentary, designating precision mathematical tools that restore continuity to sampled information.
