- c. 700s–1100s: Old English strong verb *wadan* meant broadly "to go, walk, advance, step forward, penetrate" (used for general movement or advancing into battle).
- 1200s–1400s: Middle English *waden* narrowed significantly from general walking to specifically moving through water, mud, marshland, or deep snow that impedes the feet.
- 1500s–1650s: Expanded into figurative use meaning to enter into, engage with, or penetrate deeply into a difficult subject, controversy, or hazardous enterprise.
- 1800s–Present: Solidified dual usage: literally walking through shallow waters, and metaphorically toiling through dense paperwork, bureaucratic red tape, or complex data ("wading through archives").
