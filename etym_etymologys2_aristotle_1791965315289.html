Notes: the Greek name is a compound of ἄριστος “best” and τέλος “end, purpose”. ἄριστος is the superlative of ἀγαθός “good”; its deeper origin is uncertain and may be pre-Greek, so no PIE stage is asserted here. τέλος is often connected with PIE *kʷel- “to turn”, the end being the point at which something has “come round” to completion, but the semantic development is debated. Whether the compound means “best end”, “best at the end” or “of the best purpose” is likewise unsettled. The name was a common Greek aristocratic name, not a coinage for the philosopher.
