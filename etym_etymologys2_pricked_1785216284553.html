No accepted PIE root — the family is Germanic-only and of uncertain, likely expressive (echoic) origin: Proto-Germanic *prik- "point, pointed object." Older attempts to posit a PIE *prek- root have been rejected by the OED; state this explicitly. → Proto-West Germanic *prikōjan "to prick, pierce, mark with a point" (denominative verb built on *prik-) → Old English prician "to prick, sting, pierce" (beside the noun prica "point, puncture, prickle, sting") → Middle English prikken / prick(e)n "to stab, sting, pierce; to incite, spur" → English prick (verb) "to pierce or mark with a sharp point" → PRICKED (simple past and past participle) → idiom "to prick up one's ears" (attested from the 1590s) Note on debated/nuanced points: The OED and standard handbooks treat the pr- family as of uncertain origin, probably expressive (the sharp onset miming a sudden jab), with cognates confined to West Germanic — Middle Dutch pricken, Dutch prikken, Low German pricken — and no demonstrable Romance or Indo-Aryan relatives. Older attempts to link the word to a PIE *prek- are rejected. The noun prica and the verb prician appear together in Old English; the noun is attested earliest, with the verb formed from it (or in parallel) within the same period.
