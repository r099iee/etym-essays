• Balt: A clipped, informal noun for a Baltic person or the region; identical in reference but colloquial and sometimes perceived as loose.
  • The Baltics: The informal collective for the three states; same political referent, but more journalistic and less formal than “the Baltic states.”
  • Baltic states: The standard political phrase for Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania; similar, but it excludes the sea and the language family.
  • Baltic languages: The linguistic branch (Lithuanian, Latvian, Old Prussian); narrower than the geographical sense and independent of state borders.
  • Balto-Slavic: The wider proposed family including Slavic; similar as a linguistic grouping, but broader and theoretically contested.
  • Baltic-Finnic: The Finnic branch including Estonian and Finnish; similar geographically, but unrelated to the Baltic languages and often confused with them.
  • Baltic provinces: The imperial Russian administrative units; similar territory, but a historical and colonial frame rather than a national one.
  • Baltic Sea: The body of water itself; similar as the source of the name, but strictly geographical.
  • Lettish: An older English name for Latvian; similar as a Baltic language label, but archaic and now generally replaced.
  • Baltic Shield: The geological formation; similar in name and region, but a technical term in geology.
