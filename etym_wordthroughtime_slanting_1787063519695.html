- c. 1300 - Middle English slenten to slope, slip sideways - purely physical, neutral description of incline or oblique motion
- 1520s - slant (verb) to strike or fall obliquely - gains the sense of a blow or movement hitting at an angle
- 1650s - slant (noun) a slope, oblique direction - the word extends to the direction itself, still spatial
- 18th-19th c. - slanting becomes a standard adjective in landscape writing for hills, roofs, and light (Tolkien's slanting away from the River); also used of handwriting
- 1830s onwards - figurative a point of view, bias, or angle of presentation (journalistic slant) - the word shifts from geometry into rhetoric and opinion, acquiring a mildly negative tone
- Modern - slanting keeps both spatial and figurative uses; the adjective remains strongly physical, while the noun slant carries the loaded sense of media bias
