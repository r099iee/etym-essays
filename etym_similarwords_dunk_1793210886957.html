• Dip: Similar because both mean a brief immersion in liquid; differs because a dip is the neutral general word — sheep-dip, lucky dip — while dunk is heartier, suggesting food, force, and a satisfying plunge.
  • Plunge: Similar because both describe thrusting something under the surface; differs because plunge stresses sudden deep descent, often dramatic or desperate, while dunk is quick, shallow, and usually cheerful.
  • Submerge: Similar because both put things underwater; differs because submerge is the technical term for full sinking below the surface, while dunk is momentary — in and straight back out.
  • Douse: Similar because both wet things thoroughly and suddenly; differs because douse pours or throws liquid over the object, while dunk pushes the object down into the liquid.
  • Slam: Similar because a slam dunk is slammed home; differs because slam names violent impact and noise in any direction, while dunk names directed downward scoring through a hoop or into a cup.
  • Soak: Similar because both involve liquid entering food or cloth; differs because soak is prolonged saturation — beans overnight — while dunk is the three-second dip, deliberately stopped short of sogginess.
  • Slam dunk (noun phrase): Similar because it is the full basketball form of the verb; differs because the phrase has hardened into an idiom for a certainty — a slam-dunk case — while the bare verb still does the physical work.
