PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*men-
"to remain, stay, abide"
↓
Latin (3rd–2nd c. BCE)
manēre
"to remain, stay, endure"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
remanēre
"to stay behind, be left over"
↓
Latin present participle
remanēns
"remaining, that which is left"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
remanant
"the remainder, the rest"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
remenaunt
"the rest, that which is left"
↓
Modern English (16th c.)
remnant
"small remaining part; leftover cloth"

Note: "remainder" is a doublet via Old French remeindre; the adjective "remanent" (physics) is a 19th-century reborrowing of the Latin participle.
