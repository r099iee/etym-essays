Note on historical debate and folk etymology: The literal morphological structure of Bharadvāja is transparently an archaic governing compound (*aluk-samāsa*) comprising the participial stem bharat ("bearing, carrying") and the substantive vāja ("vigor, energy, sacrificial wealth, speed"), meaning "he who brings vigor or carries off the prize of the sacrifice." However, ancient Purāṇic commentators and the Nirukta of Yāska recorded a famous folk etymology derived from a mythological adoption narrative (narrated in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa and Bhāgavata Purāṇa). When the pregnant Mamatā was approached by Bṛhaspati, the resulting infant was abandoned by both parents, who said to one another: bhara dvā-jam ("maintain this child of two fathers"). The child was rescued by the Maruts and adopted by King Bharata, hence acquiring the name Bharadvāja. Modern comparative philologists (such as Macdonell, Mayrhofer, and Witzel) demonstrate that this mythological narrative is a back-formation designed to explain the archaic Vedic compound through popular etymological wordplay.
