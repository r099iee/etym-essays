• Pulling: Similar because both mean applying force to draw something toward oneself; differs because pulling is the broad generic verb, while hauling specifically implies something heavy and a sustained, effortful draw over distance.
  • Dragging: Similar because both involve drawing something along; differs because dragging emphasises the object’s friction against the ground or surface and implies difficulty, while hauling is more neutral about contact and is often done by rope, cable, or vehicle.
  • Towing: Similar because both mean drawing a vehicle or vessel; differs because towing specifically means pulling by rope or chain behind a moving vehicle or boat, a technical sense that hauling has only in looser idiom.
  • Tugging: Similar because both are pulling actions; differs because tugging means repeated short, sharp pulls, whereas hauling is a long, steady, powerful effort.
  • Yanking: Similar because both are forceful pulls; differs because yanking is a single sudden violent jerk, while hauling is sustained and controlled.
  • Heaving: Similar because both involve great physical effort; differs because heaving often means lifting and throwing, or the rhythmic push-pull of work (“heave-ho”), whereas hauling is purely drawing or conveying.
  • Wrenching: Similar because both are forceful actions; differs because wrenching implies twisting violently or tearing away, whereas hauling is a straight line of pull.
  • Carting: Similar because both involve transporting burdens; differs because carting specifically means carrying by cart or vehicle and is informal about bulk movement, while hauling stresses the pulling effort itself.
  • Conveying: Similar because both involve moving things; differs because conveying is neutral, technical, and often used of passengers or information, carrying no implication of effort or heavy weight.
  • Trailing: Similar because both draw something along; differs because trailing is passive or gentle (something follows behind), whereas hauling is active and effortful.
