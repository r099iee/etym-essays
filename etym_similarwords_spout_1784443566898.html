• Spurt: Similar because both name a sudden jet or stream of fluid forced through an opening; differs because spurt emphasizes the short, abrupt burst, while spout can also name the fixed fixture (a kettle's lip) or a sustained stream.
  • Spray: Similar because both name liquid ejected under force; differs because spray is dispersed as fine droplets over an area, while spout is a compact directed stream.
  • Jet: Similar because both name a forceful stream of liquid; differs because jet is technical and implies high pressure from a narrow nozzle, while spout covers simple vessels' lips and gentle pours.
  • Gush: Similar because both name a copious outflow of liquid; differs because gush emphasizes volume and abruptness, while spout emphasizes the channel or fixture directing the flow.
  • Stream: Similar because both name a flow of liquid; differs because stream is a general word for any flowing body, while spout names a forced, directed, or ejected stream.
  • Spew: Similar because both mean to eject fluid violently, and both descend from the same spit/spew family; differs because spew is coarse, suggests the mouth or disgust, and has no noun sense naming a fixture.
  • Nozzle: Similar because both name the end-piece through which liquid is discharged; differs because nozzle is strictly mechanical and technical, while spout can be the fixture or the stream itself.
  • Pipe: Similar because both can be tubes that carry liquid; differs because pipe is the long conduit, while spout is the short discharging mouth at the end of a conduit or vessel.
  • Discharge: Similar because both mean to release or emit a flow; differs because discharge is formal and general, while spout is concrete, imitative, and can be a noun for the fixture.
  • Declaim: Similar because both describe pouring out speech at length (figurative spout); differs because declaim is a formal word for rhetorical delivery, while spout is colloquial and slightly disparaging.
  • Tap: Similar because both can be the controlled mouth of a vessel; differs because tap implies a valve for starting and stopping, while spout is the open lip that directs the pour.
  • Torrent: Similar because both can name a violent rush of liquid; differs because torrent is vast and uncontrolled, while spout is localized, directed, and often small.
