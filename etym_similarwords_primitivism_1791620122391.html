• Atavism: Similar because both involve a reversion to ancestral, primeval, or prehistoric traits; differs because atavism is primarily a biological or evolutionary throwback—the spontaneous re-emergence of an ancestral physical or genetic trait—or an uncontrollable instinctual regression, whereas primitivism is a conscious intellectual doctrine, artistic aesthetic, or cultural philosophy.
  • Pastoralism: Similar because both idealize a simpler, unhurried life in close communion with nature away from corrupt cities; differs because pastoralism specifically romanticizes the gentle, idyllic, civilized life of rural shepherds and farmsteads, whereas primitivism seeks the raw, untamed, wild, and pre-civilized primeval state.
  • Romanticism: Similar because both celebrate emotional intensity, nature, and the rejection of sterile mechanistic rationalism; differs because Romanticism is a vast, comprehensive artistic and philosophical epoch encompassing individualism, the sublime, medievalism, and nationalism, whereas primitivism focuses specifically on idealizing archaic, tribal, and early human origins.
  • Nativism: Similar because both prioritize indigenous or foundational modes of existence over imported modern systems; differs because nativism in political discourse refers to the protection of native-born inhabitants over immigrants, or in philosophy to innate cognitive ideas, while primitivism is the glorification of primeval simplicity.
  • Anti-modernism: Similar because both reject the technologies, commercialism, and urban alienation of contemporary modern life; differs because anti-modernism is a broad, reactive stance that can include traditional religious conservatism or feudal nostalgia, whereas primitivism specifically locates ideal virtue in hunter-gatherer or prehistoric life.
  • Naivety / Naiveté: Similar because both embody an unstudied, childlike, and unsophisticated approach to expression or reality; differs because naivety is an uncalculated, innocent lack of experience or awareness, while primitivism is often a calculated, deliberate, and sophisticated artistic posture.
  • Rousseauism: Similar because both advocate the natural goodness of primeval humanity uncorrupted by societal institutions; differs because Rousseauism refers specifically to the philosophical, educational, and political doctrines of Jean-Jacques Rousseau, while primitivism spans ancient Greco-Roman myths, modern avant-garde painting, and contemporary ecological anarchism.
