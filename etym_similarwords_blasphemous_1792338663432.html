• sacrilegious: Similar because both involve gross disrespect toward that which is holy; differs because sacrilegious specifically involves the physical desecration, theft, or profane violation of tangible sacred objects, holy places, or consecrated sacraments (e.g., vandalizing an altar, stealing communion vessels), whereas blasphemous centers primarily on verbal, written, or expressive contempt directed against God's nature or sacred truth.
  • profane: Similar because both indicate a lack of reverence for sacred things; differs because profane basically means non-sacred, secular, or common (literally "outside the temple"), often manifested through vulgar swear words, crude street language, or mundane worldly concerns, whereas blasphemous implies an active, hostile defiance and malicious repudiation of the divine.
  • impious: Similar because both denote an absence of religious piety; differs because impious emphasizes a passive lack of dutiful reverence, filial respect, or moral conscience toward God, parents, or country, whereas blasphemous represents an aggressive, explosive verbal transgression.
  • heretical: Similar because both provoke religious condemnation and ecclesiastical censure; differs because heretical denotes the formal intellectual belief in, or teaching of, theological doctrines that contradict established church dogma while still claiming to belong to the faith (e.g., Arianism), whereas blasphemous consists of offensive, scurrilous, or mocking insult toward the sacred.
  • irreverent: Similar because both show a disregard for solemn authority; differs because irreverent operates at a much milder emotional register, suggesting a cheeky, playful, or satirical lack of respect toward pompous traditions, royalty, or social conventions without malicious hatred or severe moral transgression.
  • ungodly: Similar because both describe behavior contrary to religious law; differs because ungodly is a general moral condemnation signifying sinful, wicked, or worldly living (or colloquially, unreasonable times like "an ungodly hour"), lacking the direct rhetorical attack on sacred majesty that characterizes blasphemous.
  • desecrating: Similar because both pollute that which is venerated; differs because desecrating is an active participial term emphasizing the physical defilement, degradation, or ruin of a shrine, monument, tomb, or sacred ritual.
  • iconoclastic: Similar because both attack venerated beliefs and idols; differs because iconoclastic carries a positive or intellectually daring connotation in modern prose, describing a brave thinker who dismantles outdated dogmas, cherished illusions, or conventional wisdom, whereas blasphemous carries a dark, transgressive stain of moral violation.
  • defamatory: Similar because historically both involved injurious speech; differs because defamatory is a legal and civil term concerning false statements that harm the reputation of a living mortal human or corporate entity, whereas blasphemous concerns insulting the majesty of God or inviolable sacred truths.
  • insolent: Similar because both convey arrogant, contemptuous defiance; differs because insolent refers to rude, insubordinate, and disrespectful behavior displayed by an inferior toward a human superior or social authority, without inherently touching upon religious sanctity.
  • scandalous: Similar because both provoke widespread public outrage and moral horror; differs because scandalous focuses on behavior that shocks public decency through financial corruption, sexual impropriety, or betrayal of social decorum, rather than religious profanation.
  • abusive: Similar because ancient Greek blasphemia meant verbal abuse; differs because modern abusive refers broadly to cruel, violent, or insulting treatment directed against people, animals, or institutional power, lacking the specific sacred target that defines blasphemous.
