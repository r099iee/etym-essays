• Moisture: Similar because both refer to liquid diffused or condensed in small quantities; differs because moisture is a general, neutral term for dampness or liquid in any form or substance, whereas dew specifically denotes nocturnal atmospheric condensation forming droplets on exposed surfaces.
  • Condensation: Similar because condensation is the exact thermodynamic process that generates dew; differs because condensation is a clinical, scientific term applying to any vapor turning to liquid on cold surfaces (like steam on a window pane), whereas dew is the natural, outdoor meteorological phenomenon laden with poetic associations of freshness.
  • Frost (Hoarfrost): Similar because both are nocturnal atmospheric deposits on outdoor vegetation; differs because frost occurs when the dew point is below the freezing mark, causing water vapor to sublimate directly into delicate ice crystals rather than liquid droplets.
  • Precipitation: Similar because both deliver atmospheric water to the earth's surface; differs because precipitation falls from clouds through the atmosphere as rain, sleet, or snow, whereas dew condenses in situ directly upon ground surfaces without falling from overhead clouds.
  • Mist: Similar because mist and dew frequently occur together in cool, humid conditions; differs because mist consists of microscopic water droplets suspended aloft in the air near the ground (reducing visibility), whereas dew consists of droplets deposited onto solid terrestrial surfaces.
  • Rime: Similar because both form on exposed outdoor objects overnight; differs because rime is a rough, opaque white coating of ice formed by the rapid freezing of supercooled fog droplets, whereas dew forms as clear liquid water above 0°C.
