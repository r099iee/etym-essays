• cradled: Similar because both involve holding an object or person with protective gentleness and curved support; differs because cradled emphasizes rocking, nurturing, and infant-like security within the crook of an arm or both hands, whereas cupped focuses specifically on the hollowed, concave geometric shape of the palms or a vessel.
  • hollowed: Similar because both describe a sunken, concave three-dimensional form; differs because hollowed often implies an empty cavity created by carving, excavation, or wasting disease (e.g., hollowed cheeks, hollowed logs), whereas cupped implies a functional, bowl-like curvature formed purposefully to retain contents.
  • curved: Similar because both indicate deviation from a flat plane; differs because curved is a general geometric descriptor applicable to any bend, line, or arc in one or two dimensions, whereas cupped specifically requires a multidirectional concave depression capable of acting like a basin.
  • scooped: Similar because both involve forming a concave surface to gather material; differs because scooped carries dynamic, kinetic action—digging down and lifting out fluid or loose matter (often with a tool or spoon)—while cupped describes the static, receptive configuration of the hands or object.
  • clasped: Similar because both describe hands engaged in holding or ritual posture; differs because clasped involves interlocking fingers, gripping tightly, or closing firmly around an object, whereas cupped leaves the palms open, receptive, and curved into a hollow without interlocked fingers.
  • sheltered: Similar because both imply shielding something delicate from external elements (e.g., wind or rain); differs because sheltered focuses on the environmental protection itself, which can be accomplished by walls, roofs, or overhanging rocks, whereas cupped describes the precise physical container formed by curved hands.
  • enfolded: Similar because both convey wrapping around an object; differs because enfolded suggests complete wrapping, draping, or enveloping with fabric, arms, or darkness, whereas cupped provides an open-topped concave support.
  • arched: Similar because both refer to curved bodily postures or structural forms; differs because arched usually describes a convex upward bend (e.g., an arched back, an arched bridge), while cupped is strictly concave, creating a bowl or well.
  • concave: Similar because both designate an indented, hollowed surface curved inward; differs because concave is a detached, abstract mathematical and optical term (e.g., a concave lens), lacking the warm, organic, tactile, and gestural resonance of cupped.
  • held: Similar because both describe the retention of an object in one's hands; differs because held is generic and neutral, conveying nothing about the shape, curvature, gentleness, or reverent posture of the hands.
