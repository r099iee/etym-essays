• Muted: The closest modern cousin; differs because muted is usually abstract, applied to sound or colour ("muted tones"), while muffled keeps the physical image of cloth wrapped around a thing or a voice, with concealment built in.
  • Wrapped: Similar as the plain physical core of covering; differs because wrapped says nothing of concealment or of sound — one can be wrapped in a towel without being muffled.
  • Enveloped: Similar in being completely closed over; differs because enveloped suggests something vast (fog, a mystery) with a dramatic, almost abstract ring, dropping the cloth-and-sound image that muffled combines.
  • Swaddled: Similar in being wrapped tightly in cloth; differs because swaddled implies infant-like binding and a tender, helpless overtone, whereas muffled also deadens sound and conceals form.
  • Concealed / Disguised: Similar in hiding a person's identity; differs because these name only the purpose and drop the fabric and the sound entirely — one can be concealed behind a screen without being muffled.
  • Hushed: Similar in describing quiet, indistinct sound; differs because hushed is a state of deliberate or natural silence, while muffled is a sound deadened by a wrapping layer — the image of cloth remains.
  • Stifled / Smothered: Similar in suppressing sound; differs because these add a sense of difficulty, suffocation, or restraint (a stifled sob), while muffled is softer and purely about the wrapping layer that absorbs noise.
  • Deadened / Dulled: Similar in reducing the intensity of sound; differs because deadened is mechanical and blunt, while muffled keeps the tactile image of padding and cloth, and is gentler.
  • Deafened: The false friend; differs because deafened means made unable to hear, while muffled means made hard to hear — the subject is quieter, not the hearer impaired.
