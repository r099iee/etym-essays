• Applause: Similar because both denote favourable public reception; differs because applause is the most concrete — the clapping of hands — while acclamation is specifically vocal, a shout or cheer, and carries a ceremonial or political dimension (a motion passed ‘by acclamation’ is approved by unanimous voice).
  • Ovation: Similar because both denote enthusiastic public approval; differs because ovation is prolonged, usually standing applause (from the Roman ovatio, the lesser triumph), while acclamation is the vocal cry of the crowd, and ovation lacks the unanimous, vote-like formality of an approval carried by acclamation.
  • Plaudits: Similar because both mean praise or approval for an achievement; differs because plaudits is a literary plural meaning praise generally (from Latin plaudere, ‘to clap’), often of critics or the press, while acclamation emphasises the loud, collective, vocal expression of a crowd or assembly.
  • Approbation: Similar because both denote approval; differs because approbation is official, formal, even administrative approval — cool and considered — while acclamation is warm, loud, spontaneous, and collective, the heat of the crowd rather than the nod of authority.
  • Acclaim: Similar because both denote enthusiastic praise; differs because acclaim is the shorter, less ceremonious form — it can be individual and informal, enthusiastic praise or renown (a critically acclaimed novel) — while acclamation is the collective, shouted, ceremonial act of a body.
  • Cheers: Similar because both are vocal shouts of approval; differs because cheers is the plain, everyday plural for shouts of encouragement or support (often at sporting events), while acclamation is the formal, ceremonial, near-constitutional shout of a crowd, senate, or assembly.
  • Praise: Similar because both denote approval or commendation; differs because praise is the broad, neutral term for expressing admiration — of a person, a work, or God — while acclamation specifies the loud, collective, spontaneous shout of approval, public and ceremonial rather than private.
  • Tribute: Similar because both honour a person; differs because tribute is a formal mark of respect — a speech, gift, or monument — often posthumous or ceremonial, while acclamation is the living, spontaneous shout of a present crowd.
  • Salvo: Similar because both can describe a burst of collective approval; differs because salvo is literally a simultaneous discharge of guns or a burst of applause — technical and brief — while acclamation is a sustained shout of a body, and salvo lacks the vote-like and ceremonial weight of acclamation.
  • Kudos: Similar because both denote praise; differs because kudos (from Greek, ‘glory’) is informal, modern, and individual — credit or prestige for a job — while acclamation is formal, collective, and ceremonial.
