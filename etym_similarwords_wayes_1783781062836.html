• Paths: Similar because both denote routes or courses from one place to another; differs because path emphasizes a physical or metaphorical narrow track with a clear beginning and end, while way is a broader concept embracing method, direction, conduct, and manner, not only a track.
  • Roads: Similar because both can name a physical route for travel; differs because road is a constructed thoroughfare for vehicles, whereas way is the more general word for a route, course, or method, and lacks the concrete engineering sense.
  • Routes: Similar because both can name a course taken to reach a place; differs because route stresses a planned itinerary or fixed path of travel, while way extends far beyond travel to manners, customs, and means.
  • Methods: Similar because both name ways of doing something; differs because method stresses a systematic, deliberate procedure, whereas way can mean natural, habitual, or even casual action without system.
  • Courses: Similar because both can mean a line or direction of action; differs because course highlights a formal progression over time (a course of study, a course of events), while way is the everyday word for manner or route.
  • Manners: Similar because both can mean customary modes of behaviour; differs because manners emphasizes etiquette and polite conduct, whereas ways names the full range of habits and customs, including reproachable ones ("his wicked ways").
  • Means: Similar because both can denote a method or resource by which something is achieved; differs because means emphasizes instruments, resources, or agency, while way emphasizes the manner or route, and only way retains the physical "path" sense.
  • Customs: Similar because both can name established modes of behaviour; differs because custom denotes the institutionalized practice of a group, whereas ways can refer to an individual's habits and to divine providential dealings, without the institutional weight.
