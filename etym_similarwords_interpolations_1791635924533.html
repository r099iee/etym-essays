• extrapolations: Similar because both represent mathematical and cognitive methods of estimating unknown data; differs because interpolations estimate values strictly *inside* the range of known data points, whereas extrapolations project inferences *outside* and beyond the known range into uncharted territory.
  • insertions: Similar because both denote placing foreign material or extra items between existing parts; differs because insertions is a broad, neutral physical or grammatical descriptor, whereas interpolations carries specialized textual-critical, mathematical, musical, and epistemological weight.
  • interpolators: Similar because both relate to the process of textual or mathematical interpolation; differs because interpolators refers to the human agents, scribes, or computational algorithms performing the action, whereas interpolations denotes the actual passages, values, or acts produced.
  • corruptions: Similar because in textual philology, interpolations are frequently viewed as unauthorized alterations; differs because corruptions can result from accidental scribal decay, illegibility, or physical damage, whereas interpolations are deliberate insertions of supplementary text or commentary.
  • glosses: Similar because both originate as explanatory additions to historical manuscripts; differs because glosses originally sat in margins or between lines explicitly marked as explanatory notes, whereas interpolations have been merged directly into the running main text, often masquerading as original authorship.
  • interjections: Similar because both describe elements introduced into an ongoing stream of speech or text; differs because interjections are typically spontaneous, emotional exclamations or interruptions in conversation, whereas interpolations are systematic insertions or calculated mathematical estimations.
  • approximations: Similar because in mathematics both involve calculating non-exact numerical values; differs because approximations can be derived through rounding or loose estimation without reference to boundary points, whereas interpolations must be mathematically anchored between specific known boundary values.
