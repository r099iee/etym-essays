- Old French (c. 12th–13th c.) - [Mittens, Gloves & Winter Garments] - *Mofle* / *moufle* designated a thick, fur-lined mitten or winter glove without separate fingers; the derived verb *moufler* meant to wrap or bundle up in warm gloves or furs.
- Middle English (late 14th–15th c.) - [Wrapping the Face & Disguise] - Adopted into English as *mufflen*, meaning to wrap the face, head, or neck in cloaks, scarves, or hoods for warmth or disguise.
- Early Modern English (16th–17th c.) - [Deadening Acoustic Sound & Muffling Oars] - Broadened to naval and military operations, where wrapping fabric around oars (*muffling oars*) prevented splashing noises during surprise night raids, and wrapping church bells softened funeral knells.
- 18th–19th Century (Gothic & Victorian Prose) - [Literary Horror & Atmospheric Dread] - Flourished in Gothic literature and mystery fiction (Poe, Dickens, Doyle) to describe mysterious footsteps, suppressed screams, and foggy cityscapes.
- 20th–21st Century (Contemporary) - [General Acoustic & Physical Dampening] - Standardized as the primary term for dampened sound passing through barriers (walls, masks, snow) and wrapped garments.
