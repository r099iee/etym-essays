• Unwilling: Similar because both refuse glad consent; differs because unwilling is the plain flat fact of not wanting, while reluctant adds the inner wrestle, wanting and duty pulling opposite ways.
  • Loath: Similar because both hang back from an action; differs because loath is stronger and more poetic, deep aversion, while reluctant is everyday hesitation that persuasion can overcome.
  • Disinclined: Similar because both lean away from doing something; differs because disinclined is cool and polite lack of taste for it, while reluctant feels the struggle, tug against tug.
  • Hesitant: Similar because both pause before acting; differs because hesitant stresses the pause itself, uncertain timing, while reluctant stresses the aversion behind the pause.
  • Dragging: Similar because both suggest feet that will not move gladly, as in dragging feet; differs because dragging is colloquial and physical, while reluctant is the dignified inner state.
  • Grudging: Similar because both give without grace; differs because grudging resents even after consenting, while reluctant may warm once begun.
  • Recalcitrant: Similar because both resist authority or pressure; differs because recalcitrant kicks back openly and stubbornly, while reluctant holds back quietly and often yields.
  • Averse: Similar because both turn away from something disliked; differs because averse names a settled dislike, while reluctant names a moment’s holding back that circumstance may conquer.
