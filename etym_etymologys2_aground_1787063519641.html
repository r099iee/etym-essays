Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*h₁en (prefix) + *gʰrn-t- / *gʰrew-
"in, on" + "to grind, rub down; gravel, earth"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*ana + *grunduz
"on" + "bottom, foundation, ground"
↓
Old English (c. 850–1050)
on grunde
"on the bottom, upon the earth"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200–1450)
a grounde / on ground
"upon the earth, on the sea floor"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500)
aground
"stranded on the bottom or shore"
↓
Modern English (Present)
aground
"stranded on the seabed or shore; stalled"
