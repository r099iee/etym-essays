• Pulled: Similar because both involve exerting tractive force to draw something toward oneself; differs because pulled is the generic, neutral term that encompasses smooth, gentle, or continuous drawing, whereas tugged explicitly denotes sharp, forceful, repeated, or effortful jerks against resistance.
  • Yanked: Similar because both describe forceful pulling actions; differs because yanked implies a single, sudden, violent, and often reckless wrenching motion, whereas tugged typically suggests sustained, repetitive, or purposeful muscular effort.
  • Hauled: Similar because both convey heavy physical exertion in moving a mass; differs because hauled emphasizes transporting, dragging, or carrying heavy loads over a significant distance, while tugged focuses primarily on the initial or repeated tractive strain exerted on the connection point.
  • Jerked: Similar because both involve abrupt physical impulses; differs because jerked focuses on sharp, spasmodic, or involuntary movements of short duration, whereas tugged denotes deliberate, directional pulling against counteracting weight or resistance.
  • Wrenched: Similar because both imply high physical force applied to an object; differs because wrenched involves a twisting, rotational strain that often causes distortion, sprain, or mechanical breakage, whereas tugged is predominantly linear traction.
  • Towed: Similar because both refer to drawing an object along behind a source of power; differs because towed is formal, continuous, and primarily mechanical or nautical, whereas tugged captures intermittent, strenuous, or tactile pulling.
