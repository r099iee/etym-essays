- [c. 17th c. - 18th c.] - [Associated with aristocratic magnificence, royal courts, and the exotic, sensory luxury of eastern empires in neoclassical prose]
- [c. 19th c.] - [Employed by Victorian and American novelists (e.g. Hawthorne, James) with moral nuance, contrasting ostentatious Gilded Age opulence with democratic virtue and spiritual decay]
- [c. 20th c. - Present] - [Standard descriptive term in architecture, interior design, culinary criticism, and high fashion for sensory luxury, lush textures, and premium elegance]
