• Slav: a member of a Slavic-speaking people; differs because it is the personal noun, while "Slavic" is the adjective and the family label.
  • Slavonic: an older synonym, especially in "Old Church Slavonic"; differs by register, being more formal and more closely tied to liturgical and philological usage.
  • Slavonian: an inhabitant of Slavonia in Croatia, or an archaic general term for a Slav; differs because its modern reference is strictly regional.
  • Slovak: a West Slavic nation and language; differs because it names one specific people, not the family.
  • Slovene (Slovenian): a South Slavic nation and language; differs by its specific Alpine and Adriatic territory.
  • Balto-Slavic: the wider branch containing Baltic and Slavic; differs because it includes Lithuanian, Latvian and Old Prussian alongside the Slavic languages.
  • Proto-Slavic: the reconstructed common ancestor; differs because it is a prehistoric language, not the modern family.
  • Common Slavic: the late reconstructed stage before the historical splits; differs by chronological precision and by its use in linguistic reconstruction.
  • Pan-Slavic: the political and cultural movement claiming unity among Slavs; differs because it is an ideology, not a linguistic classification.
  • Slavistics: the academic discipline of Slavic studies; differs because it names a field of scholarship rather than a people or language.
  • Slavism: a linguistic feature peculiar to Slavic, or an older term for Pan-Slavism; differs by being a countable abstraction and by its dated ideological sense.
  • Wend: a Germanic name for Slavs, surviving in place-names and in the Sorbian context; differs because it is an exonym of German origin and now archaic.
