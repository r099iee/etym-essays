PIE *plew- "to flow, to float, to swim" (c. 4000-3000 BCE) — attested by Sanskrit plavate "he floats, he sails," Greek pleo "I sail," and the Germanic *fleutan- family ↓ Proto-Germanic *fleutaną "to flow, to float" (c. 500 BCE - 200 CE) — ancestor of Dutch vlieten "to flow" and German fließen "to flow" ↓ Old English flēotan "to float, to swim, to drift" (7th-11th c.) — the strong verb fleet in its original aquatic sense, used of ships, swimmers, and things carried on water ↓ Middle English fleten "to float, to drift, to flow" (12th-15th c.) — the verb sense extended from water to general drifting movement ↓ fleeting "passing swiftly away; transitory" (adjective, from c. 14th c.) — the present participle of fleet (to float, to drift) adjectivized; the sense "drifting past" in space became "passing quickly" in time Note: The spelling and pronunciation were simplified from flēotan to fleet as the long vowel monophthongized, and the adjective fleeting coexists with the short-lived adjective fleet "swift" (from the same verb, "moving as if floating"), as in "fleet-footed.
