- Antiquity: Greek *týmpanon* and Latin *tympanum* denoted a struck drum, tambourine, or architectural membrane.
- Medieval Old French: Evolved into *timbre*, designating a stationary metal clock bell struck by a mechanical hammer, as well as a heraldic helmet crest.
- 18th century: Applied in French acoustics and music theory to describe the distinctive resonance and sound quality of musical bells and instruments.
- 19th century: Codified internationally through Hermann von Helmholtz's psychoacoustic research on harmonic overtones (*Klangfarbe* / *timbre*), and borrowed into English.
- Late 19th–21st centuries: Naturalized in English musicology, speech acoustics, and literary criticism as both acoustic tone color and the metaphorical authorial voice of a writer.
