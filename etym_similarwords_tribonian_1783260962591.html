• Justinian: Similar because both are inseparable in the story of the Corpus Juris Civilis; differs because Justinian was the emperor who commissioned and promulgated the codification, while Tribonian was the jurist who actually directed and compiled it.
  • Papinian: Similar because both are among the greatest Roman jurists and central to the Digest; differs because Papinian was a classical jurist of the 3rd century whose works Tribonian's commission excerpted, whereas Tribonian was the 6th-century editor of that material.
  • Ulpian: Similar because Ulpian's texts form over a third of the Digest Tribonian compiled; differs because Ulpian was an author of the legal opinions, not the editor-in-chief who selected them.
  • Codifier: Similar because both denote one who systematically arranges law into a code; differs because codifier is the generic function, while Tribonian names the specific historical person who performed it for Justinian.
  • Jurisconsult: Similar because both denote an expert in Roman law; differs because jurisconsult is a broad class term for legal experts, while Tribonian is one individual within that class.
  • Quaestor: Similar because Tribonian held the office of quaestor sacri palatii; differs because quaestor names an office held by many, not the man himself, and the Roman quaestor was originally a fiscal magistrate, not a legal editor.
  • Tribune (tribunus): Similar because both share the root tribus "tribe"; differs because a tribune was a magistrate or military officer of the Roman republic, whereas Tribonian was a Byzantine jurist of the 6th century.
  • Corpus Juris Civilis: Similar because the two are jointly famous in legal history; differs because the Corpus is the set of texts themselves, while Tribonian is the human agent behind them.
  • Belisarius: Similar because Gibbon lists both among the figures of Justinian's reign and both appear in the same context; differs because Belisarius was a general who reconquered the West, whereas Tribonian was a jurist who codified the law.
