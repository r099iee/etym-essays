- [14th century shared participation] - ['Communion' first means sharing in common — a mutual participation in goods or duties among a community]
- [15th-16th century Christian sacrament] - [Fixes as the central Christian rite: Holy Communion, the sharing of bread and wine, a sacred fellowship of believers]
- [17th-18th century spiritual union] - ['Communion with' extends to intimate union of minds or souls — 'communion with nature', 'communion with a new mind']
- [19th-20th century elevated intimacy] - [Used in aesthetic and spiritual prose for the deepest grade of shared mental or spiritual experience]
- [Present high register] - [Still signals the most intimate sharing; everyday speech prefers 'talk', 'connection', or 'bond', while communion keeps its almost sacramental weight]
