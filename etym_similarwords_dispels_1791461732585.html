• Scatter: the plainest physical near-synonym, sharing the image of driving a mass apart; differs by having no figurative sense of removing doubt or fear.
  • Disperse: shares the core meaning of breaking up a crowd or cloud; differs because it is often intransitive and neutral, and carries no claim that an abstraction has vanished.
  • Dissipate: similar in causing something to thin away; differs by implying gradual fading rather than a decisive drive, and it is common intransitively ("the fog dissipated").
  • Banish: similar in driving something away for good; differs by implying authority, decree and exile, and it can be used of people.
  • Expel: similar in forcing something out; differs because expel implies removal from inside a body or institution, not scattering in the open.
  • Drive away: the plain phrasal equivalent; differs by being informal and by lacking the suggestion that the thing is broken into fragments.
  • Rout: similar in scattering an opposing force; differs by being military and violent, with no abstract or reassuring use.
  • Disband: similar in breaking up a gathered group; differs by being organisational and voluntary, not forceful.
  • Clear: similar in removing fog, doubt or clutter; differs as a broader verb that often means "tidy" or "empty" rather than "scatter".
  • Lift: similar in the phrase "lift a mood/fog"; differs by suggesting a raising away rather than a scattering.
  • Allay: similar in reducing fear or concern; differs because allay calms and softens rather than removing the thing entirely.
  • Quell: similar in putting down unrest or fear; differs by implying suppression by force rather than dispersal.
  • Debunk: similar in getting rid of a myth; differs because debunk is specifically evidentiary and argumentative, while dispel can be purely psychological.
  • Refute / disprove: similar in establishing that a belief is false; differ by being logical and propositional, whereas dispel describes the belief's disappearance.
  • Dissolve: similar in making something vanish; differs by its chemical metaphor of melting into a medium, not scattering.
  • Evaporate: similar in the image of mist disappearing; differs by being intransitive and gradual, with no agent driving it.
