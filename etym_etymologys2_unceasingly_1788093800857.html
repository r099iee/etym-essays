The suffixes -ing (present participle, from earlier -ende, reinforced by the Scandinavian -andi) and -ly (adverbial, from Old English -līce "in the manner of", related to like) complete the form.

Note on the root stage: the PIE root *ḱed- “to go, yield” is the standard reconstruction for Latin cēdere, but its wider Indo-European cognate set is debated — some scholars compare Sanskrit sad- “to go/approach”, while others hold that the root may be secure only within Italic; the derivation from *ḱed- through cēdere → cessāre (frequentative, “to keep withdrawing” hence “to stop”) is, however, firmly attested at every subsequent stage.
