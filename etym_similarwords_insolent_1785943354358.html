• Impudent: Similar because both mean boldly disrespectful; differs because impudent emphasizes shamelessness and barefaced cheek ("shameless impudence"), while insolent emphasizes arrogant contempt for superiors.
  • Impertinent: Similar because both mean disrespectfully out of line; differs because impertinent highlights intrusiveness and asking what is not one's business, while insolent is openly hostile and contemptuous.
  • Brazen: Similar because both mean bold in wrongdoing; differs because brazen stresses shameless, shocking openness (a brazen lie, a brazen thief) without necessarily implying a subordinate-to-superior relationship, while insolent centers on insult to authority.
  • Disrespectful: Similar because both mean lacking respect; differs because disrespectful is the mild, general term for any failure of respect, while insolent is severe, haughty, and aggressive — open contempt, not mere discourtesy.
  • Arrogant: Similar because both involve excessive pride; differs because arrogant is about self-importance and overestimation of oneself, while insolent is specifically about contemptuous treatment of others, usually superiors.
  • Haughty: Similar because both convey lofty contempt; differs because haughty is proud and condescending by disposition, while insolent is actively rude and defiant in speech or manner.
  • Contumacious: Similar because both describe defiance of authority; differs because contumacious is formal legal terminology for stubborn disobedience to a court's orders, while insolent is general contemptuous rudeness.
  • Saucy: Similar because both mean disrespectfully bold; differs because saucy is light, playful, cheeky impertinence (often endearing or comic), while insolent is serious, contemptuous, and offensive.
  • Presumptuous: Similar because both mean overstepping proper bounds; differs because presumptuous stresses taking too much for granted and acting beyond one's rights, while insolent stresses contemptuous rudeness in what one says or how one behaves.
  • Cheeky: Similar because both mean boldly disrespectful; differs because cheeky is informal, mild, and often affectionate, while insolent is formal, strong, and genuinely offensive.
