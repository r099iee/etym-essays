Component 1 — FARM:
1. Proto-Indo-European *dher- "to hold, support, make firm" (reconstructed, c. 4000–3000 BCE).
2. Latin firmus "firm, strong, stable" (Classical Latin, from c. 3rd c. BCE).
3. Medieval Latin firma "fixed payment, rent due at a fixed time; signature" (c. 8th–9th c. CE).
4. Old French ferme "lease, rented land; farm" (c. 12th c.; also fermer "to lease").
5. Middle English ferme / farm "rent; leased land" (c. 13th–14th c.).SEMANTIC SHIFT: the word moved from "fixed rent/lease contract" to "the land leased under it" to "any land cultivated for food production."
6. English farm "agricultural holding" (15th c. onward) — first element of the compound.

Component 2 — STEAD:
1. Proto-Indo-European *stā- / *steh₂- "to stand, set up, remain fixed" (reconstructed, c. 4000–3000 BCE).
2. Proto-Germanic *stadiz "standing place, site, position" (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE; cf. Old High German stat "place", German Stadt "city").
3. Old English stede "place, position, site, standing-place" (attested from c. 8th c.; frequent in place-names like Hampstead, "home-stead").
4. Middle English stede "place" (c. 12th–15th c.).
5. English stead "place" (modern) — second element of the compound.

Compound assembly — FARMSTEAD:
1. English farm + stead combined to name the standing place of a farm (first attested in English c. late 16th century, 1570s–1600).
2. English farmstead "farm with its buildings, the site where a farm stands" — FARMSTEAD (final node).
