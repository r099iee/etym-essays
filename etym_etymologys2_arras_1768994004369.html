arras — The English word arras is an eponym: the city's name transferred to the tapestry product it made famous. There is NO PIE ancestry for the word itself; the chain below is the attested record, with the deeper Celtic/PIE hypothesis flagged as debated. Stage 1 — Gaulish (Celtic) tribal name: Atrebates "the settlers, the dwellers," from Celtic *treb- "dwelling, homestead" (attested in Greek and Roman sources; the tribe appears in Caesar's De Bello Gallico, 51 BCE). A proposed connection of the Celtic *treb- to PIE *treb- "dwelling, building" (cf. Latin trabs "beam," Lithuanian tróba "building") is a debated Celticist hypothesis, not a solid chain; note it explicitly as uncertain. Stage 2 — Latin: Atrebatum "city of the Atrebates," the Roman civitas of the tribe in Gallia Belgica (Roman period, from the 1st century BCE; the town succeeded the earlier settlement Nemetocenna). Stage 3 — Medieval Gallo-Romance: Arteis → Arras, the city of the Scarpe in Artois (attested in its modern form by the 11th century). Stage 4 — Old French product-name: arras "a rich tapestry made in Arras" (13th–14th centuries; the transfer of a toponym to its celebrated product). Stage 5 — Middle English: arras "tapestry, wall hanging made at Arras" (attested from the 14th century, e.g. in Chaucer's Knight's Tale). Stage 6 — Modern English: arras (unchanged form; now archaic-literary, preserved in the phrase "behind the arras"). Note: The word is a toponymic loan, not a normal inherited PIE noun; the only proposed Indo-European link passes through the Celtic *treb- "dwelling" and is debated, as noted above.
