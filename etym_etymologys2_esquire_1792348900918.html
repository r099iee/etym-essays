esquier / esquire

↓
Modern English (18th c.–present)
esquire
"courtesy title for gentlemen (UK); post-nominal title for attorneys (US)"

Note on historical debate & doublet formation: Historical linguists note that during the Anglo-Norman and Middle English period, initial prosthetic e- was frequently dropped through the phonetic process of apheresis (aphesis) when unstressed in colloquial speech, creating the twin doublets esquire (the formal, legal, and written honorific retaining the Old French prosthesis) and squire (the aphetic vernacular substantive applied to country landowners and chivalric companions).
