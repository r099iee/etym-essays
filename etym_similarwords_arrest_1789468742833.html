• Detain: Similar because both involve depriving an individual of freedom of movement; differs because detain implies holding someone temporarily for questioning or administrative processing without necessarily lodging formal criminal charges, whereas arrest constitutes a definitive legal deprivation of liberty under judicial authority.
  • Apprehend: Similar because both refer to capturing a fleeing or suspected offender; differs because apprehend emphasizes the physical act of catching or seizing after a chase, whereas arrest formalizes the subsequent custody and legal restraint.
  • Halt: Similar because both describe bringing physical movement to a stop; differs because halt is a purely directional or mechanical cessation without the legal, medical, or metaphorical valences carried by arrest.
  • Check: Similar because both mean to impede or slow down an advancing force; differs because check often implies restraining, testing, or curbing an advance rather than bringing it to a total, involuntary freeze.
  • Stem: Similar because both mean to stop the flow or spread of an unwanted condition; differs because stem applies predominantly to liquid dynamics or spreading metaphorical tides (e.g., stemming the flow of losses), whereas arrest applies across mechanical, biological, ocular, and legal domains.
  • Captivate: Similar because both can describe capturing human attention; differs because captivate carries exclusively pleasant, charming, and delightful connotations, whereas an arresting spectacle commands attention through striking, intense, or even startling visual power.
  • Quell: Similar because both involve putting an end to an active process; differs because quell specifically denotes suppressing a riot, disorder, or internal rebellion through forceful pacification.
