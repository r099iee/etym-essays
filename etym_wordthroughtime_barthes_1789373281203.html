- [Medieval–16th c.] - Emerges in southwestern France (Gascony, Béarn, and the Landes) as a topomastic surname designating families residing near or owning land in the barthes (low-lying alluvial floodplains and marshy brushwoods along the Adour River) - [connotation: rural Gascon landscape, river-bottom meadows, southwestern French peasant and gentry heritage]
- [17th c.–19th c.] - Established as a respected regional family name across Aquitaine and the Pyrenean foothills, borne by provincial jurists, naval officers, and physicians (such as Paul-Joseph Barthez, leading Montpellier vitalist philosopher) - [connotation: provincial bourgeoisie, medical and maritime distinction, regional identity]
- [1950s–1960s] - Catapulted into global intellectual fame by Roland Barthes with the publication of Mythologies (1957) and "The Death of the Author" (1967), establishing the surname as the international emblem of semiology and French structuralism - [connotation: avant-garde Paris intellectualism, semiotic critique, radical subversion of literary orthodoxy]
- [1970s–Present] - Solidified through late masterpieces such as The Pleasure of the Text, A Lover's Discourse, and Camera Lucida as an iconic designation for post-structuralist lyricism, the eroticism of reading, and elegiac critical reflection - [connotation: supreme literary sensibility, theoretical icon, poetic semiotics, intellectual immortality]
