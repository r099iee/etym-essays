No PIE ancestry. Origin uncertain; the earliest stages are hypotheses, not attested facts, and this is stated plainly. 1. Arabic قَزّ qazz “raw silk, silk floss” (classical Arabic; possibly of Iranian/Persian origin — debated) — the proposed eastern silk-word, one candidate source. 2. (Reconstructed transmission) Levantine silk trade → Italian/Venetian intermediaries (Venetian gasa) → Old/Middle French — the channel by which a Levantine silk-name would have reached France; not documented step by step. 3. (Folk-etymology, now doubted) Arabic غَزَّة Ġazzah “Gaza,” the ancient port of southern Palestine, claimed as the source of a “fabric of Gaza”; modern handbooks treat this as popular etymology with an economic kernel, not as proven. 4. French gaze “a light, transparent silk” — attested mid-16th c. (c. 1540s–1560s); the certain recorded carrier of the word. 5. English gaze (1560s) “a thin, transparent silk” — borrowed from French gaze. 6. English gauze (17th c.) — the spelling with ⟨-u-⟩ settles during the 17th century (earlier gase, gawze, and gaize are recorded); modern English gauze, the root word. Note on the debate: the connection to the city of Gaza is now generally treated as folk-etymology, though the economic logic (Gaza genuinely traded in such cloths) keeps the theory alive in some sources. Whichever eastern source is right — Arabic qazz or Gaza — the word entered Europe through Levantine maritime trade and has no Indo-European pedigree.
