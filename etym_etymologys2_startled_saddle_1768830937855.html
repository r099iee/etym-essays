Root 1 — STARTLED:

Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*sturtijaną
"to start, leap"
(of uncertain origin)
↓
Old English (c. 700)
styrtan
"to start, leap up"
↓
English (13th c.)
start
"to leap up, move suddenly"
↓
English (frequentative, 16th c.)
startle
"to cause to start, alarm suddenly"
↓
English (past participle)
startled
"suddenly surprised or alarmed"

Note: PGmc *sturtijaną is of uncertain origin; a connection to PIE *ster- "rigid, stiff" has been suggested but is not securely established.

Root 2 — SADDLE:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*sed-
"to sit"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*sadulaz
"saddle"
↓
Old English (c. 700)
sadol
"a saddle"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
sadel
"a saddle"
↓
English (16th c.)
saddle
"a seat for a rider on a horse"

Note: PGmc *sadulaz is built on PIE *sed- with an instrument suffix (cf. Latin sella "seat"); the word is native Germanic, not a Latin loan.
