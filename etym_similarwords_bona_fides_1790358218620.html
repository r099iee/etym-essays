• Credentials: Similar because both refer to documentation or proof of identity, authority, and qualification; differs because credentials specifically refers to formal written documents (diplomas, certificates, press passes), whereas bona fides encompasses broader moral sincerity and proven track record.
  • Authenticity: Similar because both establish that someone or something is genuine rather than counterfeit; differs because authenticity is the quality of being real or original, whereas bona fides focuses on the evidence that proves honest standing and intent.
  • Good faith: Similar because good faith is the literal translation of bona fides; differs because good faith is an abstract moral attitude and legal standard, whereas in modern English bona fides is frequently used for tangible credentials.
  • Probity: Similar because both denote high moral integrity and honesty; differs because probity is an internal ethical quality of unblemished uprightness, whereas bona fides represents the outward proof or demonstration of trustworthiness.
  • Legitimacy: Similar because both convey lawful standing and validity; differs because legitimacy emphasizes conformity with established law, rules, or lineage, while bona fides focuses on honest intent and genuine qualifications.
  • Sincerity: Similar because both imply freedom from pretense or deceit; differs because sincerity refers to emotional and psychological honesty, while bona fides has institutional, legal, and verifiable standing.
  • Trustworthiness: Similar because both describe the reliability of a person; differs because trustworthiness is a general character trait, while bona fides represents specific evidence presented to overcome doubt or skepticism.
