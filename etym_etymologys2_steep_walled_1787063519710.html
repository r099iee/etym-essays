NOTE — DEBATED / NUANCED STAGES: (1) STEEP: the relation of Proto-Germanic *staupaz to PIE *(s)teup- "to push, strike, stand" is standard but involves a semantic reconstruction: the Germanic adjective "high, towering" is usually explained as "sticking up, jutting up" (that which stands up steeply) — the connection is accepted in the handbooks (Pokorny, Kroonen), though the exact semantic bridge is inferred rather than directly attested. Old English stēap also meant "deep" (of water) and "steep", with "high, lofty" clearly the earlier sense. (2) WALLED: Old English weall is a loan from Latin vallum (the rampart made of stakes), borrowed via Germanic contact with the Roman world; the PIE root *wel- "to turn, roll" underlies vallus "stake" (a stake being a turned, rounded pole), though the ultimate semantic link is debated in detail and the root is reconstructed with variants (*wel-, *welH-, *u̯el-). All stages shown are attested or securely reconstructed; nothing is guessed.
