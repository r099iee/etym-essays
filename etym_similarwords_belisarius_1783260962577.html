• Narses: Similar because both were Justinian's greatest generals, reconquering Italy in the 6th century; differs because Narses was a eunuch court chamberlain turned general who completed the Italian conquest in the 550s, while Belisarius was the earlier, more celebrated field commander of the African and Gothic wars.
  • Scipio Africanus: Similar because both are the archetypal Roman conquerors of North Africa who saved their state from a great rival (Carthage; the Vandals); differs because Scipio (d. 183 BC) fought for the pagan Roman Republic, while Belisarius fought for the Christian Roman (Byzantine) Empire a millennium later.
  • Aetius: Similar because both are late-Roman generals remembered as "the last of the Romans," defending the West against barbarians (Attila; the Vandals/Ostrogoths); differs because Aetius served the Western emperor in the 5th century and won at the Catalaunian Fields (451), whereas Belisarius served the Eastern emperor and reconquered the lost West.
  • Caesar: Similar because both names evoke supreme Roman generalship and both were borne by commanders whose deeds became legend; differs because Caesar was a politician-dictator and the source of the imperial title, while Belisarius was a loyal general who never sought the throne and whose name never became a title.
  • Procopius: Similar because the historian's name is bound to the general's in the sources (Procopius was Belisarius's secretary and chronicler); differs because Procopius is the writer who recorded and interpreted the campaigns, while Belisarius is the subject and actor.
  • Justinian: Similar because Belisarius is inseparable from his emperor and patron, the agent of Justinian's reconquest; differs because Justinian was the ruler and lawgiver (Corpus Juris Civilis, Hagia Sophia) who could also suspect and humiliate his general.
  • Hannibal: Similar because both are great commanders whose name and legend outlived their cause and were later romanticised as tragic heroes; differs because Hannibal was Rome's enemy who lost the Second Punic War, while Belisarius was Rome's (Byzantium's) champion who won back the West.
