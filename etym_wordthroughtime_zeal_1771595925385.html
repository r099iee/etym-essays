- [14th century biblical fervour] - [Enters through Bible translations meaning passionate ardour, especially religious; connotation intense and partisan from the start]
- [16th-17th century the religious double edge] - [The Reformation makes zeal double-edged: earnest devotion to the faithful, fanaticism to opponents; 'zealot' hardens into a name for religious extremists]
- [18th-19th century secular enthusiasm] - [Ruskin disclaims 'zeal for the reputation of any individual' — zeal is the hot, partisan commitment that would compromise cool judgment; connotation of heated, consuming, almost bodily eagerness]
- [20th-21st century cause and excess] - [Retains the sense of energetic devotion to a cause, admiring in 'zeal for the work' and warning in 'zealotry'; the word still carries the ancient rivalry and striving of its Greek source — the urge to excel in a cause]
