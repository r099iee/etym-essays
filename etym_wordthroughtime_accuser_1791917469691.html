- Mid-14th c. — the person who brings a charge, in an age when prosecution was still largely a private act.
- 15th–17th c. — heavy religious use: Satan as “the Accuser”, and in heresy and witchcraft trials the accuser’s word could be fatal.
- 18th–19th c. — public prosecution displaces private accusation, so the accuser becomes the complainant while the state takes over the case.
- 20th c. — rights discourse fixes phrases such as “face one’s accuser”, and anonymity for accusers becomes a live legal question.
- 21st c. — high-profile abuse cases make “accuser” a contested public label, often replaced by “complainant” or “survivor” by those who distrust its adversarial slant.
