Linear derivation chain for REEK: 1. Proto-Germanic *reukaną "to smoke, to steam, to give off vapour" (1st half of the 1st millennium CE); the verb family is well attested: Old Norse rjūka, Old Saxon riokan, Middle Dutch roken, Old High German riohhan, German rauchen "to smoke". The noun form is *raukaz "smoke, reek". 2. Old English rēocan (class II strong verb, past rēac/rēc) "to give off smoke, steam, or vapour; to smoke; to fumigate, to dress hides with smoke", attested from the 8th c.; the noun rēc (later rēc) "smoke, vapour, reek" appears in the same period, including in Beowulf, where smoke and vapour from the mere and from the burning hall are described with the word. 3. Middle English rēken/reken "to smoke, to steam, to emit vapour" (12th–15th c.), and the noun reek/rec "smoke, vapour, steam", continuing the Old English word with regular vowel development (Old English ēo > Middle English ē/ē̄). 4. 16th–17th c. — the sense widens from vapour to smell: "to reek" gains the meaning "to emit a strong smell, to smell strongly of something" (attested c. 1600), and the noun "a reek" comes to mean a strong odour beside its older smoke sense. 5. Modern English REEK — noun "thick, foul smoke; a strong, unpleasant smell" and verb "to emit smoke/vapour, or to smell strongly and unpleasantly of something". Final node: REEK. DEBATED / NUANCED STAGE: the Germanic stage is secure (the word is attested across North and West Germanic), but no PIE etymon can be confidently reconstructed. A connection has been proposed with Baltic forms such as Lithuanian rūkti "to smoke, to reek" and with Slavic forms meaning "to roar/smoke", pointing toward a possible PIE *rewg-/*reuk- "to emit smoke/vapour", but the correspondences are not fully regular and the reconstruction is disputed; the chain is therefore deliberately left starting at Proto-Germanic rather than at a reconstructed PIE root. Note also that the Old English noun rēc and the Middle English spelling reek are the same word; the modern sense of bad smell is a seventeenth-century innovation, not an original meaning.
