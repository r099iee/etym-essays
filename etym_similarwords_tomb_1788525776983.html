• grave: Similar as a primary resting place for a corpse; differs because grave is a native Germanic word denoting an ordinary excavation or pit dug into the earth, lacking the architectural grandeur, monumentality, and stonework inherent in a tomb.
  • sepulchre: Similar in denoting a stone burial chamber or rock-cut vault; differs because sepulchre is of Latin/Biblical provenance and carries deep liturgical, sacred, and archaic religious associations (e.g., the Holy Sepulchre).
  • mausoleum: Similar as an above-ground monument housing human remains; differs because a mausoleum specifically denotes a large, stately, free-standing architectural building, typically erected for a family or illustrious ruler.
  • crypt: Similar as a stone enclosure for burials; differs because a crypt is specifically an underground stone chamber or vaulted room located beneath the floor of a church or cathedral.
  • vault: Similar as an arched burial chamber; differs because vault emphasizes the architectural masonry and arched ceiling structure, and is also widely used for secure banking storage rooms.
  • catacomb: Similar as an underground subterranean cemetery; differs because a catacomb comprises an extensive, labyrinthine network of interconnected subterranean galleries and niches rather than a single burial structure.
  • sarcophagus: Similar because it contains deceased remains; differs because a sarcophagus is specifically the ornamental stone, marble, or terracotta coffin box itself, rather than the encompassing building or chamber.
  • cenotaph: Similar as a funerary monument honoring the dead; differs because a cenotaph is an empty monument erected in memory of a deceased person whose actual body is buried elsewhere or lost at sea.
  • tumulus: Similar in serving as an ancient burial monument; differs because a tumulus (or barrow) is specifically an earthen or stone mound piled over a prehistoric burial site.
  • ossuary: Similar in holding skeletal human remains; differs because an ossuary is a dedicated receptacle, chest, or chamber for the secondary storage of skeletal bones after the flesh has decomposed.
