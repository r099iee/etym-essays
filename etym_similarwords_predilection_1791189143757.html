• Preference: Similar because it denotes choosing one thing over another; differs because it is neutral and can be momentary, while predilection implies a long-standing, characteristic bent.
  • Penchant: Similar because it means a strong liking; differs because penchant feels livelier, quirkier, and more French-whimsical, often for a pastime.
  • Proclivity: Similar because it denotes an inclination; differs because proclivity often leans toward something undesirable and collocates with toward, carrying mild disapproval.
  • Propensity: Similar because it means a tendency to behave a certain way; differs because it is more statistical and formal, often used for risk or pathology.
  • Partiality: Similar because it means favouring one side; differs because partiality stresses unfairness in judgment, while predilection stresses affectionate taste.
  • Fondness: Similar because it expresses warm liking; differs because fondness is more emotional and personal, less about repeated choice.
  • Liking: Similar because it denotes finding something pleasant; differs because liking is plain and general, lacking predilection's literate, habitual nuance.
  • Weakness: Similar because a weakness for something is an indulged liking; differs because it is colloquial and self-deprecating, implying inability to resist.
  • Fancy: Similar because to take a fancy to something is to like it; differs because fancy is sudden and capricious, opposite to predilection's durability.
  • Taste: Similar because taste denotes aesthetic preference; differs because taste implies discernment and cultivation, while predilection can be unrefined habit.
  • Bent: Similar because a bent is a characteristic inclination; differs because bent is broader and can apply to talent or direction, not just liking.
  • Inclination: Similar because it means a leaning toward; differs because inclination can be fleeting or physical, while predilection is settled and evaluative.
