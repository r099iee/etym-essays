- Old English studu - 'post, pillar, prop'; a building timber that stands upright, the oldest sense
- 13th–14th c. - the projecting head of a nail is called a stud by analogy with a post; armour and harness gain studded decoration
- 15th–17th c. - studs appear on belts, saddles, caskets, and book bindings as ornament
- 17th–19th c. - shirt studs and collar studs make 'stud' a gentleman's fastener; 'studded' spreads to figuratively dotted surfaces
- 19th c. - 'studded with stars' and similar metaphors become common in prose and verse
- 20th c. - studded tyres, studded wall frames, studded leather in fashion and subcultures
- Today - three homophonous studs (wall post, ornament, breeding stallion) fully separated; 'studded' mostly decorative and metaphorical
