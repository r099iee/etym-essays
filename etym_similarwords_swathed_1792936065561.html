• Wrapped: Similar because both mean enclosed in covering material; differs because wrapped is the plain general word (parcels, presents), while swathed stresses broad bands wound round and round in overlapping layers.
  • Swaddled: Similar because both picture infants and bandages wound snug; differs because swaddled belongs almost wholly to babies bound tight in narrow cloths, while swathed ranges from throats to hillsides.
  • Bundled: Similar because both describe dressing thickly against cold; differs because bundled stresses hasty heaping on of clothes, while swathed stresses neat encircling folds.
  • Muffled: Similar because a scarf swathed over the chin also muffles voice and hearing; differs because muffled names the deadening of sound or blow, while swathed names the wrapping that causes it.
  • Enveloped: Similar because fog and gloom envelop as cloth swathes; differs because enveloped is abstract and complete on all sides, while swathed keeps the homely image of bands and folds.
  • Shrouded: Similar because both conceal in cloth, and corpses are both swathed and shrouded; differs because shrouded is funereal and final, while swathed is usually protective and reversible.
  • Cocooned: Similar because both suggest snug total enclosure; differs because cocooned borrows the silkworm’s case and implies transformation or luxury, while swathed stays with plain woven bands.
  • Bandaged: Similar because wounds are swathed when bandaged; differs because bandaged is clinical and local to injury, while swathed is broader, softer, and more literary.
  • Draped: Similar because both lay cloth over a form; differs because draped lets fabric fall and display, while swathed winds it close and conceals.
