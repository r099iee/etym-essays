• alterations: Similar because both are changes made to something already existing; differs because alterations is the broader, plainer term and carries no implication of correction or improvement — a garment may be altered for fashion rather than for accuracy.
  • modifications: Similar because both adjust a text or arrangement; differs because modifications often implies a change of function or design, whereas amendments implies a correction within an existing framework.
  • corrections: Similar because both remove error; differs because corrections is limited to putting right what is wrong, while amendments may add, delete or reword without any prior error.
  • emendations: Similar because both are scholarly or textual improvements; differs because emendations is the specialist term for conjectural repairs to a corrupt text and is rarely used of laws or contracts.
  • revisions: Similar because both produce a new version; differs because revisions suggests a fuller reworking of the whole, while amendments are discrete, itemised changes.
  • edits: Similar because both change wording; differs because edits is informal, publishing-flavoured and can include cuts for length, whereas amendments are formal and deliberative.
  • adjustments: Similar because both are small changes; differs because adjustments is neutral and often physical or numerical, with no suggestion of formal procedure or textual authority.
  • additions: Similar because an amendment can add a clause; differs because additions names only one possible operation, whereas an amendment may also delete or substitute.
  • rider: Similar because both are attached to a bill; differs because a rider is an unrelated provision attached to a larger measure, whereas an amendment addresses the text it changes.
