• singed: Similar because both describe surface burning by flame or heat; differs because singe means scorching the surface lightly, producing singed ends and a burned smell, but without the tight curling-and-crisping that frizzle always implies.
  • scorched: Similar because both describe heat damage leaving brown, burned marks; differs because scorch emphasises discolouration over a surface without the shrivelling, curling texture of frizzling.
  • charred: Similar because both describe burning damage; differs because char implies blackening and carbonisation, a deeper and more destructive burn, whereas frizzling is a surface shrivel-and-curl.
  • curled / crimped: Similar because both describe tight, repeated coils (especially of hair); differs because these are neutral or deliberate styling terms with no implication of scorching or heat damage, while frizzled always carries a heat or crisping idea.
  • frizzy: Similar because both belong to the same word-family and describe tight, dry, tangled curls; differs because frizzy is a static hair texture caused by humidity, while frizzled is the action or result of burning or heating.
  • crisped: Similar because both describe food cooked until firm, dry, and brittle; differs because crisp is a general culinary texture (also of fresh vegetables and biscuits), whereas frizzle specifically names the curling, spitting fry on high heat.
  • sizzled: Similar because both evoke high-heat frying with spitting and hissing; differs because sizzle describes the sound and process of cooking, not necessarily the curled, crispened result that frizzled denotes.
  • shrivelled: Similar because both mean puckering and tightening up, often from heat or dryness; differs because shrivel describes moisture loss and puckering generally (age, drought), while frizzle names the specific scorch-and-curl action.
  • withered: Similar because both can describe drying up and shrinking; differs because wither implies decline from loss of vitality or water (plants, limbs), without the crisp, singed, heat-shrunk texture of frizzling.
  • seared: Similar because both are high-heat cooking verbs working on the surface; differs because sear means browning the surface quickly to lock in juices while the food stays intact, whereas frizzling curls and crisps thin items.
