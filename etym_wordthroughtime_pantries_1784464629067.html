“Pantry” entered English from medieval French for a room where bread was kept — from the word for bread itself, the bread-store of a great house.
“Pantry” through the centuries broadened from bread to all provisions: the room where food, tableware, and kitchen supplies were staged between store and table.
“Pantry” by the 18th–19th centuries was the standard small food-room of an orderly household, and in the 20th century the word spread to the built-in cupboard in any kitchen.
“Pantries” today still names the food-and-ware rooms of a house — and when a hobbit has “pantries (lots of these),” the plural itself is the joke of abundance, a geography of snack and supper.
