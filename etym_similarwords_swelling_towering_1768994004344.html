• expanding: Similar in growth of size or volume; differs because it is neutral and gradual, while swelling carries pressurized, organic, almost explosive energy — matter distending from within.
  • inflating: Similar in filling outward and upward; differs because it implies hollow air, often deceptively, whereas swelling suggests living, liquid substance thickening itself.
  • distending: Similar in stretching outward from within; differs because it is the clinical term for abnormal, strained expansion, lacking the organic life of swelling.
  • bulging: Similar in protruding outward under pressure; differs because it describes a localized outward curve, while swelling is a general increase in volume all round.
  • ballooning: Similar in rapid, outward inflation; differs because it is colloquial and evokes a single inflated sac, where swelling is steady, organic growth.
  • bloating: Similar in becoming puffed and swollen; differs because it is unpleasant and decay-tinged, associated with rot or indigestion, while swelling is neutral or even vigorous.
  • rising: Similar in moving or growing upward; differs because it is simple upward motion with no implication of bulk or pressure.
  • surging: Similar in powerful forward or upward movement; differs because it implies wave-like motion and momentum, while swelling is a static inflation of volume.
  • mounting: Similar in growing toward a greater height or level; differs because it is incremental and directional, while swelling is volumetric from within.
  • looming: Similar in dominating height that impresses or threatens; differs because it is a half-seen, menacing presence, while towering is openly monumental and static — height already achieved and held.
  • soaring: Similar in rising to great height; differs because it describes graceful upward flight in motion, whereas towering is the still, massive assertion of height over everything around it.
  • lofty: Similar in great height or elevation; differs because it is chiefly an adjective of dignified elevation — lofty ideals — and lacks towering's image of a standing structure dwarfing its surroundings.
  • colossal: Similar in enormous size that dwarfs the observer; differs because it stresses bulk and scale rather than verticality, while towering specifically stresses height.
  • monumental: Similar in greatness of scale that awes; differs because it suggests the dignity and permanence of monuments, while towering keeps the live, thrusting image of height.
  • overhanging: Similar in projecting above and menacing what is below; differs because it describes something hanging out over a space, while towering is simply standing tall above everything.
  • dominating: Similar in rising above and controlling a scene; differs because it is a general verb of control, while towering is specifically about overwhelming height.
