• Constantine: Similar because both are the great late Roman reformer-emperors whose reigns bracket the 4th century and are paired by Gibbon on this card; differs because Constantine is remembered as the first Christian emperor and founder of Constantinople, while Diocletian is remembered as the pagan persecutor and creator of the Tetrarchy.
  • Augustus: Similar because both are paradigmatic Roman emperors who reformed the state and whose names came to denote an era; differs because Augustus founded the Principate by veiling autocracy behind republican forms, while Diocletian stripped away the veil and openly remodeled the empire as the Dominate.
  • Aurelian: Similar because both are soldier-emperors of the late 3rd century who restored imperial unity after near-collapse; differs because Aurelian is credited with reuniting the empire militarily (restoring the eastern and Gallic breakaway states) but reigned only five years and made no systematic tetrarchic or administrative settlement, while Diocletian institutionalized the recovery.
  • Probus: Similar because both are Illyrian soldier-emperors of the later 3rd century; differs because Probus reigned briefly (276–282) and is remembered mainly for frontier wars and the vine-planting legend, while Diocletian reigned twenty years and built a permanent system.
  • Maximian: Similar because both were the two Augusti of the first Tetrarchy, ruling as colleagues; differs because Maximian was the western Augustus and the more warlike junior partner, while Diocletian was the senior Augustus and the architect of policy.
  • Galerius: Similar because both were members of the Tetrarchy and both shared responsibility for the persecution of the Christians; differs because Galerius was Diocletian's Caesar (later Augustus) and is usually seen as the instigator of the fiercest persecuting edicts, while Diocletian carried the formal responsibility and is remembered as the senior reformer.
  • Theodosius: Similar because both are late Roman emperors whose reigns mark decisive turns in imperial and Christian history; differs because Theodosius made Christianity the state religion and permanently divided the empire between his sons, while Diocletian preceded the Christian state and divided power among four colleagues.
  • Justinian: Similar because both are great codifying and centralizing Byzantine-Roman emperors remembered for administrative and legal reorganization; differs because Justinian (6th century) ruled a single Christian empire and produced the Corpus Juris, while Diocletian ruled a pagan empire divided among four and is remembered for price controls and the Tetrarchy.
  • the Tetrarchs: Similar because both names denote the college of four emperors instituted by Diocletian; differs because Tetrarchs names the four-man ruling group (Diocletian, Maximian, Galerius, Constantius), while Diocletian is the single emperor who created the group and is remembered personally.
  • reformer-emperor: Similar because both label Diocletian's historical role as the reorganizer of the state; differs because reformer-emperor is a descriptive category applied to several rulers (Augustus, Aurelian, Justinian), while Diocletian is the proper name that anchors the category in the late 3rd century.
