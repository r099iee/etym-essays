- [Old English (c. 9th–11th c.)] - fann borrowed from Latin vannus, denoting an agricultural winnowing basket or shovel used to toss grain into the wind to blow away the chaff - [perception: rustic, biblical, essential agricultural implement]
- [14th–15th c.] - Middle English fan / fanne expands to handheld cooling vanes and military defensive screens - [perception: practical household cooling and defensive gear]
- [16th–18th c.] - folding hand fans imported from East Asia ignite an aristocratic courtly craze; develops into a complex "language of the fan" in European high society; architecture invents "fan vaulting" - [perception: peak of courtly elegance, flirtation, architectural grandeur]
- [Late 19th c.] - American sporting slang clips fanatic into fan to describe baseball enthusiasts; mechanical and electric rotary fans invented for factories and homes - [perception: modern industrial air circulation and popular sporting culture]
- [20th–21st c.] - massive sociological explosion of fandom, digital fan communities, and CPU cooling technology; the marine "sea fan" remains a celebrated standard of oceanic wonder - [perception: foundational concept across computing, global entertainment, and marine biology]
