- [Old Norse Skaldic Era (c. 9th–11th c.)] — developed as a specialized rhetorical noun derived from the Old Norse verb *kenna* ("to perceive, know, recognize; to name, call"), signifying a descriptive mark of identification, poetical naming, or metaphorical circumlocution in Viking skaldic verse — [perception: celebrated by skalds and jarls as the supreme exhibition of intellectual virtuosity, mythological erudition, and elite aristocratic entertainment]
- [Medieval Codification (c. 1220s; Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda)] — codified systematically by Icelandic statesman and scholar Snorri Sturluson in the *Skáldskaparmál*, categorizing skaldic poetic diction into unfigured simplexes (*ókennt heiti*), substitute names (*fornafn*), and complex metaphorical compounds (*kenning*) — [perception: revered as a vital technical textbook preserving pagan mythological lore and poetic grammar in a Christianized medieval Iceland]
- [Early Modern to Enlightenment Eclipse (14th–18th c.)] — the poetic device fell dormant in mainstream European neoclassical poetics; while the base Germanic verb *ken* survived in northern English and Scots dialects ("to know, recognize, perceive"), the specific poetic substantival category of the *kenning* remained virtually unknown to English literary theory — [perception: ignored or viewed as barbarous Gothic obscurity by neoclassical critics who championed classical Greco-Latin rhetorical tropes]
- [Victorian Philological Revival (c. 1870s–1890s)] — resurrected into modern English academic literary criticism and Germanic comparative philology (by scholars translating the Icelandic sagas and Old English *Beowulf*), establishing *kenning* as the standard technical literary term for Germanic compound metaphors — [perception: embraced by Victorian scholars and Romantic poets as an authentic, primal expression of heroic northern imagination and linguistic vigor]
- [20th Century Modernist & Fantasy Resurgence] — actively embraced and creatively reinvented by major 20th-century authors and poets (most notably J.R.R. Tolkien, W.H. Auden, Ezra Pound, and Seamus Heaney) to inject tactile, elemental, alliterative resonance into modern English poetry and fantasy worldbuilding — [perception: admired as a potent artistic vehicle capable of stripping modern verse of Latinate abstraction and restoring physical, earthy evocative power]
- [21st Century Cognitive Linguistics & Comparative Poetics] — generalized across global literary scholarship to analyze figurative compounding, cognitive conceptual metaphors (Lakoff and Johnson), and descriptive periphrastic epithets across diverse world epics, including Sanskrit, Homeric Greek, and Celtic bardic traditions — [perception: recognized universally as a fundamental cognitive mechanism of poetic defamiliarization and compressed metaphorical thought]
