• Walk: Similar because both denote pedestrian locomotion; differs because walk is the neutral generic verb and noun for any stepping motion, while gait names the specific observable manner of the walking.
  • Stride: Similar because both describe how one walks; differs because stride emphasises the length and confidence of individual steps, while gait is the whole manner including rhythm and bearing.
  • Pace: Similar because both concern the speed of walking; differs because pace stresses rate or step-frequency, while gait comprehends the entire bodily manner of movement.
  • Tread: Similar because both describe the action of the feet; differs because tread foregrounds the sound and pressure of steps on the ground, while gait is the whole visible and rhythmic manner.
  • Step: Similar because both relate to the movement of walking; differs because step is a single footfall or its rhythm, while gait is the continuous overall manner of walking.
  • Carriage: Similar because both concern how a person holds and moves the body; differs because carriage focuses on posture and bearing while standing or moving, while gait is specifically the walking performance.
  • Bearing: Similar because both describe the visible demeanour of the body; differs because bearing is a general deportment independent of motion, while gait is specific to walking.
  • Posture: Similar because both are observable bodily manner; differs because posture is the static arrangement of the body, while gait is the dynamic manner of locomotion.
  • Lope: Similar because both are manners of moving on foot; differs because lope is a specific long, bounding stride, while gait is the general category of which lope is one instance.
  • Amble: Similar because both describe a way of walking; differs because amble names a specific easy, unhurried pace, while gait is the neutral category of any manner of walking.
  • Saunter: Similar because both are walks with a describable manner; differs because saunter connotes leisurely, carefree strolling, while gait is neutral and observational.
  • Stroll: Similar because both denote a walk; differs because stroll is a casual walk for pleasure, while gait is a measured, characterisable manner, often diagnostic.
  • March: Similar because both describe a manner of walking; differs because march is a disciplined, rhythmic military step — one specific gait — while gait is the general term.
  • Plod: Similar because both describe walking; differs because plod is a slow, heavy, laborious step, an emotional and physical quality, while gait is a neutral descriptive category.
  • Shuffle: Similar because both describe a manner of walking; differs because shuffle is a specific dragging, sliding step, while gait names the full manner including such a step.
  • Footfall: Similar because both concern the feet in walking; differs because footfall is a single sound or step of the foot, while gait is the continuous overall manner of moving.
