• Muganda: Similar because both refer directly to people belonging to the Ganda ethnic group; differs because Muganda is the singular noun (Class 1 prefix Mu-) denoting a single individual Ganda person, whereas Baganda is the plural collective noun (Class 2 prefix Ba-) denoting two or more individuals or the entire nation.
  • Buganda: Similar because both stem from the identical ethnocultural root -ganda; differs because Buganda is the geographical and territorial proper noun (Class 14 prefix Bu-) referring to the physical land, kingdom, and subnational autonomous realm, while Baganda refers to the people living within it.
  • Luganda: Similar because both belong to the Ganda ethnocultural family; differs because Luganda is the linguistic proper noun (Class 11 prefix Lu-) designating the Bantu language spoken by the people, whereas Baganda designates the human community that speaks it.
  • Banyoro: Similar because both are major Bantu ethnic groups inhabiting neighboring historic Great Lakes monarchies; differs because the Banyoro are the people of the Bunyoro-Kitara Kingdom, who were historic imperial rivals of the Baganda for regional military and territorial dominance.
  • Basoga: Similar because both are Bantu peoples sharing close linguistic affinity and geographic borders along Lake Victoria and the Nile River; differs because the Basoga inhabit Busoga to the east, historically organized into several chiefdoms under the Kyabazinga rather than a single unified Kabaka monarchy.
  • Banyankole: Similar because both are prominent Bantu-speaking populations of Uganda with traditional monarchies; differs because the Banyankole belong to the Ankole Kingdom in southwestern Uganda, with distinct social divisions between pastoralist Bahima and agriculturalist Bairu.
  • Batoro: Similar because both are Great Lakes Bantu monarchical societies; differs because the Batoro inhabit the Toro Kingdom in western Uganda, which originally split off from the ancient Bunyoro-Kitara empire in the early 19th century.
  • Acholi: Similar because both are major constituent ethnic nations within modern Uganda; differs because the Acholi are a Western Nilotic ethnic group from northern Uganda with distinct language, pastoral-lineage clan structures, and cultural heritage, historically contrasted with the centralized Bantu kingdoms of the south.
  • Waganda: Similar because both denote the exact same ethnic people of Buganda; differs because Waganda is the coastal Swahili form (using the Swahili plural personal prefix Wa-) adopted by early 19th-century European explorers, whereas Baganda is the authentic native Luganda ethnonym.
