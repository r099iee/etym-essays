FLUMMOXED is the past participle / participial adjective of the verb FLUMMOX. The word is a British dialectal / slang formation of obscure origin; it is NON-PIE — no accepted ancient lineage, no PIE reconstruction. One linear chain (stages are few because the attested record is short). 1. British dialectal / slang flummox “to perplex, baffle”, with dialect senses also “to throw down untidily, make a mess, fling clumsily” (first recorded in slang print c. 1830s, commonly cited 1834). Origin: uncertain — the OED treats it as of obscure origin; the leading explanation is a regional English expressive / sound-symbolic formation (southwestern and East Anglian dialect stock), the flum- onset (as in flump, flimsy) plus the muffled -m- and slack -mox mimicking a soft collapse. No Latin or Greek intermediary, no Old English ancestor, no PIE root is accepted. 2. English flummox (verb) “to baffle, nonplus, defeat completely” (naturalised quickly in literary slang; Dickens's The Pickwick Papers, 1836–37, helped fix it in print). 3. English flummoxed (past tense / past participle / participial adjective) — the card's exact inflected form — FLUMMOXED.
