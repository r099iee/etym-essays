• Following: Similar because both mean coming after; differs because following is the plain chronological word and can be purely temporal (the following day), with no causal or consequential flavour.
  • Subsequent: Similar because both denote what comes later; differs because subsequent is formal and calendar-like (in subsequent years), weaker on immediacy and consequence than ensuing.
  • Resulting: Similar because both mark an outcome; differs because resulting attaches to the results of processes (resulting damage), while ensuing often names the next phase of action or turmoil.
  • Consequent: Similar because both signal what follows as a result; differs because consequent is more technical and cause-forward (consequent upon), lacking the narrative, sequential tone of ensuing.
  • Succeeding: Similar because both mean next in series; differs because succeeding is used of generations, reigns, and enumerations, and risks confusion with successful.
  • Attendant: Similar because both name what comes with an event; differs because attendant is more formal and describes accompanying circumstances (attendant risks), not the immediate sequel of action.
  • Next: Similar because both mark what comes after; differs because next is the plain ordinal word for any following item, with no causal or consequential force.
  • Resultant: Similar because both denote what results; differs because resultant is technical and force-like (resultant force), whereas ensuing is narrative and literary.
