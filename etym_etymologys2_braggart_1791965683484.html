Middle English (late 14th c.)
braggen
"to boast, to talk big"
↓
Middle French (15th–16th c.)
braguer
"to brag, to swagger"
↓
French (16th c.)
bragard
"a boaster, a swaggerer"
↓
English (1570s)
braggart
"a habitual, loud boaster"

Note: the direction of borrowing is not certain. French braguer may be a borrowing of Middle English braggen, or both may descend independently from a Germanic source; the origin of brag itself is unknown, with imitation of loud talk among the suggested explanations. The -ard of French bragard is the depreciatory suffix also seen in coward and drunkard.
