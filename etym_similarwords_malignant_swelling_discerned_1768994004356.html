• malevolent: Similar to malignant in meaning disposed to harm; differs because malevolent wishes harm and implies a conscious agent, while malignant is a matter of inborn nature — it does not choose evil but is born evil.
  • malicious: Similar in meaning disposed to harm; differs because malicious harms from deliberate spite, while malignant harms by its very nature, whether or not it intends to.
  • virulent: Similar in describing something harmful and rapidly spreading; differs because virulent names poison or disease that spreads fast, while malignant joins inborn badness to unchecked organic growth.
  • baleful: Similar in meaning 'bringing harm'; differs because baleful works through ominous influence or appearance, while malignant names a nature that is itself evil and growing.
  • baneful: Similar in meaning 'causing harm or ruin'; differs because baneful is fatal in effect but passive, while malignant is actively, organically destructive.
  • pernicious: Similar in meaning 'highly destructive'; differs because pernicious is a broad, abstract term for ruinous effect, while malignant adds the sense of inborn, spreading evil.
  • sinister: Similar in suggesting menace and wrongness; differs because sinister evokes left-handed, lurking wrongness, while malignant is open, organic, and growing.
  • expanding: Similar to swelling in increasing in size; differs because expanding is neutral and geometric, while swelling implies growth from within under pressure.
  • distending: Similar in describing unnatural fullness; differs because distending implies strained, abnormal stretching (a belly, a vein), while swelling is the more general internal expansion.
  • billowing: Similar to swelling in describing something rising and growing in volume; differs because billowing suggests soft, cloth-like folds of cloud or sail, while swelling implies growth from within, under pressure, toward rupture.
  • bulging: Similar in describing local enlargement; differs because bulging is local and lumpy, while swelling is a general inflation of the whole body of a thing.
  • inflating: Similar in describing the act of filling with air or substance; differs because inflating implies an external source of pressure, while swelling grows from within.
  • surge: Similar in describing a powerful rising motion (of water, crowds, power); differs because surge is abrupt and wave-like, while swelling is gradual and accumulating.
  • perceived: Similar to discerned in meaning taken in by the senses; differs because perceived is neutral reception, while discerned implies effortful separation from a background.
  • detected: Similar to discerned in meaning perceived after effort; differs because detected is technical and instrumental, while discerned carries the full etymological sense of sifting apart — the patient separation of signal from noise.
  • distinguished: Similar in meaning separated one thing from another; differs because distinguished stresses the noting of difference, while discerned stresses the effort of perceiving through difficulty.
  • descried: Similar in meaning caught sight of at a distance; differs because descried means simply 'espied from afar', while discerned adds the labor of separating the thing from its surroundings.
  • recognized: Similar in meaning identified as known; differs because recognized implies prior acquaintance, while discerned works on the previously unknown.
  • noticed: Similar in meaning perceived; differs because noticed is casual and passive, while discerned is deliberate, attentive, and effortful.
