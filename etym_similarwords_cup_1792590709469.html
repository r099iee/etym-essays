• mug: Similar because both are everyday handheld drinking vessels with handles used for beverages; differs because a mug is typically taller, cylindrical, heavier, crafted from thick ceramic or metal, and used without a saucer, whereas a cup usually features a flared, hemispherical silhouette tapering to a narrow foot and is traditionally paired with a saucer.
  • chalice: Similar because both are goblet-shaped drinking vessels; differs because a chalice is a formal, elevated, often ornate religious or ceremonial vessel (especially for Eucharistic wine or royal toasts), whereas a cup is standard, utilitarian, and domestic.
  • goblet: Similar because both are bowl-shaped vessels used for liquids; differs because a goblet is characterized by a prominent tall vertical stem and broad resting base without handles, designed primarily for wine or banqueting, while a standard cup rests flat on its base and typically possesses a side loop handle.
  • beaker: Similar because both are open containers used to hold and pour liquids; differs because a beaker is either a handleless cylindrical drinking vessel (in historical usage) or a spouted, calibrated glass cylinder used in scientific laboratories, lacking the domestic aesthetic of a teacup.
  • teacup: Similar because both designate small vessels for warm beverages; differs because teacup is a specialized hyponym designating a delicate, thin-walled porcelain or china cup specifically engineered for drinking hot tea from a saucer.
  • tankard: Similar because both are handled drinking vessels; differs because a tankard is a large, heavy, often pewter, silver, or wooden vessel with a hinged lid designed specifically for drinking beer or ale in taverns.
  • tumbler: Similar because both are flat-bottomed beverage vessels; differs because a tumbler is a handleless glass used primarily for cold water, cocktails, or spirits, whereas a cup typically features a handle and serves warm drinks.
  • demitasse: Similar because both are miniature handled cups for hot beverages; differs because a demitasse is a specialized, diminutive cup (literally "half-cup") designed specifically for serving concentrated black espresso coffee.
  • flagon: Similar because both are historical vessels associated with wine and feasts; differs because a flagon is a large vessel equipped with a spout, handle, and lid used for holding and decanting liquids into smaller drinking cups rather than drinking directly from it.
  • cupule: Similar because both denote a small, concave, cup-shaped structure; differs because cupule is a technical botanical and anatomical term applied specifically to the scaly base clasping an acorn or similar plant involucre, whereas cup is the broad everyday word.
  • grail: Similar because both represent sacred or legendary drinking vessels; differs because grail specifically invokes the mythical holy chalice of Arthurian legend (the Holy Grail) symbolizing ultimate spiritual quests and unattainable perfection.
  • stein: Similar because both are ceramic vessels with handles used for drinking; differs because a stein (short for Steingut) is an earthenware or stoneware beer mug, often adorned with relief designs and a pewter thumb-lift lid.
