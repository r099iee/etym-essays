• Recluse: Similar because both name someone who lives apart from society; differs because a recluse may be entirely irreligious — anyone who shuts himself away (Latin recludere, 'to close off') — while a hermit's solitude is a chosen discipline in the wild.
  • Anchorite: Similar because both are religious solitaries; differs because an anchorite was often literally enclosed, walled into a cell attached to a church — a stricter, more institutional solitude than the hermit's free wandering.
  • Ascetic: Similar because both practise self-denial; differs because an ascetic need not be alone at all — monks are ascetics and hermits are ascetics, but the ascetic's austerity is about fasting and poverty, the hermit's about solitude.
  • Solitary: Similar because both describe one who lives alone; differs because 'solitary' is the generic word with no religious colouring — a lighthouse keeper or a widow may be a solitary — while the hermit's aloneness is his profession.
  • Monk: Similar because both are religious who have renounced ordinary society; differs because a monk lives in a community (a monastery), under a rule and among brothers, while a hermit lives utterly alone.
  • Eremite: Similar because it is the same Greek word, erēmítēs, borrowed directly into English as an archaic doublet; differs because 'eremite' is a rare, learned, archaic form, while 'hermit' is the living everyday word — the same person, two spellings, two registers.
  • Loner: Similar because both denote someone who keeps apart from others; differs because a loner is a modern, casual word for a person who prefers his own company, with no religious discipline or wilderness, while a hermit's aloneness is deliberate and often sacred.
  • Introvert: Similar because both describe people who withdraw from society; differs because an introvert is a personality type that is drained by company but lives ordinarily among people, while a hermit physically removes himself from society — inwardness of temperament versus outward withdrawal of life.
