- [Late Vedic Period, c. 900–600 BCE] - recognized in the Brāhmaṇas and Upaniṣads as the historical sovereign of Videha, a pioneer of royal metaphysical debate and the generous patron of sage Yājñavalkya - [perception: viewed with awe as an extraordinary Kshatriya monarch who possessed spiritual knowledge surpassing that of the orthodox Brahmin priesthood]
- [Epic & Purāṇic Era, c. 500 BCE–300 CE] - crystallized in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa as Sīradhvaja Janaka, the loving father of Sītā who set the trial of Lord Śiva's bow, and in the Bhagavad Gītā as the archetypal master of karma-yoga - [perception: revered as the ideal righteous ruler and devoted father who attained liberation through selfless duty without world-renunciation]
- [Classical Sanskrit & Medieval Commentarial Era, c. 500–1600 CE] - analyzed by philosophers such as Śaṅkarācārya, Rāmānuja, and Madhva as a vital case study in whether an enlightened householder can attain liberation while maintaining political power - [perception: celebrated as the premier proof that worldly stewardship (lokasaṅgraha) and supreme non-dual wisdom can seamlessly coexist in a single soul]
- [Modern Era, 19th–21st c.] - maintained in popular culture as the dignified father of Sītā and a symbol of wise, uncorrupted governance, while persisting in modern Indo-Aryan languages as a formal common noun for "father, founder, or originator" - [perception: honored as an eternal emblem of serene paternal grace, philosophical integrity, and selfless public leadership]
