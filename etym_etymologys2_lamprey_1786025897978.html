Element 1 — Latin lambere "to lick":

PIE *leb-

"to lick; lip"
(reconstructed root; also the source of
Latin labium "lip" — the root itself is
reconstructed, not attested)

↓

Latin lambere

"to lick, lap"
(attested from the 2nd c. BCE)


Element 2 — petra "rock, stone":

Greek pétra (πέτρα)

"rock, stone, cliff"
(attested from Homer; likely a
Pre-Greek substrate word — its own
origin is unknown, NOT from PIE)

↓

Latin petra

"rock, stone"
(borrowed from Greek, 1st c. BCE)


Compounding in Late Latin — the two elements merge:

Late Latin lampetra / lampreda

"lamprey; literally 'stone-licker'"
(compound of lambere "to lick" + petra "stone";
attested in Pliny, 1st c. CE)

↓

Old French lamproie

"lamprey"
(attested from the 12th c.)

↓

Middle English lamprey / lampreie

"lamprey; a prized culinary delicacy"
(attested from the 13th c.)

↓

LAMPREY

"eel-like jawless fish with a suctorial mouth;
historically a royal culinary delicacy"
(Modern English)

Note: the Greek pétra element is not of PIE origin — it is
classed as a Pre-Greek substrate word, so this branch of the
chain stops at Greek; the lambere branch descends from PIE
*leb-. The exact derivation of lampetra (whether from
lambere + petra directly, or via lambēre + petra with
vowel change) is agreed in substance but debated in detail.
