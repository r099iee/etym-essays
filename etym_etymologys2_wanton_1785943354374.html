WANTON is a compound: two root words must each be traced in isolation. ROOT ELEMENT 1 — the prefix wan- "lacking, un-":
  • Reconstructed Proto-Indo-European *h₁wenh₁- — "to lack, be empty, be exhausted" (c. 4500–3000 BCE).
  • Proto-Germanic *wanaz / *wanō — "lacking, deficient, wanting" (c. 500 BCE–200 CE).
  • Old English wan- — prefix meaning "lacking, un-, not, bad" (attested 8th c. onward), as in wanhǣd "poverty," wanhope "despair," and the compound's own wantowen.
ROOT ELEMENT 2 — the participle towen/togen "trained, brought up":
  • Reconstructed Proto-Indo-European *dewk- — "to pull, lead, draw" (c. 4500–3000 BCE).
  • Proto-Germanic *teuhaną — "to pull, draw, lead" (c. 500 BCE–200 CE).
  • Old English tēon — "to pull, draw, bring up, educate, rear" (strong verb, class II; attested 8th c. onward).
  • Old English past participle tōgen / towen — "drawn, trained, brought up, educated" — the element in the compound.
RESULTANT COMPOUND:
  • Middle English wan-towen → wantoun — "undisciplined, ill-reared, unruly, wild" (14th c.).
  • Modern English WANTON — "undisciplined; playful; unchaste; deliberately cruel" (bold target).
Notes on nuance and debate: Old English tēon is one of two homonymous verbs — tēon "to draw, lead, educate" (from Germanic *teuhaną, cognate with Latin dūcere "to lead") and a separate tēon "to accuse" (from Germanic *tīhaną, cognate with Latin dīcere "to say"); the wanton compound is built on the "draw/educate" verb. The negative prefix wan- must also be distinguished from the unrelated adjective wan "pale" (Old English wann "dark, dusky"). Latin dūcere "to lead" is the same PIE *dewk- and lies behind English educate, conduct, duct, and duke.
