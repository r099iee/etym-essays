• Side: Similar because it names the lateral part of anything; differs because it is fully general (side of a room, an argument, a person), while flank is specifically the fleshy side of a body, the tactical side of an army, or the lateral surface of an elongated object.
  • Haunch: Similar because it denotes a hind part of an animal's body; differs because it is the upper thigh and buttock region, used of quadrupeds and venison, while the flank lies further forward between ribs and hip.
  • Hip: Similar because it is a lateral body region; differs because it is the bony joint area itself, while the flank extends forward of the hip along the ribs and abdomen.
  • Loin: Similar because it occupies the neighbouring region of the body; differs because it is the lower-back and kidney zone — dorsal rather than lateral — and is primarily a culinary cut (loin of pork), whereas flank is the side and the side-cut of beef.
  • Thigh: Similar because it is part of the leg-trunk region; differs because it is the leg segment below the torso, while the flank is part of the torso wall itself.
  • Wing: Similar because it names a projecting side part of an army or building (wing of a castle, wing of an advance); differs because a wing implies a protruding extension reaching forward or outward, while a flank is the bare side line of the body seen from the side.
  • Border: Similar because it is an outer boundary; differs because it is a static geometric edge of objects, territories and pages, with no body, army or vulnerability attached.
  • Edge: Similar because it is the rim or margin of something; differs because it is the thinnest bounding line, abstract and neutral, while a flank is a substantial side surface that can be wounded, attacked or defended.
  • Skirt (verb): Similar because it means to lie along the edge of something; differs because it implies passing around or avoiding the boundary, while "to flank" is to stand at the side of, protect it, or attack it from the side.
  • Fringe: Similar because it names the outer edge or margin; differs because it suggests a decorative or marginal strip (fringe of a forest, of society), not a defensive or anatomical side to be guarded.
