• Werewolves: Similar because both are "were-" compounds naming shape-shifting or man-beast hybrids; differs because werewolves are the famous man-wolf model of folklore, while were-worms are dragon-serpents of desert legend.
  • Dragons: Similar because both are mythical serpent-like monsters of great power; differs because dragons are the generic, common fantasy creature, while were-worms keep the rare, archaic "were-" prefix and a desert-edge, mock-heroic flavour.
  • Worms: Similar because both use the old English word for "serpent, dragon"; differs because ordinary worms are annelids or larvae, while were-worms are fabulous dragon-serpents in the heroic sense of wyrm.
  • Serpents: Similar because both denote long, crawling, dangerous reptiles; differs because serpents are literal snakes in the natural world, while were-worms are legendary man-related worm-dragons.
  • Wyverns: Similar because both are dragon-type creatures of heraldry and legend; differs because a wyvern is a specific two-legged winged heraldic beast, while were-worms are a Tolkien coinage of man-related desert serpents.
  • Lindworms: Similar because both are "worm"-dragons of Germanic legend; differs because a lindworm is a specific serpent-dragon of northern European folklore, while were-worms are Tolkien's desert-edge invention.
  • Wyrm: Similar because both preserve the old serpent-dragon sense of the root; differs because wyrm is the Old English/archaic word itself, while were-worms is a compound built on its plural.
  • Monsters: Similar because both name terrifying legendary creatures; differs because monster is generic for any frightful beast, while were-worms specify the man-related dragon-serpent type.
