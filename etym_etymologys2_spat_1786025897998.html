Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*(s)pyū- / *spēu-
"to spit, hiss, blow (echoic)"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*spittaną / *spat-
"to spit, strike lightly, splash"
↓
Old English (c. 9th c. CE)
spǣtan / spittan
"to spit, emit moisture"
↓
Middle English (c. 13th–14th c. CE)
spatten / spetten
"to strike, splash, spit"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 17th–18th c. CE)
spat
"a light blow, a smack, a sound of a slap"
↓
Modern English (c. 1804–Present)
spat
"a petty quarrel, brief verbal dispute"

Note on Homonyms: Three distinct words share the form spat: (1) the noun for a petty dispute (detailed above); (2) the zoological noun for oyster spawn (from the verb spit/ejection, attested c. 1620); and (3) the footwear noun spats (short for spatterdashes, from spatter + dash, c. 1780).
