- Medieval Latin — to escort on the way: conviare names the servant's duty of walking beside a traveller or cart for protection.
- 14th c. — carrying and escorting in English: conveyen moves goods, people, and secret messages under guard, sometimes by stealth.
- 16th–17th c. — widening to every channel: pipes convey water, nerves convey spirits, words convey meaning; the load turns invisible.
- 18th–19th c. — law and politeness adopt it: deeds convey estates, letters convey thanks and compliments; the word grows genteel and official.
- Today — freight and feeling share one road: lorries and conveyor belts convey parcels while voices and screens convey ideas, one ancient way carrying both.
