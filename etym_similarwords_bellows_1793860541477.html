• Blower: Similar because both denote mechanical devices engineered to generate a directed stream of air; differs because a blower is a broad, modern industrial term typically employing a high-speed rotating fan, turbine, or electric impeller, whereas a bellows operates through the manual or mechanical expansion and compression of a flexible, pleated chamber.
  • Air pump: Similar because both devices compress and propel air through valves; differs because an air pump generally uses rigid pistons, cylinders, or diaphragms to generate high pneumatic pressure inside closed vessels (such as bicycle tires), whereas a bellows delivers a large volume of air at relatively low pressure.
  • Lungs: Similar because human and mammalian lungs function via the identical negative-pressure mechanics of volumetric expansion and contraction; differs because lungs are biological respiratory organs, though bellows is universally used as their primary mechanical metaphor.
  • Fan: Similar because both create air currents; differs because a fan circulates air continuously through revolving inclined blades without enclosing it, whereas a bellows gathers air into an airtight chamber and discharges it in focused, intermittent blasts.
  • Ventilator: Similar because both provide forced airflow to maintain combustion or respiration; differs because a ventilator is either an architectural duct system or a complex medical life-support machine that automatically regulates patient pulmonary cycles.
  • Compressor: Similar because both compress ambient air to drive a mechanical process; differs because a compressor operates under severe pressure for heavy industrial tools, refrigeration, and pneumatic piping, whereas a bellows operates at near-atmospheric pressures.
  • Accordion: Similar because an accordion utilizes pleated, folding leather-and-cloth bellows as its central acoustic engine; differs because an accordion is a musical instrument designed to channel that airflow across tuned free-metal reeds to produce pitch and melody, rather than stoking fire or moving bulk air.
