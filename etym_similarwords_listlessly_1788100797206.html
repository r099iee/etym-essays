• Languidly: Similar because both name movement emptied of energy and quickness; differs because languidly suggests a soft, graceful, often deliberate weakness (heat, fever, elegance), while listlessly suggests a dull absence of will and interest.
  • Apathetically: Similar because both mean “without feeling or concern”; differs because apathetically is colder, more clinical, and attitudinal — the person does not care — whereas listlessly is sadder and more physical: the person seems unable to want.
  • Indifferently: Similar because both can describe doing something without engagement; differs because indifferently stresses emotional flatness and unconcern toward an object, while listlessly stresses depleted energy; an indifferent waiter is rude, a listless one is ill or exhausted.
  • Sluggishly: Similar because both describe slowness; differs because sluggishly is physiological and tempo-focused (like a slug), with no necessary sadness, while listlessly is about lost desire and will.
  • Lethargically: Similar because both describe heavy, unresponsive movement; differs because lethargically belongs to medicine and disease (lethargy, sleepiness) and sounds heavier and more clinical than the mildly literary listlessly.
  • Wearily: Similar because both imply exhaustion; differs because wearily points to physical or mental tiredness caused by effort, while listlessly points to lack of motive; one is weary after work, listless before it.
  • Limply: Similar because both describe slack, unmuscled action; differs because limply is purely physical (a limp hand, a limp body) with no emotional colour, while listlessly carries apathy and pathos.
  • Idly: Similar because both describe doing nothing purposeful; differs because idly means “without occupation” and can even be contented (idle hours), while listlessly implies restless emptiness and sadness.
  • Aimlessly: Similar because both describe action without a purpose; differs because aimlessly is about direction (wandering without a goal) and is neutral, while listlessly is about energy itself having gone.
  • Half-heartedly: Similar because both describe low commitment; differs because half-heartedly implies a conscious refusal to try hard, while listlessly implies an inability or drained condition; half-hearted is chosen, listless is suffered.
  • Unenthusiastically: Similar because both describe the absence of eagerness; differs because unenthusiastically is a cooler judgement about attitude, while listlessly is a sadder observation of demeanour.
  • Spiritlessly / Lifelessly: Similar because both suggest drained vitality and mechanical movement; differs because they are stronger and more final (as if life itself were gone), while listlessly can describe temporary fatigue or mild apathy.
