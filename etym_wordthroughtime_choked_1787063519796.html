- Old English (10th c.) — strictly physical: throttled, suffocated, unable to breathe.
- Middle English (13th c.) — expands to external strangling, gagging on food, and blocked throats; also a narrowing passage.
- 16th–18th centuries — mechanical senses: choked pipes, choked drains, choked soil; the "choke" later names the engine valve that starves the carburettor of air.
- 19th century — emotional extension: "choked with emotion / anger / tears", a voice stopped by feeling.
- 20th–21st centuries — performance slang: "he choked" = failed under pressure; "choked up" remains emotional, "choke hold" is tactical, and "choke" enters electronics as an RF choke.
