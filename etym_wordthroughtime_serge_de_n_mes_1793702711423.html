- **17th-18th Century (France)** - "Serge de Nîmes" emerges as a specific, durable twilled fabric, likely wool or cotton, produced in Nîmes. It is primarily a regional textile, known for its robustness and utility, used for workwear and everyday garments.
- **Late 18th - Mid 19th Century (Global Trade)** - The fabric gains wider recognition through trade, particularly in the Americas. It is valued for its strength and indigo color, becoming a popular choice for tough work clothes among laborers, sailors, and miners. The full French phrase is used, but its length begins to lead to informal shortening.
- **Mid 19th Century (Anglicization & Shortening)** - English speakers begin to shorten and anglicize "serge de Nîmes" to "denim." The original phrase gradually recedes from common usage in favor of the abbreviated form. Its connotation shifts from a specific French product to a generic, globally recognized fabric, especially associated with American workwear.
- **Late 19th Century - Present (Historical Reference)** - "Serge de Nîmes" is now primarily a historical and etymological term, used to refer to the origin of "denim." It is no longer used to describe the fabric itself in contemporary contexts, but serves as a linguistic artifact highlighting the fabric's French roots and the process of linguistic evolution.
