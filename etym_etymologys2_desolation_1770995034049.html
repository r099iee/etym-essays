PIE *s(w)e- "oneself, one's own; apart, separate" (reconstructed, c. 4500-3500 BCE) → Latin solus "alone, sole, solitary, single" (attested 3rd-1st c. BCE) → Latin desolare "to leave utterly alone, to abandon, to lay waste" (de- "thoroughly, completely" + solus) (1st c. BCE-1st c. CE, classical) → Late Latin desolatio "a laying waste, a making desolate" (Vulgate, 4th-5th c. CE) → Old French desolacioun / desolation "ruin, destruction, misery" (12th-14th c.) → Middle English desolacioun (late 14th c.) → desolation (modern English, /ˌdɛsəˈleɪʃən/) Note: the root *s(w)e- is a reflexive pronoun "self"; the step to Latin solus "alone" carries the sense of the self as apart from the group. The biblical register ("abomination of desolation") comes through Late Latin desolatio in the Vulgate.
