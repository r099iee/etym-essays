- c. 1100–1400 - [Old Northern French / Picard] - Existed purely as a local rural toponym (*Aisencort* / *Azincourt*) in Artois, signifying the feudal farmstead or estate of a Germanic lord named Azo.
- 1415–1500 - [Late Medieval English & French Historiography] - Suddenly catapulted into international prominence following Henry V's victory on October 25, 1415; perceived as a terrifying slaughter of French nobility and a miraculous divine vindication of English royal claims.
- c. 1599–1700 - [Elizabethan & Early Modern Drama] - Elevated by William Shakespeare's *Henry V* into a patriotic monument of national identity, brotherhood, and yeoman valor; perceived as the ultimate symbol of British martial spirit.
- c. 1800–1900 - [Victorian Romanticism & Historical Novelty] - Romanticized in Victorian historical painting, poetry, and school curricula as an emblem of unshakeable British pluck against continental hegemony.
- c. 1900–Present - [Modern Cultural Idiom] - Widely used as a figurative archetype for heroic underdog triumphs across politics, business, and sports, while also serving as a classic vehicle for humorous, mock-chivalric bathos in English literature.
