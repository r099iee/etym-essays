• Cow parsley: Similar because it is the harmless twin of hemlock, the same lacy white umbellifer of spring hedges; differs because cow parsley is innocent forage and early greenery, while hemlock is spotted, mousy-smelling, and deadly.
  • Fool's parsley: Similar because it is another poisonous lookalike in the same family; differs because fool's parsley is a smaller garden weed of cultivated ground, while hemlock is the tall ditch-side killer of the wild.
  • Water dropwort: Similar because it is a white-flowered umbelliferous poison, reckoned the most deadly plant in Britain; differs because it haunts wet ditches and its tuberous roots kill, while hemlock is a plant of dry waste and its coniine paralyses.
  • Wild carrot: Similar because its white flower clusters and divided leaves closely resemble hemlock; differs because wild carrot is harmless, hairy-stemmed, and carries a single pink floret at the cluster's heart, while hemlock is smooth, spotted, and fatal.
  • Henbane: Similar because it is a classic poison plant of waste ground with a dark literary past; differs because henbane is a nightshade with sticky foul flowers and madness rather than paralysis, while hemlock kills cleanly upward.
  • Nightshade: Similar because both are the very type of the beautiful poison plant; differs because nightshade tempts with sweet black berries, while hemlock deceives by looking like dinner herbs.
  • Monkshood: Similar because it is another tall handsome killer of damp places; differs because monkshood's blue hooded flowers are unmistakable, while hemlock's danger lies in looking ordinary.
  • Foxglove: Similar because it is a tall biennial poison of hedges and clearings; differs because foxglove advertises itself in purple bells and yields heart medicine, while hemlock offers no medicine and hides among lookalikes.
  • Ragwort: Similar because it is a familiar tall yellow weed of neglected pasture; differs because ragwort poisons grazing livestock slowly through the liver, while hemlock strikes quickly at the nerves of whoever eats it.
