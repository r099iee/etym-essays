Proto-Indo-European *h₁widʰéwh₂ (built on the root *h₁widʰ- "to be empty, to separate, to be bereft") "widow; the woman separated by death" (an archaic feminine formation, attested indirectly across the whole family) → Proto-Germanic *widuwōn / *widu(w)ǭ "widow" → West Germanic → Old English widewe / widuwe / wuduwe "widow" (attested; also the legal status-word of Old English law-codes) → Middle English widwe / widow (forms levelled toward "widow", 14th c.) → Modern English widow → plural WIDOWS Note: the male counterpart widower is a later Middle English analogical formation (14th c.), built on the agent-noun pattern — showing that the base word was felt to be inherently feminine; cognates include Sanskrit vidhávā, Latin vidua, Old Church Slavonic vъdova, Gothic widuwō, German Witwe, Dutch weduwe.
