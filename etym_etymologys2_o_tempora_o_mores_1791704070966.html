O

Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*ō / *oh
"exclamatory particle of emotion"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 63 BCE)
ō
"interjection of vocative / accusative exclamation"
↓
Classical Latin Oratory (Cicero) (63 BCE)
O tempora, O mores
"Oh, the times! Oh, the customs!"

Note: Syntactically, O tempora, O mores represents an exclamatory accusative or vocative construction standard in classical Latin rhetoric. Marcus Tullius Cicero first delivered this immortal phrase on November 8, 63 BCE, in his First Catilinarian Oration, uniting the external historical circumstances (tempora) and the internal moral character of society (mores) into a singular, balanced oratorical denunciation.
