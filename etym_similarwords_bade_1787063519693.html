• Commanded: Similar because “bade” can mean “commanded”; differs because commanded is current and plain, while bade is the literary past of “bid”.
  • Ordered: Similar because both can express an authoritative instruction; differs because ordered is the everyday modern word, while bade is archaic and broader (also “asked, greeted”).
  • Instructed: Similar because both can mean giving direction; differs because instructed implies teaching or formal briefing, not a solemn command.
  • Told: Similar because both can mean directing someone to do something; differs because told is neutral and modern, while bade has a solemn, old-fashioned ring.
  • Requested: Similar because “bade” can mean “asked”; differs because requested is polite and modern, while bade's asking sense is archaic.
  • Asked: Similar because “bade” descends from the verb “to ask” (Old English biddan); differs because asked is the plain modern word for posing a question or request.
  • Enjoined: Similar because both mean urgently commanded; differs because enjoined is formal and legalistic, still current in that register.
  • Directed: Similar because both can mean giving an order; differs because directed implies pointing the way or managing, without the farewell/greeting sense.
  • Charged: Similar because both can mean entrusted with a duty; differs because charged implies a formal responsibility, while bade is simply an instruction.
  • Welcomed: Similar because “bade” can frame a greeting (“bade them welcome”); differs because welcomed is the modern verb for receiving someone, while bade merely introduces the greeting.
  • Summoned: Similar because both can mean calling someone to attend; differs because summoned is modern and implies formal authority.
