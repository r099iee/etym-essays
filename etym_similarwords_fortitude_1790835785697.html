• Courage: Similar because both represent the ability to confront fear, pain, or danger; differs because courage is the broad, common term for the active quality of heart that meets any danger, whereas fortitude emphasizes quiet, prolonged mental endurance and moral steadfastness.
  • Bravery: Similar because both involve facing peril without retreating; differs because bravery often suggests showy, spontaneous, or physical daring in immediate combat, whereas fortitude is an internal, disciplined moral virtue that endures prolonged suffering.
  • Resilience: Similar because both describe the capacity to survive adversity; differs because resilience is a modern psychological and mechanical term focusing on the ability to bounce back or recover quickly from hardship, whereas fortitude emphasizes standing firm and bearing the weight during the trial.
  • Stoicism: Similar because both involve enduring hardship without complaint; differs because stoicism often implies emotional detachment, indifference to pleasure and pain, or the suppression of feeling, whereas fortitude is active moral strength that feels pain deeply but endures it with dignity.
  • Endurance: Similar because both involve the capacity to last through suffering; differs because endurance can be purely mechanical or physiological (such as cardiovascular stamina), lacking the profound moral and spiritual dimensions inherent in fortitude.
  • Mettle: Similar because both refer to underlying strength of character; differs because mettle highlights someone's innate spirit, vigor, and fighting temperament when challenged, rather than long-term patient bearing of affliction.
  • Intrepidity: Similar because both describe fearlessness in danger; differs because intrepidity emphasizes being bold, undaunted, and daringly audacious in the face of unknown perils.
  • Hardihood: Similar because both suggest toughness; differs because hardihood often connotes rugged physical robustness, boldness, or even reckless audacity.
