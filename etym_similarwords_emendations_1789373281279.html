• Corrections: Similar because both involve remedying errors, blunders, and inaccuracies in a text; differs because correction is a broad, common term used for any simple fix (fixing a typo on a sign or correcting a grade), whereas emendation is a scholarly, formal term specifically applied to the erudite philological restoration of corrupt textual passages.
  • Amendments: Similar because both refer to formal changes introduced into a document to improve, update, or correct it; differs because amendments typically operates within legislative, political, and constitutional frameworks (amending a constitution or contract clause), whereas emendations primarily pertains to literary manuscripts, textual criticism, and editorial restoration.
  • Revisions: Similar because both involve re-examining and altering written work to improve its quality; differs because revision encompasses broad, large-scale rewriting, restructuring, and changing of tone carried out by the author themselves during composition, whereas emendations consists of targeted, surgical corrections made to eliminate corruption.
  • Rectifications: Similar because both denote setting right that which is wrong, crooked, or defective; differs because rectification emphasizes moral justice, procedural fairness, or mechanical calibration (rectifying an administrative injustice or calibrating an instrument), lacking the specific literary and scribal focus of emendation.
  • Alterations: Similar because both indicate changes made to an existing text; differs because alteration is completely neutral or even suspicious, capable of signifying arbitrary, unauthorized meddling, disfigurement, or tampering (as contrasted in the source text), whereas emendation implies a principled, legitimate correction aimed at restoring authorial authenticity.
  • Conjectures (Conjectural Emendations): Similar because both are intellectual interventions proposed by scholars to resolve corrupt textual cruxes; differs because conjecture emphasizes an educated guess or hypothesis made in the total absence of manuscript evidence, whereas emendations encompasses both manuscript-proven corrections and conjectural readings.
