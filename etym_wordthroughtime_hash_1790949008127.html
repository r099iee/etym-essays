- Mid-17th Century - Borrowed into English from French hachis as a fashionable culinary term for minced meat reheated in gravy.
- 18th Century - Emergence of colloquial figurative idioms ('make a hash of') denoting clumsy botching, mutilation of arguments, or thorough muddle.
- Late 19th Century - Slang clipping of Arabic hashish enters vernacular English, introducing an independent homophonic substance sense.
- Mid-20th Century - Computer scientists adopt 'hash' and 'hashing' to describe chopping records and mapping arbitrary keys into pseudo-random array slots.
- Late 20th to 21st Century - Telecommunications and social media cement 'hash mark' (#) and 'hashtag' as universal terms in global digital communication.
