• stripped: Similar because both remove something covering a person or entity; differs because stripping is rapid, forcible, and physical — one strips a bed or humiliates a man by stripping him of rank — while divesting is systematic, legal, even ceremonial.
  • deprived: Similar because both take something away from someone; differs because deprived is morally loaded, denying what a person needs or is owed (liberty, sight, sustenance), while divested is the formal, neutral removal of possessions, rights, or identity.
  • dispossessed: Similar because both strip a person of property; differs because dispossession is specifically legal and proprietary — the loss of land, house, or inheritance — while divesting covers clothing, office, identity, and finance as well.
  • denuded: Similar because both mean stripped bare; differs because denude is technical and belongs to land or surfaces — a hill denuded of trees — while divested is personal and systemic, used of a person and what clothes their being.
  • unclothed: Similar because both remove clothing; differs because unclothe is simple, physical, and without consequence, while divested is final and consequential — the end of a long unwinding of everything a person has been.
  • disrobed: Similar because both remove garments; differs because disrobing is ceremonial or theatrical, the formal taking off of robes, while divesting strips property, rights, and identity as well as clothing.
  • bereft: Similar because both name a state of having lost something; differs because bereft is poetic and emotive, stressing sorrowful lack, while divested is clinical and formal, stressing systematic removal.
  • bereaved: Similar because both involve severe loss; differs because bereaved is reserved for loss through death — grief, not procedure — while divested is the neutral stripping of what one possessed.
  • expropriated: Similar because both remove property; differs because expropriation is the state's or authority's seizure of private property for public use — institutional and coercive — while divesting may be reflexive, voluntary, or financial.
  • disenfranchised: Similar because both strip rights; differs because disenfranchisement specifically removes the right to vote or citizenship, a civil-status act, while divesting covers the whole range of possessions, powers, and identities.
  • defrocked: Similar because both strip a person of office and garments; differs because defrocking is the specific degradation of clergy from holy orders, while divesting is the general word for stripping office, clothing, or identity.
  • relieved: Similar because both remove a burden or duty from someone; differs because relieved is neutral and often kindly (relieved of duty, relieved of his load), while divested implies the fuller, more radical removal of everything one has been.
  • disinvested: Similar because both withdraw money from an enterprise; differs because disinvest (the finance term, also divest) is the corporate act of selling holdings or ceasing investment, while divest itself has broadened from finance to identity.
  • dismantled: Similar because both take something apart; differs because dismantle is used of structures, machines, and systems — not persons — while divest applies to a person and everything that clothes or constitutes them.
