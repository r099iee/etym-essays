PIE *dʰen- 'to strike, hit' (attested in Greek theínein 'to strike, smite'; the Germanic formations are generally taken to continue a nasal-infixed grade of this root) → Proto-Germanic *duntiz 'a blow, stroke, thrust' (the exact dental formation here has no universally agreed reconstruction and is debated) → Old English dynt 'a blow, stroke' of a weapon, fist, or fate (8th–11th c.) → Middle English dynt/dint 'blow, force,' with the variant spelling dent rising from the 14th c. and beginning to shift toward 'the mark left by a blow' → Modern English DENT (noun and verb), with dint surviving in the idiom 'by dint of.' Note on the debate: the connection of Proto-Germanic *duntiz to PIE *dʰen- 'strike' is plausible and widely cited but not universally accepted; some authorities treat the Germanic word as an isolated formation of uncertain, possibly imitative, origin. Cognates proper to the Germanic branch include Old Norse dyntr 'a blow' and Old High German tunst 'a blow, push.'
