• pestilence: Similar because it denotes deadly epidemic disease; differs because pestilence is literary-biblical and almost always lethal, while plague ranges from Black Death to classroom teasing.
  • epidemic / pandemic: Similar because they denote spreading disease; differs because they are neutral epidemiological terms of scale, while plague adds horror and moral judgment.
  • scourge: Similar because it means widespread affliction (a scourge of war); differs because scourge stresses whipping punishment, while plague stresses clinging torment.
  • affliction: Similar because it is suffering imposed; differs because affliction is formal and often spiritual, while plague is vivid and often physical.
  • torment / tormentor: Similar in the personal sense (a tormenting child); differs because torment stresses inflicted pain, while plague stresses persistent pestering.
  • nuisance: Similar because it is an everyday word for the minor-plague sense; differs because nuisance is mild and legalistic, while plague even jocularly keeps a sting.
  • pest: Similar because a pest is a tormenting person or creature; differs because pest is colloquial and small-scale, while plague can be cosmic.
  • bane: Similar because it is a cause of ruin or woe; differs because bane is archaic-poetic, while plague is biblical-medical and still productive as a verb.
  • curse: Similar because a curse afflicts like a plague; differs because curse invokes spell or oath, while plague invokes disease or harassment.
  • blight: Similar because it withers crops, hopes, and districts; differs because blight is botanical-metaphorical (mildew, decay), while plague is epidemiological and personal.
