- Pictish pert - wood, copse, thicket by a ford; grove, possibly sacred, Brittonic
- Roman Bertha - fort on the Tay three miles north; military and Latinised
- 1125 David I's burgh - walled market, castle, St John's Kirk; royal and commercial
- 14th-15th c. uncrowned capital - parliaments, Scone coronations, Gowrie power; political and splendid
- 1559 Reformation flashpoint - Knox preaches, friaries sacked; zealous and violent
- 1715-1745 Jacobite Perth - twice occupied, gateway to Highlands; contested and anxious
- 1820s Ruskin's aunts' Perth - Bridge-end, gooseberries, cold mutton; provincial and pious
- 1829 new Perth abroad - Swan River colony named for old Perth; colonial and sunny
- Modern Fair City - administrative centre, concert hall, St Johnstone FC; heritage and everyday
