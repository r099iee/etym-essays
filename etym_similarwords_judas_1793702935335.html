• Traitor: Similar because it denotes someone who betrays their country, a cause, or a person; differs because "Judas" specifically implies a betrayal of a sacred trust or a close personal relationship, often with a religious or moral dimension, carrying a heavier historical and emotional weight.
  • Betrayer: Similar because it refers to one who commits an act of betrayal; differs because "Judas" is a more specific and potent epithet, drawing on the biblical archetype, suggesting a profound and often self-serving act of perfidy.
  • Renegade: Similar because it describes someone who deserts a party or cause for another; differs because a renegade often openly changes allegiance, whereas a "Judas" implies a more insidious, deceptive, and often personal betrayal from within.
  • Turncoat: Similar because it refers to a person who switches allegiance, especially in a disloyal way; differs because "Judas" carries a stronger moral condemnation and a sense of profound personal treachery, often involving a feigned friendship or loyalty.
  • Quisling: Similar because it refers to a traitor who collaborates with an occupying enemy force; differs because "Judas" is broader, applying to personal and ideological betrayals, not just national ones, and predates "quisling" by millennia, carrying a more universal and ancient resonance.
  • Snake in the grass: Similar because it describes a treacherous person who pretends to be a friend; differs because "Judas" is a direct epithet for the betrayer, while "snake in the grass" is a more metaphorical idiom for a hidden danger or deceitful person.
