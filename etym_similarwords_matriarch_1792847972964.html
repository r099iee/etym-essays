• Patriarch: Similar because both name the supreme elder of a family or people; differs because a patriarch is the ruling father, while a matriarch is the ruling mother, and history has supplied far more of the former.
  • Dowager: Similar because both describe dignified elderly women of standing; differs because a dowager holds rank by widowhood and inheritance, while a matriarch rules by living personal authority.
  • Grandmother: Similar because most matriarchs are literally grandmothers; differs because grandmother names the bare kinship, while matriarch adds command, reverence, and sway.
  • Dame: Similar because both honour commanding older women; differs because dame is a formal title or theatrical rank, while matriarch describes earned family sovereignty.
  • Queen mother: Similar because both are revered maternal figures above the current generation's rulers; differs because queen mother is a royal constitutional position, while matriarch needs no crown but character.
  • Foundress: Similar because both can found lines, houses, or orders; differs because a foundress originates the institution, while a matriarch governs the going concern, often generations later.
  • Abbess: Similar because an abbess rules her convent as a matriarch rules her kin; differs because the abbess holds elected ecclesiastical office, while the matriarch holds sway by blood and age.
  • Clan mother: Similar because in Iroquois and other traditions she appoints chiefs and guards custom; differs because clan mother is a specific constitutional office, while matriarch is the general English word.
  • Elder: Similar because both command respect through seniority and memory; differs because elder is ungendered and often collegial, while matriarch is specifically the one great woman at the summit.
