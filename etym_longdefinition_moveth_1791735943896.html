moveth (archaic 3rd-person singular present indicative of move)

Primary Definition: Moves; changes place, position, or posture; is in kinetic physical motion; stirs, creeps, glides, ambulates, or lives. It represents the historical Early Modern and Middle English southern verbal inflection carrying the third-person singular suffix -eth (corresponding to contemporary standard English moves). In its foundational scriptural context in the King James Bible (Genesis 1:21: "every living creature that moveth"), it serves as the definitive biblical mark of animate vitality, distinguishing living, self-propelled organisms from inanimate geological matter.

Polysemy, Contextual Nuances, and Domain-Specific Usages:

  • Animate and Biological Self-Locomotion: To stir, creep, crawl, swim, or progress by organic muscular volition. In ancient Hebrew and Early Modern English cosmology, the capacity for autonomous motion was the defining ontological characteristic of animal life (Hebrew remes or sherets, translated as "creeping/moving thing"). Semantic Bridge: Grounded in direct observation; unlike plants rooted in soil or inanimate stones that remain stationary unless disturbed by external forces, living animals possess an internal spirit or vital breath that 'moveth' them across the landscape or through the waters.
  • Emotional and Spiritual Stirring (Affective Motion): To evoke deep emotional resonance, pity, tenderness, reverence, or moral compunction within the human soul or heart (e.g., the sad spectacle moveth him to tears, the spirit of God moveth upon the waters). Semantic Bridge: A direct metaphorical transposition from physical space to the human psyche; when an external stimulus alters a person's inner psychological equilibrium, the emotional state is conceptualized as being 'stirred' or 'moved' from rest into passion.
  • Judicial, Legislative, and Procedural Initiative: To formally propose a resolution, petition a magistrate, or introduce a legislative motion in a court of law or parliamentary assembly (e.g., the petitioner moveth the honorable court for relief). Semantic Bridge: In parliamentary and legal procedure, a bill or petition remains dormant and immobile until an authorized member 'sets it in motion' by formally speaking it onto the docket.
  • Divine and Cosmological Prime Mover: In classical Aristotelian-Thomistic metaphysics and theology, the continuous actuation of natural laws, heavenly bodies, and cosmic order by God as the Unmoved Mover (Primum Movens Immobile). Semantic Bridge: The philosophical conviction that every sequence of physical motion in the universe requires an antecedent cause, culminating in the divine presence that perpetually 'moveth' the cosmos.
  • Motivational and Causative Urging: To prompt, incite, persuade, or stimulate an agent toward a specific action, decision, or endeavor (e.g., what evil spirit moveth thee to this folly?). Semantic Bridge: The psychological impulse is viewed as a physical shove or kinetic impetus driving the human will.
