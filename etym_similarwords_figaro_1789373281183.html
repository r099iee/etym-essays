• Le Monde: Similar because both are prestigious French national daily newspapers of record with immense political and cultural influence; differs because Le Monde was founded in 1944 following World War II with an evening centre-left, intellectual editorial stance, whereas Le Figaro was founded in 1826 as a satirical journal and is France's leading morning centre-right broadsheet.
  • Scapin: Similar because both are celebrated French theatrical valets created by master playwrights (Molière and Beaumarchais) who use cunning tricks to outsmart their social superiors; differs because Scapin in *Les Fourberies de Scapin* is a pure comedic farceur of commedia dell'arte origin driven by mischief, whereas Figaro is an Enlightenment philosopher-servant who directly attacks feudal privilege and aristocratic tyranny.
  • Papageno: Similar because both are beloved operatic comic characters created in late 18th-century Vienna with music by Mozart; differs because Papageno in *The Magic Flute* is a childlike, superstitious bird-catcher seeking simple domestic pleasures, whereas Figaro in *The Marriage of Figaro* is a sophisticated, politically dangerous intellect outwitting a nobleman.
  • Voltaire: Similar because both represent Enlightenment French razor-sharp wit, anti-clerical satire, and the passionate defense of civil liberties and free expression; differs because Voltaire was a real-life philosopher, historian, and prolific essayist, whereas Figaro is a fictional dramatic character who came to symbolize Voltairean spirit in theatre and journalism.
  • Mercure de France: Similar because both are historic French literary periodicals that served as essential platforms for avant-garde writers and poets; differs because the Mercure de France originated in the 17th century as a royal gazette before becoming a Symbolist literary review, whereas Le Figaro developed into a mass-circulation national daily newspaper.
