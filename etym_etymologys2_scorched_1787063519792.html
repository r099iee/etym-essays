Old Norse (c. 11th c.; debated)
skorpna
"to be shrivelled up,
shrink, wrinkle"
↓
Middle English (early 14th c.)
scorchen
"to burn superficially,
singe the surface"
↓
English (16th c.)
scorch (verb)
"to burn, parch, or
dry up with heat"
↓
English (past participle)
SCORCHED
"having been burned or
singed on the surface"

Note on debate: The derivation of scorchen from Old Norse skorpna is the most commonly repeated theory but is not certain; some scholars instead relate the word to Middle Dutch or Middle Low German forms, and others treat it as an internal English formation of uncertain make.
