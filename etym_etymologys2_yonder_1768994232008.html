YONDER — single linear chain.
1. PIE *h₁ey- / *i- "this, that" (pronominal / demonstrative stem, the deictic pointing base) — cf. Vedic Sanskrit ayám (अयम्) "this, he here", Latin is, ea, id "that, this; he, she, it".
2. Proto-Germanic *jainaz "that (yonder), the one over there" — built on the same deictic base; cf. Gothic jains "that (yonder)" and its adverb jaindre "thither, over there", German jener "that one (over there)", Middle Dutch ginder/ginds "there, yonder".
3. Old English geon "that (yonder)" (adjective/pronoun) and geond "across, beyond, through, over there" (preposition/adverb) — the two faces of the deictic.
4. Middle English yonder — c. 1300, formed from geond + the adverbial/comparative suffix -er; the shape matches Gothic jaindre morpheme for morpheme (deictic stem + "there" ending).
5. English yonder (adverb/adjective) — present day.
Derived in English: yon and yond (from geon), beyond (from Old English begeondan, "be- + geondan", "on the far side of"), Scots yont.
Note: Greek ἐκεῖνος (ekeînos) "that" is from a different pronominal stem, and the Baltic/Slavic and Celtic demonstratives (e.g. Lithuanian anas, Old Irish ind) rest on other stems, so no cognate claim is made for them.
