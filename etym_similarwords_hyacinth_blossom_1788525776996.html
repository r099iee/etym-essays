• hyacinth: Similar because hyacinth is the base noun denoting the same plant species (Hyacinthus orientalis); differs because hyacinth can refer to the entire bulbous plant, the genus, or historically the precious gemstone (jacinth), whereas the compound hyacinth-blossom focuses specifically on the fragrant, delicate floral bloom itself.
  • blossom: Similar as the general term for a flower or mass of flowers on a plant; differs because blossom is generic (typically applied to fruit trees or flowering shrubs), whereas hyacinth-blossom specifies the individual spike and cultural symbolism of the hyacinth.
  • flower: Similar as the reproductive floral structure of an angiosperm; differs because flower is a broad, everyday categorical term lacking the poetic, mythological, and olfactory specificity of hyacinth-blossom.
  • lily-bloom: Similar as a fragrant, elegant monocot floral bloom associated with purity and beauty; differs because a lily (Lilium) features large, open trumpet-shaped flowers associated with pure white radiance and chastity, contrasting with the dense, dark, curling cluster and tragic melancholy of the hyacinth.
  • rosebud: Similar as an iconic literary floral emblem of romantic passion; differs because a rosebud represents unopened youth, nascent desire, and crimson passion armed with thorns, whereas the hyacinth-blossom symbolizes dark curls, sorrowful beauty, and heady Mediterranean perfume.
  • violet: Similar as a purple spring flower associated with modesty and early fragrance; differs because the violet (Viola) is a low-growing, humble, solitary woodland flower symbolizing shy modesty, whereas the hyacinth-blossom is a tall, opulent, densely clustered spike.
  • asphodel: Similar as a mythological Mediterranean monocot flower connected with ancient Greek mourning and the underworld; differs because the asphodel grew in the meadows of Hades for ordinary departed souls, whereas the hyacinth-blossom represents Apollo's personal grief, beauty, and physical hair-like curls.
  • iris: Similar as a spring-flowering Mediterranean geophyte with deep purple and blue petals; differs because the iris has upright "standards" and drooping "falls" named after the rainbow goddess Iris, lacking the sweet compact cylindrical bell-spike of the hyacinth.
  • bluebell: Similar as a spring-blooming bulbous plant of the same family (often called the wild hyacinth in Britain, Hyacinthoides non-scripta); differs because the bluebell is a delicate woodland wild flower with nodding bells, lacking the thick, dense, cultivated floral mass and classical Greek mythology of Hyacinthus orientalis.
