• Wither: Similar because both describe the drying and decline of a plant or body; differs because wither implies a slow, gradual drooping from age or drought, while shrivel is quicker, more violent, and driven by an extreme external force such as flame.
  • Wilt: Similar because both name the collapse of plant rigidity; differs because wilt is a limp drooping of turgor that can often be reversed with water, while shrivel is permanent deformation into creased, hardened folds.
  • Shrink: Similar because both involve reducing in size; differs because shrink is the general, often reversible word (wool shrinks in hot water), while shrivel is an involuntary, damaging, wrinkled contraction.
  • Contract: Similar because both describe drawing together and becoming smaller; differs because contract is neutral and can be voluntary (a muscle contracts), while shrivel is always involuntary and destructive.
  • Scorch: Similar because both are partners in destruction by fire; differs because scorch sears and blackens the surface but leaves the shape intact, while shrivel folds and deforms the whole object.
  • Wizen: Similar because both name dry wrinkledness; differs because wizen (wizened) names the lean, dry wrinkledness of age as a settled state, while shrivel is an active process of contracting under stress.
  • Wrinkle: Similar because both involve creasing of a surface; differs because wrinkle is superficial, gentle, even cosmetic (a wrinkled brow), while shrivel involves the whole shrinking and hardening of the object or body.
  • Dehydrate: Similar because both involve loss of moisture; differs because dehydrate is clinical and technical, naming the process of water-loss, while shrivel names the visible, folded, wasted result.
