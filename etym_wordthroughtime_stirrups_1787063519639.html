- Old English (c. 8th–11th century): Stīgrāp, a rope or loop used to climb/mount onto a horse's back - Connoted practical mounting assistance before iron stirrups became ubiquitous.
- Middle English (c. 12th–15th century): Stirop / stiropes, forged metal or wooden loops hanging from the saddle to support the rider in battle and travel - Became synonymous with knightly chivalric status and equestrian warfare.
- 16th century: Adopted into anatomical nomenclature (translated from Latin stapes) to designate the stirrup-shaped auditory ossicle in the human middle ear - Expanded into medical science.
- 18th to 19th century: Applied to maritime rigging (yard footrope supports) and medical/gynecological examination chairs - Broadened across seafaring and clinical surgery.
- 20th to 21st century: Standardized in civil engineering (reinforcing shear links in concrete) alongside traditional equestrian and clinical uses - Maintained diverse technical applications across engineering, medicine, and sport.
