- 18th-19th centuries - swish, an imitative word, names the hissing whistle of something cutting the air: a whip, a cane, a blade.
- 19th century - extended to the movement and sound of skirts, scythes, and swords; the gliding sibilance is its whole meaning.
- 20th century - the school register fixes it as the sound of the cane stroke; swishy emerges as slang for effeminate movement.
- Contemporary - swish is the sound of speed rather than weight, a thin, fast, airy hiss that puts the listener in the path of the blow.
