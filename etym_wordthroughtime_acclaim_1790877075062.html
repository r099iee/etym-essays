- 1st c. BCE–4th c. CE - Classical & Late Latin *acclamare*: The literal shouting of cheers, salutations, or political approvals by crowds and Roman senates.
- 14th c. - Old French *acclamer*: Legal and feudal shouting of homage, claiming rights, or proclaiming rulers.
- 16th–17th c. - Early Modern English adoption: Used both as a verb ("to salute with shouting") and a noun for thunderous public approbation.
- 18th–19th c. - Enlightenment & Romantic literary criticism: Broadened from literal acoustic cheering to formal, published artistic and critical praise.
- 20th–21st c. - Modern media & cultural validation: Solidified as the standard metric for high artistic achievement ("critical acclaim").
