- 1937 - Tolkien coins "mithril" in The Hobbit as the Elven "silver-steel": a metal "lighter than silver, harder than steel", introduced with Bilbo's coat of mail
- 1954-55 - The Lord of the Rings expands the lore: "true-silver", the treasure of Moria, worth ten times its weight in gold; the Dwarves' mining awakens Durin's Bane; Frodo's mithril shirt saves his life — the metal gains symbolic weight as value, craft, and doom
- 1960s-1980s - the fantasy boom: mithril enters Dungeons & Dragons and other games as the premium crafting metal; the word becomes generic for "strongest, lightest, rarest metal" and is respelled "mithral" in some rules to dodge trademarks
- 1990s-2000s - video-game ubiquity (World of Warcraft, RuneScape, Final Fantasy); "mithril" appears in economies, quests, and crafting trees worldwide; Peter Jackson's films give it a fixed screen image
- 2000s-2020s - pop-culture shorthand and metaphor: materials writers call titanium or graphene "mithril-like"; the word retains its Elvish glamour while serving as a general term for a legendary ultra-material
