DISENGAGE
PIE
(c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*wedʰ-
"to pledge, vow"
↓
Frankish
(c. 4th–8th c.)
*wadji
"a pledge, surety, pawn"
↓
Old French
(10th c.)
gage
"a pledge, pawn, security"
↓
Old French
(12th c.)
engagier
"to pledge, engage"
(en- "in" + gage)
↓
English
(15th c.)
engage
"to pledge, bind;
to attract, occupy"
↓
English
(17th c.)
disengage
"to release, set free, detach"
(dis- "apart" + engage)
Note: the Frankish *wadji and PIE *wedʰ- are
reconstructed; Old French gage also gave English
wage. Engage shifted from "to pledge" to "to attract,
occupy" before dis- was added.

OVERALL
PIE
(c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*h₁uper
"over, above, beyond"
↓
Proto-Germanic
(c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*uberi
"over, above"
↓
Old English
(c. 700)
ofer
"over, above"
↓
English
(12th c.)
over
"over, above"
constituent 1 of overall
↓
PIE
(c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*h₂el-
"all, whole"
(uncertain)
↓
Proto-Germanic
(c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*allaz
"all, every"
↓
Old English
(c. 700)
eall
"all, every"
↓
English
(12th c.)
all
"all, every"
constituent 2 of overall
↓
English
(14th c.)
overall
"all over, everywhere,
from one end to the other"
(over + all)
↓
English
(18th c., noun)
overall
"a loose protective garment"
Note: the PIE root *h₂el- for "all" is uncertain.
Overall was first an adverb/adjective "all over,
everywhere" (14th c.); the garment sense arose in
the 18th c.

DESCENDED
PIE
(c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*skend-
"to climb, ascend"
↓
Latin
(2nd c. BCE)
scandere
"to climb, mount"
↓
Latin
(1st c. BCE)
dēscendere
"to climb down, descend"
(dē- "down" + scandere)
↓
Latin
(1st c. BCE)
dēscensus
"a going down, descent"
(past participle)
↓
Old French
(10th c.)
descendre
"to descend, go down"
↓
English
(13th c.)
descend
"to go or move down"
↓
English
(past tense / past participle)
descended
"moved or hung downward"
Note: the English verb descend comes via Old French
descendre from Latin dēscendere; dēscensus (participle
and noun) underlies the separate noun descent.
