• tribute: Similar because both denote an expression of admiration, gratitude, or public honor toward an influential figure; differs because a tribute is often a formal gift, speech, monument, or commemorative event honoring past service or someone who has died, whereas an homage is typically an active creative or artistic work (a painting, musical motif, film scene) that deliberately emulates and salutes a predecessor's style or genius.
  • fealty: Similar because both originated as solemn oaths in the medieval feudal contract; differs because fealty was the sacred, religious oath of fidelity (fidelitas) sworn upon the Holy Scriptures or relics, whereas homage was the physical, personal act of political submission (hominium) wherein the vassal knelt and placed his hands between the lord's hands to declare himself the lord's 'man'.
  • reverence: Similar because both convey profound respect and high regard; differs because reverence is an internal emotional and spiritual attitude of awe and deep veneration (often directed toward the divine, sacred, or ancient), whereas homage is an outward, public, and deliberate act or artistic dedication.
  • obeisance: Similar because both involve acknowledgment of superior rank or mastery; differs because obeisance focuses primarily on the physical bodily gesture of deference (such as a bow, curtsy, genuflection, or prostration), whereas homage denotes a formal political pledge or intellectual tribute.
  • panegyric: Similar because both celebrate the virtues of an esteemed individual; differs because a panegyric is a formal, highly ornate eulogistic speech or written oration delivered in public praise, whereas homage can be expressed through artistic emulation, title dedication, or symbolic gesture.
  • allusion: Similar because both can involve referencing another artist or work; differs fundamentally because an allusion is an indirect, covert, or passing reference intended to be decoded by an astute audience, whereas an homage is an explicit, proud, and reverent declaration of artistic indebtedness.
  • honor: Similar because both confer high esteem; differs because honor is a broad moral quality, personal reputation, or social privilege, whereas homage specifically denotes the directed act of yielding recognition and paying deference to someone else's greatness.
  • pastiche: Similar because both imitate the style of an admired artist; differs because pastiche is often a neutral or lighthearted stylistic imitation that can border on parody or empty mimicry, whereas homage is always marked by deep respect, gratitude, and genuine artistic reverence.
