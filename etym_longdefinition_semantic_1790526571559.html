semantic (adjective): Relating to meaning in language, logic, symbolic systems, or semiotic structures; concerning the relationships between linguistic signs (words, phrases, symbols) and the external concepts, entities, or truth values to which they refer.

I. Core Lexical & Linguistic Senses

  • Linguistic Meaning & Relations: Pertaining to the subdiscipline of linguistics (semantics) that analyzes how meaning is constructed, organized, interpreted, and transformed across words (lexical semantics), sentences (compositional semantics), and discourses.
  • Semiotic & Sign-Referent Correspondence: Relating to the formal relationship between a signifier (symbol, token, sound) and its signified mental concept or real-world referent in philosophical semiotics.
  • Historical Semantic Drift: Pertaining to the historical evolution and diachronic shifts in the meanings, connotations, and usages of words across centuries (the original sense introduced by Michel Bréal in 1897).

II. Modern Polysemy, Technical Disciplines & Colloquial Idiom

  • Formal Logic & Model Theory (Truth-Conditional Semantics): In mathematical logic and formal philosophy (Tarski, Carnap, Kripke), semantics refers to the rules that assign truth values and denotations to formal sentences based on an underlying mathematical model or state of the world, explicitly contrasted with pure proof-theoretic syntax.
  • Computer Science, Compilers & Programming Languages: In computer science, semantic analysis is the phase of compilation that verifies whether source code makes computational sense (e.g., checking type compatibility, variable declarations, and scope) beyond mere grammatical correctness (syntax). Static semantics governs compile-time validity, while dynamic semantics defines the exact operational behavior during runtime execution.
  • The Semantic Web & Knowledge Graphs (Ontologies): In web architecture and information science (championed by W3C and Tim Berners-Lee), semantic technologies (such as RDF, OWL, and SPARQL) encode metadata in machine-readable formats, allowing automated algorithms and search engines to understand the contextual meaning and logical relationships of data across the global internet.
  • Natural Language Processing & Artificial Intelligence: In machine learning and NLP, semantic vectors and embeddings (Word2Vec, transformer attention mechanisms) represent the latent conceptual meanings and contextual affinities of words in high-dimensional mathematical vector spaces.
  • Colloquial Pejorative ('A Mere Semantic Dispute'): In everyday speech and informal debate, frequently deployed pejoratively to dismiss an argument as a petty quibble over verbal terminology rather than a genuine disagreement about substantive, physical facts.

III. Historical Semantic Bridges & Philosophical Evolution

  • From Divination Sign to Historical Linguistics: The Ancient Greek root sēma originally meant a visual sign, omen, mark, or grave-mound, from which sēmainein ("to signify, to indicate") developed. When French philologist Michel Bréal coined la sémantique in 1883/1897, he intended it as the historical study of the laws governing the changes in the meanings of words, creating a counterpart to phonetics (the study of sound shifts).
  • From Historical Etymology to Logical Foundation: In the 1920s and 30s, the term migrated into Anglo-American analytical philosophy through C.K. Ogden and I.A. Richards (The Meaning of Meaning, 1923), Alfred Korzybski (General Semantics), and Rudolf Carnap. This shifted semantic from a historical study of past word changes into an urgent philosophical inquiry into the logical structure of human knowledge, truth definitions, and cognitive communication.
