• Idea: Similar because both name something the mind thinks up; differs because idea is the broad neutral workhorse (good ideas, bad ideas, big ideas), while notion is lighter, vaguer, and more fleeting.
  • Concept: Similar because both denote abstract mental representations; differs because concept is systematic and philosophical (the concept of justice), while notion is casual and unexamined.
  • Conception: Similar because both describe how something is mentally framed; differs because conception stresses the forming of the picture (a grand conception), while notion stresses its slightness.
  • Thought: Similar because both are acts or products of thinking; differs because thought covers the whole activity (deep in thought), while notion is one small countable product of it.
  • Opinion: Similar because both state what someone holds true; differs because opinion claims judgement (in my opinion), while notion admits it may be no more than a fancy.
  • Inkling: Similar because both are faint glimmerings of awareness; differs because inkling is always slight and often ominous (an inkling of trouble), while notion ranges from inkling to full opinion to whim.
  • Hunch: Similar because both are unreasoned feelings about truth; differs because hunch is gut-level and predictive (a hunch it will rain), while notion is head-level and broader.
  • Whim: Similar because both arrive suddenly and unseriously; differs because whim is pure caprice of desire (a moment's whim), while notion keeps a foot in thinking — whims want, notions suppose.
  • Fancy: Similar because both are playful, unserious ideas; differs because fancy stresses imagination and liking (took a fancy to him), while notion stresses the thinking itself, however light.
