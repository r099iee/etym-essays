Note: The ultimate root is Celtic/Gaulish *kom-boro- ("carried together", from PIE *bʰer-, "to carry"), originally referring to trees felled across a road or a weir constructed of timber to trap fish or block a stream. When adopted into Medieval Latin and Gallo-Romance, it took both en- and in- prefixes. The 17th–19th-century English preference for incumbrance represents a conscious neoclassical Latinization, though both variants share an identical etymological lineage.
