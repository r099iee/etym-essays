inasmuch (adverb; only used in the subordinating phrase inasmuch as) — Since, seeing that; to the extent that; in so far as. The phrase introduces a clause giving the cause, ground, or limiting condition of the main statement: "inasmuch as the Renaissance ends in France, the French phase completes the series". It is the formal, written cousin of because, with an added nuance of proportion: inasmuch as often means "in the degree that" as much as "for the reason that".

Sense 1 — causal: since, seeing that. When the reason is complete and decisive, inasmuch as means simply "because": "We must defer, inasmuch as the evidence is incomplete." The phrase belongs to legal, scholarly, and ecclesiastical English, where exact attribution of reasons is prized.

Sense 2 — proportional/limiting: to the extent that, in so far as. When the clause measures the degree, inasmuch as limits the assertion: "The treaty binds us inasmuch as we freely signed it" — binding to exactly that extent. This is the older, more literal sense ("in as much as" = "in so much as"), and in the reading context it does double work: the Renaissance’s French ending both explains the choice of example and sets its scope.

  • Grammar:inasmuch as introduces a subordinate adverb clause; the clause may precede or follow the main clause; a comma usually separates them.
  • Register: formal-to-archaic; frequent in philosophy, law, theology, and literary criticism; rare in everyday speech.
  • Spelling history: "in as moche as" (14th c.) → in as much as → univerbated inasmuch (16th c.); the related forasmuch as (now archaic) is formed on the same pattern.
  • Modern variation: American usage tends to prefer insofar as; British literary English retains inasmuch as.

Distinctions. Against because, inasmuch as adds measured proportion and formal distance; against since, it is weightier and more legalistic; against insofar as, it keeps a mildly archaic literary flavour and a stronger causal shading. The whole phrase is a single lexical unit — inasmuch does not occur alone outside it.

The correlative pattern and word order. Historically inasmuch as also played within the paired construction "in as much as ... in so much as ...", where the measure was stated on both sides; modern usage has collapsed the pair into the single conjunction. The clause it introduces usually follows the main clause in speech-like prose ("We agreed, inasmuch as we must") but may lead when the reason sets the frame — exactly the movement of Pater’s sentence: "Inasmuch as the Renaissance ends also in France, the French phase completes the series".

Usage debate. The usage guides of the 20th century (Fowler, Gowers) note that inasmuch as is often over-used as a pompous synonym of because; they counsel reserve, while conceding that where proportion is intended ("to exactly that extent") the phrase has no plain substitute. The ambiguity — causal or proportional? — is as old as the phrase: it has carried both values since Chaucer, and context alone disambiguates.
