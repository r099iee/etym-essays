• Pebbly: Similar because both refer to surfaces covered in small, rounded water-worn stones; differs because pebbly usually suggests smaller, smoother, and gentler stone formations, whereas shingly encompasses broader, coarser gravel beds, coastal storm berms, and rugged marine formations.
  • Gravelly: Similar because both denote ground dense with loose rock fragments; differs because gravelly is a broader geotechnical and agricultural term applying to inland crushed rock, sediment, and low-pitched voices, lacking the specific maritime, tidal, and water-worn coastal associations of shingly.
  • Stony: Similar because both denote terrain dominated by rock; differs because stony is far more general, covering fixed boulders, jagged rockfaces, or metaphorical unyielding coldness, whereas shingly strictly implies loose, rounded, mobile water-worn pebbles.
  • Gritty: Similar because both describe coarse mineral textures; differs because gritty denotes fine, sharp particles of sand or dust that abrade surfaces, along with psychological resolve or unvarnished realism, rather than water-smoothed pebble accumulations.
  • Rocky: Similar because both describe terrain composed of lithic material; differs because rocky implies solid bedrock, jagged crags, or large immovable stones rather than loose, granular, water-tumbled littoral gravel.
  • Strown / Scree-covered: Similar because both describe loose rock fragments on ground; differs because scree refers specifically to angular, frost-shattered talus on mountain slopes rather than wave-rounded coastal or fluvial gravel.
