Linear derivation chain for ADUMBRATES (single root word):

*h₂ndʰ- / *andh- "dark, blind, dim" (Proto-Indo-European, Pokorny; the link to Latin umbra is traditional but debated — see note below) → Latin umbra "shadow, shade, darkness" (classical period; underlying shape conjectured as *unthrā / *umra, deeper origin uncertain) → Latin umbrāre "to shade, to cast a shadow" (denominal verb on umbra, attested from Plautus onward) → Latin adumbrāre "to shade over, to outline faintly, to represent in shadow" (ad- "towards, at" + umbrāre; attested from the 1st c. BCE, used by Cicero and Pliny both literally of drawing and figuratively of rhetoric) → Latin adumbrātus "shaded, feigned, unreal, counterfeit" (perfect passive participle of adumbrāre) → English adumbrate "to outline faintly, to foreshadow, to indicate in advance" (learned borrowing from Latin, first recorded c. 1586) → ADUMBRATES (third-person singular present indicative of adumbrate — the exact form of this card).

Note on the debate: the Proto-Indo-European stage is not universally accepted. Pokorny groups Latin umbra under *andh- "dark, blind", with Sanskrit andhá- "blind" and Greek amblýs "dim, blunt" as probable cognates, but de Vaan records no secure PIE etymology for umbra and allows that the word may lack a good Indo-European cognate. The Latin portion of the chain (umbra → umbrāre → adumbrāre → adumbrātus) is firmly attested and undisputed.
