• Branches: Similar because both name the limbs of a tree; differs because branch is the neutral, all-purpose term for any limb at any size, while bough is restricted to the largest structural arms that spring from the trunk and carries weight, age, and a poetic flavour.
  • Limbs: Similar because both name the large arms of a tree; differs because limb is more formal and chiefly American, and can also mean an animal's leg, while bough is the poetic and traditional tree-word.
  • Twigs: Similar because both are parts of a tree's growth; differs because twigs are the small, slender shoots at the tips of branches, while boughs are the great structural limbs from which twigs grow.
  • Springs: Similar because both name small shoots or branches; differs because sprigs are small cut shoots used for decoration or seasoning, the very opposite in scale of the great arms that boughs are.
  • Sprays: Similar because both name branching growth; differs because a spray is a small, fine, spreading shoot or the arrangement of leaves and flowers on it, while boughs are massive structural limbs.
  • Shoots: Similar because both name young growth of a tree; differs because shoots are the new, slender growths of the season, while boughs are the old, thick, permanent arms of the tree.
  • Sticks: Similar because both can name pieces of cut wood; differs because a stick is any cut piece of wood regardless of origin, while a bough is a specific structural limb of a tree - larger, heavier, and heavier in meaning too.
  • Logs: Similar because both can be lengths of wood for fuel; differs because a log is a length of cut trunk or thick branch, while boughs are the tree's own arms, cut or broken from the trunk.
  • Stems: Similar because both name the supporting axis of a plant; differs because stem is the general word for any plant axis, including herbs and flowers, while bough is specific to the great limbs of trees.
  • Trunks: Similar because both are parts of a tree; differs because the trunk is the main body of the tree, from which boughs spring, and is never used of the limbs themselves.
  • Bows: Similar because both are pronounced alike (homophones); differs because bow (the bent thing or the act of bending) descends from Old English bugan 'to bend', while bough descends from the 'arm' word - a coincidence of sound, not of origin.
