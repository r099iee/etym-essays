• Tulliola: Similar because it names the same person and is the form Cicero actually used in his letters; differs because it is an affectionate diminutive, not a formal name, and it belongs to the intimate register of family correspondence rather than to historical record.
  • Tullius: Similar because it is the masculine nomen from which Tullia is formed and the name of her father and brother; differs because it denotes a male member of the gens and cannot refer to the woman herself.
  • Tullus: Similar because it is the old Latin praenomen from which the nomen Tullius is probably derived; differs because it is a male given name, archaic in flavour, and names no woman.
  • Tullia Ciceronis: Similar because it is the same person and the preferred form in modern scholarship; differs because it is a disambiguating full name, combining her nomen with a family qualifier, and is not how the Romans ordinarily named her.
  • Tullia Major: Similar because she shares the exact name and belongs to the same legendary family of the Tullii; differs because she is a figure of Livy’s regal legend, the gentle elder daughter, not Cicero’s child.
  • Tullia Minor: Similar because the name is identical and the story is the most famous use of it in Roman literature; differs because she is the parricidal daughter of Servius Tullius, a byword for unnatural filial conduct, the moral opposite of Cicero’s Tullia.
  • Tullii: Similar because it is the clan name to which Tullia belongs and the source of her nomen; differs because it is a collective plural denoting the gens, not an individual woman.
  • Cicero’s daughter: Similar because it identifies exactly the same person and is the phrase a biographer would naturally use; differs because it is a relational description rather than a name and could belong to any daughter of any Cicero.
