Single root word (gleams is the inflected form of gleam). Linear chain from PIE to modern English: - Proto-Indo-European *gʰel- 'to shine, glow; the yellow or golden colour of shining things' (reconstructed; c. 4500–3500 BCE; the same root gives gold, yellow, glass, glow, Greek khlōros 'green-yellow', Sanskrit hiraṇya 'gold'). - Proto-Germanic *glaim- (also reconstructed as *glaimaz or related extended stem *glim-) 'bright light, brilliance' — a nasal-extended grade of the root (reconstructed; c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE; the same stem underlies glimmer). - Old English glǣm 'brilliant light, brightness, splendour' (attested c. 8th–11th c. CE). - Middle English glem / gleme 'a beam or ray of light; brilliance' (attested 13th–15th c.). - Early Modern English gleam (noun) 'a flash or ray of light' and (verb) 'to shine with soft light' (attested 16th c.). - Modern English gleams — the third-person singular present of the verb gleam and the plural of the noun (the root word as given on this card). Debate note: the exact Proto-Germanic antecedent is not directly attested. Scholars infer either *glaimaz (whose long diphthong explains Old English glǣm) or an extended stem *glim- from the cluster of Germanic gl-words (glimmer, glisten, glitter), all built on PIE *gʰel-. The Old English form with its long vowel points most plausibly to *glaim-, but the reconstruction is a reasonable inference, not a documented form.
