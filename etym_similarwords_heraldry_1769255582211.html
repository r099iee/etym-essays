• Armory (the heraldic sense): Similar because both concern coats of arms and armorial bearings; differs because armory in this sense is a narrower, more technical word for the rules and devices of arms themselves, while heraldry includes the officers, the records, the genealogy, and the ceremonial function of the whole system.
  • Blazonry: Similar because both name the descriptive art of coats of arms; differs because blazonry is strictly the technical language of describing a shield, whereas heraldry is the entire institutional system of which blazonry is only one part.
  • Genealogy: Similar because both deal with family lineage and descent; differs because genealogy is the study of family trees through documents and recorded descent, while heraldry concerns the hereditary insignia (the arms) that mark that lineage and the legal records of who bore them.
  • Vexillology: Similar because both are codified sciences of symbolic emblems; differs because vexillology is the narrower modern study of flags only, while heraldry covers shields, crests, badges, supporters, and the entire armorial system.
  • Pageantry: Similar because both suggest colourful ceremonial display; differs because pageantry is the general spectacle of processions and show, while heraldry is a formal, rule-governed system of hereditary symbols that merely appears in pageantry.
  • Emblem / Insignia: Similar because both are symbolic devices of identity; differs because emblem and insignia name the objects themselves, whereas heraldry names the entire art and discipline of devising, granting, and regulating them.
  • Badge: Similar because heraldic badges are part of the system; differs because a badge is a single simple device, while heraldry is the comprehensive system of shields, crests, supporters, tinctures, and registers.
  • Science / Art of arms: Similar because both phrases name the heraldic discipline itself; differs in being descriptive paraphrases rather than a single lexical word.
  • Seal / Signet: Similar because both were medieval marks of identity and title; differs because a seal is a physical impression for authenticating documents, while heraldry is the painted and emblazoned system of hereditary arms (though arms frequently appear on seals).
  • Colours (heraldic sense): Similar because both concern the symbolic tinctures of rank and house; differs because colours is a colloquial shorthand for heraldic tinctures or a regiment's flags, not the discipline itself.
  • Symbolism: Similar because both deal with symbols and their meanings; differs because symbolism is a broad literary and philosophical term for the use of symbols, while heraldry is a specific institutionalised system with fixed rules and official records.
  • Livrée / Livery: Similar because both belong to the visual apparatus of noble houses; differs because livery is the distinctive uniform or colours worn by servants and retainers, while heraldry is the formal system of hereditary arms.
