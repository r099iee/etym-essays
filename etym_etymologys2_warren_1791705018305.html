*wer-
"to cover, protect, ward off, enclose"

Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*warōną / *warnōną
"to guard, take care of, heed, defend"
↓
Frankish (c. 5th–7th c. CE)
*warjan / *warenna
"to defend; an enclosed preserve"
↓
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 11th c.)
garenne / warenne
"game park, rabbit preserve; privilege"
↓
Anglo-Norman (c. 1086)
de Warenne
"of the Warren (manor in Normandy)"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1745)
Warren (Sir Peter Warren)
"surname of British naval admiral"
↓
American English (1747)
Warren
"town in Bristol County, Rhode Island,"
"original founding home of Brown University"

Note: Named in 1747 after Admiral Sir Peter Warren (1703–1752), naval hero of the Siege of Louisbourg. The surname derives from the Anglo-Norman toponymic de Warenne, ultimately stemming from the Frankish-derived common noun warren (a game preserve, from Germanic *wer- 'to guard, protect').
