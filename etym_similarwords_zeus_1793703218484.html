• Jupiter: Similar because he is the Roman equivalent of Zeus, serving as the king of the gods and god of the sky and thunder; differs because he belongs to the Roman pantheon and has distinct cultic practices and some differing mythological narratives.
  • Odin: Similar because he is the chief god in Norse mythology, associated with wisdom, war, and magic, and often seen as a powerful, patriarchal figure; differs because his domain, attributes, and mythological context are entirely distinct from the Greek pantheon.
  • Yahweh/God: Similar because he is the supreme, singular deity in Abrahamic religions, embodying ultimate power, law, and creation; differs because he is monotheistic, transcendent, and lacks the anthropomorphic flaws and polytheistic context of Zeus.
  • King of the gods: Similar because this is Zeus's primary title and role, denoting his supreme authority over other deities; differs because it is a descriptive title rather than a proper name, and can apply to supreme deities in various pantheons.
  • Supreme deity: Similar because it refers to the highest-ranking god in a polytheistic system or the sole god in a monotheistic one, reflecting Zeus's ultimate power; differs because it is a general theological term, lacking the specific mythological and cultural context of Zeus.
