• SCABBARD: Similar because both are protective cases for a blade; differs because scabbard is conventionally reserved for the rigid, reinforced case of a sword (often leather over wood with a metal tip), while sheath is the broader, humbler term covering knives, daggers, scalpels, and biological structures — a scabbard is always a sword-case, a sheath is not.
  • HOLSTER: Similar because both are weapon-carrying coverings; differs because holster belongs to firearms, hangs the weapon at the hip or shoulder, and is designed for quick draw-and-shoot, with no sense of clinging closely to a blade.
  • CASE: Similar because both are containers for an object; differs because case implies a fitted, often boxed or rigid enclosure that the object sits in, while a sheath moulds itself to the object and clings to it like a skin.
  • COVER / COVERING: Similar because both are things that protect what they enclose; differs because a cover is generic and need not be close-fitting or dedicated to one object, while a sheath is specifically shaped to follow the contours of a single blade, tool, or organ.
  • CASING: Similar because both are enveloping protective layers; differs because casing suggests a surrounding frame or shell (a door casing, a tire casing) rather than the flexible, form-fitting envelope of a sheath.
  • ENVELOPE: Similar because both are enclosing layers; differs because envelope is a scientific word for any surrounding layer or boundary and carries no martial or blade meaning at all, while sheath always implies a close, form-fitted cover.
  • SLEEVE: Similar because both are tube-like coverings drawn over something; differs because sleeve is informal and modern (a record sleeve, a protective sleeve for a document) and lacks both the blade sense and the anatomical precision of sheath.
  • JACKET: Similar because both are outer protective coverings; differs because jacket implies a detachable outer layer (a book jacket, a cable jacket) rather than the integral, close-fitting case of a sheath.
  • HUSK / POD: Similar because both are natural coverings that enclose and protect; differs because husk and pod belong to seeds and fruits, with no sense of an object deliberately fitted into its cover, while a sheath is a made thing (or a biological sleeve) that a blade, nerve, or leaf is drawn into.
  • SKIN: Similar because both are outer layers that cling to what they cover; differs because skin names an organic membrane integral to the body, while a sheath is a separate covering — a cat can sheathe its claws, but the claws are not its skin.
