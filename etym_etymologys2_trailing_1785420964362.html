BCE; perfect stem trax-, participle tractus) → Vulgar Latin *tragulāre "to drag, to haul a sled or drag-net" (frequentative formation on trah-; not directly attested, reconstructed from Romance reflexes) → Old French trailler / trailer "to tow, to drag along" (attested 12th–13th c.; cf. Old French traille "a drag-net dragged through water") → Middle English trailen "to hang down so as to touch the ground; to be dragged along behind" (attested 14th c., c. 1300s; a borrowing from Old French) → English trail (verb) "to drag along behind; to follow the track of; to fall behind" (by 15th c.) → English trailing (present participle, verbal noun; the -ing form) — the exact form of this card → TRAILING (final node; the bold target rootWord) Note on the debated sibling branch: Proto-Germanic *draganą "to draw, pull" (→ Old English dragan → English drag, draw, draught) is cognate to Latin trahere if the PIE root is set as *dʰreǵʰ-, with the customary differences of velar treatment; this linkage is widely accepted, though handbooks express the root in varying shapes. Greek trékho (τρέχω) "to run" has also been compared, with the sense "draw along" shifted to "run," but this remains debated and is not needed for the English chain.
