• Ecclesiastical: Similar because it is the twin adjective meaning of or belonging to the Church; differs because ecclesiastical is the longer, now more usual attributive form, while ecclesiastic is older, shorter, and also serves as a noun.
  • Clerical: Similar because both describe the world of the clergy as opposed to the laity; differs because clerical stresses the ordained personnel and their status, while ecclesiastic stresses the institution, its law, and its purposes.
  • Priestly: Similar because both evoke the ministry and its sacred functions; differs because priestly centres on the priest and sacrifice at the altar, while ecclesiastic ranges across courts, revenues, vestments, and government.
  • Pastoral: Similar because both belong to the shepherding work of the Church; differs because pastoral names the caring cure of souls — letters, visits, sermons — while ecclesiastic is cooler and more institutional.
  • Canonical: Similar because both invoke the rule and law of the Church; differs because canonical means strictly conforming to canon — canonical hours, canonical texts — while ecclesiastic merely means churchly in general.
  • Liturgical: Similar because both concern the worship life of the Church; differs because liturgical is confined to rites, prayers, and services, while ecclesiastic embraces discipline, property, and polity besides.
  • Sacerdotal: Similar because both dignify the priesthood and its authority; differs because sacerdotal is lofty and learned, often used of priestly power itself, while ecclesiastic is the plainer historian's word.
  • Churchly: Similar because both mean almost exactly belonging to the church; differs because churchly is warmer and more affectionate — churchly piety — while ecclesiastic is formal, official, and institutional.
  • Cleric: Similar because as a noun it names the same person — one in holy orders; differs because cleric is the standard modern noun, while an ecclesiastic sounds like a figure from a history book or a synod roll.
  • Prelate: Similar because both name high churchmen of rank and government; differs because a prelate is specifically a dignitary — bishop, abbot, cardinal — while an ecclesiastic may be the humblest curate.
