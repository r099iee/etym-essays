- Old English (8th–11th c.) - Sacred sign, miraculous portent, and military standard - Used in *Beowulf* and Old English poetry (*bēacen*) for divine signs, royal battle standards, or the radiant sun in the sky.
- Middle English (12th–15th c.) - Defensive signal fire and early navigational aid - Evolved into structured coastal warning systems, hilltop fire-baskets, and estuarine light markers.
- Early Modern (16th–17th c.) - National defense infrastructure - Reached peak military prominence under the Tudors with nationwide networks of chain beacons warning of naval invasion.
- 18th–19th c. - Maritime engineering and moral metaphor - Transformed into modern offshore lighthouse optics (Fresnel lenses) and ubiquitous poetic metaphors for moral guidance, liberty, and enlightenment.
- 20th–21st c. - Radio navigation and micro-location technology - Broadened to aviation VOR radio beacons, emergency transponders (EPIRBs), and Bluetooth IoT proximity devices.
