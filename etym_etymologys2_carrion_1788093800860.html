Note on the debated root stage: the deep etymology of Latin carō "flesh" is disputed. The traditional derivation from PIE *ker- "to cut" (flesh as "the cut piece", cf. Greek κείρω keirō "to cut, shear", Germanic *skeran- "shear") is accepted by many handbooks, but some scholars prefer a connection with the alternative root *kreuH₂- "raw flesh" (cf. Greek κρέας kréas "flesh", Sanskrit kravíṣ, Old English hrēaw "raw"). The later chain — carō → Vulgar Latin *caronia (reconstructed from the Romance outcomes: Old French charogne, Occitan caronha, Italian carogna, Spanish carroña) → English carrion — is securely established; only the PIE affiliation of carō remains open to debate.
