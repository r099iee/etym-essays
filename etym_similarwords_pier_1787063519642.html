• Dock: Similar because both are maritime structures for mooring boats; differs because a dock strictly refers to the water basin or enclosed slipway between piers (or dry docks for repairs), whereas a pier is the physical raised platform projecting into the water.
  • Wharf: Similar because both facilitate cargo handling and vessel berthing; differs because a wharf typically runs parallel to the shoreline as a continuous stone or timber loading quay, while a pier extends outward perpendicular or oblique to the shore.
  • Jetty: Similar because both project out from the coast; differs because a jetty is primarily engineered to protect a coastline, alter current flow, or prevent channel silting (often made of rough riprap boulders), whereas a pier is an elevated platform designed for docking, walking, or load-bearing.
  • Quay: Similar because both provide mooring for ships; differs because a quay is a solid, earth-backed stone embankment built along the edge of a harbour or riverbank, whereas a pier projects out into open water and often rests on open pilings.
  • Pillar / Column: Similar because both are vertical load-bearing architectural supports; differs because a column is typically cylindrical and adheres to classical architectural orders, whereas a pier is usually rectangular, composite, or a heavy block of mass masonry.
  • Mole: Similar because both are large marine structures projecting into the sea; differs because a mole is a massive, solid stone or masonry breakwater with no water circulation underneath, designed primarily as a defensive barrier against waves rather than a light, piled walkway.
