• Crozier: Similar because it is a curved pastoral staff of authority; differs because it is Christian episcopal, symbolizing shepherding of souls, not Roman sky-divination.
  • Crosier (variant): Similar because same curved staff concept; differs because spelling variant belongs to church history, not augury.
  • Staff: Similar because it is a hand-held rod of support and office; differs because it is generic and lacks the diagnostic crook and divinatory function.
  • Wand: Similar because it is a short hand-held instrument of ritual power; differs because it suggests magic or fairy tale rather than state religion.
  • Sceptre: Similar because it is a rod emblem of sovereignty; differs because it is straight and royal, marking kingship rather than priestly observation.
  • Caduceus: Similar because it is a classical staff of office; differs because it is the winged serpent-entwined herald's wand of Mercury, for commerce and negotiation, not auspices.
  • Thyrsus: Similar because it is a ritual staff in classical religion; differs because it is a fennel stalk with pine-cone carried in Bacchic rites, ecstatic not divinatory.
  • Fasces: Similar because it is a Roman bundle-of-rods emblem of imperium; differs because it signifies coercive magisterial power with axes, not priestly sky-marking.
  • Tuba: Similar because it is the fellow Roman military trumpet; differs because it is straight with a different timbre and signaling role, not curved.
  • Cornu: Similar because it is the other Roman curved brass instrument; differs because it is G-shaped and larger, encircling the player, distinct from the lituus trumpet.
  • Baton: Similar because it is a short staff of command for conductors, marshals, or police; differs because it is modern and functional, without ancient religious sanction.
  • Ferula: Similar because it is a Latin staff or rod; differs because it suggests schoolmaster's cane or ritual blow, not augural crook.
