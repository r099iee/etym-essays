- Middle English (12th–14th century) - “Advent” enters English chiefly as the church season of Advent, the weeks before Christmas — the coming of Christ; a religious, liturgical term.
- 15th–17th century - Broadens from Christ's coming to any solemn arrival or accession — the advent of a king, of an age; still grand and providential in flavour.
- 18th–19th century - Secularises into public and political prose: Gladstone “rejoiced in [Mill's] advent” to the House of Commons — the momentous arrival of a public figure; the word keeps its landmark, destined aura.
- Contemporary - Standard in journalism and commentary — the advent of television, of the internet, of AI — always marking an era-changing arrival, never a merely routine one.
