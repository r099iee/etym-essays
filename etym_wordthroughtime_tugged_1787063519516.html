- [13th–14th c.] - [Middle English Physical Emergence] Emerged as tuggen / toggen, used by rural laborers and craftsmen to describe pulling vigorously, wrestling, hauling heavy loads, or dragging beasts of burden.
- [15th–17th c.] - [Violent Contest & Belligerence] Frequently associated with physical combat, roughhousing, tearing at garments, and interpersonal scuffles where combatants tugged and grappled with one another.
- [18th–19th c.] - [Maritime & Industrial Extension] Institutionalized through the development of steam-powered tugboats that towed and maneuvered heavy sailing vessels in industrializing ports, while also entering literature to describe tactile gestures (tugging a coat) and emotional appeals ("tugging at heartstrings").
- [20th–21st c.] - [Modern Polysemy & Idiom] Widely used across metaphorical domains to describe political deadlocks, economic tug-of-wars, psychological ambivalence, and digital/physical user interactions.
