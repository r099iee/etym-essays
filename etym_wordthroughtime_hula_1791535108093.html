- Pre-19th Century - Sacred liturgical dance, genealogical repository, and royal court performance in the Hawaiian Kingdom, revered with profound religious devotion and strict kapu.
- 1820s–1870s - Condemned and banned by American Christian missionaries as licentious and pagan, forced into clandestine rural practice as an underground cultural resistance.
- Late 19th Century - Publicly revived as a symbol of national identity and royal pride by King David Kalākaua ('The Merrie Monarch'), initiating early modern adaptations.
- Early-to-Mid 20th Century - Commercialized and exoticized by Hollywood, Tin Pan Alley, and Waikiki tourism as carefree tropical novelty, inspiring consumer fads like the hula hoop.
- 1970s–Present - Reclaimed by the Hawaiian Renaissance as high art, linguistic archive, and sacred emblem of indigenous cultural sovereignty and identity.
