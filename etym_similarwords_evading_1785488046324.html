• AVOID: Similar because both mean staying clear of something rather than confronting it; differs because avoid is neutral and blameless, while evading adds a skilled side-step, an act of conscious cleverness, and a touch of moral ambiguity.
  • ELUDE: Similar because both mean escaping something that presses or pursues; differs because elude stresses successfully escaping pursuit or detection (one eludes a pursuer; a name eludes the memory), while evading stresses the clever detour around an obstacle one is actively confronting.
  • DODGE: Similar because both mean slipping past a question or blow with a quick movement; differs because dodge is sudden, physical, and often sly, while evading can be quiet, courteous, and perfectly legitimate, even scholarly.
  • SHIRK: Similar because both mean avoiding something one might be expected to do; differs because shirk is a cowardly failure of duty and always blameworthy, while evading may be a principled, pragmatic detour with no guilt attached.
  • SIDESTEP: Similar because both use the image of moving sideways past a difficulty; differs because sidestep is informal, physical, and lighter in tone, while evading is formal and can cover law, duty, and pursuit as well as mere questions.
  • SKIRT: Similar because both mean moving along the edge of an issue rather than entering it; differs because skirting suggests grazing the boundary hesitantly, while evading suggests an active, deliberate dodging away.
  • CIRCUMVENT: Similar because both mean getting around an obstacle; differs because circumvent stresses clever, often devious or bureaucratic means of going around a rule, while evading stresses slipping away from a thing or question rather than engineering around it.
  • DUCK: Similar because both mean lowering oneself to avoid something aimed at one; differs because duck is physical and instantaneous, used of blows and questions in informal speech, while evading describes a sustained, deliberate avoidance.
  • BYPASS: Similar because both mean going around something; differs because bypass is neutral and technical, describing routes or procedures that avoid a blockage, without evading's implication of dodging something that pursued or pressed.
  • EQUIVOCATE: Similar because both are ways of not giving a straight answer; differs because equivocate means using ambiguous language to mislead, a purely verbal tactic, while evading can be silent, physical, or a matter of omitting to engage at all.
