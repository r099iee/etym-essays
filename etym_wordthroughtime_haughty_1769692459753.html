- 16th c. 'High, lofty, elevated' — carried the positive register of nobility and dignified height, drawn from the sense of standing 'high'.
- 17th–18th c. 'Proudly superior, disdainful' — takes on social censure; describes the cold, self-important bearing of the aristocracy toward those deemed beneath them.
- 19th c. onward 'Arrogantly condescending, aloof' — clearly negative, implying contemptuous reserve and self-inflated rank rather than true dignity.
- Contemporary 'Disdainfully superior' — used of a coldly proud manner (e.g., a haughty glance), almost always named as a fault of character.
