• Boots: Similar because both are footwear rising above the ankle; differs because modern boots are heavy, rigid, and often military or workaday, lacking the softness, suppleness, quietness, and archaic theatrical glamour of buskins.
  • Ankle-boots / Half-boots: Similar because they name the same calf-high cut; differs because these are strictly descriptive, modern, and neutral terms that carry none of the stage history, romance, or learned metonymy of buskin.
  • Moccasins: Similar because both are soft, supple, quiet footwear; differs because moccasins cover only the foot and carry associations of Native American footgear, while a buskin always rises above the ankle and belongs to the world of the hunt, the traveller, and the tragic stage.
  • Shoes: Similar because both are general footwear; differs because shoes is far too general and modern — a buskin is always a boot rising above the ankle, with a learned, theatrical charge no shoe ever carries.
  • Gaiters: Similar because both cover the lower leg; differs because gaiters are worn over shoes and do not themselves cover the feet, making them leg-coverings rather than footwear proper, while buskins are boots worn on the feet.
  • Cothurnus: Similar because both name the high tragic boot of the Greek stage; differs because cothurnus is the learned Latin loan of the Greek kóthornos, strictly the ancient tragic actor's boot, while buskin is the common naturalized English word that also remained a practical soft boot in ordinary life.
