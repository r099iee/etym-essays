- Ancient to Old English - flint is among the oldest English words: the hard stone knapped into hand-axes, arrowheads, and blades since the Stone Age.
- Medieval to 18th centuries - flint-and-steel fire-making: the household stone that strikes sparks, the everyday kit of traveller and soldier.
- 19th century - flintlocks and matches; the adjective flinty hardens into a figure for emotional hardness and unyielding character.
- Contemporary - flint survives in archaeology, firecraft, and the idiom 'tinder and flint'; flinty still means cold and unfeeling.
