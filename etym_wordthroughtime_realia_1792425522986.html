- [Late Latin & Scholastic Era (c. 13th–15th c.)] - originated as the neuter plural substantive of the scholastic Latin adjective realis ("pertaining to things"), signifying concrete actualities, physical things, or objective realities as opposed to abstract mental concepts or nominal terms - [perception: viewed as a rigorous metaphysical term in scholastic realism defending the objective existence of things]
- [German Philological & Pedagogical Enlightenment (late 18th–19th c.)] - adopted into German academic scholarship (*Realia*, *Realien*) to designate concrete material antiquities—coins, tools, architecture, legal institutions, and natural sciences—studied in classical curricula alongside pure literary grammar (*Reallexikon*, *Realschule*) - [perception: respected as an indispensable factual and scientific grounding balancing abstract humanistic philology]
- [Library Science & Archival Codification (early to mid-20th c.)] - formalized within Anglo-American cataloguing rules (such as AACR2) as a standard bibliographical category for three-dimensional physical artifacts, models, dioramas, and museum specimens held by libraries - [perception: treated as a necessary technical classification for non-book physical materials requiring specialized indexing]
- [Communicative Language Teaching Revolution (c. 1970s–1990s)] - surged across global second-language pedagogy (TESOL, ESL, foreign language methodology) to denote authentic everyday cultural objects—menus, metro passes, currency, timetables—brought into the classroom to connect vocabulary with lived experience - [perception: celebrated by progressive educators as a vibrant antidote to sterile rote memorization]
- [Translation Studies & Cultural Semiotics (late 20th–21st c.)] - established in translatology (popularized by scholars like Vlahov and Florin) to designate culture-specific lexical items that name unique objects, foods, offices, and traditions lacking direct target-language equivalents - [perception: analyzed as crucial cultural markers highlighting the limits and creative strategies of cross-linguistic translation]
- [Contemporary Digital & Museum Studies (2010s–present)] - increasingly contrasted with digital virtualization and augmented reality, highlighting the unique cognitive and historical resonance of physical, tactile, museum-grade material objects - [perception: prized as authentic physical touchstones in an increasingly virtualized pedagogical landscape]
