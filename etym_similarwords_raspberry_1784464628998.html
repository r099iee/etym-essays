• Blackberry: Similar because it is the closely related aggregate bramble fruit of the same genus (Rubus) with a similar compound form (black + berry); differs because the blackberry keeps its fleshy receptacle core when picked (so it is not hollow) and is darker and tarter, whereas the raspberry comes away hollow from its core and is typically red and sweeter.
  • Loganberry: Similar because it is a hybrid bramble fruit (raspberry x blackberry) of similar aggregate structure; differs because it is a cultivated hybrid with a darker, elongated berry, not the classic hollow red raspberry.
  • Dewberry: Similar because it is another trailing bramble fruit close to the blackberry; differs because it is low-growing and ripens earlier, with a bloom like a raspberry but a different growth habit and flavour.
  • Cloudberry: Similar because it is a northern aggregate berry of the rose family; differs because it is amber-orange, grows in arctic bogs, and is known for its musky flavour rather than the garden raspberry's sweetness.
  • Bramble / brambleberry: Similar because the raspberry grows on a bramble (a prickly shrub) and 'bramble' can loosely cover the fruit; differs because 'bramble' names the thorny plant (especially the blackberry bush) rather than the raspberry specifically.
  • Bronx cheer: Similar because it is the American name for the exact same tongue-and-lip spluttering gesture called 'blowing a raspberry' in British English; differs only by region and register, the Bronx cheer also evoking a noisy American audience.
  • Rasp: Similar because the word's folk etymology connects the berry's rough surface to the scraping tool and verb rasp; differs because 'rasp' is actually a coarse file or the verb 'to scrape,' and the connection to the berry's name is folk-motivated rather than documentary.
  • Jeer: Similar because both express derision; differs because a jeer is a loud spoken taunt or mockery in words, while a raspberry is a specific wordless spluttering noise made with tongue and lips.
