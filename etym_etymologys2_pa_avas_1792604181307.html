Note on historical debate: The historical origin of Sanskrit paṇava is widely debated among comparative linguists. Traditional Indian etymologists (such as the commentator Yāska and later grammatical commentators) sought to derive paṇava internally from the verbal root paṇ- ("to praise, honor, or bet/stake"), claiming the drum was named because it was struck while singing praises or celebrating stakes. However, modern historical-comparative linguists, including Manfred Mayrhofer (*Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindoarischen* [EWAia]) and F. B. J. Kuiper, reject this folk derivation. Because paṇava is absent from the oldest Rigvedic hymns and lacks cognates in Iranian or other Indo-European branches, it is widely regarded as an Old Indo-Aryan acoustic borrowing from an indigenous non-Aryan substrate language (likely Austroasiatic/Munda or Dravidian) or an onomatopoeic imitation of a sharp, clapping percussion strike (*paṇ-*).
