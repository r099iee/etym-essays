Unassessably is a rare, literary adverb meaning "to a degree that cannot be assessed, estimated, measured, or valued" — in other words, inestimably, incalculably, immeasurably. It is the adverbial form of the adjective unassessable ("not able to be assessed"), which itself is the negative of assessable ("able to be valued or taxed"), built on the verb assess ("to estimate the value of; to fix the amount of a tax or charge").

Formation and grammatical behaviour

  • Morphemes: un- (negation) + assess (verb: to value or tax) + -ably (adverbial suffix, "in a manner that can be ...ed"). The surface string is un–assess–abl(y).
  • Word class: adverb of degree; it modifies an adjective or verb that follows: "unassessably wealthy," "unassessably vast," "unassessably great."
  • Semantics: it raises a quality beyond the reach of assessment — beyond quantification, valuation, or judgement. The emphasis is on the failure or impossibility of assessment, whether because the quantity is astronomically large or because its worth transcends any standard.
  • Frequency: very rare. It belongs to high literary or rhetorical registers and is far less common than its near-synonyms inestimably, immeasurably, and incalculably.

Chief use: Tolkien's "unassessably wealthy"

The word is most famous from The Hobbit (1937), where Bilbo, bargaining with the dragon Smaug, says: "O Smaug the unassessably wealthy." The phrase is a deliberate compliment wrapped in a trap: Smaug's treasure hoard is so vast that no assessor could put a value on it, so the flattery "no one could price your wealth" is both literally true and quietly pointed. This single literary use has kept the word alive in the public imagination as a vivid, mouth-filling synonym for "astronomically rich."

Secondary meanings and semantic nuances

  • Beyond valuation (money / wealth): wealth, treasure, or property too great to be appraised — the sense Tolkien exploited.
  • Beyond measurement (quantity / extent): size, distance, or number that defeats calculation: "an unassessably wide desert."
  • Beyond estimation (qualities): abstract qualities — value, importance, influence, beauty — that cannot be judged by any standard: "an unassessably great contribution."
  • Beyond taxation (legal / fiscal): shading toward the older fiscal sense of assessable: property or income that cannot be assessed for tax is, in principle, "unassessable."
  • Indeterminate, not merely huge: unlike "incredibly," it can imply that the degree is unknowable rather than merely very large — a nuance of genuine uncertainty, not just emphasis.

Why "assess" and what it connotes

The verb assess descends from a fiscal-administrative line (see etymology): it originally meant to fix or impose a tax, then to estimate value for official purposes, and today generally "to evaluate or judge." The negative prefix un- and the adverbial -ably therefore stack three ideas: not + able to be + valued / evaluated. The result is a word that sounds technical and measured even while describing something that defeats all measuring — a pleasant irony that makes it attractive to writers.

Register and current status

  • Literary / rhetorical: used in fiction, journalism, and formal speech for grand, hyperbolic effect.
  • Near-synonym boundaries: inestimably stresses value beyond reckoning; immeasurably stresses size beyond measure; incalculably stresses computation beyond the possible; unassessably uniquely stresses the act of official estimation / taxation and is the rarest of the set.
  • Intensifier function: it functions exactly like an extreme intensifier ("unbelievably," "impossibly"), but with a formal, dignified colouring.

Unassessably is thus a narrow, dignified, very rare adverb of degree meaning "beyond all possibility of valuation or measurement," immortalised by one dragon's treasure hoard.
