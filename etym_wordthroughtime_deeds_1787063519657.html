- Old English dǣd "an act, something done" - an entirely neutral, everyday word for any action.
- Middle English dede - begins to carry moral weight: good deeds vs evil deeds in homilies and religious writing.
- Late medieval England (14th–15th c.) - gains the legal sense "a written instrument transferring land or rights" (deed poll, indenture, deed of grant).
- Renaissance epic and romance - "deeds of arms", "warlike deeds": the standard token of heroism and reputation.
- Modern usage - survives vividly in two registers: heroic exploit (epic, legend, proverb) and formal legal transfer; in ordinary speech the neutral "doing" sense has passed to "acts" and "actions", so "deeds" now sounds slightly elevated, archaic, or legal.
