Note on morphological compounding and phonetic shift: The Greek compound genealogíā was created from the nominal stem geneā́ ("generation, race") and the combining formant -logíā (derived from lógos, "discourse, computation, treatise"). The connecting thematic vowel -o- regularized the compound into genea- + -o- + -logia. In Anglo-Norman and Middle English orthography, the vowel hiatus produced variants such as genalogie, geneology, and genealogi. Standard Modern English adopted the classical Greek etymological spelling with -ea-, although a persistent colloquial misspelling (*geneology*) frequently arises due to false analogy with ubiquitous -ology suffixes (such as biology, geology, archaeology).
