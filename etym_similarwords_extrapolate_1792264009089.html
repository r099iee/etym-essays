• Interpolate: Similar because both involve mathematical estimation and inferential reasoning from data; differs because interpolate estimates missing values between existing, known data points within an observed range, whereas extrapolate projects values beyond the boundaries of the observed data into the unknown.
  • Infer: Similar because both denote deriving an unstated conclusion from observed evidence; differs because infer is a broad cognitive process of drawing logical deductions or implications from premises, whereas extrapolate specifically implies extending a continuous trend, trajectory, or pattern outward into a new domain.
  • Deduce: Similar because both arrive at conclusions from existing information; differs because deduce moves strictly from general premises or axioms downward to a necessary specific fact (deductive certainty), whereas extrapolate is an inductive projection based on observed patterns that remains inherently probabilistic.
  • Project: Similar because both involve forecasting future trends based on present data; differs because project often emphasizes visual, statistical, or quantitative charting of future scenarios without necessarily unpacking the underlying mechanistic principles, whereas extrapolate emphasizes the transfer of a governing rule or pattern into an untested domain.
  • Generalize: Similar because both expand the scope of a specific observation; differs because generalize broadens a specific rule to describe a larger category or universal class, whereas extrapolate applies an observed trajectory or structure to predict a specific outcome in an unexplored region or timeframe.
  • Conjecture: Similar because both involve reaching conclusions without complete observational proof; differs because conjecture carries a connotation of guesswork, speculation, or hypothesis based on scanty evidence, whereas extrapolate is rooted in a structured, measurable baseline of established empirical data.
  • Surmise: Similar because both form opinions without direct proof; differs because surmise implies a subjective, intuitive hunch or suspicion based on slight hints, lacking the formal, patterned methodology inherent in extrapolate.
  • Hypothesize: Similar because both formulate propositions about the unknown; differs because hypothesize establishes a provisional explanation or testable premise for scientific investigation, whereas extrapolate projects an existing line of thought or trajectory forward.
