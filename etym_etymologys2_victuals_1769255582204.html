Chain for victuals: 1. PIE *gʷeyh₃- "to live, be alive" (reconstructed; attested in Sanskrit jīvati "lives," Greek zōḗ "life," Latin vīvō "I live"; c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE). 2. Latin vīvō, vīvere "to live, be alive" (verb; attested throughout Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE, e.g., in Caesar's "vīvō" and Cicero). 3. Latin vīctus "a living, manner of life; means of subsistence, nourishment, food" — the past participle of vīvō, used substantively (attested 1st c. BCE; the sense "food, nourishment" is well established in Classical Latin). 4. Latin victuālis "of or relating to food, for nourishment" — adjective formed on vīctus (attested in Classical and later Latin, 1st c. BCE onward). 5. Late Latin victuālia "victuals, provisions, food supplies" — neuter plural of victuālis used as a noun meaning "things pertaining to food" (attested from the 4th–5th c. CE in Late Latin texts, e.g., the Vulgate). 6. Old French vitaille / vitaile "food, provisions, victuals" (from Latin victuālia; attested from the 11th–12th c. CE; in Old French the medial -c- is lost, reflecting spoken Romance pronunciation "vitaille"). 7. Middle English vitailles / vitayle / vital "food, provisions" (borrowed from Old French; attested from c. 13th–14th c. CE; the singular "vital" and plural "vitailles" both occur). 8. Early Modern English victuals — the spelling is re-Latinized: Renaissance writers restore the etymological c (from Latin victuālia), giving "victuals," while the pronunciation continues the old French-derived "vittles" (attested 16th c.; the restored spelling is the source of the modern silent c). 9. Modern English victuals (plural noun, /ˈvɪtəlz/; the standard spelling since the 17th c., with the silent c). Debated / nuanced note: There is no serious debate about the overall chain — Latin victuālia from vīctus from vīvō from PIE *gʷeyh₃- is secure — but the pronunciation–spelling split is the word's famous nuance: English kept the Old French pronunciation ("vittles") while re-Latinizing the spelling in the 16th century, and the two have never been reconciled. The stage "Old French vitaille" and "Middle English vitailles" show the pre-restoration spelling without c; the c appears only from the 16th-century learned respelling onward.
