PIE *to- "that" (demonstrative pronoun stem; the far-deictic *so-/to- paradigm, ancestor of the Germanic th-words) → Proto-Germanic *þa- / *þan- "that, then" (the demonstrative stem; English the, that, then, there, this, thus all descend from it) → Proto-Germanic *þanana "from there, thence" (ablative adverb of motion-away, formed on the stem *þan- with the ending of motion-away) → Old English þanon / þanone "from that place, from that source" (attested from the 9th c., in Alfred's prose and the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle) → Middle English thennes "from there, from that source" (with the adverbial genitive -s added, the same ending seen in once, twice, hence, whence, thus; 12th–14th c.) → English THENCE (14th c.) Note (debated): the *to- demonstrative ancestry is secure, but the intermediate ablative formation *þanana and its precise relation to Greek then "from that place" and Sanskrit tátaḥ "thence" are reconstructed and not fully recoverable; the adverbial morphology is inferred from the consistency of the Germanic forms.
