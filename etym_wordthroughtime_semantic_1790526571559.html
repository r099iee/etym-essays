- 1894–1900: Introduced into English from Michel Bréal's French sémantique - technical linguistic designation for the historical study of changes in word meanings.
- 1920s–1930s (Logical Positivism & General Semantics): Extended by Ogden, Richards, and Korzybski into philosophy and human behavior - examining how human thought and sanity are shaped by verbal symbols.
- 1950s–1970s (Formal Logic & Generative Linguistics): Solidified in formal semantics (Tarski, Montague, Chomsky) - rigorously distinguishing semantic truth conditions and meaning representations from syntactic grammar.
- late 20th c.–Present (Computer Science & AI): Expanded to computational semantics, the 'Semantic Web' (Tim Berners-Lee), ontology engineering, and semantic vectors in machine learning.
- Contemporary Colloquial: Frequently used in dismissive phrases ('merely semantic', 'a semantic distinction') to describe trivial arguments over words rather than substantive facts.
