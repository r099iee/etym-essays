- c. 1380-1500 - "Dominated by the cold, moist bodily humor" - Primarily a medical diagnosis in humoral medicine, denoting pale complexion, sluggish metabolism, and excess mucous secretions.
- c. 1500-1650 - "Sluggish, drowsy, or dull" - Shifted into vernacular psychology to denote someone naturally indolent, heavy-spirited, cowardly, or slow to respond to emotional stimuli.
- c. 1650-1800 - "Even-tempered, cool-headed, rational" - Enlightened re-evaluation: praised as the ideal temperament for magistrates, diplomats, and scholars who shunned fanaticism and religious hysteria.
- c. 1800-Present - "Imperturbable, unexcitable, calm" - Contemporary psychological sense: highly stable, unruffled composure in crisis, though occasionally criticized as overly passive, detached, or uninspired.
