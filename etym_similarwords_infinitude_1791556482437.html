• infinity: Similar because both denote the state or quality of being boundless, endless, or limitless; differs because infinity is the standard mathematical, technical, and scientific term representing numerical quantities or asymptotic limits, whereas infinitude is elevated, poetic, and philosophical, emphasizing subjective awe, cosmic vastness, or divine majesty.
  • boundlessness: Similar because both signify the absence of borders, frontiers, or limits; differs because boundlessness is a native Germanic compound evocative of open, physical terrain (such as plains or oceans), whereas infinitude is a Latinate abstract term carrying profound theological and metaphysical overtones.
  • immensity: Similar because both describe scale that surpasses human measurement; differs because immensity emphasizes colossal physical size, enormous bulk, or overwhelming scale within physical space, while infinitude denotes the total absence of any boundary whatsoever.
  • vastness: Similar because both refer to immense, expansive dimensions; differs because vastness denotes physical spaciousness or desolate emptiness that can nonetheless have eventual distant limits, whereas infinitude conceptually admits no termination.
  • limitlessness: Similar because both describe that which cannot be contained or restricted; differs because limitlessness is often applied to potential, energy, resources, or possibilities, lacking the grand cosmic, eternal, and philosophical weight of infinitude.
  • eternity: Similar because both denote unending existence; differs because eternity applies specifically and exclusively to the dimension of time, whereas infinitude encompasses space, magnitude, divine attributes, and physical reality.
  • illimitability: Similar because both express the condition of being incapable of limitation; differs because illimitability is a heavy, analytical polysyllabic construct focusing on theoretical incapacity for containment, whereas infinitude possesses organic poetic elegance and emotional depth.
  • perpetuity: Similar because both imply unending continuation; differs because perpetuity is primarily a temporal and legal term of art (e.g. perpetual trusts, perpetual covenants), lacking spatial and metaphysical grandeur.
