• Moderate: Similar in avoiding extremes; differs in that temperate often implies a moral or habitual self-restraint rooted in balance, while moderate can be a mere statistical or quantitative middleness.
  • Mild: Similar in lacking intensity; differs as mild is often applied to weather, flavour, or a gentle nature, while temperate carries a stronger sense of active, deliberate restraint.
  • Restrained: Similar in holding back; differs because restrained can imply forceful or deliberate suppression, while temperate suggests a natural, balanced moderation.
  • Abstemious: Similar in moderation, especially in food and drink; differs because abstemious is narrowly about eating and drinking, while temperate covers conduct, emotion, speech, and climate.
  • Sober: Similar in seriousness and moderation; differs because sober stresses the absence of intoxication and frivolity, while temperate stresses the general avoidance of excess.
  • Measured: Similar in being careful and unhurried; differs because measured describes a deliberate, calculated quality in speech or action, while temperate is a broader character trait.
  • Even: Similar in steadiness and absence of swings; differs because even describes emotional or temperamental constancy, while temperate implies active self-control over desires.
  • Dispassionate: Similar in freedom from strong feeling; differs because dispassionate is about detachment from emotion, while temperate is about keeping emotions and appetites in due proportion, not removing them.
  • Self-controlled: Similar in governing one’s own impulses; differs because self-controlled is a general term for mastery of will, while temperate names the specific virtue of moderation and balance.
