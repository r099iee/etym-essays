[ROOT CORE (PIE): *dhwel- / *dhel- (to dull, obscure, confuse; to stray, wander)] — branch of DWELLS
├── Proto-Germanic: *dwaljaną (to delay, hinder, lead astray, make foolish)
│ └── Old English: dwelian / dwellan (transitive: to lead astray, mislead, deceive; intransitive: to wander, err, tarry)
│ └── Middle English: dwellen (to remain, stay, linger — then to reside, abide)
│ └── Early Modern English: dwell (to reside, live, linger)
│ └── Modern English: dwells (3rd person singular present of dwell) ◄ TARGET
└── Cognate branches: Old Norse dvelja (to delay, tarry, postpone), Old High German twellen (to delay, hinder), Middle Dutch dwellen (to stun, make dull), Dutch dwalen (to wander, stray, err).
Note: The pivotal semantic shift from "to lead astray / wander" to "to remain / reside" is the central debated point of this word's history. The bridge sense "to tarry, be delayed" is well attested in Middle English and explains the reversal; the pre-Germanic reconstruction *dhwel- is accepted by most authorities (Pokorny, IEW), though the full ablaut set *dhwel-/*dhul- and its precise relation to the *dheu- 'vapour/dullness' family remain matters of scholarly discussion.
