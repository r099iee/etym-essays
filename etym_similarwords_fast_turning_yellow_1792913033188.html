• Yellowing: Similar because both name green tissue going gold; differs because yellowing is the single technical participle for the process, while fast turning yellow stresses visible speed and autumn colour.
  • Turning golden: Similar because both describe autumn leaves gaining gold; differs because golden is warmer and more poetic, prized by poets, while yellow is the plainer botanical colour word.
  • Autumnal: Similar because both belong to fall foliage and mood; differs because autumnal names the whole season and its melancholy, while fast turning yellow names one dated colour-stage within it.
  • Chlorotic: Similar because both describe leaves losing green through pigment loss; differs because chlorotic is clinical pathology from nutrient lack or disease, while fast turning yellow in the source is healthy seasonal change.
  • Withering: Similar because both track foliage decline towards fall; differs because withering stresses shrivelling and death of tissue, while turning yellow can be a vivid living gold before any wilt.
  • Browning: Similar because both are autumn colour-shifts as green fails; differs because browning marks dead tannin-stained tissue at the edge of decay, while yellowing marks the bright carotenoid stage.
  • Ripening: Similar because both are maturing colour-changes towards gold; differs because ripening promises edible sweetness in fruit and grain, while leaves turning yellow promise leaf-fall.
  • Fading: Similar because both describe colour draining away with time; differs because fading is general loss of brightness in cloth, flowers, and memory, while turning yellow is a specific pigment swap.
  • Senescing: Similar because both name programmed autumn ageing in leaves; differs because senescing is the scientific term for ordered nutrient salvage, while fast turning yellow is what the walker sees.
