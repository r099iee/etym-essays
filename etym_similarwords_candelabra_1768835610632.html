• candlestick: Similar because both are candle supports; differs because the candlestick holds a single candle and is modest and domestic, while the candelabra is branched, multiple, and monumental.
  • chandelier: Similar because both are large branched fittings for many lights; differs because the chandelier is the ornate crystal-and-glass fitting hung from a ceiling in ballrooms and palaces, while the candelabra is plainer — grand by scale rather than ornament.
  • candelabrum: Similar because it names the very same branched stand; differs because candelabrum is the strict Latin singular, the term of art for one stand, while modern English has reanalysed candelabra as a singular with its own new plural candelabras.
  • sconce: Similar because both are candle holders; differs because the sconce is wall-mounted, fixing a single light to a wall, while the candelabra is free-standing or suspended and holds many flames.
  • menorah: Similar because both are branched candle holders; differs because the menorah is the specifically Jewish seven-branched lampstand of religious ritual, with fixed symbolic design, while the candelabra is a secular decorative fixture of variable form.
  • girandole: Similar because both are branched candle holders; differs because the girandole is a large branched candlestick or wall-light, often with glass prisms or drops, typical of the eighteenth century, while the candelabra is a plainer, more classical form.
  • torchère: Similar because both are standing light supports; differs because the torchère is a tall floor stand for a single lamp or torch, not branched, while the candelabra holds several flames on arms.
