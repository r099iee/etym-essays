• Clank: Similar because both are metallic ringing sounds of struck iron; differs because a clank is heavier, duller, and more brutal, the sound of massive iron or machinery, while clink is light and delicate.
  • Clang: Similar because both are sounds of metal striking metal; differs because a clang is louder, fuller, and more resonant, a large surface struck hard, while clink is small, brief, and dry.
  • Jingle: Similar because both are bright metallic sounds of light objects; differs because a jingle is tinnier and lighter, the clatter of many small objects like coins or bells, while clink suggests single, clear struck surfaces.
  • Tinkle: Similar because both are light ringing sounds; differs because a tinkle is delicate and glassy, closer to a small bell, while clink is harder and more metallic.
  • Rattle: Similar because both name noisy contact between hard objects; differs because a rattle is the irregular shaking of loose parts rather than a clean ringing strike, and it lacks the metallic ring of clink.
  • Click: Similar because both are short, sharp percussive sounds; differs because a click is a single dry contact with no lingering ring, while a clink retains a bright metallic resonance.
  • Chink: Similar because both are light metallic sounds of coins or glass; differs because a chink is softer and higher, with a narrower, tinnier quality, and it usually denotes the sound of money more than of chains.
  • Ting: Similar because both are bright small metal sounds; differs because a ting is a higher, thinner, more bell-like ping, often of a single struck object, while clink is harder and more percussive.
  • Ping: Similar because both are short, high, metallic sounds; differs because a ping is sharper, more electronic or sonar-like, with less of the bright ringing decay that defines a clink.
