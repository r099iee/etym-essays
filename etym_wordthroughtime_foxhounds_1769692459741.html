- [17th c. breed origin] - Foxhounds were bred in England as specialized scent hounds to track foxes; the word was a plain, functional name for a working hunting dog.
- [18th–19th c. aristocratic era] - In the era of grand country estates, keeping a full pack of foxhounds became the supreme emblem of landed wealth and rural leisure; "foxhounds" acquired strong upper-class, sporting, and socially exclusive connotations.
- [20th c.–contemporary] - As organized fox hunting declined and was legally restricted in Britain, "foxhounds" gained a complex, layered register: still a respected kennel-club breed name, yet now loaded with nostalgia, tradition, and the controversy of the sport it serves.
