Component 1 — HEDGE:
1. Proto-Indo-European *kagh- "to catch, seize, enclose, weave together" (reconstructed, c. 4000–3000 BCE). DEBATE: the PIE ancestry of the Germanic hedge-words is not fully certain; some scholars treat Proto-Germanic *hagjō as isolated, though Latin caulae "sheepfolds, enclosures" and caula "enclosure" are often adduced as cognates.
2. Proto-Germanic *hagjō "hedge, fence, enclosure" (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE).
3. Old English hecg "hedge, fence" (attested from c. 700 CE; developed from *hagjō by i-umlaut).
4. Middle English hegge "hedge" (c. 12th–15th c.).
5. English hedge (16th c. onward) — first element of the compound.

Component 2 — ROW:
1. Proto-Indo-European *reie- "to scratch, tear, cut; draw a line" (reconstructed, c. 4000–3000 BCE).
2. Proto-Germanic *raiwō "row, line, series" (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE; cf. Old High German rīga, German Reihe "row").
3. Old English rāw "row, line, series" (attested c. 9th c.).
4. Middle English rowe "row" (c. 13th–15th c.).
5. English row (modern) — second element of the compound.

Compound assembly — HEDGEROW:
1. Old English conceptual union of hecg + rāw ("hedge" + "row").
2. Middle English hegerow "a row of hedges" (first recorded c. 15th century).
3. English hedgerow "a row of bushes or small trees planted as a boundary" (16th c. onward).
4. Plural: English HEDGEROWS (final node).
