Earliest stage to present QUAFF (origin obscure; no PIE root proposed): - Middle Low German / Low German quassen "to overindulge in food and drink" — the most widely favored proposed source; the double -ss- may have been read or reshaped as -ff-. (Alternative hypothesis: an onomatopoeic formation imitating the gurgling sound of deep swallowing; an older and now discredited theory connected the word to Old Irish cuäch "cup, goblet, bowl" and the Scots quaich drinking bowl.) - English quaff (verb) "to drink in large draughts, copiously, and with relish" (c. 1510s; agent noun quaffer attested 1510). - English quaff (noun) "a deep draught" (1570s). - Modern English QUAFF "to drink heartily, copiously, and with relish in large draughts." Note on the debate: no Proto-Indo-European ancestry has been convincingly proposed. The Low German loan theory and the onomatopoeia theory compete as the leading explanations, and the Celtic quaich connection, once popular, is now generally discredited.
