• Perhaps: Similar because it is the exact modern equivalent in meaning; differs because perhaps is standard, neutral modern English, while mayhaps is archaic, regional, or literary.
  • Maybe: Similar because both express possibility; differs because maybe is informal everyday speech, while mayhaps is consciously rustic or historical.
  • Peradventure: Similar because both are archaic adverbs of possibility; differs because peradventure is heavily Latinate and biblical, while mayhaps is Germanic and Scandinavian in stock.
  • Perchance: Similar because both are archaic words meaning "by chance, perhaps"; differs because perchance stresses chance happening and is more purely poetic, while mayhaps stresses mere possibility.
  • Possibly: Similar because both mark logical possibility; differs because possibly is a neutral analytical adverb of formal reasoning, carrying no archaic colour.
  • Haply: Similar because both are built on hap "chance"; differs because haply is the older, more purely poetic form, while mayhaps combines the noun with the modal may.
  • Belike: Similar because both are archaic regional words for "perhaps"; differs because belike (from "by like") is even more archaic and chiefly dialectal, now rarely used.
  • Mayhap: Similar because it is the very base form of mayhaps; differs only in the final -s, which makes mayhaps sound even more rustic, folkloric, and northern.
