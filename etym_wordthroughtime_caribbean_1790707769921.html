- Late 15th–16th Century - Derived by the Spanish from Taíno *Cariba* / *Caniba*, originally applied as an ethnonym (*Caribe*) to describe the fierce indigenous Kalinago warriors, stereotyped by Europeans as man-eaters (which gave rise to the English word *cannibal*).
- 17th–18th Century - Shifted from an ethnonym to a geographic and maritime descriptor (*Caribbee Islands*, *Caribbean Sea*), defining the cockpit of European colonial empire, sugar plantations, and privateering.
- 19th Century - Standardized in English as *Caribbean*, describing the distinct geographical region, its tropical biodiversity, and its geopolitical conflicts.
- 20th–21st Century - Embraced as a proud cultural, geopolitical, and postcolonial identity (e.g., CARICOM, Caribbean literature, music, cuisine, and diaspora), symbolizing resilience, hybridity, and creative brilliance.
