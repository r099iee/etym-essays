- 14th–16th c.: Translating, decoding, or interpreting sacred scripture and classical poetry through figurative levels (moral, anagogical, tropological) - revered as high scholarly and theological exegesis.
- 17th–18th c.: Constructing didactic literary emblems, fables, and moral tales (e.g. Bunyan, Spenser) - valued as a clear rhetorical tool for moral and spiritual edification.
- 19th c. (Romantic Era): Shift toward skepticism; Romantics (Goethe, Coleridge) contrasted organic, transcendent 'symbolism' against mechanical, intellectual 'allegorizing'.
- 20th c. (Structuralism & Postmodernism): Walter Benjamin and Paul de Man revived allegorizing as the fundamental mode of textuality, historical rupture, and semantic decoding.
- 21st c.: Both scholarly hermeneutic practice and a critical warning against over-interpreting texts, historical events, or cultural trends into rigid ideological systems.
