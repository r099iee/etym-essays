Core Definition

scanty — an adjective meaning small, meager, or insufficient in quantity or amount; barely enough or less than enough; thin, sparse, or inadequate. The word is used of supplies, portions, clothing, evidence, information, and vegetation alike, always with the core idea of falling short of what is expected, needed, or decent.

Primary Sense: Quantitative Insufficiency

  • Small in amount: a scanty meal, scanty rations, or a scanty harvest is one that is too small to satisfy or to last.
  • Falling short of requirement: scanty evidence, scanty details, or scanty information is insufficient to prove, understand, or conclude anything.
  • Barely sufficient: in a milder reading, scanty can mean "hardly enough" — a margin so thin it hovers at the edge of adequacy.

Secondary Sense: Clothing and Covering

  • Scanty attire / scanty dress: clothing that covers very little of the body; skimpy, revealing garments — "scanty swimwear," "a scanty bikini."
  • Scantily (adverb): "scantily dressed" or "scantily clad" means wearing very little.
  • Scanties (plural noun): colloquial, chiefly North American term for brief women's undergarments or small articles of underwear.

Tertiary Senses and Figurative Use

  • Spatial thinness: scanty vegetation, scanty hair, or scanty growth is thin, sparse, and patchy.
  • Mental and textual deficit: a scanty account, scanty knowledge, or a scanty bibliography is deficient in content or coverage.
  • Measure and margin: "a scanty half-hour" is barely a half-hour; a scanty allowance is one trimmed to the bone.

Register and Usage Notes

  • Neutral to slightly formal register; common in descriptive prose and journalism, less so in casual speech (where skimpy or thin often substitute).
  • Collocates with nouns of quantity and provision: rations, meal, income, means, evidence, detail, attire, growth.
  • Related forms: scant (adjective/adverb, the older base), scantiness (noun), scantily (adverb).
  • Careful: scanty is related to scant (from Old Norse) but NOT to scarce (from Old French), despite their similar meaning; the two are false friends in etymology.

Polysemy Summary

scanty carries a compact set of senses all radiating from one idea — a quantity cut short of full measure: (1) materially small or insufficient (food, money, supplies); (2) skimpy in covering (clothing, attire); (3) thin and sparse in distribution (vegetation, hair, growth); (4) deficient in content (evidence, information, accounts). In every case the semantic bridge is the ancient image of something clipped, pared down, or cut off before it reaches its full size.

Historical Semantic Bridge

Old Norse skamt, the neuter of skammr "short, brief," was borrowed into northern Middle English as scant during the Danelaw period, meaning "short, deficient, falling short of full measure." The adjective scanty was formed in the 1650s by adding -y to scant, creating a word for anything trimmed so short it no longer suffices — food supplies, allowances, and later clothing. The modern "scanty bikini" sense is a direct descendant of that idea of being cut too short.
