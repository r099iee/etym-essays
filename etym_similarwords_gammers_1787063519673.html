• Gaffers: Similar because both are dialectal words for old people, formed identically from godfather and godmother; differs because gaffer = old man, gammer = old woman — they are a matched masculine/feminine pair.
  • Hags: Similar because both denote old women; differs because hag has a strongly negative, witch-like, malicious connotation, while gammer is neutral or faintly affectionate.
  • Crones: Similar because both denote old women; differs because crone is literary and often pejorative (an ugly or wicked old woman), while gammer is mild, homely, and dialectal.
  • Old wives: Similar because both denote elderly women; differs because "old wives" is a fixed phrase carrying superstitious overtones (old wives’ tales), while gammer is a plain village word.
  • Grandmothers / Grannies: Similar because both denote older women with authority and family standing; differs because granny is a kinship or endearment term, while gammer emphasises age and garrulity generally, without implying family relation.
  • Matrons: Similar because both denote respectable older women; differs because matron implies dignity, rank, and domestic management and is not dialectal, while gammer is plain and rural.
  • Dames: Similar because both can denote elderly women; differs because dame carries archaic, theatrical, or affectionate overtones and is not restricted to old age, while gammer specifically means an old woman.
  • Gossips: Similar because both denote talkative older women and both derive historically from godparent words (godsibb); differs because gossip now means the chatter itself or a rumour-monger of either sex, while gammer denotes the person and is archaic.
  • Beldams: Similar because both denote old women, especially ugly or fierce ones; differs because beldam is a harsher, more archaic literary word, while gammer is homely and neutral.
  • Dowagers: Similar because both denote elderly women of standing; differs because dowager implies aristocratic rank and wealth, while gammer is the old woman of the village, not of the manor.
