- 1771 — Anquetil-Duperron's Zend-Avesta introduces Zend to European readers as the language of the Zoroastrian scriptures.
- Early 19th c. — "Zend" becomes a standard Orientalist label; scholars speak of the "Zend language" and "Zend philology".
- Late 19th c. — comparative Iranian studies distinguish Avestan from Middle Persian, and Zend is reclassified as the commentary rather than the language.
- 20th c. — Avestan displaces Zend in linguistics; the older word survives in book titles and in Theosophical literature.
- 21st c. — Zend is archaic and historical, chiefly used in the fixed title Zend-Avesta and in discussions of early Orientalism.
