• Struggle: Similar because both denote effort against opposition; differs because struggle is broader — physical, economic, and moral — with no implication of rules or institutions.
  • Conflict: Similar because both name opposition between forces; differs because conflict is often armed or deep-set and implies rupture, while a contest proceeds within a shared framework of law or rules.
  • Dispute: Similar because both are disagreements between parties; differs because dispute is narrower and procedural — a verbal or legal point — lacking the sustained striving for supremacy.
  • Controversy: Similar because both involve contention; differs because controversy is public intellectual argument carried on in speech and print, not a struggle for victory or power.
  • Rivalry: Similar because both are competition between parties; differs because rivalry is often personal or commercial, between equals, and does not suggest constitutional or institutional stakes.
  • Competition: Similar because both denote rivalry in a rule-bound setting; differs because competition today is the neutral word for regulated games, markets, and elections, while contest keeps an adversarial, striving ring.
  • Combat / Battle: Similar because both denote physical struggle; differs because they are concrete fighting words — too specific for a long institutional or legal struggle.
  • Match / Game: Similar because both name an organized sporting event; differs because they are purely athletic and orderly, while contest can still carry the older legal and political weight.
  • Challenge: Similar because both involve opposition to a claim; differs because a challenge is a single act of calling something into question, not a sustained struggle for supremacy.
