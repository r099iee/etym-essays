• Jacques Derrida: Similar because both were titan figures of French post-structuralism who fundamentally dismantled the traditional assumption that a text possesses a fixed, authorially anchored meaning; differs because Derrida developed the rigorous philosophical technique of deconstruction focused on linguistic contradictions and metaphysical margins (différance), whereas Barthes approached texts through semiotics, cultural sociology, sensual reading pleasure, and intensely lyrical autobiographical subjectivity.
  • Michel Foucault: Similar because both were celebrated twentieth-century professors at the Collège de France who challenged humanist notions of the autonomous author and decoded the historical systems that structure human knowledge; differs because Foucault conducted vast archaeological and genealogical investigations into institutional power, madness, clinics, and sexuality, whereas Barthes focused on the micro-mechanisms of signs, literary codes, and the aesthetics of textuality.
  • Claude Lévi-Strauss: Similar because both pioneered the application of Ferdinand de Saussure's structural linguistics to decode culture as an interconnected system of signs; differs because Lévi-Strauss was an anthropologist who mapped universal mental structures through indigenous kinship systems and Amerindian mythologies, whereas Barthes turned structuralism onto modern bourgeois mass media, fashion, photography, and literature.
  • Susan Sontag: Similar because both were brilliant, stylistically luminous cultural critics who bridged academic theory and popular culture, writing groundbreaking texts on photography (Barthes's Camera Lucida and Sontag's On Photography) and illness; differs because Sontag was an American intellectual rooted in moral, cinematic, and political critique, whereas Barthes operated entirely within the French tradition of philosophical belles-lettres and semiological analysis.
  • Umberto Eco: Similar because both were internationally renowned semioticians who analyzed popular culture, media signs, and the active role of the reader in decoding literature; differs because Eco applied semiotics to medieval aesthetics, broad communication theory, and bestselling historical fiction (The Name of the Rose), whereas Barthes cultivated an intimate, fragmentary, and confessional critical style that blurred the boundary between theory and creative prose.
  • Jacques Lacan: Similar because both revolutionized postwar French intellectual life by reimagining their fields through Saussurean linguistics (Lacan declaring the unconscious structured like a language); differs because Lacan was a clinical psychoanalyst focused on the mirror stage, the Symbolic order, and the Real, whereas Barthes investigated literature, mythic cultural signs, and the erotic pleasure of reading.
