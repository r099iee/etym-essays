Stage 1 — Pre-Roman substrate element *kar- / *karr- "stone, rock, hard mass" (Ligurian and/or Celtic; Ligurian is not securely classifiable as Indo-European, and its exact status is debated; Celtic cognates are well attested: Welsh carreg, Irish carraig, Scottish Gaelic carragh, Breton karreg, all "stone/rock/crag"). Stage 2 — Latinized carraria / carrariae "quarries, place of stone extraction" (Late Latin; a derivative of the substrate stone-word, later also interpretable as "cart-road" from carrus — see note below). Stage 3 — Italian Carrara, the town and comune in the Apuan Alps, Tuscany (attested in medieval documents; the district lies in the province of Massa-Carrara). Stage 4 — English CARRARA, the toponym used attributively in "Carrara marble" (in English from the 18th–19th centuries as the marble trade grew); in Pater's The Renaissance (1873), "the hills of Carrara." Final node: CARRARA. Notes on debate and nuance: - The stone-toponym core (*kar- "stone") is a pre-Roman Ligurian or Celtic element, not a securely reconstructed PIE root with a single Pokorny master-form; the Celtic sister words support the "stone" reading, but the Ligurian layer remains incompletely classified. - A parallel and distinct line runs from Latin carrus "wagon, cart" (a Gaulish loan, from the separate PIE root *ḱers- "to run"), giving Italian carro, French char, English car and chariot. Because carraria could be heard as "cart-road / way for wagons" to the pits, folk association may have contaminated the Latin form of the quarry name; do not merge the vehicle root into the stone-toponym tree. - The historical cultural branch (Roman marmor Lunense, Renaissance Michelangelo, modern marble brand) is onomastic and commercial, not part of the morphological derivation.
