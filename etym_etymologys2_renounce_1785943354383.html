PIE *neu- 'to shout, to call out' (traditional reconstruction, debated - see note) -> Latin nuntiare 'to announce, report, declare, bring word' (denominative verb from nuntius 'messenger, bringer of news', attested from the 2nd c. BCE) -> Latin renuntiare 'to bring back word, report back; to declare against, to disown, to renounce' (re- 'back, against' + nuntiare; in Roman politics the standard verb for officially reporting election results and, in the passive, for formally resigning a magistracy; attested from the 1st c. BCE) -> Old French renoncer 'to renounce, to relinquish, to give up' -> Middle English renouncen 'to renounce, to declare one's abandonment' (14th c., attested c. 1375) -> English RENOUNCE 'to formally declare one's abandonment of a claim, right, or belief'.

Note on the debate: the origin of Latin nuntius and nuntiare is not fully certain. The traditional link to PIE *neu- 'to shout, call out' is found in some handbooks but is doubted by others; de Vaan and other modern authorities treat nuntius as of obscure, probably Italic origin, and proposals have connected it variously with a root *nou- / *nuti- behind 'nodding' (cf. Latin nutare 'to nod, to waver') or with a lost Italic word for 'word' or 'speech'. The chain from Latin renuntiare onward is secure; only the step above nuntius is genuinely open to debate, and it is stated here as such rather than guessed.
