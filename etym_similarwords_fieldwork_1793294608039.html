• Participant observation: Similar because both mean learning a community by joining its life; differs because participant observation names the signature anthropological technique, while fieldwork is the whole enterprise of being there, including interviews, surveys, and logistics.
  • Ethnography: Similar because both describe immersive study of peoples and cultures; differs because ethnography stresses the written account that results, while fieldwork stresses the lived research that precedes it.
  • Field research: Similar because the two phrases are near-interchangeable for outdoor inquiry; differs because field research is slightly more formal and grant-shaped, while fieldwork keeps the manual, muddy, hands-on flavour.
  • Field study: Similar because both name bounded investigations conducted on site; differs because a field study is one designed project with questions and dates, while fieldwork can be the open-ended dwelling from which several studies grow.
  • Site visit: Similar because both involve going to the location; differs because a site visit is brief and inspectorial, while fieldwork implies duration, return visits, and relationships.
  • Survey: Similar because surveys are a staple fieldwork tool; differs because a survey is one instrument of questionnaires and counts, while fieldwork is the wider presence that gives those counts meaning.
  • Excavation: Similar because digs are the archaeologist emblem of fieldwork; differs because excavation means controlled digging and recording of strata, while fieldwork covers survey, lab liaison, and village interviews too.
  • Practicum: Similar because both place learners in real settings under supervision; differs because a practicum is a curricular placement for training, while fieldwork aims at new knowledge as well as competence.
  • Groundwork: Similar because both evoke preparatory labour close to the ground; differs because groundwork is metaphorical foundation-laying for any project, while fieldwork is literal research among people, rocks, or reeds.
