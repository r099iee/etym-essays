- Late 16th to 17th Century: Metaphysical and theological directness without secondary causes - Perceived as a technical philosophical term denoting God's direct governance or unmediated causal agency operating upon nature and the soul.
- 18th Century (Enlightenment): Epistemological and sensory immediacy - Shifted into cognitive philosophy and psychology to describe raw perceptual impressions that strike human senses prior to intellectual reflection or reasoned deduction.
- 19th Century (Romantic and Victorian Era): Emotional intensity, aesthetic vitality, and dramatic presence - Celebrated in Romantic poetry, painting, and theatrical criticism as the supreme virtue of art capable of seizing the heart instantaneously.
- 20th to 21st Century: Media theory, digital immersion, and instantaneous telecommunication - Ubiquitous in discussions of breaking news, virtual reality, high-speed digital culture, and the continuous demand for unfiltered, real-time experiential connection.
