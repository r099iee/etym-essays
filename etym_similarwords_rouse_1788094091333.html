• Arouse: Similar because both mean to wake or stir a person or a feeling into activity; differs because 'arouse' is generally preferred for awakening emotions like suspicion or desire, and is formed by adding the prefix a- to 'rouse'.
  • Wake / Wake up: Similar because both mean to bring someone out of sleep; differs because 'wake' is the neutral everyday verb, whereas 'rouse' implies greater energy, effort, or suddenness.
  • Stir: Similar because both mean to set something dormant into motion; differs because 'stir' is gentler and more gradual, often used of emotions, whereas 'rouse' is more forceful and sudden.
  • Incense: Similar because both denote provoking a strong reaction in someone; differs because 'incense' means specifically to make angry or enrage, a narrower and hotter emotion than the general 'rouse'.
  • Provoke: Similar because both mean to cause a reaction, often anger; differs because 'provoke' carries deliberate intent and often a negative or confrontational nuance, while 'rouse' is neutral and can be positive.
  • Excite: Similar because both mean to stir up energy or emotion; differs because 'excite' focuses on heightening feeling or interest, while 'rouse' emphasizes bringing something from inactivity to activity.
  • Galvanize: Similar because both mean to shock or stir someone into sudden, energetic action; differs because 'galvanize' implies a sudden electric jolt or shock that impels decisive action, a stronger and more specific metaphor than 'rouse'.
  • Bestir: Similar because both mean to stir oneself into activity; differs because 'bestir' is a reflexive, slightly archaic reflexive verb ('bestir oneself'), gentler and more literary.
  • Waken / Awaken: Similar because both mean to come into or bring into a waking state; differs because these focus purely on the transition from sleep to wakefulness, without rouse's additional sense of stirring into vigorous action.
