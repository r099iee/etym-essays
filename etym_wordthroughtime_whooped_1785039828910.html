- 14th century - Whopen enters Middle English as the hunter's cry and battle-holler, a loud, rising call used to rouse hounds or signal victory, deliberately pitched to carry across distance.
- 17th-18th centuries - The word cements its sound-symbolic identity so firmly that the disease with its gasping, crowing cry is named whooping cough; whooping meanwhile stays a human exclamation of excitement, triumph, or derision.
- Contemporary sense - Whooped survives as the raw, exultant shout of games, hunts, and celebrations ('whooping it up'); it still carries a half-war-cry energy and is more festive than plain shouting.
