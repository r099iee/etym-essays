Note: In Classical Latin the standard form was dēminūtiō (from dē- + minuere "to make smaller", from PIE *mey- "small"). In post-classical and Late Latin, the prefix was frequently conflated with dis- ("apart, utterly"), producing the spelling with di- (diminūtiō). In music and rhetoric, the term acquired specialized technical meanings: in counterpoint, the repetition of a subject in shorter note values; in Roman law (capitis deminutio), the loss or reduction of civil status or legal capacity.
