[First element — mono-] PIE *men- 'small, isolated, alone' (reconstructed, unattested; the derivation of Greek mónos from a PIE root is debated — alternatives include *men-, *mein-, and a possible Pre-Greek origin, so the root must be flagged as uncertain) → Greek μόνος (mónos) 'alone, single, only' (attested from Homer, 8th century BCE) → Greek combining element μονο- (mono-) 'single-, one-' used as the first element of compounds (monólogos 'speaking alone,' monarkhía 'rule of one,' later English monologue, monopoly, monotheism) → first element of the compound that becomes English monarchy (below). The mono- element survives transparently in every modern mono- word: one, single, alone. [Second element — arkh- and the compound assembly] Greek ἄρχω (árkhō) 'to begin; to lead; to rule' (attested from the 8th century BCE; ultimate origin uncertain — possibly inherited, but no secure PIE etymon is established, and a Pre-Greek substrate origin is often suspected; state this as unknown rather than inventing a root) → Greek ἀρχή (arkhḗ) 'beginning, first place, office, sovereignty' (attested 7th–5th century BCE; 'beginning' and 'rule' are the same word, since the one who begins takes precedence) → Greek μοναρχία (monarkhía) 'rule of one' (compound of μόνος 'alone' + ἀρχή 'rule,' i.e., μονάρχης monárkhēs 'sole ruler' plus the abstract suffix -ia; attested in Aeschylus and Herodotus, 5th century BCE) → Latin monarchia 'rule of one, monarchy' (learned borrowing from Greek, attested 1st century BCE–1st century CE) → Old French monarchie 'monarchy' (attested 12th century) → Middle English monarchie, monarchy 'monarchy' (attested 14th century, e.g., in Chaucer) → English monarchy 'form of government with a monarch at the head; the institution of royal government' (modern; sister words via Greek arkhē include oligarchy, anarchy, hierarchy, diarchy, patriarchy).
