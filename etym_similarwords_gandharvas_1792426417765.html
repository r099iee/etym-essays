• apsarases: Similar because both are celestial beings residing in Indra's heaven who often pair together as cosmic partners; differs because apsarases are exclusively female water and cloud nymphs renowned for their seductive dancing and alluring beauty, whereas gandharvas are male musicians, singers, warriors, and guardians of the soma.
  • kinnaras: Similar because both are semi-divine musicians in Indian mythology associated with mountain groves and celestial performance; differs because kinnaras are mythical hybrid beings (traditionally depicted with human bodies and horse heads, or human torsos with bird wings and legs) residing in the Himālayas under Kubera, whereas gandharvas possess fully anthropomorphic, radiant divine bodies.
  • vidyādharas: Similar because both are aerial, supernatural demigods possessing supernatural powers and inhabiting ethereal realms; differs because vidyādharas are literally "holders of magical knowledge" (wisdom/spell masters) celebrated for flying through the air, wielding esoteric occult sciences and magical swords, whereas gandharvas specialize in music, melody, and romantic charm.
  • yakṣas: Similar because both are classes of nature demigods and nature spirits (*upadevas*) in ancient Indian cosmology; differs because yakṣas are earthy custodians of subterranean wealth, mineral treasures, trees, and fertility ruled by Kubera, often possessing stocky or fierce forms, whereas gandharvas are airy, celestial, and refined artists of heaven.
  • devas: Similar because both dwell in heavenly spheres and enjoy celestial pleasures; differs because devas are the supreme cosmic gods (such as Indra, Agni, Varuṇa) who receive sacrificial offerings in Vedic rituals and govern cosmic laws, whereas gandharvas are subordinate demigods who serve the devas as performers and attendants.
  • asuras: Similar because both are powerful supernatural entities in Vedic literature; differs because asuras are the titanic, primordial rivals and adversaries of the gods who engage in constant cosmic warfare for supremacy, whereas gandharvas are peaceful allies and courtiers of the gods.
  • rākṣasas: Similar because both are supernatural races interacting with mortals in epic narratives; differs because rākṣasas are ferocious, carnivorous demons of darkness, night, and sorcery who terrorize ascetics and defile sacrifices, whereas gandharvas are radiant, benevolent celestial beings of light and music.
  • cāraṇas: Similar because both are celestial bards who praise the deeds of heroes and gods; differs because cāraṇas are specifically wandering celestial chroniclers and eulogists, whereas gandharvas are master instrumentalists, composers, and dancers who control the esoteric science of music (*Gāndharva-veda*).
  • nymphs: Similar because both are nature spirits in mythological traditions associated with music, woods, and romance; differs because nymphs in Greco-Roman mythology are exclusively female nature spirits associated with springs, trees, and grottos (closer to apsarases), whereas gandharvas are male celestial beings.
  • muses: Similar because both are supernatural patrons of music, song, poetry, and divine performance; differs because the nine Muses of Greek myth are goddesses who inspire human creativity and intellectual arts, whereas gandharvas are an entire celestial race who actively perform for the assembly of the gods.
