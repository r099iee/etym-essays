- Roman-era emergence - the Franks entered Roman sources in the third century as fierce West Germanic raiders on the lower Rhine, the very name (from *frank, free, bold) marking their martial liberty.
- Merovingian conversion - under Clovis the Franks conquered Gaul and embraced Catholic Christianity about 496, and the name became that of the founders of France - Francia, the land of the Franks - the one Germanic people in alliance with the papacy.
- Medieval and Byzantine usage - in Byzantium and Islam Frank came to mean any Western European or crusader, so the tribal name swelled into a general label for the Latin West.
- Contemporary sense - Franks is now a purely historical name, surviving in France, Frankfurt, and the adjective frank (free, honest), while the Franks of Procopius serve in Gibbon as Britain's real, inhabited continental neighbours across the Channel.
