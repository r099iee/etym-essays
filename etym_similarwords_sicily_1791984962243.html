• Sicilia: Similar because it is the same island under its Latin and Italian name and appears in English texts as a learned variant; differs because Sicilia belongs to the administrative and poetic register, evoking the Roman province or the modern region, while Sicily is the ordinary English geographical name.
  • Trinacria: Similar because it is an ancient name for exactly the same island, built on its three-cornered shape; differs because Trinacria is archaic and literary, used for coins, emblems and poetic effect, never for the modern region.
  • Sikelia: Similar because it is the Greek ancestor of the name and still used by historians to mark the Greek period; differs because it denotes the island as the Greeks conceived it, before the Roman province existed, and it is a technical transliteration rather than an English word.
  • Magna Graecia: Similar because it names the Greek world of southern Italy and Sicily and overlaps Sicily in every classical discussion; differs because it is a cultural label for the Greek settlements of the whole region, not a territorial name for one island.
  • Sardinia: Similar because it is the other great island of the western Mediterranean and a Roman province of the same generation; differs because Sardinia was taken in 238 BCE and remained poorer and less Hellenised, so it carries none of the Greek cultural associations of Sicily.
  • Corsica: Similar because it is a large Mediterranean island with a Roman provincial history; differs because Corsica is small, mountainous and marginal in ancient sources, whereas Sicily was Rome's granary and the scene of two great wars.
  • Malta: Similar because it lies just south of Sicily and shared its Phoenician, Greek and Roman fate; differs because Malta is a small archipelago with its own language and state, never a province of the scale that Sicily was.
  • Crete: Similar because it is a large Mediterranean island with a Bronze Age civilisation and a Roman province; differs because Crete's classical fame is Minoan and its Roman period comparatively obscure, while Sicily's importance is precisely Roman and Greek together.
