- [19th century: first appearances in English] - 'Kang' enters English through missionary and travel accounts of China as the name of the northern brick bed-platform; connotation is foreign and ethnographic - an exotic, eye-witness detail of Chinese daily life.
- [20th century: the vocabulary of Chinese studies] - The word settles into sinology and translated fiction as the standard term for the heated brick platform; connotation is cultural and technical, the marker of an authentic, lived-in Chinese interior, kept untranslated precisely because no English word does its work.
- [Contemporary sense] - Still a specialised loanword with no true English equivalent; connotation is intimate and domestic - the warm, flue-heated heart of the northern Chinese household where the family sleeps, sits, eats, and gathers through the winter.
