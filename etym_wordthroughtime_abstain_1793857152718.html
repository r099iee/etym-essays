- Late 14th–15th Century: Ascetic and devotional mortification - entered Middle English through Anglo-French and Latin religious literature, centered on fasting during Lent and subduing carnal lusts and bodily gluttony for spiritual salvation.
- 16th–17th Century: Civic virtue and moral moderation - broadened during the Renaissance and English Civil War to encompass political moderation, withholding vengeful violence, and exercising Stoic mastery over volatile passions.
- 18th–19th Century: Intellectual skepticism and critical discipline - adopted by Enlightenment philosophers and scientific empiricists (such as Hume and Menger) to describe the deliberate withholding of judgment until empirical evidence is verified.
- Mid-19th to Early 20th Century: The Temperance and Teetotal movements - colloquial perception narrowed dramatically in Britain and America to the strict, pledge-bound refusal to consume alcoholic beverages.
- Modern & Contemporary Usage: Parliamentary neutrality and dietary lifestyle - widely used today for formal non-voting in legislative bodies (e.g., the UN Security Council) alongside health-conscious dietary choices and clinical medical restrictions.
