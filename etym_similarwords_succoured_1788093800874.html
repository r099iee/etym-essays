• Helped: Similar because both mean providing assistance; differs because helped is the plain, unmarked word for any assistance however small, while succoured implies serious, urgent need and decisive relief.
  • Aided: Similar because both mean giving help; differs because aided is formal but neutral and technical (financial or military aid), without the emotional charge of rescuing someone in distress.
  • Assisted: Similar because both mean giving help; differs because assisted suggests secondary, practical support (assisting a colleague) rather than life-or-death relief.
  • Relieved: Similar because both describe bringing relief to people in hardship (the relief of a besieged town); differs because relieved also means easing pain and taking over someone's duty, and it lacks the single-stroke urgency of succoured.
  • Rescued: Similar because both describe saving people from danger; differs because rescued implies extraction from immediate peril (from fire, flood or capture), whereas succoured implies sustained relief within ongoing need.
  • Saved: Similar because both describe keeping someone from harm; differs in extremity and finality — salvation is complete and conclusive, while succour is help along the way.
  • Delivered: Similar because both describe setting someone free from danger; differs because delivered is dramatic and Biblical ("deliver us from evil") and broader in scope, spanning legal, literal and spiritual rescue.
  • Supported: Similar because both mean holding someone up; differs because supported is physical or emotional backing of any degree — weaker, more general and more continuous than succoured.
  • Befriended: Similar because both describe kindness to someone who had no claim — precisely Bard's point that the Elvenking helped the Lake-people "though they had no claim but friendship"; differs because befriended means treating someone as a friend over time, whereas succoured is a single decisive act of relief.
  • Ministered to: Similar because both describe attending to the sick or needy; differs in register — ministered to has a clerical or nursing flavour and implies tending, while succoured implies supplying relief at scale.
  • Comforted: Similar because both ease distress; differs because comforted addresses emotional suffering in particular, while succoured addresses concrete need.
  • Sustained: Similar because both describe keeping people going through hardship; differs because sustained implies continuous supply over time, while succoured is the relieving intervention itself.
  • Bailed out: Similar because both describe rescuing someone from a scrape; differs in register — bailed out is informal and largely financial, while succoured is formal and humanitarian.
  • Came to the aid of: Similar because both express going to someone's assistance; differs in being a descriptive periphrastic phrase rather than a single compact verb with the word's grave register.
