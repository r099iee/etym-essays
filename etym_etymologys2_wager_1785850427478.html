Debate: English wager reaches English along the Frankish → Old French (guaige) → Anglo-French (wageure) route shown above; a parallel Norse path runs through Old Norse veðja "to pledge, wager" (cf. Old English weddian "to pledge", source of wed and wedding). Both branches descend from PGmc *wadja- < PIE *wedʰ-. The noun (Anglo-French wageure, c. 1300) and the verb (ME wagen; re-formed in the 16th c.) have intertwined histories.
