• Proto-Indo-European *genh₁- "to give birth, beget" (also written *gen-), the root of "birth, kind, race" across the family.
  • Latin gignere "to beget, give birth", and the noun genus "birth, descent, kind" — the -gn- of benignus belongs to this family.
  • Latin bene "well" (adverb of bonus, "good") + gignere/genus root: Latin benignus "well-born, good-natured, kind" (attested Classical Latin, 1st century BCE; the antonym malignus "evil-natured" is formed with male "badly").
  • Old French benigne "kind, gracious, favorable" (10th–12th century), inherited from Latin benignus.
  • Middle English benigne/benign "kindly, gracious, favorable" (borrowed c. 1300–1400 from Anglo-French and Old French).
  • Modern English BENIGN — with the medical sense "not malignant" developing from the 19th century in pathology (contrasted with malignant).

Debate note: The morphemic analysis is secure: benignus = bene ("well") + the gn- root of gignere ("beget"), giving "born well". The only nuance is that some older handbooks glossed the second element loosely as a suffix -gnus of unknown value; modern historical linguistics ties it firmly to the "birth/kind" family. The PIE root *genh₁- is well established across Indo-European (Greek genos, English kin, kind, gender).
