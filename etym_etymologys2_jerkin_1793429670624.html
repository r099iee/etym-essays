Note: The leading philological consensus (supported by the Oxford English Dictionary and modern etymologists) derives jerkin from the Middle Dutch pet name Dirk (from Diederik, cognate with English Theodoric) combined with the affectionate diminutive suffix -kijn (Modern English -kin). Naming garments after personal pet names was a common Germanic and Romance tendency—compare French Jacques yielding English jacket, and Italian Pantalone yielding pantaloons. An alternate hypothesis derives the word from Dutch jurk ("a frock, child's coat"), but jurk is attested only from the 17th century in Dutch literature and is itself thought to be a back-formation or loan from the same base.
