• Flatfish: Similar because a halibut is a flatfish, and the word names the whole family to which it belongs; differs because “flatfish” is the generic category (plaice, flounder, sole, turbot, dab are all flatfishes), while halibut is the specific giant of the group.
  • Plaice: Similar because it is a common edible flatfish of European seas; differs because the plaice is much smaller, with orange spots on a brown upper side, and is a different genus (Pleuronectes), whereas the halibut is the huge Hippoglossus.
  • Flounder: Similar because it is an edible flatfish with both eyes on one side; differs because “flounder” covers several smaller flatfishes and also works as a verb meaning “to struggle awkwardly,” a sense halibut entirely lacks.
  • Sole: Similar because it is a prized, fine-fleshed flatfish eaten in high cuisine; differs because the sole is small, delicate, and expensive, while the halibut is a large, firm-fleshed giant, and “sole” also means the bottom of a foot or shoe.
  • Turbot: Similar because it is a large, valued flatfish with white flesh; differs because the turbot is a left-eyed flatfish of a different family (Scophthalmidae) with a diamond-shaped body, while the halibut is right-eyed and elongate.
  • Dab: Similar because it is a small flatfish of the same family; differs because the dab is tiny, insignificant, and common, a world away from the halibut’s size and value.
  • Cod: Similar because it is a cold-water, white-fleshed fish of northern waters eaten as a staple; differs because the cod is a round fish (not flat), with no eye migration and a completely different body shape and family (Gadidae).
  • Haddock: Similar because it is a northern white-fleshed food fish; differs because haddock is a round, smaller fish, smoked to make finnan haddie, with no flatfish characteristics at all.
  • Salmon: Similar because it is a large, commercially vital, prized food fish; differs because salmon is a round, anadromous fish (born in rivers, maturing at sea) of a different family, and its name is normally invariant in the plural.
  • Skate / Ray: Similar because it is a large, flat-bodied sea animal eaten as food; differs because the skate is a cartilaginous fish (a relative of sharks) with a wing-like body and a tail, not a bony flatfish with both eyes on one side.
