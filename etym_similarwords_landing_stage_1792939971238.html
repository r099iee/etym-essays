• Landing: Similar because every landing-stage provides a landing; differs because landing names the act or the place in general, while landing-stage names the built timber platform itself.
  • Jetty: Similar because both are structures reaching into water for boats; differs because a jetty juts out to make shelter or depth, while a landing-stage is specifically decked for stepping ashore.
  • Pier: Similar because piers also receive boats and walkers; differs because a pier is the grander, often iron-built structure (pleasure piers, ocean piers), while a landing-stage is humbler river furniture.
  • Wharf: Similar because both handle cargo between ship and shore; differs because a wharf is the paved bank or warehouse front itself, while a landing-stage is the projecting boards at its edge.
  • Pontoon: Similar because tidal landing-stages often are pontoons; differs because pontoon names the floating hull or raft, while landing-stage names its use as a boarding deck.
  • Quay: Similar because quays line the water where vessels lie; differs because a quay is masonry shoreline, while a landing-stage is timberwork reaching beyond it.
  • Slipway: Similar because both serve boats at the bank; differs because a slipway slopes into the water to launch and haul out hulls, while a landing-stage stays level for feet and freight.
  • Gangway: Similar because both belong to embarking; differs because the gangway is the hinged plank walked across, while the landing-stage is the platform walked onto.
  • Platform: Similar because both are raised standing surfaces; differs because platform is general (railway, oil, stage), while landing-stage is water-specific and hyphenated to its purpose.
