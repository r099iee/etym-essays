• Epistolic: Similar because it also denotes pertaining to or having the nature of an epistle; differs because 'epistolic' is rarer, more archaic, and primarily ecclesiastical, whereas 'epistolary' is the standard literary and critical adjective.
  • Missive: Similar because it concerns written correspondence; differs because 'missive' is a noun designating the physical letter itself (often formal, weighty, or official), whereas 'epistolary' is an adjective describing the mode, genre, or structure.
  • Documentary: Similar because both modes rely on non-fictionalizing artifacts (letters, reports, files) to construct a narrative; differs because 'documentary' implies factual recording, administrative archives, or journalistic realism, whereas 'epistolary' specifically prioritises interpersonal written exchanges and subjective sentiment.
  • Epistolographic: Similar because it pertains directly to letter writing; differs because 'epistolographic' specifically refers to the technical art, rhetoric, style, or scholarly study of letter composition rather than the narrative genre itself.
  • Polyphonic: Similar because epistolary works often feature multiple letter writers offering contrasting viewpoints; differs because 'polyphonic' is a broader literary/musical term for multiple distinct voices without necessitating an exchange of written letters.
  • Scriptural: Similar because both involve ancient or sacred written texts and letters; differs because 'scriptural' relates strictly to sacred religious canon and holy writ, whereas 'epistolary' covers any letter-based communication, secular or literary.
  • Confessional: Similar because epistolary writing frequently captures intimate personal confessions; differs because 'confessional' describes the raw, revelatory emotional tone or biographical genre, regardless of whether it uses letters, prose memoir, or lyric poetry.
  • Postal: Similar because both relate to the transmission of mail; differs because 'postal' concerns the civil, logistic, and physical infrastructure of mail delivery, whereas 'epistolary' concerns the literary, formal, and textual nature of correspondence.
