- [Roman republic (121–118 BCE): Provincia = the newly conquered land of the Saluvii and Vocontii, the first province beyond the Alps] - [Perception: an administrative war-name — conquered territory, a tax base; no glamour yet]
- [5th–10th c.: after Roman rule fades, Provence remains the everyday name of the region; the word outlives the empire and its provinces] - [Perception: plain geography — the south of Gaul, land of counts and bishops]
- [12th–13th c.: Provence and the troubadours — Guilhem de Peitieus, Arnaut Daniel; "poetry of Provence" becomes shorthand for courtly love] - [Perception: the name turns poetic; Europe hears Provence as the land where love was first written beautifully]
- [1481: Provence is annexed to the French crown, becoming a province of France] - [Perception: the word slips from power politics to picturesque local colour]
- [19th c.: Mistral and the Félibrige revive Provençal language and folklore; Romantics and tourists discover the region] - [Perception: rustic authenticity, sun, ancient towns; nostalgia for an un-French France]
- [20th–21st c.: Pagnol's films, lavender, markets, the Côte d'Azur; "Provence" is a global lifestyle brand] - [Perception: leisure, colour, food, Mediterranean ease — the medieval sweetness Pater described is now sold as tourism]
