Etruscan gentilic (family) name Ruma / Rumaś — attested in Etruscan inscriptions; plausibly the source of the Latin city-name Rōma "Rome" (the site lies on the Etruscan bank of the Tiber; the Roman family name Rōmilia is compared). The substrate origin is the preferred modern account; a PIE derivation is not established (see debate note) ↓ Latin Rōma, "Rome" — the city name, of debated origin (Etruscan by the preferred modern account; ancient folk-etymologies connect it with Greek rhōmē "strength" or with the river-name Rumōn, both rejected) ↓ Latin Rōmulus, "little Roman; the one of Rome" — diminutive/adjectival formation on Rōma with the suffix -ulus (cf. catulus "puppy"); borne by the legendary founder; attested in Roman tradition from the 3rd c. BCE (Ennius, Fabius Pictor, Livy) ↓ Romulus — the name as taken directly from Latin through classical historiography and the medieval legendaries ◄ TARGET DEBATED / NUANCED: The etymology of the city-name Rōma — and hence of Romulus — is genuinely uncertain. The modern consensus favours an Etruscan origin (the gentilic Ruma/Rumaś attested in Etruscan inscriptions), which matches the site's archaeology and the region's linguistic substrate. The ancient derivations from Greek rhōmē "strength" (Plutarch records it) and from the Tiber-name *Rumōn (Varro/Servius, connected with PIE *sreu- "to flow") are now universally rejected as folk-etymologies. The -ulus suffix on Rōma is secure Latin morphology; what remains open is only the origin of the base Rōma itself.
