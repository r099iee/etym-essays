- Gaulish cagion (enclosure/hedge) - A staked, fenced-in riverbank where boats could shelter and goods could be landed.
- Roman caium (dockyard enclosure) - Enclosures for beaching and storing ships; a working maritime term recorded by Roman writers.
- Old North French cai (12th c.) - The wharf or landing stage of a trading town; goods move between shore and boat.
- Middle English keye / key (c. 1390) - "Sandbank, wharf"; the word is written like "key" and pronounced the same for two centuries.
- 17th–18th century respelling quay - French spelling is adopted while the sound stays "key"; a famous spelling–pronunciation mismatch is born.
- Industrial era - Quays become grand masonry waterfronts of docks, cranes, and warehouses; the working face of world trade.
- Modern - "Quays" = the built landing stages of harbours and riversides; also fashionable redeveloped waterfronts for leisure (cafés, promenades), drifting from industry to the picturesque.
