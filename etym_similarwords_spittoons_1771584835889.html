• Cuspidor: Similar in being a receptacle designed for spitting; differs because cuspidor is the elegant alternative name, from the Portuguese verb cuspir, "to spit," used for the ornamental porcelain form in hotel lobbies — a word that sounds refined even when the thing it names is not.
  • Spit-bucket: Similar in being a container for saliva; differs because spit-bucket is the crude, colloquial alternative of saloons and barrooms — cheap, functional, and slangy — whereas spittoon is the general and now slightly old-fashioned standard name.
  • Receptacle: Similar in being a container for something discarded; differs because receptacle is the completely neutral, technical word for any container, lacking the specific bodily, historical, and institutional identity of the spittoon.
  • Basin: Similar in being a wide open vessel; differs because a basin is a general vessel for liquid of any kind (washing, mixing), with no dedicated spitting function and no historical baggage.
  • Bowl: Similar in being a round open vessel; differs because a bowl is simply any round container for food or liquid, whereas a spittoon has a single, specific bodily purpose.
  • Chamber pot: Similar in being a vessel of bodily waste kept at hand indoors; differs because a chamber pot receives urine and faeces, whereas a spittoon receives only saliva and phlegm from the mouth.
  • Spit-box: Similar in being a small vessel for expectoration; differs because spit-box is the plain, utilitarian dialect name, while spittoon is the standard term with the grander -oon ending.
  • Expectoratorium: Similar in being a place or vessel for expectoration; differs because expectoratorium is a rare, clinical Latinism, whereas spittoon is the everyday word.
  • Urinal: Similar in being a fixed receptacle for bodily waste; differs because a urinal receives urine and is wall-mounted and public, while a spittoon receives saliva and is portable and domestic or social.
