• Tinted: Similar because both describe a light application of colour to something that keeps its base appearance; differs because tint often refers to a deliberate, uniform shade or a slight variation of a colour (hair tint, window tint), whereas tinge more often suggests an incidental or external trace of a different colour and also has a strong figurative sense.
  • Coloured / colored: Similar because both name the presence of colour; differs because coloured is far broader and more neutral (any colour at all, often fully saturated), while tinged specifically means only a faint, partial wash and commonly implies figurative undertones.
  • Dyed: Similar because both involve colouring an object; differs because dyed implies deep, intentional, permanent penetration of a fabric or material with a pigment, whereas tinged is superficial, light, and often unintended.
  • Stained: Similar because both mean "given colour"; differs because stained usually implies a discoloration that is irregular, lasting, or blemishing (coffee stains, bloodstains) and rarely carries the delicate figurative sense of tinged.
  • Shaded: Similar because both describe a modification of colour; differs because shaded works with darkness and lightness relative to a base colour, while tinged adds a foreign hue and is more often figurative.
  • Suffused: Similar because both describe colour spreading through something; differs because suffused implies a fuller, more pervasive diffusion of light or colour over the whole, whereas tinged is fainter and more peripheral.
  • Tainted: Similar because both can mean "slightly contaminated or affected by a trace"; differs because tainted is almost always negative and implies corruption, spoilage, or moral blemish, while tinged is neutral-to-positive and merely descriptive.
  • Touched: Similar because both can mean "slightly affected by a quality" (touched with madness); differs because touched is broader (physical contact, emotional response) and less specifically about colour.
  • Flavoured / seasoned: Similar because both can describe a small admixture that alters character (figurative: a performance flavoured with humour); differs because these belong primarily to taste and cooking registers, whereas tinged is anchored in colour and feeling.
  • Streaked: Similar because both describe partial colouring; differs because streaked implies visible lines or bands of colour, whereas tinged is a uniform faint wash without pattern.
