• Kind: Similar because both denote a gentle, well-disposed nature; differs because kind implies active, conscious goodness and warm conduct toward others, while benign stresses an inherent, passive disposition of harmlessness and mildness.
  • Kindly: Similar because both describe a gracious, gentle character; differs because kindly is warmer and more actively affectionate, while benign is more formal and constitutional, implying a settled nature rather than a manner.
  • Gentle: Similar because both suggest mildness and non-aggression; differs because gentle emphasizes softness of manner, touch, or force, while benign emphasizes the absence of harm and a well-born, good nature.
  • Gracious: Similar because both describe a pleasant, favorable disposition; differs because gracious suggests charm, courtesy, and the condescension of rank, while benign suggests safety, mildness, and good nature without ceremony.
  • Favorable: Similar because both describe auspicious, helpful conditions; differs because favorable stresses being advantageous to a cause or outcome, while benign stresses being harmless and mild in effect.
  • Auspicious: Similar because both can describe promising, favorable circumstances; differs because auspicious implies a promising omen for future success, while benign is descriptive of a harm-free present state.
  • Harmless: Similar because both mean causing no harm; differs because harmless is purely negative and can suggest weakness or impotence, while benign implies a positively good, well-natured disposition or a structurally safe condition.
  • Innocuous: Similar because both mean not harmful; differs because innocuous emphasizes being unobjectionable and often trivial or bland, while benign can describe substantial things (even growths) that are simply not harmful.
  • Mild: Similar because both suggest absence of harshness; differs because mild refers to intensity or extremity (weather, flavour, temperament) while benign adds the idea of a good, harm-free nature or disposition.
  • Nonmalignant: Similar because it is the exact medical counterpart for growths that do not spread; differs because nonmalignant is purely a technical medical term with no moral or personal sense, while benign also means kindly and gracious.
  • Good-natured: Similar because both describe a pleasant, steady disposition; differs because good-natured is a plain everyday term for a cheerful temperament, while benign is more formal and carries the added sense of being harmless and favorable.
  • Benevolent: Similar because both denote good will; differs because benevolent stresses actively wishing and doing good for others, while benign stresses an inborn mildness and the mere absence of harm.
