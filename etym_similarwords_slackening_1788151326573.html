• Easing: Similar because both name the reduction of tension, pressure, or intensity; differs in that easing stresses gentleness and gradualness (ease off), while slackening carries the older physical image of a taut line going limp.
  • Loosening: Similar because both describe making something less tight; differs in that loosening is primarily physical (knots, screws, grip), while slackening has migrated much further into pace, effort, and economics.
  • Relaxing: Similar as the easing of tension in body or rules; differs in that relaxing is usually deliberate and pleasant, while slackening may be involuntary, unwelcome, or faintly reproachful.
  • Slowing: Similar because both name deceleration; differs in that slowing is purely about speed while slackening keeps the texture of loosening and relaxation that spreading causes.
  • Abating: Similar in describing the diminution of force or intensity (the storm abates); differs in that abate implies a natural decline of the thing itself, while slacken can be someone’s deliberate relaxation of effort.
  • Diminishing: Similar as a general decrease; differs by being wholly neutral and measurable, without the physical-moral image of the slackening line.
  • Waning: Similar as the gradual lessening of light, force, or enthusiasm; differs in being cyclical and organic (“the moon wanes”), while slackening suggests tension released rather than substance consumed.
  • Flagging: Similar because both describe vigour or interest drooping; differs in that flagging is about fatigue of the agent, while slackening is about the loosening of the activity itself.
  • Faltering: Similar as the weakening of momentum; differs in implying unsteadiness and threatened collapse, while slackening implies a smooth, even easing.
  • Letting up: Similar as an informal synonym for easing off; differs sharply in register (conversational) and in lacking the nautical-physical image of slackening.
  • Decelerating: Similar in the narrow sense of slowing; differs as a technical, statistical word for speed or growth, with none of the tactile or moral range of slackening.
