Note on historical debate and Germanic cognates: The etymology of English dot is one of the most intriguing puzzles in Germanic historical linguistics. The noun dott appears in Old English as a hapax legomenon in an 11th-century manuscript (Cotton Cleopatra A. iii) glossing Latin pustula. Philologists note that it has close cognates in North Sea Germanic dialects: Middle Dutch dotte / Modern Dutch dot ("lump of spun wool, knot, little child"), Middle Low German dotte ("egg yolk"), Old Frisian dott, and regional Norwegian/Swedish dialects dott ("lump, tuft, heap"). Most historical linguists, including the Oxford English Dictionary, conclude that the base is a Germanic nursery or substrate formation representing a small rounded clump or lump, which subsequently narrowed in writing culture to a point of ink.
