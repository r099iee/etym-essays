• pin: Similar because it fastens cloth with a point and a catch; differs because the pin is the purely functional ancestor — a bare safety pin fastens without ornament, whereas a brooch is a pin that has been promoted into jewellery.
  • safety pin: Similar because it is a spring-loaded pin that fastens cloth; differs because the safety pin is utilitarian and hidden or incidental, whereas a brooch is a visible ornament of precious metal and gems.
  • fibula: Similar because it is the ancient ancestor of the safety-pin brooch, used to fasten the Roman toga and Celtic cloaks; differs because "fibula" is the archaeological term for ancient brooches and carries no modern fashion sense, while "brooch" is the living everyday word.
  • clasp: Similar because it is a closing mechanism; differs because a clasp closes a box, bag, or necklace by interlocking parts and carries no sense of being worn decoratively on the surface of a garment.
  • clip: Similar because it fastens without sewing; differs because a clip presses or grips fabric rather than piercing it with a pin, so it lacks the brooch's piercing point and its jewellery tradition.
  • buckle: Similar because it fastens dress; differs because a buckle works through a frame and tongue on straps and belts, serving harness rather than an ornament pinned to the breast for display.
  • pendant: Similar because it is a piece of worn jewellery; differs because the pendant hangs on a chain or cord about the neck — it dangles rather than pierces the cloth, so it occupies a different part of the jewellery anatomy.
  • locket: Similar because it is a small personal ornament containing keepsakes; differs because the locket is a hinged container worn on a chain, typically about the neck, rather than a pin fastened through clothing.
  • medallion: Similar because it is a round, coin-like ornament worn for display; differs because a medallion is usually commemorative or award-bearing and hangs on a chain or ribbon, whereas a brooch fastens with a pin and is worn as personal adornment.
  • badge: Similar because it is a small emblem worn on clothing; differs because a badge signals membership, office, or rank rather than personal adornment, and is usually pinned, sewn, or attached without the brooch's jewellery function.
  • torc / torque: Similar because it is an ancient piece of Celtic personal jewellery of gold; differs because a torc is a rigid neck-ring worn around the throat, not a pin fastened through cloth, so it occupies a different part of the jewellery anatomy.
  • broach: Similar because it is the historical twin of "brooch" — once the same word, a pointed instrument; differs because "broach" is now the roasting spit, the tool for tapping casks, and the verb "to broach" (to pierce, to open up a topic), while "brooch" alone names the jewelled pin.
