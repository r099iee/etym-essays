PIE (reconstructed)
*bʰew- / *bʰuH-
"to grow, become, exist, arise"

Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)

"tree, living trunk, wooden beam"

↓

↓
Modern English (c. 1850s verb sense)
beam
"to smile radiantly, glow with pleasure"
↓
Modern English (past tense & participle)
beamed
"smiled radiantly; radiated rays of light"
Note: Proto-Germanic *baumaz yielded Dutch boom ("tree") and German Baum ("tree"). While continental Germanic retained "tree" as the primary meaning, Old English specialized bēam into architectural squared wood, the Holy Rood/Cross, and crucially, translated Latin columna (pillar) in the biblical pillar of fire (fȳrbēam). This led directly to sunnebēam ("sunbeam"), initiating English's unique semantic leap from timber to radiant light and smiling.
