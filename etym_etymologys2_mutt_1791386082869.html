Note: Mutt arose as an American English colloquial abbreviation of muttonhead ('stupid person', attested 1803, comparing a dim person's brain to the dense flesh or skull of a sheep). The animal shift from sheep to dog occurred around 1900–1904, first applied to clumsy or mongrel dogs in sporting and race contexts. An alternative dialect influence from northern English mutton (scolding cry to dogs that chased sheep) may have reinforced the association, but the direct clipping from muttonhead remains the universally accepted historical derivation.
