• Venerated: Similar because both describe being held in profound, awe-inspired respect and solemn honor; differs because venerated typically carries stronger associations with extreme old age, religious holiness, ancient tradition, or formal sacred rites (such as venerated relics or an ancient sage), whereas revered applies more broadly to modern literary masterpieces, secular heroes, moral leaders, or artistic achievements.
  • Admired: Similar because both involve looking upon someone or something with high regard and warm approval; differs because admired represents a much lighter, more accessible degree of intellectual or emotional appreciation for talent or good qualities, whereas revered denotes a solemn, awe-filled elevation that borders on the sacred (as highlighted in the context: "enjoyed and admired, not to say revered").
  • Adored: Similar because both express intense positive devotion toward a figure; differs because adored is steeped in deep emotional warmth, romantic tenderness, or familial affection (often accompanied by playful or intimate closeness), whereas revered maintains a dignified, solemn, and respectful distance of holy awe.
  • Idolized: Similar because both represent elevating an individual or work to an extraordinary height; differs because idolized implies blind, uncritical, and often superficial or irrational infatuation that borders on pagan worship or celebrity fanaticism, whereas revered implies genuine, earned solemnity and monumental moral or artistic stature.
  • Exalted: Similar because both signify being raised to supreme honor and glory; differs because exalted is an active, high-register term emphasizing the dynamic elevation of someone to supreme rank, heavenly majesty, or ecstatic mental feeling, whereas revered characterizes the steady, enduring state of being respected by others.
  • Esteemed: Similar because both convey high social or professional regard; differs because esteemed is a restrained, polite, and conventional term of formal approbation (such as an esteemed colleague), lacking the deep emotional awe, sacred gravity, and spiritual reverence of revered.
  • Hallowed: Similar because both denote entities regarded with supreme sanctity; differs because hallowed is almost exclusively religious or ceremonial, describing ground, traditions, or names made holy by formal consecration, divine presence, or historical martyrdom (such as hallowed ground), whereas revered describes the active human feeling of profound admiration and deference.
