[Root Word 1: PYRO- (Ancient Greek pyr "fire")]
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*péh₂wr̥ / *ph₂ur-
"fire, inanimate elemental fire"
Note: Archaic PIE heteroclitic neuter noun; the animate sacred fire was *h₁n̥gʷnis (Latin ignis, Sanskrit Agni).
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th–4th c. BCE)
pŷr (πῦρ) / pyr- (πυρ-)
"fire, flame, lightning, sacrificial blaze"

[Root Word 2: TECHNIST (Ancient Greek tékhnē "art, skill, craft" + -ist)]
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*tek- / *teks-
"to weave, fabricate, fashion, construct"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th–4th c. BCE)
tékhnē (τέχνη)
"art, craft, mechanical skill, systematic trade"
↓
Hellenistic Greek / Medieval Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE–15th c.)
technē (τέχνη) + -istēs (-ιστής)
"craftsman, practitioner of an art"

[Compound Synthesis: PYROTECHNIST]
Modern Neoclassical Greek-Latin Formation (c. 17th c.)
pyrotechnia (πυροτεχνία)
"the art or science of manipulating fire and gunpowder"
↓
Late 18th Century English (c. 1770–1785)
pyrotechnist
"one skilled in the art of making fireworks or managing explosives"
↓
Modern English (present)
PYROTECHNIST
