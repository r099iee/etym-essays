- Late 14th Century: Chemical liquefaction, physical decomposition, and mortal death - Denoted melting solid substances into liquid, alchemical solution, or the soul separating from the physical body upon death.
- 15th to 16th Century: Legal annulment, institutional disbandment, and parliamentary prorogation - Applied to royal decrees terminating parliament, ending treaties, or voiding marriages and legal covenants.
- 17th to 18th Century: Emotional sensibility and psychological softening - Popularized in sentimental literature and drama to depict weeping, swooning, or melting into tears ("dissolved in grief").
- 19th Century: Chemical thermodynamics and sociological evolution - Codified in physical chemistry (solute-solvent dynamics) while used by historians and social theorists (Marx, Durant) to describe the disintegration of ancient social systems.
- 20th to 21st Century: Cinematographic editing transitions and corporate bankruptcy - Ubiquitous in film transitions (cross-dissolves), corporate liquidations, and everyday figurative language for vanishing tension.
