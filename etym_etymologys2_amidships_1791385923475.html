[ROOT 2: SHIP]
PIE (reconstructed)
*(s)key- / *sek-
"to cut, split, hollow out"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*skipą
"dugout boat, vessel"
↓

scip

[COMPOUND & ADVERBIAL GENITIVE]
Nautical English (1680s–1690s)
amid- + ship + -s
"in the middle of the ship"
↓
Modern English (current form)
amidships
"in the middle of a ship; midriff"
Note: As a compound of multiple root words, both roots are traced above in isolation. The final -s represents the historical Proto-Germanic adverbial genitive (Old English -es), which formed adverbs of position and manner (as in unawares, betwixt, towards). Root 2 (*skipą) is traditionally traced to PIE *(s)key- ('to cut, hollow out' a log into a canoe), though some philologists suggest an unconfirmed non-IE northern substrate maritime origin.
