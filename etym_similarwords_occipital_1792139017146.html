• Cranial: Similar because both relate to the human skull and its internal contents; differs because cranial encompasses the entirety of the skull or braincase as a whole, whereas occipital strictly denotes the back, lower-posterior portion of the cranium.
  • Parietal: Similar because both designate paired primary cranial bones and cerebral lobes; differs because the parietal region forms the upper sides and roof of the skull responsible for somatosensory processing, whereas occipital forms the posterior base dedicated to vision.
  • Frontal: Similar because both refer to major cranial bones and lobes of the cerebral cortex; differs because frontal occupies the anterior-most region of the brain governing executive function, motor planning, and personality, opposite to the posterior occipital region.
  • Temporal: Similar because both are adjacent cerebral lobes situated within the skull; differs because the temporal lobe is situated laterally near the temples and ears (governing auditory processing and memory), whereas the occipital lobe is situated posteriorly.
  • Posterior: Similar because both indicate a position situated toward the back or rear; differs because posterior is a universal, general anatomical directional term applicable to any anatomical structure throughout the body, whereas occipital is specifically anchored to the skull and brain.
  • Nuchal: Similar because both pertain to the back of the head and upper neck region; differs because nuchal specifically designates the nape of the neck and its muscular ligaments (e.g., the nuchal ligament), whereas occipital pertains to the cranial bone and cerebral cortex above it.
  • Basilar: Similar because both relate to the base of the skull; differs because basilar refers broadly to the floor or foundation of the cranium (such as the basilar artery or skull base), whereas occipital identifies a specific structural bone and distinct cerebral lobe.
  • Cephalic: Similar because both describe the head; differs because cephalic is an overarching term for anything related to the head or directed toward the head end of an organism, lacking the exact posterior cranial specificity of occipital.
