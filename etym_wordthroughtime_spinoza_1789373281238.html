- 1650s to 1670s - Baruch Spinoza is excommunicated with the ultimate *cherem* from the Amsterdam Jewish community in 1656; publishes the *Tractatus Theologico-Politicus* anonymously in 1670, sparking universal outrage among Christian and Jewish orthodoxies across Europe.
- Late 17th to Mid-18th Century - Following the posthumous publication of the *Opera Posthuma* (including the *Ethics*) in 1677, Spinoza's works are banned across Europe; "Spinozism" becomes the most terrifying intellectual insult, synonymous with radical atheism, fatalism, and subversion of civic morality.
- Late 18th Century (The Pantheism Controversy) - Radically rehabilitated during the German *Pantheismusstreit* (1785) when F.H. Jacobi revealed that Gotthold Ephraim Lessing had confessed to being a Spinozist; Goethe, Herder, and Friedrich Schlegel embrace Spinoza's pantheism as the spiritual foundation of German Romanticism.
- 19th Century - Canonized by German Idealists; Novalis famously christens him "the God-intoxicated man," while G.W.F. Hegel declares, "To be a philosopher, one must first be a Spinozist"; George Eliot translates the *Ethics* into English.
- 20th Century to Present - Universally venerated as a pioneer of secular constitutional democracy, the historical-critical study of religion, and modern psychology; adopted by Albert Einstein as the cosmological patron saint of modern physics, and championed by French philosophers (Deleuze, Althusser) as the philosopher of pure immanence.
