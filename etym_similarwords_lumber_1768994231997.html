• Timber: Similar because both name wood for building; differs because in Britain timber means roughly hewn or structural wood and in America standing trees or unprocessed logs, while lumber is wood already sawed into boards, planks, and beams for sale.
  • Wood: Similar because both are the material of trees; differs because wood is the general natural or worked material without a commercial sense, while lumber is specifically wood as a trade good — sawed, stacked, and priced.
  • Planks: Similar because both are sawn pieces of wood; differs because planks name the individual long, flat sawn members, while lumber is the commodity collected in yards and shipped.
  • Boards: Similar because both are sawn wood; differs because boards name the individual flat pieces, while lumber is the aggregated commercial material of construction.
  • Logs: Similar because both are wood from trees; differs because logs are the raw trunks before sawing, while lumber is the finished sawn boards cut from them.
  • Beams: Similar because both are structural wood; differs because beams are large horizontal load-bearing members, a category within lumber rather than a synonym for the whole commodity.
  • Clutter: Similar to the historical sense of lumber in meaning disused, heaped-up household goods; differs because clutter is the neutral modern word for mess, while lumber (in this old sense) specifically meant stored, pawned, or cast-off bulky goods — and the word has since transferred to timber.
  • Junk: Similar to the old "pawned clutter" sense of lumber; differs because junk implies worthless cast-offs, while lumber as clutter was bulky and stored, and in America the same mass became the valuable building material.
  • Trundle: Similar to the verb "to lumber along" in slow heavy motion; differs because trundle suggests a smooth rolling with effort, while lumbering is the heavy, clumsy, swaying gait of a large body or cart.
  • Plod: Similar to "to lumber along" in heavy, effortful movement; differs because plod is the slow, laborious tread of feet, while lumbering is the bulkier, more unsteady motion of a whole heavy mass.
