• Sītā: Similar because both refer to the exact same supreme heroine of the Rāmāyaṇa and divine consort of Rāma; differs because Sītā literally means "furrow" (referring to her miraculous discovery in the plowed soil of King Janaka's sacrificial field), highlighting her divine agricultural-telluric birth, whereas Maithilī is a geographic and patronymic epithet celebrating her royal lineage and cultural heritage in the kingdom of Mithilā.
  • Jānakī: Similar because both are revered patronymics of Sītā derived from her royal family; differs because Jānakī specifically designates her relationship as the beloved daughter of King Janaka, whereas Maithilī designates her identity as the princess, native, and representative of the entire geographical and cultural realm of Mithilā.
  • Vaidehī: Similar because both are territorial and dynastic epithets for Sītā drawn from ancient eastern Indian geography; differs because Vaidehī relates to the broader ancient realm or dynasty of Videha (which also implies spiritual transcendence of bodily consciousness, *videha*), whereas Maithilī relates directly to the capital city and cultural heartland of Mithilā.
  • Princess of Mithilā: Similar because both denote Sītā's royal status as the sovereign daughter of Janaka's city; differs because Princess of Mithilā is an English descriptive translated title, whereas Maithilī is an ancient Sanskrit proper noun possessing three millennia of liturgical, literary, linguistic, and emotional resonance.
  • Bhojpuri: Similar because both are closely related Eastern Indo-Aryan (Bihari) languages spoken in adjoining geographical regions of Bihar and Nepal; differs because Bhojpuri originated in the historic Bhojpur region to the west and developed distinct grammatical and phonetic patterns, whereas Maithilī represents the eastern kingdom of Mithilā, possessing an older documented classical literary corpus (Vidyāpati) and its own historic script (*Tirhuta*).
  • Magahi: Similar because both are sister languages belonging to the Eastern Indo-Aryan Bihari language group sharing descent from Magadhi Prakrit; differs because Magahi is spoken south of the Ganges in the ancient Magadha region (Patna, Gaya) and historically possessed less written formal literature, whereas Maithilī developed north of the Ganges with a continuous elite scribal and poetic tradition.
