• Celts: Similar because both refer to the exact same ancient and modern Celtic peoples; differs because Celts is the fully anglicized, standard modern plural, whereas Celtæ is the unassimilated Latin plural preserving classical orthography and the æ ligature.
  • Kelts: Similar because both refer to Celtic peoples; differs because Kelts is a variant nineteenth-century phonetic spelling deliberately coined with a K- to enforce the hard velar pronunciation /kɛlts/ and avoid the soft sibilant /sɛlts/, whereas Celtæ adheres strictly to classical Latin spelling.
  • Gauls: Similar because the Romans often equated the two, and Caesar stated that those who called themselves Celtæ were called Gallī by the Romans; differs because Gauls is derived from Roman and French usage for continental tribes, whereas Celtæ was their own native autonym.
  • Britons: Similar because the ancient Britons were insular members of the broader Celtic family; differs because Britons refers specifically to the Celtic inhabitants of Great Britain, whereas Celtæ historically designated the continental tribes of central and western Europe.
  • Galatians: Similar because both stem from the same ancient Celtic migratory expansion; differs because Galatians specifies the Celtic tribes who migrated into the Balkan peninsula and settled in central Anatolia (modern Turkey) in the third century BCE.
  • Iberians: Similar because both coexisted in pre-Roman Western Europe, occasionally intermarrying to form Celtiberians; differs because the Iberians were an indigenous non-Indo-European populace of the Mediterranean coast, distinct in language and ethnogenesis.
