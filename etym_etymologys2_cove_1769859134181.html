CHAIN 1 — the geographic word (bay, inlet, shelter): PIE: no securely attested root; a root *gup- "hollow, cavity" is sometimes posited for this word family, but it is speculative and cannot be verified — the prehistoric stage of the Germanic word is unknown (see note) → Proto-Germanic *kuban- / *kubōn "hollow, recess, chamber, shelter" (reconstructed; the ancestor also of Old Norse kofi "hut, shed," Middle Dutch cove "hut," German Koben "pigsty, hovel," Middle Low German kove "chamber") → Old English cofa "chamber, den, recess, cave, inner room" (attested from the 8th–9th c. in glosses and prose, e.g. in the sense of an inner chamber of the mind or body in later religious prose) → Middle English cove "a recess, cave, den, or hollow place" (13th–14th c.; the Old English short vowel was retained, and the -a > -e weakening is the standard transition) → Modern English cove "a small, sheltered bay or inlet; a hollow, recess" (the geographic, coastal sense is attested from the 16th c.) → Modern English COVE "a small sheltered bay; a sheltered hollow" ◄ TARGET rootWord CHAIN 2 — the slang word (fellow, man) — a SEPARATE lexeme of the same spelling: Origin unknown: slang cove "fellow, man, chap" (attested 1567 in thieves' cant; possibly from Romany kova / kovo "that man, this person," possibly an independent English coinage — genuinely obscure) → modern dated British slang cove "fellow, chap" ◄ same spelling COVE, but a different word Debate / nuance note: The English form cove "bay, inlet" is certainly the Germanic word (Old English cofa "chamber, den"), and the geographic shift "inner room → sheltered coastal recess" is fully documented from the 16th century. Its ultimate PIE ancestry, however, is not recoverable with confidence; the Germanic *kub- stem has been linked speculatively to a root meaning "hollow" (*gup-), but this is not generally accepted. Separately, slang "cove" (fellow) is a distinct English word of unknown origin, perhaps from Romani; it is not a sense of the geographic word, and the two should not be run together.
