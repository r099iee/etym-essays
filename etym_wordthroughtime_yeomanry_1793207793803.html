- 14th c. — the household man: yoman serves in a lord’s retinue, a cut above the grooms, trusted with the pantry or the bow.
- 15th c. — the archer: yeomen draw the longbow at Agincourt (1415) and enter legend as the king’s yeomanry of the field.
- 16th–17th c. — the freeholder: the yeoman becomes the independent farmer of forty shillings a year, the voter, the ballast of the commonwealth.
- 1794 — the horseman: invasion panic raises the Yeomanry Cavalry, county volunteers mounted against France — and against English radicals.
- 1819 — the sabre’s shadow: the Manchester Yeomanry charge the crowd at Peterloo, and Cobbett’s yeomanry becomes a radical curse-word.
- Today — the title: armoured and signals regiments keep the county yeomanry names, and the Beefeaters keep the Tudor livery — legend on parade.
