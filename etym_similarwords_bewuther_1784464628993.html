• Bewilder: Similar because it denotes mental confusion and is deliberately paired with bewuthered in Tolkien's sentence, both naming a mind thrown into disorder; differs because bewilder descends from the image of being lost in a wilderness (wilder) and is a standard, fully lexicalized verb, whereas bewuther is a rare dialectal-literary formation whose core image is being battered by roaring wind.
  • Fluster: Similar because it means to make someone agitated, nervous, and confused; differs because fluster implies nervous hurry and social agitation without any weather imagery, while bewuther keeps the atmospheric picture of blustering wind.
  • Bluster: Similar because it is a wind-word with the same blustery onomatopoeic feel and a like-sounding onset; differs because bluster denotes the wind blowing boisterously or a person speaking with empty menace, whereas bewuther names the resulting state of confusion in the person who is affected.
  • Addle: Similar because it means to muddle or confuse the mind; differs because addle originally meant 'rotten' (of eggs) and suggests mental decay or feeble-mindedness, while bewuther is energetic, violent, and imagines external buffeting rather than internal putrefaction.
  • Discombobulate: Similar because it is a playful informal word for throwing someone into confusion; differs because discombobulate is a twentieth-century Americanism of comic pseudo-Latin invention, whereas bewuther is a serious regional English wind-image with genuine literary weight.
  • Rattle: Similar because it can mean to fluster or unsettle someone; differs because rattle images shock and sudden noise as the cause, while bewuther images the continuous battering force of wind itself.
  • Nonplus: Similar because it means to perplex so completely that one can do or say nothing further; differs because nonplus is a learned Latin-derived word meaning 'no more, no further,' describing speechless puzzlement, while bewuther is vernacular dialect imagery of being blown about.
  • Unsettle: Similar because it means to disturb a person's composure; differs because unsettle is a mild, neutral verb about a loss of stability, whereas bewuther is far more violent, specific, and literary in its weather-derived intensity.
