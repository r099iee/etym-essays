- “me thought” (“it seemed to me”), Middle English normal impersonal grammar - a plain statement of perception carrying no special colour.
- “methought”, Early Modern English prose and drama - the standard literary past tense for perceptions, dreams and visions; neutral-to-elevated.
- “methought”, 17th–18th c. - fades from speech, kept alive by the Bible, Shakespeare and Milton; readers accept it as biblical/literary diction.
- “methought”, Romantic poetry (c. 1798–1850) - deliberately revived by Wordsworth and others for hushed inward states; the source passage is a flagship use.
- “methought” / “methinks”, modern - conscious archaism, historical pastiche, and comic pseudo-Shakespeare (“methinks the lady doth protest too much”); perceived as quaint, lofty or funny.
