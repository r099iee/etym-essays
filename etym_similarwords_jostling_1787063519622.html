• Shoving: Similar because both involve applying physical force to push someone out of the way; differs because shoving is a deliberate, direct, and often overtly aggressive two-handed push, whereas jostling is multifaceted, irregular, and typically occurs amid crowded movement.
  • Elbowing: Similar because both describe using body parts to force a path through a crowd; differs because elbowing specifically highlights the sharp, calculated use of the elbows to jab and clear space.
  • Bumping: Similar because both describe physical contact between bodies; differs because bumping can be a single, gentle, or accidental impact, whereas jostling implies continuous, repetitive, and turbulent pushing in multiple directions.
  • Crowding: Similar because both occur in high-density environments; differs because crowding describes the static condition of being packed closely together without necessarily engaging in active physical pushing.
  • Hustling: Similar because both involve energetic, aggressive movement in crowds or commerce; differs because hustling emphasizes rapid, hurried speed, aggressive salesmanship, or illicit enterprise rather than direct physical bumping.
  • Shouldering: Similar because both describe forcing one's way past others with upper-body mass; differs because shouldering focuses specifically on using the shoulder as a wedge to push forward.
  • Scrambling: Similar because both involve competitive, disordered physical action; differs because scrambling focuses on frantic, irregular climbing or clawing with hands and feet to reach a goal or secure a prize.
  • Vying: Similar because both describe intense competition for advantage; differs because vying is purely abstract and formal, lacking any inherent physical contact or bodily collision.
  • Nudging: Similar because both involve gentle physical contact; differs because nudging is a small, quiet, deliberate poke with the elbow or hand to draw attention or give a subtle hint.
  • Pressing: Similar because both describe physical mass exerting force in a crowd; differs because pressing conveys steady, heavy forward compression (like a crowd surging against a barrier) rather than chaotic, erratic bouncing.
