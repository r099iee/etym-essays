• Moves: Similar because both represent the exact same lexeme and grammatical third-person singular present indicative; differs because 'moves' is the ubiquitous modern standard English form, whereas 'moveth' is an archaic early modern and liturgical inflection carrying scriptural gravity and historical resonance.
  • Stirreth: Similar because both are archaic '-eth' inflections denoting physical motion or emotional arousal; differs because 'stirreth' specifically emphasizes slight, trembling, or initial movement from a state of rest (or inciting agitation), whereas 'moveth' encompasses continuous locative traversal, migration, or living locomotion.
  • Creepeth: Similar because both appear frequently in biblical cadence to describe animal locomotion upon the earth or sea; differs because 'creepeth' specifically describes low, crawling, stealthy movement close to the ground (reptilian or insectoid), whereas 'moveth' is a universal term covering swimming, walking, flying, or celestial motion.
  • Walketh: Similar because both denote animate locomotion in archaic scripture; differs because 'walketh' specifically refers to bipedal or quadrupedal terrestrial stepping and often carries moral metaphor (e.g., 'he that walketh uprightly'), whereas 'moveth' encompasses all aquatic, aerial, and physical animation.
  • Glideth: Similar because both convey graceful, uninterrupted kinetic progression; differs because 'glideth' emphasizes effortless, frictionless, and smooth passage through water, air, or ice, whereas 'moveth' broadly covers any kinetic relocation, transition, or biological agitation.
  • Proceedeth: Similar because both denote ongoing forward progression; differs because 'proceedeth' is a formal, Latinate term emphasizing formal departure, linear advance from a source, or theological emanation (e.g., 'proceedeth from the Father'), whereas 'moveth' is a fundamental kinetic and biological verb.
  • Liveth: Similar because in the Genesis context ('every living creature that moveth'), movement is the outward, defining sign of life and animation; differs because 'liveth' denotes the state of biological existence or spiritual being itself, whereas 'moveth' denotes the active kinetic exertion and locomotion of that life.
