Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed, prehistoric)
*bhel-
to shine, flash, burn
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, prehistoric)
*blaikaz
white, shining, pale
↓
Old Norse (c. 9th-13th c. CE)
bleikr
pale, whitish, livid
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
bleke
pale, wan, pallid in colour
↓
Early Modern English (1550s)
bleak
bare, windswept, exposed land
↓
English (1719)
bleak
cheerless, dreary, offering no hope
↓
English (present)
bleak
cold, bare, exposed; hopeless

Note: The noun bleak (the silvery
European fish) comes from the same
Norse root via bleikja the pale one;
it is a homonym of the adjective.
