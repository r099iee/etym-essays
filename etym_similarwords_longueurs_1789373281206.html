• tedium: Similar because both denote an unbearable state of dull monotony and boredom; differs because tedium is an abstract, subjective psychological feeling or pervasive atmospheric quality, whereas longueurs specifically identifies concrete, localized structural segments or dragging passages within a narrative, performance, or text.
  • padding: Similar because both describe extraneous material that lengthens an artistic work beyond necessity; differs because padding implies a cynical, artificial addition of low-quality filler deliberately inserted to satisfy a commercial quota, runtime, or word count, whereas longueurs often result from earnest but artistically sluggish or over-elaborate storytelling.
  • dullness: Similar because both describe an absence of dramatic excitement, intellectual interest, or aesthetic vitality; differs because dullness is a comprehensive aesthetic defect that can taint an entire authorial tone, prose style, or performance, whereas longueurs specifically refers to distinct temporal intervals of narrative stalling.
  • prolixity: Similar because both involve excessive length that tries an audience's patience; differs because prolixity refers to a speaker's or writer's stylistic habit of verbosity, wordiness, and circumlocution, whereas longueurs describes the structural pacing defect and dragging duration of the resulting scenes.
  • doldrums: Similar because both characterize stagnant periods where all forward movement ceases; differs because doldrums is an oceanic and economic metaphor describing a general depressive state of inactivity, low spirits, or market stagnation, whereas longueurs is firmly situated in literary, cinematic, and theatrical criticism.
  • protraction: Similar because both involve the excessive drawing out of time; differs because protraction is a neutral, formal noun denoting the mechanical lengthening or delaying of an event (e.g. "the protraction of negotiations"), whereas longueurs carries an intrinsically critical aesthetic judgment of tedious boredom.
  • slowness: Similar because both denote a lack of rapid pace; differs because slowness can be a deliberate, neutral, or highly praised stylistic technique (as in slow cinema or meditative poetry), whereas longueurs almost invariably carries a pejorative critical evaluation of unearned, sluggish fatigue.
