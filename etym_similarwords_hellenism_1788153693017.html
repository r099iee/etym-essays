• Hellenization / Hellenize: similar because both derive from Hellēn and concern the spread or adoption of Greek ways; differs in function: Hellenization is the process (making things Greek), Hellenism the culture or spirit itself.
  • Hellenic civilization: similar because “Hellenic” and “Hellenism” both denote the Greek world; differs because Hellenic designates the classical Greek world proper, while Hellenism extends to the post-classical fusion and to the spiritual ideal.
  • Classicism: similar because both are programmes of admiring antiquity; differs because classicism admires Greco-Roman antiquity as a whole (and later French and English classicisms), while Hellenism is specifically and exclusively Greek.
  • Graecism / Grecism: similar because both mean “Greek idiom or Greek ways” and are used interchangeably in philology; differs in register — Graecism is rarer and more technical, Hellenism broader and more civilizational.
  • Atticism: similar because both name the Greek spirit in language; differs because Atticism is the narrow preference for the Attic dialect of Athens against later Greek, a dialectal and stylistic school.
  • Humanism: similar because Renaissance Hellenism and humanism overlap in the recovery of Greek letters; differs because humanism is the general movement toward classical learning and human dignity, of which Hellenism is the Greek-coloured part.
  • Hebraism (antonym): similar as the paired term in Arnold’s famous antithesis; differs in polarity: Hebraism is the strictness of conscience, Hellenism the spontaneity of consciousness.
