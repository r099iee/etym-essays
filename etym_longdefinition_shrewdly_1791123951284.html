Shrewdly is an adverb meaning in a shrewd manner — keenly, astutely, with sharp practical judgment and perceptive understanding — and, in older and literary use preserved by Dickens, piercingly or severely, of cold, pain, or voice that cuts and makes itself unmistakably felt.

The dominant modern sense is intellectual-practical: to act shrewdly is to judge character and situations accurately, to see through pretence, to anticipate consequences, to bargain wisely. A shrewdly negotiated deal saves money; a shrewdly chosen word defuses conflict; a shrewdly timed investment profits. It praises worldly wisdom rather than book learning or genius. A scholar may be brilliant but unshrewd; a market trader may be uneducated but shrewd.

  • Astutely and discerningly: observed shrewdly, guessed shrewdly, assessed shrewdly. Implies quick, accurate perception of motives and realities. Example: she shrewdly realized he was flattering her to get a loan. Near-synonyms are astutely, sagaciously, but shrewdly adds practical, unsentimental hardness.
  • Cleverly in business and strategy: invested shrewdly, maneuvered shrewdly, marketed shrewdly. The commercial heartland of the word since the 18th century. Implies thrift, calculation, advantage without dishonesty. A shrewd businessman is admired; a sly one is distrusted.
  • Piercingly, keenly, cuttingly (literary/older): the cold spoke out shrewdly in his grating voice; a shrewd wind nipped fingers. Here shrewdly describes sensory intensity that bites and cannot be ignored. Dickens personifies cold as a shrewd (sharp-tongued, severe) speaker whose voice grates. Modern dictionaries still list shrewd frost, shrewd blow as severe, though everyday speech rarely uses it.
  • Artfully or craftily (transitional, now slightly arch): Shakespearean shrewdly contrived plots. Between wicked and wise lies cunning that could be either. A shrewd trick in Elizabethan English might be admirably clever or wickedly deceitful depending on sympathy. That moral ambiguity has mostly bleached out, leaving approval.
  • Slyly humorous: a shrewdly funny observation. Combines perceptiveness with wit: seeing the truth and phrasing it pointedly. A shrewd joke wounds because it is true.

Connotation today is approving but hard-edged. Shrewdly contrasts with wisely (which sounds moral and reflective), intelligently (which sounds academic), and cunningly (which sounds deceitful). Shrewdly admires unsentimental realism: not being fooled, not overpaying, not missing the point. It can imply lack of idealism — a shrewd politician knows voters are selfish — but rarely implies cruelty.

Grammar and collocation: manner adverb modifying verbs (judged, invested, observed, spoke), adjectives in flat constructions are rare. Common collocations: shrewdly calculated, shrewdly observed, shrewdly invested, shrewdly funny, shrewdly perceptive. Comparative more shrewdly, superlative most shrewdly. Register is standard educated prose, journalism, business writing, and literary description; less common in casual chat where smartly, cleverly replace it.

Polysemy bridge: all senses share sharpness. A sharp knife cuts, sharp cold bites, sharp eyes see, sharp mind understands, sharp dealer profits. Old shrewd as painful/severe and new shrewd as perceptive are two edges of the same sharpness metaphor, which is why Dickens can use one word for both weather and wit.
