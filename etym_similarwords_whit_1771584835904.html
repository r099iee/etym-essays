• Jot: Similar because both mean a very small amount and live in negative idioms — "not a jot"; differs because jot (with its sibling iota) refers back to the smallest written stroke of the Greek alphabet, whereas whit is a particle of pure quantity.
  • Iota: Similar because both mean the smallest amount — "not one iota"; differs because iota is the more learned word, drawn from the smallest letter of the Greek alphabet, while whit is plainer and more thoroughly idiomatic.
  • Bit: Similar because both mean a small piece; differs because bit can name any small piece and be used positively ("a bit of bread"), lacking the intensive "absolutely nothing" force of whit in its negative idiom.
  • Scrap: Similar because both mean a small amount; differs because scrap implies a torn or leftover fragment of material, while whit is abstract and survives only in the negative phrase.
  • Shred: Similar because both mean a very small amount — "not a shred of evidence"; differs because shred implies a torn fragment of something that could be torn, such as cloth or paper, while whit is a pure measure of degree.
  • Tittle: Similar because both mean a tiny amount — "not a tittle"; differs because tittle is even rarer and more bookish, drawn from a scribal dot (Latin titulus), and lacks whit's currency in verse.
  • Speck: Similar because both mean a very small amount; differs because a speck is a visible particle of matter (a speck of dust), while whit is abstract degree — one says "not a whit", not "a whit of dust".
  • Particle: Similar because both mean the smallest unit; differs because particle is the general, technical word for a minute piece, while whit is confined to the negated idiom "not a whit".
