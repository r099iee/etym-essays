Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE)
*ham-
‘to enclose, border, hem’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
hemm
‘border, edge of cloth’
↓
Middle English (13th–14th c.)
hem, hemmen
‘hem; to edge with stitching’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
hem in
‘to surround and confine’
↓
Modern English (current)
hem
‘to edge; to shut in’

Note: the throat-clearing hem in hem and haw is a separate imitative formation and not part of this sewing chain, though the two homophones often meet in wordplay.
