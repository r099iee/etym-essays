• Byronic: Similar because it derives from the poet's name and belongs to the same cultural field; differs because it points to the brooding, defiant anti-hero stereotype and the pose, while Byron names the man and his oeuvre.
  • Byronism: Similar because it is built on the same name and refers to his influence; differs because it denotes the movement or cult of attitude (restless individualism, scandal, liberalism), not the person or his texts.
  • Romantic: Similar because it places him in the same literary movement; differs because it is far broader (Wordsworth, Shelley, Keats, Coleridge), while Byron represents one aristocratic, satirical, European-public pole of it.
  • Goethe: Similar because it is the other name in Pater's list of near-absolute artists; differs because Goethe signals German universal genius and breadth while Byron signals English Romantic intensity and celebrity.
  • Shelley: Similar because he was Byron's great English Romantic contemporary and friend; differs because he is a separate poet with a distinct, more idealist and visionary art, not a name for Byron's world.
  • Don Juan: Similar because it is inseparable from Byron's world and fame; differs because it names a single comic epic and its rogue-hero persona, often confused with the poet, not the man himself.
  • Childe Harold: Similar because it is Byron's most famous persona-vehicle; differs because it names a specific poem and pilgrim narrator, a mask of the poet, not the biographical person.
  • Lake Poets / Wordsworth: Similar because they belong to the same Romantic generation; differs because they represent the quieter, rural, reflective branch of Romanticism against which Byron's public, satirical celebrity defined itself.
  • The Byron legend: Similar because it is the celebrity image surrounding the name; differs because it is the myth of the 'mad, bad, and dangerous to know' aristocrat, a constructed reputation rather than the historical man or the literary work.
