• Chuck: Similar because both mean to throw or toss casually without precise aim; differs because chuck is universal in colloquial English and typically denotes an underhand throwing motion, whereas bung has a distinct British/Commonwealth register and can specifically imply shoving or cramming someone/something into an enclosure.
  • Shove: Similar because both describe forcefully propelling something into a confined space; differs because shove involves a continuous pushing contact with hands or body, while bung implies a swift, dismissive toss or rough insertion.
  • Toss: Similar because both denote an effortless, informal throw; differs because toss carries connotations of light, arcing, graceful movement, whereas bung is rougher, more colloquial, and unceremonious.
  • Cram: Similar because both involve forcing an object or person into a tight, overcrowded space; differs because cram emphasizes tight spatial constraints and high pressure, whereas bung focuses on the casual or dismissive action of putting something away.
  • Plug: Similar (in the nominal/cask sealing sense) because both refer to an object inserted to close an opening; differs because plug is a generic term for any stopper or electrical connector, while bung specifically denotes the wide, tapered stopper for casks, barrels, or large pipes.
  • Bribe: Similar (in the financial slang sense) because both refer to illicit payments made to secure an advantage; differs because bribe is the formal, legal, and universally recognized term, whereas bung is underworld/journalistic British slang with a cynical, sordid tone.
  • Stuff: Similar because both mean to pack or insert something casually or roughly; differs because stuff emphasizes filling the interior volume of a container, while bung denotes the quick act of throwing or shoving something in.
