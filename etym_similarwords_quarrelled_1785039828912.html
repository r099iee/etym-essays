• Argued: Similar because both denote opposing viewpoints pressed against each other; differs because arguing implies a reasoned exchange of positions, while quarrelling is driven by grievance and ill feeling rather than reason.
  • Disputed: Similar because both involve contesting a matter; differs because a dispute is a formal contest over facts, rights, or ownership, while a quarrel is personal, emotional, and often petty.
  • Bickered: Similar because both are peevish verbal conflicts; differs because bickering is repeated squabbling over trifles, while quarrelling is a fuller, angrier clash over a genuine grievance.
  • Squabbled: Similar because both are noisy, ill-tempered disputes; differs because squabbling is more childish and cacophonous, while quarrelling carries more heat and seriousness.
  • Wrangled: Similar because both are contentious arguments; differs because wrangling is prolonged and often over technicalities, while a quarrel is shorter, sharper, and more personal.
  • Feuded: Similar because both involve sustained hostility; differs because a feud is a long, bitter vendetta between families or parties, far more serious and long-lived than a single quarrel.
  • Fought: Similar because both involve conflict; differs because fighting crosses into physical violence, while a quarrel stops at hard words and hot tempers.
  • Clashed: Similar because both describe sharp confrontation; differs because clashing suggests a direct collision of forces or personalities, often with less sustained grievance than a quarrel.
  • Rowed (chiefly British): Similar because both are noisy verbal disputes; differs because a row is a loud, public altercation, while a quarrel may be quieter and more personal.
  • Fell out: Similar because both describe the end of good relations through dispute; differs because falling out emphasises the break in a relationship, while quarrelling emphasises the heated exchange itself.
  • Quibbled: Similar because both involve fault-finding; differs because quibbling is nitpicking over minor points, far more trivial and less emotional than quarrelling.
  • Spatted: Similar because both are brief disputes; differs because a spat is a short, minor squabble, often playful, whereas a quarrel can be prolonged and serious.
