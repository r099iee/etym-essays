Element 1 — berth (base; debated)

Old English (verb source) (c. 800-1100)
beran (to bear, carry; bear off)
"to carry; to steer clear"
↓
Early Modern English (nautical noun) (1622-1620s)
berth and byrth and birth
"convenient sea room"
↓
Early Modern English (1660s)
berth (noun) and to berth (verb)
"allotted anchoring ground; to allot"
↓
Early Modern English (1706)
berth
"place on ship to stow and sleep"
↓
Modern English (1778 to present)
berth
"bunk; marina slot; clearance"

Element 2 — -ing

Old English (c. 800-1100)
-ing and -ung
"action, process"
↓
Modern English (1660s to present)
berthing
"mooring; providing bunks; fees"

Debate note: OED labels berth of uncertain origin. Most probable is bear plus abstract -th (as in strength), i.e. bearing-off room, supported by 1627 bear (sense 37) and shared birth spellings as piecewise doublet, not maternal derivation. Alternatives — unattested Old English *beorth (shelter), Icelandic byrdi (ship's side), metathesis of dialect breith (breadth) — lack evidence and are noted as doubtful in OED and Historical Dictionary. No PIE root beyond that of bear (*bher, to carry) can be securely claimed.
