• Gruff: Similar because both denote an unfriendly, brusque exterior; differs because gruff describes a rough, gravelly voice or rough-edged manner that often masks a kind or harmless interior, whereas surly reflects genuine hostility, sullen anger, and active rudeness.
  • Churlish: Similar because both involve vulgar discourtesy and bad manners; differs because churlish emphasizes boorish, ill-bred, or uncivil behavior stemming from a lack of social breeding, while surly highlights sullen moodiness and resentment.
  • Sullen: Similar because both involve quiet, resentful unfriendliness; differs because sullen emphasizes silent, brooding, withdrawn withdrawal and passive resistance, whereas surly frequently breaks out into snappy, aggressive, or dismissive verbal interactions.
  • Morose: Similar because both describe dark, unpleasant temperaments; differs because morose emphasizes deep, gloomy, sorrowful melancholy and despondency, whereas surly is characterized by bad temper, irritation, and hostility.
  • Crabby: Similar because both mean easily irritated and bad-tempered; differs because crabby is an informal, colloquial term for petty, transient annoyance or petulance, lacking the menacing, severe hostility of surly.
  • Cantankerous: Similar because both describe uncooperative, irritable individuals; differs because cantankerous implies an argumentative, stubborn, quarrelsome nature that constantly seeks dispute (often applied to eccentric or elderly figures), whereas surly is cold, sullen, and unapproachable.
  • Curmudgeonly: Similar because both involve crusty, ill-tempered demeanor; differs because curmudgeonly often carries an affectionate or humorous connotation regarding a stubborn old-fashioned grumbler, whereas surly is strictly negative and abrasive.
  • Brusque: Similar because both describe abrupt, ungracious interpersonal behavior; differs because brusque focuses purely on brevity, bluntness, and lack of conversational tact (often due to haste), without necessarily implying underlying malice or sullen anger.
