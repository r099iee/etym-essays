• kapi: Similar because both refer to monkeys in Sanskrit literature; differs because kapi is an elemental, non-compound archaic noun with ancient Vedic roots and broader solar/botanical extensions, whereas plavaga is an explicitly descriptive action-compound meaning "one who moves by leaps and bounds."
  • vānara: Similar because both represent the simian heroes in epic texts; differs because vānara ("forest-man") carries an anthropomorphic and civilized social connotation depicting speaking, cultured beings of Kiṣkindhā, whereas plavaga highlights dynamic physical locomotion (bounding and jumping).
  • plavaṅgama: Similar because both are derived from the root plu and mean "going by leaps"; differs because plavaṅgama uses an inflected accusative compound base (plavam-gama), functioning as a metrically distinct, highly rhythmic variant in Sanskrit verse.
  • śākhāmṛga: Similar because both are vivid descriptive kennings for monkeys in epic poetry; differs because śākhāmṛga emphasizes the arboreal environment ("branch-deer" or "branch-grazer"), whereas plavaga emphasizes the physical action of jumping and leaping regardless of terrain.
  • maṇḍūka: Similar because plavaga can also designate a frog; differs because maṇḍūka is the primary, unambiguous zoological noun for a frog, whereas plavaga is a poetic epithet applied to frogs purely on account of their bounding saltatorial locomotion across wetland habitats.
  • khaga: Similar because both are descriptive Sanskrit compounds ending in the root -ga ("goer/mover"); differs because khaga means "sky-mover" (kha "sky/air" + ga "goer"), specifically designating birds and planets flying through the celestial void, whereas plavaga specifies movement by leaping, floating, or undulating (plava).
  • hari: Similar because both serve as common kennings for monkeys in the Rāmāyaṇa; differs because hari is a color-based descriptor ("tawny, yellow-brown"), whereas plavaga is a kinematic descriptor based on propulsion and velocity.
  • tarumṛga: Similar because both designate arboreal forest creatures; differs because tarumṛga explicitly identifies the creature with tree trunks and branches (taru "tree" + mṛga "wild beast"), whereas plavaga focuses on the kinetic leap from tree to tree or across water.
