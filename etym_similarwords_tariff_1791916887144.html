• duty: Similar because it is a government charge on imported goods, and the two are often interchangeable ("customs duty"); differs because duty names a single charge on specific goods, while tariff can mean the whole published schedule or the national policy regime.
  • customs: Similar because it refers to the tax collected at a frontier; differs because customs properly denotes the agency, the procedure or the duties collectively, never a published rate table, and it is not used of utility prices.
  • levy: Similar because it is an imposed charge; differs because levy is the generic act or amount of raising money by authority (a levy on imports, a capital levy) and carries no implication of a printed schedule.
  • tax: Similar because a tariff is a species of tax; differs because tax is the broad genus covering income, sales and property, while tariff is confined to cross-border goods and regulated rates.
  • excise: Similar because it is an indirect tax on goods; differs because excise is charged on production or sale within a country (alcohol, tobacco, fuel), not at import, and it is never a schedule of utility rates.
  • toll: Similar because it is a charge for passage or use; differs because a toll is paid for using a road, bridge or canal, typically per vehicle or per crossing, and has no protective-trade meaning.
  • impost: Similar because it is a tax on imports; differs because impost is archaic and literary, naming the charge itself rather than the schedule, and is now largely confined to historical writing.
  • rate: Similar because both denote a fixed charge per unit; differs because rate is the unit price within a schedule (the rate per kilowatt-hour), while tariff is the schedule or system that contains the rates.
  • fee: Similar because both are published charges; differs because a fee is paid for a service or privilege (a licence fee, a legal fee) and implies no government trade policy.
  • price list: Similar because both are tabulated charges; differs because price list is the plain, general term, whereas tariff is the institutional term with legal and fiscal overtones.
  • fare: Similar because it is a fixed charge in a schedule; differs because a fare is specifically the price of passenger transport and never a customs duty.
