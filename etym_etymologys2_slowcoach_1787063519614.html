ROOT 1: SLOW
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500 BCE)
*slēw- / *slāw-
"sluggish, dull, weary, listless"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*slāwaz
"sluggish, slow, blunt, inert"
↓
Old English (c. 850 CE)
slāw
"slow, sluggish, inactive, dull of mind"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300 CE)
slou / slowe
"slow in movement, tardy, deliberate"
↓
Modern English (Present)
slow
"taking a long time to move, act, or progress"

ROOT 2: COACH
Hungarian (Settlement of Kocs) (c. 1470 CE)
kocsi (szekér)
"carriage of Kocs (a Hungarian town famous for superior sprung carriages)"
↓
Middle French (c. 1540 CE)
coche
"large passenger carriage, wagon"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550 CE)
coache / coach
"large four-wheeled enclosed passenger carriage"

COMPOUND FORMATION:
British Regency Slang (c. 1820 CE)
slow coach
"slow horse-drawn carriage; (figurative) a slow-moving or dawdling person"
↓
Modern English (Present)
SLOWCOACH
"a person who acts, moves, or thinks slowly; a chronic dawdler"
