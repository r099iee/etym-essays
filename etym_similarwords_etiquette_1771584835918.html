• Manners: Similar because both concern polite behaviour; differs because manners are the actual, habitual behaviours of a person — individual and variable ("his manners are rough") — while etiquette is the codified system that manners are supposed to follow, a social law rather than a personal habit.
  • Protocol: Similar because both are formal codes of conduct; differs because protocol is stricter and more official, governing diplomacy, state ceremonies, and institutions, whereas etiquette covers everyday social politeness and may govern trivial matters.
  • Decorum: Similar because both mean proper, fitting behaviour; differs because decorum is the quality of seemliness and good taste appropriate to an occasion — more aesthetic and ethical, something one shows — while etiquette is the specific codified body of rules one observes.
  • Propriety: Similar because both concern correct behaviour; differs because propriety is conformity to what is proper or morally acceptable, often with a prim or prudish tone, while etiquette is conventional rules that may be perfectly trivial (which fork, who bows first).
  • Politeness: Similar because both mean courteous, considerate behaviour; differs because politeness is the personal quality of courtesy in one's dealings, while etiquette is the external code of rules that defines what counts as polite in a given group.
  • Mores: Similar because both are social customs governing behaviour; differs because mores is the anthropological term for the deeply held customs of an entire society, binding and moral, of which etiquette is only the polite, polished, relatively trivial surface.
  • Custom: Similar because both denote established ways of behaving; differs because custom is any habitual practice of a group, from folkways to law, while etiquette is specifically the code of courteous, polite behaviour.
  • Ceremony: Similar because both relate to formal, rule-governed social behaviour; differs because ceremony is the formal public rite or occasion itself, while etiquette is the code of rules governing such occasions and everyday behaviour alike.
