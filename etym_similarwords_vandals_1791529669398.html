• Barbarians: Similar because it is the broad Roman-era label for peoples outside the empire and carries the same civilizational contrast; differs because it is generic and applies to many groups, whereas Vandals names one specific people.
  • Goths: Similar as a Germanic people of the migration period who also sacked Rome (410); differs because the Goths founded lasting successor kingdoms and were partly rehabilitated, while the Vandals became the archetype of destruction.
  • Huns: Similar as a feared nomadic invader associated with devastation; differs because the Huns were steppe horsemen outside the Germanic family, and their name connotes ferocity rather than vandalism of property.
  • Visigoths: Similar as a Germanic group that entered Italy and took Rome; differs because they are a distinct branch of the Goths and their name is not used for wanton destroyers.
  • Destroyers: Similar because it captures the core modern sense of a vandal; differs because it is neutral and can describe any agent of destruction, including natural forces.
  • Iconoclasts: Similar because they attack valued images and monuments; differs because an iconoclast acts from religious or ideological conviction, whereas a vandal is usually driven by sheer destructiveness.
  • Looters: Similar because they plunder during disorder; differs because looting is motivated by theft, not by the desire to damage for its own sake.
  • Marauders: Similar because they raid and ravage; differs because marauding implies roving military plunder, not the petty defacement of property.
  • Pillagers: Similar because they strip and ruin a place; differs because the focus is on seizing goods, while vandal focuses on wrecking them.
  • Hooligans: Similar in the modern sense of wanton public damage; differs because hooliganism suggests rowdy group misbehaviour, often at sport, rather than deliberate cultural desecration.
  • Philistines: Similar as a term of contempt for those who destroy or ignore culture; differs because it describes indifference to art, not active smashing.
  • Wreckers: Similar because they ruin things; differs because it is informal and often means someone who sabotages a plan or vehicle, not a heritage criminal.
