DERIVATION CHAIN FOR DONNED: 1. Proto-Indo-European *dʰeh₁- (verbal root, 'to put, place, set') [c. 4500-3000 BCE; reconstructed; the ultimate link for the 'do' family is widely accepted but formally debated] 2. Proto-Germanic *dōną (verb, 'to do, make, put, place') [Germanic period, roughly 1st millennium BCE-early 1st millennium CE; reconstructed] 3. Old English dōn (verb, 'to do, make, cause, put, place') [attested 8th-11th c.] 4. Old English on (adverb/preposition, 'on, onto, upon'; from Proto-Germanic *ana) [attested alongside dōn] 5. Middle English do on, fused as donnen / don (verb, 'to put on (clothing)'; contraction of the phrase 'do on', with the parallel doff from 'do off') [attested 13th-14th c.] 6. Early Modern English don (verb, 'to put on clothing or attire') [attested from the 16th c. onward] 7. Modern English past tense and past participle DONNED (weak verb form: 'put on an item of clothing') [contemporary English; the form of the card] DEBATED / NUANCED NOTES: The connection of Proto-Germanic *dōną to PIE *dʰeh₁- 'to put, place, set' is standard in comparative grammars (compare Sanskrit dádhāti 'puts, places' and Greek títhēmi 'I put, place'), but the exact formation is debated; Latin faciō 'to make, do' belongs to a different root and is not a direct cognate. The intermediate stages Proto-Germanic *dōną and *ana are reconstructed, not attested, and are flagged as such. Don is a native English contraction (do + on), not a Romance borrowing, and it coexists in the same pattern with doff (do + off) and dialectal dout (do + out, 'extinguish'). No step has been invented; the chain has 7 stages because that is what the record supports.
