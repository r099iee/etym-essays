• Stones: Similar because slabs are made of stone and both name hard natural material; differs because a stone is any piece of rock whatever its shape, while a slab is specifically large, thick, and flat, with broad parallel faces.
  • Boulders: Similar because both are large masses of rock; differs because a boulder is rounded and detached, worn smooth by water or weather, while a slab is flat and plate-like, broken rather than worn.
  • Flags / Flagstones: Similar because both are flat stones; differs because a flagstone is a flat stone of regular size deliberately cut for paving, thinner and neater, while a slab is heavier, more massive, and often raw and unworked.
  • Blocks: Similar because both are solid pieces of material; differs because a block is roughly square in section, thick in all dimensions, while a slab is thin relative to its breadth and width.
  • Plates: Similar because both are flat pieces of material; differs because a plate is thin, manufactured, and usually of metal, while a slab is thick, heavy, and often unworked stone, wood, or concrete.
  • Tiles: Similar because both are flat pieces used for surfaces; differs because a tile is much thinner, more regular, and made for covering, while a slab is a massive structural or monumental piece.
  • Panels: Similar because both are flat pieces; differs because a panel is a thin manufactured sheet fitted into a frame, while a slab is a thick, heavy, raw mass.
  • Chunks: Similar because both denote solid pieces; differs because a chunk is irregular in shape and of any size, while a slab is defined by its flatness and broad parallel faces.
