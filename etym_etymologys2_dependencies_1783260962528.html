Stage 1 — PIE *(s)pend- "to pull, stretch, draw out; to spin" (with s-mobile, so forms appear with and without the initial s-. The concrete world of the root is the spinner's thread, the stretched cord, the snare pulled tight. Cognates: Germanic *spinnan giving English spin, span, spider "the spinner"; Greek pénomai "to toil", pónos "labor"; Lithuanian spę̃sti "to set a snare". DEBATED: the exact PIE reconstruction and the s-mobile alternation are matters of scholarly nuance, but the family is securely attested across the branches). Stage 2 — Proto-Italic *pend- "to stretch, hang, weigh" — the root of the hanging scale, whose pan hangs and stretches downward as it is loaded. Stage 3 — Latin twin verbs: pendēre "to hang, be suspended" (intransitive) and pendere "to weigh, pay" (transitive, because payment was weighed out on hanging scales). Stage 4 — Latin dēpendēre "to hang down from; to be dependent on, to rest upon" — dē- "down from" + pendēre "to hang" (attested in classical Latin; the physical sense of hanging from shades into logical and moral dependence). Stage 5 — Latin dēpendēns, dēpendentis "hanging down from; dependent" (present participle) → Late/Medieval Latin dēpendentia "the state of hanging down from; dependence" (abstract noun in -ia). Stage 6 — Old French dependance "dependence, subjection" (12th century; Modern French dépendance). Stage 7 — Middle English dependence "state of being dependent, subjection" (15th century); dependency "that which depends; a dependent territory" (16th century). Stage 8 — Modern English dependencies, plural territorial sense "countries or provinces subject to the control of another" (18th century, the vocabulary of empire) → FINAL NODE: DEPENDENCIES.
