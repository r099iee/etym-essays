Chain for root word 'fluttering': - PIE *plew- (also *pleu-) 'to float, to flow, to wash' (reconstructed; before c. 3000 BCE; root of 'float,' 'flow' and 'fly'). - Proto-Germanic *fleutaną 'to float, to stream, to flow' (reconstructed; 1st millennium BCE; gives Old English flēotan 'to float,' Old Norse fljóta). - Proto-Germanic *flutōną 'to float about, to drift' (reconstructed; an iterative formation on the base *flut- of *fleutaną, with the weak -ōn- verb suffix marking repeated, continuing action). - Old English flotian 'to float' (attested; Class II weak verb, from *flutōną) and its frequentative floterian 'to float to and fro, to drift about, to flutter' (attested; formed with the Germanic frequentative suffix -erian, giving repeated, unsteady motion; the direct ancestor of 'flutter'); 8th-10th c. CE. - Middle English floteren / fluteren 'to flutter, to move tremblingly and unsteadily' (attested; used of birds, banners, leaves); 14th c. CE. - Modern English flutter 'to move with quick, light, irregular motions' (spelling and sense fixed in the early modern period); 16th-17th c. CE. - Modern English fluttering, the present participle and gerund formed from flutter with -ing; attested from the early modern period onward. - Final node, bold: fluttering. Note on the root: PIE *plew- is well attested (Sanskrit plávate 'floats, swims,' Greek pleō 'I sail,' Latin pluere 'to rain'), so the earliest stage is secure; the Germanic chain from *fleutaną through *flutōną to floterian is regular and uncontroversial.
