- [Old English-Medieval clan sense] - Kin is the whole kindred - the blood clan bound by loyalty, feud and inheritance; 'kith and kin' names one's known community of friends and family.
- [16th-19th century legal pinning] - The word narrows into fixed phrases: 'next of kin' fixes it in law and inheritance, while 'kinship' becomes the anthropologist's term for systems of blood and marriage.
- [20th-century revival] - Both formal and warm: retained in legal usage, resurrected in genealogy and hobby communities, and used of species or spirits felt to be kindred.
- [Contemporary register] - Archaic-tinged and endearing; kin signals belonging by blood or spirit, warmer and older than the bureaucratic 'relatives'.
