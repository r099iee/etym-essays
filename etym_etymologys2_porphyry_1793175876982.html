Ancient Greek (8th c. BCE, Homer)
porphýra (πορφύρα)
‘purple-fish, murex, purple dye’
↓
Ancient Greek (adjective) (5th c. BCE)
porphýreos
‘purple, dark-red’
↓
Latin (borrowed) (1st c. CE, Pliny)
porphyritēs
‘porphyry stone, purple marble’
↓
Old French (13th c.)
porfire
‘porphyry stone’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
porfurie
‘precious purple stone’
↓
Modern English (current)
porphyry
‘purple imperial rock’

Note: Greek porphýra itself has no agreed deeper etymology; Beekes treats it as likely Pre-Greek substrate vocabulary, so the chain properly begins at the Greek snail rather than guessing a PIE root.
