NOTE — DEBATED / NUANCED STAGES: The relation of Latin pendere "to hang, weigh" to the PIE root is secure in general outline, but the exact root shape is debated: de Vaan reconstructs *spend- "to pull, stretch" (with the -nd- nasal infix), while older handbooks (Pokorny) cite *(s)pen- "to draw, spin, stretch". Both are legitimate reconstructions of the same family, which also includes Latin pensum and Greek pénomai "to labour". The intermediate noun pondus "weight" is a zero-grade formation from the same verbal root, and ponderare "to weigh" is its denominative verb; the metaphor of mental weighing was already fully developed in Classical Latin (Cicero uses ponderare of weighing arguments). The Old French spelling varies in the manuscripts (pondérer, ponderer); no stages are guessed — only attested or securely reconstructed forms appear above.
