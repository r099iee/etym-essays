• pebbles: Similar because both are small smooth stones on a shore; differs because pebbles are individual loose stones (countable), while shingles is a collective mass covering a beach.
  • gravel: Similar because both are loose stones in quantity; differs because gravel is smaller, angular or mixed, and usually applied as path-laying material, while shingles are larger water-worn stones deposited naturally.
  • stones: Similar because both are rock fragments; differs because stones is the generic term for any size, while shingles specifically means rounded, water-worn stones.
  • cobbles / cobblestones: Similar because both are rounded stones; differs because cobbles are large, fist-sized stones used for paving, whereas shingles are smaller and natural.
  • shingle (roof tiles): Similar because it shares the spelling; differs because it is a completely different word meaning thin overlapping roof tiles, from Latin scindere "to split".
  • shingles (the disease): Similar because identical in form; differs because it is herpes zoster, from Latin cingulum "girdle", a third separate etymology.
  • slates: Similar because both can be roof coverings; differs because slates are quarried rock slabs, not split wood, and have no pebble sense.
  • scree: Similar because both are loose stones; differs because scree is angular rock debris on a mountain slope, not rounded water-worn shingle.
  • sediment / alluvium: Similar because both are material deposited by water; differs because they are geological terms for fine material, not coarse rounded pebbles.
