• Conqueror: Similar because both denote one who overcomes and subjugates; differs because conqueror is the general English word for any victor by force, whereas conquistador is the specifically Spanish, historically American term for 16th-century conquerors of the New World.
  • Subjugator: Similar because both describe one who brings peoples under control; differs because subjugator emphasizes the ongoing oppression of a subject population, while conquistador emphasizes the expeditionary act of conquest and its imperial framing.
  • Explorer: Similar because many conquistadors were also explorers who charted unknown lands; differs because explorer stresses discovery, mapping, and scientific curiosity, whereas conquistador stresses military invasion and empire-building.
  • Colonizer: Similar because both are agents of imperial expansion who settle foreign land; differs because colonizer emphasizes the founding of settlements and the reshaping of a territory, while conquistador emphasizes the armed taking of it first.
  • Invader: Similar because both enter foreign lands by force; differs because invader is neutral about motive and usually describes military campaigns, whereas conquistador carries the specific Spanish imperial, 16th-century context.
  • Adventurer: Similar because conquistadors were private, often self-financed adventurers; differs because adventurer is general and usually non-military, whereas conquistador implies a royal licence and an armed expedition of conquest.
  • Empire-builder: Similar because both extend imperial rule over new territory; differs because empire-builder describes a long-range state-builder of any nation or era, while conquistador is fixed to the Spanish conquest of the Americas.
  • Soldier of fortune: Similar because many conquistadors fought for pay and plunder; differs because soldier of fortune is a mercenary outside any imperial project, whereas conquistador fought under the crown of Castile and the banner of Christianity.
  • Conquistadores (plural): Similar as the same word; differs only in number and in showing the Spanish plural retained in English alongside the anglicized conquistadors.
