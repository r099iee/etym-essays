- 1760 — the novella “The Life and Adventures of a Cat” makes Tom the Cat a beloved character; the name begins to stick to male cats generally.
- 1809 — tomcat is first recorded as the plain word for a male cat; the compound is colloquial, rural, and gender-specific.
- 19th c. — tomcat is standard country vocabulary for the unneutered male, with his spraying, yowling, and fighting noted in natural-history writing.
- 20th c. — “to tomcat around” emerges in slang for a man pursuing sexual conquests; the F-14 Tomcat (1970) borrows the cat's aggressive power for a fighter plane.
- 21st c. — tom-cat remains the everyday word for the entire male; in cat rescue and veterinary culture it is used precisely for the unneutered male and its behaviours, and the literary image of the gigantic tom-cat still thrills.
