- Classical Greek Antiquity: Mythic & Aesthetic Deformity - Originated in Ancient Greece as ἄμορφος (ámorphos), describing that which was misshapen, deformed, unsightly, unformed, or monstrously lacking harmonious proportions.
- Mid-18th century: Natural Philosophy & Mineralogical Classification - Formally adopted into Enlightenment scientific Latin and English to categorize rocks, uncrystallized earths, and non-crystalline mineral masses that failed to exhibit geometric faces or cleavage planes.
- 19th century: Chemistry, Histology, & Solid-State Physics - Standardized across Victorian science to define non-crystalline solid states (such as vitreous glass, waxes, and resins) and biological 'amorphous ground substance' (the clear, structureless extracellular matrix supporting connective tissues).
- Early to Mid-20th century: Gestalt Psychology & Epistemological Critique - Employed prominently in psychological treatises (such as the works of Wertheimer, Köhler, Koffka, and Arnheim) to attack associationist and behaviorist descriptions of raw sensory input as an 'amorphous agglomeration' of isolated stimuli.
- Late 20th century to Present: Advanced Material Science, Sociological Analysis, & Figurative Discourse - Ubiquitous in advanced materials (amorphous metals, amorphous silicon solar panels, drug formulations) and widely deployed in sociopolitical commentary to describe diffuse, decentralized, or ideologically fluid modern organizations.
