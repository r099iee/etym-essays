FLOPPED is an echoic (sound-symbolic) English formation with no reconstructable PIE root; its history is documented only in English. Linear chain: 1. English expressive verb flop "to flap (the wings)" — first attested c. 1600; a sound-symbolic variant within the Germanic expressive cluster flap / flip / flop. 2. English verb flop "to move heavily and awkwardly" — 17th-century extension of the flapping sense to whole bodies. 3. English verb flop "to fall or drop heavily and clumsily" — attested from 1836; the modern physical core sense. 4. English noun flop "a failure" — attested from the 1890s in American theater slang ("the play was a flop"); the verb sense "to fail" is recorded from 1919. 5. English inflected FLOPPED — past tense and past participle of flop; "fell or sat down heavily and limply." Note on the debated stage: the ultimate origin is opaque. Scholars group flop with a family of expressive Germanic monosyllables (flap, flip, flop, and continental forms such as Dutch flappen "to flap"), in which the vowel alternation iconically codes differences of motion. No PIE etymon is reconstructable, so the derivation must stop at the English expressive formation rather than continue into an invented Indo-European root.
