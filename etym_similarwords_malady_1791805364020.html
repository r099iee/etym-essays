• illness: The closest everyday synonym, naming the state of being unwell; it differs by being broader and plainer, covering the whole experience of being sick, whereas malady names the condition itself and belongs to a higher register.
  • disease: Similar in denoting a morbid bodily condition; it differs by being clinical and aetiological, implying a recognised pathological entity with a cause and course, while malady is vaguer, older and more literary.
  • sickness: Similar as a general term for ill health; it differs in being the plainest word of the group and in also meaning nausea, a sense malady never has.
  • ailment: Similar in referring to a disorder of the body; it differs by normally implying something minor and temporary, whereas malady suggests persistence, gravity and suffering.
  • infirmity: Similar in describing a lasting bodily weakness; it differs by emphasising frailty and disability, especially of old age, rather than a discrete named illness.
  • disorder: Similar as a neutral medical term; it differs by being clinical and often systemic or mental, with none of the archaic dignity or emotional colour that malady carries.
  • complaint: Similar as a euphemistic medical word for an illness; it differs by being the polite term for a specific symptom or condition and by also meaning a formal grievance.
  • affliction: Similar in denoting suffering from illness; it differs by foregrounding pain and often divine or fateful visitation, making it more emotional and homiletic than malady.
  • distemper: Similar as an archaic word for a disorder, especially a humoral imbalance or an animal disease; it differs by being obsolete in human medicine and by its specific veterinary use.
  • indisposition: Similar as a formal word for ill health; it differs by being a deliberate understatement for slight, passing illness, the opposite of the gravity malady implies.
  • infection: Similar in naming a cause of illness; it differs by asserting a pathogenic and usually transmissible origin, a claim malady deliberately avoids.
