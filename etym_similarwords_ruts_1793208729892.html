• Grooves: Similar because both are long narrow channels cut or worn into a surface; differs because grooves is the general word (records, screws, sliding doors), while ruts are specifically wheel-worn tracks in the ground, with the habit metaphor attached.
  • Furrows: Similar because both are trench-like lines scored into earth; differs because furrows are ploughed deliberately for seed (or wrinkled brows by analogy), while ruts are worn accidentally by traffic.
  • Tracks: Similar because both are marks left by passage along the ground; differs because tracks is the broad word for any trail or trace (footprints, railways), while ruts are specifically sunken grooves that grip the wheel.
  • Holloways: Similar because both are roads sunk deep by centuries of wheels and feet; differs because a holloway is the whole sunken road itself, while ruts are the individual wheel-grooves within it.
  • Potholes: Similar because both are road-surface defects born of traffic and weather; differs because potholes are round pits that jolt, while ruts are linear grooves that steer.
  • Corrugations: Similar because both are washboard road deformations from repeated wheels; differs because corrugations ripple across the direction of travel, while ruts run with it.
  • Routine: Similar because the daily rut IS a routine gone stale; differs because routine itself is neutral (surgeons need routines), while rut adds the reproach of grooves too deep to leave.
  • Dead end: Similar because both describe a predicament with no apparent way forward; differs because a dead end blocks progress absolutely, while a rut permits motion — dismally circular motion.
  • Treadmill: Similar because both image exhausting repetitive labour without advance; differs because the treadmill stresses futile effort (running to stay still), while the rut stresses unthinking repetition (rolling because the groove guides).
