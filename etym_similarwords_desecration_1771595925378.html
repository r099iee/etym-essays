• Profanation: Similar because it is the closest sister, meaning the making of holy things unholy; differs because profanation (from Latin profanus, "before/outside the temple") stresses bringing what was sacred into common, secular use — the resulting state — while desecration stresses the violent act of defiling; desecration is the active crime, profanation the condition it leaves behind.
  • Sacrilege: Similar because it names the violation of sacred things; differs because sacrilege is narrower and more technical — the specific offence of violating or stealing consecrated objects (Latin sacrilegium, "temple theft") — whereas desecration is the broader act of making unholy whatever is held sacred.
  • Defilement: Similar because it means the making of something impure; differs because defilement is broader and can touch rivers, bodies, or reputations without any sacredness, whereas desecration presupposes something consecrated that is being unhallowed.
  • Violation: Similar because it means the wrongful, contemptuous treatment of something; differs because violation is a general word for breaking or infringing anything, lacking the sacred object that desecration requires.
  • Blasphemy: Similar because it is an offence against the sacred; differs because blasphemy is specifically the speaking or acting with irreverence toward God or holy things — an offence in words — whereas desecration is a physical act of defiling a consecrated object or place.
  • Irreverence: Similar because it denotes a lack of respect for the sacred; differs because irreverence is a mild attitude of disrespect — often merely humorous — whereas desecration is a violent, grave act.
  • Unhallowing: Similar because it is the direct, literal counterpart of desecration, meaning the removal of holiness; differs because unhallowing is rarer and more technical, belonging to theological writing, while desecration is the common word for the same act.
  • Pollution: Similar because it means the making of something unclean; differs because pollution is physical or environmental defilement (air, water, soil) and lacks the specifically sacred object of desecration.
  • Contamination: Similar because it means the tainting of something pure; differs because contamination is a modern, technical, and wholly physical word (germs, radiation), while desecration names a moral-religious violation.
  • Debasement: Similar because it means the lowering of something from a high standing; differs because debasement is general — of currency, taste, or character — and does not require a sacred object, whereas desecration does.
  • Despoiling: Similar because it means the stripping or plundering of something; differs because despoiling stresses the taking away of valuables, while desecration stresses the violation of holiness — a desecration may touch nothing of value at all.
