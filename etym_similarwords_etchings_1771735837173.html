• Engravings: Similar because both are prints from a metal plate; differs because an engraving is cut with a sharp steel burin, producing a clean, flowing, calligraphic line, whereas an etching is bitten chemically by acid, freeing the artist to draw with a needle as spontaneously as a pen.
  • Drypoints: Similar because both are scratched plate prints; differs because drypoint scratches the plate directly with a needle, raising a velvety burr of metal, while an etching is bitten by acid for a wiry, sketch-like line.
  • Prints: Similar because both are images pulled from a block or plate; differs because print is the broadest term for any impression from wood, metal, or stone, while an etching is one specific acid-bitten technique within that family.
  • Aquatints: Similar because both are acid-bitten intaglio techniques; differs because aquatint produces tonal washes of light and shade through a resin ground, while etching produces the line itself.
  • Lithographs: Similar because both are printed images; differs because a lithograph is drawn on flat stone with grease and printed from a planographic surface, while an etching is bitten into metal in intaglio.
  • Woodcuts: Similar because both are prints; differs because a woodcut is cut in relief on a block of wood and printed from the raised surface, while an etching prints from lines bitten into the plate.
  • Drawings: Similar because both are graphic images by an artist’s hand; differs because a drawing is a unique work on paper, while an etching is a multiple — many impressions pulled from one plate.
  • Illustrations: Similar because both supply images for a text; differs because an illustration may be any pictorial accompaniment, while an etching is specifically an acid-bitten intaglio print.
  • Mezzotints: Similar because both are intaglio prints with rich tonal effects; differs because a mezzotint is made by roughening the whole plate and scraping back the lights, while an etching draws its lines with acid.
  • Plates: Similar because both can name the printed image; differs because a plate is strictly the metal printing surface itself (or one printed impression), while an etching names both the technique and its product.
