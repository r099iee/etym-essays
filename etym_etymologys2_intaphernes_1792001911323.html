PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3000 BCE)
*weyd- + *bʰer- / *spalg-
"*weyd- 'to see, discover, find'; *bʰer- 'to carry, shine' (radiance, glory)"
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (c. 2000 BCE)
*windat- + *pʰar-nas-
"he who finds / acquires + divine royal splendor, glory"
↓
Old Persian (c. 522 BCE)
Vindafarnā (𐎻𐎡𐎭𐎳𐎼𐎴𐎠)
"He who finds glory / He who obtains divine royal luster"
[Attested on DB IV.83 at the Behistun Inscription; compound of vinda- + farnah-]
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 5th c. BCE)
Ἰνταφρένης (Intaphrénēs)
"Greek phonetic transcription of Old Persian Vindafarnā"
[Initial V- rendered as vowel iota / breath; nasalized cluster -nt-; metathesis -ren-]
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. CE)
Intaphernes
"Latinized personal name of the Persian noble"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th c.)
Intaphernes
"English historical form adopted in classical translations of Herodotus"
↓
Modern English (present)
Intaphernes
"the Persian conspirator and subject of Herodotus's famous kinship account"
