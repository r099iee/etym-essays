• Stemmy: Similar because both describe plants dominated by stems rather than leaves or flowers; differs because stemmy is the slightly more technical term, while stalky is the everyday garden and kitchen word.
  • Leggy: Similar because both describe plants drawn up into long bare stems, especially seedlings; differs because leggy stresses stretched legginess from poor light, while stalky stresses thickness and coarseness of stem.
  • Reedy: Similar because both evoke tall thin upright plant growth; differs because reedy suggests slender elegance or fragility, while stalky suggests stiffness and coarse substance.
  • Woody: Similar because both describe plant tissue gone hard and fibrous; differs because woody means lignified like a shrub or tree, while stalky stays herbaceous, all coarse green stem.
  • Fibrous: Similar because both name stringy toughness in vegetables and stems; differs because fibrous is about texture and eating quality, while stalky is about visible structure and proportion.
  • Weedy: Similar because both describe rank unwanted growth of the kind in the source sentence; differs because weedy names the plant's low worth, while stalky names its physical build.
  • Rank: Similar because both describe overgrown coarse vegetation; differs because rank adds luxuriance and sometimes smell, while stalky is drier and purely structural.
  • Spindly: Similar because both picture thin elongated stems; differs because spindly stresses weakness and fragility, while stalky stresses stiffness and coarse strength.
  • Coarse: Similar because stalky growth is the very type of coarse vegetation; differs because coarse is general roughness of texture or manners, while stalky pins it to stems.
