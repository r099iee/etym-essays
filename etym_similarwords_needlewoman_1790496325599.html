• seamstress: Similar because it is the closest modern synonym for a woman sewing for pay; differs because seamstress stresses paid plain sewing, while needlewoman can also praise unpaid schoolgirl skill.
  • dressmaker: Similar because she makes clothes to order; differs because dressmaker implies designing and fitting gowns, while needlewoman may only hem and seam.
  • sewer (sewing sense): Similar because it is the gender-neutral term for one who sews; differs because sewer is technical and ambiguous in print with sewer (drain), while needlewoman is unambiguously human and historical.
  • tailor / tailoress: Similar because they cut and make outer garments; differs because tailor (even tailoress) implies structured menswear and cutting skill, while needlewoman implies needlework and finishing.
  • embroiderer / embroideress: Similar because she works decorative needle art; differs because embroiderer specifies ornament, while needlewoman covers plain and fancy alike.
  • milliner: Similar because she is a female needle-trade worker; differs because milliner makes hats and bonnets specifically, while needlewoman sews garments and linen generally.
  • modiste: Similar because she is a fashionable dressmaker; differs because modiste is French-borrowed and high-fashion, while needlewoman is plain English and often humble.
  • lace-maker: Similar because she produces needle or bobbin lace; differs because lace-maker is a narrow specialism, while needlewoman is the general trade.
  • quilter: Similar because she joins fabric with needlework; differs because quilter makes bedcovers and coverlets, while needlewoman makes or mends wearables and household linen.
  • mender / darner: Similar because they repair with the needle; differs because mending is only repair, while a needlewoman both makes new and repairs.
