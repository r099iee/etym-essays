Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*peyk- / *peig-
"to cut, incise, prick, mark"
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 6th c. CE)
*piccāre
"to prick, pierce, strike"
↓
Old Occitan / Provençal (c. 12th c. CE)
picar
"to strike, tap, coin money"
↓
Provençal / Occitan (16th–17th c. CE)
picalhon / picaioun
"small copper coin of Piedmont"
↓
Louisiana French (18th c. CE)
picaillon / picayune
"Spanish half-real coin (6¼ cents)"
↓
American English (c. 1804–1837)
picayune
"small coin; petty, trifling"

Note: Historical linguists debate whether Occitan picalhon derives from the verb picar ("to strike, mint", referring to the striking of dies or the clinking sound of coins) or from an expressive/onomatopoeic Mediterranean stem *pikk- indicating a small point or sharp puncture. Both hypotheses lead directly to the Provençal coin name that traveled to Louisiana.
