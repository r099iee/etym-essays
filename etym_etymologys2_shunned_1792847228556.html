Old English (before 12th c.)
scunian
‘to shrink from, fear, abhor’
‘also spelled schenian, schonian’
↓
Early Middle English (c. 1200)
shunien, schonen
‘to avoid, eschew, fear’
↓
Late Middle English (14th–15th c.)
shonen, shonnen
‘to keep clear of, escape’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
shun
‘to avoid deliberately’
↓
Modern English (regular past form)
shunned
‘deliberately avoided, cut off’

Note: the deeper origin of Old English scunian is uncertain and debated. It has no secure cognates elsewhere in Germanic, and older guesses linking it with shy or with roots meaning to cover remain unproven, so responsible handbooks derive modern shun from the attested Old English verb and mark everything before it as unknown.
