• Steps: Similar because both denote the biomechanical act of placing the foot down upon a ground surface; differs because stepping is a neutral, basic locomotive action, whereas treading carries connotations of weight, deliberate pressure, continuous downward contact, or crushing force.
  • Tramples: Similar because both describe pressing down upon something with the feet; differs because trampling implies violent, destructive, chaotic, and heavy stamping that flattens, crushes, or ruins the object beneath, whereas treading can be measured, purposeful, rhythmic, stealthy, or gentle.
  • Paces: Similar because both refer to walking with deliberate cadence; differs because pacing emphasizes rhythmic, repetitive walking back and forth across an enclosed space (frequently driven by anxiety or intense contemplation), whereas treading emphasizes the downward physical contact and weight placed upon the ground.
  • Encroaches: Similar because the idiom "treads on toes" denotes infringing upon personal boundaries; differs because encroaching is an abstract legal or political term meaning gradual, stealthy advancement onto another's territory or rights, whereas treading on toes evokes visceral, physical contact and blunt disregard.
  • Strides: Similar because both describe walking movements; differs because striding denotes confident, long, energetic steps projecting authority, haste, or pride, whereas treading focuses on the pressure applied to the surface or obstacle beneath the foot.
  • Plods: Similar because both denote heavy-footed walking; differs because plodding emphasizes slow, weary, laborious, and exhausted trudging through mud or heavy conditions, whereas treading can be agile, cautious ("treading on eggshells"), or aggressive.
