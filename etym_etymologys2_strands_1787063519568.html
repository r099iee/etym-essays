Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*stren- / *ster-
"stiff, stretched tight, tight cord, string"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*stran- / *strind-
"strip, line, tight cord, edge"
↓
Middle Dutch / Middle Low German / Old French (c. 13th-14th c.)
strānde / estran
"twisted cord, rope-component"
↓
Late Middle English (c. 1490)
strande
"one of the twisted yarn-lengths forming a rope"
↓
Modern English (plural)
strands
"individual fibers/threads twisted into a rope; thematic elements (plural)"
Note: Distinct origin from Old English *strand* (seashore/beach).
