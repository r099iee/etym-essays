WAYLAID is the past form of the compound verb waylay, built from two English roots: way (Proto-Germanic *wegaz) and lay (Proto-Germanic *lagjanan). Each root has its own chain, and the compound has its own chain. CHAIN 1 — 'way': - PIE *weǵʰ- 'to carry, transport, travel' (reconstructed; c. 4500-3500 BCE) — source also of Latin vehere 'to carry', Sanskrit váhati 'he conveys' → Proto-Germanic *wegaz 'way, road, path' (reconstructed; c. 500 BCE-500 CE) → Old English weg 'way, road, path' (c. 700-1100) → Middle English wey / way 'way, road' (c. 1200-1500) → Modern English way (1500-present) CHAIN 2 — 'lay': - PIE *legh- 'to lie down, recline' (reconstructed; c. 4500-3500 BCE) — source also of Greek lékhos 'bed', Latin lectus 'bed' → Proto-Germanic *ligjanan 'to lie' (intransitive) (reconstructed; c. 500 BCE-500 CE) → Proto-Germanic *lagjanan 'to lay, cause to lie' (causative of *ligjanan) (reconstructed; c. 500 BCE-500 CE) → Old English lecgan 'to lay, place, put' (c. 700-1100); past tense legde → Middle English leien / leyen 'to lay' (c. 1200-1500); past tense leide → Modern English lay (1500-present); past tense laid (from Middle English leide) CHAIN 3 — the compound 'WAYLAY' / past 'WAYLAID': - Middle Dutch wegelagen / Middle Low German wegelagen 'to lie in wait beside a road' (weg 'way' + lagen 'to lie in wait'; attested c. 1250-1400) → Middle English wei-leyn / wayleyen 'to lie in wait for along the way' (attested c. 1400) — a calque of the continental compound → Modern English waylay (1500-present) → past tense and past participle waylaid, formed on the pattern of lay → laid (legde → leide → laid) → WAYLAID NOTE ON DEBATE: English waylay may be a native calque of the Middle Dutch / Middle Low German wegelagen or may be an independent parallel formation; the Middle English form wei-leyn is attested by c. 1400. The past tense waylaid follows the strong-irregular pattern of lay → laid (Old English legde → Middle English leide → Modern English laid). The PIE roots for both 'way' and 'lay' are well established; the compound is a medieval European coinage rather than an inherited word.
