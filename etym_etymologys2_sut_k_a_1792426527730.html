"exceedingly sharp; name of the revered forest sage"

Note on phonology and morphology: In Sanskrit grammar (Pāṇini 3.2.167), tīkṣṇa is derived from the verbal root tij- (to be sharp or keen) with the unāḍi suffix -kṣṇa (or root-final palatal j alternating with the velar k before the sibilant cluster -sna, yielding -kṣṇa with cerebralization under the ruki rule). When combined with the intensifier prefix su- ("thoroughly, beautifully, exceedingly"), it forms the tatpuruṣa/karmadhāraya compound sutīkṣṇa, applied both as an adjective ("extraordinarily keen or acute") and as an eponymous proper noun for the forest anchorite whose inner spiritual fire and discernment were incomparably sharp.
