PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*dʰeh₁- / *deh₃-
"to put, place, set / to give, bestow"
Note: In Proto-Italic verbal compounding, descendants of PIE *deh₃- ('to give') and *dʰeh₁- ('to put, place') merged into Latin dare / -dere (as seen in credere, abdere, perdere).

Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*didō / *dā-

Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
perdere
"to destroy utterly, ruin, squander, waste, lose"
Note: Formed from intensive/pejorative prefix per- ('through to destruction, completely away') + dare ('to give, put'). Literally 'to give over completely to ruin'.
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
perditus
"lost, ruined, hopeless, abandoned, desperate"
↓
Late / Vulgar Latin (c. 4th–7th c. CE)
*perdūtus
"lost, ruined"
Note: In Vulgar Latin, the classical participial suffix -itus was replaced analogically by the productive past participle suffix -ūtus across Western Romance (producing French -u, Italian -uto).
