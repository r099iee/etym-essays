• Sin: Similar because both denote moral wrongdoing and transgression against established ethical standards; differs because sin is inherently theological, implying an offense against God, cosmic order, or divine law, whereas vice emphasizes an ingrained, habitual character flaw or societal moral decay.
  • Folly: Similar because both represent common human shortcomings and subjects of satirical condemnation; differs because folly stems from ignorance, poor judgment, or silly absurdity, whereas vice involves conscious moral depravity and corrupt intent.
  • Depravity: Similar because both describe deep moral corruption; differs because depravity signifies a state of complete, perverse moral bankruptcy and wickedness, whereas vice can refer to discrete, individual bad habits without total collapse of conscience.
  • Iniquity: Similar because both denote profound wickedness; differs because iniquity carries an archaic, biblical gravity denoting gross injustice, unrighteousness, and monstrous violation of human rights.
  • Corruption: Similar because both signify ethical decay; differs because corruption emphasizes the perversion, rotting, or bribery of an otherwise sound institution, system, or individual, while vice describes the settled immoral practice itself.
  • Defect: Similar because both designate an imperfection or blemish; differs because defect is a neutral, non-moral term applied to mechanical, anatomical, or functional shortcomings, whereas vice carries heavy ethical stigma.
  • Weakness: Similar because both can excuse or describe human failing; differs because weakness evokes fragility, sympathy, or lack of emotional fortitude, whereas vice implies culpability, indulgence, or malicious appetite.
  • Peccadillo: Similar because both designate departures from rectitude; differs because a peccadillo is a trivial, harmless indiscretion or pardonable slip, while vice signifies an entrenched, destructive habit.
  • Profligacy: Similar because both denote shameless, dissolute living; differs because profligacy specifically highlights reckless, spendthrift extravagance and squandering of wealth or resources.
  • Turpitude: Similar because both denote moral degradation; differs because turpitude is a formal, legal term denoting inherent baseness, vileness, or depravity that shocks the public conscience (as in ‘crimes of moral turpitude’).
