Linear chain for PRESENTMENT (compound of *per- "forward, before" + *es- "to be"; two root chains converge in Latin and continue as one): Chain for the element prae- (PIE *per-): Stage 1 — PIE: *per- ("forward, before, through"), an adverbial/prepositional root attested across the Indo-European family (cf. Greek pró "before," Sanskrit prá "forward," Latin pro "before, for," Old English fore "before"). Stage 2 — Latin: prae- ("before, in front of"), the prepositional prefix developed from *per-. Chain for the element esse (PIE *es-): Stage 1 — PIE: *es- ("to be"), one of the most widely attested verbal roots (cf. Sanskrit ásti "is," Greek estí "is," Latin est "is," English is). Stage 2 — Latin: esse ("to be"), the copula verb built on *es-. Combined chain (from the convergent Latin stage): Stage 3 — Latin: praesens, praesent- ("being before, present, at hand"), from prae- ("before") + the present participle of esse ("to be"); literally "being before." Stage 4 — Latin: praesentare ("to place before, to present, to show"), a verb built on praesens, meaning "to set before the eyes or the mind." Stage 5 — Late Latin / Old French: presentement ("the act of presenting"), a nominal formation in -ment from presenter ("to present"), carried into Anglo-Norman and Middle English. Stage 6 — Middle English: presentment ("the act of presenting; a representation"), attested from the 13th century, with both the general sense and the emerging legal sense. Stage 7 — Modern English: presentment ("representation, depiction, a setting before the mind or eyes; (law) a grand jury's formal statement of an offense on its own knowledge; (commercial law) the formal offering of a bill of exchange or note for acceptance or payment") — PRESENTMENT. Note on cognates: the same Latin praesens underlies English present, presence, presentable, presentation, and representation; the PIE *per- root underlies pro-, peri-, fore-, former, foremost, and Latin pro/progress/proceed; the PIE *es- root underlies is, am, entity, essence, and Latin est/sum/esse. These are collateral cognates, not stages in the direct chain to presentment.
