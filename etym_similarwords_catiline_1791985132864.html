• conspirator: Similar because it names the role Catiline actually filled, a person engaged in a secret plan to seize power; differs because it is a plain common noun with no implication of noble birth, debt or a specific historical plot.
  • plotter: Similar because it stresses the secret, premeditated planning behind the rising; differs because it is weaker on the public and political dimension, suggesting a cabal rather than a movement with an army.
  • traitor: Similar because it condemns betrayal of the state, which is exactly the charge Cicero pressed; differs because it implies a broken allegiance and a single act of betrayal rather than a sustained conspiracy, and it carries a heavier moral verdict.
  • subversive: Similar because Catiline aimed to overturn the constitutional order; differs because the word is modern and ideological, whereas Catiline's programme was personal power and debt relief, not social transformation.
  • agitator: Similar because it captures his popular following and his rhetoric about debt; differs because agitation stops short of armed insurrection and does not imply the murder of magistrates.
  • demagogue: Similar because Catiline courted the indebted and the dispossessed; differs because a demagogue may work entirely within the law, and Catiline's methods were those of an oligarchic clique rather than a popular movement.
  • Guy Fawkes: Similar because the name is a British byword for a domestic plot against the state; differs because Fawkes was a religious conspirator and a foreign agent, not a patrician insider standing for office.
  • Benedict Arnold: Similar because the name means treason to one's own polity; differs because Arnold was a serving officer selling a fortress, without Catiline's political following or his programme of cancellation of debts.
  • Quisling: Similar because it is a proper name turned into a common noun of political abuse; differs because it denotes collaboration with a foreign occupier, not an internal coup.
  • Judas: Similar because it is the archetypal name for a betrayer; differs because Judas betrays a person and a master, whereas Catiline is accused of betraying a commonwealth.
