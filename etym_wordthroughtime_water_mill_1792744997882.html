- [Antiquity (1st c. BCE–5th c. CE)] — Celebrated by Vitruvius and deployed extensively across the Roman Empire (e.g., the monumental 16-wheel milling complex at Barbegal, France) — [perception: marvel of Roman hydraulic civil engineering that liberated human beings from the brute labor of hand-grinding querns]
- [Medieval Era (6th–15th c.)] — Proliferated across every manor in Western Europe, codified in feudal law via compulsory "mill socage" and chronicled extensively in the Domesday Book (1086) — [perception: essential heart of village economic survival, though heavily burdened by feudal tolls and the cultural stereotype of the dishonest, grain-stealing miller]
- [18th–Early 19th c. Industrial Revolution] — Expanded into massive multi-story textile and ironworks (such as Arkwright's Cromford Mill) before being overtaken by coal-fired steam engines — [perception: catalyst of early industrial capitalism, gradually romanticized as rural water power was replaced by soot-choked urban factories]
- [Late 19th–20th c. Romanticization & High Fantasy] — Immortalized in landscape painting (Constable) and pastoral literature (George Eliot, Tolkien's Shire) as the quintessential symbol of rustic idyll and environmental harmony — [perception: nostalgic symbol of traditional rural life, clean river craftsmanship, and peaceful village independence]
- [21st c. Green Technology & Micro-Hydro] — Re-examined in renewable energy engineering and eco-preservation as the founding historical blueprint for carbon-neutral, river-integrated micro-hydroelectric power — [perception: inspiring historical template of eco-friendly, zero-emission technology integrated into the local ecosystem]
