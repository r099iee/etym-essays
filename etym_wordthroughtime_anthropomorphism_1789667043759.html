- Ancient critique without the word — Xenophanes mocks human-shaped gods, Aesop delights in talking beasts; perception splits between philosophical scorn and popular pleasure.
- 1753 coinage as theological description — English anthropomorphism for the doctrine of a human-bodied God; perception is learned and neutral, a heresy label turned taxonomy.
- 19th-century scientific pejorative — Darwin, Huxley and behaviourists wield anthropomorphism as a charge against sloppy natural history; perception is disciplinary, policing the border of real science.
- 20th-century literary negotiation — nature writers accept the risk as unavoidable: to write at all is to humanise a little; praised restraint means almost never descending; perception is aesthetic-ethical.
- Contemporary cognitive and AI reframing — theory of mind and social robotics show anthropomorphism as built-in and sometimes useful; critical anthropomorphism rehabilitated; perception is dual: error in explanation, resource in relation.
