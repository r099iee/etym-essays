- 1789 English borrowing — an exotic Alpine technical word for a snow-slide, tied to the Alps.
- 19th century — broadens to any falling mass (rock, earth, ruin) and enters figurative use for an overwhelming onrush of misfortune, grief, or emotion.
- 1900s–1930s — becomes a staple of disaster journalism and mountaineering writing; "avalanche of ..." phrases multiply in political and commercial prose.
- 1950s–2000s — gains scientific precision in avalanche forecasting and control; the "avalanche effect" enters electronics and computing as a cascade metaphor.
- Present day — a familiar emblem of unstoppable momentum, used across news, sport, technology, and advertising, while the literal sense governs ski-season warnings.
