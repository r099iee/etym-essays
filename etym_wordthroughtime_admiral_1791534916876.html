- Early 13th c. – Saracen military commander or emir - Denoted powerful Muslim princes, generals, or regional warlords encountered in the Levant during the Crusades.
- Late 13th to 14th c. – High commander of an armada - Transitioned in Anglo-Norman and Middle English administration to designate royal supreme officers of maritime war fleets (e.g., Lord High Admiral).
- 16th to 18th c. – Specialized naval flag officer & lead ship - Formalized into rigid hierarchical naval divisions (Rear Admiral of the Blue, Vice Admiral of the White, Admiral of the Red); also designated the flagship leading an expedition.
- Late 18th c. – Brush-footed butterfly (Red/White Admiral) - Transferred colloquially through folk-etymological corruption of "the admirable" into the common name for the showy Nymphalidae butterfly.
- 19th to 20th c. – Supreme naval authority & strategic statesman - Exemplified by heroic maritime figures like Nelson, embodying elite strategic doctrine, technological naval power, and four-star executive leadership.
- Modern Era – Senior joint military command - Denotes supreme operational theater commanders, chiefs of naval operations, and NATO maritime authorities overseeing modern carrier strike groups and nuclear fleets.
