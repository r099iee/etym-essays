- [Old English c. 900–1100] - [Aquatic drifting: Derived from 'floterian', denoting floating or drifting erratically upon choppy water waves and ocean swells.]
- [Middle English c. 1200–1450] - [Aerial hovering: Shifted from water to air, describing falling leaves, smoke, and birds hovering lightly with beating wings.]
- [16th–17th c.] - [Dynamic motion & social agitation: Applied in Renaissance literature to beating wings, fluttering garments/fans, and scattering enemies with rapid disturbance.]
- [18th–19th c.] - [Psychological turmoil & mild gambling: Emerged as a noun for emotional nervousness ('all in a flutter') and British colloquial betting ('a flutter on the horses').]
- [20th–21st c.] - [Technical & medical precision: Formally codified in cardiology ('atrial flutter'), aerospace engineering ('wing flutter'), and acoustic fidelity ('wow and flutter').]
