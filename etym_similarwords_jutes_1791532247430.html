• Angles: A fellow member of Bede's triad, migrating from the same northern region; differs because the Angles were far more numerous, gave England its name, and founded the kingdoms of Mercia and Northumbria, whereas the Jutes are associated chiefly with Kent and the Isle of Wight.
  • Saxons: The third and most powerful partner in the migration; differs because the Saxons were a large confederation whose name spread across Wessex, Essex and Sussex and became a general label for the English, while the Jutes remained a small, localized people.
  • Frisians: North Sea coastal people sometimes proposed as participants in the migration; differs because the Frisians kept a continental homeland and language, while the Jutes were absorbed into England and Denmark and left no surviving language.
  • Danes: The later Scandinavian inhabitants of Jutland and the Viking-Age invaders of England; differs because "Dane" is a later and broader Scandinavian identity, whereas "Jute" is an early migration-period name preserved mainly by Bede.
  • Jutlanders: The modern regional term for people from Jutland; differs because it is a geographical and contemporary designation, not the ancient ethnonym.
  • Geats: A Scandinavian people of southern Sweden known from Beowulf; differs because they inhabited a different region and are not part of the English origin story, despite occasional confusion with the Jutes.
  • Goths: A major East Germanic people; differs because the Goths migrated across Europe and founded kingdoms in Italy and Spain, while the Jutes were a small North Sea group with no such diaspora.
  • Teutons: A loose classical label for Germanic peoples; differs because it is an umbrella term, not the name of this specific people.
  • Kentishmen: The later regional identity of Kent; differs because it is a territorial English identity that absorbed Jutish traditions rather than a name for the original settlers.
