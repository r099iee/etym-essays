• Roundabout: Similar because both describe a route that goes around rather than direct; differs because roundabout is more everyday and can mean confusing or evasive in a chatty way.
  • Indirect: Similar because both mean not going straight to a point; differs because indirect is broader, covering manner, speech, and causation, not just paths.
  • Winding / Meandering: Similar because both describe curving routes; differs because winding/meandering describe the physical curves of a road or river, whereas circuitous emphasises going around obstacles or taking a long way.
  • Twisting: Similar because both describe a non-straight path; differs because twisting implies tight turns, not necessarily a longer journey.
  • Tortuous: Similar because both mean full of turns and long ways; differs because tortuous also implies painful, complex difficulty and often a bad road.
  • Devious: Similar because both can describe indirect paths; differs because devious has a moral overtone (deceitful) when applied to persons or methods.
  • Oblique: Similar because both mean slanting away from the direct; differs because oblique is chiefly used of angles, light, or indirect speech.
  • Long-winded: Similar because both describe something drawn out; differs because long-winded applies to speech and writing, not to routes.
  • Digressive: Similar because both move away from the direct course; differs because digressive applies to argument or narrative rather than travel.
  • Periphrastic: Similar because both avoid the direct way; differs because periphrastic is a linguistic term for roundabout phrasing.
  • Serpentine: Similar because both describe winding paths; differs because serpentine suggests S-shaped slithering curves in the path itself.
