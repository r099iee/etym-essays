• Cognizant: Similar because both involve possessing knowledge; differs because cognizant (usually cognizant of) means formally aware, mindful, or having conscious realization of a fact or situation, without any implication of secrecy or insider status.
  • Apprised: Similar because both describe having information; differs because apprised (usually apprised of) means formally informed, notified, or briefed by someone else, whereas privy to emphasizes being admitted into a secret circle.
  • Informed: Similar because both denote possessing knowledge; differs because informed is a general, neutral term indicating that one has received facts or education, lacking the exclusive, confidential aura of privy.
  • Confidential: Similar because both involve secrets; differs because confidential describes the sensitive nature of the information itself or a trusted person trusted to keep secrets (e.g., a confidential report, a confidential assistant), whereas privy describes the state of possessing that secret knowledge.
  • In on: Similar because both mean sharing secret knowledge; differs because in on is an informal conversational idiom (e.g., "in on the joke", "in on the scheme"), whereas privy to belongs to an elevated, formal, or literary register.
  • Private: Similar because both derive from Latin privatus; differs because private has a vastly broader scope (private property, private life, private soldier), whereas privy is specialized for confidential access, royal institutions, legal privity, or latrines.
  • Acquainted: Similar because both denote familiarity; differs because acquainted with implies general familiarity, personal knowledge, or contact, rather than exclusive access to classified or secret information.
