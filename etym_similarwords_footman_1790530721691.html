• Valet: Similar because both are male domestic servants; differs because a valet is a master's personal gentleman's servant attending to dressing, wardrobe, and grooming in plain clothes, whereas a footman is a liveried servant performing general household, carriage, and door duties.
  • Butler: Similar because both serve in the indoor domestic hierarchy; differs because the butler is the senior head of the male household staff in charge of wine cellars, silver, and dining room management, directly supervising the footmen.
  • Manservant: Similar because both describe male domestic staff; differs because manservant is a general, umbrella term for any male attendant, whereas footman designates a specific liveried rank.
  • Lackey: Similar because both refer to liveried male attendants; differs because lackey carries an intensely servile, derogatory, or contemptuous connotation of fawning obedience.
  • Flunkey: Similar because both describe liveried male servants; differs because flunkey is a colloquial, satirical, or pejorative term highlighting snobbery and obsequiousness.
  • Retainer: Similar because both are dependent members of a household staff; differs because a retainer is a broader historical term for any feudal follower, long-serving servant, or personal dependent.
  • Infantryman: Similar because footman historically designated a foot soldier; differs because infantryman is the modern, exclusively military term for a ground combatant.
  • Squire: Similar because a squire attended a knight; differs because a squire was an aspiring nobleman and knight's armor-bearer, holding gentle social rank rather than being a domestic servant.
