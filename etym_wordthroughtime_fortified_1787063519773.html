- 15th century: "strengthened with defensive works" - castles, walls, and towns made strong against siege; the flagship military sense.
- 16th–19th centuries: figurative growth - hearts, spirits, arguments, and hopes "fortified"; the word broadens from stone to resolve.
- 19th century: structural and technical use - fortified concrete, fortified positions; still military-led but expanding.
- 20th century: the great new senses - fortified foods (vitamin-fortified staples from the 1920s–40s) and fortified wines (port, sherry, madeira) - the word leaps from castle to kitchen.
- Present: fully modern and productive across military, culinary, nutritional, financial ("fortified balance sheets"), and digital ("fortified networks") domains; positive connotation of added safety and strength.
