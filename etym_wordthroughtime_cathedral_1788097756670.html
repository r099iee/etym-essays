- The bishop's chair (4th c. CE) - "cathedral" begins as an adjective meaning "of the cathedra", the bishop's throne; perception: authority, not yet architecture.
- The mother church (5th-11th c.) - the word is nominalised to the diocese's head church, the see's physical home; perception: clerical power and regional sway.
- The stone giant (11th-14th c.) - the Gothic building wave makes the cathedral the very heart of the city; perception: piety, ambition, civic pride measured in metres of spire.
- Reformation and heritage (16th-19th c.) - cathedrals are stripped, recast as state churches and then rediscovered as national monuments; perception: history, craft, continuity.
- Secular metaphor and tourism (19th-21st c.) - "cathedrals of industry, learning and sport" multiply, and the buildings themselves become heritage destinations; perception: awe, monumentality, the sublime in stone and glass.
