• Chimera: Similar because the Chimera is also a composite monster of Greek myth with a lion’s parts; differs because the Chimera is the fire-breathing lion–goat–serpent of Lycia slain by Bellerophon — an elemental natural force — while the Sphinx is a riddle-trap that tests intelligence: her victims die not by flame but by ignorance.
  • Griffin (gryphon): Similar because the griffin is another lion-bodied composite beast, and both guard thresholds and treasures; differs because the griffin is lion-and-eagle (no human head) and guards gold in legend; it poses no riddles and destroys no one who fails an answer.
  • Minotaur: Similar because the Minotaur is another human-animal hybrid monster of Greek myth, confined in the Labyrinth and fed on youths like the Sphinx’s prey; differs because the Minotaur is a bull-man of brute appetite in a maze, whereas the Sphinx is a thinking trap on the open road who kills through questions.
  • Harpy: Similar because harpies are winged female monsters like the Greek sphinx’s avian form; differs because harpies are storm-spirits of theft, filth and sudden snatching — they punish and dirty things — while the Sphinx is a guardian and riddler, not a thief.
  • Manticore: Similar because the manticore is a lion-bodied, human-headed, tail-spiked monster with a taste for men; differs because it belongs to Persian and Indian marvellous-beast lore and is a creature of appetite and venom, never a riddle-setter or a stone monument.
  • Enigma: Similar because “a sphinx” metaphorically is an enigma — a baffling person, face or matter; differs because “enigma” is the abstract noun for the baffling quality itself, while “sphinx” keeps the concrete image of the silent stone guardian who holds the secret.
  • Riddle: Similar because the Sphinx’s cultural function is to pose a riddle, and “sphinx” can name the poser where “riddle” names the puzzle; differs because the riddle is the question (and by extension any baffling problem), while the sphinx is the being who asks it — question versus questioner.
  • Colossus: Similar because the Great Sphinx is a colossal statue, kin to a colossus; differs because “colossus” means simply an oversized statue or giant (the Colossus of Rhodes) and carries none of the composite-monster meaning or the riddle — size, not strangling or secrecy.
