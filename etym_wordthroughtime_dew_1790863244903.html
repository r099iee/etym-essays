- Old English (c. 8th–11th c.) - dēaw denoted the natural condensation of moisture on plants overnight, imbued with agricultural importance and biblical symbolism of life-sustaining divine blessing.
- Middle English (c. 12th–15th c.) - dew / deaw expanded deeply into courtly romance and religious allegory, serving as a primary metaphor for the conception of Christ and the immaculate purity and tenderness of the Virgin Mary.
- Early Modern English (c. 16th–17th c.) - Elizabethan and Jacobean poets (such as Shakespeare and Donne) cemented dew as the quintessential motif of youth, tears, fleeting beauty, and morning freshness.
- 18th to 19th Century - Folk slang adopted mountain dew for illicit Highland and Appalachian moonshine; Romantic poets used dew to evoke communion with pristine, untouched nature.
- 20th to 21st Century - Retains classic poetic and meteorological senses while expanding heavily into cosmetics (dewy skin finish) and brand iconography.
