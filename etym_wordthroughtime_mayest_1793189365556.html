- Prehistory — power itself: PIE magh-, to be able, gives Germanic a verb of strength and capacity.
- Old English — ability owned: magan means to be able, to avail; þu meaht is thou canst in all but spelling.
- Middle English — permission blooms: mowen, mayen slide from can toward may, granting leave as well as owning power.
- 16th–17th c. — thou mayest: the -est ending fixes the form in Bible, prayer book, and stage, blessing and permitting in high style.
- 18th–19th c. — the grammar retires: thou fades from speech while may divides its labour with can, keeping permission and yielding ability.
- Today — costume permission: mayest survives only as deliberate archaism, but the wonder-tale horse still grants it, as in the source sentence.
