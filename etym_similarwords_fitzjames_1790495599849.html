• James V (King of the Commons): Similar because FitzJames is his disguised poetic double; differs because James V is the historical Stewart king, while FitzJames is Scott's incognito alias.
  • Knight of Snowdoun: Similar because it is FitzJames's title in the poem; differs because it names the disguise (Stirling Castle's rock), while FitzJames names the man.
  • Roderick Dhu: Similar because he is FitzJames's noble antagonist and fellow chief; differs because Roderick is the outlawed Alpine clan leader, while FitzJames is the hidden king who pardons him.
  • Douglas (Ellen and her father): Similar because Ruskin pairs FitzJames with the Douglases as models of noblesse; differs because Douglases are exiled nobles restored by mercy, while FitzJames is the mercy-giver.
  • Malcolm Graeme: Similar because he is the young rival lover spared by FitzJames; differs because Graeme is the fortunate youth, while FitzJames is the mature king who yields Ellen to him.
  • Marmion (Lord Marmion): Similar because he is Scott's other chivalric hero Ruskin knew; differs because Marmion is flawed, doomed at Flodden, while FitzJames is ideal and triumphant.
  • Goodman of Ballengeich: Similar because it is James V's historical disguise-name in ballads; differs because Goodman is folk disguise, while FitzJames is literary-Norman disguise.
  • James FitzJames, Duke of Berwick: Similar because he bears the identical name as bastard son of James II; differs because Berwick is the real Jacobite marshal, while Scott's FitzJames is the fictional incognito king.
  • Fitzroy: Similar because it is the parallel Fitz- royal bastard name (son of the king); differs because Fitzroy marks any king's son, while FitzJames marks a James's son specifically.
  • Waverley heroes (Edward Waverley, Ivanhoe): Similar because they are Scott heroes learning chivalry; differs because they are English outsiders in Scotland, while FitzJames is Scotland's own king at home.
