- 13th-15th century assessing - to tax first means to assess or reckon a charge, the financial sense that still anchors the word; a tax is a duty exacted from a subject.
- 16th-17th century moral pressing - the verb extends from assessing money to pressing home a fault: to tax someone with a matter is to charge them with it face to face, as if the fault were a debt owed and collectable; the word takes on an accusatory, bookish colour.
- 17th-19th century literary currency - the accusatory sense thrives in formal and moral prose, almost always with "with" (taxed him with neglect, taxed with ingratitude), and appears naturally in the Mill biography.
- Contemporary use - the accusatory sense survives only in archaic and literary usage; modern English uses taxed chiefly for money, or figuratively for strain ("the journey taxed his strength").
