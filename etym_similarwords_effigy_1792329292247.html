• Simulacrum: Similar because it denotes a representation, image, or physical likeness of a person or object; differs because a simulacrum often implies a superficial, deceptive, or hollow imitation that conceals the lack of underlying reality, whereas an effigy is an overt, deliberate likeness fashioned for ritual, funerary, or demonstrative display.
  • Mannequin: Similar because it is an articulated, three-dimensional fabricated human figure; differs because a mannequin is primarily a commercial or functional model used for displaying apparel or conducting safety crash tests, carrying zero political malice, funerary piety, or ritual hostility.
  • Puppet: Similar because it is an artificial human figure operated by human agency; differs because a puppet is engineered for dynamic mechanical movement and theatrical performance (or metaphorically controlled by hidden masters), whereas an effigy is static, iconic, and created to be displayed, venerated, or publicly destroyed.
  • Dummy: Similar because it refers to a fabricated, stuffed stand-in or mock figure representing a person; differs because dummy has a distinctly utilitarian, informal, or crash-testing register, lacking the profound ritual solemnity or fervent political outrage inherent in an effigy.
  • Statue: Similar because it represents a three-dimensional carved, sculpted, or cast likeness of a human being; differs because a statue is an enduring, celebrated civic or aesthetic monument designed for permanent public veneration, whereas an effigy is frequently crude, ephemeral, and deliberately engineered for immolation.
  • Icon: Similar because it embodies a sacred or revered visual representation of an individual; differs because an icon is predominantly a flat, devotional panel painting or mosaic venerated within orthodox religious traditions, while an effigy is a three-dimensional sculpted or constructed physical figure.
  • Caricature: Similar because it captures the identifiable likeness of an individual for satire, ridicule, or public commentary; differs because a caricature is a graphic, visual, or literary distortion that exaggerates physical features on paper or canvas, whereas an effigy is a tangible, three-dimensional physical construct.
  • Fetish: Similar because it is a crafted physical object believed to embody or channel spiritual influence; differs because a fetish is venerated as an animate vessel of magical potency, protection, or obsessive psychological attachment, rather than an overt political likeness.
