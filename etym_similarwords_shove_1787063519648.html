• push: similar because both mean the core action; differs because push is the neutral, polite general word — you push a trolley but shove a rival; shove is abrupt and rude.
  • thrust: similar forceful pushing; differs because thrust is sharp, directed, often weapon-like (thrust a sword), while shove is broad and bodily.
  • jostle: similar pushing in a crowd; differs because jostle is mutual, incidental bumping, while shove is deliberate and personal.
  • elbow: similar pushing with the elbow; differs because elbow names the body part and is usually defensive, while shove uses hands and shoulders and is aggressive.
  • nudge: similar a small push; differs because nudge is gentle, secret, and friendly — the exact opposite register of shove.
  • ram: similar violent pushing; differs because ram is head-on, mechanical, and unstoppable (ram a door with a battering ram), while shove is human-scale.
  • barge: similar pushing through (barge in); differs because barge is clumsy intrusion, while shove is deliberate expulsion.
  • heave: similar effortful pushing/lifting; differs because heave emphasises lifting weight upward, while shove is horizontal and abrupt — the pair appears together in the source text's "heaved and shoved".
  • propel: similar causing forward motion; differs because propel is formal, scientific, and impersonal, while shove is physical and personal.
  • butt: similar pushing with the head; differs because butt is head-first (of animals or rams), while shove uses hands and shoulders.
  • leave / depart / scram (the "shove off" sense): similar because all mean going away; differs because "shove off" is ruder than leave, more colourful than depart, and less vulgar than scram — a salty nautical dismissal with a specific history.
