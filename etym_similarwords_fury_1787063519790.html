• rage: Similar because both mean intense, violent anger and are near-interchangeable in "a fury / a rage"; differs because rage is the broader, more modern and clinical term that can name a slow-burning or chronic state, while fury suggests a hot, explosive, often short-lived eruption touched with frenzy.
  • wrath: Similar because both denote extreme anger; differs because wrath is more solemn, literary, and morally weighted (divine or kingly punishment), while fury is wilder, less dignified, and closer to madness.
  • anger: Similar because it is the plain family word for the same emotion; differs because anger covers every intensity from mild irritation upward, whereas fury sits at the extreme top end of the scale and is never mild.
  • ire: Similar because it is a literary synonym for anger; differs because ire is cold, dignified, and often smouldering in style, whereas fury is hot, violent, and spectacular.
  • frenzy: Similar because both describe an overpowering, irrational excitement; differs because frenzy is not limited to anger (it can be joy, panic, or haste), while fury is specifically rage.
  • indignation: Similar because both are angry emotions; differs because indignation is righteous, controlled, and rooted in a sense of injustice — the opposite of fury's uncontrollable heat.
  • vehemence / ferocity: Similar because both name violent intensity; differ because these are about force rather than emotion, whereas fury is an emotion (though it can stand for force, as in "the fury of the storm").
  • passion: Similar because fury historically meant a violent passion; differs because passion now covers love, enthusiasm, and any strong emotion, with none of fury's destructive anger.
  • temper: Similar because a "fierce temper" can approach fury; differs because temper is a general disposition or a loss of control of variable strength, and in idiom "a fury" is a violent fit while "a temper" is a mood.
  • madness / frenzy of battle: Similar because both link rage with unreason; differs because madness is a clinical or general mental state, while fury keeps its specific angry character.
