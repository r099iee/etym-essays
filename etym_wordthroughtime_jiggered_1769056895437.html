- Early 19th c. - jigger (from jig + -er) becomes a slang noun for a small device or thing; the verb to jigger means to jerk about, to tamper with - hence to spoil - and the raw material for several senses is laid down.
- 1830s - 'I'll be jiggered!' appears: a genteel minced oath, a nonsense softener for the profane 'I'll be buggered'; the exclamatory sense of astonishment and consternation is born, and the word begins to mean a man rattled out of his composure.
- 19th–20th c. - the other two senses settle: jiggered 'broken, ruined, spoilt' (chiefly British, from the verb) and jiggered 'slightly intoxicated' (from the bartender's jigger measure, chiefly American).
- 20th–21st c. - the word ages into a dated, half-jocular schoolboy exclamation; a modern speaker would say gobsmacked or dumbfounded, so jiggered now works as deliberate period flavour - the sound of a gentleman being jolted, his voice gone queer.
