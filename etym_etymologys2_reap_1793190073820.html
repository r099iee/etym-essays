Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE)
*rīpan-
‘to cut grain, reap’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
rīpan, reopan
‘to reap, cut corn’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
repen
‘to reap the harvest’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
reap
‘to harvest; receive’
↓
Modern English (current)
reap
‘to harvest; obtain’

Note: handbooks frequently group reap with ripe, from Old English rīpe meaning mature for harvest, as a native harvest-word family, but the deeper PIE source of that family is uncertain and reconstructions are offered only tentatively, so the chain stops at Proto-Germanic rather than guessing.
