- c. 1300 — procure enters English meaning “to bring about, contrive, instigate”, often of results and deaths; the tone is formal and causative.
- 14th–15th c. — the sense “to obtain, acquire by care or arrangement” develops and becomes dominant in legal, commercial and administrative writing.
- 16th–17th c. — the sexual euphemism appears: to procure a woman, and the nouns procurer and procuress for a pander; moral stigma attaches to the verb.
- 18th–19th c. — military and imperial logistics make procure a supply word — provisions, stores, transport — and procurement becomes an office function.
- 20th–21st c. — procurement grows into a profession and a branch of law, while the verb itself becomes bookish in ordinary use; the sexual sense survives mainly in legal language and historical fiction.
