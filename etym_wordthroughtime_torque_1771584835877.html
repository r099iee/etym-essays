- [19th century: antiquarian revival] - 'Torque' (also 'torc') is revived by antiquarians from Latin as the name of the ancient Celtic neck-ring of twisted metal; connotation is purely archaeological - the treasure of Iron-Age Gauls and Britons.
- [20th century: the mechanics homonym] - The same spelling is simultaneously adopted in physics for a twisting force; connotation is technical and mechanical, and the two words now coexist without confusing speakers, distinguished purely by context.
- [Modern usage] - 'Torque' still names both the ancient neck ornament and, in engineering, rotational force; the connotation of the jewellery word remains archaic, heroic, and ceremonial - a badge of rank in the form of a twisted band of metal.
