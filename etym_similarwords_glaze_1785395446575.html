• Stare: Similar because both describe the eyes and the act of looking fixedly; differs because a stare is deliberate, sustained, directed looking with the eyes alive and active, whereas a glazed eye does not look at anything — it merely reflects, like glass, with the attention withdrawn.
  • Shine: Similar because both involve a glossy, light-reflecting surface; differs because shine is emotionally neutral and names any emission or return of light, whereas glazing eyes are bright for a terrible reason — the light is all reflection because the life is gone.
  • Gloss: Similar because gloss also names a smooth, shiny surface finish laid over something; differs because gloss suggests a surface polish of any kind, while glaze insists on the specifically glassy, vitreous, translucent quality — which is why only glaze fits a dying eye.
  • Enamel: Similar because enamel is likewise a hard, vitreous, light-reflecting coating fused to a surface; differs because enamel is usually coloured and opaque and is applied in thick layers, whereas glaze is the thin, smooth, transparent glassy finish — the difference between a coloured casing and a clear shining film.
  • Lacquer: Similar because lacquer is a glossy coating laid over wood or metal; differs because lacquer is a resinous, organic finish that dries by evaporation, not a vitreous glass-like skin, and it never transfers to the body-language of a glassy eye.
  • Varnish: Similar because varnish produces a transparent, shiny protective coat; differs because varnish is a resin-solvent finish used on wood and paintings, softer and less mineral than a true glaze, and it has no figurative eye-sense.
  • Coat: Similar because to coat is to lay a covering over a surface; differs because a coat can be any covering, however dull or opaque — a coat of dust, a coat of paint — while glaze names the specific smooth, glassy, reflecting finish, and only glaze can describe what happens to a dying eye.
  • Glassy / Vacant: Similar because both describe a fixed, unseeing look; differs because glassy is an adjective describing the appearance (bright, hard, reflective) and vacant stresses the emptiness of the mind behind the eyes, whereas glaze is the verb naming the process by which the eye becomes that glassy vacant thing.
