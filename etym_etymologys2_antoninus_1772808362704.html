Linear derivation chain for ANTONINUS (single root word; NON-PIE, Etruscan origin — no Indo-European root can be assigned): 1. Etruscan personal name-stock (unreconstructed; meaning unknown). Latin Antonius is standardly judged to be of Etruscan origin; no PIE reconstruction is possible and none should be invented. Date: prehistoric, before the attested Roman period. 2. Latin Antonius — the nomen gentilicium (clan name) of the gens Antonia, a plebeian family attested from the 3rd century BCE; the most famous Republican bearer is Marcus Antonius, the triumvir (83-30 BCE), whose gens-name passes into later literature. 3. Latin Antoninus — a cognomen derived from Antonius with the derivative suffix -inus ("belonging to the Antonii, of the Antonian family"); attested in the second century AD as an imperial name: Titus Aurelius Fulvus Boionius Arrius Antoninus (the future emperor Antoninus Pius, AD 138-161) and Marcus Aurelius Antoninus (emperor AD 161-180; Gibbon's "Marcus Antoninus"). 4. English ANTONINUS — the learned / historiographical form of the cognomen, used by Gibbon and eighteenth-century history for the last of the Antonines. Notes and debate: - The origin of the nomen Antonius is opaque; the standard view (e.g., Schulze, "Zur Geschichte lateinischer Eigennamen") treats it as Etruscan. The popular meanings "priceless" and "flower" (from Greek anthos) are folk glosses, not reconstructions. - The legendary claim that the Antonii descended from "Anton", a son of Hercules, is political myth, not etymology. - The English given name "Anthony" derives from Latin Antonius via French Antoine; its -h- (Anthony) is an unhistorical medieval insertion based on the false association with Greek anthos "flower". It is a spelling accident of a separate name-line, not an ancestor of ANTONINUS.
