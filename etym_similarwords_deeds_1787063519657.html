• acts: Similar because both name things that are done; differs because "acts" is the more general, neutral term for any action, whereas "deeds" carries a weight of significance, achievement, or moral quality.
  • actions: Similar because both name things one does; differs because "actions" is a broad behavioural term lacking the heroic or legal overtones of "deeds".
  • exploits: Similar because both can name remarkable, brave achievements; differs because "exploits" stresses daring adventure and glamour, while "deeds" can be plain and moral as well as heroic.
  • feats: Similar because both name impressive accomplishments; differs because a "feat" emphasises skill and difficulty, whereas a "deed" emphasises the moral or consequential character of an act.
  • achievements: Similar because both name things accomplished; differs because "achievements" foregrounds success and result, while "deeds" foregrounds the doing and its moral weight.
  • accomplishments: Similar because both name things completed; differs because "accomplishments" often refers to cultivated skills and finished works, while "deeds" refers to actions performed.
  • misdeeds: Similar because it shares the root and the sense of performed acts; differs because "misdeeds" is specifically negative — evil or unlawful acts — whereas "deeds" is neutral to positive.
  • documents / instruments: Similar in the legal sense because both are written legal materials; differs because "deeds" refers specifically to signed, witnessed written instruments transferring property or creating obligations, whereas "documents" is the general term for any written record.
  • titles: Similar in the legal domain because both relate to ownership of property; differs because "title" is the legal right or evidence of ownership itself, whereas a "deed" is the specific written instrument that conveys it.
