• Handsome: similar in denoting pleasing appearance, with an emphasis on well-proportioned dignity; differs in scale and gender-valence — handsome leans toward the striking and stately, often of men, while comely favours gentleness and modesty.
  • Pretty: similar as an everyday word for pleasing looks; differs in register and intensity — pretty can trivialise or suggest small-scale charm, whereas comely implies wholesomeness and propriety.
  • Beautiful: similar in naming beauty; differs in degree — beautiful is the superlative aesthetic word, while comely is a quieter, more domestic beauty.
  • Seemly: similar in the "fitness and propriety" sense; differs in that seemly has no physical sense and is purely about decorum, while comely unites looks and conduct.
  • Becoming: similar — fitting and flattering to a person; differs in being chiefly predicative ("a becoming outfit") and about appropriateness to an occasion, not a standing quality of the person.
  • Decorous: similar in the propriety sense; differs in register — decorous is cool, formal, rule-bound, whereas comely is warm and aesthetic.
  • Fair: similar as an archaic or literary word for beautiful; differs in that fair is far broader (pale, light, just, favourable) and more archaic in this sense.
  • Shapely: similar in praising physical form; differs in narrowing strictly to figure and proportions, with no behavioural or moral sense at all.
  • Graceful: similar in uniting beauty with ease of movement or manner; differs in pointing to poise and fluidity rather than to a settled quality of appearance or character.
  • Bonny: similar Scots and Northern English word for pretty and healthy; differs in dialect colour and in carrying hearty, rural vitality.
