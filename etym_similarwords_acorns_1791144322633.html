• nuts: Similar because acorns are tree nuts used as food; differs because nuts is generic while acorns are specifically oak fruits with cups.
  • mast: Similar because it is forest nuts as animal feed; differs because mast is collective and includes beechmast, more technical and archaic.
  • beechnuts: Similar because they are similar woodland nuts eaten by wildlife; differs because they come from beech with spiny husks, smaller and triangular.
  • chestnuts: Similar because they are large edible tree nuts; differs because chestnuts are sweeter, larger, from a different family and prized for humans.
  • hazelnuts: Similar because they are wild nuts cached by squirrels; differs because they have woody shells without cups and different trees.
  • walnuts: Similar because they are cached, oil-rich tree nuts; differs because they are much larger with fleshy husks and brain-like kernels.
  • seeds: Similar because acorns are oak seeds for reproduction; differs because seeds is general and tiny, while acorns are large specialized fruits.
  • kernels: Similar because the edible inner part of an acorn is a kernel; differs because kernel is the inner grain, not the whole cupped fruit.
  • berries: Similar because they are small wild fruits eaten by birds; differs because berries are fleshy and soft, acorns dry and tannic.
  • conkers (horse chestnuts): Similar because they are shiny autumn tree fruits collected by children; differs because conkers are inedible and from horse-chestnut, not oak.
  • pine nuts: Similar because they are wild harvested tree seeds stored for food; differs because they come from cones and are tiny and oily.
  • galls / oak-apples: Similar because they grow on oaks and look fruit-like; differs because they are insect growths, not true fruits.
