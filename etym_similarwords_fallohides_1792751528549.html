• Harfoots: Similar because both are foundational ancestral breeds of Hobbits who undertook the great westward migration into Eriador; differs because Harfoots were the most numerous, browner-skinned, shorter, dwelt in hillside burrows, and were deeply conservative homebodies, whereas Fallohides were the rarest, fair-skinned, taller, tree-loving, and naturally adventurous.
  • Stoors: Similar because both represent ancestral divisions of the Hobbit race described in the Prologue to The Lord of the Rings; differs because Stoors were broader, heavier, had larger hands and feet, loved rivers and boating, and wore boots in mud, whereas Fallohides were slender, light-footed woodland hunters who preferred forests to water.
  • Hobbits: Similar because Fallohides constitutes one of the three primary genetic and cultural branches of Hobbitkind; differs because Hobbits is the overarching name for the entire race, whereas Fallohides specifically denotes this minority kindred of fair-skinned, woodland leaders.
  • Halflings: Similar because both refer to the small humanoid mortals of Middle-earth; differs because Halflings is a general exonym used by Men and other races, whereas Fallohides is a specific historical breed name.
  • Periannath: Similar because both terms denote the diminutive folk of Eriador; differs because Periannath is an elevated Sindarin Elvish collective noun, whereas Fallohides is a rustic Germanic-style historical ethnonym.
  • Tooks: Similar because the Took family of the Great Smials represents the preeminent living embodiment of the Fallohide bloodline in the Shire; differs because Tooks is a specific clan and family surname, whereas Fallohides is the ancestral genetic stock that endowed the Tooks with their boldness and wanderlust.
  • Brandybucks: Similar because the Masters of Buckland possessed strong Fallohide ancestry, explaining their bold colonization of the eastern forest borders; differs because the Brandybucks also absorbed substantial Stoor blood and customs from their riverside environment.
  • Elves / Eldar: Similar because the Fallohides shared close cultural affinities with Elves, admiring their songs, learning their languages, and possessing fairer features and greater height than other hobbits; differs because Elves are immortal Firstborn children of Ilúvatar endowed with supernatural power and ethereal grace, whereas Fallohides are mortal Halflings.
  • Dúnedain: Similar because both served as bold leaders, rangers, and chieftains across the wilderness of Eriador; differs because the Dúnedain are tall, long-lived Men of royal Númenórean descent who ruled kingdoms, whereas Fallohides are four-foot-tall hobbits.
  • Wood-elves: Similar because both possessed a profound love of trees, forest craft, hunting, and woodland song; differs because Wood-elves are immortal Silvan and Sindarin Elves inhabiting great realms like Mirkwood and Lothlórien, whereas Fallohides were woodland-dwelling mortal hobbits.
