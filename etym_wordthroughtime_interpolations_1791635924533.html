- 1610s–1650s: Textual Criticism and Scribal Falsification - Entered Early Modern English to describe the illicit tampering, patching, or alteration of ancient manuscripts, legal charters, and holy scriptures by later editors.
- Late 17th–18th c.: Mathematical Analysis and Astronomy - Formulated by John Wallis, Isaac Newton, and Carl Friedrich Gauss as a rigorous mathematical method for calculating missing planetary positions between observed coordinates.
- 19th c.: Philological Codification and Higher Criticism - Became a dominant technical weapon in biblical and classical scholarship for stripping away later rabbinic, monastic, or editorial additions from original Homeric and biblical texts.
- Mid-20th c.: Digital Signal Processing and Cartography - Expanded into computer science, image upscaling, and digital audio to describe algorithmic pixel generation, sample-rate conversion, and geographic elevation mapping.
- Late 20th–21st c.: Cognitive Psychology, Neurobiology, and Hip-Hop Music - Applied to describe how human memory fills sensory gaps, and in modern music production to denote re-recording an existing melodic hook rather than directly sampling recorded master audio.
