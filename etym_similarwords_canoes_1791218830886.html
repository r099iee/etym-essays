• kayaks: Similar because both are light paddle craft; differs because kayaks are decked and use double-bladed paddles while seated.
  • pirogues: Similar because both are dugout or small boats; differs because pirogue often has a sail or outrigger and is tropical.
  • dugouts: Similar because both are hollowed-log boats; differs because dugout specifies construction method, canoe is the general type.
  • rafts: Similar because both are simple watercraft of logs; differs because rafts are flat floating platforms, not hollowed hulls.
  • rowboats: Similar because both are small open boats; differs because rowboats use oars pivoted on oarlocks facing backward.
  • skiffs: Similar because both are small shallow boats; differs because skiffs are flat-bottomed and often motorized or rowed.
  • dinghies: Similar because both are small auxiliary boats; differs because dinghies accompany larger ships and may have sails or motors.
  • outriggers: Similar because both are paddle canoes; differs because outriggers have a lateral float for stability in ocean swell.
  • birch-bark canoes: Similar because a subtype of canoe; differs because it names bark-covered Algonquian construction specifically.
  • waka / proas: Similar because both are Indigenous paddle craft; differs because these are Pacific terms with sails and asymmetric hulls.
  • punts: Similar because both are flat shallow craft poled or paddled; differs because punts are square-ended and poled from a platform.
