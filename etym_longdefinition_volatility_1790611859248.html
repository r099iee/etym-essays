volatility denotes the quality or state of being volatile: liable to change rapidly and unpredictably, especially for the worse, describing prices, tempers, situations, substances, and, by extension, texts migrating from page to screen. At its core it combines swiftness, instability, and potential energy: what is volatile may evaporate, explode, or swing. In the contrast from permanence to volatility, it names the new condition of digital text as fluid, revisable, linkable, and erasable rather than fixed and monumental.

In general usage, volatility suggests unreliability and risk. A volatile person is quick to anger; a volatile situation may erupt; a volatile market swings wildly; volatile emotions fluctuate. Connotation is usually negative or cautionary, though in creative and technological contexts it can imply liveliness, responsiveness, and freedom from rigidity. Collocations such as reduce volatility, manage volatility, and volatility index encode attempts to tame it.

In finance and economics, volatility is technical: a statistical measure of dispersion of returns, typically standard deviation or variance, annualized, with implied versus historical variants. High volatility means large price swings and uncertainty; low volatility means stability. Derivatives, options pricing, and risk management revolve around forecasting and hedging it. Chemistry preserves the oldest sense: volatility as tendency to vaporize readily at normal temperatures, measured by vapor pressure and boiling point, as in volatile organic compounds or volatile solvents.

  • Chemical-physical sense: evaporative: readily vaporizing, as in volatile liquids, volatile oils, volatile memory that loses data without power.
  • Financial sense: fluctuating: subject to rapid price variation, as in market volatility or earnings volatility.
  • Psychological sense: temperamental: prone to sudden mood shifts or violence, as in a volatile temper or volatile crowd.
  • Situational sense: unstable: liable to erupt or collapse, as in political volatility or volatile negotiations.
  • Media-textual sense: fluid and revisable: changeable, interactive, and impermanent, as in from permanence to volatility in digital textuality.
  • Computing sense: transient: held temporarily and lost on failure, as in volatile storage versus non-volatile flash.

Polysemy is bridged by flying. Latin volatilis from volare to fly meant winged, swift, fleeting. That which flies away evaporates quickly, changes position quickly, and cannot be held. Chemistry kept flying as evaporating; psychology and finance kept flying as sudden movement; computing keeps flying as disappearing. The transparency of the metaphor explains rapid extension without confusion: speakers intuit wings even when speaking of stocks.

Literary nuance exploits ambivalence. Permanence suggests authority, memory, and monument; volatility suggests immediacy, participation, and loss. To describe screen text as volatile is both celebration and elegy: celebration of updating, linking, and animating; elegy for stable citation, authorial control, and slow reading. Mastering the word means hearing both flight and danger in every use.
