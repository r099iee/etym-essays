- shafts (Old English 'sceaft') - the long, straight, cut rod: spear or arrow shaft, weapon-stock, the carpenter's and warrior's word for shaped timber.
- shafts (Middle & Early Modern English) - extends into the built world: the stem of a column, the channel of a mine, the pole of a carriage — a straight, measurable length of stone or wood.
- shafts (from the 16th century) - applied to light: a beam felt to be a solid, measurable thing, a lance of brightness traveling in a straight line, so that a 'shaft of light' turns illumination into an act of penetration.
- turfed (Old English 'turf') - the cut sod of the peasant's hearth and the peat of the upland moors, the solid ground of home; 'to turf' = to lay sod.
- turfed (by the 19th century) - 'the Turf' becomes the racecourse and its world; as an adjective 'turfed' names ground upholstered in living sod — root, soil and grass bound into one soft, deep mat, the horizontal foil to the piercing shafts.
