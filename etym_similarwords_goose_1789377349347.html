• Gander: Similar because both refer to adult birds of the exact same waterfowl species (family Anatidae); differs because gander specifically and exclusively denotes the adult male bird, whereas goose denotes the adult female (in poultry breeding) or serves as the general common name for the species as a whole; furthermore, gander has a unique informal slang meaning of taking a quick look ("take a gander").
  • Swan: Similar because both are large, long-necked aquatic waterfowl belonging to the family Anatidae; differs because swan belongs to the genus Cygnus, is significantly larger with an even longer, more graceful neck, has pure white or black plumage, and carries an elevated cultural connotation of aristocratic beauty, elegance, and poetic tragedy, whereas goose is an earthier, agricultural bird associated with farmyards, feasting, and comedy.
  • Waterfowl: Similar because both refer to birds that swim and forage in aquatic environments; differs because waterfowl is a broad, collective taxonomic and ecological umbrella term encompassing all ducks, geese, and swans (order Anseriformes), whereas goose refers to a specific, well-demarcated subset of large grazing species.
  • Duck: Similar because both are common aquatic birds of the Anatidae family with webbed feet and water-resistant plumage; differs because duck is generally much smaller, has a shorter neck, a broader flattened bill, feeds primarily by dabbling in water, and produces a distinct "quack" rather than the loud, trumpet-like honk or hiss of a goose.
  • Simpleton: Similar because both can be used as colloquial pejoratives to describe a foolish, naive, or silly person; differs because simpleton is a direct, often harsher insult implying genuine cognitive deficiency or lack of common sense, whereas "goose" (especially in "silly goose") is an affectionate, gentle, or playful chiding.
  • Gosling: Similar because both designate the goose in its biological essence; differs because gosling specifically designates a young, newly hatched or immature bird covered in soft down, whereas goose denotes the fully grown, mature adult bird.
