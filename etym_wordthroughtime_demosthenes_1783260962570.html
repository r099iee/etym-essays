- 4th-century Athens - Demosthenes (384-322 BC) was the greatest orator of ancient Athens, whose Philippics against Philip of Macedon made his name synonymous with political eloquence and with the doomed defence of a free city-state against monarchy.
- Ancient critical tradition - from Cicero to Quintilian, Demosthenes stood as the perfection of power in oratory, the standard against which all later speakers were measured, and his name remained the byword for the spoken word turned into a weapon.
- Later literary use - Macaulay set him in the triad of Greek masterpieces with Sophocles and Plato, the voice of a freedom that had perished, still being copied and glossed in decaying Constantinople.
- Contemporary sense - Demosthenes still denotes eloquence and civic resistance, and his famous story - overcoming a stammer by practising with pebbles in his mouth - keeps his name in popular memory.
