baccarat is a noun denoting a high-stakes comparing card game, in casino form a two-handed game in which punters bet on Player or Banker hands, the winning hand being that closest to nine, tens and face cards counting zero. In the source sentence Aunt Dahlia wouldn't have played baccarat if Bertie hadn't gone to Cannes — i.e. she would have avoided the Riviera casino vice that epitomises wealthy idleness.

Rules and variants:

  • Values: Ace = 1, 2–9 = pip, 10/J/Q/K = 0 (hence folk etymology Italian baccarà zero). Hand total is modulo 10: 7+8=15 → 5. Natural 8 or 9 wins at once. This zero-value core makes the game almost automatic.
  • Baccarat banque: historic three-person parlor zero-sum game (Van-Tenac 1847), banker versus two tables. Logistically heavy, now rare except in Monte Carlo high rooms.
  • Chemin de fer: two-person game with rotating bank, players passing the shoe like a railway; some drawing choice remains. Bond's game in Casino Royale. Bridge is simplification for speed.
  • Punto banco / North-American baccarat: house-banked, no skill, fixed draws, 5% commission on Banker (1.06% edge). Mini-baccarat is the same at low stakes. Macau's dominant game — over 90% of revenue. Bridge is casino economics: eliminate skill to speed hands and guarantee edge.

Polysemy and extensions:

  • Casino glamour: "baccarat pit, baccarat whale". Connotes tuxedos, hushed rooms, £100k hands. The bridge is sociology: from aristocratic salon to Vegas high-limit pit, the aura stuck.
  • Argot failure (French 1935): faire baccara = to go bankrupt. The bridge is losing everything at the table generalised to any ruin.
  • Baccarat crystal (homonym): luxury glass from Baccarat, Lorraine (1764). No etymological link to the game beyond spelling; the town name is separate. Confusion is common and commercially convenient.
  • Metaphorical gamble: "diplomatic baccarat". Any all-or-nothing bet on two outcomes. The bridge is structural: Player vs Banker as binary fate.

Usage: uncountable as game ("to play baccarat"), countable for sessions ("a baccarat"). Collocations: play / deal / bank baccarat, baccarat shoe, baccarat table, chemin-de-fer baccarat. Pronunciation RP /ˈbæk.ə.rɑː/ or /ˌbæk.əˈrɑː/, French [bakaʁa]. Register is upscale-leisure; never everyday. To play baccarat in Wodehouse is to announce you have money to lose and time to kill on the Riviera — Aunt Dahlia to perfection.

Strategic and cultural notes: unlike poker, baccarat offers almost no decisions after the bet — Banker draws on 0–2, stands on 7, and follows tableau on 3–6 — so prestige comes not from skill but from stake size and composure. Casinos therefore place it in roped pits with high minima, tuxedoed dealers and ritual shoe-passing. Online and mini versions preserve the tableau automatically. To learn baccarat is to learn casino mathematics in miniature: why 5% commission exists, why Banker (45.86%) beats Player (44.62%) beats Tie (9.52% at 8-to-1), and why Wodehouse chose it — not bridge or poker — to signal Riviera recklessness in a single word.
