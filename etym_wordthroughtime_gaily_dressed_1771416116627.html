- [14th-18th century] - 'Gaily' means brightly, showily, joyfully; 'gaily dressed' describes splendid, colourful clothing in poems and chronicles without a hint of censure.
- [19th-century fashion writing] - The phrase flourishes in novels and fashion columns for festive, brightly coloured dress - cheerful, conspicuous and admired.
- [20th-century semantic collision] - As 'gay' comes to denote homosexuality, the adverb and its compounds sound dated; careful writers avoid them, and 'gaily' retreats to older texts.
- [Contemporary register] - Largely literary and period-flavoured; when used today it deliberately evokes the bright, cheerful clothing of the past.
