Root 1: clair ("clear, bright")
PIE (reconstructed)
*k̑el- / *k̑le-ro-
"to shout, call, sound loudly"
(source of Latin calare, clamare, Greek kalein)
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*klāros
"clear-sounding, loud, distinct"
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE)
clārus
"clear, distinct, audible; bright, illustrious"
↓
Old French (11th c.)
cler / clair
"clear, bright, luminous, transparent"

Root 2: voyance ("sight, seeing, vision")
PIE (reconstructed)
*weyd-
"to see, know"
(ancestor of Sanskrit veda, Greek eidos, Latin videre)
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*widēō
"to see, observe"
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE)
vidēre
"to see, perceive with the eyes, discern"
↓
Old French (10th c.)
veoir
"to see"
↓
Middle French / French (14th–16th c.)
voir + -ance (from Latin -antia)
"the faculty or act of seeing, vision"

Compound Formation & English Adoption
French (late 17th c.–1718; Académie française)
clairvoyance
"acute discernment, penetration, clear-sightedness"
(formed as compound of clair + voyance)
↓
French (Mesmerism / Animal Magnetism) (c. 1784; Puységur)
clairvoyance
"paranormal vision under hypnotic or magnetic trance"
↓
English (c. 1830s–1840s; borrowed directly from French)
clairvoyance
"extrasensory perception of distant or hidden things; intuitive foresight"
