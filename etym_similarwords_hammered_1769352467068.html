• pounded: Similar because both mean repeated heavy blows; differs because pounded can be delivered by fists, waves, or feet with no tool involved, while hammered carries the heritage of the blacksmith's hammer and the forge.
  • beaten: Similar because both describe repeated striking and defeat; differs because beaten is the broader, blunter word — it covers any hitting or losing — while hammered is more percussive and more specific to tool-like blows.
  • battered: Similar because both describe repeated damaging hits; differs because battered implies visible disfigurement and rough wear (a battered car, a battered wife), while hammered stresses the force and rhythm of the blows rather than the resulting damage.
  • forged: Similar because both describe shaping metal by striking; differs because forged emphasises constructive creation — forming an object on the anvil — while hammered can be merely destructive or compulsive.
  • struck: Similar because both name the delivery of a blow; differs because struck is the general, one-off term (a single blow), while hammered implies repeated, rhythmic beating.
  • bashed: Similar because both are violent hitting verbs; differs because bashed is cruder and more colloquial, a blunt smash, while hammered suggests the regular, tool-like rhythm of a hammer.
  • clobbered: Similar because both mean severely hit or defeated; differs because clobbered is slangy and comically heavy (to clobber is to bludgeon), while hammered keeps a precise percussive image.
  • thrashed: Similar because both mean thoroughly beaten in a contest; differs because thrashed literally means flogged with a whip or stick — a swinging blow — while hammered is a downward, pounding blow.
  • smashed: Similar because both are slang for being very drunk; differs because smashed implies complete shattering and collapse, while hammered implies the pounding effects of the drink — both are strong-drunk registers, but with different images.
  • wasted: Similar because both are slang for heavy intoxication; differs because wasted is broader, covering drugs and total incapacitation with a sense of being used up, while hammered keeps the image of being beaten by drink.
  • pelted: Similar because both mean struck repeatedly; differs because pelting is a shower of small missiles (rain, stones, criticism), while hammered is the heavy, single-instrument blow.
