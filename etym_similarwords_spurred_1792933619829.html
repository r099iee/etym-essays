• Goaded: Similar because both drive a beast or person forward with a sharp point; differs because goaded stresses persistent tormenting prods (a goad is a cattle-stick), while spurred is one decisive jab that triggers a burst of speed.
  • Prodded: Similar because both mean jabbed into motion; differs because prodded is gentler and often curious (prodded awake, prodded into answering), while spurred is violent and urgent.
  • Urged: Similar because both press someone toward action; differs because urged works through words and pleading, while spurred works through a sharp stimulus — encouragement with steel in it.
  • Galvanized: Similar because both mean shocked into sudden energetic action; differs because galvanized suggests an electric jolt transforming passivity into motion, while spurred suggests a rider driving an already-willing mount faster.
  • Stimulated: Similar because both name something that quickens activity (spurred/stimulated growth); differs because stimulated is clinical and neutral (stimulated demand, stimulated nerves), while spurred keeps the drama of hoofbeats.
  • Prompted: Similar because both name the trigger of a response; differs because prompted is mild and procedural (prompted by a question), while spurred implies force strong enough to overcome reluctance.
  • Incited: Similar because both drive people to strong action; differs because incited leans toward the unlawful (incited a riot), while spurred is morally neutral — it can spur rescue as readily as revolt.
  • Propelled: Similar because both launch something forward with force; differs because propelled is mechanical (propelled by engines, jet-propelled), while spurred is animal and personal — a living rider behind the thrust.
  • Drove: Similar because both compel onward movement under pressure; differs because drove is broad and continuous (drove cattle, drove himself hard), while spurred is the sharp instant that starts or quickens the drive.
