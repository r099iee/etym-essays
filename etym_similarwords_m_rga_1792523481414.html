• deśī: Similar because both represent the two foundational, interdependent taxonomical categories of traditional Indian music, dance, and cultural performance; differs because deśī denotes regional, vernacular, and ever-evolving folk styles designed primarily for secular sensory enjoyment (*rañjana*), whereas mārga is the timeless, rule-bound, canonical mode revealed by the gods for spiritual purification and liberation (*mokṣa*).
  • patha: Similar because both literally mean "path, road, or route" in Sanskrit; differs because patha is a common, neutral noun for a physical beaten trail or footway, lacking the primordial hunting origins (tracking game) and elevated musicological and aesthetic applications of mārga.
  • vartman: Similar because both denote a path, route, or course traversed by travelers; differs because vartman derives from the root vṛt ("to turn, roll"), originally signifying the circular wheel-rut left by a chariot or an orbital cycle, whereas mārga emphasizes an intentional trail pursued through tracking and seeking.
  • ayana: Similar because both signify a path, course, or progressive motion; differs because ayana emphasizes a broad celestial or seasonal transition (such as Uttarāyaṇa, the northern solar course, or Rāmāyaṇa, the life-journey of Rāma), whereas mārga denotes a codified discipline, practical method, or specific musical road.
  • paddhati: Similar because both denote a pathway, methodology, or systematic procedure; differs because paddhati literally means "foot-stride" (*pad-hati*) and evolved into liturgical manuals, ritual handbooks, or instructional guidebooks, whereas mārga denotes a broader cosmological orientation and aesthetic musical system.
  • rīti: Similar because in Sanskrit literary theory (*Alaṅkāraśāstra*) both terms describe poetic style and artistic diction; differs because rīti (from root rī, "to flow") specifically denotes stylistic flow, syntactic texture, and decorative sound combinations, whereas mārga was Daṇḍin's earlier foundational category and encompasses both music and soteriological philosophies.
  • tariqah: Similar because in mystical traditions (specifically Sufism) tariqah designates the spiritual path or inner road toward divine reality, conceptually mirroring the soteriological usage of mārga in Hindu and Buddhist thought; differs because tariqah is embedded in Semitic Islamic monastic orders, whereas mārga is rooted in Indic Vedic, Buddhist, and musicological systems.
  • dao (tao): Similar because in classical Chinese philosophy Dao represents the cosmic Way, the path of nature, and the disciplined method of mastery; differs because Dao is the primordial, unnameable ontological ground of all reality, whereas mārga is specifically the human track, discipline, or path trodden to reach enlightenment or musical perfection.
  • yāna: Similar because both represent spiritual vehicles or ways leading across the ocean of saṃsāra to enlightenment (as in Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna); differs because yāna emphasizes the vehicle, raft, or vessel that carries the seeker, whereas mārga denotes the actual ground, route, or course trodden.
  • vīthī: Similar because both denote a roadway or pathway in Sanskrit; differs because vīthī specifically refers to an urban street, bazaar lane, market avenue, or a distinct theatrical genre (a one-act street play in the Nāṭyaśāstra), completely lacking the canonical musical prestige of mārga.
  • padavī: Similar because both refer to a path or track; differs because padavī frequently shifts semantically to denote a dignitary's social rank, academic post, official title, or legal status, whereas mārga remains primarily an aesthetic, spiritual, or physical thoroughfare.
  • vidhi: Similar because both represent an authorized, correct method of doing something; differs because vidhi denotes a specific ritual injunction, statutory rule, or cosmic destiny, whereas mārga is the overarching pathway or holistic mode in which that action is situated.
