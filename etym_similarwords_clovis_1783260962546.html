• Charlemagne: Similar because both are monumental Frankish kings at the head of French history; differs because Charlemagne ruled three centuries later (Carolingian Empire, crowned 800) while Clovis founded the Merovingian Frankish kingdom (reigned 481–511).
  • Louis: Similar because both are forms of the same Germanic name "famous in battle"; differs because Louis is the modern French descendant of the name, borne by 18 French kings, while Clovis is the early Frankish form borne by the founding king.
  • Ludwig: Similar because both descend from the same Germanic name *Chlodowig; differs because Ludwig is the German descendant form attached to German royal and cultural figures, while Clovis is the Frankish/French form.
  • Chlodovech / Chlodowig: Similar because both are the reconstructed Frankish forms from which Clovis derives; differs because Chlodovech is the historical Germanic original meaning "famous in battle," while Clovis is its later Latinized and French shape.
  • Merovech: Similar because both belong to the Merovingian dynasty; differs because Merovech is the legendary (possibly mythical) ancestor after whom the dynasty was named, while Clovis is the first historically documented unifier of the Frankish kingdom.
  • Alaric: Similar because both are Germanic founder-kings of the migration period named on this card; differs because Alaric was a Visigoth who sacked Rome in 410, while Clovis was a Frank who converted to Catholic Christianity and founded the kingdom of France.
  • Theodoric: Similar because both are 6th-century Germanic kings in the same roll-call; differs because Theodoric ruled Italy for the Ostrogoths from Ravenna and remained Arian in religion, while Clovis ruled Gaul and converted to Catholic Christianity.
  • Alboin: Similar because both are Germanic dynasty-founders in the continental provinces of the Roman empire, bracketed together on this card; differs because Alboin led the Lombards into Italy in 568, a generation after Clovis united the Franks in Gaul.
