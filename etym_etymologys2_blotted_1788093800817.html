Old Norse (probable source, pre-13th c.)
*blettr
"spot, stain" (not attested in early MSS; cf. Icelandic blettur)

↓

Middle English (c. 1300)
blot (noun)
"a stain, spot, blotch"

↓

Middle English (early 15th c.)
blotten (verb)
"to stain, spot, smudge"

↓

Modern English (16th c. – present)
BLOTTED
"stained or spotted; blotted out: erased, obliterated"

Note: No PIE chain can be reconstructed: the OED judges the origin of Middle English blot obscure, with a Norse connection (blettr) possible but unproven because the form is not attested in early manuscripts. Unrelated to backgammon blot "exposed piece", from Dutch bloot "bare, exposed".
