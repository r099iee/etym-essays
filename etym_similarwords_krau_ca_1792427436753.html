• sārasa: Similar because both refer to large, elegant wading cranes of the Indian subcontinent renowned for romantic fidelity; differs because sārasa is a descriptive Sanskrit term literally meaning "belonging to a lake or lotus-pool" (*saras*), whereas krauñca is an ancient onomatopoeic name highlighting the bird’s distinctive clarion trumpeting call and its sacred status in the foundational myth of poetry.
  • baka: Similar because both denote long-legged, wetland wading birds in Sanskrit literature; differs because baka (herons, egrets, or storks) is frequently used in Indian fables as a symbol of hypocrisy and deceitful asceticism (*baka-dhyāna*, "heron-meditation"), whereas the krauñca is universally celebrated for genuine fidelity, innocence, and noble grief.
  • haṃsa: Similar because both are venerated aquatic birds in classical Sanskrit poetics; differs because the haṃsa (swan or wild goose) symbolizes spiritual discrimination (*viveka*), pure transcendence, and the liberation of the soul from worldly bonds, whereas the krauñca specifically symbolizes conjugal romantic passion, domestic attachment, and tragic earthly pathos.
  • cakravāka: Similar because both are legendary avian pairs celebrated in Indian poetry for profound romantic devotion; differs because the cakravāka (ruddy shelduck) is famous for its tragic nightly separation caused by an ancient curse, calling out across the river until dawn, whereas the krauñca is celebrated for constant physical inseparability during life and devastating grief upon death.
  • mayūra: Similar because both are iconic, sacred birds of the Indian landscape featured prominently in classical poetry; differs because the mayūra (peacock) is associated with rain dances, pride, vibrant iridescent beauty, and martial triumph, whereas the krauñca evokes quiet riparian marshes and tender, poignant pathos.
  • chāṣa: Similar because both are wild birds observed in epic forest hermitages; differs because the chāṣa (the Indian roller or blue jay) is a small arboreal bird admired primarily for its auspicious bright blue plumage, whereas the krauñca is a massive wading bird central to epic narrative and aesthetic theory.
