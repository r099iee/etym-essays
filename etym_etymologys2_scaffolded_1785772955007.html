The word is a Romance (Gallo-Romance) formation of debated internal composition; no single PIE root governs the whole compound. Chain for the platform element: Latin fala "scaffold, wooden tower used in sieges; raised platform, stage" (attested in classical Latin military and theatre vocabulary; earlier origin unknown, possibly Etruscan) → Vulgar Latin *catafalicum "scaffold, raised platform" (reconstructed; cf. Italian catafalco, French catafalque; the compound adds Greek katá "down from, against"; composition debated) → Old North French escafaut / Old French eschafaut "raised wooden platform, staging, stand, execution platform" (12th–13th c.) → Middle English scaffold / scaffalde / scafold "temporary wooden platform for building, viewing, or execution" (c. 1300) → Modern English scaffold (noun; verb to scaffold "to furnish with a scaffold," attested from the same period) → SCAFFOLDED "provided with a scaffold; supported by temporary staging" (past tense, past participle, and participial adjective). Debated points: (1) The internal make-up of *catafalicum is not settled — the second element may be Latin fala or a Germanic borrowing (some connect it to Proto-Germanic *skaft "shaft, pole," source of English shaft), and even the Greek katá element is contested; the OED treats the whole as of uncertain origin. (2) English scaffold entered via Old North French escafaut rather than directly from the Latin form; the -d- of the modern spelling reflects the French consonant structure. (3) Latin fala itself has no secure PIE ancestor.
