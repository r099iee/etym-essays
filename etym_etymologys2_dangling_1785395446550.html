Expressive / echoic base *dang- / *ding- 'to sway, swing to and fro' (imitative of loose motion; NO credible PIE ancestry — the OED classifies the word as probably imitative or expressive in origin) → Old Icelandic dingla 'to dangle, hang loose' (attested in medieval Norse) → Swedish dangla, Norwegian dangla, Danish dangle 'to swing about, dangle' (modern Scandinavian cognates) → English dangle 'to hang loosely and sway' (borrowed from Scandinavian, first recorded 1580s) → dangle + present-participle suffix -ing → DANGLING 'hanging loosely, swinging free' (participial form attested 17th c.).

NOTE ON DEBATE: The exact source dialect is uncertain (Danish, Swedish, or Norwegian all present themselves); the reduplicated formations dingle-dangle, ding-dong, and jingle-jangle are formal parallels sharing the same a/i ablaut, but are not proven cognates; no PIE derivation exists, so the chain begins with the expressive base rather than a reconstructed Indo-European root.
