• Stumbling: Similar because both mean clumsy, tripping movement; differs because stumbling is a trip-and-near-fall, while blundering is blind, careless crashing and error at once.
  • Clumsy: Similar because both mean lacking skill or grace; differs because clumsy is the adjectival state of awkwardness, while blundering is the active participle of clumsy-moving-and-erring.
  • Barging: Similar because both mean pushing through forcibly; differs because barging stresses intrusive, uninvited force, while blundering adds blindness and careless stupidity to the motion.
  • Lurching: Similar because both mean uneven movement; differs because lurching is a swaying, pitching gait, while blundering is blind, noisy, careless progress through obstacles.
  • Erring: Similar because both mean making mistakes; differs because erring is purely cognitive and morally neutral, while blundering keeps the noisy, clumsy physical image attached to the error.
  • Fumbling: Similar because both mean awkward, clumsy action; differs because fumbling is small-scale and often hesitant (fumbling in a pocket), while blundering is large-scale crashing motion.
  • Staggering: Similar because both mean unsteady movement; differs because staggering suggests imbalance or shock (staggering under a load), while blundering suggests blind, careless forward motion.
