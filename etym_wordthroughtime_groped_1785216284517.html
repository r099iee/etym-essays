- [Old English–Middle English: the blind tactile search] - to feel about with the hands when sight fails, the cautious, outstretched probing of darkness or blindness; a plain, homely, physical word.
- [Early Modern–18th century: groping in the dark] - the grand metaphor for ignorance: to grope after truth, knowledge, or meaning is to move tentatively without understanding, the mind's hand stretched into the unknown.
- [19th–20th century: tentative progress] - extended to halting, experimental advance in thought or policy (groping toward a solution); still uncertain, but now the uncertainty of endeavour rather than of panic.
- [Late 20th–21st century: the dominant public sense] - the word narrows and darkens: grope now most often names unwanted sexual touching, and the noun has become a political and legal term, so that the word's old literary innocence can no longer be assumed.
