• collection: Similar because both denote a gathering of multiple individual items; differs because a collection typically implies an orderly grouping of similar or related items gathered intentionally around a specific theme (e.g., coin collection, stamp collection), whereas an assemblage implies a heterogeneous conglomeration of disparate, often unmatching elements brought together into a novel structural whole.
  • gathering: Similar because both describe the coming together of separate entities; differs because a gathering predominantly applies to human beings convening for social, religious, or political occasions and suggests an ephemeral event, whereas assemblage applies equally to physical sculpture, mechanical machinery, archaeological strata, and philosophical systems.
  • collage: Similar because both are revolutionary modern art techniques based on combining disparate fragments; differs fundamentally because a collage is two-dimensional, consisting of paper, fabric, or flat materials pasted onto a planar surface, whereas an assemblage is explicitly three-dimensional, incorporating freestanding physical objects and sculptural relief into space.
  • montage: Similar because both involve the juxtapositional editing of separate components; differs because montage is primarily a temporal, cinematographic, or composite photographic technique relying on rapid sequential transitions or overlapping exposures, rather than physical three-dimensional sculptural mass.
  • bricolage: Similar because both involve creating from leftover or found materials; differs because bricolage describes the improvised process, method, or pragmatic mindset of tinkering and problem-solving with whatever tools are at hand, whereas assemblage denotes the completed tangible work of art or formalized ontological entity.
  • conglomeration: Similar because both describe an accumulation of disparate components; differs because conglomeration carries a connotation of haphazard, chaotic, or clumsy piling together without internal harmony or artistic design, whereas assemblage implies an intentional structural or aesthetic synthesis.
  • agglomeration: Similar because both refer to clusters; differs because agglomeration is an economic, geographic, or geological term describing urban sprawl, population density, or mineral clusters, lacking the artistic, philosophical, and mechanical connotations of assemblage.
  • aggregation: Similar because both denote a composite whole formed from separate parts; differs because aggregation is an abstract statistical or biological term where individual units retain their distinct identity without creating a new expressive synthesis.
