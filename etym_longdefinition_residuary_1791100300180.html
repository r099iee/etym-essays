Residuary is a legal adjective meaning of or concerning the residue — what is left of a dead person's estate after debts, taxes, funeral costs, and specific gifts have been paid. A residuary legatee is the person named to take that remainder; a residuary estate or residuary bequest is the remainder itself. In the Dickens source Scrooge is Marley's sole residuary legatee, meaning after every named legacy is satisfied, everything left falls to Scrooge, a formula that makes him universal successor as well as executor and friend.

Probate practice explains the weight. A competent will gives specific legacies (my watch to John, £500 to Mary), pays creditors, then disposes of the residue, which is often the bulk: houses, investments, and cash not itemised. Without a residuary clause the leftover falls into partial intestacy and passes by statute, causing delay and tax. Hence solicitors insist every will name a residuary beneficiary, often the spouse or children, and alternate residuary beneficiaries in case the first dies. Residuary legatees bear the risk of shrinkage: if debts are large, the residue may be little; if the estate grows, they gain the windfall.

  • Residuary legatee: person taking the remainder; e.g. she was sole residuary legatee of her aunt.
  • Residuary estate / fund: the remainder itself; e.g. the residuary estate was divided equally.
  • Residuary bequest / devise: the clause giving the remainder; e.g. a residuary bequest to charity.
  • Residuary account: executor's final account of residue distributed.
  • Figurative residue: by analogy, any leftover after operations; e.g. residuary powers in constitutional law for what is not otherwise assigned.
  • Contrast with specific and general legacies: specific gifts of named items and general gifts of sums come first; residuary takes last and bears fluctuation.

Register is formal, technical, and almost exclusively legal. Ordinary speech says what's left or the rest; wills say residuary estate. Pronunciation in RP is /rɪˈzɪdjʊəri/ with stress on the second syllable. Collocations are fixed: residuary legatee, beneficiary, estate, gift, clause, trust. Dickens piles sole five times for dirge-like emphasis: sole executor to bury, sole administrator to manage, sole assign to represent, sole residuary legatee to inherit, sole friend and mourner to grieve — one man doing the work of pallbearers, lawyers, heirs, and friends, which tells us Marley died as unloved as Scrooge lives.

Do not confuse residuary with residual, though they share the root. Residual is the general adjective for leftovers in science and finance (residual heat, residual income), while residuary is reserved for wills and constitutional leftovers (residuary powers). Nor is a residuary legatee an heir in strict law: heirs take by intestacy, legatees by will. The bridge from sitting to inheriting is Latin metaphor: what sits back (residuum) after the rush of creditors is the quiet remainder, and residuary names who sits waiting for it.
