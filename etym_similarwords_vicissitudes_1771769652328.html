• Fluctuations: Similar because both denote alternations of condition; differs because fluctuations are quantitative oscillation (prices, moods, markets) without fortune's moral weight or historical grandeur.
  • Reversals: Similar because both denote changes of fortune; differs because a reversal is a single abrupt about-face in outcome, whereas vicissitudes imply a continuing sequence of alternating turns.
  • Mutability: Similar because both name changeableness; differs because mutability is abstract and philosophical — changeability as a principle — while vicissitudes are concrete, episodic, and often unwelcome.
  • Ups and downs: Similar because both name alternations of fortune; differs because "ups and downs" is the colloquial, informal cousin of the classical, formal vicissitudes.
  • Twists and turns: Similar because both stress unpredictable changes of course; differs because it suggests plot-like or narrative surprises, while vicissitudes carry the heavy wheel-of-fortune gravity.
  • Ebb and flow: Similar because both name alternating movement; differs because ebb and flow stresses gradual, rhythmic movement (of tides, markets, or popularity), not the harsh reverses of fortune.
  • Vagaries: Similar because both denote unpredictable changes; differs because vagaries suggest caprice and whim ("the vagaries of fashion"), while vicissitudes suggest fate and hardship.
  • Tribulations: Similar because both denote severe trials; differs because tribulations name the suffering itself, whereas vicissitudes name the turns that bring the suffering.
  • Changes: Similar because both mean alterations of state; differs because changes is the neutral, colourless hyperonym, without vicissitudes' formal, fate-laden resonance.
  • Turns of the wheel: Similar because both evoke fortune's alternations; differs because this is a live metaphor from the wheel of Fortune, while vicissitudes has become a conventional formal noun.
