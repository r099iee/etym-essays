- 18th Century (Scots and Northern English Dialects) - yank emerged in regional speech denoting a quick sharp blow, whip crack, or sudden jerk.
- Early 19th Century (c. 1818, American English) - Flourished in American colloquial vocabulary specifically for a sudden, powerful physical pull or snatch.
- Late 19th to Early 20th Century - Acquired widespread sports jargon usage (especially in baseball for pulling an ineffective pitcher from the game).
- Mid-20th Century - Broadened into broadcasting and entertainment industry slang for abruptly canceling or taking a show off the air.
- Late 20th to 21st Century - Pervasive across everyday global English in both physical and highly figurative contexts (yanking one's chain, yanking a product).
