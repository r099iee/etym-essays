• Stubbornness: Similar because both mean unyielding adherence; differs because it is the plain, everyday, morally neutral synonym, while obstinacy is formal with a darker, more judgmental charge.
  • Obduracy: Similar because both mean hardness of will; differs because obduracy implies a hardening of the heart against what is right, more severe and theological in flavour.
  • Pertinacity: Similar because both mean persistent clinging to a purpose; differs because pertinacity can be admiring ("pertinacity in pursuit of truth"), while obstinacy always resists reason.
  • Tenacity: Similar because both mean firm holding on; differs because tenacity is a positive virtue of gripping strength, while obstinacy is persistence without justification.
  • Perseverance: Similar because both mean continued effort; differs because perseverance is an admired quality of sustained effort through difficulty, with no implication of unreason.
  • Intransigence: Similar because both mean uncompromising refusal; differs because it is the modern political term, often principled and sometimes respectable, while obstinacy insists on unreason.
  • Recalcitrance: Similar because both mean resistance; differs because it suggests open, defiant rebellion against authority, more active and noisier than obstinacy's sullen fixity.
  • Doggedness: Similar because both mean stubborn persistence; differs because it carries an admiring tone of grim determination, while obstinacy is culpable rigidity.
  • Mulishness: Similar because both mean stubborn refusal; differs because it is colloquial and mock-animal, implying foolish, braying obstinacy rather than weighty moral censure.
  • Pig-headedness: Similar because both mean blind stubbornness; differs because it is informal, comic, and contemptuous, without the formal gravity of obstinacy.
