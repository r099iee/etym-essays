• Deduce: Similar because it is the drawing of a conclusion from evidence, the nearest partner of infer in the language of reasoning; differs because deduce is the strictest member — it names an inference that follows by logical necessity from premises, something proven — while infer is looser and more courteous, a reasonable conclusion that usually follows but is not compelled.
  • Conclude: Similar because it means to reach a judgment after reasoning; differs because conclude is the endpoint of any reasoning and can rest on mere opinion, while infer specifically names the carrying of a conclusion forward from given evidence.
  • Imply: Similar because it is infer's constant partner, both describing meaning carried beyond the letter of speech; differs because imply is the speaker's act of suggesting something without stating it, while infer is the hearer's act of drawing the suggestion out — the two are complementary, and confusing them is a famous usage error.
  • Surmise: Similar because it means to form an opinion without full proof; differs because surmise is more speculative and guess-like, while infer is grounded in evidence and reasoning.
  • Presume: Similar because it means to take something as true on evidence; differs because presume often carries the overtone of taking for granted, even of overstepping, while infer is the careful act of reasoning from what is given.
  • Assume: Similar because it means to accept something as a basis for reasoning; differs because assume means to take something as given without proof — to postulate — while infer means to derive something from evidence that is already given.
  • Gather: Similar because it means to understand or conclude from indications; differs because gather is plainer and more everyday, while infer carries the formal logical machinery of premises and conclusions.
  • Reason: Similar because it means to think through from premises to a conclusion; differs because reason is the whole process of connected thought, while infer names the specific act of drawing one conclusion out of it.
  • Extrapolate: Similar because it means to infer beyond the given data; differs because extrapolate is the technical word for extending a known trend or set of data to what lies beyond it, while infer need not project beyond the evidence.
  • Derive: Similar because it means to obtain by reasoning from a source; differs because derive (from Latin de-rivare, "to lead from a stream") emphasises tracing something back to its source, while infer emphasises building a conclusion forward from evidence.
  • Conjecture: Similar because it means to form a judgment on incomplete evidence; differs because conjecture is explicitly uncertain and hypothesis-like, while infer claims a genuine evidential basis, however partial.
  • Construe: Similar because it means to interpret or take the meaning of something; differs because construe is about interpreting a text or act, while infer is about passing from evidence to a conclusion beyond the text.
