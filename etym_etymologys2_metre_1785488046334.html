LINEAR CHAIN (single root word METRE) Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *meh₁- “to measure, to mete out, to distribute” (c. 4000 BCE); the root of the measuring-rod and the counting hand, attested through its many descendants across the family. Stage 2 — Greek μέτρον (métron) “a measure, a rule, a standard; a measured verse” (attested from the Homeric period onward); built from *meh₁- with the instrument suffix *-trom, it already carried the two futures of English metre: the instrument of measurement and the measured line of verse. Stage 3 — Latin metrum “a measure; verse-measure, metre” (1st c. BCE), a direct Greek loan, used by the Roman poets and grammarians (Horace, Quintilian) for the rhythmic scheme of a line. Stage 4 — Old French metre / metr(e) “metre, verse” (10th–12th c.), the Romance descendant of Latin metrum that handed the word on to English. Stage 5 — Middle English metre “poetic measure; verse” (first recorded c. 1350), borrowed through Anglo-Norman and Old French during the period when French supplied much of English literary vocabulary; Chaucer writes of “metre” in the prologue to his Treatise on the Astrolabe. Stage 6 — Modern English metre “the rhythmic structure of verse” (accentual-syllabic in English); in Britain the -re spelling (modelled on Latin metrum) is standard. The same form additionally names the SI unit of length (late 18th c., from the French metric system). The card’s form is the plural METREs. NOTES - The American spelling meter (19th c.) applies spelling reform to both senses; the measuring-instrument word meter (thermometer, gas meter) is a different formation but ultimately the same Greek root through Latin metrum / French -mètre. - The prosodic sense and the length-unit sense are separate historical extensions of the same root: the verse sense is ancient (Greek), while the unit sense is a coinage of the French Revolution (1790s), fixed by the mètre des Archives in 1799. - The Greek word μήτρα (mḗtra) “womb” is a near-homophone but an unrelated root and must not be conflated with μέτρον. - Metronome (French métronome, 1815, literally “measure-law”) is a modern scholarly coinage on the same root, showing the root’s continuing productivity. - The reconstruction *meh₁- (a laryngeal-final root) is standard in modern scholarship (Rix, LIV²); older sources write *mē-.
