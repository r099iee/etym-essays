- Late 1500s archery field — the 'upshot' is the final, decisive arrow of a match: the shot that rises and closes the contest, settling which archer wins.
- Early 1600s courtroom & counting-house — the word migrates into figurative use as the 'last reckoning': the final sum a dispute or account arrives at, the bottom line of a calculation.
- 18th–19th century prose — the term generalises to any process: the 'upshot' names the practical outcome that all the wrangling eventually lands on, carrying a faintly legal, summing-up cadence.
- Contemporary English — an ordinary but slightly archaic-flavoured noun for the practical bottom line of a discussion or event; the archery image (the shot that closes the match) still glimmers behind its flat, verdict-like register.
