• Bun: Similar because it is hair gathered and coiled at the back of the head; differs because a bun is the everyday, unmarked term — high or low, neat or careless — while a chignon is specifically the low nape-knot with a refined, dressed-up connotation.
  • Knot: Similar because it names a twisted mass of gathered hair; differs because a knot is looser and vaguer, without implying the nape position or the careful construction of a chignon.
  • Updo: Similar because it describes hair worn up off the neck; differs because an updo is a broad modern salon term covering every elevated style, while a chignon names a single precise silhouette.
  • French twist: Similar because it is an elegant formal back-of-head style; differs because the French twist rolls the hair vertically into a fold rather than coiling it into a low knot, with its own distinct technique and shape.
  • Topknot: Similar because it is a knot of hair; differs because a topknot sits high on the crown of the head, the exact opposite placement of the low nape chignon.
  • Roll: Similar because it is a rounded mass of hair at the back of the head; differs because a roll is a cylindrical fold rather than a coiled knot, and the term is less refined and less precise.
  • Coiffure: Similar because it denotes an arranged hairstyle, of which the chignon is one example; differs because coiffure is a general French-derived word for any styled hairdo, while chignon specifies the low coil.
  • Ponytail: Similar because it gathers the hair at the back of the head; differs because a ponytail leaves the hair hanging down in a tail rather than coiling it into a knot, and is casual rather than formal.
  • Ballerina bun: Similar because it is a chignon-shaped coil of hair; differs because the ballerina bun is worn high on the crown and is functional stage practice, whereas the chignon is the low, ornament-ready knot of formal dress.
