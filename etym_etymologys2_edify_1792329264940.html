Note on semantic transition: The etymological compound aedificāre (whence also English edifice) was strictly a term of physical masonry and carpentry in Republican Rome, literally meaning "to make a hearth-dwelling or temple." The profound shift toward moral and spiritual instruction occurred in Early Christian theology: translators of the Greek Septuagint and Pauline epistles used Latin aedificāre to render Greek oikodomeō ("to build a house", used by Saint Paul to signify building up the Christian community and strengthening personal character). By the 16th century in English, native Germanic build had reclaimed all physical construction contexts, leaving edify to specialize exclusively in the moral, intellectual, and spiritual realms.
