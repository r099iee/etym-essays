• cage: Similar because both are enclosures for animals; differs because a cage is typically of wire or bars and open to the air all round, while a hutch is a solid wooden box with a wire front — a cage is for birds and zoo animals, a hutch for small pets kept at home.
  • pen: Similar because both are animal enclosures; differs because a pen is usually a large, open fenced area with no roof (a sheep pen), while a hutch is a small, roofed, enclosed box for a single small animal.
  • coop: Similar because both are small wooden housing for domestic animals; differs because a coop is specifically for poultry, raised and roofed like a hutch, while a hutch is for rabbits and pet rodents — the two are neighbours on the farmyard but not interchangeable.
  • run: Similar because both are enclosures for animals; differs because a run is an open, wired exercise area attached to a hutch or coop (a "rabbit run"), while the hutch itself is the enclosed sleeping and shelter box.
  • enclosure: Similar because both are spaces that confine animals; differs because enclosure is the broad, formal term for any fenced or walled area, while hutch is the specific small wooden box.
  • kennel: Similar because both are small shelters for domestic animals; differs because a kennel houses a dog and can range from a small box to a large boarding facility, while a hutch is the rabbit-and-rodent box.
  • warren: Similar because both relate to rabbits; differs because a warren is the rabbits' natural network of underground burrows, or a commercial breeding farm, not a wooden cage.
  • cabinet: Similar because both are pieces of storage furniture; differs because a cabinet is general indoor furniture with doors and drawers, while a hutch specifically has a chest below and open shelves above — a hutch is a kind of cabinet, not the same thing.
  • sideboard: Similar because both are dining-room storage; differs because a sideboard is a low, flat-topped cupboard for serving, while a hutch adds the upper display shelves and often the word "china."
  • chest: Similar because both are wooden storage boxes; differs because a chest is a box with a hinged top lid, freestanding and simple, while a hutch is more elaborate — and today is more likely to be an animal cage.
  • bin: Similar because both are containers for storing things; differs because a bin is an open or lidded container, usually plain and mass-produced, while a hutch is a wooden, roofed structure for animals or furniture.
  • crate: Similar because both are wooden containers; differs because a crate is a rough transport box, open-latticed and disposable, while a hutch is a finished, enclosed shelter.
