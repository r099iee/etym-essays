Expressive / Phonosymbolic Coining (early 18th c.)
*yah-u*
"guttural animal cry or brute grunt"
↓
Early Modern English (1726)
Yahoo
"filthy humanoid brute in Swift"
↓
Modern English (18th–19th c.)
yahoo
"crude, boorish, or loutish person"
↓
Modern English (current)
Yahoos
"plural: uncouth brutes; Swiftian creatures"

Note: Unlike words descending from Proto-Indo-European roots, Yahoo was an ex nihilo literary coinage by Jonathan Swift in Book IV of Gulliver's Travels (1726). Linguistic scholars (e.g., Clark, Pons, Ehrenpreis) observe that it was formed phonosymbolically to mimic a harsh, guttural howl or animal grunt, standing in sharp phonetic opposition to the melodious, equine name of the noble Houyhnhnms (mimicking a horse's whinny). Speculations connecting the word to Hebrew Yahweh or Native American dialects remain unevidenced.
