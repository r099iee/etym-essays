- 1st c. BCE — In Cicero's own letters Terentia is a working partner in property, debts and politics, alternately praised for vigilance and blamed for mismanagement.
- 1st–2nd c. CE — Plutarch and the biographical tradition turn her into a type, the managing wife, and the hostile anecdotes about her temper and her money harden into commonplace.
- 18th c. — Enlightenment lives of Cicero use her as a moral lesson in wifely conduct, citing the letters as evidence of domestic virtue or of its failure.
- 19th c. — Victorian classicists romanticise or condemn her; the word “Terentia” becomes shorthand in biography for the unsympathetic helpmeet of a great man.
- 20th–21st c. — Social and feminist historians recover her as an estate manager and political actor, and the name now signals revisionist writing on Roman family life.
