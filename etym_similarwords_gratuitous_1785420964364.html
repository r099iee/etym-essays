• Unwarranted: Similar because both assert that something lacks justification; differs because unwarranted stresses the absence of a warrant or adequate basis and is common in legal and logical usage, while gratuitous carries the historical echo of "freely given" and a stronger note of moral or aesthetic censure.
  • Uncalled-for: Similar because both describe remarks or behaviour that are unprovoked and unjustified; differs because uncalled-for is the colloquial, spoken-language equivalent, while gratuitous is formal-to-mid and condemns any unnecessary addition, not only behaviour.
  • Unjustified: Similar because both deny that something has a reason; differs because unjustified is a neutral, report-like denial of grounds — a claim or act without basis — while gratuitous implies an addition that was not only ungrounded but actively objectionable.
  • Superfluous: Similar because both describe something more than is needed; differs because superfluous merely states an excess that may be harmless, whereas gratuitous asserts that something was added without cause and carries censure.
  • Needless: Similar because both describe something that could be dispensed with; differs because needless is a plain, practical word for what is not required, while gratuitous adds the sense of being added with no reason at all.
  • Unnecessary: Similar because both describe what is not required; differs because unnecessary is the neutral umbrella term, while gratuitous implies that the thing was not merely unneeded but actively unwarranted, even offensive.
  • Wanton: Similar because both describe acts done without just cause; differs because wanton adds deliberate, unprovoked, often malicious intention (wanton cruelty), whereas gratuitous emphasizes the absence of cause rather than the presence of ill will.
  • Arbitrary: Similar because both describe something not determined by good reason; differs because arbitrary stresses randomness or capricious choice — the result of mere whim — rather than the mere absence of justification.
  • Voluntary: Similar because both descend from the idea of doing something without compulsion; differs because voluntary keeps the positive, neutral sense "done of one's own will," the older sense of gratuitous now largely displaced by the pejorative one.
  • Free / gratis: Similar because all three derive from the Latin idea of giving without payment; differs because free and gratis keep the literal "no charge" sense alive, while gratuitous has mostly migrated to "uncalled-for."
