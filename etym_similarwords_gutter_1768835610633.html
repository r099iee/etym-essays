• sputter: Similar because both describe a flame in distress; differs because sputter emphasises the explosive, spitting noise and irregular bursts of a flame or engine, while guttering is the quieter, more visual wax-flow phenomenon of melting and running.
  • flicker: Similar because both describe a wavering flame; differs because flicker is a rapid variation in brightness, as of a wind-blown flame, without the melt-and-run element of guttering.
  • flare: Similar because both describe a flame's behaviour; differs because flare is to blaze up suddenly and often violently — the very opposite of the dwindling, wasting gutter.
  • smoulder: Similar because both describe a declining burn; differs because smoulder burns slowly without flame, producing smoke, whereas guttering involves a visible spluttering flame drowning in its own melted wax.
  • waver: Similar because both describe unsteadiness; differs because waver is general unsteadiness of any kind — of light, sound, resolve, or decision — while guttering is specifically the candle's uneven melt-and-run failure.
  • splutter: Similar because both describe a flame's choked, irregular burning; differs because splutter, like sputter, stresses the spitting sound and sudden bursts, while gutter names the slower wax-channel process and its visual wasting.
