• vrīhi: Similar because both are ancient Sanskrit terms designating domesticated rice (Oryza sativa); differs because vrīhi refers specifically to coarse, fast-maturing autumn rice sown directly by broadcasting, whereas śāli designates high-grade, winter-harvested transplanted rice possessing superior culinary and medicinal status.
  • ṣaṣṭika: Similar because both are cultivars of rice recognized in ancient Indian agricultural science; differs because ṣaṣṭika is a rapid-maturing rice harvested in sixty days during hot weather, whereas śāli is a slow-maturing winter crop requiring meticulous aquatic paddy transplantation.
  • dhānya: Similar because both refer to agricultural grains; differs because dhānya is an overarching, generic Sanskrit term for all cereals, grains, and agricultural produce in general (or unhusked paddy), whereas śāli specifically denotes the elite winter rice variety.
  • taṇḍula: Similar because both denote rice; differs because taṇḍula designates rice grains that have already been threshed, dehusked, and winnowed ready for the cooking pot, whereas śāli refers to the whole living plant, the field crop, or unhusked grain.
  • akṣata: Similar because both involve sacred rice in Hindu rituals; differs because akṣata refers specifically to whole, unbroken rice grains consecrated with turmeric, vermilion, or holy water for ritual worship and blessings, whereas śāli is the botanical and agricultural taxon.
  • bhatta: Similar because both represent edible rice; differs because bhatta (Prakrit/Sanskrit bhakta) refers specifically to boiled or cooked rice served as a meal, whereas śāli is the raw agricultural crop.
  • paddy: Similar because both refer to wetland rice crops grown in flooded fields; differs because paddy is a modern English term (borrowed from Malay padi) denoting any unhusked rice grain or flooded rice field, lacking the deep Ayurvedic taxonomy and literary elegance of śāli.
  • basmati: Similar because both refer to premium, long-grain, aromatic Indian rice varieties; differs because basmati (literally "fragrant") is a modern commercial cultivar designation developed over recent centuries, whereas śāli is the ancient classical taxonomic category encompassing hundreds of historic indigenous varieties.
