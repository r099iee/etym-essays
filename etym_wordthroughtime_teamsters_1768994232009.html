teamster(s)
- 1777 — "teamster" is recorded: one who drives a team, from team + the agent suffix -ster (once feminine, as in spinster, later generalized to masculine agents like gamester and trickster).
- 19th-century American frontier — the teamster is the linchpin of overland freight: the man of the six-mule team and the great wagon, whose moustaches, widebrim hat, and low round crown mark the trade like a uniform; the word names a class, rough, practical, weather-beaten.
- 1903 — the drivers organize into the International Brotherhood of Teamsters, institutionalizing the name as the great American freight union.
- Contemporary — a "teamster" is commonly a truck driver and union member; the animal team survives only in the word's own history, as the occupational label passes from the mule to the motor.
