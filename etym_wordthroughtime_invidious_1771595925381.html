- [17th century what stirs envy] - [Enters English describing what provokes envy or ill-will in others; connotation focuses on the rankled feeling of the onlooker]
- [18th century unfair discrimination] - [Shifts toward the quality of a judgment that discriminates unfairly between persons — the sting is in the inequality; enters legal phrase 'invidious distinction']
- [19th century critical polemic] - [Ruskin anticipates that his singling out of Turner will 'sound invidious' — woundingly partial, offensively preferential; connotation wary, forensic, self-aware]
- [20th-21st century legal and political] - ['Invidious comparison' and 'invidious distinction' remain alive in law and political speech, now almost always naming judgments that rank or single out people unfairly; the resentment it stirs is the core of its meaning]
