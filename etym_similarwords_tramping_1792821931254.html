• Hiking: Similar because both mean long walks in the countryside for pleasure; differs because hiking is the modern leisure-industry word (gear, trails, apps), while tramping is older, heavier, and muddier, with the boot-thud still audible.
  • Rambling: Similar because both describe leisurely countryside walking, often in company; differs because rambling suggests gentle wandering with pauses for views and pubs, while tramping suggests steadier, heavier, more purposeful mileage.
  • Trekking: Similar because both mean sustained walking over distance, often rough ground; differs because trekking implies expedition and hardship (Nepal, deserts), while tramping can be a mere afternoon across familiar fields.
  • Marching: Similar because both involve rhythmic heavy steps over distance; differs because marching is disciplined and collective (soldiers, bands, keeping time), while tramping is looser, wearier, and individual.
  • Stomping: Similar because both name heavy noisy footfalls; differs because stomping stresses anger or emphasis (stomping off, stomping grapes), while tramping stresses steady onward travel.
  • Trudging: Similar because both picture weary heavy walking; differs because trudging foregrounds exhaustion and reluctance (trudging through snow), while tramping can be cheerful and companionable.
  • Wandering: Similar because in the source sentence Frodo both tramps and wanders; differs because wandering is aimless and solitary (no route, no timetable), while tramping goes somewhere, even if only round the Shire and back.
  • Roaming: Similar because both describe covering ground freely on foot; differs because roaming is wide-ranging and animal-like (hills, moors, no path needed), while tramping keeps to the road or track with its thudding beat.
  • Footslogging: Similar because both mean dogged walking mile after mile; differs because footslogging is deliberately comic-military slang for the grind itself, while tramping is the older plain word that can still sound romantic.
