• Ages: Similar because both name a very long period of time; differs because "an age" is loose and can mean a human lifetime or a geological era, while aeons marks a scale so vast it dwarfs all human history and cannot be counted.
  • Eras: Similar because both name a large span of time; differs because an era is a formal geological unit, a subdivision of an eon, smaller and strictly ordered, whereas aeons is the loose, maximal, uncountable unit.
  • Epochs: Similar because both name major divisions of time; differs because an epoch is a technical geological unit smaller than an era, precisely defined, while aeons is deliberately vast and uncountable.
  • Millennia: Similar because both indicate a very long span; differs because millennia are exact and countable (thousands of years), too human-scaled for cosmic antiquity, while aeons need not be finite or countable at all.
  • Centuries: Similar because both are periods of time; differs because a century is a short, ordinary, countable unit (a hundred years), utterly dwarfed by the scale aeons commands.
  • Eternity: Similar because both suggest time beyond ordinary measure; differs because eternity is time without end, infinite and heavily theological, while an aeon is an enormously long but finite age, usable in geological and planetary contexts.
  • Forever: Similar because both suggest vast duration; differs because forever is an adverb of infinite continuation, not a noun-named span, and lacks the formal, scientific weight of aeons.
  • Infinity: Similar because both exceed ordinary measure; differs because infinity is a mathematical and philosophical concept of unboundedness, while aeons remains a unit of finite, if immense, time.
