- 18th c. - Adopted into English from French as both a noun (*fête*) and verb (*to fête*), initially restricted to aristocratic circles meaning 'to entertain guests at a lavish outdoor festival, masquerade, or grand gala.'
- Early 19th c. - Shifted to mean publicly honoring a distinguished dignitary, military hero, or royal visitor with formal civic banquets and commemorative ceremonies.
- Late 19th–20th c. - Broadened to the cultural and artistic sphere, describing acclaimed authors, painters, opera singers, and Hollywood celebrities being welcomed, hosted, and showered with praise during publicity tours.
- 21st c. - Pervasively used in cultural journalism, literary criticism, and film reviews to describe works of art or creators receiving prestigious awards and widespread critical adoration (sometimes with a subtle, wry note regarding the fleeting nature of cultural trends).
