• Glass: Similar because both denote a plain drinking vessel; differs because glass is the generic, material-based term for any drinking vessel, while tumbler specifies shape (stemless, flat-bottomed) and also names an acrobat, a lock part, a bird, and a machine.
  • Goblet: Similar because both are drinking vessels; differs because a goblet has a stem and foot and a wine-serving, ceremonial register, whereas a tumbler is stemless and utilitarian.
  • Beaker: Similar as a flat-bottomed cylindrical vessel; differs because beaker is primarily laboratory glassware or a child's sipping cup and never denotes acrobats or locks.
  • Cup: Similar as a generic drinking vessel; differs because a cup typically has a handle, a saucer culture, and ceramic or metal composition, while a tumbler is handleless glass.
  • Chalice: Similar as a ritual drinking vessel; differs in its liturgical, ornate register — a tumbler is secular and plain.
  • Acrobat: Similar to the acrobat sense of tumbler; differs because acrobat implies trained showmanship across disciplines (tightrope, trapeze), while a tumbler specifically performs ground somersaults and flips — and the word has four unrelated senses.
  • Gymnast: Similar as an athletic performer; differs because gymnast denotes a formal apparatus athlete (Olympic sport), while tumbler is the folk- and street-performer sense.
  • Contortionist: Similar as a circus entertainer; differs by its focus on flexible posing rather than rolling motion.
  • Tumbler pigeon / Pouter / Carrier: Similar within pigeon-fancier vocabulary as domestic breeds; differs because the tumbler breed is defined by its backward-somersaulting flight, not by throat inflation (pouter) or message-carrying (carrier).
  • Lever / Pin (locksmithing): Similar mechanical parts in locks; differs because lever is the generic moving piece, while tumbler is the specific pivoting element that must be lifted to a precise height by the key, and in modern cylinder locks the pin tumbler is a spring-loaded pin set.
