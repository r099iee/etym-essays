PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*gʰalgʰ- or *gʰalgh-
"long slender branch, flexible rod, pole, stake"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE)
*galgô
"pole, stake, cross, gallows for hanging"
↓
Old English (c. 8th–10th c.)
gealga / galga
"cross, execution stake, gallows"
Note: In Old English poetry, also used for the Christian cross upon which Christ was hung.
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
galwes / galowes
"gallows, execution frame (plural used as singular)"
Note: Shifted to plural form galwes reflecting the two or three wooden posts comprising the structure.
↓
Early Modern English (c. 15th–17th c.)
gallowes / gallows
"apparatus for execution by hanging"
↓
Modern English (present)
GALLOWS
