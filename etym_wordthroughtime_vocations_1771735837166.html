- [15th century the divine call] - [Vocation enters as a religious term — the summons of God to a religious life; connotation sacred and individual, a call heard within]
- [16th century the Protestant widening] - [The Reformation broadens it: every honest profession is a vocation — one's calling in daily work — so ordinary labour gains sanctity; connotation of consecrated work]
- [19th century professionalization] - [Applied to the specialized scholarly callings that grew up within the discipline of history — the archaeologist-historian, the economic historian; connotation of institutionalization combined with consecration]
- [20th-21st century aptitude and role] - [Vocation now chiefly means a strong feeling of suitability for a career; the plural 'vocations' names the distinct professional roles of a field, retaining a faint echo of being summoned]
