• rule: Similar because both prescribe standards of behavior; differs because a rule can be arbitrary, purely mechanical, or casual (e.g., rules of a card game, traffic rules), whereas a precept carries moral, philosophical, spiritual, or legal authority aimed at ethical conduct or deep wisdom.
  • principle: Similar because both serve as fundamental guides to action; differs because a principle is often an abstract, underlying truth or foundational value (e.g., the principle of conservation of energy, principles of justice), whereas a precept is an explicit, actionable instruction or practical commandment.
  • maxim: Similar because both express concise moral guidance; differs because a maxim is typically an aphoristic, pithy saying or personal motto (e.g., "waste not, want not"), whereas a precept is an authoritative, often institutional or sacred injunction.
  • commandment: Similar because both are authoritative ethical orders; differs because a commandment possesses absolute, divine, or sovereign imperativeness backed by punitive religious sanctions (e.g., the Ten Commandments), whereas a precept functions more as a guiding rule of discipline, wisdom, or practical regulation.
  • tenet: Similar because both represent core components of a belief system; differs because a tenet is an article of faith or doctrine that one holds to be true (what one believes), whereas a precept is a behavioral instruction (how one must act).
  • ordinance: Similar because both represent authoritative decrees; differs because an ordinance is specifically an authoritative civic decree, municipal statute, or public ecclesiastical law, whereas a precept extends widely to personal ethical maxims and internal moral standards.
  • canon: Similar because both establish recognized standards; differs because a canon refers to an entire body of authoritative law, sacred scripture, or aesthetic standards against which things are judged, whereas a precept is a single operational rule or directive within such a system.
  • dictum: Similar because both convey authoritative guidance; differs because a dictum is a formal authoritative pronouncement or judge's passing opinion, whereas a precept is an enduring behavioral rule designed for regular obedience.
