- 1490s–1520s legal title - conquistador as honourable Crown licensee entitled to conquer and settle, positive and aspirational in Castile
- 1520s–1550s fearsome reputation - after Mexico and Peru, conquistadores means irresistible steel, horses, and gold-hunger in both Spanish triumph and Indigenous catastrophe
- 1550s–1700s Black Legend and heroic chronicle - Las Casas denounces cruelty while court chroniclers glorify daring, splitting perception into villains vs. heroes
- 1800s romantic adventurers - independence-era and Anglo histories recast them as bold explorers, morions and galleons picturesque
- 1900s academic conquistadores - historians stress contracts, allies, disease, and institutions over lone heroes, making the word analytical
- Present contested memory - statues fall and curricula debate: conquistadores as founders or perpetrators, with the Spanish -es plural often kept to mark distance
