• Bedspread: Similar because both name the outer covering of a bed; differs because bedspread is the modern everyday term and usually covers more (often reaching to the floor and covering the pillows), while coverlet is lighter, more decorative, and more literary.
  • Quilt: Similar because both are bed coverings laid over the bed; differs because a quilt is a padded, stitched layer with filling, made chiefly for warmth, whereas a coverlet is usually a thinner top cloth whose main job is display or finish.
  • Counterpane: Similar because it is the historical and literary near-synonym for the same decorative bed-cover; differs because counterpane is now largely archaic and carries a stronger period flavour, while coverlet remains in current, if slightly literary, use.
  • Blanket: Similar because both are bed coverings; differs because a blanket is principally a functional warmth layer, often woollen and plain, while a coverlet is primarily decorative and lighter.
  • Duvet: Similar because both cover the bed; differs because a duvet is a thick filled bag usually enclosed in a removable cover, while a coverlet is a thin flat top layer without filling.
  • Comforter: Similar because both are bed covers; differs because a comforter is a thick, bulky, filled cover, while a coverlet is thin and built for lightness or decoration.
  • Throw: Similar because both are decorative covers used on a bed; differs because a throw is a small blanket meant for draping (often on a sofa), not a full bed-covering, while a coverlet covers the whole bed.
  • Afghan: Similar because both are decorative bed covers; differs because an afghan is a knitted or crocheted coverlet-style throw, handmade in style, whereas a coverlet is generally woven or quilted and not restricted to that technique.
  • Cover: Similar because both derive from the idea of covering something; differs because cover is the generic verb and noun for putting something over anything, while coverlet is specifically a bed-covering textile with a fixed noun form.
