- [Original sense (16th c.)] - A soft cushion, wad, or bundle of straw and cloth used for lying on or for protection and stuffing; a plain, humble domestic word with no stigma.
- [Anatomical extension (17th–19th c.)] - Extended to any soft shock-absorbing cushion, including the elastic fleshy underparts of animal feet; in natural-history and rural writing the foot-pad sense becomes standard and purely descriptive, the neutral register Steinbeck draws on.
- [20th c. broadening & slang turns] - "Pad" multiplies: a notepad, shoulder pad, landing pad, mouse pad — any flat cushion-like surface — while slang converts it into a bed and then one's room or apartment ("crash pad"), and "to pad" (a bill, a speech) gains a dishonest, stuffing connotation.
- [Contemporary sense] - The animal foot-pad meaning remains the standard zoological one, keeping its neutral, physical register; in daily speech "pads" still names soft cushioning or flat blocks, alongside the informal, faintly dated slang sense of a room or dwelling.
