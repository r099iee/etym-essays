• hanging: Similar because both describe something supported from above with the rest free; differs because hanging is the neutral, static state of being fixed or suspended, whereas dangling implies a loose end free to sway at the slightest movement.
  • suspended: Similar because both denote something held up with the lower part free; differs because suspended is the formal, technical word suggesting engineering, ceremony, or deliberate fixing, whereas dangling is informal, slack, and weight-driven.
  • swinging: Similar because both involve loose, pendular motion; differs because swinging is active back-and-forth movement through an arc, whereas dangling is primarily the state of hanging slack, with sway only potential until disturbed.
  • trailing: Similar because both describe something long and loose extending from a point; differs because trailing means dragging along behind or in contact with something else, whereas dangling hangs free and clear — Gollum's feet dangle, they do not trail in the water.
  • hovering: Similar because both suggest a thing held up without fixed support; differs because hovering implies hanging in the air unsupported, as a bird or craft, whereas dangling always has one end attached.
  • lolling: Similar because both describe hanging slackly; differs because lolling adds limpness, sprawl, or unrestraint (a lolling tongue), whereas dangling is a neutral, gravity-driven slackness without the suggestion of abandonment.
  • drooping: Similar because both mean hanging downward; differs because drooping implies bending or wilting from weakness, weight, or fatigue, whereas dangling is a straight, free-hanging suspension ready to sway.
  • sagging: Similar because both describe a downward hang; differs because sagging implies a loss of tautness or support under weight in the middle, whereas dangling hangs freely from a single attachment point at one end.
