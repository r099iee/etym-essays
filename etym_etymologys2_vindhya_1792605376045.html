Note on philological derivation: Traditional Sanskrit lexicographers and grammarians (such as Amarasiṁha in the Amarakośa and the commentator Mallinātha) analyze Vindhya as an agentive derivation from the verbal root vidh- / vyadh- ("to pierce, divide, obstruct") combined with the affix -ya: vindhati deśān ("that which divides the countries") or vindhati sūryasya mārgaṁ ("that which obstructs the path of the sun"). Western comparative philologists connect this to the Proto-Indo-European root *weydʰ- ("to divide, separate"), which developed in the Italic branch into Latin dīvidere (source of English divide, division, and individual) and in Germanic into words like Old English widuwe ("widow", literally "the woman separated from her spouse").
