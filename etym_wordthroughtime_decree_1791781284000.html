- 14th c. — the word establishes itself in English as the term for an ordinance of a sovereign or a church authority; it carries the weight of unmovable command.
- 15th–17th c. — it expands into equity and canon law: a decree becomes the final order of a court of chancery, and "the decree of God" a standard providential phrase.
- 18th–19th c. — the vocabulary of absolutism and revolution: "rule by decree" marks tyranny in liberal polemic, and Napoleon's decrees give the word fresh publicity.
- 1873–75 — the Judicature Acts in England standardise "judgment", and "decree" retreats to matrimonial, ecclesiastical and Scottish usage.
- 20th–21st c. — revived by executive and emergency rule and by the US consent decree; in journalism it is now a synonym for diktat, while in law it remains technical and precise.
