• Hanuman langur: Similar because both are widespread Indian primates prominent in Hindu folklore and temple settings; differs because the Hanuman langur (Semnopithecus entellus) is a slender, long-tailed colobine monkey with a black face and pale silvery-gray coat that feeds primarily on leaves in arboreal habitats, whereas the Rhesus macaque is a stockier, omnivorous cercopithecine monkey with a pinkish face, medium-length tail, and aggressive terrestrial foraging behavior.
  • bonnet macaque: Similar because both belong to the genus Macaca and thrive alongside human settlements in India; differs because the bonnet macaque (Macaca radiata) is endemic to southern peninsular India and easily recognized by a circular whorl or cap of radiating hair on its crown, whereas the Rhesus macaque dominates northern and central India and lacks the whorled crown.
  • Barbary macaque: Similar because both are hardy, highly adaptable species of the genus Macaca; differs because the Barbary macaque (Macaca sylvanus) is native to the Atlas Mountains of North Africa and Gibraltar, possesses almost no tail (vestigial), and lives in temperate cedar forests, whereas the Rhesus macaque is Asian and retains a functional tail.
  • cynomolgus monkey: Similar because both are widely used Asian macaques in biomedical research; differs because the cynomolgus monkey or crab-eating macaque (Macaca fascicularis) has a noticeably longer tail (exceeding body length), prefers coastal and mangrove habitats in Southeast Asia, and has darker facial skin.
  • baboon: Similar because both are cercopithecine Old World primates with strong sexual dimorphism, complex troop hierarchies, and terrestrial habits; differs because baboons (genus Papio) are much larger, possess elongated dog-like muzzles, and are native exclusively to Africa and the Arabian peninsula.
  • Japanese macaque: Similar because both are northern-adapted, highly intelligent Asian macaques with pink faces and dense fur; differs because the Japanese macaque (Macaca fuscata, the famous "snow monkey") is endemic to the Japanese archipelago, has a shorter stub tail, and exhibits specialized thermal spring bathing behaviors in sub-zero alpine climates.
  • chimpanzee: Similar because both have been essential primate model organisms in biomedical and cognitive science; differs because the chimpanzee (Pan troglodytes) is a tailless hominid ape native to Africa, possessing vastly greater brain volume, complex tool-making abilities, and closer genetic proximity to humans.
  • lion-tailed macaque: Similar because both belong to the genus Macaca in India; differs because the lion-tailed macaque (Macaca silenus) is an endangered, shy arboreal specialist endemic to the rainforests of the Western Ghats with a striking silver-white mane and tufted tail, whereas the Rhesus macaque is an ecologically versatile generalist.
