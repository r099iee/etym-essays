Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*ad- + *bʰleigʷ-
"to, toward"
+ "to strike, beat"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*ad- + *flīgō
"to, toward"
+ "to dash, strike down"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
afflīgere (pp. afflīctus)
"to dash against, overthrow,"
"to ruin, weaken, torment"
↓
Old French (c. 13th c.)
aflit / aflicter
"tormented, distressed"
↓
Middle English (c. 1390s)
afflicten (pp. afflicted)
"troubled, cast down, tormented"
↓
Modern English (c. 16th c.)
afflicted
"troubled by disease or grief;"
"burdened with an imperfection"

Note: Latin afflīctus is the perfect passive participle of afflīgere (ad- 'to' + flīgere 'to strike down, beat'). Flīgere is cognate with Greek θλίβω (thlībō, 'to squeeze, press, afflict').
