• Tracery: Similar in naming open, ornamental interlacing patterns; differs because tracery is the architectural openwork of Gothic windows, carved in stone, while filigree is worked in fine metal wire.
  • Lace: Similar in the delicate, open, threadlike appearance; differs because lace is a fabric of fibre, woven or knotted, while filigree is constructed of soldered metal strands.
  • Fretwork: Similar in being intricate decorative openwork; differs because fretwork is cut from a solid sheet of wood or metal with a saw, while filigree is built up strand by strand from wire.
  • Latticework: Similar in being a patterned open structure; differs because latticework is a regular criss-cross grid of bars, functional as much as ornamental, while filigree is free-form and purely decorative.
  • Granulation: Similar as a metalworking decoration of tiny grains fused to a surface; differs because granulation uses beads welded flat on the metal, while filigree is built from wire into an open three-dimensional web.
  • Damascening: Similar as a fine ornamental metalwork technique; differs because damascening inlays contrasting gold or wire into etched steel, while filigree is freestanding openwork made of wire itself.
  • Repoussé: Similar as a jeweller’s decorative technique; differs because repoussé pushes metal outward from behind into raised relief on a solid sheet, while filigree leaves the piece largely empty space.
  • Openwork: Similar in describing any work with deliberate holes or open spaces; differs because openwork is a general category (including carved or pierced work), while filigree specifies wire-based tracery.
