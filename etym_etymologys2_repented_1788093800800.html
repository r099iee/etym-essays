Latin (attested from 2nd c. BCE)
paenitēre — impersonal paenitet
"to cause regret; it grieves, it repents (me)"
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 4th–8th c. CE, reconstructed)
*penitīre
"to regret, to be sorry" (ae > e)
↓
Old French (attested 11th c.)
repentir (se repentir)
"to repent; re- 'again' + pentir 'to feel regret'"
↓
Anglo-French / Middle English (early 14th c.)
repenten
"to feel regret or remorse, esp. for sin"
↓
Modern English — regular weak form:
repented
"past: felt remorse, regretted"

Note: Latin paenitēre has no securely attested PIE etymon; it is often
connected with the adverb paene "almost, nearly" (itself of uncertain origin),
on the idea of "not quite what one might wish", and it was secondarily associated
with poena "punishment, penalty" (cf. penance, penitence). The re- prefix is
Latin re- "again, in reverse". Stage to PIE: not securely reconstructable.
