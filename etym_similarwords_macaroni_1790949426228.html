• Dandy: Similar because both denote an excessively well-dressed man preoccupied with elegance and high fashion; differs because 'dandy' is a broader, long-lived 19th-century term (epitomized by Beau Brummell) emphasizing immaculate tailoring, restraint, and understated poise, whereas 'macaroni' specifically refers to the flamboyant, exaggerated 18th-century craze for towering Italianate wigs and extravagant mannerisms.
  • Fop: Similar because both refer to a vain, fashion-obsessed man who behaves with affected refinement; differs because 'fop' carries a sharper moral critique of foolish vanity, empty-headedness, and silly posturing across Renaissance to Victorian eras, whereas 'macaroni' was a specific subcultural movement tied to the Grand Tour.
  • Beau: Similar because both designate an attractive, fashionable gentleman who frequents polite society; differs because 'beau' primarily emphasizes courtly charm, romantic gallantry, and suitor status, without the eccentric excesses of the macaroni.
  • Buck: Similar because both were prominent 18th-century terms for fashionable young men about town; differs because a 'buck' was characterized by boisterous masculinity, sporting prowess, high spirits, and daring escapades, whereas a 'macaroni' was characterized by delicate, effeminate Continental aesthetics.
  • Popinjay: Similar because both mock men who strut about in garish, showy attire; differs because 'popinjay' (originally a parrot) emphasizes annoying, vain chatter and strutting conceitedness across many centuries, whereas 'macaroni' focuses on high-fashion cosmopolitan affectation.
  • Coxcomb: Similar because both describe a conceited show-off obsessed with his outward appearance; differs because 'coxcomb' specifically derives from a jester's cap and denotes foolish, shallow vanity and pretension.
  • Peacock: Similar because both serve as metaphors for individuals flaunting extravagant plumage or wardrobe; differs because 'peacock' is a general metaphor applied to anyone vainly displaying colorful plumage, whereas 'macaroni' is an exact historical socio-cultural designation.
  • Dude: Similar because both originally referred to an exaggeratedly overdressed dandy; differs because 'dude' originated in late 19th-century New York slang for fastidious city dressers before shifting into a generic modern informal address for any male.
