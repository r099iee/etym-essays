• Nineveh: Similar because both were colossal Mesopotamian imperial capitals celebrated for their wealth, architectural magnificence, and prominent biblical condemnation; differs because Nineveh was the northern Tigris-based capital of the Assyrian military machine known for swift desolation in 612 BCE, whereas Babylon was the southern Euphrates-based center of culture, astronomy, law, and long-lasting religious prestige.
  • Rome: Similar because both were massive imperial metropolises that conquered Judea, destroyed the Jerusalem Temple, and became equated in apocalyptic literature with tyrannical worldly corruption; differs because Rome became the enduring administrative and legal foundation of Western Europe and the seat of the Catholic Church, whereas ancient Babylon remained an archaeological ruin after the Hellenistic period.
  • Sodom: Similar because both serve as proverbial biblical watchwords for extreme moral depravity and decadent sin; differs because Sodom is characterized purely by sexual and moral degeneracy leading to catastrophic divine fire, whereas Babylon represents organized imperial pride, political tyranny, wealth, and cultural sophistication.
  • Megalopolis: Similar because both describe massive, densely populated urban centers characterized by vast diversity and commercial frenzy; differs because megalopolis is a neutral sociological and geographical term for an urban corridor, whereas Babylon carries heavy moral, historical, and mythological connotations.
  • Vanity Fair: Similar because both are literary archetypes representing the hollow, materialistic distractions and ethical compromises of worldly commercial life; differs because Vanity Fair is an allegorical market from Bunyan's The Pilgrim's Progress, whereas Babylon is a real historical empire imbued with biblical prophecy.
  • Gotham: Similar because both are used as dramatic nicknames or literary avatars for massive, dark, complex, and crime-ridden urban landscapes; differs because Gotham is specifically associated with New York City folklore and comic fiction, whereas Babylon carries millennia of theological weight.
