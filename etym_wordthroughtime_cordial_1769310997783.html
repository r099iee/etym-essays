- Of the heart (14th c.) - Imported from Old French as a medical word meaning 'of the heart' or 'heart-reviving'; a cordial is a restorative medicine that stirs the heart back to strength.
- The heart-restoring drink (15th c.) - The noun solidifies as a sweet liqueur or restorative cordial, literally something that revives the heart; the word's anatomy, Latin cor 'heart,' stays visible on its sleeve.
- Heartfelt and sincere (16th-17th c.) - The adjective drifts from the physical organ to feeling: genuine, heart-felt warmth in manner and word, still formal and courtly.
- Warm and friendly (17th c. onward) - Settles into the modern sense of sincere, heart-rooted friendliness; Austen's 'no very cordial feelings' measures the coldness of withheld heart-warmth with devastating precision.
