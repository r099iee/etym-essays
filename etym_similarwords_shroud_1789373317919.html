• Pall: Similar because both denote a heavy covering associated with death, mourning, darkness, or burial; differs because a pall is specifically the heavy velvet or cloth draped over a coffin or tomb (or metaphorically a gloomy, suffocating atmosphere like a "pall of smoke"), whereas a shroud is the winding-sheet wrapped directly around the deceased's body, or an obscuring mantle that conceals completely.
  • Cerement: Similar because both refer specifically to funerary grave-clothes used to envelop a corpse; differs because cerement (from Latin cēra, wax) historically denotes a waxed or greased cloth used to wrap embalmed corpses to exclude air and retard decay, with an archaic, highly gothic register, whereas shroud is the universal English term for any burial sheet or concealing veil.
  • Winding-sheet: Similar because both designate the sheet or garment in which a corpse is wrapped before burial; differs because winding-sheet is a literal, descriptive Germanic compound that emphasizes the manual spiraling or wrapping of fabric around the corpse, whereas shroud carries extensive poetic, figurative, nautical, and engineering polysemy.
  • Veil: Similar because both describe a flexible textile or mist that conceals something from sight; differs because a veil is typically lightweight, delicate, semi-transparent, and often worn by the living for modesty, mourning, or bridal ceremony, allowing partial vision, whereas a shroud denotes complete, opaque envelopment, isolation, and mortuary burial.
  • Mantle: Similar because both denote an enveloping cover, cloak, or layer that conceals what lies beneath; differs because a mantle is primarily a loose, dignified sleeveless cloak worn by royalty or scholars, or a geological layer (Earth's mantle), symbolizing protective authority, warmth, or power ("assume the mantle"), whereas shroud evokes mortality, dread, secrecy, and funerary burial.
  • Cloak: Similar because both act as protective outer coverings that conceal identity or truth ("cloak and dagger"); differs because cloak is an active, wearable outerwear garment that can be easily discarded or wrapped at will, whereas shroud emphasizes immobility, finality, and entombment.
