• Bracing: Similar because both denote preparing oneself for strain or effort; differs because bracing is fully modern and internalised (one braces the body/mind), while girding keeps a faint image of the belt and the garment, and is archaising.
  • Steeling: Similar because both express hardening oneself against a trial; differs because steeling suggests emotional callusing (steel = hard metal) whereas girding suggests readiness to move and act, not hardness.
  • Preparing: Similar as the general verb for getting ready; differs in that preparing is neutral and comprehensive (prepares everything), while girding is specific — the self, the loins, the sword — and stylistically high.
  • Encircling: Similar because both mean surrounding on all sides and overlap in landscape prose ("girdling/encircling walls"); differs because encircling is purely spatial and carries no preparation sense.
  • Belting: Similar because both name the act of fastening with a belt; differs because belting is modern, technical, or colloquial (even "striking"), without the biblical and ceremonial dignity of girding.
  • Girdling: Similar etymologically and spatially (an encirclement, the ring-barking sense in arboriculture); differs because girdling has specialised to "cutting a ring of bark" and "surrounding a tree", a narrower concrete sense.
  • Banding / Binding: Similar because both mean tying round with a strip; differs because binding is generic (books, bundles, prisoners) while girding always concerns the body or the enclosing of a place in elevated style.
  • Fortifying: Similar as a figurative sense of strengthening a position or resolve; differs because fortifying presupposes a fortress or defence one builds around oneself, whereas girding presupposes garments one fastens before moving.
  • Arming: Similar because both can mean equipping oneself for combat and share the "gird on the sword" image; differs because arming is military and physical, girding has outgrown the purely military into moral preparation.
  • Steeling the nerves / psyching up (colloquial): Similar in modern function; differs in register — casual, sporting, psychological, with none of the scriptural resonance of girding.
