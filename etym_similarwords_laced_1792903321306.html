• Interlaced: Similar because both describe things woven across one another into one fabric; differs because interlaced stresses mutual crossing of equals, while laced often implies one active cord fastening or threading through a plainer ground.
  • Interwoven: Similar because both picture threads or branches crossing into cloth-like unity; differs because interwoven belongs to looms and grand tapestries, while laced is lighter, the work of a single cord or of nature imitating cord.
  • Entwined: Similar because both name growths or limbs wrapped together; differs because entwined suggests clinging spirals like ivy, while laced suggests neat crossing over and under like shoelaces.
  • Braided: Similar because both join strands by crossing; differs because a braid plaits three or more strands into one rope, while laced threads one cord through holes or across a gap.
  • Tied: Similar because both fasten with cord; differs because tied is the plain general word for any knot, while laced names the specific criss-cross threading through eyelets or branches.
  • Fastened: Similar because both secure something shut; differs because fastened covers buttons, zips, and clasps of every kind, while laced narrows to cord-work alone.
  • Trimmed: Similar because both can decorate an edge with fine material; differs because trimmed is any neat edging or cutting back, while laced in this sense means edged specifically with openwork lace.
  • Spiked: Similar because both add a strong dash to a mild drink or dish; differs because spiked is blunt and punchy, while laced suggests the addition threaded delicately all through.
  • Veined: Similar because both streak one substance with thin lines of another; differs because veined is geological or anatomical, marble and leaves, while laced keeps the handmade image of cord across cloth.
