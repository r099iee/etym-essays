habēre

praebēre

praebenda

↓

provende, provendre
‘allowance, ration, fodder’
↓
Middle English (14th c., borrowed)
provendre, provender

↓
Modern English (current)
provender
‘laid-in food; fodder; stores’

Note: Latin habēre (to have) from PIE *ghabh- (to give and take) with prefix prae- (before, from PIE *per-, forth) yields praebēre, to furnish — literally to hold forth. The doublet prebend entered through the Church direct from praebenda, while provender came by the French stable-yard.
