- [16th century church decree] - [Enters from Greek via Latin meaning an authoritative ecclesiastical decision or doctrine; connotation of official fixed truth, laid down as incontrovertibly true]
- [17th-18th century philosophy and science] - [Extends to any principle an authority lays down as beyond question; philosophy and early science adopt and then challenge the word]
- [19th-20th century the unfalsifiable] - [Contrasted with the open, empirical temper of science; dogma becomes the name for assertion that refuses argument, connotation increasingly pejorative outside the church]
- [20th-21st century broadened rigidity] - [Used for any rigidly held, unquestioned belief — religious, political, ideological; 'dogmatic' means refusing to argue, and Gibbon is praised for the opposite, an arguing rather than decreeing temper]
