PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*menk-
"to knead, to press,"
"to mix by working"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*mangijaną
"to knead together,"
"to mix, to mingle"
↓
Old English (c. 500–1100)
mengan
"to mix, combine,"
"to mingle, to confuse"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200–1400)
mengen / minglen
"to mix, blend,"
"frequentative: to mix repeatedly"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500–1700)
mingle
"to mix, to associate,"
"to blend socially"
↓
Modern English (c. 1700–present)
mingling
"present participle: mixing,"
"blending, associating"
Note: deeper PIE link *menk- to knead is plausible but not fully certain; some connect *mang- to a purely Germanic formation with frequentative -l-.
