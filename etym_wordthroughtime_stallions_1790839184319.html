- 12th–13th c. (Old French / Medieval Latin) - *Estalon* / *stallio* denoted a horse kept in a stall (stable) specifically for breeding, rather than common field labor.
- 14th c. (Middle English) - Adopted into English as *stalion* / *stalyon*, symbolizing noble equine blood, chivalric chivalry, and breeding vigor.
- 15th–17th c. (Early Modern English) - Standardized across European stud farms and military cavalry; metaphorically applied to virile, robust men.
- 18th–19th c. (Victorian Era & Industrial Farming) - Central to the development of modern draft breeds (Clydesdale, Shire) and Thoroughbred racing dynasties.
- 20th–21st c. (Contemporary English) - Universal equestrian and zoological term, alongside widespread colloquial use as a metaphor for male vitality and athletic power.
