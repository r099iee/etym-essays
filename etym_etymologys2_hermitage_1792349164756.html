Note on historical debate & orthography: Philologists document two key historical nuances: (1) the initial non-etymological h- was absent in classical Greek erēmos and Latin erēmīta, but developed in Old French and Anglo-Norman, alternating for centuries between ermitage and hermitage until the h- became fixed in standard English spelling (though retained without h- in modern French ermitage); and (2) in Indology, the adoption of hermitage to translate Sanskrit āśrama (forest academy of Vedic sages) represents a cross-cultural semantic extension, as Indic ashramas were dynamic multi-generational communities rather than strictly solitary desert anchorholds.
