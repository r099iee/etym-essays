=== ROOT WORD: FIDDLE (native Germanic name) === Stage 1 — Proto-Germanic *fiþulōn / *fiþulǭ ‘fiddle; bowed or stringed folk instrument’ (reconstructed; the standard Germanic source) — but see debate note on a possible Medieval Latin parallel Stage 2 — Old English fiðele ‘fiddle’ (attested; the native instrument-word, related to Old High German fidula, Old Norse fiðla) Stage 3 — Middle English fithele / fidhel / fedele ‘fiddle’ (12th–15th c.) Stage 4 — Early Modern English fiddle (16th c.) Stage 5 — Modern English fiddle + plural -s → fiddles (the plural form used in the text) => fiddles (modern English plural noun). DEBATE / NUANCE: The deeper origin is contested. The Germanic forms (*fiþulōn, Old High German fidula) are securely attested as a family, but many handbooks note possible contact with Medieval Latin vitula / vitella, an obscure Latin instrument-name of debated origin (sometimes linked to vitulārī ‘to celebrate’ and the goddess Vitula, goddess of joy). That Latin stem also feeds Romance viola / viole and, later, English viol, viola, and violin. The honest reconstruction shows a solid Old English–Middle English–Modern English line, with the Latin/Romance stem as a parallel or reinforcing source rather than a proven single ancestor.
