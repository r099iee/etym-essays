Note: skirt and shirt are a doublet: both descend from PGmc *skurtijōn "a skirt, shirt" (from PIE *sker- "to cut", i.e. a cut piece of cloth). English borrowed Old Norse skyrta as skirt, while the native Old English cognate scyrte became shirt. The "edge/border" sense extends the idea of the outer rim of a garment.
