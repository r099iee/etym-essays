• Irascible: Similar because both denote an acute tendency to flare up in sudden anger; differs because irascible focuses specifically on the constitutional sensitivity or hair-trigger ease of provocation, whereas choleric carries deep classical humoral resonance linking temperament to fiery, bilious physiological excess.
  • Bilious: Similar because both originate from the digestive humors of the liver and gall bladder; differs because bilious evokes a sour, nauseated, sickly peevishness and bitter resentment, whereas choleric describes active, domineering, and explosive rage.
  • Petulant: Similar because both describe unreasonable bad temper; differs because petulant connotes childish, sulky, or insolent irritability over trivial slights, whereas choleric implies a formidable, commanding, and intimidating fury.
  • Splenetic: Similar because both locate emotional discord in internal visceral organs (the spleen vs. yellow bile); differs because splenetic emphasizes spiteful, malicious sarcasm and melancholy acrimony rather than the swift, incandescent combustion of a choleric outburst.
  • Testy: Similar because both refer to an easily irritated disposition; differs because testy suggests snappy, sharp-tongued impatience under immediate stress, lacking the sustained dramatic weight or volcanic power of choleric.
  • Peppery: Similar because both convey a stinging, easily ignited temperament; differs because peppery is a colorful culinary metaphor suggesting feisty zest and sharpness, whereas choleric is a dignified, formal term denoting destructive wrath.
  • Cantankerous: Similar because both indicate persistent ill humor; differs because cantankerous suggests stubborn, quarrelsome, and contrarian uncooperativeness (often associated with elderly eccentricity), whereas choleric centers on immediate, violent fury.
  • Dyspeptic: Similar because both associate digestive discomfort with a sour mood; differs because dyspeptic highlights gloomy, indigestion-induced malaise and gloomy grumpiness, whereas choleric is characterized by energetic, aggressive outrage.
  • Churlish: Similar because both describe difficult interpersonal demeanor; differs because churlish emphasizes vulgar, boorish discourtesy and a lack of refined breeding, whereas choleric focuses strictly on the violent emotional explosion of temper.
  • Touchy: Similar because both denote hypersensitivity to external provocation; differs because touchy points to an oversensitive defensive vulnerability, whereas choleric denotes an outward, aggressive offensive counter-attack of rage.
