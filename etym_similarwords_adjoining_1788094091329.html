• Neighbouring: Similar because both describe what lies close by, often next door. Differs because neighbouring implies proximity in a neighbourhood and does not require physical contact; adjoining requires a shared boundary or opening.
  • Adjacent: Similar because both mean lying next to something. Differs because adjacent stresses position and vicinity (Latin iacēre, “to lie”) and tolerates a gap, while adjoining implies actual contact; in hotels, “adjoining rooms” share a door, “adjacent rooms” may not.
  • Contiguous: Similar because both mean touching along a common edge. Differs because contiguous is the formal Latinate term of geometry, statistics, and law (the 48 contiguous states), while adjoining is the ordinary descriptive word.
  • Abutting: Similar because both mean touching along a boundary. Differs because abutting is chiefly legal-surveying jargon (abutting owners, abutting lots) meaning physical contact along a line, often shared; adjoining is general-purpose English.
  • Bordering: Similar because both mean lying along a boundary of something. Differs because bordering can mean merely running along an edge (a path bordering a wood) without the two things being considered a pair; adjoining implies a relation of contiguity.
  • Attached: Similar because both describe things joined together (an attached garage, an adjoining room). Differs because attached emphasizes that the parts were fastened or built together as one unit; adjoining only reports that they touch.
  • Connected: Similar because both can describe rooms linked by a door. Differs because connected implies a deliberate link (pipes, corridors, wires) and can span a distance; adjoining is contact without any link device.
  • Next-door: Similar because both describe immediate neighbours. Differs because next-door is casual, domestic, and presupposes dwellings or rooms; adjoining is neutral and used of land, texts, and buildings alike.
  • Proximate / Proximal: Similar because both mean near. Differs because proximate is technical-abstract (proximate cause, proximal bone) and never requires touching; adjoining is concrete and requires contact.
  • Near / Close: Similar because both describe small distance. Differs because near and close are purely relative distance words with no contact implied; adjoining fixes an actual shared line or wall.
  • Juxtaposed: Similar because both describe things placed side by side. Differs because juxtaposed is the art-critical and comparative term for deliberate placement for contrast; adjoining is unplanned physical contiguity.
  • Touching: Similar because both imply physical contact. Differs because touching is a general contact word (hands touching, issues touching), often momentary; adjoining is a standing spatial relationship.
  • Conterminous / Coterminous: Similar because both mean sharing exactly the same boundary or extent. Differs because coterminous is the precise term of law, history, and astronomy for equal boundaries or simultaneous limits; adjoining allows partial contact.
  • Side-by-side: Similar because both describe things next to each other. Differs because side-by-side is informal and implies an imagined line of parade, while adjoining is technical about actual contact.
