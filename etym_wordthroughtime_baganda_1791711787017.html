- Pre-Colonial Era (c. 14th to 19th Century): Sovereign Bantu kingdom and regional superpower - The collective people of Buganda governed by the Kabaka, famed for centralized bureaucracy, road networks, and naval flotillas on Lake Victoria.
- Late 19th Century (1860s–1890s): European contact, missionary rivalries, and colonial treaties - Encountered by Speke, Stanley, and Lugard; Swahili coastal traders rendered Buganda as 'Uganda', which the British adopted for the entire protectorate.
- Colonial Period (1894–1962): Privileged colonial administrative status - The 1900 Buganda Agreement granted the Baganda substantial autonomy and administrative dominance, generating complex tensions with other ethnic groups.
- Post-Independence to Present (1962–Present): Abolition, cultural restoration, and constitutional federalism - The Kingdom was abolished by Milton Obote in 1966, forced into suppression, and restored as a cultural institution in 1993 under Kabaka Ronald Muwenda Mutebi II.
