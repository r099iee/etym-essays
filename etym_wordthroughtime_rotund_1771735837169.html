- [18th century the round form] - [Enters as a Latin loan meaning round — 'wheel-shaped'; applied to buildings (the rotunda) and then to bodies; connotation neutral and dignified]
- [19th century the portly gentleman] - [Applied to persons meaning pleasantly plump, round-bodied, even globular; connotation warm and humanizing — the well-fed man of letters, without cruelty]
- [20th-21st century literary roundness] - [Remains a literary, mildly formal word picturing an even, wheel-like roundness of outline; it stays on the dignified side of plumpness, distinct from the clinical 'obese' and the childish 'chubby', and applies as readily to domes as to people]
