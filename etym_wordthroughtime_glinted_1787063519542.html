- [13th–14th c.] - [Middle English Germanic Phonestheme] Emerged in Northern and Midland Middle English as glenten / glinten, meaning to glance aside, slip rapidly, or cast a sudden, fleeting look or flash of light.
- [15th–17th c.] - [Scottish & Northern Dialectal Preservation] Preserved with intense vitality in Scots and Northern English poetry (e.g., Robert Burns) to describe dew shimmering on heather and quick flashes of steel weapons in border skirmishes.
- [18th–19th c.] - [Standard Literary Revival & Ocular Psychology] Re-assimilated into standard literary English by Romantic and Victorian authors (Sir Walter Scott, Lord Tennyson) to describe gemstones, moonlight on waves, and expressive glances ("a glint in the eye").
- [20th–21st c.] - [Modern Polysemy & Cinematic Imagery] Ubiquitous in descriptive prose, cinematography, and everyday language to capture specular highlights, subtle micro-expressions, and metaphorical sparks of hope.
