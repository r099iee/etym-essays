• anthology: Similar because both name a collection of choice literary extracts; differs because anthology is the everyday English word, while florilege is a rare, learned loanword with a distinctly scholarly and archaic register.
  • collection: Similar because both name a set of gathered items; differs because collection is generic and can be random or personal, while florilege implies gathering only the finest, choicest pieces.
  • compilation: Similar because both bring together material from many sources; differs because compilation is neutral and need not imply quality, while florilege presupposes careful selection of excellent extracts.
  • digest: Similar because both are selected bodies of writings; differs because a digest condenses and summarizes its material, while a florilege gathers extracts in full without abbreviation.
  • miscellany: Similar because both are collections of varied pieces; differs because a miscellany is deliberately varied and need not be uniformly excellent, while a florilege is curated as the finest of its kind.
  • sampler: Similar because both offer a selection of the best; differs because a sampler is a brief illustrative selection (often of merchandise), while a florilege is a full gathered volume.
  • reader: Similar because both are compiled volumes of extracts; differs because a reader is a pedagogical textbook, while a florilege is an aesthetically curated bouquet of the excellent.
  • treasury: Similar because both suggest a store of choice items; differs because treasury emphasizes abundance and preciousness, while florilege emphasizes the floral metaphor of the picked finest.
  • garland: Similar because both use the flower image for a literary collection; differs because a garland (as a book title) stresses woven wreaths and is even more archaic, while florilege stresses the gathering act.
  • florilegium: Similar because both are the same word in different languages; differs because florilegium is the Latin form used in scholarly and manuscript studies, while florilege is the anglicized form.
  • bouquet: Similar because both image the finest gathered flowers; differs because bouquet is literal (cut flowers) and only metaphorically literary, while florilege is the established book-term.
