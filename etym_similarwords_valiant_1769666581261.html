• Brave: Similar because both mean showing courage in danger; differs because brave is the broad, everyday word for fearlessness, while valiant is more formal, literary, and adds a note of honor and worth.
  • Courageous: Similar because both mean possessing courage; differs because courageous emphasizes the inner strength of heart in facing fear, while valiant emphasizes brave action and noble deeds, especially in battle or adversity.
  • Heroic: Similar because both describe great bravery worthy of a hero; differs because heroic stresses extraordinary, larger-than-life deeds, while valiant can apply to smaller but still worthy courage.
  • Gallant: Similar because both describe brave, noble conduct; differs because gallant adds courtly politeness and romantic chivalry (a gallant gentleman), while valiant is purely about courage and resolution.
  • Intrepid: Similar because both mean fearless in the face of danger; differs because intrepid stresses unshakable fearlessness and endurance (an intrepid explorer), while valiant stresses brave action and moral worth.
  • Fearless: Similar because both mean without fear; differs because fearless is a plain descriptive word for lacking fear, while valiant is an admiring word for courage shown in worthy deeds.
  • Bold: Similar because both mean daring and unhesitating; differs because bold emphasizes audacity and confidence (a bold move), while valiant emphasizes honorable courage under danger.
  • Daring: Similar because both involve risk-taking courage; differs because daring stresses willingness to take risks, often adventurous, while valiant stresses resolute bravery in worthy defense or effort.
  • Plucky: Similar because both describe spirited courage against odds; differs because plucky is informal, cheerful, and small-scale (a plucky underdog), while valiant is formal, dignified, and grand in tone.
  • Stout-hearted: Similar because both mean firm and courageous in spirit; differs because stout-hearted emphasizes sturdy resoluteness and is more folksy, while valiant is elegant and chivalric.
  • Lion-hearted: Similar because both mean exceptionally brave; differs because lion-hearted is a vivid, somewhat hyperbolic idiom (Richard the Lionheart), while valiant is the standard literary adjective for heroic courage.
  • Dauntless: Similar because both mean not discouraged by danger; differs because dauntless stresses the absence of intimidation, while valiant stresses positive courageous action.
  • Doughty: Similar because both mean brave and resolute in a somewhat old-fashioned way; differs because doughty is quaintly archaic and suggests sturdy toughness, while valiant suggests noble, dignified heroism.
  • Mettlesome: Similar because both mean spirited and courageous; differs because mettlesome stresses high spirit and liveliness, while valiant stresses grave, honorable courage.
