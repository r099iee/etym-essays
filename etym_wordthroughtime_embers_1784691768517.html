- Old English sense - ember is the old word for the glowing remains of a fire, the small pieces of coal or wood that smoulder with a dull red or orange heat after the flames have died; the sense has been steady for a thousand years
- Medieval & early modern hearth sense - embers are the practical heart of the household: they cook food, dry clothes, keep a camp warm overnight, and carry fire from one day to the next; 'to rake the embers' is a daily domestic rite
- 18th-19th century figurative extension - the word becomes a metaphor for anything smouldering beneath the surface: resentment, love, revolt or memory that still glows when the visible flame is gone; the ember is hopeful, for it can be blown back into flame
- Contemporary sense - the physical sense of a dying fire's glowing coals and the metaphor of latent, banked feeling coexist; embers mean warmth, memory and quiet endurance rather than flash
