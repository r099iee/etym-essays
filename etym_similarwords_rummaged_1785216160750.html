• Search / Searched: Similar because both mean looking through a mass of objects; differs because search is neutral and methodical, implying a goal, whereas rummage is messy, hasty, and careless of how things lie afterward.
  • Ransack: Similar because both mean turning a place upside down in a hunt for plunder; differs because ransack is far more violent, stressing destruction of the place itself, while rummage stresses the mess of the contents rather than wreckage.
  • Rifle: Similar because both mean a quick search through possessions; differs because rifle is furtive and intent on stealing, while rummage is open, rough, and almost comic in its disorder.
  • Forage: Similar because both mean searching through things; differs because forage is a wandering search for provisions (food or fodder), while rummage has no goal of survival — only haste and turned-over disorder.
  • Scour: Similar because both mean an energetic search; differs because scour is systematic, exhaustive, and methodical, while rummage is aimless, careless, and unmethodical.
  • Scrounge: Similar because both mean hunting through things; differs because scrounge seeks to obtain something (often by wheedling or low methods), while rummage is a physical search with no social or acquisitive nuance.
  • Scavenge: Similar because both mean searching through discarded things; differs because scavenge seeks usable food or cast-offs, often for survival, while rummage is a general untidy search for anything.
  • Poke about / Root: Similar because both mean searching by touch in a desultory way; differs because poke about and root are even more aimless and are chiefly British and informal, while rummage implies a fuller, faster turning-over of the whole mass.
  • Fumble: Similar because both involve clumsy hand-search; differs because fumbling is awkward handling of objects in hand, while rummaging is a vigorous turning-over of a mass of objects.
  • Sift: Similar because both mean going through a mass; differs because sifting is careful and discriminating, separating what is wanted from what is not, the exact opposite of rummaging's haste.
