Note on semantic inversion of with: Old English wið originally signified physical opposition ("against, hostilely facing", preserved in withstand, withhold, withdraw), while mutual association was denoted by mid (cognate with German mit). During the late Old English and early Middle English periods, wið underwent a profound semantic flip, absorbing the associative sense of mid ("in company with, by means of") until mid was entirely displaced. Consequently, the compound þǣrwiþ, which originally meant "against that" or "in opposition thereto," evolved into Middle English therwith, meaning "accompanied by that" and "by means of that."
