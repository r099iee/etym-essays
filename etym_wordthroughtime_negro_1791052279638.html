- Exotic foreigner - 1550s-1600s: Negro as traveller's word for Africans brought by Spanish/Portuguese; curious, commercial, already tied to slavery.
- Legal chattel label - 17th-18th c.: Negro slave in plantation codes; bureaucratic, dehumanising; word marks property status.
- Polite standard - Late 19th-1950s: capitalised Negro demanded by Black leaders as dignified; proud institutions (Negro press, leagues, colleges); respectful, progressive.
- Contested accommodation - 1960s: younger militants hear Negro as Uncle Tom, Jim Crow (Negro waiting room); respect splits by generation.
- Rejected archaism - 1970s-1980s: Black replaces Negro almost overnight (I am Black and proud); Negro feels embarrassing, white-imposed; census and media switch.
- Quotation-only relic - Present: appears in King quotes, historic names, scholarship; use outside quotation is dated/offensive; memory of dignity remains for elders, taboo for outsiders.
