- "Traveling, roving" — In medieval romance this was the dominant, largely neutral-to-admiring sense: the knight-errant who roams the world in search of chivalric adventure and quests.
- "Straying from the proper course" — As the travel sense blurred with the older idea of erring/wandering off the right path, the word acquired a judgmental tone, describing people or forces that break free of rules, boundaries, or containment.
- Contemporary Sense — Used of anything that has slipped off its intended path (an errant arrow, errant behaviour); now formal and slightly literary, carrying an undercurrent of roguish, dangerous unpredictability rather than mere accident.
