- [Vedic Era, c. 1500–500 BCE] — Consecrated in the Ṛgveda, Yajurveda, and Atharvaveda as the essential ritual grass spread upon the sacrificial altar (*barhis*) to seat the gods, fashion the purifying ring (*pavitra*), and repel demonic harassers — [perception: perceived as an intrinsically pure, divine vegetal shield connecting heaven and earth]
- [Epic & Classical Sanskrit Period, c. 500 BCE–700 CE] — Embedded in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata, and Manusmṛti as the obligatory, dignified seat (*āsana*) for hermits, sages, and kings performing penance (*tapas*), meditation (*dhyāna*), or ancestral libations (*tarpaṇa*) — [perception: revered as the ultimate physical foundation of austere renunciation, spiritual access, and flawless ritual focus]
- [Medieval Purāṇic & Tantric Era, c. 800–1600 CE] — Associated mythologically with the cosmic churning of the ocean (*samudra-manthana*) as the divine hair of Viṣṇu's tortoise avatar washed ashore, and mandated for preserving cooked food and water during solar and lunar eclipses — [perception: regarded as a mystical, cosmic anti-microbial and anti-radiation talisman safeguarding ritual hygiene]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era, 19th–21st c.] — Maintained across global Hindu diaspora communities for domestic pujas, cremation ceremonies, and yoga meditation mats, while attracting ethnobotanical and pharmacological investigation for its genuine biochemical antimicrobial, antioxidant, and radiation-shielding properties — [perception: respected as a profound ecological symbol of ancient Vedic botanical science and timeless ritual sanctity]
