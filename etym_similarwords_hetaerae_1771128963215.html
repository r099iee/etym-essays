• Courtesan: Similar because both denote high-status companions to wealthy or powerful men; differs because courtesan is colored by Renaissance and early-modern European court politics and financial patronage, while hetaerae refers to the intellectually elite institution of ancient Greece.
  • Concubine: Similar because both are secondary female partners in a patriarchal order; differs because a concubine is a legally recognized domestic partner lacking the public, intellectual, and social agency central to the hetaera.
  • Geisha: Similar because both are trained, artistically accomplished professional companions; differs because geisha centers on traditional Japanese performance and ritual, not on the philosophical and political conversation of the Greek symposium.
  • Prostitute: Similar because both are women who provide sexual companionship for payment; differs because prostitute is blunt, stigmatized, and general, while the hetaera's prestige lay in education and refinement.
  • Mistress: Similar because both are women in relationships outside marriage; differs because mistress implies a private modern affair, while the hetaera was a public, professional, culturally celebrated figure.
  • Companion: Similar because both mean "a person who accompanies another"; differs because companion is neutral and has lost the ancient narrowing to a paid, educated courtesan.
