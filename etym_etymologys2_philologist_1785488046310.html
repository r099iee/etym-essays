ROOT 1 (the philo- element, “loving”): Proto-Indo-European *bʰel- “to bloom, thrive” (Pokorny’s set; the sense development to “dear, good” passes through “flourishing, thriving”) → derivative *bʰil- “dear, good, friendly” → Greek phílos (φίλος) “dear, loved; friend”, attested in Homer (8th c. BCE) → phileîn (φιλεῖν) “to love” → combining form philo- (φιλο-) “loving, fond of” → first element of philólogos and philología. ROOT 2 (the -logist element, “word, study”): Proto-Indo-European *leǵ- “to gather, collect” → Greek légein (λέγειν) “to gather, pick out; to count; to say, speak” → lógos (λόγος) “word, speech, reason, account” → suffix -logía (-λογία) “speech of, study of”, built on lógos. THE FUSION: Greek philólogos (φιλόλογος) “fond of words, studious, argumentative” (Plato, 4th c. BCE) → philología (φιλολογία) “love of learning, literature, disputation” (Plato, Aristotle) → Latin philologia “love of literature” and philologus “learned, scholarly man” (Cicero’s age, 1st c. BCE) → New Latin philologista / French philologue (16th c.) → English philologist (c. 1650s) “a student of language and literature” → PHILOLOGIST (the exact form of this card).
