• shiver: Similar because it is a trembling of the body; differs because shiver is the cold-weather twin — rapid, fine, surface-level shaking, often with chattering teeth — while a shudder is heavier, grosser, and belongs to horror, revulsion, and dread more than to temperature.
  • tremble: Similar because it is an involuntary vibration of the body; differs because tremble is the unmarked general word for sustained shaking from weakness, age, anger, excitement, or fear, and lacks the single violent jerk of a shudder.
  • quiver: Similar because it is a bodily trembling; differs because quiver is finer and faster still — a light ripple that can express excitement or eagerness and is rarely alarming, where a shudder is a sharp reflex of repulsion.
  • quake: Similar because it is a violent shaking; differs because quake is whole-body and often external — the earth quakes — and its fear-sense is terror on a seismic scale, far grosser than the brief personal convulsion of a shudder.
  • shake: Similar because it is the blunt physical action of moving rapidly to and fro; differs because shake is the unmarked, emotionally neutral verb, whereas shudder adds a specific charge of horror, revulsion, or uncanny dread.
  • convulse: Similar because it is a violent involuntary bodily shaking; differs because a convulsion is a medical-grade, often uncontrollable and more prolonged fit, whereas a shudder is brief, lighter, and far less alarming.
  • jerk: Similar because it is a sudden, sharp, involuntary movement; differs because a jerk is a plain physical motion with no dread or revulsion implied, while a shudder is that jerk plus a distinct emotional shuddering of horror or cold.
  • twitch: Similar because it is a small involuntary muscular movement; differs because a twitch is localised and often trivial (an eye twitch), whereas a shudder sweeps over the body and carries emotional weight.
  • flinch: Similar because it is a sudden involuntary recoil; differs because flinching is a guarded reaction to a present threat or blow, while a shudder is an internal tremor of revulsion or dread that need involve no immediate stimulus.
  • wince: Similar because it is a brief involuntary reaction to pain or distress; differs because a wince is a facial tightening in response to pain or embarrassment, whereas a shudder is a whole-body tremor of horror or cold.
  • start: Similar because it is a sudden startled movement; differs because a start is a quick jump of surprise or alarm triggered by a stimulus, while a shudder is a longer, deeper tremor of revulsion or dread.
  • recoil: Similar because it is a withdrawal from something unpleasant; differs because recoil is a deliberate or instinctive drawing-back of the body, whereas a shudder is an involuntary tremor without any movement away.
