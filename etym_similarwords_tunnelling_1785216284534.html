• dig: Similar because both mean excavating the earth; differs because digging is the general term for any hollowing, from a garden hole to a mine, whereas tunnelling specifically means cutting a long, horizontal, traversable underground passage.
  • burrow: Similar because both mean excavating underground; differs because burrowing is a small animal's cramped, irregular scratching, often furtive and concealed, whereas tunnelling is the deliberate creation of a corridor large enough to pass through.
  • excavate: Similar because both describe large-scale removal of earth; differs because excavate is the formal, technical word for any digging out in construction or archaeology, whereas tunnelling names the specific making of an underground passageway.
  • mine: Similar because both involve underground work; differs because mining stresses the extraction of minerals and ores, whereas tunnelling stresses the creation of a traversable passage or road beneath the ground.
  • bore: Similar because both mean cutting through rock or earth; differs because boring is mechanical, circular drilling for shafts or blast-holes, whereas tunnelling drives a long, linear passage that something can pass through.
  • drill: Similar because both involve penetrating the ground; differs because drilling makes a hole with a rotating tool and is typically narrow and vertical, whereas tunnelling creates a long, wide, walkable corridor.
  • undermine: Similar because both involve digging beneath a surface; differs because undermining aims to weaken or destroy what is above, whereas tunnelling aims to create a passage — though a tunnel may incidentally undermine.
  • sap: Similar because both are terms of covert underground excavation; differs because sapping (from sap, a trench dug toward an enemy position) is specifically military and siege-driven, whereas tunnelling is general excavation for passage.
  • carve: Similar because both describe cutting through material; differs because carving is a general word for cutting a form out of any substance and lacks the underground, passage-making sense of tunnelling.
  • gouge: Similar because both describe scooping out material; differs because gouging is a rough, forceful hollowing out of any surface, without the engineering of a long, traversable underground route.
