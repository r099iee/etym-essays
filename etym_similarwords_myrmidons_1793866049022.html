• Henchmen: Similar because both refer to loyal, ruthless subordinates who carry out the questionable or illegal orders of a powerful leader; differs because henchman is a broad, common term that emphasizes personal thuggery, criminal allegiance, or political sycophancy, whereas Myrmidon carries a higher literary register and emphasizes cold, disciplined, militaristic or bureaucratic obedience.
  • Minions: Similar because both denote subservient underlings dependent upon an authority figure; differs because minion (from French mignon, 'darling, favorite') implies obsequious fawning, weakness, servility, or insignificant triviality, whereas Myrmidons are formidable, disciplined, and dangerous executors of force.
  • Lackeys: Similar because both designate obedient followers who execute a superior’s bidding; differs because lackey (originally a footman or liveried manservant) emphasizes humiliating menial servitude, bootlicking, and errand-running, rather than the ruthless, aggressive enforcement characteristic of Myrmidons.
  • Cohort: Similar because both can refer to a disciplined group of loyal associates or military units; differs because cohort (from Roman military cohors) is a neutral sociological or statistical term for a generational group or professional band of colleagues, lacking the inherent pejorative connotation of unprincipled obedience.
  • Stormtroopers: Similar because both describe fierce, unquestioning shock troops who execute ruthless state commands; differs because stormtrooper (from German Sturmtruppen / SA) carries specific modern twentieth-century totalitarian and Nazi associations, whereas Myrmidon draws upon classical antiquity and literary allusions.
  • Janissaries: Similar because both denote elite, fiercely disciplined military contingents devoted entirely to their sovereign; differs because Janissaries were an actual historical Ottoman military corps recruited through the devshirme system, carrying specific Islamic imperial and historical institutional connotations.
  • Automata: Similar because both execute instructions mechanically without independent moral deliberation; differs because automata are literal machines or robots, whereas Myrmidons are living humans whose moral agency has been completely subordinated to another's will.
