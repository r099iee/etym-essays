- Mid-17th c. — Scientific & Scholastic Emergence: Coined in Early Modern English during the scientific revolution (c. 1640s–1650s) to express Cartesian and Baconian logic: ideas capable of being deduced from primary axioms.
- Late 17th c. — Natural Law & Rational Theology: Cambridge Platonists and natural theologians (John Locke, Ralph Cudworth) use deducible to argue that moral obligations and divine laws are deducible by human reason from nature.
- 18th c. — Augustan Irony & Moral Philosophy: Employed by Enlightenment satirists (Swift, Hume, Fielding) to probe the limits of reason, mocking pedants who believed human passion could be cured by what was logically ‘deducible’ on paper.
- 19th c. — Mathematical & Jurisprudential Rigor: Enters formal jurisprudence, Benthamite utilitarianism, and Victorian physical sciences to classify conclusions derived strictly from legal codes or physical laws.
- 20th–21st c. — Analytical Philosophy & AI: Thrives in analytical logic, computer science, and legal theory to describe theorems, database queries, and formal inferences that can be computationally or logically derived.
