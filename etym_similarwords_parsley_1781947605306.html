• Celery: Similar because both share the Greek co-root selinon, which once named both plants; differs because modern English reserves celery for the stalk vegetable (Apium graveolens) and parsley for the leaf herb (Petroselinum crispum).
  • Coriander / Cilantro: Similar because both are leafy culinary herbs used as garnish and seasoning; differs because coriander/cilantro brings a pungent, soapy aroma foreign to parsley's mild greenness.
  • Chervil: Similar because both are delicate, finely divided garnish herbs of the carrot family; differs because chervil is more delicate and anise-scented, a herb of French haute cuisine rather than English cottage medicine.
  • Dill: Similar because both are Apiaceae herbs with fine leaves; differs because dill is strongly aromatic with a pickle-like flavour and is prized for its seeds and fronds, not as a mild corrective.
  • Fennel: Similar because both are Mediterranean herbs of the carrot family; differs because fennel is a tall, anise-flavoured plant grown for bulb, fronds, and seeds, whereas parsley is low and mild.
  • Basil: Similar because both are culinary herbs used fresh; differs because basil is a warm, sweet, intensely aromatic leaf of the mint family, strong-flavoured, not the mild digestive-soother parsley is.
  • Mint: Similar because both are kitchen-garden herbs with folk-medicinal use; differs because mint is pungent, cool, and mentholated, used for confection and tisanes, whereas parsley is savoury and near-tasteless.
  • Thyme: Similar because both are hardy garden herbs with culinary and medicinal value; differs because thyme is a low, woody, intensely aromatic shrublet used in stews, while parsley is a soft leafy seasoning herb.
  • Chives: Similar because both are mild, finely cut garnishes; differs because chives are onion-flavoured and belong to the allium family, quite unlike the flat-leafed parsley.
  • Sage: Similar because both are traditional European garden herbs with medicinal lore; differs because sage is a grey, strongly camphoraceous leaf of the mint family, used with rich meats, not a mild fresh seasoning.
  • Rosemary: Similar because both are Mediterranean herbs; differs because rosemary is a pungent, resinous needle-leaf shrub, strong and woody, the opposite of parsley's gentle freshness.
  • Herb: Similar because parsley is an herb; differs because herb is a generic category covering aromatic plants of any use, lacking parsley's specific botanical identity and digestive folklore.
  • Garnish: Similar because parsley is the archetypal garnish; differs because garnish names the decorative function or the food decorating a dish, not the plant itself.
  • Rock celery / Alexanders: Similar because the Greek petroselinon literally meant "rock celery" and named a wild relative; differs because modern English parsley is the domesticated Petroselinum crispum, while Alexanders is a distinct wild umbellifer.
