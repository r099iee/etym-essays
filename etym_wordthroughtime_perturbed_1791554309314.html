- Late 14th to 15th Century: Violent physical disorder, civil tumult, and spiritual torment - Late Middle English borrowing describing chaotic civil riots, bodily afflictions, or demonic spiritual torment.
- 16th to 17th Century: Renaissance psychological disquiet and moral perturbation - Heavily featured in Elizabethan drama and Protestant theology to express deep emotional agitation, guilt-stricken conscience, and lovers' unrest.
- 18th to 19th Century: Scientific formalization in Newtonian celestial mechanics - Adopted into astrophysics to denote the gravitational deflection of planets and comets from regular elliptical orbits.
- 20th to 21st Century: Understated interpersonal disquiet and systemic complexity - Connotes polite, restrained anxiety or moral disapproval in diplomatic prose, alongside mathematical and ecological chaos in complex systems.
