DUCAL is a single-root word; its chain is linear: 1. Proto-Indo-European *deuk- 'to lead, draw, pull' (c. 4000–3500 BCE) — source of Sanskrit duh- 'to milk' (to draw out milk), Greek daidússesthai 'to be drawn, pulled', and Proto-Germanic *teuhaną 'to lead, draw' (Gothic tiuhan, Old English téon, German ziehen). 2. Latin dūcere 'to lead, guide, draw, consider' (attested from the 3rd c. BCE), from *deuk-. 3. Latin dux, ducis 'leader, commander, general' (root noun of dūcere, attested from the 2nd–1st c. BCE). 4. Late Latin ducālis 'of or pertaining to a dux / duke' (attested from c. the 4th–6th c. CE in administrative and legal Latin). 5. Middle French ducal 'of a duke' (14th–15th c.). 6. English ducal — borrowed from Middle French in the mid-16th century (first recorded c. 1530s–1540s); modern English DUCAL.
