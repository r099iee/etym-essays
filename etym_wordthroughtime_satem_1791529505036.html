- 1890s — European philologists adopt Avestan satəm as the name of the eastern group whose reflexes of "hundred" begin with a sibilant.
- Early 20th c. — "satem language" becomes a standard classroom category, paired with "centum" and treated as a primary division.
- Mid-20th c. — Hittite, Tocharian and Albanian data weaken the binary, and the label's genetic claims are questioned.
- Late 20th c. — satem is redefined as an areal isogloss, a wave of assibilation rather than a branch.
- 21st c. — the term survives as technical shorthand in Indo-European studies, while its geographical and racialized misuses are rejected.
