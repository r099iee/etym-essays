Bolstered (verb, past tense and past participle of bolster; also used participially as an adjective) signifies supported, sustained, reinforced, upheld, or buttressed, whether physically by means of a cushion, prop, or structural support beam, or metaphorically by means of arguments, empirical evidence, institutional alliances, financial subsidies, or psychological encouragement.

Epistemic & Rhetorical Context ("Bolstered by Two Central Ideas"):

  • Theoretical & Argumentative Reinforcement: In the contextual reading ("This optimism, perhaps born out of ignorance, is bolstered by two central ideas..."), bolstered operates in an intellectual and epistemic sense. An otherwise fragile, tenuous, or unfounded psychological disposition (optimism born of ignorance) is prevented from collapsing under critical scrutiny because it is continuously propped up and undergirded by two underlying theoretical premises. The verb conveys that the belief does not stand firmly on its own intrinsic merits; rather, external intellectual scaffolding provides artificial stability and rhetorical endurance.
  • From Physical Prop to Abstract Support: The metaphor derives its potency from everyday mechanical intuition: just as a sagging mattress or drooping patient requires a firm, stuffed bolster cushion to remain upright, an abstract ideology, thesis, or market position requires supportive data and ideological reinforcements to endure external pressure.

Polysemy, Heavy Engineering & Industrial Architecture:

  • Structural Carpentry & Timber Architecture (The Bolster Beam): In traditional heavy timber framing and classical masonry construction, a bolster (also known as a corbel cap, cushion piece, or saddle) is a horizontal block of timber or stone placed between the top of a vertical wooden post or stone column and the horizontal girder or lintel resting upon it. By expanding the bearing surface area, the bolster distributes crushing downward shear loads, preventing the concentrated weight of the roof from splitting or indenting the main beam.
  • Heavy Manufacturing & Toolmaking (The Bolster Plate): In mechanical manufacturing, metal stamping, and industrial hydraulic press shops, a bolster plate is a massive, precision-ground block of structural steel bolted securely onto the bed of the stamping press. It acts as the foundational mounting plate for the lower stamping die, absorbing millions of pounds of cyclic hydraulic impact without deflecting, thereby ensuring micro-millimeter precision in metal forming.
  • Railway Transport Engineering (The Truck Bolster): In railroad locomotive and freight car engineering, the bolster is the heavy transverse steel casting positioned within each wheel bogie (truck). It spans the center of the wheel assembly, resting directly upon heavy suspension coil springs to carry the immense downward weight of the freight car body, absorbing track vibrations, shocks, and lateral kinetic forces.
  • Cutlery & Bladesmithing (The Knife Bolster): In high-end forged culinary and hunting knives, the bolster is the thick, solid junction of steel situated between the sharpened blade and the handle tang. Forged integrally from the steel bar, it provides critical mechanical balance, shifts the center of gravity to the user's hand, strengthens the knife against bending at its weakest structural point, and forms a protective finger guard preventing the cook's hand from sliding onto the cutting edge.
  • Macroeconomics & Public Policy: In financial and political reporting, the term is widely deployed as a transitive verb: central banks intervene in currency markets to "bolster the domestic currency," governments pass stimulus packages to "bolster consumer demand," and military pacts are signed to "bolster regional deterrence."
