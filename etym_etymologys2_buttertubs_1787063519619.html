ROOT 1: BUTTER
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*gʷou- (cow) + *tūro- (cheese/curd)
"cow-cheese, curdled cow's milk"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 500 BCE)
βούτυρον (boútyron)
"cow-curd, butter (pastoral Scythian dairy)"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 100 BCE)
būtȳrum
"butter (used primarily as ointment or medicine)"
↓
West Germanic Borrowing (c. 400 CE)
*butirā
"churned dairy butter"
↓
Old English (c. 800 CE)
butere
"butter, fatty dairy spread"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200 CE)
botere / buttre
"butter"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500 CE)
butter
"churned cream dairy spread"

ROOT 2: TUBS
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*dʰub- / *dʰeub-
"deep, hollow; cavity, vessel"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*dub- / *tubn-
"hollowed wooden vessel, deep vat"
↓
Middle Low German / Middle Dutch (c. 1200 CE)
tubbe
"wide staved wooden vat or container"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300 CE)
tubbe (plural: tubbes)
"open wooden vessel for washing, brewing, or packing"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500 CE)
tub (plural: tubs)
"staved wooden containers"
↓
Modern English (Compound c. 1650 CE)
BUTTERTUBS
"wooden tubs for packing salted butter; rotund figures; limestone potholes"
