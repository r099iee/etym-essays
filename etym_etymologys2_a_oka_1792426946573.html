"without sorrow; dispelling grief; the Saraca tree"

Note on semantic evolution: The noun śoka derives from the PIE root *k̂ewk- ("to shine, glow, burn"), which in Sanskrit produced the verbal root śuc ("to flame, burn with heat, suffer inward distress"). The semantic development moved from physical burning heat to the burning psychological anguish of grief and mourning. By attaching the privative prefix a- (from PIE *n̥-), Sanskrit formed the bahuvrīhi compound aśoka ("having no burning grief; dispelling sorrow"). When applied to the botanical tree Saraca asoca, ancient botanists and poets believed that its dazzling, cooling blossoms had the psychological power to dispel sorrow and bring joy to human hearts.
