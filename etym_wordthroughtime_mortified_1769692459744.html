- Literal religious sense (14th-17th c.) - To put to death or deaden the flesh: mortification as the ascetic discipline of killing bodily desires and lusts.
- Figurative psychological death (18th-19th c.) - The death becomes internal: to mortify one's pride or vanity is to strike it a killing blow, leaving the self numb, wounded, and humiliated — the sense behind Elizabeth Bennet's 'he has mortified mine.'
- Contemporary sense - Softened to mean acute embarrassment or chagrin; the literal killing has faded, leaving a vivid metaphor for the social 'death' of being deeply shamed.
