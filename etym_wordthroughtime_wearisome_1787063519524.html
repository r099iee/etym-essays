- 1000s–1300s: [Grueling Journeys] - Formed in Late Old English from *wēriġ* + *-sum* to describe grueling physical tracks and arduous pilgrimages.
- 1300s–1500s: [Devotional Pilgrimage] - Pervaded Middle English religious writing to characterize mortal life as an exhausting spiritual trial in a fallen world.
- 1500s–1750s: [Biblical & Tragic Elevation] - Immortalized in the King James Bible (Job, Ecclesiastes) and Jacobean drama for prolonged grief and sleepless suffering.
- 1750s–1900s: [Industrial & Bureaucratic Grind] - Adopted by Victorian social novelists to depict soul-crushing factory labor and endless legal proceedings.
- 1900s–Present: [Elevated Formal Register] - Standard in literature and political commentary for protracted negotiations, administrative red tape, and chronic fatigue.
