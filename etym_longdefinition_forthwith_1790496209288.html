forthwith is a formal adverb meaning immediately, at once, without delay or interval. In the source sentence — John Ruskin in Praeterita recalling a harsh domestic anecdote, that his grandfather, perceiving his mother to have told a lie, sent the servant out forthwith to buy an entire bundle of new broom twings to whip her with — it carries its full historical force: peremptory, instantaneous obedience. There is no pause for reflection, no softening interval; the command is executed in the same breath in which it is uttered. The comedy and the horror of the scene both depend on that immediacy: a Victorian patriarch's anger translated into errand-boy action within seconds.

In modern use forthwith survives almost entirely as a formal, legal, and bureaucratic word. Courts order a defendant to surrender a passport forthwith; statutes require a notice to be forwarded forthwith to the registrar; a chairman demands that a meeting be reconvened forthwith. Here it does not merely describe speed but imposes obligation: to act forthwith is to act as promptly as circumstances allow, with no lawful excuse for waiting. Lawyers distinguish it from immediately in fine shades — forthwith traditionally allows whatever minimal time is reasonably necessary to perform the act, whereas instantly would demand the impossible — but in ordinary reading it simply means right now dressed in a wig and gown.

  • Core temporal sense: at once, straight away: no intervening time or action; e.g. he left forthwith, the servant was sent forthwith, payment must follow forthwith. This is the dictionary head sense and the source-sentence sense. It answers the question when? with now, and with nothing in between.
  • Legal and official command sense: with binding immediacy; e.g. to vacate the premises forthwith, to produce the documents forthwith, the order was executed forthwith. Registers include court orders, police instructions, parliamentary procedure, military commands, and contractual clauses. The bridge from the general sense is institutional authority: private haste becomes public duty.
  • Literary and scriptural immediacy: in the King James Bible, Shakespeare, Scott, and Ruskin, forthwith drives narrative pace; e.g. forthwith Jesus gave them leave (Matthew 8:32), forthwith a change was wrought. Here the word is stylistic, lending antique decisiveness to sudden turns of plot.
  • Pragmatic force: peremptoriness and refusal of negotiation: to say forthwith rather than soon or when convenient signals that delay itself would be disobedience or contempt. A master says forthwith; a friend says when you get a moment. The source anecdote exploits exactly this: the grandfather's forthwith exposes an authoritarian temper that treats a childish lie as a capital emergency.
  • Collocations and grammar: forthwith is a sentence adverbial, normally placed after the verb it modifies (sent out forthwith, departed forthwith, to be paid forthwith) or at the head of an official direction (forthwith, all licences are suspended). It never inflects, never forms a comparative, and has no adjectival use; one cannot speak of a forthwith departure except facetiously. Common pairings are forthwith and without delay, immediately and forthwith, henceforth and forthwith in deeds doubling synonyms for certainty.
  • Near-synonym boundaries: immediately is the neutral everyday equivalent; instantly stresses the same instant with scientific precision; directly stresses no detour; promptly stresses willing quickness; straightaway is the colloquial twin. forthwith differs from all of them by register: it is deliberately stiff, public, and commanding, and would sound comic if used for trivial domestic haste (pass the salt forthwith is a joke precisely because the word summons a courtroom to the dinner table).
  • What it does not mean: it never means soon, shortly, in due course, and never means away from here despite containing forth. The spatial element in forth has been fully bleached into time: forthwith points to the next moment, not to the next place. Nor does it mean together with in any modern sense, though that was the literal Glue of its compound origin.

The reason forthwith endures while dozens of other forth- compounds (forthgoing, forthputting, forthy) have died is precisely its institutional niche. Ordinary speech has abandoned it to right away, ASAP, straight away, but law, liturgy, and historical narrative keep it alive as a marker of gravity. To meet it in Ruskin is therefore to meet a word already slightly stiff and old-fashioned in the 1880s, chosen to make a grandfather sound like a magistrate pronouncing sentence — which, in the child's eyes, he was.
