Ancient Greek (origin uncertain; possibly Pre-Greek)
σφίγγω (sphíngō)
“to squeeze tight, bind, strangle”

↓
Ancient Greek (c. 700 BCE, Hesiod)
σφίγξ (sphínx; early Boeotian Φίξ)
“the Strangler; the Theban riddle-monster”

↓
Latin (1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
sphinx
“the monster; later also the Egyptian colossus”

↓
Middle English (early 15th c.)
sphinx (also sphynx)
“the Theban monster of the riddle”

↓
Modern English (16th c.–present)
SPHINX
“monster, monument and metaphor of mystery”

Note: The Greek noun is a regular formation from σφίγγω (“the strangler”). Beekes treats σφίγγω itself as of uncertain, possibly Pre-Greek origin, so no Indo-European root can be safely reconstructed; a popular guess that the Greek word borrowed an Egyptian name is generally rejected (the Egyptian term was šsp-ꜥnḫ, “living image”, unrelated in form).
