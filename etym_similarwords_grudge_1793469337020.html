• Begrudge: Similar because both verbs mean to envy someone the possession of something or to give with reluctance; differs because begrudge is the modern, more frequent standard verb for envious giving (e.g., 'begrudge him his success'), whereas simple grudge can also function directly as the noun for the long-held resentment itself.
  • Resent: Similar because both involve feeling indignant displeasure at an action or benefit; differs because resent describes the internal emotional offense and indignation felt toward a perceived wrong, whereas grudge emphasizes the reluctance to yield, give, or acknowledge another's merit.
  • Grievance: Similar because both involve a lingering sense of being wronged; differs because a grievance is often an objective, legitimate, or formal complaint (such as a union grievance or political injustice), whereas a grudge is personal, subjective, bitter, and often petty or vindictive.
  • Pique: Similar because both denote feelings of annoyance or offense; differs because pique is a fleeting, sudden irritation caused by wounded pride or a minor snub, whereas a grudge is chronic, durable, and nurtured over months or years.
  • Rancor: Similar because both describe deep-seated, bitter ill will; differs because rancor is an elevated, literary noun emphasizing the corrosive, sour toxicity of hatred, while grudge is an everyday, down-to-earth term centered on a specific past dispute or score to settle.
  • Stint: Similar because both involve withholding or giving sparingly; differs because stint relates to quantitative rationing and frugal restriction of resources (e.g., 'do not stint on food'), lacking the personal envy, grievance, and emotional malice inherent in grudging.
  • Envy: Similar because feeling a grudge often springs from envy; differs because envy is the covetous desire for another's advantages or possessions, whereas a grudge is focused squarely on past injuries, slights, and retaliatory ill will.
  • Score: Similar in idiomatic contexts of settling accounts (e.g., 'a score to settle'); differs because a score is the metaphorical debt or ledger entry of injury, whereas a grudge is the ongoing emotional fire that keeps the desire for settlement alive.
