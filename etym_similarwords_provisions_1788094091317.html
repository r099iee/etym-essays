• Supplies: Similar because both mean stores of needed goods; differs because supplies is broader (includes tools, fuel, ammunition), while provisions is specifically food and drink.
  • Stores: Similar because both mean accumulated goods; differs because stores is a general word for any stock, while provisions names food for consumption.
  • Victuals: Similar because both mean food supplies; differs because victuals is archaic or humorous today, while provisions still appears in serious narrative.
  • Rations: Similar because both are food supplies; differs because rations are a fixed, measured allowance per person — usually military — while provisions are a bulk store.
  • Foodstuffs: Similar because both mean food materials; differs because foodstuffs is a technical commercial word for edible goods, without the journeying sense.
  • Sustenance: Similar because both mean what keeps people alive; differs because sustenance is abstract (nourishment as a concept), while provisions are concrete goods in a store.
  • Provender: Similar because both are stored food; differs because provender is feed for animals (or archaic for food) and is now rare.
  • Fare: Similar because both concern food; differs because fare is what is served or available to eat, not a stored supply for a journey.
  • Groceries: Similar because both are food bought in quantity; differs because groceries are everyday household purchases, while provisions belong to expeditions, sieges and voyages.
  • Stock: Similar because both mean accumulated goods; differs because stock is general commercial inventory, without the food-and-journey implication.
