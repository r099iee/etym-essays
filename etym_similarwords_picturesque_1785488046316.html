• Scenic: Similar in describing attractive views of landscape; differs because scenic is a neutral word for natural scenery and says nothing of painterly composition, whereas picturesque implies a scene composed as if for a picture.
  • Beautiful: Similar in expressing aesthetic approval; differs because beautiful aims at ideal, harmonious loveliness of a high order, while picturesque is rougher, more informal, and charming rather than perfect.
  • Pretty: Similar in visual appeal; differs because pretty is small-scale, dainty, and mild, whereas picturesque suggests a larger scene with painterly variety and character.
  • Quaint: Similar in old-fashioned charm; differs because quaint stresses curious, old-fashioned oddity, often of objects, while picturesque stresses the visually picture-like quality of a scene.
  • Charming: Similar in appealing attractiveness; differs because charming is personal and winning in manner, not necessarily visual, whereas picturesque is anchored to the look of things.
  • Striking: Similar in demanding attention; differs because striking emphasises the force of the impression — surprising or dramatic — while picturesque emphasises charm and pictorial composition.
  • Photogenic: Similar in looking good in an image; differs because photogenic is the modern, camera-specific word for subjects that flatter the lens, while picturesque is the painter's and guidebook's word for composed scenes.
  • Pictorial: Similar in being like a picture; differs because pictorial is a technical word for anything of or relating to pictures — pictorial art, pictorial language — without the approval built into picturesque.
  • Painterly: Similar in the manner of a painter; differs because painterly is a term of art-criticism for the handling of paint itself, while picturesque describes scenes and descriptions that look like pictures.
  • Graphic: Similar in vivid, detailed description; differs because graphic stresses force and clarity — graphic language, graphic violence — with no implication of beauty, while picturesque implies a pleasing picture.
  • Vivid: Similar in calling up a strong mental image; differs because vivid is about brilliance and intensity of impression in any medium, while picturesque keeps the specifically aesthetic 'like a painting' sense.
  • Idyllic: Similar in an appealing rural scene; differs because idyllic adds peacefulness and rural simplicity — a pastoral ideal — while picturesque needs only the look of a picture.
  • Bucolic: Similar in rural charm; differs because bucolic is specifically pastoral and rustic, while picturesque can apply to towns, coastlines, ruins, and even language.
