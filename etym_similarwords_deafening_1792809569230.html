• Thunderous: Similar because both describe overwhelming roars that shake the listener; differs because thunderous borrows the deep rolling voice of storms and crowds (often applause), while deafening stresses volume at the edge of pain, whatever its tone.
  • Roaring: Similar because a roaring crowd and a deafening crowd are equally loud; differs because roaring names the lion-like sound itself, while deafening names its effect on the hearer — the stolen hearing.
  • Ear-splitting: Similar because both claim the noise wounds the ears; differs because ear-splitting is the cruder, more violent cousin (shrieks, feedback, screams), while deafening is grander — cannons, choruses, dragons.
  • Blasting: Similar because both evoke explosive overwhelming sound; differs because blasting stresses the outward force (trumpets, furnaces, dynamite), while deafening stresses the inward consequence — ears blanked and ringing.
  • Booming: Similar because both are big resonant noises that fill space; differs because booming is deep and hollow (guns, bass drums, foghorns, orators), while deafening cares only about magnitude — shrill can deafen as surely as bass.
  • Stunning: Similar because both describe being overwhelmed past response (stunned silence, deafening news); differs because stunning strikes the whole mind (grief, surprise, a blow), while deafening strikes through the ear, even when used of silence.
  • Overwhelming: Similar because both name forces too great to resist; differs because overwhelming is general (numbers, grief, kindness), while deafening is specifically sonic — the overwhelm arrives as noise.
  • Resounding: Similar because both describe great ringing sounds that fill halls and valleys; differs because resounding is triumphant and musical (a resounding success, resounding bells), while deafening is sheer force, admiring nothing.
  • Clamorous: Similar because both picture loud demanding multitudes; differs because clamorous is literary and usually disapproving (clamorous factions, clamorous bells), while deafening is neutral about merit — cheers deafen as readily as riots.
