PIE *spek- "to observe, watch, look at" (reconstructed) → Latin specere (also spiciō) "to look at, observe, regard" (attested from early Latin) → Latin specula "watchtower, look-out post" (Classical Latin) → Latin speculārī "to watch, spy out, observe, keep a lookout" (Classical Latin) → Latin speculātus (past participle of speculārī) "observed, spied out" (Classical Latin) → English speculate "to observe; to engage in abstract contemplation" (1590s, from Latin speculātus) Note on debate: the intellectual sense was developed in Medieval Latin, where speculātiō became the technical term for abstract intellectual contemplation, and English took the verb over in that high philosophical sense. The financial sense ("to buy or sell on conjecture of future price movements") arises only in the 18th century amid the stock markets of London and Amsterdam and is a semantic extension of the intellectual sense, not a separate etymology. Zero guesswork: the Latin-to-English stages are attested; only the PIE root is reconstructed and flagged as such.
