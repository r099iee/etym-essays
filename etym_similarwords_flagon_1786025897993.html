• Pitcher: Similar because both are large vessels with handles and spouts used for pouring liquids; differs because pitcher is the modern, everyday domestic term (often lidless), whereas flagon evokes historical, liturgical, or tavern contexts and frequently features a hinged lid.
  • Carafe: Similar because both are tabletop containers for decanting and serving beverages; differs because a carafe is typically made of glass, lacks a handle or lid, and is associated with modern dining and wine service.
  • Decanter: Similar because both hold wine decanted from a primary container; differs because a decanter is specifically designed for aerating fine wines and displaying spirits, usually crafted from ornamental cut crystal with a glass stopper.
  • Tankard: Similar because both are historical drinking vessels often made of pewter or wood with handles and hinged lids; differs because a tankard is an individual drinking mug, whereas a flagon is a larger serving vessel used to pour into drinking cups.
  • Flask: Similar because both share an etymological origin and hold liquids; differs because a flask is a compact, portable, sealable bottle designed to be carried on one's person or in a bag.
  • Jug: Similar because both are handled containers for holding liquids; differs because jug is a broad, utilitarian modern term without the historical, ecclesiastical, or medieval associations of flagon.
  • Amphora: Similar because both are historical ceramic vessels; differs because an amphora is an ancient Greco-Roman two-handled storage jar with a tapered base, used for maritime shipping and bulk storage rather than table service.
