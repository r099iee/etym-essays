• Stimulus: Similar because both mean something that provokes action; differs because stimulus is scientific and psychological, while spur is concrete and figurative at once.
  • Incentive: Similar because both mean something that motivates; differs because incentive suggests a reward or advantage offered, while spur suggests urgency or pressure.
  • Goad: Similar because both are pointed instruments and both mean incitement; differs because goad is a stick for oxen and carries a harsher, more painful sense of compulsion.
  • Prod: Similar because both mean a poke that urges on; differs because prod is gentler, more informal, and lacks the chivalric and geographic senses.
  • Impetus: Similar because both mean a driving force; differs because impetus is abstract and physical (momentum), while spur retains its instrumental image.
  • Trigger: Similar because both mean something that sets off action; differs because trigger suggests a sudden single event (like firing a gun), while spur suggests continued urging.
  • Catalyst: Similar because both mean something that causes change; differs because catalyst is a scientific term implying the agent is not consumed, while spur implies direct, continued pressure.
  • Incitement: Similar because both mean urging to action; differs because incitement is often negative or legal (incitement to violence), while spur is usually positive or neutral.
  • Motivation: Similar because both mean a reason for action; differs because motivation is an internal psychological state, while spur is an external push.
  • Prompt: Similar because both mean a cue to act; differs because prompt is mild and quick, while spur implies energetic or urgent driving.
  • Projection: Similar because a geographic spur is a projection of a mountain; differs because projection is the general word for anything jutting out, while spur names the long lateral ridge descending from a height.
  • Ridge: Similar because a spur is a kind of ridge; differs because ridge is the general word for any long elevation, while spur is a ridge that branches laterally from a mountain mass.
