• Farriery: Similar because both belong to the care of horses' feet; differs because farriery is the name of the whole craft — trimming, treating and shoeing the hoof — while shoeing names specifically the act of fitting the iron shoe.
  • Horseshoeing: Similar because both name the fitting of shoes to a horse; differs because horseshoeing makes the animal explicit, while plain shoeing can also cover human footwear and other animals.
  • Shod: Similar because both concern the state of having shoes; differs because shod is the adjective or past participle marking the completed condition ('well-shod'), while shoeing names the ongoing act.
  • Shoe-making / Shoemaking: Similar because both concern shoes; differs because shoe-making is the manufacture of footwear in the factory or workshop, while shoeing is the fitting of shoes (usually iron horseshoes) to a foot or hoof.
  • Blacksmithing: Similar because both are smith's crafts that shape iron; differs because blacksmithing is the general working of iron, while shoeing is the specific farrier task of fitting horseshoes.
  • Smithing: Similar because both name the craftsman's working of metal; differs because smithing is the generic term for forging metal, while shoeing is the specific equine application.
  • Hoof-care / Hoof trimming: Similar because both concern the maintenance of the horse's foot; differs because hoof-care and hoof trimming are the general and preparatory maintenance of the hoof, while shoeing is the separate act of fitting the shoe.
  • Unshoeing: Similar because both are the -ing action of a shoe-verb; differs because unshoeing is the removal of shoes, the direct opposite of the fitting that shoeing names.
  • Footwear: Similar because both concern coverings for the feet; differs because footwear is the neutral collective noun for human shoes and boots, while shoeing is the active process of fitting them (or fitting metal shoes to animals).
  • Equipping / Arming: Similar because both mean furnishing with what is needed; differs because equipping and arming are general words for providing gear, while shoeing is the specific, material fitting of foot-coverings.
