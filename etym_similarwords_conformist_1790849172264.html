• Conventional: Similar because both adhere to established standards; differs because conventional often describes ordinary, customary practices without necessarily carrying the negative psychological stigma of blind subservience inherent in conformist.
  • Orthodox: Similar because both adhere to authorized doctrine; differs because orthodox specifically emphasizes theological, intellectual, or doctrinal correctness and tradition, whereas conformist emphasizes the social/behavioral act of yielding to pressure.
  • Traditionalist: Similar because both resist radical departure from norms; differs because a traditionalist consciously values historical heritage and continuity, whereas a conformist may simply yield to whatever current peer pressure or prevailing authority dictates.
  • Compliant: Similar because both involve obeying external rules; differs because compliant focuses on passive yielding or formal submission to orders, whereas conformist implies adopting the entire mindset, appearance, and values of the majority.
  • Obedient: Similar because both follow authority; differs because obedient is a neutral or virtuous description of carrying out duties and commands, while conformist carries critical connotations of unoriginality and intellectual cowardice.
  • Docile: Similar because both are easily led; differs because docile emphasizes gentle tractability and submissiveness of temperament, whereas conformist denotes a social alignment strategy to avoid ostracism.
