↓
Old English (pre-1100)
scrēawa
"shrew-mouse; small insectivorous
mammal"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
shrewe
"shrew-mouse; a spiteful,
scolding person"
↓
English (16th c.)
shrewd
"sharp-witted, cunning, astute;
earlier 'malignant, cursed'"
Note: scrēawa has no secure PIE/Germanic etymon (origin obscure). 'Shrewd' passed from 'wicked, cursed' — the shrew-mouse was reputed venomous — to 'cunning, astute' by the 16th c.
