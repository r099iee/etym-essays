• Strand: Similar because both name a thin, elongated piece of material (hair, rope, smoke); differs because a strand is linear, structured, and can be strong or deliberate, whereas a wisp is loose, airy, and fragile.
  • Streak: Similar because a wisp of smoke or light can appear as a thin trail; differs because a streak implies a marked, usually coloured or straight line with defined edges, not the delicate curling of a wisp.
  • Tendril: Similar because both suggest a thin, curving, outward-reaching form; differs because a tendril is a grasping plant organ with organic purpose, while a wisp is passive, drifting matter.
  • Curl: Similar because wisps often curl as they rise; differs because a curl names a specific spiral shape in hair, smoke, or paper, while wisp names the substance's delicate fineness and faintness regardless of exact shape.
  • Waft: Similar because both belong to the world of drifting vapour, smoke, and scent; differs because a waft is a noun of motion or a verb (“to waft”), describing a gentle current, while a wisp is a physical object, however thin.
  • Shred: Similar because both are small, thin pieces of something larger; differs because a shred is a torn remnant of a solid, often implying damage or neglect, whereas a wisp is whole in itself and implies delicacy, not tearing.
  • Sliver: Similar because both denote a thin, narrow piece; differs because a sliver is a cut or splinter of a solid (wood, glass), sharp and tangible, whereas a wisp is soft, vaporous, or hair-like.
  • Ghost: Similar because both are faint, insubstantial, and fleeting — “a wisp of smoke” is ghost-like; differs because a ghost is a supernatural being, whereas a wisp is an ordinary physical phenomenon used for its ghostly atmosphere.
  • Haze / Mist: Similar because a wisp of vapour is mist-like; differs because haze or mist is a diffuse, continuous layer filling the air, while a wisp is a discrete, isolated, thin strand within such a layer.
