PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*sekʷ-
‘to say, utter’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*sagjaną
‘to say, tell’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
secgan, sægst
‘to say; thou sayest’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
seyen, sayest
‘to say; thou sayest’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
say + -est
‘say; thou-marker’
↓
Modern English (archaic) (current)
sayest
‘thou sayest, you say’

Note: the -est ending continues Old English -est, from Proto-Germanic *-izi, the inherited second-person singular marker; the stem's deeper link to PIE *sekʷ- is the standard handbook connection via the suffixed form *sokʷ-yo-, though the exact vocalism is discussed rather than fully certain.
