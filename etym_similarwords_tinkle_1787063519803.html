• Clink: Similar because both name light metallic sounds, often of glass or coin; differs because a clink is a single sharp strike of harder objects (glasses, chains), while a tinkle is lighter, higher and often repeated.
  • Jingle: Similar because both denote light ringing sounds of small metal objects; differs because jingle suggests a sequence of mixed, rattling tones (sleigh bells, coins in a pocket), while tinkle is more delicate and bell-like.
  • Ring: Similar because both are sounds made by bells or struck metal; differs because ring is louder, purer and sustained, while tinkle is small, high and brief.
  • Chink: Similar because both denote light sounds of metal or glass; differs because chink is a thin, sharp, brief sound (of coins or glasses) with a harder edge, while tinkle is rounder and more musical.
  • Ting: Similar because both imitate the high-pitched ring of metal; differs because ting is a single, thin, ping-like sound, while tinkle implies lighter, smaller, often repeated ringing.
  • Tintinnabulation: Similar because both name bell-ringing sounds; differs because tintinnabulation is Latinate, grandiose and means loud or sustained ringing of bells, while tinkle is small and delicate.
  • Twinkle: Similar because both share the frequentative -le and a light, repeated quality; differs because twinkle is about light (stars, eyes), while tinkle is about sound.
