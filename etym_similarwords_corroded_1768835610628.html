• eroded: Similar in being worn away slowly; differs because eroded belongs to wind, water, and landscapes - surfaces worn by external friction, while corroded implies an active chemical agent gnawing from the surface inward.
  • rusted: Similar in being damaged by chemical action; differs because rusted is metal-specific, naming the brown oxidation of iron without the sense of consumption, while corroded conveys slow eating that leaves the object smaller, darker, and rougher.
  • tarnished: Similar in losing surface quality; differs because tarnished is a superficial loss of sheen - the moral dimming of reputation - while corroded involves real consumption of mass by acid or time.
  • decayed: Similar in gradual breakdown; differs because decayed describes organic rot, decomposition by living processes, while corroded is the mineral, chemical gnawing of metals and stone - the invisible teeth of chemistry.
  • decomposed: Similar in breaking down into parts; differs because decomposed implies disintegration into constituent elements, chiefly of organic matter, while corroded is the surface consumption of a material by chemical action.
  • dissolved: Similar in being destroyed by chemical action; differs because dissolved means dispersed entirely into liquid, vanishing as a solid, while corroded leaves a residue - the object survives, smaller and rougher.
  • eaten away: Similar in being consumed gradually; differs because eaten away is the plain, physical phrase without the Latinate, quasi-forensic weight of corroded - the invisible teeth of chemistry and time.
  • pitted: Similar in having a roughened, cratered surface; differs because pitted describes only the surface marking, the result, while corroded names the whole slow gnawing process that produced the pitting.
  • consumed: Similar in being used up or destroyed; differs because consumed is total and general, while corroded is partial and specific - a gnawing from the surface inward that leaves the core intact but diminished.
