• Transcendent: Similar because both originate from Latin transcendere; differs because transcendent denotes an entity or quality that exists completely outside, above, and independent of the physical universe (e.g. a transcendent deity), whereas transcendental in philosophy refers to the internal cognitive conditions that allow the mind to experience the world.
  • Metaphysical: Similar because both deal with reality beyond immediate physical sensation; differs because metaphysical is an umbrella term covering all questions of ontology, being, and cosmic substance, while transcendental specifically focuses on the structures of cognition or elevated spiritual intuition.
  • Mystical: Similar because both involve realities beyond mundane empirical observation; differs because mystical focuses on direct, emotional, ecstatic union with the divine, whereas transcendental can describe rigorous cognitive philosophy, literature, or mathematical constants.
  • Supernatural: Similar because both evoke realms beyond nature; differs because supernatural implies miraculous violations or suspensions of natural laws (ghosts, magic, divine intervention), whereas transcendental concerns the fundamental ground of nature and human understanding itself.
  • Noumenal: Similar because in Kantianism both pertain to the non-empirical domain; differs because the noumenal designates the unknowable thing-in-itself, whereas transcendental designates the human mental apparatus that actively constructs empirical phenomena.
  • Abstract: Similar because both concern non-material concepts; differs because abstract simply denotes separation from concrete particulars (e.g. abstract nouns), lacking the spiritual elevation or epistemological depth of transcendental.
  • Empirical (Antonym): The primary philosophical antonym, signifying that which is based entirely on direct sensory observation, physical experimentation, and posteriori experience.
