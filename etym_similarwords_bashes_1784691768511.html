• Thumps: Similar because both are heavy, blunt blows; differs because thumps stress the dullness of the impact, while bashes stress the violence of the strike and the bruise it leaves.
  • Blows: Similar because both are strikes delivered; differs because blows is colourless and general, while bashes is vivid, physical, and comic, picturing cudgels and swelling knuckles.
  • Clouts: Similar because both are heavy blows, often to the head; differs because clout is now dialectal or dated, while bash is living, informal English that also means an attempt or a party.
  • Bangs: Similar because both are hard hits; differs because bang stresses the sound of the impact, while bash stresses the force and its result.
  • Whacks: Similar because both are strong blows with an implement; differs because whack suggests a single flat stroke (often with a bat or cane), while bash pictures smashing or battering and the bruises it leaves.
  • Knocks: Similar because both are impacts; differs because a knock is lighter and more often a summons at a door, while a bash is heavy and damaging.
  • Bruises: Similar because both name the mark a heavy strike leaves on the flesh; differs because a bruise is the result only, while bashes also name the blows themselves.
  • Contusions: Similar because both denote bruising wounds; differs because contusion is the formal medical term, while bash is informal and physical.
  • Smashes: Similar because both involve violent, shattering force; differs because smash emphasises the destruction of the object struck, while bash emphasises the blow and the wound.
  • Wallops: Similar because both are heavy blows; differs because wallop is older and more dialectal, while bash is modern and wider-ranging in its extensions.
  • Raps: Similar because both are blows; differs because a rap is quick, light, and often knuckle- or cane-on-surface, while a bash is heavy and bruising.
