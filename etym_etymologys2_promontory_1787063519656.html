PIE (reconstructed, c. 4500–3000 BCE)
*men-
"to project, stand out, tower"
↓
Latin (compound verb, attested from 1st c. BCE)
prōminēre
"to jut forward, project"
↓
Latin (classical noun, formed on prōminēre)
prōmunturium
"projecting ridge, headland"
↓
Latin (reshaped by folk etymology after mōns "mountain")
prōmontorium
"headland, promontory"
↓
Middle French (15th c.)
promontoire
"headland, promontory"
↓
English (first attested c. 1530)
promontory


Note: the simple Latin verb minēre is not attested on its own; it survives only inside compounds (ēminēre, imminēre, prōminēre). Its descent from the PIE root *men- "to project" is widely accepted but necessarily reconstructed. The classical noun appears both as prōmunturium and as prōmontorium; the latter is a folk-etymological reshaping under the influence of mōns, montis "mountain".
