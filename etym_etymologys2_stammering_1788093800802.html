Expressive Germanic base (no secure PIE etymon)
*stam- / *stamm-
"to falter in speech" — imitative/onomatopoeic
creation within Germanic
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*stamraz (adj.)
"stammering, stuttering" — cf. Old Norse stamr
↓
Old English (attested c. 10th–11th c.)
stamerian
"to stammer, stutter"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
stameren
"to stammer" — frequentative -er suffix
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
stammer
"to falter in speaking"
↓
Modern English — -ing forms:
stammering
"speaking with repetitions, prolongations or blocks"

Note: whether an Indo-European etymon exists is debated; most handbooks
treat the base as expressive/onomatopoeic, coined within Germanic (cognates:
Old Norse stamr, Old High German stamelōn, German stammeln, Dutch stamelen).
English "stutter" (ME stutten) is a sibling formation of the same kind.
