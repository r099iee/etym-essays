Primary Definition & Core Concept
Entreaties (singular: entreaty) are earnest, impassioned, humble, or urgent pleas, prayers, or requests made to persuade someone to grant a favor, alter a decision, or show mercy. As a plural count noun, it denotes repeated or heartfelt acts of begging or petitioning, where the petitioner appeals directly to the emotions, compassion, moral duty, or goodwill of an authority figure, parent, judge, or deity.

Polysemy, Contextual Nuances & Literary Senses

  • Interpersonal Pleading & Emotional Appeals (Core Context): In familial, interpersonal, and dramatic literature (such as Daniel Defoe's Robinson Crusoe, where Crusoe acts against 'all the entreaties and persuasions of my mother and other friends'), entreaties represents deep, affectionate, and desperate appeals made out of love, concern, or sorrow to prevent someone from embarking on a ruinous or perilous path.
  • Legal, Diplomatic & Sovereign Petitions: In formal statecraft, diplomatic history, and jurisprudence, an entreaty is an official, deferential petition or formal overture presented to a monarch, governing council, or court seeking redress, pardon, peace terms, or executive clemency. It emphasizes the profound disparity in power between the supplicant and the sovereign entity.
  • Religious & Devotional Supplication: In theological and devotional literature, entreaties refers to fervent, humble prayers and intercessions directed toward God, saints, or divine providence, beseeching grace, forgiveness, or protection in moments of spiritual crisis.
  • The Underlying Verbal Sense ('To Entreat'): The base verb entreat historically meant 'to treat or handle someone in a specified manner' (e.g., 'he entreated them with great hospitality' or 'ill-entreated'), but gradually specialized exclusively into the meaning of 'to earnestly beseech, solicit, or importune'.
  • Rhetorical & Dramatic Function: In classical rhetoric and theatrical tragedy (from Shakespeare to Racine), entreaties serve as pivotal dramatic devices where protagonists attempt to avert impending doom through eloquent, tearful supplications, highlighting themes of free will versus tragic destiny.

Semantic Reasoning & Historical Bridges
The semantic evolution of entreaty is a remarkable journey from physical physical traction to social negotiation, and ultimately to emotional supplication. Originating from Latin trahere ('to drag, draw, pull'), the frequentative form tractāre ('to handle, manage, discuss') passed into Old French as traitier. By adding the intensive prefix en-, Anglo-French coined entraiter ('to handle, treat, discourse with'). Through late medieval diplomacy and courtly discourse, 'negotiating or discussing terms with someone' shifted into 'urgently seeking favorable terms', and by the 16th century, the nominal form entreaty crystallized specifically around passionate emotional and humble petitioning.
