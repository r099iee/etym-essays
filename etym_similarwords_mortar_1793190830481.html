• Pestle: Similar because mortar and pestle are the inseparable grinding pair, bought and used together; differs because the pestle is the club that pounds while the mortar is the bowl that holds.
  • Bowl: Similar because a mortar is bowl-shaped and holds material; differs because bowl is the light general vessel for serving and eating, while mortar is thick-walled and built to be beaten upon.
  • Crucible: Similar because both are stout heat- and blow-resistant vessels of craft and laboratory; differs because a crucible endures fire to melt metal, while a mortar endures pounding to crush solids.
  • Grinder: Similar because both reduce solids to powder; differs because grinder usually means the mill or machine with moving parts, while mortar is the hand-powered stone cup.
  • Cement: Similar because both bind masonry; differs because cement is the dry powder ingredient, while mortar is the wet workable paste spread between bricks.
  • Concrete: Similar because both are grey hardening building mixtures; differs because concrete includes coarse aggregate for mass structures, while mortar is the finer bedding and jointing paste.
  • Plaster: Similar because both are spreadable setting pastes of the building trade; differs because plaster coats and smooths surfaces, while mortar beds and binds units together.
  • Cannon: Similar because a mortar is a kind of short cannon; differs because cannon fires flat and far with a long barrel, while mortar lobs shells steeply with a stubby tube.
  • Howitzer: Similar because both are indirect-fire artillery lobbing shells in arcs; differs because a howitzer is the longer-ranged gun firing at varied angles, while a mortar is the light steep-angle tube of the infantry.
