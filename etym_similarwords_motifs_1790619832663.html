• themes: Similar because they are recurrent central ideas; differs because themes are abstract propositions while motifs are concrete repeatable units.
  • patterns: Similar because they involve repetition; differs because patterns stress all-over regularity while motifs stress discrete identity.
  • figures: Similar because they are pictorial shapes; differs because figures can be unique, while motifs must recur.
  • devices: Similar because they are artistic techniques; differs because devices stress artifice and function rather than visual identity.
  • leitmotifs: Similar because they are recurrent associative ideas, especially musical; differs because leitmotifs explicitly tag a person or idea, often Wagnerian.
  • tropes: Similar because they are recurrent rhetorical forms; differs because tropes are conventional and often verbal, with pejorative overuse nuance.
  • emblems: Similar because they are symbolic images; differs because emblems carry fixed allegorical meaning.
  • ornaments: Similar because they decorate surfaces; differs because ornaments can be non-repeating embellishment.
  • refrains: Similar because they repeat to unify; differs because refrains are strictly musical or poetic choruses.
  • cells: Similar because in music they are germinal short figures; differs because cells stress generative brevity rather than recognisability.
