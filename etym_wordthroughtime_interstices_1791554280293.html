- 15th Century: Late Middle English anatomical and surgical terminology - Perceived as a technical medical descriptor for natural spaces, cavities, or channels separating flesh, organs, and bones.
- 16th to 17th Century: Expansion to physical geometry, architecture, and natural philosophy - Used to describe narrow structural openings between stones, lattice threads, and planetary orbs; famously deployed in Samuel Johnson's 1755 dictionary definition of network.
- 18th to 19th Century: Solidification in crystallography, histology, and geological sciences - Acquired precise empirical status in scientific taxonomy, denoting crystal lattice voids (interstitial defects) and microscopic extracellular fluid spaces.
- 20th to 21st Century: Sociological, political, and philosophical abstraction - Retains formal scientific rigor while becoming a vital critical metaphor for marginal cultural niches, systemic blind spots, and unregulated spaces between powerful institutions.
