- Late 16th–17th Century: Legal & Ecclesiastical Interim - Emerged in Early Modern English legal jurisprudence, diplomatic treaties, and canon law to denote privileges, appointments, or statutes granted on a conditional basis pending royal or papal confirmation.
- 18th–19th Century: Parliamentary Codification & Revolutionary Governance - Widely utilized in constitutional statecraft and parliamentary procedure to describe temporary legislative measures, interim budgets, and 'provisional governments' during regime transitions.
- Late 19th–Mid 20th Century: Epistemic & Scientific Heuristic - Broadened into formal logic, scientific methodology, and philosophy of perception (notably Gestalt psychology), denoting working hypotheses or perceptual assumptions accepted temporarily to resolve incomplete data.
- Late 20th–21st Century: Institutional & Digital Pragmatism - Standardized across patent law (provisional patent applications), software development (provisional builds, provisional access), and corporate administration as the quintessential term for tentative operational authorization.
