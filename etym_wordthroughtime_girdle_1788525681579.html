- [Old English: c. 800–1100] - gyrdel - A functional leather, hemp, or woven belt worn around the waist to fasten garments and carry purses, keys, or weapons - [perception: essential everyday clothing item and symbol of readiness in Anglo-Saxon life]
- [Middle English: c. 1150–1450] - gurdel / girdel - Elevated into a powerful cultural symbol of knighthood, chastity, martial honor, and monastic humility (e.g., the Green Girdle in Sir Gawain and the Green Knight) - [perception: chivalric emblem, religious vestment, and moral talisman]
- [Renaissance & Early Modern: c. 1500–1700] - girdle - Poetic and theatrical usage flourishes (Shakespeare, Milton); applied metaphorically to the equator ("the girdle of the earth"), circular city walls, and floral perimeters - [perception: lyrical metaphor for containment, cosmic orbits, and aesthetic boundaries]
- [18th & 19th Century: c. 1750–1900] - girdle - Systematized in vertebrate anatomy (pectoral/pelvic girdle), gemology (diamond girdle), and forestry (tree girdling), while retaining lyrical power in Romantic and Aesthetic poetry (as in Wilde's Nightingale) - [perception: precise scientific anatomical term alongside elevated poetic diction]
- [20th–21st Century] - girdle - Became the definitive name for mid-20th-century elastic women's foundation shapewear, while persisting in medicine, botany, astronomy, and jewelry design - [perception: vintage fashion shapewear alongside enduring scientific and literary terminology]
