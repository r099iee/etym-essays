chirp
English (c. 1440; echoic coinage)
"to utter the short sharp note of a bird"
↓
chirrup
English (1570s; lengthened variant of chirp,
understood as chirp + "up", as in cheer up)
"to chirp softly and continuously,"
"with a trilled, cheerful note"
↓
chirrup + -ed
English (regular weak past form)
"made short cheerful trills"
↓
CHIRRUPED
English (modern form)
"chirped cheerfully and continuously"

Note: as a wholly onomatopoeic formation, "chirrup" has no PIE root, Latin ancestor, or sound-law descent — it was coined directly from the sound of small birds, with the "-up" element (if genuine) added by analogy with "cheer up".
