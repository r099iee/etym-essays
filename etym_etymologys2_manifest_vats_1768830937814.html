Root 1 — MANIFEST:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*man-
"hand"
↓
Latin (2nd c. BCE)
manus
"hand"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
manifestus
"palpable, clear, evident"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
manifeste
"evident, clear, obvious"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
manifest
"clearly visible or obvious"

Debate: Latin manifestus is possibly manus "hand" + *-festus, from PIE *gʰedʰ- "to seize" — "seized by the hand", i.e. obvious; this second element is debated.

Root 2 — VATS:

PIE (uncertain)
*ped-
"to go; to contain?"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–500 CE)
*fatą
"a vessel, container"
↓
Old English (8th c.)
fæt
"a vat, vessel, container"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
fat
"a vat, vessel"
↓
Modern English (16th c.)
vat
"a large container for liquids"
(initial f- voiced to v- in southern English dialects)
↓
Modern English (plural)
vats
"large containers for liquids"

Debate: the PIE link *ped- "to go, contain" is uncertain and not firmly established; the Proto-Germanic *fatą is secure (cf. Gothic fat, Old Norse fat, German Fass).
