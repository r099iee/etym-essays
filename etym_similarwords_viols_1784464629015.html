• Violin: Similar because both are bowed string instruments of old European music; differs because the violin is the later, unfretted family with arched back and shoulders, tuned in fifths and played overhand, while the viol is fretted, flat-backed, tuned in fourths and played underhand.
  • Viola: Similar because it shares the name-family and the bowed principle; differs because the viola is the alto member of the violin family, an arm-held unfretted instrument, not a fretted gamba-type viol.
  • Cello (Violoncello): Similar because the cello shares the bass register of large viols and is held between the knees; differs because it is an unfretted violin-family instrument with no consort history.
  • Double Bass: Similar because it covers the same deep bass range as the great bass viol (violone); differs because the modern double bass has no frets, a rounded back, and belongs to orchestral (mostly violin-family) practice.
  • Viola da gamba: Similar because it is the leg-held member of the viol family proper; differs in that the term specifically names the gamba type, whereas “viol” spans the whole consort family from treble to violone.
  • Violone: Similar because it is the largest bass viol; differs because “violone” names only that biggest instrument, while “viols” plural names the whole matched consort.
  • Vielle: Similar because it is a medieval bowed instrument and part of the word-family's ancestry; differs because the vielle (medieval fiddle) was a single melody instrument of the Middle Ages, with a flat-topped body and no fretted consort system.
  • Rebec: Similar because it is another bowed medieval instrument; differs because the rebec is small, pear-shaped, often carved from one piece of wood with three strings, played like a folk fiddle, not part of a fretted consort.
  • Lute: Similar because it is a plucked Renaissance/Baroque instrument with frets and consort culture; differs because it is plucked with fingers or plectrum, pear-shaped, and not bowed.
  • Lyre: Similar because both are old, courtly string instruments; differs because the lyre is ancient, yoke-framed, and plucked, not a fretted bowed instrument of the Renaissance.
  • Harp: Similar because both belong to the old European instrumentarium of high music; differs because the harp is a plucked, framed instrument with strings standing free, not a bowed, fretted instrument held in the hand.
  • Fiddle: Similar because both are bowed string instruments and “fiddle” can be a colloquial term for any bowed fiddle-type; differs because a fiddle in modern usage is a folk or violin-type instrument, unfretted and informal, while a viol is a specific historical fretted instrument.
