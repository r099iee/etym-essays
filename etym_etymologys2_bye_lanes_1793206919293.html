Element 1 — by:
PIE (reconstructed)
*h₂m̥bʰi
‘around, on both sides’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*bi
‘near, at, by, around’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
bi, by
‘near, beside, by’
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
bi, by
‘aside, near; secondary’

Element 2 — lane:
Old English (9th c.)
lanu
‘narrow passage, lane’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
lane
‘narrow way between hedges’
↓
Modern English (plural)
bye-lanes
‘minor side lanes’

Note: lane has no certain deeper etymology; Old English lanu stands without an agreed Proto-Germanic or PIE ancestor, and handbooks leave it obscure rather than force a root.
