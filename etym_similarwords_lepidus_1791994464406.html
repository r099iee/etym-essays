• Triumvir: Similar because Lepidus was historically one of the official triumvirs of Rome; differs because triumvir is a formal constitutional title for any magistrate sharing three-man supreme authority, whereas Lepidus designates this specific historical figure or serves as a literary metaphor for an overshadowed partner.
  • Figurehead: Similar because Lepidus is remembered as an ineffective, exploited member of a ruling council; differs because a figurehead is an explicitly ceremonial leader possessing zero actual authority by design, whereas Lepidus initially commanded massive veteran armies, held legitimate constitutional imperium, and ruled major provinces before being politically outmaneuvered.
  • Mark Antony (Marcus Antonius): Similar because both were veteran Caesarian commanders and triumvirs who allied against Cicero and Caesar's assassins; differs because Antony was a magnetic, brilliant military general who commanded the Roman East, whereas Lepidus lacked Antony's military dominance and oratorical power.
  • Octavian (Augustus Caesar): Similar because both were triumvirs claiming Julius Caesar's political inheritance; differs because Octavian was Caesar's adopted son and a consummate political mastermind who systematically eliminated both Lepidus and Antony to establish the Roman Empire.
  • Crassus (Marcus Licinius Crassus): Similar because Crassus was also the third, eventually eclipsed member of a Roman three-man alliance (the First Triumvirate with Caesar and Pompey); differs because Crassus was the wealthiest plutocrat in Rome who perished in battle against Parthia at Carrhae, whereas Lepidus survived peacefully in house arrest into old age.
  • Junior Partner / Cat's-paw: Similar because in modern political commentary "a Lepidus" denotes a minor or exploited partner in an executive coalition; differs because general idiom lacks the rich historical resonance of Roman patrician politics, civil war proscriptions, and the sacrosanct office of Pontifex Maximus.
