• Uncontrollably: Similar in meaning without control; differs because uncontrollably is the broad, neutral adverb for any loss of control, lacking both the convulsive quality and the medical-historical freight of hysterically.
  • Frantically: Similar in meaning with agitated urgency; differs because frantic stresses desperate, hurried action (rushing about frantically) and is more panicked than overcome, whereas hysterically suggests an overflow of emotion such as laughter or sobbing.
  • Convulsively: Similar in meaning with physical spasming; differs because convulsively emphasises the mechanical jerking and twitching of the body without the emotional colour, whereas hysterically names the wild emotion that drives the spasms.
  • Desperately: Similar in meaning with intense urgency; differs because desperately implies hopelessness and a grim, goal-directed purpose, while hysterically means dissolved and lost in feeling.
  • Wildly: Similar in meaning in an uncontrolled manner; differs because wildly is the loose, everyday intensifier, while hysterically ties the lack of control to overwhelming emotion.
  • Manically: Similar in meaning with frenzied energy; differs because manically implies agitated excitation and restless activity, whereas hysterically can describe grief or mirth that is not at all energetic.
  • Frenziedly: Similar in meaning with wild agitation; differs because frenziedly pictures a frantic rush or fury, while hysterically pictures an emotional breakdown such as uncontrolled weeping or laughter.
  • Violently: Similar in meaning with extreme force; differs because violently stresses physical force or intensity of effect, not the emotional convulsion that hysterically names.
  • Excessively: Similar in meaning to an extreme degree; differs because excessively is a purely quantitative word, whereas hysterically describes the quality and cause of the excess (overwhelming emotion).
  • Helplessly: Similar in meaning without the power to control oneself; differs because helplessly is general and passive, while hysterically specifies the convulsive, emotional character of the helplessness.
