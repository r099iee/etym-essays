No reconstructable PIE root. *knok- / *knuk- is an imitative, sound-symbolic Germanic base meaning "to strike sharply, tap, knock" (the sound of a blow itself). - Proto-Germanic *knuk- / *knok- (imitative base; c. 1st millennium BCE–CE, no secure PIE ancestor) → Old English cnocian (West Saxon) / cnucian (Anglian) "to pound, beat; to knock at a door" (attested from c. 8th c.) → Middle English knokken "to strike a blow, hit against, tap" (12th–15th c.) → Middle English knokke (noun, 14th c.) "a blow, or the sound of a blow" → Middle English knokker(e) (agent noun, 14th c.) "one who or that which knocks" → Modern English KNOCKER "a device, heavy tool, or instrument used for striking forcefully to create noise or pound objects" (sense stable from 14th c.; door-knocker from 16th c.; knocker-up "paid waker" 19th c.; figurative "detractor" after 1892). Note on debate: the kn- cluster is part of a wider expressive Germanic set (knob, knoll, knuckle, knurl) that has no reliable Indo-European pedigree; some older dictionaries speculated about links to PIE *gen- "to press" or *ken- "to rub", but these are not accepted. The word is best treated as imitative/sound-symbolic, and no stage between the Germanic base and Old English is attested.
