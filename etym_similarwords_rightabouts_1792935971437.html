• Right-about turn: Similar because it names the same 180-degree pivot to the right; differs because right-about turn is the full modern drill phrase for one exact movement, while rightabouts is older, fused, and often plural or adverbial.
  • About-face: Similar because both reverse front to rear on command; differs because about-face is the generic parade reversal without specifying the pivoting side, while rightabouts insists the turn goes over the right shoulder.
  • Wheel: Similar because cavalry and artillery wheel right or left in ordered arcs; differs because a wheel is usually a quarter-turn by a body of men, while a rightabout is a full half-turn by each man.
  • Volte-face: Similar because both describe a sharp reversal of course or opinion; differs because volte-face is literary and political, borrowed from French fencing, while rightabouts is homely drill and frontier geography.
  • Thereabouts: Similar because it shares the -abouts machinery meaning surrounding parts; differs because thereabouts points vaguely near some mentioned place or figure, while rightabouts points directionally to the right-hand quarter.
  • Whereabouts: Similar because it belongs to the same family of -abouts locality nouns; differs because whereabouts asks or states position openly, while rightabouts already answers: off to the right.
  • Offside: Similar because both divide the world into right and left of a line of travel; differs because offside is the driver’s right-hand side of a road or team, technical and lateral, while rightabouts is the whole country on that hand.
  • Starboard quarter: Similar because it names the right-rear sector from which a turn begins; differs because it is strictly nautical and relative to a vessel’s stern, while rightabouts belongs ashore to feet, hoofs, and wheels.
