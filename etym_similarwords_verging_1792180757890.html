• Bordering: Similar because both denote occupying the immediate boundary or threshold of a state (e.g., bordering on the absurd); differs because bordering evokes a flat, static geographic perimeter, whereas verging suggests dynamic movement, momentum, or a leaning tendency toward a threshold.
  • Approaching: Similar because both indicate decreasing distance toward a specific state or condition; differs because approaching is a neutral, open-ended directional descriptor that can occur far from the destination, while verging implies being directly on the very brink or cusp.
  • Skirting: Similar because both describe activity taking place around the outer edge or margin of a category; differs because skirting emphasizes deliberately avoiding, evading, or steering clear of the core (e.g., skirting the law), whereas verging denotes leaning dangerously close to falling into it.
  • Hovering: Similar because both express proximity to a critical threshold (e.g., hovering around freezing); differs because hovering implies a suspended, indecisive, or fluctuating state above or near a mark, while verging emphasizes a directional incline toward an extreme condition.
  • Inching: Similar because both denote gradual progression toward a limit; differs because inching focuses on the slow, laborious increments of physical or metaphorical distance traveled, whereas verging characterizes the acute qualitative closeness of the states.
  • Touching: Similar because both signify minimal or zero spatial/conceptual separation; differs because touching denotes actual physical or conceptual contact, whereas verging preserves the delicate distinction of being immediately adjacent without having completely crossed the line.
  • Nearing: Similar because both indicate close proximity to a destination or state; differs because nearing is broader and more common in register, lacking the elevated, precarious, and intense connotations characteristic of verging.
  • Approximating: Similar because both convey high similarity to an ideal or extreme state; differs because approximating belongs to analytical, mathematical, or empirical registers signifying likeness of measurement, while verging carries an organic, often perilous narrative tension.
  • Impinging: Similar because both describe coming into close contact with a boundary; differs because impinging denotes an aggressive encroachment, collision, or intrusive impact upon someone else's territory or rights, whereas verging denotes self-inclination toward a condition.
