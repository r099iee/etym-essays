• Capes: Similar because both are projecting points of land extending into the sea; differs because a cape is typically a larger, more prominent coastal feature (often a major turning-point of navigation), while a headland can be any size of projecting rocky point.
  • Promontories: Similar because both name high land jutting into water; differs because promontory is the more formal, Latin-derived term (from prominere "to jut out") used in geography, while headland is the everyday English word.
  • Points: Similar because a point is a narrow tapering projection of land into water; differs because point usually implies a low, sharp, small projection, while a headland is typically high and cliff-lined.
  • Forelands: Similar because foreland names a projecting point of land before (in front of) the coast; differs because foreland is more technical and often denotes the outer, seaward edge of a region, while headland is the general term.
  • Ness: Similar because ness (Old English næss) is an old Germanic word for a headland or promontory (surviving in place-names like Sheerness); differs because ness is now archaic/dialectal, while headland is the modern standard.
  • Bluffs: Similar because a bluff is a steep headland or cliff overlooking water; differs because bluff emphasises the steep, broad front face, while headland emphasises the projecting, jutting-out nature of the land.
  • Bays (complementary): Similar because bays and headlands always occur together along irregular coasts; differs because a bay is the indentation or recessed water area, the exact complement of the projecting headland.
