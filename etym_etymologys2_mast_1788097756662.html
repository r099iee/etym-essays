Note: Latin mālus “mast” is a cognate — a sister word, not an ancestor — usually derived from *mazdos with an irregular Italic change of the -zd- cluster; the detail is debated (the change is sometimes called “Sabine”). The other English word “mast” (beech-mast, oak-mast, “nuts and fruit fallen for fodder”) is an unrelated homonym, from a Germanic root meaning “food, fattening”, possibly continuing PIE *mad- “to be moist, juicy”.
