• Alfred: The personal name alone; similar in naming the same man, but it lacks the royal title and the honorific, and it can refer to thousands of other people.
  • Ælfrǣd: The Old English form of the name; similar in being the historical name, but it is the ninth-century spelling and pronunciation, not the modern English form.
  • Alfred of Wessex: A neutral historical designation; similar in identifying the king, but it emphasises his kingdom rather than his later reputation.
  • King of the Anglo-Saxons: The style used in some of Alfred's charters and by Asser; similar as a royal title, but it is a contemporary formulation, not the modern epithet.
  • the Great: The honorific alone; similar in conveying the same judgement, but it is a generic label shared with Charlemagne, Cnut and others.
  • Charlemagne: Another monarch styled "the Great"; similar as a comparison, but he was a Frankish emperor and a very different kind of ruler.
  • Cnut the Great: A Danish king of England also called "the Great"; similar in ruling England and in the epithet, but he belonged to the eleventh century and to the Danish dynasty.
  • Æthelstan: Alfred's grandson and the first king of a united England; similar as a foundational monarch, but his reputation rests on conquest and coinage rather than on learning and law.
  • Edward the Elder: Alfred's son and successor; similar in continuing his father's work, but he is remembered as a soldier and administrator, not as a sage.
  • Guthrum: The Danish leader defeated at Edington; similar in being central to the same story, but he is Alfred's antagonist, not a synonym.
  • Asser: Alfred's Welsh biographer; similar in being bound up with the king's reputation, but he is a source for the legend rather than a name for the man.
