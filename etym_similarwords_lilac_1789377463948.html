• Lavender: Similar because both denote pale, soothing shades of light purple derived from fragrant garden shrubs; differs because lavender has distinctly cool, bluish undertones and is named after the mint-family herb *Lavandula*, whereas lilac possesses warmer, softer, pinkish-purple undertones and is named after the olive-family shrub *Syringa*.
  • Mauve: Similar because both are soft, muted, pale purple hues popular in 19th-century fashion; differs because mauve is a slightly darker, grayer, and more desaturated violet (famously synthesized as the first synthetic aniline dye by William Perkin in 1856 from French *mauve*, "mallow"), whereas lilac is lighter, brighter, and more floral.
  • Violet: Similar because both are floral terms naming purple colors; differs because violet represents a deep, intense, saturated spectral hue (the shortest wavelength of visible light) named after the small ground flower *Viola*, whereas lilac is an airy, pale, pastel tint with significant white and pink components.
  • Amethyst: Similar because both describe shades of purple associated with beauty and ornament; differs because amethyst denotes a rich, transparent, vitreous gemstone purple of moderate to high saturation with deep royal brilliance, whereas lilac is an opaque, delicate, chalky pastel shade.
  • Heliotrope: Similar because both are Victorian floral terms naming fragrant garden flowers and their associated purple hues; differs because heliotrope is a warmer, deeper, distinctly reddish-purple or magenta-tinted shade named after *Heliotropium arborescens*, whereas lilac remains cooler and softer.
  • Orchid: Similar because both are delicate, feminine pastel shades of purple named after flowering plants; differs because orchid is a brighter, more vibrant pinkish-purple with strong magenta tones, whereas lilac is paler, dustier, and more understated.
