CHAIN 1 — DWELLINGS PIE *dheu- 'to be cloudy, smoky, obscure' (reconstructed), extended form *dhwel- 'to become dim, be confused, be led astray' → Proto-Germanic *dwaljan 'to lead astray, deceive, delay' (causative of the 'clouded' root) → Old English dwellan 'to hinder, mislead, cause to linger' (attested before 1000 CE); noun dwola 'error, heresy' → Middle English dwellen 'to tarry, linger, remain' (12th–13th c.); sense shift: 'to be delayed' becomes 'to stay' → Middle English dwellen 'to reside, make one's home' (14th c.) → English dwell 'to live, reside' + gerund suffix -ing → English dwelling 'a place of residence' (target root word; the noun formed from the gerund) CHAIN 2 — LOOMED (No secure PIE root: the verb is of obscure, probably Germanic-dialectal origin, first recorded in the 1540s. The chain below gives the probable source candidates and states the uncertainty explicitly.) ? East Frisian lōmen / lomen 'to move slowly, be awkward, be weary' (dialectal) ? Swedish loma 'to move slowly' (dialectal) ? Danish lome 'to move slowly' (dialectal) → English loom 'to appear indistinctly, to take shape darkly and largely' (1540s) → English loom 'to tower oppressively; of an event, to impend' (16th–19th c. extensions) → English loomed (past tense; target root word) NOTE ON THE HOMOGRAPH: The noun loom 'a weaver's frame' is a DIFFERENT word, from Old English gelōma 'tool, implement, utensil', and is unrelated to the verb to loom 'to appear indistinctly'. No verified PIE ancestor for the verb exists; the older suggestion connecting the verb to the weaver's loom has been rejected. The visual sense 'to appear darkly and largely' developed from the sense 'to move slowly and heavily' recorded in the North Sea coastal dialects.
