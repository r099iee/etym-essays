LINEAR CHAIN (ASCRIBE) 1. PIE *skreibʰ- / *sker-ibʰ- (to scratch, cut, separate) — the reconstructed root of the "cut/scratch → write" family; attested only by reconstruction, not in any written document (estimated 5th–4th millennium BCE). 2. Latin scrībere (to write; originally "to make marks by scratching or cutting on a surface") — attested from the earliest Latin records (7th–1st c. BCE); continues the PIE "scratching" image of writing as incising. 3. Latin adscrībere / ascrībere (ad- "to, toward" + scrībere; literally "to write in addition / write to"; then "to add in writing, enroll, register on an account, impute, attribute") — classical Latin, 1st c. BCE onward; the prefix ad- assimilates to as- before the cluster sc-, giving the surface form ascribere. 4. English ascribe (to attribute, assign credit, blame, cause, or origin; also earlier spelled adscribe) — borrowed c. 1440–1470, either directly from Latin ascribere or via Middle French ascrire; the authorship sense ("ascribe a work to an author") is recorded from the 16th century onward. Derivatives: ascription (16th c.), ascribable (18th c.). NOTE ON DEBATE: The OED notes that English ascribe may have entered via Middle French ascrire rather than straight from Latin; the direct-Latin route cannot be proved, though the form is near-identical on both paths. The PIE reconstruction of the writing-root is also debated — some scholars reconstruct only *sker- "to cut" with various extensions; *skreibʰ- is a standard but not certain reconstruction.
