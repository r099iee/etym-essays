- "To this place" - the standard everyday adverb of approach for all of Old and Middle English; used in ordinary speech ("come hider") with no literary colour.
- One of a four-way spatial set (hither / thither / whither / here) that governed how English speakers pointed in space for a thousand years.
- 16th–17th centuries: still normal speech but already the high register of Bible and stage; Shakespeare's and the King James Bible's standard word for approach.
- 18th–19th centuries: retreats from colloquial speech to formal and poetic prose; fixed in "hither and thither" and "hitherto."
- 20th–21st centuries: effectively a fossil phrase-word — "hither and thither," "come-hither look," mock-archaic and fantasy usage; understood by everyone, produced by almost no one.
