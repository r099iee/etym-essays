• Explicitly: Similar because both mean ‘stated in clear, plain terms’. Differs because explicitly stresses the clarity of the statement itself, while expressly adds deliberate intent and a formal, often legal commitment.
  • Deliberately: Similar because both can mean ‘on purpose’. Differs because deliberately emphasises volition and premeditation without implying anything was said, whereas expressly usually presupposes words or a declared purpose.
  • Specifically: Similar as an adverb of precise reference (‘that very thing’). Differs because specifically narrows the reference — ‘especially this’ — while expressly marks explicitness and intention: ‘in express terms’.
  • Purposely: Similar in the ‘intentionally’ sense. Differs because purposely is informal and says nothing about language or statement, while expressly carries the whole weight of ‘express terms’.
  • Avowedly: Similar as an adverb of open, public declaration. Differs because avowedly emphasises admission and confession of a position, whereas expressly emphasises the exact words of an order, term or intention.
  • Unambiguously: Similar in the ‘clear-meaning’ sense. Differs because unambiguously is a negative-path word (free of ambiguity), while expressly is positive: the thing has been pressed out into distinct speech.
