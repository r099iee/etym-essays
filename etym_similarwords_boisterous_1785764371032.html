• Rowdy: Similar because both describe loud, unruly behaviour among people; differs because rowdy more strongly suggests disorder, roughhousing, or troublemaking that borders on the objectionable, while boisterous more readily allows joyful, celebratory exuberance without implying malice or near-violence — the sailors in the card’s sentence are singing, not brawling.
  • Exuberant: Similar because both convey high energy and an overflow of spirits; differs because exuberant need not be noisy or physically rough — one can be exuberantly quiet in one’s enthusiasm — while boisterous requires audible, kinetic commotion as part of its core image.
  • Rambunctious: Similar because both describe playful, energetic disorder, especially of children and animals; differs because rambunctious is more colloquial (particularly American) and restricted in scope, while boisterous is broader in register and also standard for winds and seas — a sea is boisterous, never rambunctious.
  • Turbulent: Similar because both can describe agitated, disordered movement; differs because turbulent is serious and unstable — political turmoil, violent air currents — with no festive human cheer, while boisterous, when applied to people, carries warmth and energy, and its weather uses are vigorous but less technical than turbulent.
  • Uproarious: Similar because both are loud and full of commotion; differs because uproarious centres on hilarious laughter and uproar, the noise of mirth, while boisterous covers general energetic racket — play, song, movement — not only amusement.
  • Noisy: Similar because both involve much sound; differs because noisy is a plain, neutral descriptor of volume, carrying no judgement of energy, spirit, or disorder, while boisterous adds exuberance, vigour, and often warmth — a noisy fan can be tedious, a boisterous one merely lively.
  • Unruly: Similar because both describe behaviour that escapes control; differs because unruly stresses disobedience, resistance to rule, or disorder, without necessarily being loud or cheerful, while boisterous stresses noisy high spirits and need not involve any defiance of authority.
  • Clamorous: Similar because both describe loud outcry and sound; differs because clamorous emphasizes the din itself — the clamour, the shouting — and often carries urgency or protest, while boisterous emphasizes rough, energetic, often festive spirits rather than the mere volume of the noise.
