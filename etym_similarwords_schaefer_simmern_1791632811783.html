• Britsch: Similar because Gustaf Britsch provided the theoretical foundation of visual conceiving; differs because Britsch was a German theoretician who died young leaving abstract manuscripts, whereas Schaefer-Simmern tested and proved these concepts through extensive practical studio experiments in America.
  • Arnheim: Similar because Rudolf Arnheim championed the Gestalt psychology of visual form and worked closely with Schaefer-Simmern; differs because Arnheim was primarily an academic perceptual psychologist and writer, whereas Schaefer-Simmern was an active studio instructor and experimental educator.
  • Herbert Read: Similar because both advocated that art education is vital for overall human psychological growth and democratic society; differs because Read approached art pedagogy from a broad philosophical, literary, and psychoanalytic angle, whereas Schaefer-Simmern relied strictly on structural visual-gestalt development.
  • Viktor Lowenfeld: Similar because both were mid-century Austrian/German refugee art educators in America who analyzed developmental stages in art; differs because Lowenfeld categorized children into psychological personality types (visual vs. haptic), whereas Schaefer-Simmern focused on the universal morphological unfolding of visual order.
  • John Dewey: Similar because Dewey strongly validated Schaefer-Simmern's findings as real-world proof that aesthetic creation is rooted in natural biological experience; differs because Dewey was a general pragmatist philosopher rather than an experimental visual educator.
  • Franz Cižek: Similar because both recognized child art as an autonomous, genuine creative expression rather than deficient adult art; differs because Cižek emphasized spontaneous untutored emotional expression, whereas Schaefer-Simmern demonstrated lawful structural-visual progression.
