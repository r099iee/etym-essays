• Dining: Similar because both describe taking a meal, especially the main or evening meal; differs because dining is formal, elegant, multi-course and socially grand, while supping is homely, intimate and redolent of simple evening fare.
  • Eating: Similar because both denote consuming food; differs because eating is the neutral, universal term, whereas supping is restricted to the evening meal or to the slow taking of liquids or spoon-food, and carries an archaic, literary warmth.
  • Sipping: Similar because both describe taking liquid in small quantities; differs because sipping is confined to liquid taken in tiny drops or tastes, while supping can mean either sipping liquid or eating the whole evening meal.
  • Spooning / Lading: Similar because both describe taking soup or broth by spoonfuls; differs because spooning and lading are purely instrumental actions (using a spoon or ladle), while supping may be by any means and also covers the evening-meal sense.
  • Tasting / Sampling: Similar because both involve small amounts of food or drink; differs because tasting is exploratory, professional and culinary-analytical, while supping is a full, habitual act of nourishment.
  • Feasting: Similar because both denote eating, often in company; differs because feasting implies abundance, revelry and celebration, while supping is subdued and simple.
  • Guzzling / Swilling: Similar because both denote drinking; differs because guzzling and swilling are greedy, rapid and noisy — the direct opposite of supping's measured, gentle rhythm.
  • Nibbling: Similar because both describe taking food in small increments; differs because nibbling applies to dry food taken in tiny bites, while supping applies to liquid or semi-liquid food taken in mouthfuls or spoonfuls.
  • Drinking: Similar because both denote taking in liquid; differs because drinking is generic and applies to any beverage, while supping is specific to small, rhythmic mouthfuls, spoon-taking, or the evening meal.
  • Savoring: Similar because both imply slow, appreciative consumption; differs because savoring is a mental and gustatory attitude of appreciation applied to any food, while supping is the concrete act of evening dining or sipping broth.
