PIE *klā- / *kleh₂- "to spread out flat, to lay out" (reconstructed; the root commonly cited for the Germanic "load" family — this PIE reconstruction is itself debated, and some authorities prefer to leave the Germanic verb without a secure Indo-European etymology) → Proto-Germanic *hlaþaną "to load, to pile up; to draw up water" (attested across the family: Old English hladan, Old Norse hlaða, Dutch laden, German laden, c. 500 BCE–200 CE) → Old English hladan "to load, to burden, to charge; to draw up water" (attested from the 9th century in glosses and prose; the past participle hladen "loaded, charged") → Middle English lade, laden "to load; the participle laden" (11th–15th c.; the participle also reinforced by Old Norse hlaða) → Modern English lade (verb, now rare) and laden (past participle / adjective, "heavily loaded, weighed down").

NUANCE / DEBATE NOTE: The PIE reconstruction *klā- is not universally accepted; Kroonen and others treat Proto-Germanic *hlaþaną as of unclear ultimate origin. The noun "load" is historically a separate word (Old English lād "way, course, journey") whose sense converged with "lade" in Middle English; no known Indo-Iranian (Sanskrit/Hindi) cognate exists, the root being confined to the western branches of Indo-European.
