Old French (12th–13th c.)
rascaille
'rabble, mob, outcast'
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
rascaile
'rabble; camp-followers'
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
rascal
'rogue, villain, knave'
↓
Modern English (16th c. onward)
rascal + -ly
'rogue' + 'like, -ish'
↓
Modern English (current)
rascally
'roguish; worthless'

Note: the deeper origin of Old French rascaille is debated and uncertain: older guesses linked it to raschier meaning to scrape or to raser meaning to shave, as if the rabble were the scrapings of society, but no derivation is secure, so the chain honestly begins at the attested Old French form.
