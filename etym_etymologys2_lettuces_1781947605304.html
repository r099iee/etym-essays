Derivation chain for LETTUCES (single root word, the plural of lettuce): 1. Proto-Indo-European *g(a)lág- / *glakt- "milk" (reconstructed root; reflexes include Greek gála, gen. gálaktos "milk", Latin lac "milk", Tocharian klāk "milk"), c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE. 2. Latin lac, lactis "milk" (from PIE *glakt-; attested from the 3rd c. BCE, e.g. Plautus). 3. Latin lactūca "lettuce" — an adjective formation on lac/lact- with the suffix -ūcus, so called from the milky sap that exudes when the plant is cut; attested 1st c. CE, Pliny the Elder, Naturalis Historia. 4. Old French laitue "lettuce" — the regular Gallo-Romance development of lactūca, with the cluster -ct- softening to -it-; attested from the 10th–12th c. 5. Anglo-French / Middle English letuse, lettuse, letuce "lettuce" — borrowed from Old French; attested 14th c. (c. 1387). 6. Early Modern English lettuce "lettuce" — re-spelt with -tt- under the influence of the Latin etymon lactūca; 16th c. 7. Modern English plural LETTUCES "lettuce plants, heads of lettuce" — plural formed with the inherited Germanic plural suffix -s/-es (from Old English -as); 17th c.–present. Note: The reconstructed PIE root behind "milk" is standardly given as *g(a)lág- (with a variant *glakt- for the attested Greek and Latin oblique forms); the exact ablaut shape is debated among Indo-Europeanists, but the family connection is secure. The English plural form is the target word on this card.
