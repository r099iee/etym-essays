Note: Latin oblitterāre is an explicit compound of ob- ('over, across') and littera ('letter of the alphabet'), reinforced in Roman popular usage by semantic contamination with oblīvīscī ('to forget'). The Latin noun littera is of disputed ultimate origin, generally regarded by historical phonologists as an Etruscan borrowing from Western Greek diphthera ('prepared leather, parchment writing tablet').
