• Airāvata: Similar because both are primordial cosmic guardian elephants (*Diggajas*) created by Brahmā to support the earth; differs because Airāvata is the pristine, pure white king of all elephants, guardian of the East, and personal mount of Indra, whereas Añjana is the glossy, pitch-black guardian of the West (or Southwest) paired with the cow-elephant Añjanāvatī.
  • Vāmana: Similar because both are celebrated *Aṣṭa-Diggajas* cited in the *Rāmāyaṇa* as the primordial ancestors of Ayodhyā's royal war-elephants; differs because Vāmana is the guardian of the Southern quarter (and shares the name of Viṣṇu's dwarf avatar), whereas Añjana guards the West and is specifically characterized by his ink-black collyrium hue.
  • Puṇḍarīka: Similar because both belong to the eight cosmic world-bearing bull elephants; differs because Puṇḍarīka (literally "white lotus") guards the Southeast and possesses a luminous lotus-white complexion, contrasting with the dark collyrium hide of Añjana.
  • Diggaja: Similar because Añjana is an exemplary Diggaja; differs because Diggaja is the generic categorical Sanskrit compound ("quarter-elephant" or "world-bearer") denoting any of the eight cosmic elephants supporting the universe, whereas Añjana is the proper name of one specific individual guardian.
  • Sārvabhauma: Similar because both are cosmic guardian bull elephants supporting cardinal points; differs because Sārvabhauma ("ruler of the entire earth") is the guardian elephant of the North, associated with the god Kubera, whereas Añjana guards the West.
  • Kumuda: Similar because both are *Aṣṭa-Diggajas* bearing the cosmic globe; differs because Kumuda (literally "white water-lily") guards the Southwest (or South) with a distinctive pale lily tint, unlike the glossy black pigment of Añjana.
  • Puṣpadanta: Similar because both are cosmic bull elephants supporting the earth; differs because Puṣpadanta ("flower-toothed") guards the Northwest and is noted for his magnificent floral-contoured tusks.
  • Supratīka: Similar because both are celestial bull elephants of ancient myth; differs because Supratīka ("of handsome countenance") guards the Northeast, associated with Lord Śiva (Īśāna), while Añjana presides over the West.
  • collyrium: Similar because collyrium is the exact Western pharmaceutical and medical translation of Sanskrit añjana; differs because collyrium is a sterile Greek/Latin medical term (from Greek kollyrion, "eye-salve roll") lacking the rich mythological, epic elephant, and Vedantic spiritual layers intrinsic to añjana.
  • kohl: Similar because both denote the traditional black cosmetic eye-paint applied around the eyelids; differs because kohl is an Arabic loanword (from al-kuḥl) used primarily in Middle Eastern and modern cosmetic contexts, whereas añjana is the ancient Sanskrit terminology carrying deep liturgical, Ayurvedic, and philosophical connotations.
  • unguent: Similar because both are soothing, semi-solid medicinal substances smeared upon the body; differs because unguent is a broad general English term for any ointment, salve, or balm applied to any part of the body, whereas añjana is overwhelmingly specialized for the eyes.
  • ointment: Similar because both are fatty or oily medicinal preparations rubbed onto skin or mucous membranes; differs because ointment is an ordinary pharmaceutical category, lacking the specific ocular focus, antimony mineral heritage, and mythic dimension of Sanskrit añjana.
