PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*weǵʰ-
‘to go, move, carry’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*wegaz
‘way, road, course’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
weg
‘way, path, road’
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
weyside
‘margin beside the road’
↓
Modern English (current)
wayside
‘roadside margin’

Second element — side:
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*sīdōn-
‘broad, long, flank’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
sīde
‘side, flank, lateral surface’
↓
Modern English (current)
side
‘lateral edge or surface’

Note: way from PIE *weǵʰ- is secure and widely attested, while the deeper origin of Germanic *sīdōn- is less certain, with handbooks divided between a length-word and a flank-word, so the second chain is probable at the Germanic stage and debated below it.
