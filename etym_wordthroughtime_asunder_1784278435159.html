Old English Fused Phrase - Asunder descends from the Old English prepositional phrase on sundran, literally "in a separate state," fused by the 13th century into a single adverb meaning apart or into pieces.
Violent Physical Register - From its earliest use asunder describes physical rupture under force — things torn, rent, or burst asunder — the word of shattered objects and divided bodies in medieval and early modern English.
Scriptural and Formal Use - Through the Bible and the Book of Common Prayer, asunder enters the solemn marriage formula ("what God hath joined together let not man put asunder"), fixing the word in a narrow band of elevated, ceremonial contexts.
Contemporary Sense - Today asunder is archaic and literary, surviving mainly in the formulaic pairings torn/rent/split asunder and in the marriage service, while apart is the everyday word for separation.
