- 15th Century (c. 1420s): Late Middle English Medical Importation - Borrowed from Old French and Latin into surgical and anatomical manuscripts to denote gangrenous wounds, mortified flesh, and corrupt bodily humors.
- 16th–17th Century: Humoral Pathology & Plague Diagnostics - Widely codified in early modern medicine under diagnoses such as 'putrid fever' (typhus or septicemia), where internal humors were believed to rot within the living body.
- 18th–19th Century: Environmental Miasma & Gothic Literature - Centralized in sanitary debates regarding the noxious 'putrid miasmas' rising from urban slaughterhouses and churchyards, while serving Gothic fiction to evoke corpses and vaults.
- Mid-20th Century: Colloquial Slang & Hyperbolic Disdain - Developed an informal colloquial sense in British and American youth slang meaning thoroughly unpleasant, wretched, worthless, or of terrible quality ('a putrid movie').
- 21st Century: Forensic Precision & Moral Condemnation - Retains its rigorous biochemical and pathology definitions in forensic science, while continuing as a devastating figurative indictment of moral and institutional corruption.
