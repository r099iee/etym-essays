- Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE): *Articulus* - "A little joint, knuckle, segment, part, or moment of time" (diminutive of *artus*, "joint").
- Middle English (c. 13th – 14th Century): Borrowed via Old French *article* - Applied to formal clauses of Christian faith (*Articles of the Creed*), legal statutes, and grammar particles.
- 16th – 17th Century: Apprenticeship contracts and journalistic essays - Applied to binding indenture contracts (*articles of apprenticeship*) and written sections of periodicals.
- 18th – 19th Century: Individual trade goods and wardrobe items ("article of dress") - Standard formal English for any physical item, manufactured good, or garment (as Holmes asks Watson to examine "this article" regarding the battered hat).
- 20th – 21st Century: Universal digital journalism, constitutional law, and trade commerce - Pervasive across digital news media (online articles), constitutional law (Article 5), and international trade tariff codes.
