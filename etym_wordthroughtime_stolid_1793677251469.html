- c. 1600 - "Foolish, slow-witted, dull" - Borrowed directly from Latin via Middle French, retaining the classical pejorative sense of intellectual deficiency and blockish ignorance.
- c. 1660-1750 - "Insensible, unmoved by passion" - Began shifting from cognitive defect to emotional indifference and unyielding resistance to excitement, disturbance, or provocation.
- c. 1800-1900 - "Steadfast, impassive, enduring" - Widely adopted in Victorian prose to depict reliable, unexcitable working-class stamina, stalwart sentinels, or unshakeable bovine calm in times of crisis.
- c. 1950-Present - "Emotionless, unflinching, or unimaginative" - Bipolar modern perception: valued for unflinching composure under acute stress, but critiqued for emotional flatness and absence of creative dynamism.
