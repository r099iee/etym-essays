- 15th century - a 'retainer' is a servant or follower retained in the service of a lord, the kept core of a great household
- 16th-17th century - a parallel legal sense appears: a fee paid to 'retain' counsel's services
- 18th-19th century - 'faithful retainers' become a stock feature of aristocratic fiction and historical writing
- Contemporary - primarily legal and financial (a lawyer's retainer fee); the loyal-servant sense survives mainly in historical or literary contexts
