Chain 1 — the post that became the ornament.
PIE (c. 4500–3500 BCE)
*steu-
"to push, thrust, knock, strike"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*studō
"post, prop, support"
↓
Old English (c. 700–1100)
studu, stuthu
"post, pillar, prop"
↓
Middle English (13th–15th c.)
studde
"projecting nail-head; ornamental knob"
↓
English (c. 16th c.)
stud (verb)
"to set or cover with studs"
↓
STUDDED

Chain 2 — the breeding horse (homophone, NOT this sense).
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*stōdō
"herd of breeding horses"
↓
Old English (c. 700–1100)
stōd
"stud, herd of horses kept for breeding"
↓
Modern English (c. 17th c. on)
stud
"breeding stallion; virile man"

Note on the debate: the PIE stage *steu- is a Pokorny reconstruction and is debated; the OED is cautious about connecting *studō to it. The two chains are independent words that happen to share a spelling.
