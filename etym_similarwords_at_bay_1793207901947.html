• At arm's length: Similar because both keep something from closing in; differs because at arm's length suggests cool deliberate detachment, while at bay suggests desperate resistance against an active threat.
  • Held off: Similar because both describe a threat resisted and delayed; differs because held off is plain and general, while at bay paints the specific hunting tableau of encirclement and barking.
  • Checked: Similar because both stop an advance in its tracks; differs because checked stresses the abrupt halt itself, while at bay stresses the prolonged standoff that follows.
  • Staved off: Similar because both fend off hunger, ruin, or danger for a time; differs because staved off stresses postponement by thin means, while at bay stresses confrontation and standing ground.
  • Warded off: Similar because both repel harm before it lands; differs because warded off suggests a charm or guard that turns evil aside, while at bay suggests a ring of force meeting force.
  • Repelled: Similar because both drive an attacker back; differs because repelled completes the repulse, while at bay leaves the enemy still present, circling, and dangerous.
  • Contained: Similar because both prevent a danger from spreading; differs because contained is managerial and confident (a contained fire), while at bay is dramatic and precarious.
  • Cornered: Similar because the bayed beast is literally cornered; differs because cornered stresses the victim's helplessness, while at bay stresses that the cornered one still fights and may yet wound.
  • At a distance: Similar because both maintain separation; differs because at a distance is neutral spacing, while at bay is hostile spacing, held under pressure.
