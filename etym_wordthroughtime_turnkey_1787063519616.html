- 17th c. [Early Modern Custodial Slang] - Emerged as literal compound for the under-jailer whose physical duty was turning the heavy key in lock mechanisms.
- 18th–19th c. [Standard Victorian Prison Term] - Associated with grim, corrupt penal institutions (Newgate, Fleet) and depicted vividly in Dickensian literature.
- Early 20th c. [Commercial & Industrial Metaphor Shift] - Shifted into engineering and business ("turnkey contract") signifying a project delivered in complete, immediately operational state.
- 21st c. [Real Estate & Software Standard] - Dominates modern commerce as an adjective for move-in ready houses and plug-and-play IT systems, while the noun survives in historical literature.
