Latin (classical)
perpetuus
"continuous, unbroken, everlasting"
↓
Late Latin (late classical)
perpetualis
"perpetual, continuous"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
perpetual
"lasting forever, endless"
↓
Modern English (15th c. onward)
perpetually
"continually, without end"
↓
Modern English (present)
PERPETUALLY
"continually, endlessly, invariably"

Note: The word is fully Latin-derived — perpetuus from per- ("through") + petere ("to seek, tend toward"). A PIE root can be cited for petere (*pet- "to rush, fly, seek"), but the standard chain for the English adverb is the Latin-to-English sequence given above.
