Old English (9th–11th c.)
smorian
"to suffocate, choke, stifle"
↓
Middle English (late 13th c.; frequentative in -er)
smolderen
"to suffocate, smother; burn slowly"
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
smoulder
"to burn and smoke without flame"
↓
Modern English (present)
smouldering
Note: The Germanic family behind smorian is old but its Indo-European connections are uncertain; some scholars link it to Greek smūchein "to smolder" from a PIE *smew- "to smoke, burn slowly", while others treat it as an isolated Germanic formation. The frequentative suffix -er also appears in the related smother. The sense "burning without flame" is first attested only in the 16th century.
