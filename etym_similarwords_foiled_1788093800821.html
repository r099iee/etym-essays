• Thwarted: Similar because both mean "actively prevented from succeeding"; differs because thwarted is the most exact formal synonym and often takes "in" + a goal ("thwarted in his ambition"), while foiled stresses the interception of an attempt.
  • Frustrated: Similar because both mean "prevented from achieving"; differs because frustrated also names the resulting emotion (annoyance, helplessness) and is far broader.
  • Defeated: Similar because both mean "overcome"; differs because defeated is general (battles, games, arguments) and does not imply that a plan was intercepted.
  • Balked: Similar because both mean "checked, stopped short"; differs because balked also has an intransitive sense ("to balk at" = to refuse or hesitate), from a horse refusing a jump.
  • Baffled: Similar because both mean "stopped from achieving"; differs because baffled emphasises confusion and perplexity rather than decisive interception.
  • Stymied: Similar because both mean "blocked, prevented"; differs because stymied is colloquial American English (from golf's stymie) and suggests being stuck rather than defeated.
  • Checkmated: Similar because both mean "decisively defeated"; differs because checkmated is a chess metaphor for final, inescapable defeat.
  • Outwitted: Similar because both mean "defeated"; differs because outwitted specifies defeat by superior cleverness, while foiled may be by luck or force.
  • Circumvented: Similar because both mean "got the better of"; differs because circumvented means bypassed or gone around, not directly defeated.
  • Forestalled: Similar because both mean "prevented"; differs because forestalled means prevented in advance, before the attempt even began.
  • Stumped: Similar because both mean "unable to proceed"; differs because stumped means puzzled or at a loss, without an active adversary.
  • Nonplussed: Similar because both mean "stopped short"; differs because nonplussed describes mental confusion, not external defeat.
  • Scuppered: Similar because both mean "ruined, defeated" (British); differs because scuppered is informal slang from the nautical scupper (drain hole), implying ruin rather than heroic interception.
  • Dashed: Similar because both mean "destroyed"; differs because dashed is used almost exclusively of hopes and expectations ("hopes were dashed").
  • Nipped in the bud: Similar because both mean "stopped early"; differs because it is an idiom of early prevention, whereas a foiled plan may be stopped at any stage.
  • Blocked / Hindered: Similar because both mean "prevented"; differs because they are weaker and partial — a plan can be hindered yet still succeed.
