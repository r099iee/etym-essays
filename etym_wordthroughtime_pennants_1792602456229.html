- [Medieval Era (c. 11th–15th c.)] - the ancestors pennon (a knight's pointed lance-streamer, from Latin penna) and pendant (a hanging cord/streamer, from Latin pendere) circulated in Anglo-French warfare and chivalric romance - [perception: revered as vibrant symbols of personal chivalric valor, feudal rank, and fortress defense]
- [Early Modern Era (c. 1610s–1650s)] - fused phonetically in the Royal Navy into the hybrid form pennant, becoming the official commissioning flag flown by warships to denote active command under a sovereign commission - [perception: respected as a stern, solemn emblem of naval authority, national sovereignty, and maritime supremacy]
- [18th–19th c. Naval Hegemony & Gothic Revival] - solidified in international maritime warfare (the long coach-whip pennant) and romantic historical fiction (Walter Scott depicting colorful pennants on gothic castle towers) - [perception: associated with the heroic age of sail, naval battles, and romantic medieval architecture]
- [Late 19th–20th c. Baseball & American Sporting Era] - adopted into Major League Baseball as the triangular felt flag awarded to league champions, immortalizing the phrases "pennant race" and "winning the pennant" - [perception: transformed in popular American culture into the ultimate symbol of season-long athletic triumph]
- [21st c. Contemporary Era] - maintained across international naval protocol, sports journalism, and technical financial chart analysis (triangular continuation patterns) - [perception: universally recognized as an emblem of victory, active mission, and directional momentum]
