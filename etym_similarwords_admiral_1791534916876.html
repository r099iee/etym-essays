• Commodore: Similar because both designate senior naval officers placed in command of a squadron or naval detachment; differs because commodore is historically an intermediate rank directly below rear admiral, often an operational or temporary appointment ranking below full flag rank.
  • Captain: Similar because both are naval officers holding positions of sovereign authority over maritime operations; differs because a captain commands a single individual ship or vessel, whereas an admiral exercises command over an entire fleet, strike group, or naval theater.
  • General: Similar because both represent the apex four-star flag/general officer ranks in armed forces; differs because a general commands terrestrial army, marine, or air forces, whereas an admiral's authority is exclusively maritime and naval.
  • Commander: Similar because both refer to naval leaders exercising command over ships and crews; differs because commander is a mid-level naval officer rank (equivalent to a lieutenant colonel in the army) positioned two grades below flag rank.
  • Flag officer: Similar because an admiral is the quintessential naval flag officer entitled to fly an official distinguishing rank flag from the masthead; differs because flag officer is an umbrella categorical term that encompasses commodores, rear admirals, vice admirals, and fleet admirals.
  • Rear admiral: Similar because rear admiral is a specific subordinate tier of the admiralty; differs because a rear admiral historically commanded the rearguard of a battle line and ranks below a full admiral and vice admiral.
  • Vice admiral: Similar because vice admiral is a three-star flag rank operating as a principal maritime deputy; differs because a vice admiral acts in place of (vice) the fleet commander, ranking immediately below the full admiral.
