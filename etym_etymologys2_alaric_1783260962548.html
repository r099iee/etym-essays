ALARIC — compound of two Germanic name-elements, each with its own chain. CHAIN 1 — first element 'all' (the mainstream reading): PIE uncertain (no secure cognates outside Germanic; proposals linking Germanic *allaz to PIE *h₂el- 'all' or to *al- 'to grow, nourish' are aired but undisputed by none) -> Proto-Germanic *allaz 'all, whole' (reconstructed) -> Gothic ala- 'all, wholly' (attested in compounds; cf. Alemanni 'all-men', attested from 3rd c.) -> Gothic compound *Alareiks 'ruler of all' (name, 5th c.) -> Latinized Alaricus (6th c., Jordanes' Getica; the chronicles) -> English Alaric (as in Gibbon, Macaulay, 18th-19th c.). ALTERNATIVE READING OF CHAIN 1 (debated): A minority of onomasticians read the first element as Gothic aljis 'other', giving 'ruler of others'; the earliest spellings with -ala- (not -alja-) and the parallel of the Alemanni weigh against this, but it must be reported as a live scholarly alternative. CHAIN 2 — second element 'ruler': PIE *h₃rēǵ- 'to rule, set straight, direct' (reconstructed; attested as Sanskrit rāj- 'king', Latin rēx/rēg- 'king', Gaulish rīx 'king') -> Celtic *rīg- 'king' (attested Gaulish -rix, 1st c. BCE) -> Proto-Germanic *rīks 'ruler, king' (Celtic loanword; cf. Gothic reiks, 4th c., Wulfila) -> Gothic *-reiks 'ruler' in the compound *Alareiks -> Latinized Alaricus -> English Alaric. NOTE ON THE CHAIN: The second element is the same Celtic-loaned *-rīks found in Theodoric and Ermanaric. The deep ancestry of the first element 'all' is genuinely uncertain and is stated as such; the compound Alareiks -> Alaricus -> Alaric is securely attested.
