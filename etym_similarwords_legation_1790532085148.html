• Embassy: Similar because both denote permanent diplomatic missions established by a sovereign state in a foreign capital; differs because an embassy is the highest-ranking mission, historically headed by an Ambassador who represented the personal dignity of the head of state, whereas a legation was a secondary diplomatic mission headed by an Envoy or Minister Plenipotentiary.
  • Consulate: Similar because both are official government offices located in foreign countries; differs because a consulate primarily manages commercial relations, visas, passports, and consular protection for individual citizens, lacking the broad political and diplomatic treaty-making authority of a legation.
  • Mission: Similar because both refer to an official diplomatic delegation in a foreign state; differs because mission (or permanent mission) is a broad umbrella term that includes embassies, legations, delegations to international organizations (like the UN), and temporary diplomatic dispatches.
  • Delegation: Similar because both denote a body of authorized representatives sent on an official assignment; differs because a delegation is typically temporary, convened for a specific conference, treaty negotiation, or summit, whereas a legation was a permanent diplomatic outpost and physical residence.
  • Deputation: Similar because both refer to persons commissioned to act on behalf of a sovereign or organization; differs because a deputation is usually an ad-hoc group sent to present a petition, address, or protest, lacking permanent diplomatic extraterritorial status.
  • Chancery: Similar because both relate to diplomatic administration; differs because a chancery refers specifically to the administrative offices and physical working premises of an embassy or legation (where paperwork and dispatches are drafted), rather than the diplomatic mission as a whole.
  • Ministry: Similar because both are high government administrative bodies; differs because a ministry (e.g., Ministry of Foreign Affairs) is a domestic governmental department headed by a cabinet minister, whereas a legation was an overseas outpost.
  • Nunciature: Similar because both involve diplomatic and ecclesiastical representation; differs because an Apostolic Nunciature is the specific diplomatic embassy of the Holy See headed by an Apostolic Nuncio (equivalent to an ambassador).
  • Envoyship: Similar because both pertain to the role of an envoy; differs because envoyship describes the abstract post, role, or tenure of an envoy, whereas legation encompasses the physical headquarters, staff, and institutional mission.
  • Diplomatic Post: Similar because a legation is a type of diplomatic post; differs because diplomatic post is a general modern descriptive category, whereas legation is the precise historical and legal term for a mission headed by a minister.
