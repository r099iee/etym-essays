Note on historical nuance: Morphologically, Śāradā is the feminine form of the adjectival vṛddhi derivative śārada ("belonging to or produced in autumn"), derived from the feminine consonant-stem noun śarad ("autumn"). The shift from a seasonal descriptive adjective to the theonym of the goddess of learning occurred through the liturgical association of the autumnal festival (*Śāradīyā Navarātri*) with Sarasvatī, symbolizing the pristine clarity of the post-monsoon autumn skies as an emblem of intellectual purity. Because Kashmir was universally hailed as Śāradā-pīṭha (the seat of the Goddess Śāradā), the distinct Brahmic script perfected in Kashmiri scriptoria for recording philosophical and sacred works naturally inherited the name of the valley's divine protector, yielding the compound designation Śāradā-lipi. Modern historical linguists (Mayrhofer, EWAia II 632) trace the ancestral noun śarad back to Proto-Indo-Iranian *ćárHt- ("autumn, year"), cognate with Avestan sarəd- and Old Persian θard- ("year"), ultimately rooted in the PIE base designating growth, heat, and seasonal maturation.
