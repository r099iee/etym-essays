- Roman coinage (3rd–4th c.) - Picti as outsider nickname for painted raiders; connotation fearful-contemptuous: savage bodies beyond the Wall.
- Early medieval insiders (6th–9th c.) - Picts as Christian kingdoms (Bede, Adomnán); perception shifts to fellow Christians and kings, still fierce but legitimate.
- After absorption (9th–14th c.) - Picts as vanished ancestors in Scottish king-lists; connotation legendary-mysterious: a lost people who became Scots.
- Antiquarian revival (16th–19th c.) - Picts as tattooed savages or noble ancestors; perception romantic-racialised, serving Scottish, British, and imperial myths.
- 20th c. evolutionist anthropology (Durant) - Picts as cannibal primitives proving universal savagery; connotation primitivist, now discredited.
- Contemporary archaeology - Picts as sophisticated sculptors, monks, and farmers (Portmahomack, Rhynie); perception respectful: a literate-adjacent early-medieval civilisation, not mere painted savages.
