• Stalks: Similar because both are upright plant supports bearing leaves; differs because stalks are usually green, crop-like, and single-season (corn, nettles), while stems covers green shoots and woody trunks alike.
  • Trunks: Similar because tree stems are trunks; differs because trunks are massive woody boles of mature trees, while stems includes saplings, flowers, and grasses too.
  • Shoots: Similar because both are young growing plant axes; differs because shoots stress newness and tenderness (bamboo shoots, fresh growth), while stems is neutral about age.
  • Canes: Similar because both are slender jointed plant supports; differs because canes are the tough hollow or pithy rods of raspberries, bamboo, and roses, while stems is the general botanical term.
  • Boughs: Similar because both are woody tree structure in Tolkien woods; differs because boughs are the large spreading branches overhead, while stems are the vertical trunks and stalks below.
  • Stock: Similar because both name the source from which growth branches; differs because stock stresses stored lineage and supply (nursery stock, family stock), while stems stresses the visible upright bearer.
  • Petioles: Similar because both support leaves; differs because petioles are the tiny leaf-stalks joining blade to stem, technical and small, while stems are the main axes themselves.
  • Haulms: Similar because both are the stems of crops and grasses; differs because haulms is the farmer word for dried stalks of beans and cereals, while stems serves garden, wood, and laboratory.
  • Axes: Similar because botanists call stems the plant axis; differs because axes is abstract geometry (plural of axis), while stems is living tissue you can grasp and cut.
