• Transitional: Similar because it marks a passage between states; differs because "transitional" is a neutral, general adjective (a transitional government, a transitional period), whereas "liminal" emphasises the eerie, ambiguous, threshold-experience itself — being on the boundary rather than merely moving through it.
  • Threshold: Similar because it is the Latin ancestor and names the very same boundary; differs because "threshold" is a noun for the boundary, the doorway, or the quantitative cut-off point, while "liminal" is the adjective describing the state of being on or near it.
  • In-between: Similar because it describes a position between two things; differs because "in-between" is everyday, colloquial, and value-neutral, while "liminal" carries academic weight and a sense of instability or transformation.
  • Intermediate: Similar because it means lying between two extremes; differs because "intermediate" implies an orderly middle stage on a scale (intermediate level, intermediate goods), whereas "liminal" implies a boundary-crossing, often unsettling, threshold-state.
  • Betwixt: Similar in meaning "between"; differs because "betwixt" is archaic and literary (betwixt and between), used for playful or dialectal effect, while "liminal" is modern, scientific, and scholarly.
  • Borderline: Similar because it is located on a boundary; differs because "borderline" usually implies questionable, marginal, barely acceptable status (a borderline case, borderline personality), while "liminal" stresses the transformative passage across the boundary rather than the question of acceptability.
  • Subliminal: Similar because it shares the same root and sits in the same psychophysical system; differs because "subliminal" means below the threshold — not consciously perceived, as in subliminal advertising — while "liminal" means at or on the threshold itself.
  • Supraliminal: Similar because it is the mirror term in psychophysics; differs because it means above the threshold, clearly perceptible, while "liminal" marks the threshold itself, and "supraliminal" is far rarer, confined to technical writing.
  • Marginal: Similar because it means at the edge or boundary; differs because "marginal" concerns edges in space, society, or economics (marginal land, marginal utility) and implies peripherality, while "liminal" concerns the crossing of a threshold and implies transformation.
  • Liminality: Similar because it is the noun of the very same idea; differs because "liminality" names the state or concept (especially in Turner's anthropology), while "liminal" is the adjective applied to things, places, times, and states.
  • Twilight: Similar because twilight is the classic liminal time — between day and night; differs because "twilight" names a specific time of day (or a figurative decline, as in twilight years), while "liminal" generalises the in-betweenness to any domain and any sense.
  • Interstitial: Similar because it means lying in the spaces between things; differs because "interstitial" is a technical, spatial word (interstitial tissue, interstitial space) with no sense of transformation, while "liminal" implies a threshold that is being crossed.
  • Anomalous: Similar because a liminal state is anomalous, fitting no settled category; differs because "anomalous" merely notes deviation from the norm, without the image of a threshold or the experience of passage.
