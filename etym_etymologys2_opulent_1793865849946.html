Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*h₃ep- / *op-
"to work, produce in abundance, hold power, wealth"
[Source of Latin ops, opus, copia, Sanskrit apnas]
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*opis
"ability, power, wealth, resources"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
ops (genitive opis)
"power, might; (in plural: opēs) riches, wealth, resources"
[Also Ops, Sabine/Roman goddess of harvest abundance]
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
opulentus
"wealthy, rich, splendid, sumptuous, abounding"
[Formed with adjectival suffix -lentus "full of"]
↓
Middle French (c. 14th c.)
opulent
"rich, wealthy, lavish, abundant"
[Learned borrowing in humanistic translations]
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500–1530)
opulent
"wealthy, possessing riches, lavish"
[Found in Sir Thomas Elyot and Shakespeare's King Lear]
