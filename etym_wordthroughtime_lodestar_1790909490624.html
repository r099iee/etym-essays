- 14th c. - Formed in Middle English (lode-sterre) as a maritime navigational term for the North Star (Polaris), guiding sailors on long voyages.
- 14th–16th c. - Swiftly adopted into poetic literature by Chaucer and Shakespeare as a metaphor for divine guidance, guiding principles, and captivating beauty that draws the human heart.
- 17th & 18th c. - Maintained in navigational texts while solidifying in moral philosophy and political rhetoric as a symbol of unchanging truth and constitutional virtue.
- 1970s - Adopted into American legal terminology (the 'lodestar method') as the foundational formula for calculating court-awarded attorney fees.
- 21st c. - Highly prized in elevated journalism, artistic biography, and critical theory as the quintessential emblem of an inspiring ideal or guiding mentor.
