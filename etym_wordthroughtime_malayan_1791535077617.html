- Late 18th to early 19th century: Demonym & Racial Taxonomy - Introduced into English scientific treatises and colonial reports; utilized by European physical anthropologists (notably Blumenbach in 1795) as a broad racial label for island Southeast Asians, and by the British East India Company to describe the regional trade sphere.
- 1840s to 1940s: British Imperial Administrative Identity - Solidified under the British Empire to describe the geography, economy (rubber and tin concessions), and multi-ethnic population of British Malaya; perceived as an exotic, commercially lucrative crown possession in Southeast Asia.
- 1948 to 1963: Post-War Political Federation & Cold War Conflict - Functioned as the official civic and national identity for the Federation of Malaya during the Malayan Emergency and the transition to Merdeka (independence in 1957); connoted anti-communist resilience, nascent democratic nationhood, and post-colonial constitutional development.
- 1963 to 1990s: Historical Retrospective Shift - Following the expansion of the federation into Malaysia in 1963, 'Malayan' was largely supplanted in contemporary political, civic, and national discourse by 'Malaysian'; increasingly treated as a historical retronym.
- 2000s to Present: Biogeographical, Historical, and Linguistic Term - Employed today primarily by historians studying the pre-1963 colonial and independence era, by linguists analyzing the Malayic branch of Austronesian languages, and by zoologists/botanists designating endemic Sundaland wildlife (e.g., Malayan tiger, Malayan tapir).
