- Late 16th Century (c. 1590s) - Introduction into English: Formed during the Renaissance through Latinate and French models (*irréprochable*) to signify blameless moral conduct and religious purity.
- 17th Century - Theological and civic ethics: Widely used in theological treatises, sermons, and legal philosophy to describe upright, godly magistrates whose integrity defied slander.
- 18th to 19th Century - Social etiquette and craftsmanship: Expanded from strict moral innocence to encompass aristocratic manners, social pedigree, sartorial taste, and exquisite craftsmanship (e.g. 'irreproachable manners', 'irreproachable lace').
- Contemporary Usage - Retains an elevated, elegant, and formal register; commonly employed in legal, diplomatic, academic, and literary prose to describe unblemished integrity, flawless character, or impeccable standards.
