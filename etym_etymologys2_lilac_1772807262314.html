SINGLE ROOT WORD (lilac) — a long-distance loan chain: Sanskrit → Persian → Arabic → Romance → English. Sanskrit nīla 'dark blue, indigo, blue-black' (attested in the Rigveda, c. 1500–1000 BCE; further Indo-European ancestry of nīla is uncertain—no secure PIE twin should be forced) → Persian nīl 'indigo, blue' (loan from Indic) and Persian līlak / nīlak 'bluish; the lilac colour and flower' (a diminutive / colour derivative of nīl) → Arabic līlak / līlāk 'lilac' (loan from Persian; Arabic is a transmission stage, not a Semitic source) → via Ottoman and Iberian contact, French lilas 'lilac shrub' (16th c.) and Spanish lila / lilac → English lilac 'the ornamental shrub; its pale-violet colour' (1620s) → LILAC. Debate / nuance note: Arabic is a carrier language, not a Semitic root: the tree must not invent a Semitic triliteral for 'lilac'. The exact immediate source of the English word—French lilas or Spanish lilac—is debated, but the family is one. The ultimate lexical core is Indo-Aryan (Sanskrit nīla), not a native Germanic colour word.
