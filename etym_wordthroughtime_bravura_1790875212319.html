- 16th to 17th Century (Italy) - Emerged in Renaissance Italian as a noun denoting spirited courage, bravery, and wild swagger.
- Mid-18th Century (Musicology) - Adopted into European musical terminology across Italy, France, and Britain for the brilliant, highly ornamental aria di bravura.
- 19th Century - Expanded from music to painting (virtuoso brushwork) and literature (spectacular rhetorical passages).
- 20th Century - Flourished in theater and film criticism as the quintessential accolade for breathtaking, charismatic acting performances.
- 21st Century - Universal aesthetic term denoting fearless virtuosity, technical mastery, and brilliant showmanship in any creative field.
