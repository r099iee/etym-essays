• Pasterns: Similar because pasterns are adjacent lower leg structures in horses located immediately below the fetlock; differs because the pastern is the sloping segment connecting the fetlock joint to the hoof capsule (housing the long and short pastern bones), whereas the fetlock is the major hinge joint and projection located directly above the pastern.
  • Hocks: Similar because both are critical lower limb joints in horses; differs because the hock is the large, tarsal joint situated higher up on the hind leg (homologous to the human ankle and heel), whereas the fetlock is the lower joint present on all four limbs homologous to the human knuckle or ball of the foot.
  • Stifles: Similar because stifles are equine leg joints; differs because the stifle is the high upper joint on the hind leg homologous to the human knee (housing the patella), located close to the horse's flank, far above the lower fetlocks.
  • Cannons: Similar because cannon bones articulate directly with the fetlock; differs because the cannon is the long, straight vertical bone (metacarpal/metatarsal) extending between the knee/hock and the fetlock joint.
  • Ankles: Similar because the fetlock is frequently referred to colloquially and non-technically as a horse's "ankle"; differs because anatomically the true human ankle corresponds to the horse's hock, whereas the fetlock corresponds biomechanically to the human metacarpophalangeal knuckle joint.
  • Coronet (Coronary Band): Similar because both are equine lower limb landmarks; differs because the coronet is the delicate growth ring situated at the very top edge of the hoof where hair meets horn, located below the pastern and fetlock.
