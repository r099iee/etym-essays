• Paradise: Similar because both name an ideal place of perfect happiness and bliss; differs because paradise (ultimately from Persian paridaiza, "walled garden") is the broad general concept of heaven or supreme perfection, while Eden specifically denotes the biblical garden and its name.
  • Heaven: Similar because both can name the blessed afterlife; differs because heaven is the celestial abode of God and the blessed, a fully theological and otherworldly concept, whereas Eden begins as an earthly garden and keeps an earthly, natural colour even in its figurative uses.
  • Utopia: Similar because both denote ideal perfection; differs because utopia is a man-made ideal society (coined by Thomas More), the product of human design and politics, while Eden is a divine, uncorrupted natural creation predating human society.
  • Arcadia: Similar because both evoke a pristine, blissful place; differs because Arcadia refers to the classical Greek pastoral ideal of rural wilderness harmony, whereas Eden is the biblical divine garden, not a landscape of shepherds.
  • Shangri-La: Similar because both name a remote, idyllic paradise; differs because Shangri-La is a fictional Tibetan utopia from James Hilton's novel, exotic and hidden, while Eden is a biblical, universal symbol of lost innocence.
  • Elysium: Similar because both are ancient concepts of a blissful place; differs because Elysium is the classical Greek abode of the blessed dead, whereas Eden is the garden of the living first humans.
  • Idyll: Similar because both suggest serene perfection; differs because an idyll is a scene or literary work of rural peace, not a named sacred place, lacking the religious and ethical weight of Eden.
