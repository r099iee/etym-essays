• Adhering: Similar because both involve sticking closely to a surface; differs because adhering is a formal, objective, or scientific term denoting molecular or chemical sticking, whereas clinging carries intense physical drama, desperation, or emotional warmth.
  • Grasping: Similar because both involve holding something with the hands; differs because grasping emphasizes the initial dynamic act of seizing or grabbing, while clinging emphasizes the sustained, continuous, and immovable state of holding on.
  • Cleaving: Similar because both describe remaining steadfastly attached to an object or companion; differs because cleaving is an elevated, archaic, and biblical term often signifying profound moral devotion, loyalty, or marital union.
  • Clutching: Similar because both convey desperate, tight contact; differs because clutching emphasizes a tight, tense, and fearful grip of the hands or claws around a discrete object (e.g., clutching a purse), while clinging implies whole-body adhesion or surface attachment.
  • Gripping: Similar because both involve exerting strong physical hold; differs because gripping focuses on mechanical friction and force applied by fingers or tools, whereas clinging implies reliance on the anchor for support or survival.
  • Sticking: Similar because both describe surface attachment; differs because sticking is a generic, everyday term lacking the emotional desperation, textile drape, or survival stakes of clinging.
  • Embracing: Similar because both involve holding someone close; differs because embracing is a deliberate, mutual gesture of affection, greeting, or philosophical acceptance, whereas clinging often implies one-sided neediness or panic.
  • Fastening: Similar because both result in connection; differs because fastening is a deliberate mechanical action using ties, buckles, pins, or adhesives, whereas clinging is an organic or behavioral holding fast.
