Old English (before 12th c.)
bisig (also bysig)
‘occupied, diligent, busy’
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
bisi (also besy)
‘busy; prying, meddling’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c., compounded)
busie-body
‘meddlesome person’
↓
Modern English (18th c. fused)
busybody
‘one who meddles’
↓
Old English (before 12th c.)
bodig
‘body, trunk, person’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
body
‘body, person, group’
↓
Modern English (in compound)
busybody
‘meddlesome person’

Note: neither half has a secure deeper ancestry: no certain Proto-Germanic form for busy beyond reconstructed *bisīgaz of unknown further origin, and no agreed PIE root for body at all, so both chains honestly begin at Old English rather than inventing unattested roots.
