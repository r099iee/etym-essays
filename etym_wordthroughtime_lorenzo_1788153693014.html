- [Ancient Latium: Laurentum, coastal city of the Latins; the name means "of Laurentum"] - [Perception: an old, local, land-bound identity, fixed to a place and its (legendary) laurels]
- [Roman Republic: Laurentius becomes a family and personal name] - [Perception: respectable middle-rank citizen name, saint-elect, no special glamour]
- [Christianity (3rd–4th c.): St Lawrence, martyr deacon of Rome] - [Perception: the name acquires sanctity, charity and the gridiron legend; San Lorenzo churches multiply across Europe]
- [Medieval Florence: the Medici adopt San Lorenzo as their parish saint] - [Perception: wealthy, pious, dignified — banking under a saint’s shadow]
- [Renaissance Florence (15th c.): Lorenzo il Magnifico] - [Perception: the name inflates into a symbol of princely taste, artistic patronage and civilised peace; an era is called after it]
- [Romantic/Victorian art history: Pater, Burckhardt, Symonds write "the age of Lorenzo"] - [Perception: the name becomes shorthand for the Italian Renaissance itself — beautiful, subtle, complete]
- [Today: given name worldwide] - [Perception: stylishly Italian; for the learned reader it still winks at the Medici and the Magnificent]
