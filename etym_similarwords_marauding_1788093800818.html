• raiding: Similar because both mean attacking to seize goods; differs because raiding implies a targeted strike on a specific objective (to raid a village), while marauding implies aimless roving for whatever can be taken.
  • plundering: Similar because both mean taking goods by force; differs because plundering names the theft itself (seizing valuables), while marauding names the roaming-and-seizing behaviour.
  • pillaging: Similar because both mean violent seizure of property; differs because pillaging is military (sacking a town, from French piller) and focuses on the act of stripping, while marauding focuses on the roving movement.
  • looting: Similar because both mean stealing during disorder; differs because looting is opportunistic theft in chaos (riots, disasters), while marauding is lawless but mobile plunder.
  • ravaging: Similar because both mean destructive sweeping; differs because ravaging emphasises the damage inflicted, while marauding emphasises the seizure of goods.
  • foraying: Similar because both mean expeditions for plunder; differs because a foray is a single defined incursion, while marauding is ongoing wandering.
  • prowling: Similar because both mean moving stealthily in search of prey; differs because prowling is stalking (animals, thieves) without necessarily stealing, while marauding includes open plunder.
  • roving: Similar because both mean wandering; differs because roving is aimless travel with no predatory purpose, while marauding is predatory by definition.
  • preying: Similar because both involve seizing from others; differs because preying is animalistic hunting, while marauding is usually human or military behaviour (though animals can maraud).
  • harassing: Similar because both mean aggressive disturbance; differs because harassing is persistent annoyance without plunder, while marauding takes goods by force.
  • depredating: Similar because both mean plundering; differs because depredating is formal and technical (depredation = act of plundering), while marauding is more vivid and mobile.
  • sacking: Similar because both mean the violent seizure of a place's wealth; differs because sacking is the complete stripping of a captured town, while marauding is roaming plunder, often of the countryside.
