friskaz
"fresh, lively,"
"new, vigorous"

"lively, young"

(Germanic loan)

"plaster"

affresco, fresco

↓
French intermediary (16th-17th c.)
fresque

↓
Modern English (1598-present)
fresco
"wet-plaster painting,"
"fresh-wall art"

Note: Proto-Germanic friskaz is secure (Old English fersc, Old Norse ferskr), but its deeper Indo-European source is debated and unverified; some propose a connection to per- (around) or preis- without consensus, so no PIE stage is asserted here. The Romance borrowing of a Germanic freshness word is certain; Italian a fresco as painters' shorthand is first fully described by Cennini (c. 1400) and Vasari (1550).
