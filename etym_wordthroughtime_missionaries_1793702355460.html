- Early 17th Century (Religious Context) - Coined to describe members of religious orders (especially Jesuits) sent to propagate Christianity abroad. Connotation: Dedicated, zealous, often seen as agents of salvation and civilization.
- 18th-19th Century (Colonial Expansion) - Protestant missions expand globally, often alongside colonial powers. Connotation: Complex – still dedicated, but increasingly associated with cultural imposition, Westernization, and sometimes complicity in colonial exploitation. Seen by some as heroes, by others as disruptors.
- 20th Century (Post-Colonial Era) - Shift towards humanitarian aid, development work, and cultural sensitivity. Connotation: More nuanced – still dedicated, but with greater awareness of historical baggage. Efforts to partner with local communities and empower indigenous leadership. Can be viewed as altruistic helpers or as perpetuators of cultural hegemony.
- 21st Century (Globalized World) - Continued focus on service, interfaith dialogue, and sustainable development. Connotation: Highly varied depending on perspective – from selfless servants of God to agents of cultural imperialism. Debates around proselytization vs. pure humanitarianism persist.
