• Tarnish: Similar because both degrade brightness or reputation; differs because tarnish suggests gradual chemical dulling or slow loss of luster, sometimes without an agent, while besmirch implies active smearing of filth or calumny onto something previously clean.
  • Slander: Similar because both damage repute; differs because slander is specifically spoken false defamation, a legal category, while besmirch covers spoken, written, and behavioral staining and need not meet the tests of defamation law.
  • Libel: Similar because both attack a good name; differs because libel is written or published defamation — technical and legal — whereas besmirch is general literary language for any staining of standing.
  • Defile: Similar because both dirty what should stay pure; differs because defile leans toward sacred, bodily, or ritual pollution and desecration, while besmirch is the social-reputation term — honor, name, public record — not temple or body.
  • Smear: Similar because both paint dirt on a target in politics; differs because smear is punchier, more journalistic and colloquial, and can still mean a literal greasy coating, while besmirch is more literary and almost always figurative of honor.
  • Stigmatize: Similar because both attach lasting disgrace; differs because stigmatize brands a group or person with a social mark of deviance and belongs to sociological analysis, while besmirch focuses on the staining act against a previously good name.
  • Bedaub / besmear: Similar because both are be- intensives meaning "cover all over with a substance"; differs because they stay literal and physical (paint, mud, grease), while besmirch alone has become the standard word for reputational attack.
