Proto-Indo-European (Onomatopoeic Root) (c. 3rd mill. BCE)
*kēi- / *kēu-
"to screech, call out sharply (of a bird)"
[imitative root of avian shrieks and diving calls]
↓
Ancient Greek (Common Noun) (c. 8th c. BCE)
κῆϋξ (kēÿx)
"a plunge-diving seabird (tern, shearwater, or gannet)"
[attested in Homer, Aristophanes, and Aristotle for sea divers]
↓
Ancient Greek (Mythological Personal Name) (c. 8th–7th c. BCE)
Κῆϋξ (Kēÿx)
"Ceyx (heroic king of Trachis transformed into a diving bird)"
[personification in Hesiodic epic and mythological genealogies]
↓
Classical Latin (1st c. BCE)
Cēyx
"Ceyx, king of Trachis and husband of Alcyone"
[immortalized in Ovid's Metamorphoses Book XI]
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
Ceyx / Seys
"legendary king in dream vision poetry"
[adapted by Geoffrey Chaucer in The Book of the Duchess]
↓
Modern English & Scientific Zoology (1799–present)
Ceyx
"mythical king of Thessaly; taxonomic genus of dwarf kingfishers"
[codified by Lacépède for miniature Asian and Australasian river kingfishers]
