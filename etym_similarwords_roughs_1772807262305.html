• Thugs: Similar because both denote brutal criminals or hired bashers; differs because thug is the nearest current everyday equivalent while roughs is dated and period-flavored.
  • Ruffians: Similar because both stress violent, boisterous lawlessness; differs because ruffian is more literary and slightly archaic, without the specific hired-muscle coloring of roughs.
  • Goons: Similar because both name hired muscle; differs because goons has a comic or gangster-film tint and emphasizes dumb brute force over unrefined manners.
  • Hooligans: Similar because both describe disorderly violent men; differs because hooligans imply public vandalism and gang rowdiness rather than paid private violence.
  • Toughs: Similar because both are plural nouns for street fighters or hoodlums; differs because toughs is American slang and suggests physical resilience, while roughs stresses lack of refinement.
  • Hoodlums: Similar because both name lawless young men of the street; differs because hoodlum is twentieth-century American slang with a juvenile-gang flavor, not the British period sense of roughs.
  • Rowdies: Similar because both name disorderly, brawling men; differs because a rowdy is chiefly noisy and quarrelsome, while a rough is more seriously violent and criminal.
  • Heavies: Similar because both name hired enforcers; differs because heavies is theatrical and gangland slang emphasizing size and intimidation, while roughs emphasizes coarseness.
  • Henchmen: Similar because both are men who do a powerful person's dirty work; differs because a henchman implies personal loyalty and service, while roughs are hired, disposable enforcers.
  • Muscle: Similar because both name hired physical force; differs because muscle is an uncountable abstract term for the capacity, not a plural noun for the men themselves.
  • Bruisers: Similar because both name violent, tough men; differs because a bruiser stresses size and fighting ability rather than moral or social coarseness.
  • Roughnecks: Similar because both build on the same 'rough' idea of coarse, violent men; differs because roughneck usually denotes a manual laborer (especially oilfield) or a rowdy, not specifically a criminal.
  • Rowdies / Brawlers: Similar because both collect men who fight in the street; differs because they lack the hired-muscle and lawless-criminal shading of roughs.
  • Rogues: Similar because both can name unprincipled men; differs because a rogue is a rascal or trickster, often charming, whereas roughs are brutal and unpolished.
  • Yobs: Similar because both are British terms for loutish, violent young men; differs because yob is modern British slang (back-slang of boy) for disorderly youths, not hired enforcers.
