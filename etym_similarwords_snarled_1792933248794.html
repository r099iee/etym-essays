• Growled: Similar because both name a low hostile throat-sound of warning; differs because growled is the broader term (stomachs and engines growl too), while snarled adds bared teeth and visible viciousness.
  • Snapped: Similar because both are sudden canine threats, often paired together; differs because snapped is the quick bite itself, while snarled is the sustained warning that precedes it.
  • Barked: Similar because both are loud dog vocalisations aimed at intruders; differs because barked is sharp, rhythmic, and often merely alerting, while snarled is continuous, guttural, and openly menacing.
  • Bared its teeth: Similar because it describes the same warning display as a snarl; differs because it names only the visual half (the lifted lip), while snarled fuses face and voice into one threat.
  • Sneered: Similar because both curl the lip in hostile contempt; differs because sneered is cold, human, and often silent superiority, while snarled is hot animal aggression.
  • Glowered: Similar because both are silent hostile displays aimed at an enemy; differs because glowered works through the eyes (a dark stare), while snarled works through mouth and voice.
  • Bellowed: Similar because both are loud aggressive vocal threats; differs because bellowed is deep, open-throated roaring (bulls, sergeants), while snarled is constricted, teeth-baring menace.
  • Tangled: Similar because it matches the twin sense of snarl (knotted, snarled traffic); differs because tangled is neutral disorder, while snarled suggests a knot with temper — hostile, tight, and resistant.
  • Entangled: Similar because both describe lines, affairs, or traffic caught in knots; differs because entangled is formal and often legal or romantic, while snarled is blunt and bad-tempered.
