- shrivelled (past participle / adjective) — mid-16th century: first appears as the participle of the new verb "shrivel", meaning contracted and wrinkled by heat or dryness — chiefly of leaves, fruit, and skin.
- 17th century: enters botanical and medical language — plants shrivel in drought, wounds and flesh shrivel with age or disease; the word is clinical and physical.
- 18th–19th century: figurative extension — hopes, ambitions, reputations, and affections are "shrivelled up"; the participle gains an emotional register of diminished, dried-up vitality.
- 19th-century Gothic and Victorian prose: "shrivelled" becomes a stock adjective of horror and decay — shrivelled crones, shrivelled mummies, withered flesh — strongly negative, grotesque, and morbid.
- 20th–21st century: still double-edged — literal desiccation (raisins, autumn leaves, dehydrated skin) and figurative shrinkage (a shrivelled economy, shrivelled dreams); contemporary usage also has a lighter comic tone of shrinking up.
