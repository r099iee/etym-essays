• Straddling: Similar because both describe having legs or parts on opposite sides of a central dividing line; differs because straddling is a dynamic participle that often carries strong figurative connotations of political fence-sitting, non-commitment, or hedging, whereas astride functions primarily as a static spatial preposition/adverb of posture and dominance.
  • Bestriding: Similar because both involve spanning across an object or domain; differs because bestriding is an active transitive verb that conveys commanding dominance, colossal stature, and mastery over what is beneath it.
  • Spanning: Similar because both describe extending from one side to the other across an open expanse; differs because spanning emphasizes the structural bridge or distance covered between endpoints, lacking the physical leg-parting posture intrinsic to astride.
  • Across: Similar because both convey position on or movement from one side to another; differs because across is a broad, generic preposition that lacks the specific bifurcated, straddling orientation inherent in astride.
  • Mounted: Similar because both describe being seated atop an animal or vehicle; differs because mounted emphasizes the status of being placed upon a mount (often in formal, equestrian, or military terms) rather than the physical leg geometry.
  • Flanking: Similar because both describe being on either side of something; differs because flanking implies two separate entities situated beside a central object, whereas astride denotes a single entity bifurcating over and sitting atop the central object.
  • Overarching: Similar because both describe positioning above an expanse; differs because overarching implies an elevated curve or overarching conceptual roof above something, without physical contact on both sides.
