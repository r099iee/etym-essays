• Jailer / Gaoler: Similar because both refer to an officer responsible for the custody of prisoners within a penal institution; differs because jailer represents the overall administrative master or keeper of the prison, whereas turnkey specifically emphasizes the physical, mechanical task of carrying keys and locking cell doors.
  • Warder: Similar because both supervise inmates and preserve order within a prison block; differs because warder is a formal British administrative title carrying institutional rank and disciplinary authority, whereas turnkey is an older, more vivid historical and colloquial term.
  • Correctional Officer: Similar because both maintain physical containment and security over convicts; differs because correctional officer is contemporary institutional terminology emphasizing behavioral rehabilitation, legal protocol, and institutional management rather than mere manual locking of gates.
  • Prison Guard: Similar because both provide custodial security over detainees; differs because prison guard is a generic modern functional description devoid of the evocative historical connotations of eighteenth-century heavy iron keyrings.
  • Castellan: Similar because both control locks and gates within fortified stone architectures; differs because a castellan is the high-ranking governor or noble military commander of an entire castle or fortress, rather than a subordinate lock-keeper.
  • Bailiff: Similar because both are historical officers of the legal apparatus; differs because a bailiff serves legal writs, maintains courtroom order, or executes debt seizures, rather than maintaining physical watch over cell blocks.
  • Constable: Similar because both are peace officers historically enforcing legal restraint; differs because a constable exercises broad community policing, arrest powers, and municipal peacekeeping rather than custodial prison confinement.
