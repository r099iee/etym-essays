Sanskrit (c. 4th–8th c.)
malaya (मलय)
"mountainous range; hill; southern India"
↓
Old Malay / Classical Malay (c. 7th–15th c.)
Melayu (ملايو)
"Malay people; Kingdom of Melayu in Sumatra"
↓
Portuguese (early 16th c.)
malaio
"native of Malacca or the Malay Peninsula"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1590s)
Malay
"indigenous people and language of Malaya"
↓
Modern English (c. 1820s–1830s)
Malayan
"pertaining to Malaya, its people, or language"

Note: Formed in English by attaching the Latinate adjectival/demonymic suffix -an to Malay. The ultimate origin of Melayu is traditionally traced to Sanskrit malaya ("mountain, mountain range", likely related to Dravidian malai "hill"), originally designating a kingdom along the Batang Hari river in eastern Sumatra (attested in 7th-century Chinese records as Mòluóyú) before spreading to the Malay Peninsula.
