• Swayed: Similar because both mean to move from side to side; differs because swaying is a slower, larger, more graceful arc of a tall or broad body, while wagging is short, rapid, and rhythmic.
  • Nodded: Similar because both are repeated motions of a head or loose part; differs because nodding is a deliberate, abrupt downward head gesture (assent or greeting), not a loose oscillating play.
  • Fluttered: Similar because both describe light, repeated movement; differs because fluttering is lighter, irregular, and often air- or wing-driven, whereas wagging is a firmer back-and-forth motion of a solid appendage.
  • Flickered: Similar because both can describe small repeated movement; differs because flickering is chiefly irregular variation of light or flame, not the motion of a solid mass like a beard or tail.
  • Wobbled: Similar because both mean unsteady to-and-fro motion; differs because wobbling implies instability, teetering, and an irregular axis, while wagging is a steady, rhythmic oscillation.
  • Shook / Shaken: Similar because both mean moved to and fro; differs because shaking is more violent, vigorous, or general (bodies, hands, buildings), while wagging is short, quick, and playful.
  • Wiggled: Similar because both mean small repeated movement; differs because wiggling suggests squirming, sinuous movement of a flexible thing, while wagging is a distinct side-to-side or up-and-down beat.
  • Quivered: Similar because both mean rapid small movement; differs because quivering is a trembling vibration, often from cold, fear, or tension, not a deliberate rhythmic wag.
  • Oscillated: Similar because both mean to move to and fro; differs because oscillating is the neutral, technical term for any regular swinging, lacking wag's lively, appendage-like, comic quality.
  • Undulated: Similar because both describe wave-like motion; differs because undulating is a smooth, flowing, wavelike rise and fall, while wagging is a short, jerky, repeated swing.
