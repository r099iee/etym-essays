• Constantinople / Byzantium: Similar because both were imperial capitals and the twin poles of the late Roman world, and Ravenna was, in effect, Constantinople's western arm; differs because Constantinople was the eastern, New Roman capital that endured for a thousand years, while Ravenna was the shorter-lived western seat.
  • Rome: Similar because both were seats of imperial and later ecclesiastical power in Italy, and Ravenna briefly superseded Rome as the western capital; differs because Rome kept a continuous papal and urban life across the Middle Ages, while Ravenna's greatness was concentrated in the 5th–6th centuries.
  • Milan: Similar because Milan was the western imperial capital immediately before Ravenna (until 402), so the two names mark consecutive phases; differs because Milan was then eclipsed by Ravenna, which far surpassed it in Byzantine-era importance.
  • Rimini: Similar because it is a nearby Adriatic city of the same Romanized coast of Italy; differs because Rimini never became an imperial or exarchal capital and lacks Ravenna's mosaic inheritance.
  • Venice: Similar because both are marsh- and lagoon-based cities with deep Byzantine ties and glorious artistic cultures; differs because Venice rose to maritime dominance in the medieval and early modern periods while Ravenna declined into a provincial town.
  • Exarchate: Similar because Ravenna was the seat of the exarchate, so the two words are historically inseparable; differs because exarchate names the Byzantine administrative unit (from Greek exarchos "governor"), not the city.
  • Mosaic: Similar because Ravenna is celebrated precisely for its mosaics, and the city is a metonym for them; differs because mosaic is the name of the art-medium (tesserae), not of any place.
  • Classe: Similar because Classe was Ravenna's port and the site of the great church Sant'Apollinare in Classe, now part of the modern urban area; differs because it is a distinct historical settlement, originally separate from the city proper.
  • Late Antiquity: Similar because Ravenna epitomises that historical period in the West; differs because the phrase names the era (roughly the 3rd–8th centuries), not a place.
