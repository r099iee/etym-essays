- [Late 18th c. Enlightenment & Regency Era] — Coined in Britain as the moral and stylistic antithesis of the impudent, pushy behavior condemned as "obtrusive" in social conduct literature and philosophical treatises — [perception: a distinguished social virtue denoting gentle breeding, polite manners, and modest avoidance of vulgar self-assertion]
- [19th c. Victorian Era] — Widely adopted into domestic servant manuals, etiquette books, and architectural landscape treatises (such as those by J. C. Loudon), praising country cottages and servant duties that blended harmoniously into the estate without disrupting the picturesque view — [perception: essential mark of professional domestic servitude, decorous modesty, and architectural harmony]
- [Early to Mid-20th c. Modernist Design & Psychology] — Extended to functionalist industrial design, interior decor, and behavioral psychology to praise clean lines, concealed engineering, and well-adjusted introverted personalities — [perception: celebrated design principle where form follows function without decorative excess or psychological imposition]
- [Late 20th–21st c. Digital Information & Tech Age] — Institutionalized in computing, software engineering, and cognitive ergonomics (e.g., "unobtrusive JavaScript", ambient computing, background telemetry) — [perception: hallmark of sophisticated high-tech engineering that respects user attention, cognitive bandwidth, and aesthetic minimalism]
