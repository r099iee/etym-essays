- [1540s: borrowed from French; a formal noun for decay and corruption, applied to churches, states, and manners] - [Perception: homiletic and historical gravity, always an accusation]
- [1734: Montesquieu's 'grandeur et décadence des Romains'; Gibbon's Decline and Fall after] - [Perception: becomes the technical term for the terminal phase of civilisations, linked to luxury and effeminacy]
- [1880s–90s: the Decadent movement — Verlaine, Huysmans, Symons, Wilde; 'decadence' claimed as a style] - [Perception: scandalous then glamorous; the insult becomes a badge of the ultra-modern and beautiful]
- [Early 20th c.: Nietzsche's cultural diagnosis, Spengler's 'Decline of the West', 'decadent West' polemics] - [Perception: ominous, civilisational, fought over by partisans]
- [Today: noun keeps its gravity (the decadence of an age); adjective 'decadent' lightened to printable luxury — 'a decadent dessert'] - [Perception: split personality — doom on one page, indulgence on the menu]
