Broadsides is the plural noun and third-person singular present verb form of broadside. Stemming from the Age of Sail naval technology, the term encompasses physical ship anatomy, simultaneous naval artillery fire, large single-sheet printed publications, and an influential figurative usage denoting fierce, concentrated verbal, critical, or political attacks.

Primary Naval and Military Definitions:

  • Ship Anatomy (The Broadside): The entire exposed side or flank of a wooden warship or vessel above the waterline, stretching from the bow to the quarter.
  • Simultaneous Artillery Discharge: The coordinated, simultaneous firing of all artillery pieces, cannons, or carronades mounted along one complete side of a warship:
    • Weight of Broadside: In naval gunnery, the collective weight (measured in hundreds or thousands of pounds of iron shot) that a warship could discharge in a single salvo against an enemy target.
    • Raking Broadside: A devastating tactical maneuver in which a ship fired its entire broadside down the longitudinal axis of an enemy vessel through the vulnerable, unarmored stern or bow, sweeping the full length of the decks, destroying guns, and decimating the crew.
    • Historical Tactical Context: In John Ruskin's Modern Painters, he draws directly on this naval imagery to depict his unsupported frontline position: "receiving, unsupported, the broadsides of half the enemy's fleet."

Secondary Metaphorical Sense: Verbal, Polemical, and Critical Assaults:

  • Blistering Rhetorical Barrages: A severe, aggressive, and comprehensive public critique, editorial denunciation, polemical essay, or verbal condemnation aimed at an opponent, artistic school, or institution.
    • Semantic Bridge: Just as a warship brought all its heavy guns to bear at once to overwhelm an opposing vessel with maximum destructive power, 18th- and 19th-century essayists and journalists adopted broadside metaphorically to describe delivering an all-out, multi-faceted rhetorical offensive against critical adversaries.

Tertiary Print Culture and Publishing Definition:

  • Single-Sheet Printed Publications (Broadside / Broadsheet): Large sheets of paper printed on one side only, intended to be posted on walls in public squares, sold cheaply by street peddlers (chapmen), or distributed widely.
    • Broadside Ballads and Proclamations: From the 16th to 19th centuries, broadsides served as the primary mass medium for royal proclamations, official notices, political commentary, sensational crime accounts, and popular folk songs (broadside ballads).
    • Evolution into Broadsheet: Laid the historical foundation for large-format newspapers (broadsheets), distinguished from smaller tabloid formats.

Quaternary Automotive and Collision Usages:

  • Side-Impact Collisions (Broadsided): In modern vehicular terminology, to strike or be struck perpendicularly on the side by another vehicle or moving object (often referred to as a T-bone or broadside collision).
