• Provost: Similar because both refer to senior executive officials in academic or ecclesiastical hierarchies; differs because a provost in modern North American universities is primarily the chief academic officer serving beneath a president or chancellor, whereas a rector has historically served as the supreme presiding head of the institution.
  • Chancellor: Similar because both denote high-ranking heads of academic universities; differs because a chancellor is often a ceremonial or supreme titular head representing the university externally, while a rector historically exercises direct, on-the-ground administrative and pedagogical governance.
  • President: Similar because both designate the chief institutional executive of an educational college or university; differs because president is the standard modern American executive title, which supplanted the older title of rector at institutions like Harvard and Yale during the eighteenth century.
  • Vicar: Similar because both are ordained parish priests in the Church of England and Anglican Communion; differs fundamentally in historical canon law because a rector was the incumbent of an unappropriated parish entitled to receive both great and small tithes, whereas a vicar served as a deputy or substitute in an appropriated parish, receiving only small tithes while great tithes went to an absentee patron or monastery.
  • Parson: Similar because a parson is the legal representative and incumbent of a parochial church; differs because parson is a traditional, semi-informal legal term (*persona ecclesiae*) encompassing any parish incumbent, whereas rector is a precise canonical title denoting full tithe rights and institutional authority.
  • Pastor: Similar because both terms describe a clergyman with spiritual charge over a congregation; differs because pastor emphasizes the metaphorical role of a caring shepherd feeding and guarding his spiritual flock, whereas rector stresses administrative governance, jurisdictional rule, and institutional direction.
  • Dean: Similar because both occupy administrative leadership roles in collegiate universities or cathedral chapters; differs because a dean typically presides over a specific academic division, faculty, or cathedral clergy, whereas a rector governs the entire collegiate body or parish.
  • Curate: Similar because both are Anglican clerics ministering within a parish setting; differs because a curate is an assistant or subordinate priest licensed to aid the rector, functioning under the rector's direct authority and supervision.
