• ignorant: Similar because both describe a lack of knowledge; differs because ignorant is the neutral, everyday word for simply not knowing, with no implication that darkness has fallen or that the person is helpless, whereas benighted implies a whole condition of darkness, being engulfed by ignorance as night engulfs a traveller.
  • unenlightened: Similar because both describe absence of knowledge or understanding; differs because unenlightened is the Enlightenment-era metaphor, the mind not yet lit by reason, closer in spirit to benighted but more neutral and less vivid, describing a state without the image of being overtaken by night.
  • uneducated: Similar because both can describe lack of learning; differs because uneducated is factual and social, referring to formal schooling, while benighted is metaphorical and moral, a person can be uneducated but sharp, or educated yet benighted in their delusions.
  • backward: Similar because both are used of people or places deemed intellectually or culturally undeveloped; differs because backward implies a judgment of progress and modernity, a backward region, whereas benighted stresses darkness and helplessness rather than slowness, and is the more literary word.
  • uncivilized / uncivilised: Similar because both can condemn a people or place as lacking culture and enlightenment; differs because uncivilized implies a lack of the trappings of civilisation, law, arts, cities, while benighted specifically implies darkness of mind, moral and intellectual night, and is less bluntly social.
  • dark: Similar because both can metaphorically mean lacking light, either literally or intellectually; differs because dark is extremely broad, dark room, dark mood, dark secret, while benighted is the specialised participle meaning "enveloped in night," with a fixed moral and ignorance sense attached.
  • unlettered: Similar because both describe lack of learning; differs because unlettered is a precise, sympathetic word for being unschooled in letters, carrying no sense of engulfing darkness or helplessness, while benighted is grander, darker, and more judgmental.
  • primitive: Similar because both are applied to peoples and eras deemed early or undeveloped; differs because primitive is an anthropological term for an early stage of development, value-neutral in theory and often used of technology and art, while benighted is moral and metaphorical, always carrying the image of darkness.
  • barbarous: Similar because both can condemn a people as uncultured; differs because barbarous stresses savagery and cruelty, from the Greek barbaros, "stammering, non-Greek," while benighted stresses darkness of mind rather than violence, one may be barbarous and brilliant, benighted and gentle.
  • overtaken / waylaid: Similar because both describe being caught or stopped, as the benighted traveller is caught by night; differs because overtaken and waylaid are the plain physical verbs of being caught up with on a journey, with none of the moral or intellectual sense of benighted.
  • night-bound: Similar because both literally describe being bound or caught in the darkness of night; differs because night-bound is a rare, purely literal compound with no figurative sense at all, whereas benighted has carried the moral sense for three centuries.
  • lost: Similar because both describe someone unable to find their way or groping in confusion; differs because lost is the broad, everyday word for being unable to find one's way, or for a vanished thing, while benighted specifies the cause, enveloping night, and adds the moral extension.
  • deluded: Similar because both describe being misled by darkness of mind; differs because deluded implies active false belief or deception, while benighted implies passive engulfment in darkness, the deluded person holds a wrong belief, the benighted person simply cannot see.
  • obscure: Similar because both can mean covered in darkness; differs because obscure is used of things that are dim, indistinct, or hard to understand, an obscure reference, and lacks the personal, pitying helplessness of benighted as applied to people.
