• Combray: Similar because both refer to the identical pastoral setting of Marcel Proust's childhood memories; differs because Illiers is the actual, tangible geographical town in Eure-et-Loir, whereas Combray is the fictionalized, immortalized literary realm in *In Search of Lost Time*.
  • Chartres: Similar because both are historic towns in the Eure-et-Loir department closely connected by geography and Proustian biography; differs because Chartres is a major administrative city world-famous for its massive High Gothic cathedral, whereas Illiers is a quiet, small market town centered on provincial domesticity.
  • Cabourg: Similar because both are real French locations where Proust stayed that served as templates for key settings in his novel; differs because Cabourg is a fashionable Norman seaside resort on the English Channel that inspired the fictional "Balbec," whereas Illiers is an inland agrarian town in the Beauce that inspired Combray.
  • Balbec: Similar because both represent foundational physical places that anchor the emotional development of Proust's narrator; differs because Balbec is a fictional maritime town of grand hotels and ocean storms, whereas Illiers is a real inland village of gardens, churches, and quiet bedrooms.
  • Giverny: Similar because both are small French rural towns forever transfigured and renamed or preserved in honor of a master artist's sensory vision; differs because Giverny is celebrated for Claude Monet's Impressionist water-lily gardens and painting studio, whereas Illiers is celebrated for Proust's literary exploration of memory and childhood.
