Note on ancient etymological debate: While classical philologists since Pindar (who sang in Nemean Ode 2 of "the singers of stitched verses", rha-ptōn epeōn aoidoi) have derived rhapsōidós from rháptein ("to stitch") + ōidḗ ("song"), an alternative ancient folk etymology connected the first element to rhábdos (ῥάβδος, "staff, wand"), interpreting the rhapsodist as a "singer with a staff". However, modern historical linguistics conclusively affirms the "song-stitcher" etymology, reflecting a widespread Indo-European poetic metaphor comparing the composition of poetry to weaving or stitching fabric (cf. Sanskrit sūtra "thread / aphorism", Latin textus "woven cloth / text").
