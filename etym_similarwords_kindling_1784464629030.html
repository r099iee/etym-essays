• Igniting: Similar because both mean setting on fire; differs because igniting is technical or scientific (from Latin ignis "fire"), while kindling is homely and can name the fuel as well as the act.
  • Lighting: Similar because both mean starting a fire; differs because lighting is mild and general (a lamp, a pipe, a candle), without kindling's dry-sticks and small-beginnings blaze image.
  • Setting fire to / setting alight: Similar because both mean causing something to burn; differs because the set-phrases are plain and deliberate, while kindling implies the slow building of a fire from tinder and carries a hint of creation.
  • Torching: Similar because both mean setting ablaze; differs because torching is modern slang for deliberate criminal arson, not the natural fire-starting verb.
  • Burning: Similar because both are about fire; differs because burning names the result or ongoing consumption, while kindling is the act of starting the fire and the material used.
  • Sparking: Similar because both involve beginning a fire or a reaction; differs because sparking names the small initial flash itself, while kindling covers the whole process from tinder to flame.
  • Firing: Similar because both can mean causing to ignite; differs because firing (of a kiln, furnace, or gun) is more mechanical or industrial, while kindling is domestic and gradual.
  • Inflaming: Similar because both mean arousing or setting ablaze, especially figuratively; differs because inflaming stresses anger, passion, or disease (from Latin inflammare), while kindling retains the homely hearth image.
  • Arousing: Similar because both can mean exciting feelings; differs because arousing is the general figurative verb with no fire image, while kindling keeps the flame metaphor visible.
  • Enkindling: Similar because it is a rarer, more literary doublet of kindling; differs because enkindling (with the en- prefix) is bookish and archaic in tone, while kindling is the ordinary modern form.
  • Tinder: Similar because both name material that catches fire; differs because tinder is specifically the very flammable material that catches a spark, while kindling is the small wood that burns once lit.
  • Fuel: Similar because both name material burned; differs because fuel is any material burned for energy, while kindling is specifically the small fire-starting wood of a hearth or campfire.
