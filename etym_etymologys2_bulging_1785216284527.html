Root word BULGING descends from a Celtic-Latin chain, one linear derivation:
1. PIE *bʰelǵʰ- "to swell, bloat, puff up" (prehistoric reconstruction; attested in Celtic, Germanic, and Indo-Aryan branches).
2. Proto-Celtic *bolgos "bag, belly" (reconstructed from Old Irish bolg, Welsh bol, Gaulish bulga).
3. Gaulish bulga "leather bag, knapsack" (attested; loaned into Latin).
4. Latin bulga "leather sack, knapsack" (attested 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE; a Celtic loanword).
5. Old French boulge / bouge "wallet, leather pouch" (attested 12th c.; from Latin bulga).
6. Middle English bulge / bouge "wallet, bag; hump" (attested 13th c.).
7. English bulge (verb) "to bend outward, to swell out" (attested 16th c., developed from the noun sense "hump, protuberance").
8. English BULGING (present participle used as adjective, 17th c.) "swelling or protruding outward" — as in "big bulging blind eyes."

Note on the debate: the noun's older sense was "leather bag, wallet," and the modern "swelling" sense arose in the 16th century when the noun's secondary meaning "hump" generated the verb "to bulge." The PIE root *bʰelǵʰ- is reconstructed; its Germanic descendant *balgiz is the ancestor of English belly and bellows, and its Indo-Aryan descendant is Sanskrit √bṛh "to grow great, to swell," giving Hindi बृहत् (bṛhat) "large, great."
