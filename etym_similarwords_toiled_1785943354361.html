• Labored: Similar because both mean working hard, with the same weight of strain; differs because "labored" also describes heavy breathing ("labored breath") and forced, effortful writing or style, while "toiled" keeps its centre on physical or mental work.
  • Worked: Similar because both name the act of doing a job; differs because "worked" is the neutral, all-purpose verb with no implication of hardship, while "toiled" insists on strain, duration, and weariness.
  • Slaved: Similar because both mean working exhaustingly; differs because "slaved" is informal and hyperbolically evokes bondage and unfair servitude, while "toiled" is dignified and neutral in moral colouring.
  • Drudged: Similar because both mean performing routine hard labour; differs because "drudged" stresses the dull, menial, uninspiring monotony of the work, while "toiled" stresses physical exertion and effort rather than boredom.
  • Strove / striven: Similar because both mean making great effort; differs because "strove" focuses on striving toward a goal or battling obstacles, while "toiled" focuses on the sustained, wearying labour itself.
  • Moiled: Similar because both appear in the archaic doublet "toiled and moiled"; differs because "moil" specifically implies working in mud, wet, or confusion, and is now dialectal and rare.
  • Slogged: Similar because both mean working or moving doggedly through difficulty; differs because "slogged" is informal and suggests plodding through drudgery or mud, with none of "toil"'s literary dignity.
  • Sweated: Similar because both evoke the physical heat and exhaustion of hard work; differs because "sweated" focuses on the body's sweating (and can mean exploiting workers), while "toiled" names the labour itself.
  • Ground / grinded: Similar because both suggest sustained, oppressive, repetitive effort; differs because "ground" is used of machines and millwork and, figuratively, of being worn down, whereas "toiled" is the labourer's own effort.
  • Travailed: Similar because both are literary words for painful, arduous effort; differs because "travail" (from Latin tripalium, a torture instrument) carries older, more severe overtones of suffering and childbirth, while "toiled" is about steady hard work.
  • Struggled: Similar because both imply difficulty and effort; differs because "struggled" emphasises resistance against obstacles or opponents, while "toiled" emphasises sustained labour without the fight imagery.
  • Strived: Similar because both mean attempting hard to achieve something; differs because "strived" is about ambition and aspiration toward a goal, while "toiled" is about the grinding exertion of the work itself.
