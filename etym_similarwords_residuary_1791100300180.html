• remaining: Similar because it means left over; differs because remaining is general while residuary is a legal term of art for estates.
  • residual: Similar because it denotes the remainder; differs because residual is broader (residual income, heat) while residuary is probate-specific.
  • leftover: Similar because it means what remains; differs because leftover is colloquial and often for food, never for wills.
  • surplus: Similar because it is excess over need; differs because surplus implies abundance, while residuary is neutral remainder.
  • balance: Similar because it is what is left in an account; differs because balance is financial and general, not testamentary.
  • remainder: Similar because it is the closest lay synonym; differs because remainder is plain English while residuary signals legal drafting.
  • rest: Similar because it means the others left; differs because rest is informal and vague.
  • legatee: Similar because a residuary legatee is one kind; differs because legatee alone covers any inheritor under a will.
  • beneficiary: Similar because both receive under a will or trust; differs because beneficiary is broader and includes insurance and trusts.
  • heir: Similar because heirs inherit; differs because heir strictly inherits by intestacy while residuary legatees inherit by will.
  • devisee: Similar because a devisee takes real property; differs because devisee is for land, while residuary covers the whole leftover estate.
  • executor: Similar because executors administer residue; differs because executor is the office managing, not the person receiving.
  • testamentary: Similar because it belongs to wills; differs because testamentary is the adjective for all will matters, not just residue.
