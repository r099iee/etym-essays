Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*rē- / *ret-
"net, sieve, woven mesh"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*rēti-
"woven net for fishing/hunting"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
rēte
"net, snare, web"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
rēticulum
"little net, mesh bag, network"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. CE)
rēticulātus
"made like a net, net-like"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1650s)
reticulated
"netted, having a mesh pattern"

Note: Latin rēte is cognate with Lithuanian rėtis ('sieve, sifter'), though some historical linguists suggest it may have entered Proto-Italic as an ancient Mediterranean substrate term for woven fishing and hunting gear.
