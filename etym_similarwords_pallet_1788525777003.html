• cot: Similar because both denote small, narrow, lightweight, simple beds; differs because a cot (originally Anglo-Indian from Hindi khāṭ) typically features a collapsible metal or wooden frame with taut fabric or springs, whereas a historical pallet is a simple straw-filled sack or mat laid directly on or near the floor.
  • bunk: Similar because both refer to austere, space-saving sleeping arrangements; differs because a bunk is typically a narrow built-in berth fixed to a wall or stacked vertically in tiers (as in ships, barracks, or sleeper trains).
  • mattress: Similar because both provide cushioning for sleep; differs because a mattress (from Arabic al-maṭraḥ "place where something is thrown down") is a modern, upholstered, thick cushioned foundation containing springs, foam, or batting, whereas a pallet denotes a crude, thin, rudimentary pad.
  • paillasse: Similar because it is the exact historical and etymological French equivalent (from French paille "straw"), denoting a coarse sack stuffed with straw; differs because paillasse (or palliasse) is an archaic or technical term often used specifically for the bottom straw layer supporting a feather mattress.
  • futon: Similar because both are thin, flexible mattresses that can be laid directly upon the floor and rolled or folded away; differs because a futon is a traditional Japanese quilted cotton bedding system with distinct cultural and design standards.
  • trundle bed: Similar because both are low-profile sleeping accommodations kept close to the floor; differs because a trundle bed (or truckle bed) is a low bed on castors designed to roll underneath a master bedframe when not in use.
  • stretcher: Similar because both serve as portable, temporary, flat surfaces for resting or sleeping; differs because a stretcher (or litter) is a specialised medical apparatus with carrying handles used to transport wounded or incapacitated persons.
  • hammock: Similar because both represent simple, non-traditional beds popular among sailors and travellers; differs because a hammock is suspended from cords between two hanging points rather than resting on the ground.
  • palette: Similar as an exact homophone often confused in spelling; differs because palette specifically refers to an artist's paint-mixing board or colour scheme, whereas pallet refers to a straw bed or industrial shipping platform.
  • skid: Similar in industrial warehousing as a flat platform for handling heavy bulk cargo; differs because a skid has top decking and runners without bottom deck boards, whereas a modern pallet features both top and bottom deck boards to enable four-way forklift entry.
