• Contemporary: Similar because both relate to the present moment; differs because "contemporary" merely marks coexistence in time without implying public discussion or current interest.
  • Timely: Similar because both concern relevance to the moment; differs because "timely" stresses well-judged practical timing and urgency, while "topical" refers to whatever is the subject of current public debate.
  • Current: Similar because both indicate present relevance; differs because "current" is a neutral marker of the present (current price, current affairs) without the sense of active discussion that "topical" carries.
  • Fashionable: Similar because both signal what is popular at a given moment; differs because "fashionable" emphasizes social trend and can trivialize, whereas "topical" keeps its link to the day's actual topics of concern.
  • Modish: Similar because both suggest the intellectual vogue of the moment; differs because "modish" is overtly dismissive of mere trendiness, while "topical" may be neutral or positive.
  • Relevant: Similar because both describe something bearing on the matter at hand; differs because "relevant" is the broad term for pertinence to any subject, while "topical" specifically ties pertinence to the current moment of public debate.
  • Newsworthy: Similar because both describe matters of present public interest; differs because "newsworthy" belongs to journalism and implies something worth reporting, while "topical" describes the subject of discussion whether or not it is a news event.
  • Up-to-date: Similar because both suggest connection with the present; differs because "up-to-date" means modern in information or style, while "topical" means engaged with the live issues being discussed now.
  • Local: Similar because both can mean "relating to a particular place"; differs because "local" is the ordinary word for a neighbourhood or district, while "topical" in this sense is a rarer, more formal synonym retaining the Greek topos.
  • Of-the-moment (topical "medical/local" twin, a homonymous usage): Similar as the same word in one of its senses; differs in that the medical usage denotes application to a spot on the body and must be distinguished contextually from the news-value sense.
