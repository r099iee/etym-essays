pennants (plural noun) — Long, narrow, tapering flags or streamers, typically triangular or swallow-tailed, flown from the mastheads and rigging of naval warships, the battlements and turrets of medieval fortifications, and modern sports stadiums. Beyond their primary vexillological role as symbols of command, naval commission, and military allegiance, the term encompasses championship banners awarded in professional sports (especially North American baseball), specialized rigging cords in nautical seamanship, and continuation chart patterns in financial technical analysis.

Primary sense — naval vexillology, maritime protocol, and military flags. In maritime history and sovereign naval protocol, pennants (traditionally spelled and pronounced pendants in British nautical parlance) serve as essential visual instruments of status and communication at sea. The most famous is the commissioning pennant (or masthead pennant), an extraordinarily long, thin streamer flown day and night from the mainmast of a sovereign warship to signify that the vessel is in active commission under a commanding naval officer holding a military commission. In the International Code of Signals, specialized numeral and substitute pennants (distinct from rectangular letter flags) are hoisted in tactical combinations to transmit semaphore messages, tactical maneuvers, and distress warnings between vessels. In historical military architecture—directly matching the source text ("It had pennants on its tall towers")—pennants were colorful heraldic streamers mounted on castle turrets, watchtowers, and keep battlements to display the territorial heraldry and garrison readiness of the feudal lord.

Sense 2 — chivalric heraldry and knights' lances. In medieval heraldry and chivalric warfare, the term shares deep historical roots with the pennon and pennoncel. A pennon was a small, pointed or swallow-tailed streamer attached directly below the steel spearhead of a knight's lance. Bearing the knight's personal heraldic charge or badge, it fluttered in cavalry charges, serving both as an inspiring rallying point for retainers and a vivid marker of aristocratic rank. When a knight performed exceptional valor on the battlefield, the feudal commander would occasionally cut off the swallow-tailed points of his pennon, converting it into a rectangular banner and promoting him to the prestigious rank of knight banneret.

Sense 3 — sports championships and the American baseball "pennant race." In North American sports culture, especially Major League Baseball (MLB), a pennant is an iconic, triangular felt or cloth commemorative flag awarded to the club that wins the league championship (the American League or National League), thereby earning the honor of advancing to the World Series. Derived from 19th-century traditions where winning clubs flew victory flags from their stadium centerfield flagpoles, the word has spawned indelible sports idioms: the pennant race designates the high-stakes, dramatic competitive struggle among top teams during the final weeks of the regular season, while "capturing the pennant" represents the culmination of a franchise's season-long campaign.

Sense 4 — nautical rigging and mechanical hoisting tackle. In practical seamanship, sailing ship rigging, and marine engineering, a pennant (often maintaining its traditional nautical spelling pendant) is not a flag at all, but a short, heavy length of wire cable, braided rope, or chain fitted with an eye splice, thimble, or tackle block at one or both ends. It is secured to a masthead, boom, or deck cleat to provide a heavy-duty attachment point for purchase tackles, mooring lines to harbor buoys, or cargo hoisting derricks.

Sense 5 — financial technical analysis: chart continuation patterns. In technical market analysis and stock trading, a pennant is a well-known short-term continuation chart formation. It develops following a sharp, almost vertical price advance or decline (known as the "flagpole"), followed by a brief consolidation phase where price volatility contracts between two converging trendlines, forming a miniature symmetrical triangle that resembles a triangular pennant on a staff. Technical analysts interpret this pattern as a temporary resting phase before the security breaks out decisively in the direction of the original trend.

  • In naval warfare and seamanship — commissioning pennant, broad pennant (flown by a commodore), paying-off pennant (an extremely long streamer flown when a warship returns home from a foreign commission to be decommissioned).
  • In feudal and fortress architecture — tower pennants, castle streamers: decorative, weather-resistant heraldic flags flown from stone battlements to project feudal power.
  • In professional sports culture — pennant race, pennant winner, league pennant: designates baseball championship flags and the late-season competition to secure them.
  • In nautical rigging — rudder pennant, mooring pennant: a short, heavy wire or cordage used to tether vessels or secure marine equipment.
  • In financial trading — bullish pennant, bearish pennant: triangular chart consolidation patterns signaling continuation of momentum.

Register, tone, and stylistic boundaries. Pennants operates across varied registers, from grand historical literature and naval protocol to high-energy sports broadcasting. Its imagery perpetually evokes flapping fabric in high winds, chivalric honor, maritime majesty, and hard-fought victory.
