• Tasmanian: Similar because it is the singular and adjectival base of the same word; differs because it denotes one person or an attribute, not the plural peoples.
  • Palawa: Similar because it denotes Aboriginal Tasmanians in self-designation; differs because it specifically marks Indigenous identity and lutruwita belonging.
  • Aboriginal Tasmanians: Similar because it is the precise modern term for Indigenous islanders; differs because it explicitly distinguishes them from settler Tasmanians.
  • Australians: Similar because Tasmanians are Australian citizens and share settler history; differs because Australians broadly includes mainlanders, while Tasmanians marks island specificity.
  • Victorians: Similar because they are fellow southeastern Australian state residents; differs because they belong to mainland Victoria, not the island state.
  • New South Welshmen: Similar because they appear in the same source sentence as parallel colonials; differs because they belong to New South Wales, the oldest mainland colony.
  • Maoris: Similar because they are another Indigenous peoples of Oceania discussed in parallel; differs because they are Polynesian peoples of Aotearoa New Zealand with distinct language.
  • Aborigines: Similar because Victorian writers classed Tasmanians with mainland Aboriginal peoples; differs because mainland groups have different languages and continuous occupation narratives.
  • islanders: Similar because Tasmanians are island dwellers; differs because islander is generic and may imply small-island exoticism rather than state identity.
  • Convicts: Similar because Van Diemen's Land Tasmanians were famously convicts; differs because convict denotes penal status, not geographic origin.
  • Veddahs: Similar because old histories pair them as primitive types; differs because Veddahs are Sri Lankan forest hunter-gatherers, unrelated to Tasmania.
  • Bushmen: Similar because they are paired hunter-gatherer exemplars in stadial histories; differs because they are San peoples of southern Africa.
  • Truganini: Similar because she symbolises Tasmanian Aboriginal history; differs because she is an individual, long mythologised as the last Tasmanian, not the peoples.
