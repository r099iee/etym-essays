CHAIN — single root to English yew: - PIE *h₁eyH- (often extended *h₁eiH-u̯o-) "yew tree; reddish-brown, mottled wood" (tree-name root, late PIE; exact laryngeal and extension debated). - Proto-Germanic *īwaz / *īhwaz "yew; yew bow" (reconstructed, 1st millennium BCE). - Old English īw, ēow, ēoh "yew tree; (ēoh) the rune-name ᘋ" (attested 8th–11th c.). - Middle English ew, u, yw "yew" (11th–15th c.; the initial y- is an orthographic device for the sound /juː/). - Modern English yew "the tree; its wood" (present-day form). NOTE ON DEBATE: The PIE base is reconstructed, not attested, and is written variously *h₁eyH-, *h₁iH-u̯o-, or with other laryngeal notations; some scholars connect the root to a colour sense "reddish-brown, mottled" (supported by the Balto-Slavic shift of the word to "bird cherry" and a possible Italic reflex in Latin ūva "grape, cluster of grapes"), while the Western branches (Celtic, Germanic) preserve the plain meaning "yew." The early links of the chain are therefore reconstructions; the Old English and later stages are directly attested.
