- 4th Century BCE - Attested as one of the most revered and highest-paid tragic actors in Athens, performing lead roles in Sophocles' tragedies.
- 2nd Century CE - Immortalized in Roman literature by Aulus Gellius and Plutarch as the supreme exemplar of genuine emotional immersion (*perezhivanie*) in theatrical art.
- 18th Century - Rediscovered by Enlightenment thinkers (notably Denis Diderot in *The Paradox of Acting*) as the central historical case study debating emotionalism versus technical detachment.
- 20th–21st Century - Celebrated in theatrical theory and drama history as the earliest recorded precursor to Method Acting and affective memory.
