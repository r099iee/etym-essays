- Late 19th c. (1875–1890s) - Received with intense scholarly fascination and controversy across Austro-German universities as a radical pioneer who divorced state formation from divine right and liberal contract theories.
- Turn of the 20th c. (1899–1914) - Gained widespread academic prestige in the United States following Albion Small's translation of The Outlines of Sociology; hailed by Lester Ward as a foundational architect of macro-sociology.
- Interwar Period (1920s–1930s) - Subject to ideological reinterpretation and tragic distortion; while his conflict theory inspired Franz Oppenheimer's anti-authoritarian political analysis, his terminology of Rassenkampf was vulgarized and mischaracterized by rising fascist ideologues despite his strictly sociological, non-biological framework.
- Mid-to-Late 20th c. - Re-evaluated as an indispensable forerunner of conflict theory, political anthropology, and the sociological critique of state sovereignty, read alongside Max Weber and Vilfredo Pareto.
- 21st c. - Regarded in academic sociology and political philosophy as a classic foundational theorist of state formation through conquest, studied for his early insights into ethnocentrism, group dynamics, and institutionalized coercion.
