• Republic (Politeia): Similar because both are masterpiece dialogues by Plato addressing ideal order and the Theory of Forms; differs because the Republic focuses primarily on justice, political philosophy, education, and the structure of the ideal city-state, whereas the Timaeus investigates cosmology, natural physics, mathematics, and the physical creation of the universe.
  • Critias: Similar because both were conceived as part of a projected Platonic trilogy and share the same interlocutors; differs because the Critias is an incomplete narrative fragment focusing almost exclusively on the political and military history of Atlantis and prehistoric Athens, lacking the comprehensive mathematical and physical metaphysics of the Timaeus.
  • Phaedo: Similar because both dialogues feature profound discussions of the immortality of the soul and the realm of eternal Forms; differs because the Phaedo is set during Socrates' final hours on death row and focuses on personal eschatology and the nature of death, whereas the Timaeus explores the grand cosmological generation of the physical cosmos.
  • De Caelo (On the Heavens): Similar because both are foundational ancient Greek cosmological treatises addressing the structure of the heavens and four earthly elements; differs because De Caelo is written by Aristotle with an empirical, non-mythological teleological approach, rejecting Plato's geometric atomic theories of the Timaeus in favor of eternal circular motion and an uncreated cosmos.
  • Phaedrus: Similar because both dialogues explore cosmic order, the chariot of the soul, and divine inspiration; differs because the Phaedrus centers on rhetoric, love (Eros), and the psychology of beauty, whereas the Timaeus addresses the structural mathematics of the World Soul and the anatomy of human physiology.
