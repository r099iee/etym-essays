• Scones: Similar because both are flat baked breads; differ because scones are smaller, finer, often sweetened and oven-baked, while bannocks are coarse whole-grain cakes on a hearthstone or griddle.
  • Oatcakes: Similar because both are traditional oat breads; differ because oatcakes are thin, crisp biscuit-cakes, not the thick flat loaves of a bannock.
  • Griddle cakes: Similar because both are griddle-baked; differ because griddle cakes are thin batter fried in a pan, lighter and wetter than a bannock's dense meal.
  • Pancakes: Similar because both are flat and griddle-fried; differ because pancakes are thin batter with a soft, spongy crumb, whereas a bannock is a dense, unleavened or lightly leavened meal cake.
  • Damper: Similar because both are hearth-baked camp breads; differs because damper is the Australian bushman's camp loaf of the colonial frontier, a different tradition from the bannock's Gaelic and Scottish home.
  • Bread: Similar because both are baked grain foods; differs because bread is the general, usually oven-baked, yeast-leavened loaf, while a bannock is flat, coarse, and cooked on the fire.
  • Flatbread: Similar because both are flat baked breads; differs because flatbread is a broad international category (tortilla, roti, lavash), while a bannock names the specific British-Isles griddle loaf.
  • Ashcake / johnnycake: Similar because both are cornmeal hearth-breads baked directly in the embers or on a stone; differ because these are American and usually corn-based, while a bannock is the barley- or oatmeal loaf of Scotland and the North of England.
