• Armistice: Similar because both are agreements to stop fighting; differs because an armistice is a formal, usually large-scale suspension of hostilities between states (as in 1918), typically a prelude to peace talks, whereas a truce may be small, local and informal.
  • Ceasefire: Similar because both halt combat; differs because a ceasefire is a modern operational term of limited scope (a halt to shooting), while a truce is a broader pact of mutual restraint.
  • Peace: Similar because both end fighting; differs fundamentally in duration and permanence — peace is the desired permanent settlement of a conflict, while a truce is temporary by definition.
  • Treaty: Similar because both are agreements between parties; differs because a treaty is a permanent legal instrument that may cover any subject, while a truce is a temporary military arrangement.
  • Suspension of arms: Similar because the phrase formally means the same; differs in being legalistic and periphrastic rather than a distinct noun of everyday speech.
  • Lull: Similar because both name a quiet period in fighting; differs because a lull is an unplanned, often natural pause, whereas a truce is deliberate and agreed.
  • Respite: Similar because both mean a break from pressure; differs because respite is about relief from hardship or duties in general and need not involve two opposing parties.
  • Standstill: Similar because both mean a halt; differs in being neutral and physical — traffic or work can come to a standstill without any agreement between enemies.
  • Moratorium: Similar because both are formal temporary suspensions; differs in domain — moratoria apply to payments, laws or debts, not to fighting.
  • Détente: Similar because both describe easing of tension between states; differs because détente is a long-term political climate, not a specific agreement about arms.
  • Modus vivendi: Similar because both are working arrangements between conflicting parties; differs in being a broader, usually lasting accommodation rather than a military armistice.
  • Parley: Similar because both belong to the same negotiation scene; differs because a parley is the meeting or conference itself, not the agreement halting the fight — in the source sentence the dwarves must request a truce and a parley.
