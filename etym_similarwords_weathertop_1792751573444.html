• Amon Sûl: Similar because it is the exact identical geographic location and structure in Middle-earth; differs because Amon Sûl is the high Sindarin Elvish name ("Hill of the Wind") favored in royal Gondorian chronicles and ancient lore, whereas Weathertop is the common Westron vernacular name used by hobbits, rangers, and common men.
  • Hill: Similar because both denote a natural elevated landform rising above the surrounding terrain; differs because hill is a generic geological classification, whereas Weathertop is a specific historic peak crowned by an ancient imperial citadel.
  • Peak: Similar because Weathertop forms the highest pointed summit of the Weather Hills; differs because peak generally implies a jagged alpine mountain crest, whereas Weathertop is a weathered, conical highland knoll.
  • Summit: Similar because both refer to the highest apex or crown of an elevation; differs because summit is a geometric or topological descriptor of the very top point, whereas Weathertop designates the entire hill and its historical fortress complex.
  • Watchtower: Similar because Weathertop was famous primarily for the fortified tower built upon its summit by Elendil; differs because a watchtower is a man-made military architectural structure, whereas Weathertop is the natural geological mountain upon which the tower stood.
  • Citadel: Similar because the complex atop Weathertop served as a fortified stronghold and sanctuary during the wars of Arnor; differs because citadel emphasizes a heavily garrisoned military fortress within a walled city, whereas Weathertop was an isolated wilderness outpost.
  • Promontory: Similar because Weathertop juts out prominently as the southernmost vantage point overlooking the plains of Eriador; differs because a promontory typically projects into a body of water or drops off into a sheer precipice, whereas Weathertop is an inland hill.
  • Beacon hill: Similar because Weathertop functioned historically as a primary fire-beacon station communicating strategic warnings across the North Kingdom; differs because beacon hill is a generic functional descriptor, whereas Weathertop possessed profound political, palantír-housing, and mythic importance.
  • Barrow-downs: Similar because both are ancient, eerie highland landmarks in Eriador steeped in the tragic history of the fallen North Kingdom of Arnor; differs because the Barrow-downs are ancient burial mounds infested with undead Barrow-wights, whereas Weathertop is a singular defensive observation peak.
  • Orthanc: Similar because both were ancient Númenórean fortresses constructed of unbreakable stone to house a Seeing Stone (palantír); differs because Orthanc was an intact, black-stone spire built in a mountain ring (Isengard) in Gondor, whereas the watchtower on Weathertop was toppled and ruined by the forces of Angmar in T.A. 1409.
