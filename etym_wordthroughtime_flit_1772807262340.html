- [Light swift movement] - "Flit" in older English means to move lightly and quickly, darting from place to place, especially of birds or butterflies.
- [Stealthy departure] - The informal idiom "do a flit" (also "moonlight flit") develops in British English for secretly and hurriedly moving away, especially to dodge rent or other obligations.
- [Twin modern senses] - Today the word splits: a literary sense of light darting movement and an informal sense of absconding, the criminal flavour strongest in "do a flit."
