paskhein

↓

pathos
"suffering, feeling, emotion"
↓
Latin (borrowing) (c. 1st c. BCE - 16th c.)
pathos
"emotion, passion (rhetorical term)"
↓
English (c. 1590s)
pathos
"emotional appeal, poignant quality"
↓
Modern English (present)
pathos
"quality evoking pity"

Note: exact PIE reconstruction debated (often *kwenth- or *skw-); Greek development paskhein to pathos via labiovelar and aspirate shifts is secure; sympathy, empathy, pathetic share the root.
