• Thucydides: Similar because both are the canonical Greek historians whose work defines the genre of contemporary war history. Differs because Thucydides wrote of the Peloponnesian War in which he himself served, with austere political analysis, while Procopius served a general a thousand years later and produced a double corpus of panegyric and secret invective.
  • Herodotus: Similar because both are Greek historians whose narratives mix reportage with wonders and marvels (Gibbon's "as Procopius had been told" echoes Herodotean inquiry). Differs because Herodotus is the "father of history" writing ethnography and inquiry, while Procopius is the bureaucratic courtier-historian of a Christian empire.
  • Ammianus Marcellinus: Similar because both are the last great classicizing historians of the later Roman world, writing in the 4th century (Ammianus in Latin, Procopius in Greek). Differs because Ammianus wrote a continuation of Tacitus in Latin prose and was a soldier, while Procopius wrote in Greek as Belisarius's secretary and added the scandalous Secret History.
  • historian: Similar because Procopius is the preeminent example of the Greek secular historian of late antiquity. Differs because historian is the generic category term, while Procopius names the specific author with his specific double reputation.
  • chronicler: Similar because Procopius chronicled the reign of Justinian. Differs because a chronicler records events year by year with little analysis, while Procopius wrote elevated literary history with speeches, digressions, and a hidden satirical agenda.
  • panegyrist: Similar because the Buildings is court panegyric, making Procopius a panegyrist of Justinian. Differs because panegyrist describes only half his output; the Secret History makes him equally an enemy of the court.
  • satirist: Similar because the Secret History is savage satire of Justinian and Theodora. Differs because satirist ignores his official histories, while Procopius is defined by the contrast between the two.
  • Belisarius: Similar because the general and his secretary are inseparable in the wars' narrative. Differs because Belisarius is the military actor and subject of the histories, while Procopius is the recorder who both celebrated and savaged him.
  • Justinian: Similar because emperor and historian are the two poles of the same reign, both fixed in the historical record by Procopius's pen. Differs because Justinian is the ruler, the subject of both panegyric and invective, while Procopius is the observer.
  • Byzantinist: Similar because both terms belong to the study of the Byzantine world. Differs because a Byzantinist is a modern scholar of Byzantium, while Procopius is a primary source, the man the Byzantinists study.
