APOTHEOSIS denotes the act of raising a mortal to the rank of a god, and by extension the glorification, idealisation or perfect embodiment of a person, principle or type. In the sentence the magnificent hunter, the ______ of the wild, the falcon on the king's gloved fist, the peregrine is presented as the wild made divine: not merely a fine example of wildness but wildness itself deified, concentrated into feathers, speed and killing poise. The apposition hunter equals apotheosis equals falcon on the fist builds a crescendo from function to metaphysics to heraldic image, before the tragic reversal into helplessness under pesticides.

Classically the word belonged to Roman imperial cult, the formal consecration of a dead emperor among the gods by senatorial decree, with witness, pyre, eagle released heavenward and temple honours. Christianity suppressed the rite but preserved the vocabulary for saints, heroes and artistic exaltation. Modern usage is therefore almost always figurative and literary, retaining the aura of incense and marble while applying it to athletes, musicians, ideas or, as here, animals. Register is high, solemn and slightly ironic when applied to the mundane; to call a falcon an apotheosis dignifies it with liturgy.

  • Primary sense: deification, elevation to divine status. The apotheosis of Augustus, of Heracles, of the Virgin in Baroque ceilings where painted figures spiral into heaven. Theological and art-historical, it implies ritual, witnesses and a change of ontological rank from human to god. Semantic bridge: directly continues Greek apotheoun to make a god plus -osis action-noun, via Latin apotheosis deification of emperors. The falcon sentence borrows this gravity to say the bird was worshipped as wildness incarnate in falconry culture.
  • Secondary sense: glorified ideal, quintessence, highest point. She is the apotheosis of grace, his performance was the apotheosis of cool, the 1960s E-Type as apotheosis of automotive design. Here no one claims literal divinity; the word means perfected culmination, the moment a type achieves its purest form before decline. Bridge: metaphorical bleaching from rite to rhetoric, mapping divine elevation onto aesthetic perfection. The bridge is reinforced by visual art where apotheosis scenes literally place the hero highest in the composition, making highest equal most divine. In the passage the falcon is the wild at its highest before DDT brings the fall.
  • Tertiary and technical senses. In literary criticism apotheosis can denote the climactic glorifying scene or chapter, the hero's transfiguration. In popular culture it titles works, bands, games and comics to signal ultimate form: Apotheosis as final boss evolution. In Mormon theology exaltation is sometimes glossed apotheosis, the belief humans may become gods. In biology and conservation rhetoric, to call a predator the apotheosis of a habitat is to grant it umbrella-species sanctity. Each preserves the core image of lifting up while dropping literal polytheism, showing how a pagan rite survives as a superlative.

Apotheosis differs from near-synonyms by retaining vertical motion and sacred light: deification stresses the change of nature, glorification stresses praise, epitome stresses summary, culmination stresses temporal end. Apotheosis uniquely combines all three — raised up, praised in light, summing a kind. Its plural apotheoses is pronounced with long e. To master it is to hear both temple smoke and gallery hyperbole whenever a writer crowns a hunter, a car or a falcon as god for a paragraph.
