PIE *weid- “to see, to perceive, to know” (c. 4500–2500 BCE) — the great see/know root, whose founding equation is “I have seen” = “I know” → Latin vidēre “to see” (attested from the 3rd c. BCE) → Latin vīsus (past participle) “seen”; noun vīsus “sight, appearance, aspect, the thing seen” → (Vulgar Latin contraction *vissum) → Old French vis “face, appearance — the seen thing, the look of a person” (c. 9th–12th c.) → Old French visage “face, countenance, appearance” (vis + the nominal suffix -age; attested c. 1080 in the Chanson de Roland) → Anglo-Norman visage → Middle English visage (attested c. 1300) → English visage “face, countenance” (modern). Note the same Latin participle produced visor/vizor “the face-covering of a helmet,” French vis-à-vis “face to face” (borrowed 18th c.), and dévisager “to stare at”; the Greek branch of the same root gave idea, history, idyll, eidos; the Germanic branch gave wit, wise, witness; the Indo-Iranian branch gave Vedic veda “knowledge,” Hindi vidyā “learning,” vidita “known.”
