- Early Tahitian plant name - neutral ethnobotanical label for a shore stupefacient plant used in lagoon fishing
- 18th–19th c. European contact recording - perceived as exotic curiosity, transcribed variably as hutu, hotu, huteo, hora in missionary and voyage vocabularies
- Early anthropological literature - perceived as evidence of primitive ingenuity, catalogued among fish poisons in comparative war-of-species narratives
- Modern botany - receding perception as obsolete vernacular, superseded by Barringtonia asiatica, retained only as historical synonym
- Present English encounter - perceived as confusing homograph of Latin hour, requiring explicit disambiguation as fish plant, not time
