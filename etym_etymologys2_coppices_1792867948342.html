Ancient Greek (classical period)
κόλαφος (kolaphos)
‘a blow, cuff, box on the ear’
↓
Latin (borrowed) (late, c. 4th c.)
colaphus
‘a cuff, buffet’
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 5th–8th c.)
*colpus
‘a blow, stroke’
↓
Old French (11th–12th c.)
colp, later coup
‘blow, stroke, cut’
↓
Old French (verb) (12th–13th c.)
couper
‘to cut, strike off’
↓
Old French (participle noun) (13th c.)
copeiz
‘a cutting, cut-over wood’
↓
Middle English (c. 1400)
copys, copies
‘thicket grown for cutting’
↓
Modern English (plural)
coppices
‘managed thickets’

Note: Greek kolaphos itself has no certain deeper etymology and is treated by modern scholars as probable substrate vocabulary; the chain above follows the standard handbook derivation through Latin and French without positing an unattested PIE root.
