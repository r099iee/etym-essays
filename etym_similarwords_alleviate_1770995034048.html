• mitigate: Similar because both reduce the severity of something bad; differs because mitigate softens the harshness of an impact or its consequences, whereas alleviate literally lifts weight or pressure.
  • relieve: Similar because both ease suffering or pressure; differs because relieve is more immediate and can be complete, whereas alleviate suggests gradual, partial reduction.
  • assuage: Similar because both reduce unpleasant feelings; differs because assuage soothes an inner emotion or appetite (guilt, fear, thirst), whereas alleviate lightens any burden, physical or figurative.
  • palliate: Similar because both reduce the harshness of symptoms or problems; differs because palliate masks or softens without addressing the cause, whereas alleviate genuinely lightens the load.
  • ameliorate: Similar because both make a bad situation better; differs because ameliorate actively improves toward a better state, whereas alleviate only lessens the negative without promising improvement.
  • ease: Similar because both make something less severe; differs because ease is broader, more casual, and also means "to relax or make comfortable," whereas alleviate is formal and specifically about lifting a burden.
  • allay: Similar because both reduce suffering or unease; differs because allay chiefly calms fears, doubts, or suspicions, whereas alleviate also applies to physical pain and material burdens.
  • lessen: Similar because both reduce a degree or amount; differs because lessen is general and colourless, whereas alleviate is specifically about lightening a burden.
  • lighten: Similar because both share the core idea of reducing weight; differs because lighten is literal and informal, whereas alleviate is metaphorical and formal.
  • moderate: Similar because both reduce intensity; differs because moderate tempers excess toward a middle course, whereas alleviate lifts a heavy load.
  • soothe: Similar because both ease discomfort; differs because soothe comforts and calms by gentle action, whereas alleviate reduces the severity of a burden or pain.
