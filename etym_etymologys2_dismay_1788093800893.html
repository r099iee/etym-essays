↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 100 CE)
*maganą
"to be able, have power"
(the root of English may, might)

↓
Frankish (borrowed into Gaul) (5th–8th c.)
*magan
"to be strong, able"
(the Germanic word enters Gallo-Roman speech)

↓
Vulgar Latin (6th–9th c.)
*exmagāre
"to deprive of strength, to disable"
(ex- "out" + *magāre, from Germanic)

↓
Old French (11th c.)
esmaier
"to trouble, alarm, frighten"

↓
Old French (11th–12th c.)
desmaier
"to dismay; to alarm utterly"
(des- intensive prefix added)

↓
Anglo-Norman / Middle English (c. 1300)
dismaien
"to alarm, terrify, dishearten"

↓
Modern English (16th c. – present)
DISMAY

Note: The Germanic core is secure — the same root *magan appears in English may and might — but the exact date and route of the borrowing into Vulgar Latin (whether through Frankish directly into Gallo-Romance) is the standard scholarly reconstruction, not a documented form.
