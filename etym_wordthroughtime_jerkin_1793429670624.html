- Early 16th c. — Arrival in Tudor Wardrobe: Borrowed into English from the Low Countries as a fashionable, snug over-jacket worn by nobles and prosperous citizens over the doublet.
- Late 16th to Early 17th c. — The Defensive Armor of the Yeoman: Widely adopted in heavy buff leather by soldiers, sailors, pikemen, and huntsmen; becomes synonymous with sturdy English commoners and practical military resistance.
- Late 17th c. — Displacement by the Frock Coat: The introduction of the three-piece suit by Charles II (coat, waistcoat, breeches) pushes the jerkin out of mainstream gentleman's fashion into rustic, provincial, and working-class use.
- 18th to 19th c. — Romantic Archaising and Literary Trope: Survived almost entirely as a historical term in historical romances (Sir Walter Scott) and nautical adventures, symbolizing antique Tudor and Stuart ruggedness.
- Early to Mid-20th c. — Military Trench Revival: The British War Office officially revives the leather jerkin for frontline service in World War I and World War II, transforming it into an iconic piece of 20th-century combat cold-weather gear.
- Contemporary Era — Historical Reenactment and Fantasy Fashion: Today primarily encountered in historical scholarship, museum exhibitions, Renaissance fairs, and period cinema as the quintessential emblem of medieval and early modern masculine attire.
