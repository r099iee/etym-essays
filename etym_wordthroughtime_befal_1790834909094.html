- c. 800s-1100s (Old English): Befeallan meant to fall upon physically, to be deprived of, to fall to ruin, or to happen to someone - Highly physical and dynamic action.
- c. 1150s-1400s (Middle English): Befallen shifted firmly into the primary narrative verb for events occurring by chance, providence, or fate - Standard narrative formula in romances and chronicles ('so bifel that...').
- c. 1500s-1700s (Early Modern English): Befal / befall established as the standard term for grave occurrences, misfortunes, and momentous destinies - Prominent in Shakespeare, the King James Bible, and Addisonian essays.
- c. 1800s-Present: Gradual retreat from colloquial speech into elevated literary, poetic, and philosophical prose - Connotes solemnity, dramatic tension, and existential vulnerability.
