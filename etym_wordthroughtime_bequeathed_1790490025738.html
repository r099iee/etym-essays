- Old English becwethan - to say, speak to, exhort, also to declare one's will; broad speech verb
- 12th–13th c. narrowing - general say-sense dies; only leave-by-will survives, kept alive in wills
- 14th–17th c. testamentary standard - bequeathed sums, goods, and souls in wills and parish registers; solemn and legal
- 18th–19th c. moralised inheritance (Ruskin) - debts as well as fortunes bequeathed; honour in accepting burdens; Victorian
- 20th–21st c. figurative bequeathing - governments, empires, and generations bequeath deficits, warming, and art; journalistic and rueful
- Modern legal precision - bequeathed (personalty) versus devised (realty) versus inherited (heir's view); technical
