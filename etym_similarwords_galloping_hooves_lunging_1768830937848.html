• Canter (vs. galloping): Similar in being a horse's running gait; differs because the canter is a restrained three-beat gait, smooth and collected, whereas the gallop is the full four-beat extension in which all four feet leave the ground in turn.
  • Trot (vs. galloping): Similar in being a horse's gait; differs because the trot is a two-beat diagonal rhythm, brisk and businesslike, while the gallop is the fastest, leaping, four-beat gait.
  • Lope (vs. galloping): Similar in being a horse's running gait; differs because the lope is a slow, relaxed gallop, while the true gallop is the horse stretched to its limit, accelerating and hard to stop.
  • Sprinting (vs. galloping): Similar in running at top speed; differs because sprinting is the human and general word for running flat out over a short distance, while galloping is specifically the horse's four-beat gait.
  • Racing (vs. galloping): Similar in moving at speed; differs because racing implies competition or haste toward a goal, while galloping describes the mode of motion itself.
  • Dashing (vs. galloping): Similar in swift, vigorous movement; differs because dashing is a general word for headlong motion and also means stylish (a dashing figure), while galloping is specific to the equine gait.
  • Paws (vs. hooves): Similar in being the feet of animals; differs because paws are padded, clawed, and quiet, whereas hooves are hard horny casings engineered for impact — single weight-bearing toenails that beat the ground.
  • Feet (vs. hooves): Similar in being parts of the body used for standing and moving; differs because feet are soft, flat, bare, and human, while hooves are the hard keratin casings of ungulate animals.
  • Claws (vs. hooves): Similar in being hard coverings of animal feet; differs because claws are sharp, curved, and retractable or semi-retractable for gripping, while hooves are broad, blunt weight-bearing casings for impact and speed.
  • Pouncing (vs. lunging): Similar in a sudden forward movement; differs because pouncing is downward and predatory, while lunging is a horizontal full-body thrust inherited from fencing.
  • Leaping (vs. lunging): Similar in a sudden forward movement; differs because leaping emphasizes height and the leaving of the ground, while lunging emphasizes the horizontal extension of the whole body.
  • Bounding (vs. lunging): Similar in vigorous springing movement; differs because bounding suggests repeated light, elastic springs, while lunging is a single explosive horizontal thrust.
  • Darting (vs. lunging): Similar in quick movement; differs because darting is fast but small and light, while lunging is a large, violent, full-body extension.
  • Thrusting (vs. lunging): Similar in a forward pushing movement; differs because thrusting is any forceful forward push, often of a weapon, while lunging is specifically the whole body being thrown forward, as in a fencing attack.
  • Springing (vs. lunging): Similar in sudden forward motion; differs because springing implies leaping upward or forward off the ground, while lunging is a grounded horizontal drive.
