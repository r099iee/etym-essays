• Presentation: Similar because both mean the act of offering or showing something; differs because presentation is the neutral, general term for any act of offering or showing, while presentment retains a more formal, often legal and aesthetic colouring of deliberate, solemn laying-before.
  • Representation: Similar because both denote a showing-forth of something to the mind; differs because representation stresses standing for something else, as a symbol or image stands for its object, while presentment emphasizes the direct exhibition of the fact itself rather than a substitute.
  • Depiction: Similar because both mean rendering a subject to the eye or mind; differs because depiction belongs to the arts and focuses on how an artist or writer renders a subject, while presentment covers any vivid setting-forth before the mind, from the aesthetic to the legal.
  • Portrayal: Similar because both mean a representation of a subject; differs because portrayal is specifically artistic and character-centred (the portrayal of a figure in a novel or painting), while presentment is broader and carries a formal, often legal gravity.
  • Exhibition: Similar because both mean a display of something before view; differs because exhibition implies a public showing of objects (an art exhibition), while presentment implies a mental or formal laying-before of facts, a bill, or a vivid image.
  • Depiction of fact / showing: Similar because both mean making something apparent; differs because showing is the plain everyday word, while presentment is the elevated, bookish term that adds solemnity and vividness.
  • Indictment (in the legal sense): Similar because both are grand-jury instruments laying an offense before a court; differs because an indictment is a formal written charge framed by a prosecutor and presented by the jury, while a presentment is the jury's own statement of an offense on its own knowledge, without a prosecutor's bill.
  • Accounting/presentation of a bill: Similar because both involve formally offering a financial instrument; differs because the commercial "presentment" is the technical act of presenting a bill or note for acceptance or payment, a sense not shared by the general word presentation.
