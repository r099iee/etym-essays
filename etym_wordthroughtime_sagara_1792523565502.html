- [Late Vedic & Early Epic Era, c. 1000–500 BCE] — preserved in oral dynastic genealogies of the Solar dynasty (*Sūryavaṃśa*) as a formidable warrior king born with neutralized poison (*sa-gara*) who reclaimed his usurped throne and initiated the imperial horse sacrifice — [perception: remembered as an uncompromising martial restorer of royal legitimacy and dharma]
- [Classical Epic Period, c. 500 BCE–200 CE; Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa & Mahābhārata] — immortalized in the celebrated narrative of his sixty thousand sons excavating the earth, incinerated by sage Kapila, and giving birth to the ocean (*Sāgara*) and the descent of the Gaṅgā — [perception: archetypal cautionary figure of imperial ambition, tragic paternal grief, and cosmic destiny]
- [Purāṇic & Classical Sanskrit Era, c. 300–1200 CE] — the patronymic derivation *Sāgara* became the standard poetic and cosmic word for "ocean" across all Sanskrit literature, while Sagara himself was celebrated in legal land grants as a model donor king (*Sagarādibhiḥ*) — [perception: immortalized as the mythic father of the world's oceans and the royal standard of dynastic munificence]
- [Modern & Contemporary Global Context, 19th–21st c.] — King Sagara remains a central figure in Indian classical dance, theatre, and Ramayana lore, while his linguistic legacy *sāgar* / *sāgara* serves as the everyday word for ocean across South and Southeast Asian languages — [perception: beloved mythic king whose familial tragedy forever shaped the geographical nomenclature and sacred geography of India]
