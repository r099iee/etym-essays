• Kazimir Malevich: Similar because both were preeminent early-20th-century pioneers of pure geometric abstraction who formulated radical movements (Mondrian's Neoplasticism and Malevich's Suprematism); differs because Malevich utilized dynamic diagonal compositions, circles, and crosses against infinite white space (*Black Square*), whereas Mondrian strictly maintained orthogonal perpendicular grids grounded in structural equilibrium.
  • Wassily Kandinsky: Similar because both were founding fathers of abstract art whose visual revolutions were motivated by deep esoteric/theosophical spiritual quests; differs because Kandinsky championed lyrical, expressive, free-form organic abstraction and synesthetic musical emotion, while Mondrian championed cool, objective mathematical restraint and pure geometric order.
  • Theo van Doesburg: Similar because both co-founded the De Stijl movement in 1917 and championed Neoplasticism; differs because Van Doesburg introduced dynamic diagonal lines ("Elementarism") and embraced Dadaist typography and architectural agitation, causing a famous ideological rift with the uncompromisingly orthogonal Mondrian.
  • Paul Klee: Similar because both were seminal modernists who analyzed color theory and underlying geometric structures at the Bauhaus; differs because Klee combined geometry with whimsical, child-like figurative fantasy, hieroglyphic symbols, and poetic narrative, whereas Mondrian strictly banished all representation.
  • Josef Albers: Similar because both explored the interaction of geometric form and pure color; differs because Albers focused systematically on optical color relativity within nested squares (*Homage to the Square*), whereas Mondrian explored asymmetrical cosmic balance through linear grid intervals and primary hues.
  • El Lissitzky: Similar because both were avant-garde pioneers who merged geometric abstraction with modern utopian architectural vision; differs because Lissitzky was a Russian Constructivist who created three-dimensional spatial "Prouns" and political propaganda, whereas Mondrian remained dedicated to metaphysical two-dimensional easel painting.
  • Mark Rothko: Similar because both created monumental abstract paintings intended to evoke profound spiritual contemplation through pure color and rectangular formats; differs because Rothko was an Abstract Expressionist who used luminous, soft-edged, atmospheric color fields evoking tragic human emotion, while Mondrian used crisp, hard-edged, rational primary grids.
  • Ad Reinhardt: Similar because both were ascetic purists who reduced painting to its ultimate conceptual limits through monochrome and geometric grids; differs because Reinhardt worked exclusively in near-black, subtle cruciform monochromes in the 1960s, while Mondrian celebrated the dynamic, balanced counterpoint of primary colors.
