• Oarlocks: Similar because it names the exact same device — the fitting that holds and pivots an oar on a boat's gunwale; differs because oarlock is the standard American English term, while rowlock is British/Commonwealth usage, and oarlock is more transparently compositional ("oar" + "lock").
  • Tholes / thole-pins: Similar because both are gunwale fittings that support the oar as a pivot; differs because a thole is a single vertical wooden peg (or pair) against which the oar pivots, while a rowlock is a U-shaped or two-sided "lock" fitting that surrounds and secures the shaft.
  • Rowing gate: Similar because a modern gate is the swivelling rowlock on a racing boat's outrigger, holding the oar; differs because gate is sport-rowing jargon for the modern spring-loaded device, while rowlock names the general class of fittings.
  • Pivot / fulcrum: Similar because the rowlock functions as the mechanical pivot or fulcrum of the oar; differs because pivot and fulcrum are abstract mechanical terms naming the point of rotation, not the physical hardware on a boat.
  • Swivel: Similar because a swivel rowlock rotates to follow the oar blade's angle; differs because swivel emphasizes the rotational mechanism rather than the oar-holding function, and is used of any rotating joint.
  • Gunwale: Similar because the rowlock is mounted on the gunwale; differs because the gunwale is the top rail of the boat itself, not the fitting that holds the oar.
