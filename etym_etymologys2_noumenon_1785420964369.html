PIE *gneh3- "to know, to come to know, to recognize" (zero-grade *gnh3-; the source of Greek gignosko "to know," Latin noscere "to know," Sanskrit jna- "to know," English know) [reconstructed; c. 4th-3rd millennium BCE] > Greek noos / nous (νόος / νοῦς) "mind, intellect, thought" — a nominal stem traditionally connected with the knowing root, though the derivation is DEBATED (Beekes treats noos as of uncertain origin; an older alternative links it to PIE *nes- "to return to oneself, come to oneself") [attested from Homer, c. 8th century BCE] > Greek noein (νοεῖν) "to perceive with the mind, to think, to apprehend by intellectual intuition" [attested from the 6th century BCE onward] > Greek nooumenon (νοούμενον), neuter present passive participle of noein: "a thing being thought of, an object of intellectual apprehension" [attested in Plato and later authors] > German Noumenon — coined by Immanuel Kant in the Kritik der reinen Vernunft, first edition 1781, to denote the thing-in-itself as opposed to the phenomenon [1781] > English noumenon "the thing as it is in itself, knowable only by the intellect" [first recorded c. 1796] Note on the debated stage: the derivation of nous from *gneh3- is traditional but not secure. If nous belongs instead to *nes-, the "knowing" root enters the chain only indirectly, and the honest beginning is simply the attested Greek noun nous "mind" itself. Kant's coinage, however, is fully attested and dated, and the later part of the chain is not in doubt.
