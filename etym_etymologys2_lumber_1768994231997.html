ROOT WORD — lumber (timber) The word is a loan-derivation from a tribal ethnonym — it does NOT descend from a PIE root meaning "wood." Its chain runs: 1. PIE *dl̥n̥gʰ- "long" (c. 4000 BCE) — the root of "long," preserved in Latin longus, Greek dolikhós, Sanskrit dīrghá- (Hindi dīrgh "long, prolonged"). 2. Proto-Germanic *langaz "long" (c. 200 CE) — the first element of the tribal compound. 3. PIE *bʰardʰ- "beard" (c. 4000 BCE) — the root of "beard," preserved in Latin barba, Balto-Slavic (Russian boroda), and Germanic *bardaz. 4. Proto-Germanic *bardaz "beard" (c. 200 CE) — the second element. 5. Proto-Germanic *Langabardaz "Long-beard, Langobard" (compound: *langaz "long" + *bardaz "beard"; the tribal name attested from the 1st c. CE in Roman sources as Langobardi). 6. Latin Langobardus / Longobardus "a Langobard, a Lombard" (Roman and medieval Latin) — the Germanic people who migrated into Italy in the 6th c., giving their name to Lombardy. 7. Medieval Italian Lombardo "a Lombard; a man of Lombardy" — and by extension, because the Lombards were the great moneylenders, bankers, and pawnbrokers of medieval Europe, "a pawnbroker, a money-changer" (attested in the Italian banking cities; London's Lombard Street is the banking street named for them). 8. Middle English lomber / lumber "disused household goods, pawned clutter, stored junk" (1550s) — what a Lombard pawnbroker stored; a "lumber room" was the attic or cellar for such cast-offs; to "lumber" a place was to fill it with clutter. 9. American English lumber "roughly sawed timber" (1660s, New England) — the bulky stored "clutter" of the New World storehouse transferred to the building material itself, the raw wood that filled colonial wharves and yards. 10. English LUMBER "timber sawed into boards, planks, or beams" — the form and sense on this card. NOTE ON THE SEPARATE VERB: The verb "to lumber along" (to move heavily and clumsily) is a DIFFERENT word — probably from the "clutter/mass" sense or possibly from Scandinavian (Norwegian dialect lomre "to move heavily") — and must not be merged with the timber word in the derivation chain. The tribe-name etymology for the timber sense, though surprising, is the standard account (OED, etymonline); the two roots of the compound (long, beard) each have full separate Indo-European histories given above in steps 1–4.
