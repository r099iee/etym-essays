• Tint (vs. tinged): Similar because both name a slight colouring; differs because a tint is a lightened, pastel amount of a colour, while tinged implies the dyeing process itself and can carry moral or emotional colouring.
  • Shade (vs. tinged): Similar because both name a small admixture of colour; differs because shade darkens toward black and is a discrete degree of a hue, while tinged describes the act of staining and the diffused result.
  • Movement (vs. stirring): Similar because both name motion; differs because movement is general and flat, while stirring names the very first, faintest motion — an awakening out of stasis that also reaches into feeling ("a stirring of hope").
  • Tremor (vs. stirring): Similar because both name a small, early motion; differs because tremor is involuntary and physical, often of the body or the earth, while stirring is a beginning of movement that can be of air, feeling, or thought.
  • Invisible (vs. imperceptible): Similar because both lie beyond perception; differs because invisible concerns only sight, while imperceptible is perceptual and gradual across all the senses — a change too fine to grasp, not merely unseen.
  • Inaudible (vs. imperceptible): Similar because both name what cannot be detected; differs because inaudible is confined to hearing, while imperceptible covers every sense channel at once.
  • Subtle (vs. imperceptible): Similar because both name something fine and hard to detect; differs because subtle implies craft, delicacy, or indirectness in a positively appreciated way, while imperceptible is a neutral statement of being beyond the senses.
  • Breeze (vs. draft): Similar because both are flows of air; differs because a breeze is larger, outdoor, and usually pleasant, while a draft is a narrow, sheltered, often unwelcome current — the cold seam of air one feels in a warm room.
  • Current (vs. draft): Similar because both name a steady flow; differs because current is most at home in water or electricity, while draft is the specific word for air drawn along a confined path.
  • Wind (vs. draft): Similar because both name moving air; differs because wind is broad, natural, and often violent, while a draft is small, artificial, and directional — a current in a room rather than a weather event.
