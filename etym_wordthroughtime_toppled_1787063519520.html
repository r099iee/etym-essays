- 1570s–1600s: [Physical Tumble] - Emerged in Early Modern English describing persons or objects falling top-foremost or tumbling head over heels in chaotic motion.
- 1600s–1750s: [Structural Failure] - Elevated by Jacobean and Augustan literature to describe high towers, chimneys, and castle ramparts collapsing in violent storms.
- 1750s–1900s: [Political Overthrow] - Widely adopted during the democratic revolutions of Europe and America to describe the deposing of monarchies, tyrannical regimes, and ministries.
- 1900s–Present: [Global Polysemy] - Pervasive across political journalism, corporate finance (collapsing conglomerates), and sports commentary (defeating dominant champions).
