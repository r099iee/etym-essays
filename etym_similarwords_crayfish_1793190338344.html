• Lobsters: Similar because both are large-clawed armoured decapod crustaceans with prized tail meat; differs because lobsters are marine giants of cold seas, while crayfish are their small freshwater cousins of stream and lake.
  • Crabs: Similar because both are familiar clawed crustaceans caught for the pot; differs because crabs walk sideways with a broad flattened shell and folded tail, while crayfish keep the long lobster body and swim with tail flips.
  • Prawns: Similar because both are edible jointed crustaceans boiled pink for eating; differs because prawns are slender marine swimmers without great pincers, while crayfish carry heavy front claws.
  • Shrimps: Similar because both are small edible shellfish of boil and bisque; differs because shrimps are tiny saltwater delicacies, while a crayfish is a hand-sized armoured animal with claws.
  • Yabbies: Similar because both are freshwater crayfish seined from inland waters; differs because yabby is the Australian name for southern-hemisphere species, while crayfish is the global standard.
  • Crawdads: Similar because both name the identical animal; differs only in register — crawdad is the affectionate American creek-side folk word, crayfish the formal and scientific one.
  • Krill: Similar because both are crustaceans harvested as food; differs because krill are tiny planktonic swarms feeding whales, while crayfish are solitary bottom-dwelling claw-bearers.
  • Woodlice: Similar because both are armoured jointed arthropods that hide under stones; differs because woodlice are small terrestrial isopods of the garden, while crayfish are substantial aquatic decapods.
