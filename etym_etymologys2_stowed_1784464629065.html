STOWED is the past participle of the native English verb stow, a compound of two elements; the linear chains follow in isolation. Chain 1 — the base stow: (origin of Proto-Germanic *stōwō 'place' uncertain; no secure PIE etymon — see note) → Proto-Germanic *stōwō 'a place, standing-place' (attested through reflexes: Old Saxon stō; the noun survives in English place-names and in the archaic stow 'a place') → Old English stōw 'a place' (noun, attested, esp. in place-names like Stow-on-the-Wold) → Old English stōwian 'to place, put' (verb, attested) → Middle English stowen 'to place, put, arrange' (c. 1300) → English stow 'to pack or put away into a designated space' (nautical sense of arranging cargo and sails prominent from the 16th c.) → base element of the inflected form. Chain 2 — the past-participle suffix -ed: PIE *-tós (suffix forming verbal adjectives, 'having been …-ed'; cf. Latin amātus 'loved', Greek philētós 'loved') → Proto-Germanic *-þaz/-daz (past-participle suffix of weak verbs; cf. Gothic nasids 'saved') → Old English -od / -ed (past-participle of weak verbs) → Middle English -ed / -id → modern English -ed → fused with stow → STOWED (card form). Note on the debate: the ultimate origin of Germanic *stōwō 'place' is uncertain. Some scholars connect it to PIE *steh₂- 'to stand' (a 'standing-place', like stead), but the connection is not secure and no attested intermediate stages exist, so the honest record begins at the Germanic stage. The verb's nautical specialisation is an English and continental Germanic maritime development, not part of any ancient meaning.
