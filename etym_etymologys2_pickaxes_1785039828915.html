ROOT 1 — pick (the "picking" element; note that the true source is NOT the English verb "to pick"): Origin: Latin pīcus "woodpecker, 'the pecker'" (imitative; the bird is named for its pecking beak; no reliably established PIE ancestor) → Medieval Latin picōsa "pick, pointed digging tool" → Old French pocois / picois "pointed digging tool, mattock" (11th c.) → Anglo-French piceis "pickaxe" → Middle English pikas / pikeis "digging tool" (mid-13th c.) → Early 15th c. folk etymology: reanalysed as "pick + axe" → Modern English pickaxe (n.) "one pointed end, one broad blade" → PICKAXES (plural) ROOT 2 — axe (the folk-etymological graft): Earliest root: PIE *h₂eǵ- / *agʷesi- "axe" (reconstructed) → Proto-Germanic *akusjō "axe" → Old English æx "axe, pickaxe, hatchet" → Middle English ax / axe → Modern English axe (grafted onto "pick" by folk etymology in the 15th c.) Debated / nuanced note: The folk etymology is historically false but morphologically crucial. The word's second syllable is not the native English "axe": it is a re-interpretation of the Old French ending -ois, complete by the early 15th century, which reshaped pikas into pick-axe and later enabled the back-formed verb "to pickaxe." A parallel native verb fed the modern sense of "pick": Old English *pician "to prick" blended with Old Norse pikka "to peck".
