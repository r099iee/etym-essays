- Old English (gielpan) - primarily 'to boast, to exult': a cry of pride and self-assertion, condemned as vainglory in Anglo-Saxon homilies.
- 13th c. (Middle English yelpen) - 'to cry out, to shout', still with the boast-sense attached; both faces of loud vocal self-assertion.
- 14th c. - applied to hounds: to give tongue, to cry shrilly; the animal sense begins to take over.
- 15th c. - narrows decisively to the short, sharp cry of a dog in pain or sudden excitement; the human sense fades.
- 17th c.-modern - the boasting sense vanishes entirely; yelp becomes exclusively the brief, high-pitched, involuntary outcry of pain or fright, slightly childish and animal in tone, with the figurative 'yelp in protest' for a small sharp complaint.
