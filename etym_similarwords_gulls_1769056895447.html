• seagull: Similar because it names the same grey-and-white seabirds of family Laridae; differs because "seagull" is the colloquial, everyday word while plain "gull" is the technical and literary one, and naturalists object that gulls also live inland.
  • tern: Similar because it is a small, slim seabird of the same coastal world; differs because a tern is a different genus (Sternidae) — smaller, with forked tail and graceful, darting flight — while gulls are bulkier, blunt-winged, and far more omnivorous.
  • cormorant: Similar because it is another coastal seabird of the rocks; differs because the cormorant is a dark, diving bird (Phalacrocoracidae) that swims underwater for fish, whereas gulls are pale, surface-feeding and rarely dive.
  • petrel: Similar because it is an ocean-going seabird; differs because petrels (and shearwaters) are small, dark, far-ocean birds that patter over the waves, while gulls are larger, paler coastal birds that nest and forage on shore.
  • guillemot: Similar because it is a cliff-nesting seabird whose eggs are gathered from ledges; differs because the guillemot is an auk (Alcidae), black-and-white and upright, that lays one pointed egg on bare rock, whereas gulls build nests and are grey-and-white with a raucous cry.
  • gull (plain plural): Similar because it is the very same noun, "gull" without the possessive; differs because plain "gulls" just names the birds, while "gulls'" adds the genitive meaning "belonging to gulls" — the apostrophe is the whole grammatical point.
  • to gull / gullible: Similar because it is the identical-sounding word in popular speech; differs because it is a separate etymon meaning "to trick, dupe", and "gullible" ("easily deceived") derives from that verb, not from the seabird — a homograph, not a sense of this word.
  • geese's / birds': Similar because they show the same plural-possessive pattern (apostrophe after a plural -s or -es); differs because those are different root words, while "gulls'" is specifically the genitive plural of "gull" with the whole seabird vocabulary attached.
