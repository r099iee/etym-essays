• Almighty: Similar because both mean possessing total and unlimited power; differs because almighty is a native Germanic compound (all + mighty) carrying an intimate, direct devotional warmth in religious worship, whereas omnipotent is a formal, Latinate philosophical term used in scholastic theology and analytical discourse.
  • All-powerful: Similar because it is the literal Germanic calque of omnipotent; differs because all-powerful is a plain, everyday descriptive compound, lacking the elevated scholastic and poetic resonance of omnipotent.
  • Supreme: Similar because both indicate the highest degree of power; differs because supreme denotes holding the highest rank, position, or authority in a hierarchy, rather than literally possessing infinite causal power.
  • Sovereign: Similar because both denote unconstrained rule; differs because sovereign is primarily a political and legal term denoting supreme, independent authority over a territory or state, without implying metaphysical omnipotence.
  • Invincible: Similar because both suggest power that cannot be defeated; differs because invincible refers to being incapable of being conquered or overcome in battle or contest, which is a defensive capability rather than boundless creative agency.
  • Puissant: Similar because both denote great power; differs because puissant is an archaic, literary Romance borrowing emphasizing majestic, impressive might and martial vigor, without denoting infinite power.
  • Insuperable: Similar because both involve overcoming resistance; differs because insuperable refers to obstacles, difficulties, or barriers that cannot be surmounted.
