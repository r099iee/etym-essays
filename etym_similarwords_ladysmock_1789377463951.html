• Cuckooflower: Similar because it is the primary common synonym for the exact same botanical species (*Cardamine pratensis*); differs because cuckooflower highlights the phenological arrival and song of the migratory cuckoo bird in mid-spring, whereas ladysmock emphasizes the visual resemblance to bleached linen smocks and the Marian religious association with Lady Day.
  • Bittercress: Similar because both belong to the exact same botanical genus (*Cardamine*) in the mustard family and share a pungent, mustard-oil flavor profile; differs because bittercress is the broader taxonomic and horticultural common name encompassing many weedy, smaller, and less ornamental species (such as hairy bittercress, *Cardamine hirsuta*), whereas ladysmock specifically denotes the showy, large-flowered meadow species *Cardamine pratensis*.
  • Watercress: Similar because both are edible, peppery, moisture-loving crucifers (*Brassicaceae*) traditionally harvested in spring from wet habitats for salads and tonics; differs because watercress (*Nasturtium officinale*) grows submerged or floating in running shallow streams with rounded dark-green leaves and tiny white flowers, whereas ladysmock is an upright meadow plant with conspicuous lilac-to-white petals.
  • Meadowfoam: Similar because both are delicate white-flowered annual or perennial plants that carpet moist spring meadows with an illusion of foamy sheets; differs because meadowfoam (*Limnanthes douglasii*) belongs to the Limnanthaceae family, is native to western North America, and features yellow-centered cup flowers, whereas ladysmock is a native Eurasian crucifer with cross-shaped petals.
  • Milkmaids: Similar because it serves as an authentic English country dialectal synonym for *Cardamine pratensis*; differs because milkmaids is an informal, localized colloquialism referring to the rural lasses who gathered the flowers while driving dairy herds to spring pasture, lacking the literary and religious weight of ladysmock.
  • Columbine: Similar because both are beloved historical cottage garden flowers mentioned alongside each other in pastoral literature (such as Oscar Wilde's tale); differs because columbine (*Aquilegia*) belongs to the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae) and features complex, spurred, nodding blossoms resembling doves, whereas ladysmock is a simple four-petaled crucifer.
