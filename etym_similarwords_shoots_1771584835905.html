• Sprout: Similar because both name new young growth from a seed, bud, or root; differs because sprout stresses the act of emerging and carries a humble, edible connotation (bean sprouts, brussels sprouts), while shoot stresses the upward, outward “shooting” of the stem and is the more elegant, poetic word used in the novel.
  • Twig: Similar because both are small stems of a plant; differs because a twig is the older, hardened, woody small branch of the second year onward — structural and stiff — while a shoot is fresh, green, still growing, and still flexible.
  • Tendril: Similar because both are young slender growths; differs because a tendril is a specialised climbing organ that grasps a support by coiling around it, while a shoot does not coil or cling — it simply extends outward and upward.
  • Bud: Similar because both belong to the earliest stage of plant growth; differs because a bud is the embryonic swelling, a closed packet of developing tissue, from which the shoot emerges, not the growing stem itself.
  • Scion: Similar because both are young shoots of a plant; differs because a scion is specifically a shoot or slip cut for grafting onto a rootstock — a horticultural tool-name — while a shoot is the natural growth left to grow.
  • Sucker: Similar because both are new growths arising from the plant; differs because a sucker springs from the root or lower stem and is often unwanted, sapping the parent, while a shoot is the desired fresh growth.
  • Runner: Similar because both are new growths that extend the plant; differs because a runner (stolon) creeps horizontally along the ground and roots at its nodes to form new plants, while a shoot grows upward in the air.
  • Stem: Similar because both are parts of a plant's axis; differs because stem names the whole supporting axis of the plant, mature or young, while shoot names only the newest, actively growing portion.
  • Cutting: Similar because both are pieces of a plant used for growth; differs because a cutting is a detached piece of stem or leaf planted to take root — a propagation term — while a shoot is attached growth, not yet detached.
