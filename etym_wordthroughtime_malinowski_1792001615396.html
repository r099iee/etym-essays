- 1884–1914 — Born into the Polish intellectual aristocracy in Kraków; earned a doctoral degree in physics and mathematics before reading Frazer and moving to the London School of Economics to study anthropology.
- 1915–1918 — Conducted his historic solitary fieldwork in the Trobriand Islands of Melanesia during World War I, pioneering the modern method of participant observation.
- 1922 — Published Argonauts of the Western Pacific, which transformed European anthropology overnight and established the gold standard for ethnographic monographs.
- 1920s–1930s — Held the first Chair of Social Anthropology at the London School of Economics, training a brilliant generation of scholars and formulating functionalist theory.
- 1942 — Died suddenly of a heart attack in New Haven, Connecticut while serving as professor of anthropology at Yale University, widely mourned as the undisputed master of field ethnography.
- 1967 — The posthumous publication of his private field diary (A Diary in the Strict Sense of the Term) ignited a massive disciplinary revolution, giving birth to modern reflexivity and post-colonial self-critique in social science.
- 21st c. — Celebrated globally as an eponymous intellectual titan whose surname represents the pinnacle of long-term ethnographic field immersion.
