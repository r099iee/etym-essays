Note: only the Old English onward stages are securely attested. The Proto-Germanic *kaukō and the Indo-European *geu- are reconstructed and debated; the link between ācēocian and cēoce "cheek, jaw" (the image of something caught in the jaw or throat) is widely accepted, but some scholars treat choke as an isolated Germanic formation without a convincing Indo-European root.
