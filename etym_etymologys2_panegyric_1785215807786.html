The word is a compound built from Greek elements; the full chain is given, followed by separate chains for its component morphemes. Chain 1 — the full word panēgyrikós → English panegyric: - PIE *h₂ger- “to gather, to collect” (attested root; also reconstructed as *ger-) → Greek ageírō “to gather together, to collect, to assemble” (attested in Homer) → Greek ágyris / agorá “assembly, gathering” (from ageírō) → Greek panēgyris “a general assembly, a festival gathering of the whole people” (pan- “all” + ágyris “gathering”; attested from the classical period) → Greek panēgyrikós “of a festival assembly, festive; fit for a festival” (adjective in -ikós; the phrase panēgyrikós lógos “a festival speech” is the technical term of the genre) → Latin panēgyricus “a public eulogy, a panegyric” (from the Greek; attested in Roman imperial rhetoric, 1st–4th centuries CE) → English panegyric (noun, 17th century, via Latin; first recorded c. 1603) “a formal public speech or written composition in high praise” → Modern English PANEGYRIC. Chain 2 — the element pan- “all”: - Greek pâs / pân “all, every” (neuter pân) — of uncertain or debated Indo-European derivation; the traditional reconstruction is PIE *pant- / *ph₂nt-, sometimes connected with the notion of totality, but no secure PIE etymon is universally accepted → Greek pan- (combining form) “all-,” as in panēgyris and hence in panegyric. Chain 3 — the suffix -ic: - PIE *-ko- (adjectival suffix) → Greek -ikós (adjectival suffix) → Latin -icus → English -ic, converting panēgyris into the adjective panēgyrikós, later substantivised as a noun. Note on debate: the composition pan- + agyr- (the non-contracted root of agorá “assembly”) is securely attested in Greek, but the PIE root of ageírō “to gather” is debated between *h₂ger- and a laryngeal-less *ger-; the Germanic cognates of English “gather” belong to a different root (*gad-) and are irrelevant here. The Latin and English stages are undisputed. The Greek suffix -ikós is a very common adjectival ending whose PIE shape is only generally reconstructable.
