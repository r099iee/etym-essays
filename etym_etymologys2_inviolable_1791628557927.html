Note: Derives from Latin inviolabilis, formed by combining the negative prefix in- ("not") with the denominative verb violare ("to injure, dishonor, violate", historically built upon the noun vis, "force, bodily violence") and the passive verbal suffix -bilis ("capable of"). The word entered English during the 15th-century Lancastrian renaissance via Anglo-Norman administrative and theological translations.
