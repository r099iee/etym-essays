First element — snuff, the powdered tobacco:
Middle Dutch (13th c.)
snuffen
‘to sniff, snuffle, snort’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
snuffen
‘to sniff, inhale through the nose’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
snuff
‘powdered tobacco for inhaling’
↓
Second element — box, the container:
Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE)
πύξος (pyxos)
‘boxwood, the box-tree’
↓
Latin (1st c.)
buxis
‘boxwood; a small box’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
box
‘a wooden box, container’
↓
Modern English (1680s, compound)
snuffbox
‘small box for powdered tobacco’

Note: the deeper origin of Middle Dutch snuffen beyond the medieval period is imitative and uncertain, and no PIE root is asserted for it here; the box chain above follows the standard pyxos to buxis to box derivation.
