- 1200s–1300s - [Old Norse Loan & Physical Yawning/Gaping] - Borrowed from Old Norse *geispa*, originally meaning to yawn or gape the mouth open in exhaustion.
- 1400s–1500s - [Suffocation & Dying Breaths] - Evolved in Middle and Early Modern English to describe catching breath in acute suffocation, drowning, and the final moments of dying ('last gasp').
- 1600s–1700s - [Emotional Shock & Theatrical Reflex] - Solidified across English drama as the involuntary intake of breath caused by sudden horror, terror, or astounding revelations.
- 1800s–Present - [Narrative Inquit Tag & Athletic Exhaustion] - Ubiquitous in modern storytelling as a dynamic dialogue tag depicting characters speaking while fighting for breath.
