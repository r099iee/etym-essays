- [Late 14th–15th c.] - [Nautical & Mechanical Origin] Used primarily by English mariners and shipwrights as tought/towght to denote rigging, cables, sails, and ropes drawn completely tight and firm against the wind.
- [16th–17th c.] - [Physical & Anatomical Extension] Expanded from maritime jargon into general physical descriptions of stretched membranes, drumheads, bowstrings, tendons, and muscles held in firm isometric tension.
- [18th–19th c.] - [Psychological & Atmospheric Shift] Adopted by novelists and poets to describe intense nervous strain, hyper-vigilant emotional states, and charged, uneasy social atmospheres.
- [20th–21st c.] - [Critical, Aesthetic & Technical Senses] Widely adopted in literary, cinematic, and journalistic criticism to praise lean, disciplined, unbloated narratives, as well as in engineering and fitness to describe optimal structural tensile strength and athletic muscle tone.
