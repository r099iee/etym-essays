- Old English helm - a helmet; also 'protector', applied poetically to kings and warriors who 'cover' their people
- Middle English helm(e) - still the everyday word for head armour in war and tourney
- 14th c. - the nautical helm (tiller and steering gear) becomes a firmly separate, technical sense
- 17th c. - 'helmet' (the Old French diminutive) takes over ordinary speech; helm recedes to poetry and nautical jargon
- 19th c. - 'at the helm' and 'take the helm' harden into fixed leadership metaphors
- Today - three parallel lives: an archaic literary 'helmet', the ship's steering gear, and a universal figure for command
