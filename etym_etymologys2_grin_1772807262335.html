PIE (uncertain)
*gʰer-
"to gape, yawn"
↓
Proto-Germanic (1st millennium BCE)
*grenaną
"to grimace, grin"
↓
Old English (c. 1000)
grinnian
"to grin, snarl, bare the teeth"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
grennen
"to grin, snarl, show the teeth"
↓
English (16th c.)
grin
"a broad smile that reveals the teeth"

Debate: the sense shifted from "snarl, bare the teeth" to "smile"; the PIE link *gʰer- "to gape, yawn" is uncertain — grinnian is also related to gurn and grunt.
