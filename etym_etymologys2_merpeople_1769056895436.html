MERPEOPLE (compound of mer + people)

Element 1 — mer:
↓ PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*mori-
"sea"
↓ Latin (1st c. BCE)
mare
"the sea"
↓ English (combining form, 19th c.)
mer-
"sea"

Element 2 — people:
↓ Latin (1st c. BCE)
populus
"a people"
↓ Old French
peuple / pople
"people"
↓ Middle English (13th c.)
peple / people
"people"
↓ English
people
"people"

Compound:
↓ English (19th c.)
mer-people
"mythical sea people"
↓ English
merpeople
"mythical beings, half-human
and half-fish, who live in the sea"

Note: mer- is from Latin mare "sea", from PIE *mori- "sea" (also seen in mermaid). Latin populus has no certain Indo-European etymology and may be an Italic or Etruscan word, so the chain for people begins at Latin.
