• Negotiation: Similar because both denote discussions to settle a dispute; differs because negotiation is the modern formal word for any bargaining over terms, while parley is specifically military (enemies under truce) and archaic in feel.
  • Conference: Similar because both are formal meetings for discussion; differs because a conference can be any official meeting (scientific, business), while a parley presupposes opposing sides — usually armed — meeting to talk.
  • Council: Similar because both are deliberative meetings; differs because a council is often one side's own assembly of advisers (a war council), while a parley takes place between the sides.
  • Discussion: Similar because both mean an exchange of views; differs because discussion is the everyday neutral word for any talk, while parley keeps the flavour of enemies and truce.
  • Talks / peace talks: Similar because both mean conversations aimed at agreement; differs because "talks" is the modern diplomatic cliché (summit talks, arms talks) and carries no archaic colour, while parley is specific and antique.
  • Dialogue: Similar because both denote two-way exchange of speech; differs because dialogue is used of philosophy, diplomacy and drama — any structured conversation — while parley is confrontational and negotiated.
  • Colloquy: Similar because both are formal words for conversation; differs because colloquy is an elevated literary or ecclesiastical term for any formal talk, without the military meaning.
  • Confab: Similar because both are words for a discussion; differs because confab is casual and friendly (a gossip), the opposite of parley's guarded confrontation.
  • Powwow: Similar because both can mean a meeting to talk things over; differs because powwow (from an Algonquian ceremonial word) is informal and jocular, while parley is formal and historical.
  • Summit: Similar because both are high-level meetings between rivals; differs because a summit is the modern state-level conference of leaders, while a parley is the older, more local and more precarious encounter at the battle-line.
  • Deliberation: Similar because both involve weighing matters before deciding; differs because deliberation is the internal or solitary process of weighing (a jury deliberates), while parley is spoken exchange between parties.
  • Mediation / arbitration: Similar because both are structured dispute processes; differs because they involve third parties who broker or judge, while a parley is direct talk between the principals.
  • Truce / armistice: Similar because both belong to the vocabulary of suspending war; differs because a truce is the suspension of hostilities itself, while a parley is the talk that may arrange it.
  • Interview (older sense): Similar because in 16th-century English "interview" (from French entrevue) could mean a formal meeting of sovereigns or enemy leaders — almost a synonym of parley; differs because the modern interview is questioning for a job or a news report.
