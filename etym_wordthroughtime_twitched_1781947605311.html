- To pluck or pull sharply - In Middle English (from twicchen) twitched first meant to pluck or snatch at something, an active pulling motion.
- Narrowing to involuntary motion - By the 18th-19th centuries the verb narrows to the small, quick, involuntary convulsive movements of body parts — a tail, an eye, a lip — losing the deliberate plucking sense.
- Modern tell-tale device - Today twitched is a stock narrative verb for a single betraying flicker of concealed emotion, prized for animating a frozen scene with one live motion.
