- Set free / freed from restraint - In its early English life, derived from the notion of a freed slave or one set loose, applied to 16th–17th-century freethinkers who rejected religious dogma; the tone was intellectual and mildly provocative.
- 17th–18th century moral decay - Shifted from “free-thinker” to a man of dissolute, immoral habits, especially in sexual and social conduct, gaining a distinctly reproachful and condemnatory register.
- Contemporary Sense - Today it denotes a person who behaves without moral principles or responsibility, carrying an uncompromisingly dark, judgmental weight rather than any admiration.
