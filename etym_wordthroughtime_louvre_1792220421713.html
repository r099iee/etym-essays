- Late 12th c. (c. 1190) — Attested in Medieval Latin charters as Lupara / Lupera, the fortified military castle built by King Philip Augustus on the banks of the Seine to guard Paris against Anglo-Norman attacks.
- 14th c. — Mentioned in Old French chronicles as le Louvre; converted by Charles V the Wise into an ornate Gothic royal residence housing the first royal library.
- 16th c. — Demolished and rebuilt as a Renaissance classical palace under Francis I and architect Pierre Lescot, becoming the primary Paris residence of the Valois monarchs.
- 17th c. — Vastly expanded by Henry IV (the Grande Galerie) and Louis XIV (Perrault's Colonnade), before the royal court relocated to Versailles in 1682, leaving the palace to royal academies and artists.
- 1793 — The French Revolutionary National Convention opens the Muséum central des arts within the Louvre, pioneering the modern democratic concept of the universal public art museum.
- 19th c. — Expanded by Napoleon Bonaparte (renamed temporarily the Musée Napoléon) and Napoleon III; canonized across international literature as the preeminent temple of European painting.
- Late 20th–21st c. — Modernized via François Mitterrand's "Grand Louvre" project and I. M. Pei's iconic glass pyramid; established as a global cultural brand with international branches (Louvre Abu Dhabi).
