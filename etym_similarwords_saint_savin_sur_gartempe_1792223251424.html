• Abbey Church of Sainte-Foy (Conques): Similar because both are premier French Romanesque ecclesiastical monuments and UNESCO World Heritage sites dating from the 11th century; differs because Conques is famous worldwide for its sculpted stone Last Judgment tympanum and golden reliquary treasury along the pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela, whereas Saint-Savin is celebrated for its painted interior nave vault murals.
  • Basilica of Saint-Sernin (Toulouse): Similar because both are monumental Romanesque churches in southwestern France featuring barrel-vaulted naves and Benedictine monastic roots; differs because Saint-Sernin is a colossal brick pilgrimage basilica with extensive double aisles and radiating chapels, whereas Saint-Savin is a hall church (halle) designed specifically to maximize continuous painted plaster surfaces.
  • Abbey of Saint-Germain (Auxerre): Similar because both preserve extraordinary, rare French medieval wall frescoes; differs because the frescoes at Auxerre date from the Carolingian period (9th century) and are situated inside subterranean crypts, whereas Saint-Savin features High Romanesque murals prominently displayed across the expansive upper nave.
  • Vézelay Abbey (Basilica of Saint Mary Magdalene): Similar because both are iconic Burgundian/Poitevin Romanesque monastic sites of the 11th and 12th centuries; differs because Vézelay is world-renowned for its visionary sculpted stone capitals and majestic narthex tympanum depicting the Pentecost, whereas Saint-Savin's glory lies in its graphic, painted vault cycles.
  • Brinay (Church of Saint-Aignan): Similar because both are French churches possessing remarkable 12th-century Romanesque wall paintings depicting biblical scenes; differs because Brinay is a modest rural parish church with frescoes confined to its small choir, while Saint-Savin is a monumental abbey of European architectural stature.
