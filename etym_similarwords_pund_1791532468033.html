• pound: The direct Modern English descendant and the ordinary word for the same weight and currency; differs only in spelling and pronunciation, and in having acquired a much wider range of technical compounds.
  • lb: The abbreviation of Latin libra, used for the same weight; differs in being a written symbol rather than a spoken noun, and in pointing to the Roman scale rather than to pondus.
  • libra: The Latin name for the Roman pound and the source of the £ sign; similar as a weight-and-money unit, but it belongs to Roman metrology, not to English usage.
  • quid: British slang for one pound sterling; similar in denoting the currency, but markedly informal and uninflected in the plural ("five quid").
  • sterling: The adjective and noun naming the British currency system; similar in referring to the same money, but it names the standard of fineness rather than the unit itself.
  • punt: The former Irish pound; the same unit and ultimately the same Latin inheritance through English, but tied to the Irish state and now replaced by the euro.
  • livre: The French pound, from Latin libra; a parallel development of the same Roman inheritance, but historically French and now obsolete.
  • peso: A Spanish-derived currency whose name means "weight" (Latin pensum); similar because it too turns a weight word into money, but it belongs to the Iberian and American tradition.
  • mark: A medieval European unit of weight and account; similar as a weighed monetary standard, but it was usually two-thirds of a pound and is now purely historical.
  • talent: An ancient Greek and Roman unit of weight and money; similar in fusing the two senses, but far larger and confined to antiquity.
  • stone: A British unit of weight equal to fourteen pounds; similar as a customary body-weight measure, but it is a multiple of the pound rather than a synonym.
  • ounce: The subdivision of the pound; similar in belonging to the same weight system, but it names the smaller unit (Latin uncia, "a twelfth").
