- Late 16th century - Appears in the 1580s, of obscure origin, for hauling oneself along or over ground on hands and feet - ungraceful, urgent, all-fours climbing.
- 17th-19th centuries - The word broadens fast: scrambled eggs (the eggs clambered together in the pan), and by the 19th century a 'scramble' is any disorderly competitive rush - the scramble for Africa, soldiers scrambling for cover.
- 20th century - Gains a technical sense: to scramble a signal or a message is to jumble it beyond recognition; the word now runs from clumsy climbing to frantic competition to coded secrecy, always keeping its image of disorderly haste.
