Note: Formed from Latin in- (assimilated to il- before the liquid /l/, functioning as an intensive or locative prefix, ‘in, upon’) + lustrāre (‘to shine light upon, purify through light’), which derives from lustrum (‘purification sacrifice, ceremonial cleansing performed by Roman censors’), from PIE *lewk- (‘light, brightness’). In classical Latin, illūstris shifted naturally from literal visual illumination (‘lit up, bright’) to epistemological clarity (‘evident, plain’) and social prominence (‘famous, distinguished, noble’). In the late Roman Empire under Diocletian and Constantine, Illustris (or Vir Illustris) was codified as the highest official aristocratic rank for the highest state ministers and patricians, surpassing Spectabilis and Clarissimus.
