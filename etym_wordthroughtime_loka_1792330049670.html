- Vedic Antiquity (c. 1500–800 BCE) — Originally denoted any acoustic voice, loud shout, praise, or auditory report celebrating deities, with no requirement of fixed meter.
- Epic Genesis (c. 500–200 BCE) — Fixed in literary consciousness through the Rāmāyaṇa origin myth, where Vālmīki's sorrow (śoka) over a hunter slaying a krauñca bird crystallized into harmonious verse, inaugurating classical poetry (kāvya).
- Classical Sāstric Period (c. 200 BCE–1000 CE) — Became the standard national vehicle for law, medicine, philosophy, and astronomical mathematics, perceived as the most dignified and easily memorized linguistic form.
- Medieval Devotional Era (c. 1000–1800 CE) — Pervaded domestic religious life as the premier format for daily prayer, moral stanzas, and temple recitation across all linguistic regions of India.
- Modern Global Usage (19th c.–Present) — Adopted into global English dictionaries to designate classical Indian poetic verses, while remaining the ubiquitous term among millions of practitioners for Sanskrit devotional couplets.
