- 14th century - 'Trailen' enters Middle English from Old French, meaning to hang down so as to touch the ground or to drag along behind; a purely physical word for garments, branches, and ropes
- Later medieval to early modern - Extends into natural imagery: vines creeping along the ground, smoke and dust drifting in a long wake behind a moving body
- 18th-19th century - Migrates into competition and pursuit: a suspect being 'trailed,' a candidate or team 'trailing' in a race or election - falling behind in a drawn-out sense
- Dueling age onward - The idiom 'trailing one's coat' moves from a studied insult (dragging a cloak so a challenger steps on it) to a gentlemanly metaphor for deliberately provoking argument
- Modern - Flexible register: neutral and descriptive in the physical senses, informal-to-cultured and rakishly combative in the idiomatic use
