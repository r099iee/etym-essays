• Mithilā: Similar because both refer to the exact same ancient geographical and civilizational sphere in northern Bihar and southern Nepal; differs because Videha refers to the entire kingdom, territorial state, or ethno-political realm, whereas Mithilā specifies the metropolitan capital city and urban administrative center.
  • Kosala: Similar because both were prestigious ancient late Vedic and epic kingdoms situated in the fertile Gangetic basin; differs because Kosala was located to the west with its capital at Ayodhyā and was governed by the Ikṣvāku dynasty, whereas Videha lay to the east of the Sadānīrā River under the Janaka dynasty.
  • Magadha: Similar because both were prominent ancient kingdoms of eastern India that played decisive roles in late Vedic and early Buddhist history; differs because Magadha (south of the Ganges) evolved into a militaristic, centralized empire under the Mauryas, whereas Videha (north of the Ganges) remained an aristocratic kingdom that later transitioned into a republican confederacy (Vajji).
  • Kuru: Similar because both were influential Vedic kingdoms celebrated in sacred literature; differs because Kuru represented the conservative, ritualistic western core of the Vedic heartland along the Yamunā-Gaṅgā Doab, whereas Videha represented the innovative eastern frontier where Upanishadic philosophy blossomed.
  • Pañcāla: Similar because both were late Vedic monarchies where kings patronized intellectual debates between scholarly Brahmins; differs because Pañcāla was closely aligned with Kuru ritualism, whereas Videha boldly challenged orthodox sacrificial dogmas in favor of interiorized self-knowledge.
  • Aṅga: Similar because both were ancient eastern kingdoms bordering the Ganges; differs because Aṅga lay further southeast (around modern Bhagalpur and Munger) and was traditionally ruled by King Karṇa in the Mahābhārata, whereas Videha was located north of the river and ruled by Janaka.
  • Licchavi: Similar because both were prominent clans and regions within ancient northern Bihar that merged into the historic Vajji republican confederacy; differs because Licchavi was a distinct clan whose primary capital was at Vaiśālī, whereas Videha was the older Vedic realm centered at Mithilā.
  • Vṛji (Vajji): Similar because the historical territory of Videha formed an integral part of this confederation; differs because Vṛji refers to the entire confederation of eight republican clans, whereas Videha designates the specific historical kingdom and cultural territory within that league.
