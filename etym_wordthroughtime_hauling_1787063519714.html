- 14th c.: hauling enters English as the heavy, sustained pulling of ropes, nets, and weights — a working word of docks and labour.
- 16th–18th c.: the great age of sail makes hauling a defining nautical verb: hauling on sheets and halyards, hauling up boats, hauling the wind.
- 19th c.: industrialisation carries hauling ashore — horses, railways, and engines hauling barges, wagons, ore, and freight; haulage and hauler are coined.
- 20th–21st c.: hauling remains the everyday word for heavy pulling and big-load transport (haulage industry, trucking), with colloquial offshoots — “hauling in” money, “hauling off” wrongdoers — while the physical force of the original pull survives intact.
