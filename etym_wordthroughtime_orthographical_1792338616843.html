- [5th–4th c. BCE: Classical Greece] - coined as the Greek noun orthographía (from orthós 'straight, right' + gráphein 'to write'), used by philosophers, grammarians, and dramatists to signify correct handwriting, proper grammatical spelling, and right-angled architectural drawing - [perception: viewed as a mark of rigorous philosophical education, civic refinement, and mathematical mastery]
- [1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE: Roman Empire] - borrowed into Latin as orthographia by Cicero, Quintilian, and Suetonius, and applied by the architectural master Vitruvius to designate flat, perpendicular structural elevations - [perception: revered as the indispensable foundation of Latin literacy, oratorical prestige, and monumental engineering]
- [16th c.: Renaissance & Early Modern English] - adopted into Early Modern English as orthographie and adjectival orthographical during a period of acute orthographic chaos, when English spelling lacked standardized rules and printers spelled words idiosyncratically - [perception: championed by scholarly "orthoepists" and humanists who sought to bring order, logic, and classical dignity to the chaotic vernacular]
- [18th c.: Enlightenment & Dictionary Standardization] - codified through Samuel Johnson's 1755 dictionary and the rise of prescriptive grammar; spelling became a rigid social shibboleth and indicator of elite breeding - [perception: perceived as a moral and intellectual virtue, where orthographical deviation was condemned as vulgar ignorance or intellectual slovenliness]
- [19th c.: American Reforms & Technical Drawing] - popularized in the United States by Noah Webster’s patriotic spelling reforms (e.g., favor, defense) and simultaneously institutionalized in mechanical engineering as orthographic projection for blueprints - [perception: celebrated in America as democratic linguistic common sense, and in engineering as the objective language of industrial manufacture]
- [20th–21st c.: Modern Linguistics & Digital Age] - transformed into an objective, descriptive science in cognitive psychology (orthographical processing) and computational linguistics (orthographic depth, NLP tokenization), while everyday orthographical correctness is automated by digital spellcheck algorithms - [perception: viewed analytically by scientists as a visual decoding system, while in public life regarded as an essential standard maintained effortlessly by automated software]
