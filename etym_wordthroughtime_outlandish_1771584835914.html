- [Old English: belonging to the out-lands] - 'Outlandish' first means simply foreign, of a country beyond the border; connotation is neutral and geographical - the quality of an outlander, of the 'out-land' beyond the cultivated fields.
- [14th-17th century: the fall into barbarism] - The word darkens: foreign becomes alien, alien becomes uncouth and bizarre; 'outlandish' names speech, dress, and manners so strange they seem to come from beyond civilisation - connotation is mocking and condescending, the judgment of settled society on the outsider.
- [Contemporary sense] - Anything wildly unconventional, bizarre, or grotesque, no longer requiring actual foreignness; connotation is vivid and pejorative - strangeness that feels like trespass, as if the thing belonged to another world entirely.
