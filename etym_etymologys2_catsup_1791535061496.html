Southern Min (Hokkien) (17th c.)
鮭汁 (kê-tsiap)
"brine of fermented fish or shellfish"
↓
Malay (c. 1680)
kicap
"fermented fish sauce or soy sauce"
↓
Early Modern English (1690s)
catchup
"spiced East Indian table liquor"
↓
18th Century English (1730)
catsup
"mushroom, walnut, or fish sauce"
↓
Modern English (19th c.–pres.)
catsup
"thick seasoned tomato table condiment"

Note: Transcribed into English via multiple phonetic approximations (ketchup, catchup, catsup) from maritime Malay kicap, itself borrowed from Amoy/Hokkien Chinese kê-tsiap (鮭汁, meaning fish brine). The spelling catsup gained prominence in 18th-century English literature and became a major American commercial brand spelling.
