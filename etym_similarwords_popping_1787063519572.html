• Bulging: Similar because both describe swelling or protruding outward beyond normal boundaries; differs because bulging is a gradual, static, or continuous swelling (e.g., bulging pockets or muscles), whereas popping conveys a sudden, dynamic, almost explosive protrusion.
  • Protruding: Similar because both involve thrusting outward; differs because protruding is a formal, neutral anatomical or geometric description without acoustic, explosive, or emotional connotations.
  • Bursting: Similar because both involve rupturing from internal pressure; differs because bursting implies complete structural disintegration and forceful release of contents, whereas popping often involves a smaller, cleaner, localized release or sound.
  • Snapping: Similar because both produce sharp acoustic reports; differs because snapping is associated with tensile breakage or biting jaws, whereas popping is associated with gas expansion, cavitation, or sudden protrusion.
  • Starting: Similar in ocular expressions (eyes starting from the head); differs because starting is more archaic and literary, emphasizing the sudden jump or leap of surprise.
