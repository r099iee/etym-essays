• Anything: Similar because both serve as indefinite pronouns referring to an unspecified object or event; differs because anything is the standard, neutral modern word used across all casual and formal registers, whereas aught is distinctly archaic, literary, and elevated.
  • Whatever: Similar because both encompass all possible things without restriction; differs because whatever functions flexibly as a relative determiner, pronoun, or dismissive interjection, while aught is strictly an indefinite entity marker.
  • Something: Similar because both refer to an entity; differs because something is positive and specific (affirming the presence of a definite thing), while aught is indefinite, non-specific, and typically triggered by interrogative, conditional, or negative environments.
  • Naught: Similar because both share the exact same morphological Germanic architecture and poetic register; differs because naught is the absolute negative antonym ("nothing, zero, ruined"), formed with the negative prefix *ne-*, while aught is the positive assertion ("anything").
  • Ought: Similar because they were historically homophonic variants in Middle English spellings; differs because ought in modern grammar is almost exclusively an auxiliary modal verb expressing moral duty or probability ("you ought to go"), whereas aught is a pronoun.
  • Anywise: Similar because both can function adverbially to mean "in any manner"; differs because anywise emphasizes method and manner, while aught emphasizes quantity, degree, or substantive existence.
