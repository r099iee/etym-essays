Note: From Latin ob- ("toward, against, upon") + cadere ("to fall", from PIE *ḱad- "to fall"), literally "that which falls out or happens opportunely". The verb sense used in Ruskin's text ("to occasion much misconception") arose via functional shift from the noun in the late 15th to 16th century, meaning "to serve as the occasion or inciting cause for something". In philosophy, it gave rise to the doctrine of *occasionalism* (Malebranche).
