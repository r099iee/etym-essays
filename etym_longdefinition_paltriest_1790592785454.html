Paltriest is the morphological superlative of the adjective paltry, denoting the lowest degree on scales of quantity, value, significance, or worth: the most trivial, meagre, insignificant, despicable, or worthless among a set. When Wodehouse writes of nicking a man for the paltriest sum, he means even the smallest, most trifling amount of money—so small as to be hardly worth mentioning—still provokes an outsized squawk of protest. The force is both quantitative and evaluative: paltriest marks minimal amount and maximal contempt, implying the sum is unworthy of dispute and the complainer is miserly.

The primary sense is quantitative meagreness. A paltriest wage, pension, portion, or donation is miserably small, inadequate to need or expectation. Secondary senses stress qualitative worthlessness. The paltriest excuse, novel, performance, or argument is the feeblest, most contemptible, least convincing, deserving dismissal. A tertiary, now rarer sense preserves an older association with raggedness and physical shabbiness: paltry originally suggested patched, threadbare clothing, so the paltriest beggar or hovel is the most ragged and squalid. Across all senses the evaluative stance is dismissive and slightly indignant, voicing the speaker's disappointment that so little was offered or that so little causes so much fuss.

Grammatically, paltriest follows regular -y → -iest superlative formation for disyllabic adjectives, contrasting with periphrastic most paltry used for emphasis or in formal prose. Its use is often ironic or hyperbolic: speakers invoke the paltriest sum not after measurement but to dramatize miserliness, ingratitude, or disproportion. It belongs to a rhetorical pattern of superlative minimization—slightest pretext, smallest favour, paltriest sum—where extreme smallness morally condemns the reaction it provokes.

  • Money and commerce: Paltriest sums, tips, raises, and debts foreground stinginess; central to comic complaints about skinflints who guard pennies as if pounds.
  • Excuses and efforts: Paltriest pretexts and apologies are transparently inadequate; emphasizes intellectual or moral poverty rather than cash value.
  • Art and performance: Paltriest entertainment is cheap, trashy, or incompetent; carries snobbish dismissal of low culture.
  • Appearance and condition: Archaic but still intelligible sense of most ragged or squalid; links moral contempt to physical shabbiness.
  • Rhetorical register: Elevated-comic diction; paltry/paltriest sounds more literary than tiny or lousy, allowing Wodehouse to mock both miser and высокопарность.

Polysemy bridges through metaphors of rags to rubbish. If a rag is a worthless scrap of cloth, then a paltry thing is rag-like: frayed, insubstantial, unworthy of respect. The superlative intensifies this to the ultimate rag. The squawk heard at Land's End in the example is funny precisely because the stimulus is paltriest: maximal noise for minimal cause reveals character. Unlike smallest, which is neutrally dimensional, paltriest is inherently scornful; unlike worst, which is general, paltriest specifically scorns through smallness; unlike cheapest, which concerns price, paltriest concerns worth and dignity. Collocations include paltriest sum, paltriest excuse, paltriestrogue in older texts, and modern paltry excuse, paltry sum with paltriest reserved for emphatic, often humorous peaks.

Phonetically, /ˈpɔːltriɪst/ with clustered /ltr/ and hissing /ɪst/ sounds appropriately thin and dismissive, a phonesthetic match for triviality. Mastering paltriest thus means mastering English gradation of contempt: to call something paltry is to dismiss it; to call it the paltriest is to crown it king of rubbish.
