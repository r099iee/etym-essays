NOTE: The origin of mound is uncertain and debated. The OED records the word from c. 1500 (Scottish texts) in the sense "a hedge, fence, or enclosure"; the most widely repeated theory connects it with Old English mund "hand; protection" and Middle Dutch mond(e) "protection, defence" — the mound as "that which protects" (a fence, then a defensive bank) — but this link is not universally accepted, and some scholars derive the word independently from the "raised bank" sense without the protection metaphor. Because the word is first attested only around 1500, no PIE chain can be reconstructed for it; under the mund-theory the ultimate source would be the Indo-European root *man- "hand" (the hand as the instrument of protection), but this remains speculative.
