• Growls: Similar because both are low guttural threat-sounds; differs because growls is the broader genus (engines and stomachs growl too), while snarls adds bared teeth and visible ferocity.
  • Snaps: Similar because both are aggressive jaw-actions on the edge of biting; differs because snaps are quick striking bites, while snarls are sustained warning sounds before any bite lands.
  • Barks: Similar because both are loud canine warnings; differs because barks are sharp and often merely alerting, while snarls are low, continuous, and unambiguously hostile.
  • Hisses: Similar because both are sustained repellent sounds; differs because hisses are breathy and serpentine, while snarls are throaty and mammalian.
  • Grumblings: Similar because both voice discontent at low pitch; differs because grumblings are muffled complaints without bared teeth, while snarls put the teeth on show.
  • Sneers: Similar because both curl the lip in contempt; differs because sneers are cold silent superiority, while snarls are hot audible menace.
  • Tangles: Similar only in spelling, as the rival snarl family names knots; differs because tangles are knotted disorder in rope or hair, while growl-snarls are sounds of threat — true homographs, unrelated in blood.
  • Yaps: Similar because both are noisy dog-like hostility; differs because yaps are shrill and slightly ridiculous, while snarls carry genuine menace.
