• Respected: Similar because both mean held in positive regard; differs because "respected" emphasises general good opinion and reputation, while "esteemed" is warmer and implies sincere, often personal admiration.
  • Admired: Similar because both convey high regard; differs because "admired" stresses active appreciation of qualities or achievements, while "esteemed" stresses worth and dignity.
  • Revered: Similar because both convey deep respect; differs because "revered" is far stronger, suggesting awe or devotion approaching worship, while "esteemed" is warm but more measured.
  • Venerated: Similar because both convey profound respect; differs because "venerated" has religious or quasi-religious overtones and a higher register, while "esteemed" is secular and formal.
  • Valued: Similar because both imply holding in high regard; differs because "valued" focuses on worth, usefulness, or price, while "esteemed" focuses on honour and dignity.
  • Honoured: Similar because both convey respect; differs because "honoured" suggests conferred distinction, awards, or official recognition, while "esteemed" need not involve ceremony.
  • Reputable: Similar because both imply good standing; differs because "reputable" is about public trustworthiness and good name, without the emotional warmth of "esteemed."
  • Distinguished: Similar because both describe someone worthy of respect; differs because "distinguished" emphasises prominence, achievement, and eminence, while "esteemed" emphasises the regard others feel.
  • Celebrated: Similar because both describe someone highly thought of; differs because "celebrated" emphasises fame and public acclaim, while "esteemed" can describe a modest person respected by a few.
  • Prized: Similar because both imply high value; differs because "prized" suggests treasured, cherished, almost possessive value, while "esteemed" is about respect for worth.
