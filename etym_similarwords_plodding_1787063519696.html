• Trudging: Similar because both describe heavy, effortful walking; differs because trudging stresses the weary foot-by-foot effort of travel, while plodding can also be a general noun for slow, dull work.
  • Laborious: Similar because both denote slow, effortful work; differs because laborious describes the task's difficulty, while plodding describes the slow heavy pace of the doer.
  • Dragging: Similar because both imply slow heavy motion; differs because dragging suggests pulling a weight or moving reluctantly.
  • Lumbering: Similar because both describe heavy slow movement; differs because lumbering implies bulk and clumsy noise, whereas plodding emphasises steady persistence.
  • Steady: Similar because both can imply unremitting regularity; differs because steady is neutral or positive (reliable), while plodding suggests dullness or lack of flair.
  • Dull: Similar because plodding often connotes dullness; differs because dull describes the quality of being boring, while plodding names the slow laborious action.
  • Dogged: Similar because both imply persistent slogging; differs because dogged is admiring (tenacity), while plodding is often mildly dismissive.
  • Ponderous: Similar because both mean slow and heavy; differs because ponderous suggests weight, solemnity, or long-windedness, while plodding suggests humble foot-slogging.
