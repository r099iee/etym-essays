Old French (14th c.)

↓
Middle French (15th–16th c.)
sombresaut
‘tumbler’s leap, somersault’
↓
Early Modern English (1520s, borrowed)
somerset, somersault
‘tumbler’s heel-over-head leap’
↓
Modern English (current)
somersault
‘full aerial revolution of the body’

Note: the reshaping of French sobre-/soubre- (from Latin supra, super, over) into English somer- is unexplained — almost certainly folk etymology after summer or the surname Somerset; the leap itself comes straight from Latin saltus, a jump.
