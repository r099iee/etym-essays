Note on morphology: The root kempt represents the regular phonologically mutated past participle of Old English cemban ("to comb"). When the verb comb was later regularized in Middle English under the influence of the noun comb (yielding modern combed), the archaic form kempt became obsolete in positive standalone use, surviving exclusively within the fossilized negative compound unkempt.
