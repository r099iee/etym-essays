• weariness: Similar because it denotes tiredness from exertion; differs because it is general and neutral, while lassitude implies heaviness and loss of drive.
  • fatigue: Similar because it means depletion of strength; differs because fatigue is more clinical and muscular, lassitude more subjective and listless.
  • lethargy: Similar because it means sluggish inactivity; differs because lethargy stresses drowsiness and abnormal sleepiness, lassitude stresses heaviness without necessarily sleepiness.
  • languor: Similar because it means dreamy tiredness and lack of vigour; differs because languor is often pleasant or eroticized, lassitude more medical and oppressive.
  • exhaustion: Similar because it means extreme tiredness; differs because exhaustion implies resources fully used up, lassitude can occur without great exertion.
  • torpor: Similar because it means mental and physical inactivity; differs because torpor implies numbness and stupor, as in hibernation, stronger than lassitude.
  • listlessness: Similar because it means lack of energy and interest; differs because listlessness stresses aimlessness of attention, lassitude stresses heaviness of body and will.
  • enervation: Similar because it means loss of vitality; differs because enervation is a draining away of nerve-force, formal and medical, while lassitude is the felt state.
  • apathy: Similar because it means lack of feeling and initiative; differs because apathy is purely psychological indifference, lassitude includes bodily weariness.
  • debility: Similar because it means weakness; differs because debility implies chronic feebleness of constitution, lassitude a temporary state.
  • prostration: Similar because it means collapse from exhaustion; differs because prostration is extreme and acute, often lying down, lassitude can be mild and lingering.
  • malaise: Similar because it means vague unwellness and discomfort; differs because malaise emphasizes unease whose cause is unknown, lassitude emphasizes heaviness and lack of energy.
