• English: The standard modern name for the language and the direct descendant of the same Old English word; differs because it is the ordinary, unmarked term, while Anglish is either archaic or a marked revival with ideological overtones.
  • Anglian: The dialectological adjective for the Old English dialects of Mercia and Northumbria; differs because it is a neutral technical term for a regional variety, not a name for the whole language or a purist movement.
  • Anglo-Saxon: The older scholarly name for the Old English language and the early English period; differs because it is a historical and academic label, whereas Anglish is a variant spelling or a modern style.
  • Old English: The name for the language from roughly 450 to 1150; differs because it denotes a chronological stage with its own grammar, not a spelling of the modern name or a modern register.
  • Englisc: The attested Old English form of the name; differs because it is the historical spelling with its own pronunciation, while Anglish is a later variant.
  • Saxon: A name sometimes used for the English and their language, especially the West Saxon standard; differs because it foregrounds the Saxons rather than the Angles and is not used for the modern purist movement.
  • Plain English: Clear, unpretentious English; differs because it is about clarity and style, not about the etymology of words, and it happily uses Latin vocabulary.
  • Germanic: The language family to which English belongs; differs because it is a comparative linguistic category, not a name for English or for a purist register.
  • Purism: The general practice of resisting foreign influence in a language; differs because it names the ideology or method, while Anglish names the particular English variety and community.
  • Anglophone: An English-speaking person or community; differs because it describes speakers worldwide and has no connection to the purist movement.
