• Gangplank: Similar because both name the boarding plank between quay and ship; differs because a gangplank is always the simple loose plank, while gangway covers railed platforms, jet bridges, and indoor aisles as well.
  • Jet bridge: Similar because both are enclosed boarding passages into a vessel or aircraft; differs because jet bridge names only the modern telescopic airport tunnel, while gangway is the older, broader word from ferries to theatres.
  • Aisle: Similar because both are narrow indoor passages between rows of seats; differs because aisle is the polite church, supermarket, and cabin word, while gangway keeps a nautical, workmanlike tang and doubles as a shouted command.
  • Passageway: Similar because both are narrow going-ways through a building or ship; differs because passageway is the general architectural term, while gangway stresses boarding, crossing a gap, or squeezing past a crowd.
  • Corridor: Similar because both are enclosed narrow routes for moving through; differs because a corridor is a permanent roofed hall inside a building, while a gangway is often temporary, open, or portable, laid down for one crossing.
  • Walkway: Similar because both are narrow paths for feet; differs because walkway is broad and neutral (garden walkways, cliff walkways), while gangway implies tightness, boarding, or a crowd to thread through.
  • Footbridge: Similar because both bridge a gap for walkers, as the ferry plank bridges water; differs because a footbridge is a fixed spanning structure, while a gangway is portable or adjustable and belongs to the vessel as much as the shore.
  • Catwalk: Similar because both are narrow elevated walking strips; differs because a catwalk serves machinery, stages, and fashion shows, while a gangway serves boarding, seating blocks, and thoroughfares.
  • Gangway ladder: Similar because both give access up a ship's side; differs because the ladder is specifically the stepped or cleated climb, while gangway names the whole boarding opening, platform, or passage.
