• Nobility: Similar because both name a high, privileged social class; differs because the nobility is the titled class (dukes, marquesses, earls, viscounts, barons) seated in the House of Lords, while the gentry are the untitled landowners below them.
  • Peerage: Similar because both are ranked social classes; differs because the peerage is the precise legal body of hereditary titles created by the Crown, while the gentry hold no such titles.
  • Aristocracy: Similar because both denote an elite ruling class; differs because in strict English usage aristocracy means the titled nobility, whereas gentry means the untitled landed gentlemen who governed the counties.
  • Squirearchy: Similar because both name the county gentlemen and their rule; differs because squirearchy is a more particular and slightly mocking word for the local ascendancy of the squires.
  • The quality: Similar because both name people of rank and respectability; differs because “the quality” is an old informal, deferential term for people of fashion and rank, while gentry is a formal class term with a precise landed meaning.
  • Bourgeoisie: Similar because both name a middle-to-upper social stratum; differs because the bourgeoisie is the urban, commercial, professional middle class, while the gentry are the landed class rooted in the countryside and in gentle birth.
  • Upper class: Similar because both denote the top of the social hierarchy; differs because “upper class” is a loose modern term covering both titled and untitled wealth, while gentry has the precise historical meaning of untitled landowners.
