- [16th century: scenting] - The noun nose is turned into the verb nosen, "to smell or scent" — a hunter's and hound's word, purely physical, an animal reading the air.
- [17th century: prying] - "To nose about" extends the word from snout to busybody: nosing gains a transferred, faintly contemptuous sense of intrusive meddling in what does not concern one.
- [19th century: nosy] - The adjective nosy (1880s) fixes the word's inquisitive register, and "to nose out" means to discover by persistent scenting — the word now means both sleuthing and snooping.
- [Contemporary sense] - Nosing shuttles between literal head-first probing — a boat or aircraft nosing forward into a space — and the same transferred meddling, "nosing into other people's business," keeping its double life of sniffing and prying.
