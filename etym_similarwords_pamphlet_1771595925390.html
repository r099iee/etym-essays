• Booklet: Similar because both are small books; differs because booklet is the neutral, descriptive term for any small bound work, with no implication of argument, whereas a pamphlet is devoted to a single polemical or topical subject.
  • Brochure: Similar because both are small printed works; differs because brochure is commercial and promotional — travel, product, and company literature, glossy and persuasive — whereas a pamphlet is argumentative and topical.
  • Leaflet: Similar because both are small printed works for distribution; differs because a leaflet is one or a very few cheap sheets used in mass campaigns, whereas a pamphlet is a stitched booklet developing one argument at length.
  • Tract: Similar because both are short booklets on a single subject; differs because tract is the older religious or moral term for a short exhortation (Tracts for the Times, the Tractarian movement), narrowly didactic, whereas a pamphlet may be secular and political.
  • Monograph: Similar because both treat a single subject in a small book; differs because a monograph is the scholar's formal, exhaustive, and often lengthy single-subject book, whereas a pamphlet is short, cheap, and typically argumentative rather than exhaustive.
  • Broadside: Similar because both are printed for quick circulation; differs because a broadside is a single large sheet printed on one side, used for ballads and announcements, whereas a pamphlet is a folded, stitched booklet.
  • Flyer: Similar because both are printed to publicize; differs because a flyer is a single sheet for announcements and advertising, whereas a pamphlet develops an argument in several pages.
  • Handout: Similar because both are brief informational pieces; differs because a handout is an informal sheet given to an audience at a meeting or class, whereas a pamphlet is a finished, usually printed booklet.
  • Squib: Similar because both are short satirical or polemical pieces; differs because a squib is a single sharp lampoon or attack, often in verse, whereas a pamphlet is a sustained prose argument.
  • Tractate: Similar because both are short treatises; differs because tractate is a learned or rabbinic term for a treatise, whereas pamphlet is the common word for the polemical booklet.
