PIE deictic stem *k̂o- / *ki- "this, here" (the demonstrative root of the "this/here" family; it also stands behind Latin cis "on this side") → Proto-Germanic *hiz "this" (demonstrative, reconstructed), extended with the directional adverbial suffix -drē to form *hidrē "hither, to this place" (reconstructed; directly witnessed by Gothic hidrē "hither" and Old Norse heðra "hither") → Old English hider (also hyder) "hither, to this place," attested from the early Old English period (8th–9th c., in poetic and glossarial texts) → Middle English hider/hyder; from the 12th century the form shifts to hithder/hither, with the -d- replaced by -th- under the analogical influence of the parallel thither and whither → Modern English HITHER (adverb) "to or toward this place," now archaic and literary; the adjective sense "nearer, on the near side" and the compounds hitherward and come-hither derive from the same form. NOTE ON THE CHANGE: the -th- is purely analogical, not a regular sound change; the original consonant was -d- (Old English hider), as the Gothic and Old Norse cognates show.
