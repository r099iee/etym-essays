• Talon: Similar because both are sharp, curved animal nails used for seizing; differs because talon refers specifically to the claws of birds of prey (and, by extension, of eagles and hawks), while claw covers mammals, reptiles, and crustaceans as well.
  • Nail: Similar because both name the hard covering at the end of a digit; differs because nail is the neutral, human-centred word (fingernail, toenail), whereas claw implies an animal's curved, functional weapon.
  • Pincer: Similar because both are grasping, pinching appendages of animals or tools; differs because pincer names the jointed chela of a crustacean or a squeezing tool, while claw emphasizes the curved horny point of a digit.
  • Hook: Similar because both describe a curved implement or part that seizes and holds; differs because hook is a general mechanical term for any curved catch, while claw retains the biological and predatory sense of an animal digit.
  • Scratch: Similar because both describe raking with nails; differs because scratch implies light, superficial surface contact, whereas claw implies deep, aggressive tearing or desperate effort.
  • Scrape: Similar because both involve abrasive contact with a surface; differs because scrape is a broad mechanical motion (any edge, not just nails), while claw specifically means hooked, digging effort.
  • Tear: Similar because both describe ripping something apart; differs because tear describes the result (the parting of material) and can be done by any force, while claw describes the hooked, scratching action that produces the tear.
  • Grasp: Similar because both involve seizing hold of something; differs because grasp implies firm, deliberate holding, while claw implies frantic, desperate, or fierce reaching.
  • Grapple: Similar because both involve struggling to get hold of something; differs because grapple suggests wrestling or close physical combat between opponents, while claw emphasizes tearing and scrambling rather than locking holds.
  • Grab: Similar because both mean seizing suddenly; differs because grab is a neutral quick snatch with the hand, while claw carries the image of curved nails digging in.
  • Clench: Similar because both involve tightening the grip; differs because clench means squeezing the fist or teeth shut and holding, while claw means repeatedly raking or digging.
  • Snatch: Similar because both describe seizing greedily or violently; differs because snatch emphasizes the quickness of the theft, while claw emphasizes the hooked, tearing manner of the grip.
