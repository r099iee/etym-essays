Note on philological doublet: Modern English possesses two distinct words from the Proto-Germanic root *wagnaz. The inherited native Old English form was wæġn (with palatal *ġ* vocalizing into a glide), which evolved directly into modern English wain (preserved in poetry and *Charles's Wain*). In the 16th century, English merchants and soldiers borrowed the Dutch form wagen to describe heavy continental freight conveyances, giving rise to wagon / waggon. Thus, wain and waggon are native sister and continental loan doublet pairs of the identical Indo-European root.
