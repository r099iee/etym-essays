- 1611 (King James Version) - "Hinnom" appears in English as a plain biblical toponym - the valley of Hinnom - a routine place-name in the tribal land allotments of Joshua.
- The same English name, scripturally infamed - The KJV attaches it to the Tophet, the fire-pit where children were offered to Molech, and to Jeremiah's curse: a valley of slaughtered children and burning.
- 19th-20th c. cultural weight - As Gehenna became the New Testament's standard word for hell, the older English name "Hinnom" accumulated the same dread in allusion - a byword for fire, destruction, catastrophe.
- Contemporary usage - "To go down through Hinnom" = to brave hell's own geography: a real ravine outside Jerusalem that became a word for the place of no return.
