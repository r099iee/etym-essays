- Material not worked or manufactured — Old English formation (unworht); a plain, neutral descriptive word for ore, metal, stone and timber in their natural state.
- The technical-commercial sense crystallises — in bullion law, assay records, customs usage and treasure-inventories from the Middle Ages onward, “wrought and unwrought” marks the boundary between finished goods and raw material.
- A formal, weighty literary word — in 19th–20th-century prose and fantasy (Tolkien’s The Hobbit, 1937) it carries antique dignity, evoking treasure, precious metal and epic bargains.
- Today — survives mainly in trade classifications, metallurgy, assayers’ reports and deliberately archaic writing; in ordinary speech it has been displaced by raw, unprocessed, unfinished.
