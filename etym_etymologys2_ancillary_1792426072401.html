Note on morphological evolution: Historical linguists (Ernout-Meillet, Michiel de Vaan) identify Latin ancilla as an ancient diminutive formation from archaic ancula ("handmaid"), formed from the pre-verb am- ("around", from PIE *h₂mbʰi-) and the zero-grade of *kʷel- ("to turn"). The word is an exact cognate of Ancient Greek amphípolos (ἀμφίπολος, "attendant, handmaid", literally "one who turns/bustles around"). The semantic shift from a household female slave to modern institutional and corporate auxiliary systems occurred via medieval Christian scholastic theology, where philosophy was famously declared the "handmaid of theology" (philosophia ancilla theologiae), cementing the concept of a subordinate yet indispensable discipline.
