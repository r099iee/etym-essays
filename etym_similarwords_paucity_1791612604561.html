• dearth: Similar because both denote an acute, lamentable lack or insufficiency of something necessary; differs because dearth carries stronger historical connotations of famine, costly scarcity, and urgent physical deprivation, whereas paucity is more cerebral, analytical, and academic.
  • scarcity: Similar because both describe an inadequate supply relative to demand; differs because scarcity is an economic and practical term describing tangible market shortages (e.g., water scarcity, scarcity of oil), whereas paucity emphasizes smallness of quantity in intellectual, empirical, or evidential realms.
  • deficiency: Similar because both denote a falling short of a required standard; differs because deficiency focuses on a qualitative defect, malfunction, or physiological lack of an essential component (e.g., vitamin deficiency, character deficiency), whereas paucity focuses strictly on smallness of number or volume.
  • meagerness: Similar because both describe a paltry, thin, or deficient amount; differs because meagerness carries sensory, visceral imagery of physical emaciation, thinness, or pathetic skimpy rations, whereas paucity maintains cool scholarly detachment.
  • famine: Similar because both express severe shortage; differs because famine is an extreme, life-threatening crisis of starvation or total deprivation, whereas paucity describes a measured, intellectualized shortfall.
  • shortage: Similar because both denote an inadequate quantity available; differs because shortage is a common, mundane, practical term for logistical or supply deficits (e.g., a chip shortage, staff shortage), lacking the high rhetorical register of paucity.
  • lack: Similar because both signal the absence or insufficiency of something; differs because lack can denote a total, complete absence (100% missing), whereas paucity specifically indicates that a few or a small number exist, but not nearly enough.
  • paltryness: Similar because both convey trivial or contemptible smallness; differs because paltryness conveys emotional contempt and petty worthlessness, while paucity is an objective, respectful diagnostic term.
