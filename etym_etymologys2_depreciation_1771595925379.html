Chain for DEPRECIATION (single root, Latin): Stage 1 — PIE *per- "to hand over, to sell, to traffic" (Pokorny *per-; also cited as *perH₂ in some reconstructions). This market-root is the standard, though debated, source of Latin pretium. The connection is not entirely certain: some scholars instead derive pretium from a separate, non-PIE or Italic source, so the *per- link must be presented as the mainstream but debated etymology. Stage 2 — Latin pretium "price, value, worth, reward" (attested throughout Classical Latin, 2nd–1st c. BCE; the noun of valuation in Roman law and commerce). Stage 3 — Latin dēpretiāre / dēpretiārī "to lower the price of, to undervalue" (imperial Latin; formed with the prefix dē- "down, away" + pretiāre "to price, to value"). Stage 4 — Latin dēpretiātiō, dēpretiātiōnis "a lowering of value" (Late Latin abstract noun; also written dēpreciātiō in some manuscripts). Stage 5 — Old French dépréciation "a lowering of value" and déprécier "to lower the value of" (12th–14th c.; the Old French verb form is used of value in trade and esteem). Stage 6 — Middle English depreciacioun "a lowering of value, undervaluation" (14th–15th c., chiefly in legal and mercantile contexts), and the verb depreciate (17th c., from Latin dēpretiāt-, past-participle stem). Final node — English DEPRECIATION (18th c. onward; the noun becomes fully established in financial, monetary, and figurative usage; the modern accounting sense — spreading an asset's cost over its useful life — solidifies in the 19th–20th c.). Note on debate: the derivation of Latin pretium from PIE *per- "to sell/traffic" is the traditional view (Pokorny), but the OED and several modern handbooks treat the ultimate source as uncertain, and alternative comparisons (e.g., with *per- "to pass through," or a substrate/Italic origin) have been proposed. No intermediate stages are invented; the direct path pretium → dēpretiāre → dēpretiātiō is securely attested within Latin. The English word comes via the French noun; the verb entered through the Latin participial stem.
