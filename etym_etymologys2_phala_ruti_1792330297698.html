ROOT 1: phala (फल)
Pre-Indo-Aryan Substrate / Dravidian (c. 2000 BCE)
*paḻam / *palam
"ripe fruit, yield, product of a plant"
[Proto-Dravidian root *paḻ- "to ripen"; cf. Tamil paḻam, Malayalam paḻam; alternatively linked to PIE *bʰel- "to blow, swell, bloom"]
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1200 BCE)
phála (फल)
"fruit, kernel, agricultural yield, consequence"
[Attested in Rigveda; develops metaphorical sense of "karmic result"]

ROOT 2: śruti (श्रुति)
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*ḱlew- / *ḱlew-ti-
"to hear, listen; that which is heard, fame"
[Source of Greek klýō, kléos "glory"; Latin cluere; English loud, listen]
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (c. 2000 BCE)
*ćru-ti-
"act of hearing, listening, oral tradition"
[Noun formed with abstract feminine suffix *-ti-]
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1200 BCE)
śrutí (श्रुति)
"hearing, ear, sacred oral revelation, recital"
[Primary verbal noun from root śru- "to hear"; sacred Veda as heard by rishis]

COMPOUND SYNTHESIS:
Epic & Classical Sanskrit Compound (c. 4th c. BCE)
phalaśruti (फलश्रुति)
"statement of the fruits of hearing a sacred text"
[Tatpuruṣa / Karmadhāraya compound: phala ("fruit") + śruti ("hearing/recital")]
↓
Modern Indological & Religious English (present)
phalaśruti
"concluding benediction stating the rewards of hearing"
