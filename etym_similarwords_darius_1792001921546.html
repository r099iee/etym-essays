• Cyrus: Similar because both were legendary Achaemenid emperors who expanded Persian power; differs because Cyrus the Great founded the empire through rapid military conquest and conciliation, whereas Darius I was the master administrator who institutionalized satrapies, laws, taxation, and roads.
  • Xerxes: Similar because both were supreme Persian Great Kings who waged war against mainland Greece; differs because Xerxes was the son and successor of Darius whose massive 480 BCE invasion ended in catastrophic naval defeat at Salamis, whereas Darius laid the bureaucratic bedrock of the empire.
  • Artaxerxes: Similar because both were Achaemenid dynasty monarchs wielding autocratic power from Susa and Persepolis; differs because Artaxerxes represents later generations of Persian rulers characterized by diplomatic intrigue, gold subsidies, and internal succession crises.
  • Cambyses: Similar because both were early Persian emperors of the sixth century BCE; differs because Cambyses II was Cyrus's son who conquered Egypt but whose erratic reign descended into chaos, providing the opening for Darius to seize the throne.
  • Satrap: Similar because both possessed supreme governing authority within the Persian imperial apparatus; differs because a satrap was a provincial viceroy subordinate to the crown, whereas Darius was the King of Kings (*Shahanshah*), answering only to the god Ahuramazda.
  • Autocrat: Similar because both denote an absolute ruler with unrestricted political power; differs because autocrat is a general political classification, whereas Darius is the specific historical personification of ancient Persian kingship.
  • Monarch: Similar because both signify an individual head of state reigning over a kingdom; differs because monarch is a universal title, whereas Darius ruled a polyethnic, transcontinental empire comprising dozens of distinct subject nations.
