CHAIN — "blister" (early stages debated — see note) - Proto-Indo-European *bhel- "to swell, blow up, bubble" (reconstructed; the semantic family of blow/blast/bladder) - Proto-Germanic *blēs- "to blow" and *blēstr- "a blowing up, swelling" (reconstructed; not directly attested as the immediate source of the French form) - Old French blestre "a swelling, pustule, blister" (attested 12th c.; a loan from Germanic) - Middle English blister / blyster "blister" (attested c. 1300) - Modern English blister (16th c.–present) → blister (target root word) Debate / nuance note: The etymology is debated. The OED holds that English blister was borrowed from Old French blestre, which is itself generally treated as a Germanic loan (cf. Old High German blāsa "blister, pustule" and blāsan "to blow"). However, some scholars instead derive English blister from a native or North Sea Germanic form (cf. Middle Dutch bluyster, Middle Low German bluster), which would make it cognate with rather than a borrowing from the French word. The precise intermediate Germanic form between *bhel- and blestre is not directly attested; the chain above marks the uncertain link explicitly.
