- 17th Century: Introduced into English philosophical prose from Latin positus to mean formally laying down a foundational axiom or proposition.
- Late 18th–19th Century: Greatly elevated through English translations of German Idealism (Kant, Fichte, Hegel) to denote the fundamental creative act of consciousness setting up reality.
- Mid-20th Century: Became standard terminology in philosophy of science and logical positivism for postulating unobservable theoretical entities to explain data.
- Late 20th–21st Century: Widely employed across academic disciplines, jurisprudence, and critical theory to denote active, constructive conceptual framing.
