*leh₂k- / *lak-
"to entice, lure, ensnare"

"alluring, dainty, voluptuous, tender"

delicat
"tender, pleasing, fine"
↓
Middle French (16th c.)
délicatesse

↓
German (18th c.)
Delikatesse (pl. Delikatessen)
"culinary luxury, fine delicacy"
↓
German (19th c.)
Delikatessenhandlung
"specialty shop selling fine prepared foods"
↓
English (via German-American) (c. 1877–present)
delicatessen
"specialty food store selling prepared foods;"
"the prepared delicacies themselves"

Note: English delicatessen was borrowed directly from the German plural Delikatessen ("delicacies"). A common folk etymology claims the word incorporates the German verb essen ("to eat"); in reality, the suffix -esse comes via French délicatesse from Latin -itia (forming abstract nouns of quality), completely unrelated to the Germanic verb essen.
