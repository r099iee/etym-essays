- Homeric origin - Scylla appears in the Odyssey as the six-headed, man-eating sea-monster of the Strait of Messina, whose name (from Greek skyllo, to rend) meant the Renderer.
- Classical and proverbial afterlife - paired with the whirlpool Charybdis in Latin literature, the two monsters became the fixed emblem of an impossible choice, popularized by Erasmus's Adagia as between Scylla and Charybdis.
- Contemporary sense - Scylla survives mainly as the idiom for a dilemma with no safe option, used in politics, business, and ethics, while retaining its literary force as a proper name for the monster of the strait.
