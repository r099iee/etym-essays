- Old English 'acursian' - to place under a formal curse, excommunicate; the adjective meant 'subject to a curse or anathema'
- 14th-17th century - chiefly religious: the excommunicated or God-forsaken; 'accursed' names what is damned by heaven
- 18th-19th century - broadens from damnation to intense loathing: 'detestable, execrable' used as a strong intensifier of anger
- Contemporary - archaic-literary flavor; survives as a heavier, more biblical 'cursed', used for dramatic effect in elevated prose
