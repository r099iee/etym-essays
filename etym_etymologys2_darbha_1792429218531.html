Note on historical debate and botanical distinction: Sanskrit lexicographers (such as Amarasimha in the Amarakośa) and modern botanical philologists have debated whether darbha and kuśa were originally identical plants or two distinct species. While ancient texts frequently use them as liturgical synonyms, several technical treatises specify that darbha represents the broader genus of long, tufted ritual grasses harvested in thick bunches (from PIE *derbʰ-, "to bunch, wind"), whereas kuśa specifically denotes the sharper, thinner mature shoots of Desmostachya bipinnata. Etymologically, European cognates include Lithuanian dìrbti ("to work, craft, prepare") and dárbas ("work, labor"), pointing to an archaic PIE agricultural association with gathering, binding, or working stiff fibrous stalks into sheaves.
