- Late 13th–14th Century: Feudal Ceremony of Submission - Entered Middle English from Anglo-Norman and Old French (*homage*), designating the sacred legal ritual where a tenant or vassal knelt before a lord and pledged to become his 'man' (*homme*) in exchange for land tenure.
- 15th–16th Century: Broadening to Sovereign & Divine Allegiance - Extended metaphorically beyond landholding to denote general political allegiance to a king, devotion to God in religious liturgy, or courtly adoration paid to a lady in chivalric romance.
- 17th–18th Century: Secularized Respect & Compliment - Shifted with the decline of feudalism into a refined term of social and intellectual deference; paying homage came to mean expressing public esteem, honor, or polite reverence to eminent individuals of genius.
- 20th–21st Century: Artistic Dedication & Intertextual Tribute - Established as the canonical term in visual art, cinema, music, and literature for a deliberate creative work or stylistic reference made to honor, celebrate, and acknowledge an influential master (e.g., George Orwell's *Homage to Catalonia*, or 'Homage to Arnheim').
