GOWN — of obscure, likely non-Indo-European origin; no verifiable PIE ancestor exists. The chain below follows the attested medieval trail, with the earlier links reconstructed on the best available evidence and flagged as such.

1. Byzantine Greek γούνα (goúna) “a fur, a skin, a coarse garment of skin or fur” (attested in Byzantine Greek sources; the word is itself of uncertain, possibly Balkan or Illyrian substrate origin — a pre-Greek, likely non-Indo-European word whose ultimate source is unknown).
2. Late Latin gunna “a leather garment; a fur or skin garment” (attested in glossaries from the 6th century onward; borrowed from Byzantine Greek γούνα).
3. Medieval Latin gunna “a long robe, a loose garment” (attested in medieval Latin texts; the garment and word spread through the medieval Mediterranean and Europe).
4. Old French goune / gonne “a loose outer garment, a robe” (attested from the 11th–12th centuries; borrowed from Late Latin gunna).
5. Anglo-Norman goune “gown” (attested from the 12th–13th centuries in English court and legal records; the route by which the word entered English).
6. Middle English goune / gowne “a long loose outer garment” (attested c. 1300 onward; spelled goune, gowne, goun).
7. English GOWN “a long loose outer garment; a woman’s long formal dress” (modern form and senses).

Note on the origin: the early history of the word is genuinely uncertain. The standard dictionaries (OED, Etymonline) trace gown to Late Latin gunna, but the ultimate source is debated. The most plausible hypothesis derives gunna from Byzantine Greek γούνα “fur, skin garment,” and that Greek word may itself come from a Balkan or Illyrian substrate language — a non-Indo-European tongue, or one whose records have not survived. Attempts to connect the word with the PIE root *wes- “to clothe, to dress” (the source of English wear, Latin vestis, Sanskrit vas-) have been made but are not accepted by the standard etymological dictionaries; the honest entry for gown reads “origin unknown.” A separate, contested proposal links gunna to a Celtic source (cf. Old Irish gunna or Gaulish forms), but this too is speculative and lacks a secure base. No single PIE reconstruction can be responsibly asserted for this word.
