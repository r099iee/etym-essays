• steward: Similar as a high-ranking official overseeing a noble household or estate; differs because steward is of native Germanic origin (from OE stig-weard 'hall-guardian') and historically emphasized general estate management, agriculture, and banquet provision rather than intimate custody of the sovereign's private chamber and palace access.
  • chancellor: Similar as a senior court and state official; differs because a chancellor is primarily a judicial, legislative, and secretarial officer of state (keeper of the Great Seal and legal archives) rather than an administrator of domestic palace apartments.
  • seneschal: Similar as a major domestic officer in a royal or noble medieval household; differs because seneschal is an archaic feudal title specifically denoting a chief steward who often exercised judicial and military authority over provincial estates.
  • majordomo: Similar in managing a noble household; differs because majordomo (from Latin major domus) is of Spanish/Italian provenance, denoting the head butler or chief household manager without the high political and state prerogatives of a royal chamberlain.
  • treasurer: Similar in managing institutional funds and financial disbursements; differs because treasurer focuses purely on fiscal accounts and financial management, lacking the ceremonial, courtly, and palace-access duties of a chamberlain.
  • camerlengo: Similar as an exact etymological cognate; differs because camerlengo is used specifically and exclusively for high dignitaries in the Vatican and papal administration.
  • equerry: Similar as an officer of a royal household; differs because an equerry is specifically an officer attending to horses, stables, and accompanying the royal family during public journeys.
  • castellan: Similar as a high feudal officer; differs because a castellan is the military governor or warden of a castle or fortified stronghold.
  • marshal: Similar as a great officer of state; differs because a marshal historically managed the cavalry and military forces rather than domestic and private palace apartments.
  • comptroller: Similar as a senior household officer; differs because a comptroller specifically audits, supervises, and controls financial expenditures and accounts.
