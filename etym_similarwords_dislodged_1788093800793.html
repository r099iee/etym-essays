• Displaced: Similar because both mean moved from one's or its place; differs because displace is the broad, neutral term (displaced persons, displaced water) with no implication of fixity, resistance or force, while dislodged specifically means something firmly settled that had to be knocked out.
  • Dislocated: Similar because both mean forced out of position; differs because dislocate is anatomically precise — a joint out of its socket (or figuratively a whole arrangement disrupted) — whereas dislodged covers any lodged object, not only joints.
  • Removed: Similar because both mean taken away from a place; differs because remove is the all-purpose neutral word (remove a stain, a name, an obstacle) with no sense of prior lodging or of force overcoming resistance — dislodged adds both.
  • Ousted: Similar because both mean driven out of a position (oust a leader); differs because oust is always personal and institutional-legal (ousting from office or property by process or coup), and is never used of physical objects.
  • Evicted: Similar because both are forcible removal from a place; differs because evict is legalistic and tenant-focused (evicting from housing by due process), whereas dislodged is physical or tactical and usually extra-legal.
  • Expelled: Similar because both mean forced out; differs because expel is ejection from within an enclosed space or institution (expelled from school; air expelled from the lungs) by internal pressure or authority, while dislodged pries something out from without.
  • Ejected: Similar because both are forcible removal; differs because eject is abrupt, mechanism-driven expulsion (eject a cassette, eject a pilot) — mechanical and instantaneous — versus the resistance-and-settlement nuance of dislodged.
  • Prise / pry loose: Similar because both mean forcibly loosen something wedged or stuck; differs because prying implies levering with a tool (a pry bar, a chisel) against rigid fixity, while dislodged includes blows, shocks and shoves without any tool.
  • Knocked loose: Similar because both describe casual physical dislodging (a brick knocked loose); differs because knock loose is colloquial and purely percussive, lacking the military, medical and formal register of dislodged.
  • Wrenched: Similar because both involve pulling or twisting something from its seat; differs because wrench is violent torsion, usually damaging (wrench an ankle, wrench free), whereas dislodged can be clean and non-destructive.
  • Unseated: Similar because both remove someone or something from a position; differs because unseat implies being thrown from a seat or saddle, or defeated in an election or competition — riding and contest frames rather than lodged-objects.
  • Uprooted: Similar because both pull something from where it was established; differs because uproot is specifically root-depth removal (plants, communities, habits) carrying a sense of severed origin, deeper and more destructive than dislodged.
  • Routed: Similar because in the military sense both mean driving an enemy from ground; differs because routing implies total disorganised flight of a defeated force, whereas to dislodge only requires evicting them from the position — they may retreat in order.
  • Extricated: Similar because both free something from a stuck position; differs because extricating is careful, skilful freeing (extricating a car from a ditch, oneself from a difficulty) — patient and deliberate — while dislodged is direct force applied to the lodged object.
