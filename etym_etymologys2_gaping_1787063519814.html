Proto-Germanic (c. 1st millennium CE)
*gapōn
"to open the mouth wide; to gape"
↓
Old Norse (c. 800–1300)
gapa
"to open the mouth, gape, stare"
↓
Middle English (13th–15th c.)
gapen
"to open the mouth wide; to stare in wonder; to yawn"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
gape
"to open wide; to stare with open mouth"
↓
gape + -ing → GAPING

Note on the origin: the ultimate root of *gapōn is unknown; the word is almost certainly expressive or imitative (the open vowels suggest the sound and shape of a wide-open mouth), and its Germanic cognates — Old Norse gap, Middle Dutch gapen, German gaffen "to stare" — all carry the same sense. The noun gap derives from Old Norse gap "chasm, open mouth," the same family.
