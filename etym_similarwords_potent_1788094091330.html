• Powerful: Similar because both mean having great power. Differs because powerful is the broad everyday word for any kind of strength or capacity (a powerful engine), while potent suggests concentrated, result-producing efficacy, especially of drugs, drinks, and arguments.
  • Strong: Similar because both can describe a potent wine, drug, or argument. Differs because strong is the vast general word for resistance, force, or intensity of any kind; potent is narrower and implies effectiveness in small quantity.
  • Mighty: Similar because both describe great power, especially of rulers and forces. Differs because mighty is poetic and epic, suggesting sheer bulk and grandeur; potent is analytical and can apply to a drop of poison.
  • Forceful: Similar because both describe something that produces a strong effect. Differs because forceful is used of people, speech, and style (a forceful speaker); potent extends naturally to chemicals and drinks.
  • Efficacious: Similar because both mean producing the intended effect. Differs because efficacious is clinical-jargon for remedies and procedures; potent additionally names the strength behind the effect.
  • Effective: Similar because both describe things that work. Differs because effective is neutral about degree — a weak remedy can still be effective; potent asserts high strength per dose.
  • Influential: Similar because both describe power over others (a potent/influential figure). Differs because influential is limited to persons and things that sway opinion; potent also covers chemistry, physics, and sheersensory force.
  • Commanding: Similar because both describe authority that compels (a potent presence). Differs because commanding is about bearing and rank; potent is about actual capacity or effect.
  • Puissant: Similar because it is the French cousin of potent — both from Latin potens, both meaning mighty. Differs because puissant is archaic-poetic and rarely used after the 18th century outside historical prose.
  • Stalwart: Similar because both can describe strong persons. Differs because stalwart stresses loyal, sturdy reliability rather than effectiveness; potent stresses power that produces results.
  • Robust: Similar because both describe strength. Differs because robust means healthy, sturdy, and tolerant of abuse (a robust system); potent means strong in action or effect.
  • Vigorous: Similar because both describe energetic power. Differs because vigorous is about liveliness and activity; potent is about concentrated force and outcome.
  • Virile: Similar because both can describe male sexual capacity. Differs because virile is about manliness and masculinity in general, while potent in this sense is the clinical reproductive term.
  • Intense: Similar because both describe high degree (a potent/intense flavour). Differs because intense measures degree of sensation or feeling; potent measures power to cause effects.
  • Concentrated: Similar because both describe strength in small volume (potent/concentrated poison). Differs because concentrated is literal about dilution levels; potent is about effect, which may also be figurative.
  • Heady: Similar because both describe strong drink that goes to the head (potent/heady wine). Differs because heady is colloquial and stresses intoxication; potent is formal and covers all effective agents.
  • Intoxicating: Similar because both can describe potent wine. Differs because intoxicating names the drunkenness caused; potent names the power that causes it.
  • Potentate (related noun, not synonym): Similar because it shares the root and denotes one who wields power, a ruler. Differs because it is a noun with the specific sense “sovereign”, now historical or literary — not an adjective describing efficacy.
