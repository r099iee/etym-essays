• Nook: Similar because both denote small, secluded, cozy spatial recesses within a larger area; differs because a *nook* is typically an informal corner, angle, or naturally occurring pocket of space, whereas an *alcove* is an intentionally designed, often arched or vaulted architectural feature set into a wall.
  • Recess: Similar because both signify an indentation or set-back opening in a wall or room; differs because *recess* is a broad, neutral architectural and geological term for any set-back space, while *alcove* specifically implies a functional or aesthetic compartment large enough for furniture, a bed, or seating.
  • Niche: Similar because both are ornamental indentations set into walls; differs because a *niche* is generally smaller, shallower, and specifically designed to display a statue, vase, or decorative artwork, whereas an *alcove* is a habitable spatial compartment designed for human use or major furnishings.
  • Inglenook: Similar because both are intimate, recessed architectural spaces within a room; differs because an *inglenook* is specifically a fireside chimney recess featuring built-in benches flanking an open hearth, whereas an *alcove* can occur anywhere in a room, garden, or cliff.
  • Bay: Similar because both represent partitioned or recessed spatial compartments; differs because an architectural *bay* typically projects outward beyond the exterior wall line of a building (as in a bay window), while an *alcove* recesses inward from the room's boundary.
  • Arbor / Bower: Similar because both can describe sheltered outdoor garden retreats; differs because an *arbor* or *bower* is primarily an open-air garden structure formed of intertwined tree branches, vines, or standalone trellises, whereas an *alcove* is an architectural recess set into a wall or masonry structure.
  • Cubbyhole: Similar because both denote small, partitioned compartments; differs because a *cubbyhole* is an informal, cramped storage space or tiny enclosure, whereas an *alcove* carries classical architectural elegance and intentional design.
  • Apse: Similar because both are arched, semicircular, or vaulted architectural recesses; differs because an *apse* is a monumental, vaulted semicircular recess specifically terminating the sanctuary, choir, or nave of a church or basilica.
