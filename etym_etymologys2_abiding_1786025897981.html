PIE *bʰeydʰ-
"to trust, persuade, confide"
(reconstructed; root also of Latin fides "faith")

↓

Proto-Germanic *bīdaną
"to wait, remain, await"
(reconstructed)

↓

Old English bīdan
"to stay, wait, remain" (attested)
↓

Old English ābīdan
"to remain, await, endure"
(intensive ā- "away/out" + bīdan; attested)

↓

Middle English abiden
"to stay, wait, endure, dwell" (12th-14th c.)

↓

Early Modern English abiding
present participle used as adjective
"enduring, permanent, law-observant" (16th c. onward)

↓

Modern English ABIDING
"enduring, lasting, steadfast, law-abiding"

NOTE: The semantic bridge from PIE "to trust" to Germanic "to wait" runs through the idea of waiting confidently, i.e. trusting that what is awaited will come; the link is plausible but the intermediate semantics are not fully recoverable.
