- "Sleepiness as disease (medieval medicine) - lethargy is a named humoral condition, feared as the doorstep of coma and death"
- "Broadening to the mind (17th-18th c.) - from the sickbed to the soul: lethargic ages, nations, and parliaments enter political and moral prose"
- "The languid century (19th c.) - nervous exhaustion and the Victorian invalid: lethargy becomes psychological, a diagnosis of the over-civilised"
- "Epidemic and mass politics (20th c.) - encephalitis lethargica (1917-26); 'the lethargy of the masses' becomes a standing complaint of democracy and revolutionary rhetoric"
- "Energy economics today (21st c.) - lethargic markets, metabolic slumps, screen-fatigue: the word now measures how much energy a body, or an economy, refuses to spend"
