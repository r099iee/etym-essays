FOILED is the past tense and past participle of the verb foil — "to prevent (a plan or attempt) from succeeding, to frustrate, to defeat" — used both in the passive voice ("the raid was foiled") and as a participial adjective ("a foiled ambition"). It implies that an attempt has been actively intercepted and brought to nothing by an adversary or by circumstances: the failure is not accidental but engineered, the result of someone or something getting in the way.

Primary sense

  • Thwarted, frustrated, defeated: "the escape was foiled", "foiled by a broken lock". The word commonly appears in crime and security reporting — "foiled robbery", "foiled bomb plot", "foiled kidnap attempt" — where it stresses that a planned act was stopped before or during its execution.
  • Register: formal-to-literary; a staple of headlines and narrative prose, it carries a theatrical flavour ("Curses! Foiled again!") inherited from Victorian melodrama, where villains routinely lament being foiled at the last instant.

Secondary senses and near-homonyms (crucial to distinguish)

  • Fencing — the foil: a blunted practice sword; the noun is of debated origin but the phrase "foil a thrust" (to parry and turn aside an attack) helped push the verb toward its modern sense of "defeat an attack".
  • Metal leaf — a foil: a thin sheet of metal (tinfoil, aluminium foil), from Latin folium "leaf". This is a different word entirely, but it generated the sense "a person or thing that sets off another by contrast" (a dramatic foil), from the practice of placing dark foil behind a jewel to make it shine.
  • Hunting: in medieval and Renaissance hunting terminology, to "foil" meant to tread down or spoil an animal's scent-trail; the noun "foil" meant "the track or scent of a hunted animal". This hunting sense is the direct ancestor of the modern verb.
  • Archaic — to blunt: "foil" once meant "to dull or blunt" (a blade), a sense that overlaps with the fencing history and reinforces the trampling-to-defeating bridge.

Semantic bridge

  • The chain runs: trampling cloth (the fuller's work) → trampling a scent-trail (hunting) → crushing a scheme (warfare and drama) → "foiled" as the standard word for an intercepted plot.
  • The hunting sense supplies the core image: a hound's pursuit is foiled when the scent is destroyed; by metaphor, a pursuer, a plan, or an ambition is foiled when its "trail" is cut off.
  • The fencing sense supplies the martial image: a thrust is foiled when it is turned aside; by metaphor, an attack or scheme is foiled when it is parried.

Grammar and collocations

  • Passive constructions dominate: "be foiled by/in"; participial adjective: "a foiled attempt".
  • Common collocations: "foiled attempt/plot/escape/robbery/coup"; "foiled at the last moment"; "foiled again".
  • The word is emphatic: a foiled plan is not merely unsuccessful but decisively stopped, usually by an identifiable agent — the guard, the alarm, the hero, or the lake that quenches a dragon's fire, as in the context sentence where Smaug's assault on the bridges "was foiled".
