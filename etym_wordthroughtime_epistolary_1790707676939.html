- Mid-17th Century - Technical, scholarly, and ecclesiastical register referring strictly to apostolic letters, papal epistles, or formal rhetorical correspondence.
- 18th Century - Surged to cultural prominence during the Enlightenment as the defining term for the 'epistolary novel' (exemplified by Richardson's *Pamela* and *Clarissa*, and Rousseau's *Julie*), celebrated as the peak of moral sentiment and psychological realism.
- 19th Century - Experienced a relative decline in prestige as the omniscient third-person narrator became the dominant literary convention, leading critics to view the epistolary format as clumsy or contrived, though it was revived brilliantly in Gothic suspense (e.g., Stoker's *Dracula*).
- 20th–21st Century - Broadened into multimodal and digital spheres (transmedia storytelling, email novels, recovered data logs, text message fiction), retaining its primary cultural association with immediate, visceral, first-person documentary authenticity.
