Linear derivation chain for STAG (single root word): 1. PIE *stegh- "to stick, to prick, to pierce" (reconstructed root; Pokorny *steg-), with the closely related sharp-point extension *steig- / *stig- - the probable, though not certain, ultimate source of English stag: the male deer as "the pricking animal," named for its antlers. 2. Proto-Germanic: a stage that must be posited but whose exact form is not directly attested. Old English *stagga (masculine) "male deer" survives only in place-names, and Old Norse steggr "a male animal, a male bird" (attested c. 13th c.) is the probable Scandinavian parallel that many scholars see behind the English word. 3. Middle English stagge "an adult male deer, especially the red deer" (attested c. 1200). 4. Modern English stag "adult male deer, especially the red deer," with the extended senses of male-only gathering ("stag party") and speculative investor (1840s) - STAG. Note on the debate: the Oxford English Dictionary records the origin of stag as "obscure." The Scandinavian connection (Old Norse steggr) and the membership in *stegh- "to prick" (the antlered deer as "the pricking animal") are both plausible but neither is proven; the Old English *stagga is reconstructed from place-names, not attested in surviving texts. Some scholars have alternatively connected the word to a Germanic base meaning "large" or to *stak- "a stake." These alternatives are noted but unconfirmed; the chain above states the best-supported reconstruction and flags the uncertainty explicitly.
