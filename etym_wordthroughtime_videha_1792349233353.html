- [Axial Age / Early Buddhist Era, c. 6th–4th c. BCE] - restructured from a classical monarchy into an aristocratic republic, integrating into the famous Vajjian / Vṛji republican confederacy - [perception: admired in early Buddhist and Jain scriptures as a thriving, politically sophisticated confederated republic with democratic assemblies]
- [Epic & Classical Sanskrit Period, c. 500 BCE–500 CE] - permanently enshrined in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa as the righteous, peaceful realm of Sītā's birth and the homeland of ideal spiritual kingship - [perception: venerated as a sacred, peaceful civilizational haven marked by high ethical virtue and divine romantic destiny]
- [Classical Vedānta & Scholastic Period, c. 800–1600 CE] - adopted in Advaita Vedānta and Yogic texts as the definitive technical concept for absolute spiritual liberation attained after physical death (*videha-mukti*) - [perception: transformed in philosophical terminology into the supreme state of bodiless, unconditioned spiritual emancipation]
- [Modern Era, 19th–21st c.] - preserved in historical geography, Indological scholarship, and regional identity as the ancient classical designation for the Mithila / Tirhut cultural homeland - [perception: celebrated as a glorious symbol of ancient Indian philosophical freedom, republican statecraft, and rich cultural heritage]
