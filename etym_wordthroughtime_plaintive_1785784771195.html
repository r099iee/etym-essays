- Latin planctus / plangere (conceptual source) — Grief as striking the body and wailing; "complaint" still physical and ritual.
- Old French plaintif / plaintive — Person or utterance that laments or complains; legal and emotional senses still intertwined.
- Middle English plaintif / plaintive — Borrowed senses: mournful expression; overlap with emerging legal plaintiff begins to sort domains.
- Early Modern English plaintive — Adjective stabilizes as "mournfully expressive," especially of voice and music; plaintiff monopolizes courtroom "complainer."
- 18th–19th c. plaintive — High literary fashion in poetry and novels for tender, elegiac sound-description; strong association with sensibility culture.
- 20th–21st c. plaintive — Still standard but slightly formal/literary; typical of reviews, nature writing, and fiction; rarely slang; retains soft, pleading sorrow rather than anger.
