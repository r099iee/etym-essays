• Protrude: Similar because it means standing out from a surrounding surface, like jut; differs because it is the neutral, technical term — anything may protrude with no implication of force, angle, or menace, while jut insists on an abrupt, angular thrust.
  • Project: Similar because it means extending outward from a surface; differs because project is a colourless, formal word for mere standing-out, whereas jut adds the muscular force of a violent or weighty extrusion.
  • Extend: Similar because it means reaching out from a line; differs because extend merely adds length or reach in any direction and carries no sense of rupture, angle, or attitude.
  • Overhang: Similar because it means projecting beyond a line or edge; differs because overhang specifies projection above a void, usually horizontal and often menacing, while jut can break the plane in any direction.
  • Bulge: Similar because it means extending outward from a surface; differs because a bulge is a rounded, swelling protuberance — soft where a jut is sharp, full where a jut is angular.
  • Stick out: Similar because it means standing out from a surface in plain speech; differs because stick out is the colloquial, colourless phrase, while jut is literary and charged with thrust and angle.
  • Thrust: Similar because it means pushing forward forcefully; differs because thrust is the active verb of pushing, an action performed, while jut describes the resulting state of standing out — the thrust fossilised as position.
  • Protuberance (noun): Similar because it names a thing that bulges or sticks out; differs because protuberance is the clinical noun for any such part, while 'a jut' is a sharp, angular projection of land or bone.
