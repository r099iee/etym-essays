• Hydroplaning: Similar because both mean skimming on water; differs because hydroplaning is the American term for the motoring skid, while aquaplaning is British and also keeps the old board-sport sense.
  • Water-skiing: Similar because both are towed water sports standing on a surface; differs because water-skis are narrow paired skis, while an aquaplane is a single broad board.
  • Wakeboarding: Similar because both ride towed boards on the surface; differs because wakeboarding uses bindings and tricks off the wake, while classic aquaplaning is simply towed flat holding a rope.
  • Surfing: Similar because both ride a board planing on water; differs because surfing uses wave power unaided, while aquaplaning uses motorboat tow.
  • Skimming: Similar because both glide on a surface film; differs because skimming is generic, while aquaplaning specifies water-planing physics.
  • Skidding: Similar in the motoring sense of lost grip sliding; differs because skidding covers ice, oil, or gravel, while aquaplaning is specifically on standing water.
  • Slithering: Similar because both slide slickly; differs because slithering is sinuous animal motion, while aquaplaning is flat high-speed lift.
  • Parasailing: Similar because both are towed Riviera thrill rides; differs because parasailing lifts into the air by parachute, while aquaplaning stays on the water surface.
  • Planing (nautical): Similar because both describe a hull lifting to skim; differs because planing is the general naval-architecture term, while aquaplaning names the sport or skid.
