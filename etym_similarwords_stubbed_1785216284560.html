• knock: Similar because both describe striking against something hard; differs because knocking is generic contact, often deliberate or aimed at a door, with no special link to toes or feet.
  • bang: Similar because both describe a painful impact against a fixed object; differs because a bang implies the heavier blow of a broader body part or object, not the sharp, localized jolt of a toe.
  • strike: Similar because both mean dealing a blow; differs because striking is deliberate and forceful, whereas stubbing is accidental and reserved for a toe against a fixed projection.
  • trip: Similar because both describe a foot-catching mishap on uneven ground; differs because tripping involves losing balance and falling, whereas stubbing merely strikes the toe and leaves you standing.
  • collide: Similar because both mean coming forcefully into contact; differs because a collision requires two moving bodies, while the obstacle in stubbing is fixed and still.
  • bash: Similar because both describe a hard accidental impact; differs because bashing implies greater force and a broader area, often of the shin or head rather than the toe.
  • bark: Similar because both describe injuring a limb on a hard object; differs because “to bark one's shin” scrapes the skin, while stubbing is a blunt, bruising impact rather than a scrape.
