• fetid: Similar because both adjectives describe an offensive, nauseating smell; differs because fetid is a formal, elevated Latinate term typically applied to stagnant water, industrial emissions, or clinical decay, whereas stinking is an earthy, blunt Germanic word possessing immediate visceral impact and widespread informal versatility.
  • malodorous: Similar because both mean bad-smelling; differs because malodorous is a clinical, polite, and technical euphemism preferred in scientific and formal reports, whereas stinking is direct, sensory-laden, and carries strong emotional disapproval.
  • noisome: Similar because both denote offensive smells; differs because noisome is an archaic literary term emphasizing that a smell is noxious, harmful, or unwholesome, whereas stinking focuses strictly on the overwhelming nasal revulsion of the odor itself.
  • putrid: Similar because both describe foul, sickening odors; differs because putrid specifically denotes the advanced bacterial decomposition of organic flesh and animal proteins, whereas stinking applies broadly to any foul scent (chemical fumes, sulfur, pungent weeds, sweat, or burning rubber).
  • rank: Similar because both describe heavy, disgusting smells; differs because rank emphasizes stale, stagnant, oily, or overgrown heaviness (such as rank sweat or rancid fat), while stinking highlights the sharp, piercing intensity of the odor.
  • mephitic: Similar because both describe repulsive emanations; differs because mephitic is a specialized geological and environmental term applied to poisonous, suffocating vapors arising from caves, sewers, or volcanic vents.
  • smelly: Similar because both describe the emission of an odor; differs because smelly is an informal, mild, often juvenile adjective (smelly socks, smelly cheese), whereas stinking conveys far greater intensity, disgust, and pejorative condemnation.
  • vile: Similar because both evoke profound disgust; differs because vile is primarily an ethical, aesthetic, or evaluative judgment of baseness and wickedness, whereas stinking begins in concrete olfactory revulsion.
