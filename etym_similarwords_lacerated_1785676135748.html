• TORN: Similar because both mean separated by force; differs because torn is the broad, everyday word for anything ripped or pulled apart (paper, fabric, muscle) without specifying the shape of the edge, while lacerated specifies a jagged, irregular tear made by rough violence — all lacerations are tears, but not all tears are lacerations.
  • RIPPED: Similar because both mean violently pulled apart; differs because ripped is informal and can describe clean or rough separation with any kind of edge, while lacerated is formal, medical-leaning, and insists on the ragged, splintered character of the wound.
  • MANGLED: Similar because both mean badly torn or destroyed; differs because mangled implies crushing and mutilation beyond a simple tear — something deformed, crushed, or cut to pieces — while lacerated stays closer to the idea of a jagged tearing wound, without the implication of wholesale destruction.
  • SHREDDED: Similar because both mean torn into strips or fragments; differs because shredded emphasizes reduction to small narrow pieces (shredded paper, shredded cloth), while lacerated typically describes one or several jagged wounds rather than the reduction of the whole object to shreds.
  • RENT: Similar because both mean torn apart; differs because rent is the archaic and poetic past tense of rend, now literary and solemn (rent garments in the Bible), while lacerated is the standard modern, medical-to-formal word for a jagged tearing.
  • GASHED: Similar because both mean cut deeply; differs because a gash is a deep, long, open wound, often made by a sharp or blunt cutting edge and fairly clean-edged, while a laceration is specifically ragged and torn, made by tearing rather than by a slicing force.
  • SLASHED: Similar because both mean cut open; differs because slash implies a sweeping cut with a blade, leaving a long straight opening, while lacerate implies a jagged tear with irregular edges — the blade versus the rock in the card's sentence.
  • CUT: Similar because both mean to wound by separation; differs because cut is the neutral, all-purpose word covering everything from a clean surgical incision to a scratch, while lacerated is precise about the ragged, torn, violent character of the wound — a cut is a fact, a laceration is a tearing assault.
  • WOUNDED: Similar because both mean injured; differs because wounded is a broad term for any injury, usually to a person or animal in combat or accident, while lacerated is a specific injury type describing how the wound was made (jagged tearing).
  • MAIMED: Similar because both mean seriously injured; differs because maim implies permanent disablement or loss of a limb or its function, while lacerated describes the wound itself and does not imply lasting disability — one can be lacerated and fully recover.
  • MUTILATED: Similar because both mean severely damaged; differs because mutilate implies disfiguring destruction, often of a body after death or as deliberate cruelty, while lacerate describes the act or result of jagged tearing without the implication of deliberate disfigurement.
  • TATTERED: Similar because both describe torn fabric; differs because tattered is almost exclusively about worn, torn clothing or flags hanging in rags, a state rather than an act, while lacerated can describe flesh as well as fabric and emphasizes the violent tearing act.
  • CRUSHED: Similar because both can result from rough force; differs because crushing compresses and squashes without the ragged separation of a tear, while lacerating tears and splits — a laceration is a torn wound, a crush injury is a compressed one, though the two often occur together.
