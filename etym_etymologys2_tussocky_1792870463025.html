Middle English (14th c.)
tuft
‘cluster, crest, clump’
↓
Early Modern English (1540s)
tussock
‘tuft or clump of grass’
↓
Modern English (18th c.)
tussock + -y
‘clump’ + ‘abounding in’
↓
Modern English (current)
tussocky
‘abounding in grass clumps’

Note: the step from tuft to tussock is the standard handbook suggestion, treating tussock as tuft reshaped with the diminutive suffix -ock, but the Oxford English Dictionary marks the origin of tussock as uncertain, so the earliest link is probable rather than proven, while the addition of adjectival -y is regular and fully attested.
