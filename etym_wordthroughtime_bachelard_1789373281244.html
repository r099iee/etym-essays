- Late 19th to Early 20th Century - Originates as an unassuming French regional patronymic associated with rural Champagne and postal service civil administration.
- 1930s to 1940s - Gains elite intellectual currency in Parisian academia as Gaston Bachelard publishes groundbreaking epistemological treatises challenging Cartesian positivism and Bergsonian duration, ascending to the Sorbonne professorship.
- 1950s to 1960s - Reaches the apex of literary and cultural prestige with the publication of *The Poetics of Space*, transforming the name into an international touchstone for phenomenological architecture and the psychology of intimate dwellings.
- Late 20th Century - Solidified across continental philosophy and literary theory through the endorsement of post-structuralist thinkers (Foucault, Althusser, Derrida), who canonized Bachelard's "epistemological break" as a foundational methodology.
- 21st Century - Continues as an active theoretical eponym across architectural design, eco-criticism, memory studies, and Proustian scholarship, signifying the profound dialectic between scientific rigour and poetic daydreaming.
