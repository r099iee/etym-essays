• Stub: Similar because both refer to a short, blunt remnant left after a larger piece has been removed or worn away (e.g., pencil stub vs. pencil stump); differs because stub usually implies a smaller, thinner, or more incidental fragment (like a ticket stub or cigarette stub), whereas stump typically implies a thicker, heavier, rooted, or structurally anchored base.
  • Trunk: Similar because both refer to the main woody stem of a tree; differs because the trunk is the full, upright, living vertical body of the tree reaching up toward the branches, whereas a stump is only the severed, residual bottom remnant remaining after the tree has been cut down.
  • Baffle: Similar because both mean to frustrate comprehension and leave someone unable to solve a problem; differs because baffle is a formal psychological term implying confusion caused by contradictory, deceptive, or intricate circumstances, whereas stump is a more punchy, colloquial term highlighting total cognitive stoppage.
  • Nonplus: Similar because both denote a state of being rendered utterly unable to answer or act; differs because nonplus carries a higher, literary register and specifically emphasizes shock, surprise, or awkward social perplexity alongside cognitive standstill.
  • Perplex: Similar because both indicate making someone confused by complexity; differs because perplex implies a lingering, complicated state of mental anxiety and uncertainty, whereas stump indicates hitting a solid intellectual wall where one simply possesses no answer.
  • Wicket: Similar because stumps form the primary structural component of a cricket wicket; differs because the wicket encompasses the entire tripartite apparatus including all three stumps and both balancing bails, or the playing pitch as a whole.
