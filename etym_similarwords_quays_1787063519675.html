• Wharves / wharfs: Similar because both are built landing places for loading ships; differs because a wharf may project from the shore while a quay typically runs along the bank, and "quay" carries a more formal, slightly literary tone.
  • Piers: Similar because both are waterfront structures for landing and mooring; differs because a pier usually projects into the water on piles and is often used for pleasure, ferries, or promenading rather than heavy cargo work.
  • Docks: Similar because both belong to port facilities; differs because technically a dock is the enclosed water basin (or the whole port installation), whereas a quay is the built landing edge beside the water.
  • Jetties: Similar because both are landing structures; differs because a jetty is typically a small projecting platform for ferries or a breakwater, lacking the solid waterfront scale of a quay.
  • Landings / landing stages: Similar because both are places where boats come alongside; differs because a landing is often a simple wooden platform or set of steps, while a quay is a substantial engineered structure.
  • Berths: Similar because both name where a vessel ties up; differs because a berth is the specific mooring space a ship occupies, not the built structure itself.
  • Moorings: Similar because both concern securing boats; differs because a mooring may be just an anchor point or buoy with no built platform at all.
  • Embankments: Similar because both are built water's-edge structures; differs because an embankment is a retaining wall against the water, not necessarily usable for cargo handling.
  • Key (archaic English spelling): Similar because this was the original English spelling of "quay"; differs because it is obsolete and survives mainly in place-names, while "quay" is the modern form.
