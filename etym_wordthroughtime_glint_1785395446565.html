- Old Norse loan (13th-14th c.) - "to glint" meant to gleam or sparkle, and also to cast a quick sidelong glance; a bright dart of light first heard in northern English dialects.
- 16th-17th c. - the noun "a flash or gleam" and the verb "to glance aside" coexisted; the word stayed brisk, sudden, and somewhat informal.
- 19th c. onwards - settled as a precise noun for a brief, hard flash of reflected light from a polished or metallic surface, used both for literal light and for a sudden betraying look.
- Modern register - cool, precise, slightly literary; its core is now the momentary metallic point of light, with no warmth, steadiness, or festive sparkle.
