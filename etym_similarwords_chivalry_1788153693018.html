• Gallantry: similar because both name courteous bravery, especially toward women; differs because gallantry is lighter and more social — the compliment, the gesture — while chivalry denotes the whole institution and code.
  • Knighthood: similar because both concern the knightly order; differs because knighthood is the status and degree (the ceremony of being made knight), chivalry the ethos and the class acting together.
  • Courtesy: similar because both prescribe refined, courteous conduct; differs because courtesy is general good manners anywhere, while chivalry is the specifically martial-aristocratic code with honour at its centre.
  • Valour / prowess: similar because the knight’s courage is the heart of the code; differs because valour is a personal quality, while chivalry is the full social institution in which valour is one duty among many.
  • Courtly love (fin’amor): similar because the chivalric ideal and the cult of romantic love grew together in the same French courts; differs because courtly love is the amorous liturgy addressed to the lady, whereas chivalry is the whole ethical-military system that contained it.
  • Honour: similar because the knight’s honour is what chivalry polices; differs because honour is the abstract commodity — reputation, word, renown — while chivalry is the concrete code that earns and spends it.
  • Cavalier: similar because both descend from the same horse-word and denote the mounted gentleman; differs in historical accent — cavalier names the Royalist horseman of the English Civil War and carries a swaggering, careless note, chivalry is the courtly ideal.
