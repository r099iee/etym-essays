- 14th to 15th Century - Coined in Late Middle English as *gunpoudre* following the introduction of cannons into European siege warfare (e.g., at the Battle of Crécy, 1346); initially regarded as terrifying alchemical witchcraft or "devil's dust."
- 16th to 17th Century - Becomes the supreme instrument of global military conquest and centralized state power; indelibly burned into British national memory following the thwarted Gunpowder Plot of 1605.
- 18th to 19th Century - Standardized industrially through "corning" processes for mass naval cannonades, mining excavation, and Victorian civil pyrotechnics (as featured in Oscar Wilde's 1888 fairy tale); metaphorical idioms ("powder keg", "dry powder") saturate political discourse.
- Late 19th to 20th Century - Replaced in military small arms and naval artillery by smokeless nitrocellulose powders and cordite; relegated primarily to historic reenactments, mining safety fuses, and commercial fireworks displays.
- 21st Century - Continues as an evocative literary and historical metaphor for explosive socio-political instability, revolutionary upheaval, and the technological dawn of mechanized warfare.
