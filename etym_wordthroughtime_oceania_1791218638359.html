- 1812–1850: Cartographer's neologism — Océanie / Oceania as a fifth part of the world in atlases; learned, French-tinged, competing with Australasia.
- 1850–1920: Ethnological culture area — the natives of Oceania in evolutionist anthropology; houses, canoes and custom compared stage by stage.
- 1920–1950: Colonial administrative mosaic — British, French, German, American Oceanic possessions; maps colour-coded by empire.
- 1949: Orwellian hijack — Oceania as totalitarian superstate in Nineteen Eighty-Four; literary shadow over the geographic term.
- 1950–2000: Decolonised region — independent island states, UN Oceania statistical division, Oceania sport federations; neutral continental label.
- Present: Celebrated and contested — embraced in Pacific regionalism and rugby, questioned in decolonial curricula favouring Pacific or Moana; climate-vulnerable frontline identity.
