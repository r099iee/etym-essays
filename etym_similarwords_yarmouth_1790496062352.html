• Great Yarmouth: Similar because it is the full official form of the same town; differs because Great is formal and disambiguating, while Yarmouth alone is colloquial and ambiguous with the Wight port.
  • Lowestoft: Similar because it is the neighbouring Suffolk herring port and rival; differs because Lowestoft is south across the county border and claims the most easterly point, while Yarmouth guards the Yare mouth.
  • King's Lynn: Similar because it is another historic Norfolk Wash port; differs because Lynn stands on the Ouse and traded corn and wool, while Yarmouth traded herring.
  • Hull (Kingston upon Hull): Similar because it is the great Humber port of Crusoe's embarkation; differs because Hull is a city and deep-water estuary port, while Yarmouth is a spit haven and fishing station.
  • Grimsby: Similar because it became the later east-coast fishing capital; differs because Grimsby is Humber-side and trawler-based, while Yarmouth is drift-net herring and resort.
  • Yarmouth, Isle of Wight: Similar because it shares the identical Yar-plus-mouth formation; differs because it is a tiny Solent ferry town on the Western Yar, while Norfolk Yarmouth is a large North Sea port.
  • Gorleston (Little Yarmouth): Similar because it faces Great Yarmouth across the Yare; differs because Gorleston is the southern suburb and former separate port, while Yarmouth proper is north of the river.
  • Norwich: Similar because it is the county capital linked to Yarmouth by the Yare; differs because Norwich is inland, cathedral and textile, while Yarmouth is coastal and maritime.
  • Blakeney and Cley: Similar because they are other Norfolk sailing and fishing villages; differs because they are small north-coast creeks, while Yarmouth is the county's great harbour.
  • The Broads: Similar because the Yare flows through them to Yarmouth; differs because the Broads are inland lagoons and holiday waterways, while Yarmouth is the sea gate where they meet the North Sea.
