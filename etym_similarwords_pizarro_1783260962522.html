• Cortes: Similar because both are Spanish conquistadors who destroyed great American empires and are paired in the same sentence by Macaulay; differs because Cortes is the emblem of conquest with strategy — alliances and self-justification — while Pizarro stands for plain rapacity and extraction.
  • Conquistador: Similar because Pizarro is the archetypal conquistador and the name can stand for the whole class; differs because conquistador is the generic Spanish class noun covering hundreds of soldiers, while Pizarro names one specific, notorious individual.
  • Conqueror: Similar in meaning one who subdues a people or realm by force; differs because conqueror is the broad, neutral, non-Spanish word applicable to all times and places, whereas Pizarro carries the specific historical and moral freight of the Peruvian conquest.
  • Adventurer: Similar because 16th-century sources describe Pizarro as an adventurer, a fortune-hunter licensed by royal contract; differs because modern adventurer has lost the predatory, commercial meaning and now suggests romantic risk-taking.
  • Colonizer: Similar because Pizarro extended European dominion to the New World; differs because colonizer implies settlement and lasting rule, while Pizarro's enterprise was seizure and extraction without building durable institutions.
  • Explorer: Similar as a figure of the European age of discovery; differs because explorer implies the advance of knowledge, cartography, and science, none of which Pizarro's expedition pursued.
  • Caudillo: Similar in denoting a Spanish-speaking military strongman who seizes power by force; differs because caudillo is a modern Latin American political term for a post-independence strongman, not a 16th-century royal captain.
  • Imperialist: Similar in denoting an agent of empire; differs because imperialist is an abstract modern political label, while Pizarro is a concrete historical person whose name became a moral archetype.
