- Late 18th century (Kant, Critique of Pure Reason, via German Noumenon) - introduced as a strict philosophical coinage: the thing-in-itself, thinkable by pure intellect yet unknowable through the senses.
- 19th century (British idealism: Coleridge, later Bradley) - adopted as an exotic, formidable term for ultimate reality beyond appearance in idealist philosophy and literary reflection.
- 20th century (literary criticism and aesthetics: Empson) - metaphorically extended: the ideal, mind-constructed object of a work of art as opposed to its sensory surface.
- Present day - remains a learned philosophical term for hidden reality; in general usage a rare, rarified word carrying Kantian and quasi-mystical weight.
