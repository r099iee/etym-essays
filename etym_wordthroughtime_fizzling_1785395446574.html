- [17th-century onomatopoeia - the hiss] - 'Fizz' was coined for the hissing sound of gas or liquid, imitative of escaping air; 'fizzle' named the weak, sputtering hiss of something damp burning.
- [Weak, failing flame (19th c.)] - 'Fizzling' settled as a description of a candle or torch that spits and sputters with uncertain light — fire so damp it can barely serve its own purpose.
- [To fail utterly - 'fizzle out' (19th c. American)] - The phrase 'fizzle out' became the dominant sense: any plan, career, or enterprise that sputters and dies without ever succeeding, draining the word of fire entirely.
- [Modern comic-deflationary register] - Today 'fizzling' is almost always deflationary, a damp squib: its sibilant onset mimics water meeting flame, its suffix (-ling) shrinks the action to something small, repeated, and faintly pathetic, and every 'fizzled' venture quietly carries the joke of a torch that cannot light the night.
