- [Medieval-Renaissance prestige] - Embroidery is the needleworked ornament of church vestments, coronation robes and heraldic banners - a costly, quasi-sacred craft of the highest rank.
- [16th-17th century figurative birth] - Alongside the textile sense, 'to embroider' begins to mean embellishing a story - gilding truth with added detail - a metaphor that survives today.
- [18th-19th century domestic turn] - Embroidery becomes the quintessential feminine accomplishment of the parlour - samplers, house linens and ornamental dress - genteel, virtuous and taught to girls.
- [Contemporary register] - Both the handcraft (and its machine-made imitation) of decorative stitchery and, figuratively, the act of embellishing facts; the concrete sense now ranges from folk craft to fine art.
