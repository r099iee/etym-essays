Proto-Romance / Vulgar Latin (c. 5th–7th c.)
*murr-
"snout, muzzle, pouted face"
↓
Old French (c. 14th c.)
morgue
"haughty countenance, grimace, proud stare"
↓
Middle French (c. 15th c.)
morguer
"to stare fixedly, inspect haughtily or closely"
↓
Early Modern French (Parisian Slang) (c. 1600)
la morgue
"prison intake vestibule for scrutinizing inmates"
↓
French (Administrative Law) (1804)
la Morgue
"public building for identifying dead bodies"
↓
English (c. 1821)
morgue
"public mortuary; body storage; dead archive"

Note: The semantic trajectory from 'pouted snout' to 'mortuary' is uniquely Parisian. Turnkeys at the Grand Châtelet prison scrutinized (morguaient) incoming prisoners in a special room (morgue). In the 18th century, unidentified cadavers were deposited in that same room so passersby could stare at their faces for identification, permanently shifting the noun from a scrutinizing gaze to the room of the dead.
