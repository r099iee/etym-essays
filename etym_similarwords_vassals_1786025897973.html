• Retainers: Similar because both serve a lord; differs because retainers are household servants or armed followers in personal service, whereas vassals held land through formal feudal contracts of homage.
  • Subjects: Similar because both owe allegiance to a ruler; differs because subjects applies to all citizens under a crown, while vassals specifically holds land under feudal tenure with military obligations.
  • Subordinates: Similar because both are lower in rank; differs because subordinate is a neutral, modern organizational term, while vassal carries historical feudal weight and connotations of oath and honor.
  • Puppets: Similar because both lack independent authority; differs because puppet implies covert manipulation from behind the scenes, whereas vassal implies open, legal allegiance and obligation.
  • Serfs: Similar because both are feudal subordinates; differs because serfs were unfree peasants bound to the soil and owing labor, whereas vassals were free nobles holding land and owing military service.
  • Clients: Similar because both describe dependent relationships with a patron; differs because client is a modern political or commercial term, while vassal is bound by oath and land tenure.
  • Dependents: Similar because both rely on a superior; differs because dependent implies economic or emotional reliance, while vassal implies a formal, reciprocal feudal contract.
  • Henchmen: Similar because both are followers who do a master's bidding; differs because henchmen are low, unscrupulous thugs, while vassals are noble retainers bound by oath and honor.
  • Liegemen: Similar because both are sworn feudal followers; differs because liegemen specifically owe supreme liege homage to one paramount lord, while vassals can hold from several lords in a hierarchy.
