Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*burþiniz / *burþinnō
"that which is borne, load"
↓
Old English (before 9th c.)
byrðen
"load, weight; affliction"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
burdene
"load, burden, trouble"
↓
Modern English (present)
BURDENS
"plural: loads carried; weights of care"

Note: The word descends from the Germanic root for "to bear" (cf. bear, birth, burden are all related to the verb bear "to carry"), which continues an Indo-European root *bher- "to carry." The Germanic chain is secure; the PIE link via *bher- is standard but, as with all PIE reconstructions, is a scholarly reconstruction.
