Chain for NERVA (single root word, the Latin cognomen): 1. PIE *sneh₁- / *sneu- — reconstructed root meaning "to twist, spin, wind" (the verbal idea behind sinew, tendon, and cords); attested indirectly through its many descendants across Indo-European (uncertain stage, reconstructable but not directly attested). 2. Proto-Italic / early Latin *snēu- type — the Italic reflex of the PIE root developing toward the noun for a twisted cord or sinew (reconstructed; not directly attested). 3. Latin nervus — "sinew, tendon, nerve, bowstring, cord"; attested throughout Latin literature from the 2nd century BC onward; by extension "vigour, strength, force" (of body, mind, or style). Note: the initial cluster was reanalyzed/lentied within Latin; the -er- spelling is the standard Latin form. 4. Latin Nerva — cognomen formed from nervus, meaning "sinewy, vigorous"; attested as a Roman family name, notably in the gens Cocceia, before the future emperor's time (various bearers attested 1st century BC–1st century AD). 5. Marcus Cocceius Nerva — the future emperor's full name (praenomen Marcus, nomen Cocceius from gens Cocceia, cognomen Nerva); born probably AD 30–35. 6. Emperor Nerva — ruled AD 96–98 after Domitian's assassination (AD 96); adopted Trajan in AD 97; his reign inaugurates the Nerva–Antonine era. 7. English NERVA — the conventional English form, used by Gibbon and modern historiography, and the note's rootWord. Debated/nuanced note: The PIE reconstruction *sneh₁- is standard in handbooks, but the exact details of the Italic development (loss of the initial s- or laryngeal treatment, the -erv- vs. -eu- vowel pattern) are debated; some scholars treat Latin nervus as borrowed or reshaped under Sabine influence. The emperor's name, however, is securely the cognomen derived from the common noun nervus "sinew, vigour," so the chain's core is solid even where fine-grained phonetic detail is disputed.
