• Cynical: Similar because both denote a refusal to take claims and sincerity at face value; differs because a cynic assumes people act from self-interest and regards sincerity itself as a pose, while a sceptic merely withholds assent until evidence is adequate, without imputing bad faith.
  • Doubtful: Similar because both express uncertainty; differs because doubtful is weaker and more passive — simple uncertainty about a fact — while sceptical denotes a settled habit of demanding rigorous proof.
  • Incredulous: Similar because both involve unwillingness to believe; differs because incredulous describes the state of not being able to believe something astonishing, carrying surprise, whereas sceptical is a deliberate, principled disposition.
  • Disbelieving: Similar because both refuse to accept a claim; differs because disbelieving asserts a positive conclusion that something is false, while sceptical merely withholds assent pending sufficient evidence.
  • Suspicious: Similar because both involve mistrust; differs because suspicious fixes on hidden motives and wrongdoing, imputing guilt or concealment, while scepticism is aimed at the truth-value of claims rather than at people's character.
  • Dubious: Similar because both hover between doubt and distrust; differs because dubious is milder, more colloquial, and often attached to a specific proposition ('dubious value'), whereas sceptical is a broader, more systematic attitude.
  • Distrustful: Similar because both imply withholding confidence; differs because distrustful names a settled lack of faith in a person or institution, while sceptical names intellectual doubt about a claim's truth.
  • Questioning: Similar because both refuse passive acceptance; differs because questioning describes the active act of inquiry and can be open and constructive, while sceptical implies a leaning toward doubt of what is presented.
  • Unconvinced: Similar because both withhold belief; differs because unconvinced is the neutral state of not yet being persuaded, while sceptical implies an active, principled resistance to persuasion.
  • Agnostic: Similar in holding back from commitment to belief; differs because agnostic is used chiefly of religious or metaphysical claims and asserts the unknowability of the question, while sceptical is a general doubt applied to any claim.
