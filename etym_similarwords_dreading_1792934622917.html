• Fearing: Similar because both name apprehension of coming harm; differs because fearing is the plain general verb (fearing the worst), while dreading stresses prolonged anticipation — fear stretched across waiting time.
  • Anticipating: Similar because both look forward to what is coming; differs because anticipating is neutral (one anticipates good news too), while dreading anticipates only evil, real or tedious.
  • Awaiting: Similar because both involve the wait before an event; differs because awaiting is neutral and formal (awaiting trial), while dreading adds the cold flinch — the wait itself hurts.
  • Apprehending: Similar because apprehension is exactly fear-before-the-fact; differs because apprehending is formal and slightly clinical, while dreading is the everyday word with the sinking stomach in it.
  • Despairing: Similar because both are gloomy states before bad outcomes; differs because despairing has given up hope after the blow, while dreading still waits for it — despair is post, dread is pre.
  • Trembling: Similar because both are bodily displays of fear; differs because trembling is the physical shake in the moment, while dreading is the mental rehearsal beforehand — one may dread for weeks without a single tremor.
  • Shuddering: Similar because both flinch at imagined horror; differs because a shudder is a sudden spasm at the thought, while dreading is the long continuous state in which such shudders arrive.
  • Regretting: Similar because both are painful attitudes to unwelcome things; differs because regretting looks backward at what is done, while dreading looks forward at what is coming — the two divide time between them.
  • Loathing: Similar because one may dread a meeting one also loathes; differs because loathing is hatred and disgust toward the thing itself, while dreading is fear of meeting it — disgust versus anticipation.
