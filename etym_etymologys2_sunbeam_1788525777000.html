=== ELEMENT 1: SUN ===
PIE (reconstructed)
*sóh₂wl̥ / *seh₂u-l-
"the sun, solar celestial body"
(heteroclitic r/n stem; ancestor of Greek hēlios, Latin sōl, Sanskrit sūrya)
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*sunnǭ / *sōwul-
"sun"
↓
Old English (pre-10th c.)
sunne
"the sun, heavenly source of light and heat"
↓
Middle English (12th–14th c.)
sunne / sonne
"sun"
↓
Modern English (base noun)
sun
"the central star of our solar system; sunlight"

↓
=== ELEMENT 2: BEAM ===
PIE (reconstructed)
*bʰew- / *bʰuH-
"to grow, thrive, become, exist"
(root of words for living plants, growth, and building structures; cf. English 'be')
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*baumaz
"tree, growing trunk, wooden post"
(cognate with German Baum 'tree', Dutch boom)
↓
Old English (pre-10th c.)
bēam
"living tree, wooden post, timber; radiant column / ray of light"
(shifted semantically from 'tree trunk/post' to 'column/shaft of radiant light')
↓
Middle English (12th–14th c.)
beem / beam
"structural timber; shaft/ray of light"
↓
Modern English (base noun)
beam
"timber beam; directed ray of light"

↓
=== COMPOUND SYNTHESIS ===
Old English (c. 9th c.)
sunnebēam
"shaft of sunlight, solar ray"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
sunnebem / sonne-beme
"sunbeam, ray of sunlight"
↓
Modern English
sunbeam
"a visible ray of sunlight; a bright, joyful person"

Note: The word sunbeam is an authentic native Germanic compound dating back over a thousand years to Anglo-Saxon England. Its second element, beam, preserves a fascinating semantic shift: from Proto-Germanic *baumaz ("tree, trunk", preserved in German Baum), Old English speakers conceived a radiant vertical shaft of light as a straight, sturdy "tree trunk" or "pillar" of illumination.
