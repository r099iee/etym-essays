• tele- (combining form): Similar because it is the exact morpho-syntactic prefix from which the standalone colloquial unigram is clipped; differs because the bound morpheme tele- requires a root stem (e.g., telescope, telegraph) and carries precise functional spatial semantics, while tele (or telly) is a free-standing colloquial noun or industry jargon.
  • distant: Similar because both express significant separation in physical or conceptual space; differs because distant is a standalone native Germanic/Romance descriptive adjective denoting location or demeanor, whereas tele functions primarily as a technological prefix or shorthand.
  • remote: Similar because both describe operating or existing at a distance; differs because remote emphasizes seclusion, isolation, or indirect electronic control (e.g., remote control), whereas tele designates distance-bridging transmission.
  • far-off: Similar because both denote great distance; differs because far-off is an evocative, literary compound adjective typically modifying exotic places, distant lands, or past memories, lacking the systematic scientific utility of tele-.
  • long-range: Similar because both indicate operating over extensive expanses; differs because long-range is an adjective applied to weapons, forecasts, strategies, or aircraft capable of traveling across vast distances without refueling or reaiming.
  • telecommunications: Similar because tele is universally used as the corporate and industrial clipping for the telecommunications sector (e.g. telecoms); differs because telecommunications is the full formal academic and industrial noun describing the electronic transmission of signals and data.
  • telly: Similar because both are informal clippings of television; differs because telly is the dominant, highly colloquial British, Australian, and Irish vernacular spelling and pronunciation, whereas tele is the standard classical Greek root form and continental clipping.
