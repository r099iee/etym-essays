- [15th-century 'bring under yoke'] - In its current form since Middle English (from Latin subjugare, 'to bring under the yoke'), 'subjugated' has meant reduced to complete subjection - harnessed like a team of oxen.
- [Colonial and imperial use] - In 18th and 19th century prose (as in Macaulay) it described the total conquest of peoples - a nation subjugated by Roman arms - the heavy, thorough word of empire.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'subjugated' still means completely conquered and held in subjection, its yoke metaphor still felt, used both of peoples and figuratively of wills, desires and minds.
