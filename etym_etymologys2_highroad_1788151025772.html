ELEMENT 1 — HIGH (derivation chain)
↓
PIE(c. 4000–3000 BCE)
*kowk- / *kewk-
"hill, height; to bend, to arch"
↓
Proto-Germanic(c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*hauha-
"high, lofty, elevated"
↓
Old English(c. 8th–11th c. CE)
hēah
"high, tall, lofty; exalted, chief"
↓
Middle English(12th–15th c. CE)
heigh / high
"high; chief, principal"
↓
high

ELEMENT 2 — ROAD (derivation chain)
↓
PIE(c. 4000–3000 BCE)
*reidh-
"to ride; to travel in a vehicle"
↓
Proto-Germanic(c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*raidō
"a riding, a journey, an expedition"
↓
Old English(c. 9th c. CE)
rād
"a riding, a journey, a hostile incursion; a course"
↓
Middle English(12th–15th c. CE)
rode / rood / road
"a riding-way; a place for riding; a public way"
↓
road(sense "public way" by late 14th c.)

COMPOUND FORMATION
↓
English(attested from the 17th c.)
high-road
"the chief public road; a principal route"
↓
highroad

Note on nuance: the PIE base behind high is reconstructed as *kowk-/*kewk- "hill, height," but the exact ablaut grade is debated among comparative linguists; the Germanic stage *hauha- and the Old English hēah are securely attested. The figurative sense "a sure or principal course to a result" is an English metaphorical extension of the 17th–19th centuries, not a separately inherited word; the compound itself is transparently high + road.
