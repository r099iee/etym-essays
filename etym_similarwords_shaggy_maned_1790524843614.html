• Thick-maned: Similar because both indicate an abundance of dense hair growing along the neck crest; differs because thick-maned describes volume and density without necessarily implying the unkempt, rough, or weather-tangled texture denoted by "shaggy."
  • Rough-maned: Similar because both emphasize a coarse, harsh, or uneven hair texture; differs because rough-maned highlights the tactile coarseness or bristle of the hair, whereas shaggy-maned specifically highlights long, matted, tufted, and hanging locks.
  • Wild-maned: Similar because both evoke an untamed, free, and natural appearance; differs because wild-maned carries a stronger romantic or psychological overtone of fierce, untamed temperament, whereas shaggy-maned is primarily an objective physical description of hair length and condition.
  • Unkempt-haired: Similar because both describe hair that has not been combed, clipped, or groomed; differs because unkempt carries a mildly pejorative connotation of neglect, messiness, or untidiness, whereas shaggy-maned is an evocative, naturalistic, and often majestic descriptor.
  • Hirsute: Similar because both denote extreme hairiness or a heavy covering of hair; differs because hirsute is a formal, Latinate, and clinical term applied to generalized body hair on humans or plants, lacking the specific equestrian and zoological focus on neck crest hair.
  • Bushy-crested: Similar because both depict thick, protruding tufts of hair along an animal's upper ridge; differs because bushy implies dense, spreading fullness (like a fox's tail or a thicket), whereas shaggy emphasizes hanging, coarse, and jagged strands.
  • Bristly-maned: Similar because both characterize coarse neck hair; differs because bristly specifies short, stiff, prickling hairs standing erect (like a wild boar's hogged crest), whereas shaggy-maned indicates long, drooping, tufted hair.
  • Tousled: Similar because both describe hair in a state of disarray; differs because tousled suggests soft, lightly ruffled hair (as from a gentle breeze or sleep), while shaggy implies a heavier, thicker, and more enduringly rugged texture.
  • Rugged-coated: Similar because both convey a durable, weather-hardened animal exterior; differs because rugged-coated applies to the entire pelt or fleece of an animal, whereas shaggy-maned focuses specifically on the elongated crest hair of the neck.
  • Disheveled-haired: Similar because both portray untidy, disordered locks; differs because disheveled focuses on human disarray or emotional fluster, whereas shaggy-maned is a natural zoological and aesthetic descriptor.
