• Attack: Similar because both denote aggressive action against a target; differs because attack is the general word for any aggression — a blow, a campaign, or a piece of criticism — while onslaught insists on overwhelming mass and momentum rather than a single blow.
  • Assault: Similar because both mean a violent attack; differs because assault is more formal and legal, a direct physical attack on a person or position, while onslaught emphasizes the sheer crushing weight of the aggression pressing forward.
  • Charge: Similar because both involve attacking; differs because a charge is a headlong onrush by troops, cavalry, or animals, a forward motion into contact, whereas an onslaught is the whole weight pressing in upon a stationary victim.
  • Barrage: Similar because both denote concentrated, sustained fire; differs because a barrage is specifically a curtain of continuous artillery fire, while an onslaught may be of anything — armies, criticism, advertising, or rocks.
  • Blitz: Similar because both denote intense, overwhelming attacks; differs because a blitz (from German for "lightning") stresses suddenness and rapidity, while an onslaught stresses overwhelming mass and inevitability.
  • Raid: Similar because both are attacks; differs because a raid is a sudden, brief incursion for a limited purpose, while an onslaught is heavy, sustained, and destructive.
  • Onset: Similar because both can mean an attack or the beginning of something unpleasant; differs because onset is older, more formal, and now often medical or natural (the onset of winter), while onslaught remains aggressively violent.
  • Deluge: Similar because both denote an overwhelming, inescapable quantity pressing in (an onslaught/deluge of criticism); differs because a deluge is properly a flood of liquid or of sheer quantity, while an onslaught is an attack, keeping its aggressive, martial force.
