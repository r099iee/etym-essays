• Slanders: Similar because both denote unlawful defamation that damages a person’s reputation; differs because slanders are oral, spoken, and transitory, whereas libels are fixed in permanent, visible media such as print, digital text, or broadcasts.
  • Defamations: Similar because both cover injury to character and good standing; differs because defamation is the broad umbrella legal category encompassing both written and oral offenses, while libels refers specifically to written or published defamatory publications.
  • Lampoons: Similar because both involve published attacks on individuals; differs because a lampoon relies on biting satire, caricature, and ridicule for artistic or comic effect, whereas libels are characterized by their defamatory falsehood and actionable legal harm.
  • Calumnies: Similar because both denote false and malicious accusations; differs because calumny emphasizes the deliberate, venomous fabrication of lies intended to destroy character, regardless of whether the medium is written or spoken.
  • Pasquinades: Similar because both represent satirical public attacks; differs because a pasquinade is specifically an anonymous squib, lampoon, or posted public placard (historically affixed to the statue of Pasquino in Rome), usually brief and witty.
  • Squibs: Similar because both are sharp public attacks in print; differs because a squib is a brief, fiery, sarcastic paragraph or pamphlet designed for immediate passing notice, whereas libels can be extensive, grave, and legally devastating treatises.
  • Vilifications: Similar because both seek to degrade someone’s public standing; differs because vilification focuses on the psychological act and rhetoric of making a person appear vile and contemptible, while libels designates the concrete published instruments of defamation.
  • Invectives: Similar because both express severe verbal hostility; differs because invective consists of passionate, denunciatory, and abusive language or railing, without necessarily asserting false facts that damage legal reputation.
  • Diatribes: Similar because both deliver scathing criticism; differs because a diatribe is an extended, bitter, and angry rant or lecture attacking an ideology or person, lacking the legal framework and falsity of a libel.
  • Aspersions: Similar because both cast doubt upon reputation; differs because aspersions are damaging remarks, insinuations, or derogatory slurs, often subtle and indirect, whereas libels are overt, published accusations.
