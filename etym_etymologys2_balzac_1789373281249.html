Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*bʰel- or *bʰelg-
"to swell, blow up; beam, bulge, protruding rock"
↓
Gaulish / Pre-Celtic Substrate (c. 6th–1st c. BCE)
*balso- / *balzo-
"steep rock, cliff, rocky escarpment, crag"
Note: Cognate with Italian balza ('cliff, precipice, ledge'). In French toponymy, this root formed regional place-names denoting rocky heights.
↓
Gallo-Roman / Late Latin (c. 4th–8th c.)
Baltiacum / Balsianum
"estate on the rocky cliff; domain of Baltius"
Note: Formed either from Celtic *balz- ('rock') or the Gallo-Roman personal name Baltius combined with the Celtic locative suffix *-acon (-acum).
↓
Old Occitan (c. 11th–14th c.)
balsa / balza
"steep cliff, crag; rocky bluff"
↓
Middle French / Regional Occitan (c. 15th–18th c.)
Balssa / Balzac
"toponymic surname in Languedoc and Charente"
Note: Honoré's father, Bernard-François Balssa (1746–1829) of peasant origin in Canezac (Tarn), altered the family spelling to the aristocratic-sounding Balzac in Paris c. 1771; Honoré added the particle 'de' in the 1830s.
↓
Modern French (1799–1850)
Honoré de Balzac
"titan of French realism and creator of La Comédie humaine"
↓
Modern English (present)
BALZAC
