• Feasts: Similar because both are large celebratory meals with abundant food; differs because feast is the older, broader word (any great meal, including religious and seasonal ones), while banquet implies a formal, ceremonial, organised dinner.
  • Dinners: Similar because a banquet is a kind of dinner; differs because dinner is the ordinary meal of any size, while banquet is the grand, public, formal extreme.
  • Galas: Similar because both are grand celebratory events; differs because a gala may involve no meal at all (opera gala, charity gala), while a banquet is defined by the meal.
  • Receptions: Similar because both are formal social gatherings; differs because a reception is often standing, with drinks and canapés rather than a seated multi-course meal.
  • Buffets: Similar because both offer a lavish spread of food; differs because a buffet is self-service and informal, while a banquet is served and ceremonial.
  • Spreads: Similar because a spread is a plentiful display of food; differs because spread is colloquial and informal, with no formality or ritual.
  • State dinners: Similar because a state dinner is a formal banquet held by a head of state; differs because it is the specific, protocol-bound sub-type for visiting dignitaries.
  • Symposiums / Symposia: Similar because the Greek symposium was a drinking-feast; differs because the word now means a scholarly conference and has lost its food-and-drink sense.
  • Convivia: Similar because the Roman convivium was a banquet; differs because the word is historical or archaeological jargon for Roman feasts, not used for modern ones.
  • Carousals / Revels: Similar because both involve boisterous feasting and drinking; differs because they stress noise and excess rather than formal hospitality.
  • Repasts: Similar because a repast is a meal, often substantial; differs because it is archaic or literary and can refer to any meal, not necessarily grand.
