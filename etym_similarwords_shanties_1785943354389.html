• CHANTEY / CHANTY: Similar because they are the same word as the song sense of "shanty," the original spelling of the sailor's song; differs because chantey/chanty keep the French spelling that shows the source (chanter "to sing") and are used especially in nautical and American writing, while "shanty" is the modern dominant spelling used for both the song and the hut.
  • SEA SONG: Similar because both name songs of the sea and sailors; differs because a sea song is any song about the sea or sung by sailors, while a shanty is specifically a rhythmic work song designed to coordinate physical labour — hauling, pumping, heaving — with its call-and-response structure.
  • WORK SONG: Similar because both are songs sung to accompany and coordinate work; differs because a work song is the general category (songs of field hands, chain gangs, rowers), while a shanty is the specifically nautical type used aboard sailing ships for group hauling and winding.
  • SAILOR SONG: Similar because both describe songs of the sailor's world; differs because "sailor song" is a general descriptive phrase with no fixed form, while a shanty has a specific musical structure (solo shantyman + answering chorus) and a specific work function.
  • BALLAD: Similar because both are traditional narrative or communal songs; differs because a ballad is a narrative song telling a story (often of love or adventure), sung for entertainment, while a shanty is a functional work song whose rhythm drives labour and whose words are secondary to the task.
  • HUT: Similar because both denote a small, roughly built dwelling, and a shanty is a type of hut; differs because "hut" is the general word for a small simple shelter of any kind, while "shanty" specifies a crude, makeshift, often squalid dwelling, typically built of scrap by the poor or by workers in remote camps.
  • SHACK: Similar because both denote a crudely built, often dilapidated dwelling; differs because "shack" is the everyday American word for a rough small house, while "shanty" carries a stronger note of the makeshift hut of the poor or of the lumber camp, and also the wholly separate musical meaning.
  • HOVEL: Similar because both denote a miserable, crude dwelling; differs because "hovel" emphasizes squalor and poverty more strongly, with a tone of pity or contempt, while "shanty" is more neutral and can even carry a romantic or historical colour (the lumberjack's shanty, the old shantytown).
  • CABIN: Similar because both are small, simple dwellings, and in lumbering a cabin and a shanty can be near-synonyms; differs because "cabin" is a more solid, respectable small house (a log cabin) or a room on a ship or aircraft, without the "makeshift, impermanent, scrap-built" connotation of "shanty."
  • SHED: Similar because both are small rough buildings; differs because a shed is a storage or workshop building, not a dwelling, while a shanty is a dwelling, however humble, and also the sea song.
  • LEAN-TO: Similar because both denote rough, simple shelters; differs because a lean-to is specifically a shelter whose roof leans against a wall or larger building, a structural type, while a shanty is any crude makeshift dwelling, built of whatever is at hand.
  • SLUM DWELLING / TENEMENT: Similar because both describe the housing of the urban poor, and shanties cluster in shantytowns as tenements cluster in slums; differs because a tenement is a multi-storey rented apartment block, built of brick, while a shanty is a one-room hut of scrap material; the shantytown is the informal, self-built settlement that the tenement slum is not.
  • FOLK SONG: Similar because both are traditional songs passed down among a people, and shanties are a type of folk song; differs because a folk song is the general category of traditional song, sung for pleasure and community, while a shanty is a specific occupational song of sailors, tied to work rhythms and the sea.
  • CAROL / CHANT: Similar because both are forms of communal singing with repeated patterns; differs because a carol is a song of joy, especially Christmas, and a chant is a liturgical or monotone recitation, while a shanty is the sailors' rhythmic work song with its hauling chorus.
