- c. 1600–1650 - [Galenic Physiology & Pathology] - Pertaining directly to the four cardinal bodily fluids (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile); regarded as the definitive, unchallenged scientific explanation for physical disease, somatic health, and constitution.
- c. 1650–1750 - [Literary & Psychological Drift] - Associated with psychological dispositions, constitutional idiosyncrasies, and dramatic character types governed by internal fluid dominance; semantic center began shifting from literal anatomy to subjective mood and temperament.
- c. 1750–1850 - [Archaic Prescientific Relic] - Increasingly perceived as an obsolete, prescientific medieval superstition as Morgagni's organ pathology, Bichat's tissue pathology, and Virchow's cellular pathology dismantled fluid-based medical paradigms.
- c. 1890–1920 - [Immunological Renaissance] - Rescued and rehabilitated by Behring, Kitasato, and Ehrlich to designate soluble antitoxins and antibodies circulating in cell-free blood serum, initiating the great historical debate against cellular immunity.
- c. 1950–Present - [Modern Molecular Immunology & Physiology] - Fully established as a premier biomedical technical term denoting B-cell-mediated antibody synthesis and systemic non-neural fluid regulation, while preserving an indelible historical connection to classical medicine.
