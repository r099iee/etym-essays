- Early Medieval era (8th–11th c.) — Germanic personal name Razo / Rätzo (derived from Proto-Germanic *rēdaz, "counsel, wisdom") merged with Old High German hof ("farmstead, estate") to name Bavarian agricultural settlements like Ratzenhof and Ratzenhofen.
- High to Late Middle Ages (12th–15th c.) — In Upper Austria and Bavaria, freeholders or dwellers of these settlements were recorded in monastic tithe books and feudal registers as Ratzenhofer (with suffix -er indicating residence or origin).
- Early Modern era (16th–18th c.) — Hereditary surname established across the Habsburg territories, Upper Austria, and Bavaria, borne by landed farmers, burghers, and local officials.
- Late 19th c. (1890s) — Achieved international intellectual fame through Gustav Ratzenhofer (1842–1904), Austrian Field Marshal Lieutenant and sociologist, whose works Wesen und Zweck der Politik (1893) and Die sociologische Erkenntnis (1898) founded modern conflict sociology.
- Early 20th c. — Imported into American academia by Albion Small (Chicago school of sociology) and Franz Oppenheimer, making Ratzenhofer a fundamental citation in theories of state origins and interest-group politics.
- Contemporary era — Studied in political sociology and the history of ideas as the classic paradigm of the conquest theory of the state and interest-driven institutional analysis.
