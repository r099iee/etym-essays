• ORAL: Similar because it is the paired bodily-sense adjective of the same Latinate type (-ālis), a near-homophone naming the mouth-channel as aural names the ear-channel, and the two are routinely confused; differs because oral means "of the mouth or speech" while aural means "of the ear or hearing" — the memorable pairing is auricle (the outer ear) versus orifice (a mouth-opening).
  • AUDITORY: Similar because both name the hearing sense and overlap heavily in meaning; differs because auditory is the more clinical word for the physiological apparatus (auditory nerve, auditory cortex, auditory perception), while aural is the broader adjective for anything of the ear or of listening (aural training, aural experience).
  • ACOUSTIC: Similar because both relate to sound; differs because acoustic belongs to the physics of the sound wave itself (acoustic properties, acoustic guitar, acoustics), independent of any listener, while aural always describes sound as received by the ear.
  • AURICULAR: Similar because both descend from Latin auris "ear" and both mean "of the ear"; differs because auricular is the strictly anatomical or ecclesiastical term (auricular region, auricular confession — literally whispered in the ear) and is older and rarer, whereas aural is the ordinary technical adjective.
  • OTIC: Similar because it also means "of the ear"; differs because otic is the Greek-derived medical term (otic drops, otitis, otology), built on the stem ōt- of Greek oûs "ear," and is confined to clinical registers, while aural ranges over medicine, education, and criticism.
  • AUDIBLE: Similar because both concern hearing; differs because audible describes only what CAN be heard — a property of a sound's intensity relative to the ear — whereas aural describes anything whatever that relates to the ear or to the act of hearing, whether or not a particular sound is in question.
  • SONIC: Similar because both belong to the world of sound; differs because sonic relates to sound waves or speeds as physical phenomena (sonic boom, ultrasonic, subsonic) with no necessary reference to the human ear, while aural always implies the listening organ and its experience.
