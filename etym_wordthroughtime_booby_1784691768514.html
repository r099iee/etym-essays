- 16th-17th century sense - a booby is a mild, comic word for a foolish, clumsy, blundering person — a simpleton who lacks wit and address; lighter in tone than idiot or imbecile, a word of mockery and schoolboy banter
- 17th-18th century nautical sense - sailors give the name to the large tropical seabirds of the genus Sula, so tame and clumsy on land that they looked stupid and let themselves be caught; from this comes the modern compound booby-trap
- 19th-20th century compounds - a booby trap is first a practical joke played on a simpleton, then, in the wars, a concealed explosive device; the booby prize is a joke award for the worst performance; the word multiplies into these fixed forms
- Contemporary sense - booby remains a mildly comic insult for a foolish, clumsy person, and it lives on in the seabirds, the booby prize and the booby trap; the tone is mock-affectionate rather than savage
