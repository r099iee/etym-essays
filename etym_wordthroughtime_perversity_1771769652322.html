- [15th-16th century turned away] - [From the image of turning aside; describes anything twisted off the straight or correct course, physical or moral]
- [17th-18th century deliberate contrariness] - [Names willful wrong-headedness and contrarian stubbornness; in Gibbon it is an endearing twist of taste, witnessed 'with delight']
- [19th century moral corruption] - [The word darkens; perversity drifts toward moral depravity and, by the century's end, toward 'perversion' with sexual overtones]
- [20th century to present moral-sexual weight] - [Usually implies a tainted, unwholesome bent; the old playful contrariness survives mainly in gentler literary or ironic use]
