- Homeric age - the Ionians were the earliest Greek seafarers and singers of epic, the men who first mapped the Mediterranean's dangers in myth, regarding the straits of Scylla and the city of the Laestrygonians with terrified wonder.
- Scientific and philosophical heyday - in the sixth century BC Ionia (the Aegean coast of Asia Minor) became the birthplace of Greek science and philosophy, the Ionian school of Thales and Heraclitus, so the name carried an intellectual prestige.
- Eastern afterlife - because the Ionians were the Greeks the Near East met first, their name became the standard word for all Greeks in Persian (Yauna), Hebrew (Yavan), and Sanskrit (Yavana).
- Contemporary sense - Ionians is a formal, learned plural used in ethnography and ancient history, attached to the Ionian Sea, the Ionian Islands, the Ionic dialect and order, and the Ionian school of philosophy.
