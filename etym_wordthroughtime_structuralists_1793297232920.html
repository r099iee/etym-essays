- 1916 — Saussure seed: the Course teaches language as form not substance, and nobody yet says structuralism.
- 1920s–1930s — Prague and Moscow rehearsal: phonologists and formalists practise system-thinking on sounds and tales, Propp lists functions in 1928.
- 1955–1963 — Lévi-Strauss ignition: Tristes Tropiques and Structural Anthropology name the myth as language, and Paris catches fire.
- 1960s — high fashion: Barthes reads striptease and steak-frites as signs, Foucault orders knowledge, students call themselves Structuralists with pride.
- 1971 — folklore stocktaking: the Marandas structural semantics of myth shows what the school can do with oral tradition, and orality theorists note what it missed, the page.
- 1970s onward — succession and slang: Derrida and company outflank the masters, and Structuralist settles into history books and friendly insult for system-lovers.
