First element — dia-, through:
PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*dis-
‘apart, asunder, in two’
↓
Ancient Greek (8th c. BCE, Homer)
διά (dia)
‘through, across, by’
↓
Modern English (19th c. prefix)
dia-
‘through, across time’

Second element — chronic, of time:
Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE)
χρόνος (khronos)
‘time, duration, age’
↓
Ancient Greek (classical)
χρονικός (khronikos)
‘of time, temporal’
↓
Late Latin (4th c.)
chronicus
‘of time; lingering’
↓
Middle French (14th c.)
cronique
‘record of events’
↓
English (15th–19th c.)
chronic + -al + -ly
‘of time’ + adverbial
↓
Modern English (20th c.)
diachronically
‘through successive times’

Note: Greek khronos has no certain PIE etymology and is often treated as Pre-Greek substrate vocabulary; the derivation of dia from *dis- is the standard handbook account though the exact prehistory of the preposition remains nuanced.
