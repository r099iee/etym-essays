• Reek: Similar because it denotes a strong, unpleasant smell that pervades an area; differs because reek suggests smoke or a drifting, heavy odour rather than a specifically organic one, and it is a single word rather than a dragon-linked compound.
  • Stink: Similar because it is the blunt native word for a foul smell, sharing the same root family as stench; differs because stink is more informal and can be a verb, whereas stench is heavier, more literary and noun-like.
  • Fetor: Similar because it denotes an offensive, putrid odour; differs because it is Latinate and clinical, used in medicine and formal prose, and lacks the visceral, folktale force of worm-stench.
  • Miasma: Similar because it describes a heavy, unhealthy air or atmosphere that is often foul; differs because miasma refers to the air itself and has a pseudo-scientific, Victorian history, not to a specific animal odour.
  • Odour: Similar because it is the general term for a smell; differs because it is neutral in value (good or bad), whereas worm-stench is intensely negative and specific.
  • Pong: Similar because it is an informal word for a bad smell; differs because it is light and jocular (chiefly British slang), the opposite of the grim, archaic force of worm-stench.
  • Whiff: Similar because it denotes a smell that is caught on the air; differs because a whiff is brief and faint, while a worm-stench is heavy, pervasive and lingering.
