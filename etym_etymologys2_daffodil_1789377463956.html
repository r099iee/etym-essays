Pre-Greek / Mediterranean Substrate (c. 2000–1200 BCE)
*asphodel-
"asphodel plant, pale yellow underworld flower"
Note: Beekes identifies the root as non-Indo-European Pre-Greek substrate due to its phonetic structure.
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th–4th c. BCE)
asphódelos (ἀσφόδελος)
"asphodel, immortal floral plant of Elysium"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE)
asphodelus
"asphodel plant"
↓
Late / Medieval Latin (c. 6th–12th c.)
affodillus
"asphodel, wild yellow narcissus"
Note: Initial /asf-/ simplified to /af-/ through phonetic reduction in provincial European speech.
↓
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 13th–14th c.)
affodille / asphodile
"asphodel flower, narcissus"
↓
Middle Dutch (c. 14th–15th c.)
affodille (de affodille)
"the asphodel or yellow narcissus"
Note: Flemish and Dutch bulb traders combined the definite article 'de' with the flower name.
↓
Middle English (c. 1400–1450)
affodill / affadille
"asphodel or wild narcissus plant"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1540–1600)
daffodil / daffodilly
"wild yellow trumpet narcissus (Narcissus pseudonarcissus)"
Note: Agglutination of Dutch article 'de' or French preposition 'd'' (d'affodille) created the initial 'd-'.
↓
Modern English (present)
DAFFODIL
