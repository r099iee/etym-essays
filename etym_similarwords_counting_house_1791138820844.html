• office: Similar because it is a room for business work; differs because it is general and modern, while counting-house is specifically historical and account-focused.
  • counting-room: Similar because it is the same account-room under another name; differs only in that room sounds plainer than house.
  • bureau: Similar because it is a writing office for business; differs because it is French-derived and can mean a desk or agency, not just ledgers.
  • back office: Similar because it handles accounts behind the scenes; differs because it is modern corporate jargon for a department, not a single gloomy room.
  • accounts department: Similar because it keeps books and money; differs because it is a modern division with staff, while counting-house could be one man and a clerk.
  • firm: Similar because a house can mean a business firm; differs because firm is the company itself, not its room.
  • business house: Similar because house historically meant a trading firm; differs because it names the enterprise, not specifically its ledger room.
  • shop: Similar because merchants work there; differs because a shop sells to walk-in customers, while a counting-house hides ledgers upstairs.
  • warehouse: Similar because it is another commercial house; differs because it stores goods, not accounts.
  • custom-house: Similar because it is a historical house for duties and papers; differs because it is governmental, for customs, not private trade.
  • study: Similar because it is a private workroom with desk; differs because it is domestic and scholarly, not commercial.
  • chambers: Similar because professionals work in chambers; differs because it suggests lawyers or lodgings, not merchants' ledgers.
