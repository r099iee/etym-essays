- [16th century: manner of conduct] - 'Deportment' enters English meaning the manner in which one conducts oneself, one's behaviour; connotation is neutral and behavioural.
- [17th-19th century: the trained carriage] - The word narrows toward physical bearing - posture, walk, and gesture - and becomes a schooled attainment taught in etiquette manuals and young ladies' academies; connotation is disciplined and class-coded, the body as a record of upbringing.
- [Contemporary sense] - The way a person carries, holds, and conducts the body in public; connotation is formal and faintly old-fashioned - the vocabulary of manners and breeding, where clothing can be put on but deportment must be trained into the body, and where even the way a maid stands announces which rank she serves.
