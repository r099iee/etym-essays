• Trapped: Similar because both mean caught and unable to escape; differs because trapped is general and can be accidental (trapped by fire, by snow), while ensnared implies a designed device with bait and a setter.
  • Entrapped: Similar because both name capture through artifice, often by law or deceit; differs because entrapped stresses deliberate luring into wrongdoing (entrapment defences), while ensnared stresses the tightening loop itself.
  • Entangled: Similar because both picture being wound round and held; differs because entangled stresses knotted confusion (entangled in lies, in undergrowth), while ensnared stresses capture by another's device.
  • Enmeshed: Similar because both evoke nets closing over the victim; differs because enmeshed stresses being woven into a system (enmeshed in bureaucracy), while ensnared stresses the sudden springing of the trap.
  • Enveloped: Similar because both describe being closed round by a surrounding force; differs because enveloped can be gentle and neutral (enveloped in fog), while ensnared is always hostile capture.
  • Beguiled: Similar because both involve charm that leads the victim astray; differs because beguiled stresses the deceiving of the mind (beguiled by words), while ensnared stresses the resulting captivity.
  • Lured: Similar because both begin with bait and attraction; differs because lured names only the drawing in (lured to the city), while ensnared names the completed capture after the lure worked.
  • Hooked: Similar because both picture a hidden point seizing the victim, literally or as addiction; differs because hooked is colloquial and pierces (hooked on praise, on pills), while ensnared is literary and tightens round.
  • Implicated: Similar because both leave the victim bound up in guilt or difficulty; differs because implicated stresses involvement suggesting guilt (implicated in the plot), while ensnared stresses loss of freedom to escape.
