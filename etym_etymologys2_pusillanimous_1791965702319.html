pūsus
"boy, lad"

Latin (1st c. BCE)
pusillus
"very small, tiny; petty"
↓
Late Latin (4th c. CE)
pusillanimis
"small-souled, faint-hearted"
↓
English (1580s)
pusillanimous
"cowardly, mean-spirited"

Note: pusillus is a diminutive of pūsus, a rare nursery word for "boy" whose own origin is unknown and whose traditional link with puer is not demonstrable; the second element, animus "mind, spirit", descends regularly from PIE *h₂enh₁- "to breathe" (whence anima "breath, soul"). The Late Latin compound probably renders Greek ὀλιγόψυχος, "little-souled", a calque pattern rather than an inherited word.
