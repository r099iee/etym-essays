• Cheerfully: Similar because both mean doing something with good spirits; differs because cheerfully is the plain, neutral modern word for a settled inward good humour, while gaily is more literary, dated, and showy.
  • Merrily: Similar because both denote lighthearted mirth; differs because merrily points specifically to laughter, festivity, and song, while gaily can mean merely bright or showy without any mirth.
  • Happily: Similar because both describe cheerful action; differs because happily is the broadest and most ordinary word for gladness, while gaily adds a touch of glittering display.
  • Blithely: Similar because both can mean with cheerful unconcern; differs because blithely stresses heedless irresponsibility more strongly, while gaily retains the sense of brightness and appearance.
  • Jauntily: Similar because both suggest smart, self-confident sprightliness; differs because jauntily is confined to bearing, dress, and manner, while gaily also covers speech, action, and colour.
  • Breezily: Similar because both suggest easy, confident good spirits; differs because breezily implies casual, offhand ease, while gaily implies brightness of colour or mood.
  • Brightly: Similar because both can describe brilliant appearance; differs because brightly is the plain word for intense light or colour, while gaily adds the older sense of fine, gay dress.
  • Showily: Similar because both can mean with fine, eye-catching display; differs because showily is pejorative, suggesting ostentation, while gaily is normally admiring or merely descriptive.
  • Joyfully: Similar because both denote a glad manner; differs because joyfully expresses deep, heartfelt joy, a stronger and more serious emotion than gaily's light cheer.
  • Gleefully: Similar because both name a bright, cheerful manner; differs because gleefully suggests mischievous or triumphant delight, while gaily is lighter and more innocent.
  • Sportively / Frolicsomely: Similar because both describe playful, lighthearted action; differs because they stress play and gambolling, while gaily merely means cheerfully or brightly.
