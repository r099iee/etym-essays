• Burped / eructated: Similar because both describe gas leaving the stomach through the mouth; differs because they are exclusively physiological and clinical (eructated) or infantile-comic (burped), lacking belch's violent figurative power over fire and smoke.
  • Erupted: Similar because both suggest violent bursting release of matter; differs because eruption implies an explosive opening of a surface (volcano, blister), whereas belching is a repeated, choked, gasping discharge from an already-open vent.
  • Spewed: Similar because both mean violently discharging contents; differs because spew focuses on liquid or semi-liquid matter (vomit, blood, molten rock) and carries disgust, while belch centres on gas with fire/smoke imagery.
  • Gushed: Similar because both describe a sudden strong outflow; differs because gush implies smooth, copious liquid flow (water, praise), not a choked, noisy gas-burst.
  • Emitted / discharged: Similar because both are neutral verbs for sending something out; differs because they are clinical and deliberate, with none of belch's involuntary, spasmodic, noisy force.
  • Vomited: Similar because both are bodily expulsions through the mouth; differs because vomiting is liquid, sickening, and strictly human/animal, whereas belching is gaseous and extends metaphorically to inanimate vents.
  • Spouted: Similar because both describe matter shooting from an opening; differs because spout is a focused jet through a narrow tube (spout, blowhole) rather than a diffuse, thunderous puff.
  • Cough (as in "coughed up smoke"): Similar because both are choked, noisy emissions; differs because coughing is sharp, hacking, and brief, while belching is a deep, cavernous, gaseous roll.
