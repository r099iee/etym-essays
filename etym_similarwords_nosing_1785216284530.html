• sniffing: Similar because both involve using the nose to investigate; differs because sniffing is pure olfaction — drawing air through the nose to smell — whereas nosing sends the whole head and snout forward, shading into tactile groping and, figuratively, into meddling.
  • prying: Similar because both mean intrusive, unwelcome curiosity; differs because prying is the fully human, moralized word for meddling in others' affairs, while nosing keeps the animal image of the probing nose and can be literal (a dog nosing) as well as figurative.
  • rummaging: Similar because both mean searching about for something; differs because rummaging is manual — turning things over with the hands in a disorganized way — where nosing is done head-low, following scent and touch.
  • ferreting: Similar because both mean hunting out by persistent, driven search; differs because ferreting (from the ferret, the hunting animal) implies flushing prey from cover, while nosing implies scenting and probing rather than flushing.
  • foraging: Similar because both involve searching about; differs because foraging is the neutral search for food, chiefly of animals, with no sense of curiosity or intrusion.
  • scenting: Similar because both mean detecting by smell; differs because scenting emphasizes the passive perception of an odour in the air, whereas nosing emphasizes the active, probing movement of the head into something.
  • snooping: Similar because both mean secretly investigating what is not one's concern; differs because snooping is purely human and sneaky — spying on others — while nosing retains its animal, sensory base and is less condemnatory.
