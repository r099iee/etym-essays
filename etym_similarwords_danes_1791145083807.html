• Vikings: Similar because Danes were the archetypal Vikings; differs because Viking is an activity/era label covering Norwegians and Swedes too, Danes one nationality.
  • Norsemen: Similar because it denotes medieval Scandinavians; differs because Norsemen is literary and pan-Scandinavian, Danes specifically Danish.
  • Northmen: Similar because Normans and Danes were northmen; differs because Northmen is archaic and includes Norwegians, Danes is modern-precise.
  • Normans: Similar because Normans were Viking descendants (Rollo's Danes/Norwegians); differs because Normans became French Christians and conquered England in 1066, Danes remained Danish.
  • Swedes: Similar because they are fellow Scandinavians and Vikings; differs because Swedes went east (Rus, Byzantium), Danes went west (England, France).
  • Norwegians: Similar because they raided Britain alongside Danes; differs because Norwegians settled Scotland, Ireland, Iceland, Danes settled England and Normandy.
  • Saxons: Similar because they were Germanic neighbours and enemies; differs because Saxons were earlier settlers turned English victims of Danes.
  • Jutes: Similar because they came from Jutland like Danes; differs because Jutes were 5th-c. invaders of Kent, Danes 9th–11th c. invaders of the Danelaw.
  • Angles: Similar because Angles came from Denmark's borderlands; differs because Angles were 5th-c. migrants who became English, Danes later invaders of those English.
  • Picts: Similar because they faced Viking/Danish raids and appear in Durant's list; differs because Picts were Celtic Britons, Danes Germanic Scandinavians.
  • Iberians: Similar because they appear alongside Danes in Durant's savage list; differs because Iberians were Iron-Age Spaniards, Danes medieval Scandinavians.
  • Irish: Similar because Irish suffered Danish raids and appear in Durant's list; differs because Irish were Celtic islanders, Danes continental raiders.
