Middle English (late 14th c.)
throbben
“to pulsate, beat, throb”
↓
Modern English (15th–21st c.)
throb
“to beat or pulse with a dull rhythm”
↓
Present-day English (21st c.)
THROB
“to pulsate rhythmically; a dull rhythmic beat or ache”
NOTE: No attested ancestor before Middle English; the word is judged imitative or echoic, its form mirroring the sound of a heavy beating pulse. Older dictionaries compared it to dialectal Germanic words for “to beat”, but no secure cognate or PIE etymon exists.
