- 12th–13th Century: In Anglo-Norman and Middle English, denoted the formal legal delivery or dispensing (*livrée*) of food, wine, and cloth allowances by a lord to his household.
- 14th–15th Century: Shifted specifically to the distinctive heraldic clothing and badges worn by a lord's retainers and armed retinue (as well as London guild robes).
- 16th–17th Century: Codified into the specialized uniforms of domestic servants (footmen, coachmen) and the livery stable trade for boarding horses.
- 18th–19th Century: Flourished across European aristocratic mansions as an essential status symbol of wealth, with footmen dressed in elaborate silk and plush liveries.
- 20th–21st Century: Expanded into commercial aviation, motorsport, and public transportation to designate the standardized exterior color and graphic scheme of vehicle fleets.
