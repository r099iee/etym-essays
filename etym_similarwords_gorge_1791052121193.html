• gobble: Similar because it means eating fast and greedily; differs because it stresses haste and noise, more comic and less about overfilling to sickness.
  • devour: Similar because it means eating hungrily and completely; differs because it stresses violent speed and can be admiring, while gorge stresses over-stuffing past fullness.
  • gulp: Similar because it involves swallowing greedily through the throat; differs because it is about a single large swallow, not a whole binge.
  • stuff: Similar because to stuff oneself means overeating; differs because it is colloquial and general, without the throat/ravine image.
  • cram: Similar because it means filling full with food; differs because it can apply to any container or study (cram for exams) and is less specifically gluttonous.
  • glut: Similar because it means to feed to excess; differs because it is more formal/literary and often transitive (glut the market).
  • guzzle: Similar because it means consuming greedily; differs because it is typically used for drink, not solid food.
  • binge: Similar because it means an episode of excessive consumption; differs because it is modern clinical (binge-eating) and covers drink, drugs, media, not just food.
  • feast: Similar because it means eating abundantly; differs because feast is celebratory and positive, while gorge implies loss of control.
  • ravine: Similar because as a noun gorge means a narrow steep valley; differs because ravine is the generic landform, gorge stresses narrowness and rockiness.
  • canyon: Similar because it is a deep valley; differs because canyon is typically larger, arid-region, while gorge can be small and wooded.
  • defile: Similar because it means a narrow passage; differs because it is military/literary and stresses passage for troops, not river-erosion.
