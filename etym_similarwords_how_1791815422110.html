• whereby: Similar as an instrumental relative meaning "by which", the nearest formal equivalent of relative how; it differs by requiring an explicit antecedent ("the means whereby") and by never introducing a direct question.
  • however: Built on the same word; it differs by being concessive ("no matter how") or contrastive ("but"), and it can never ask a plain question of manner.
  • thus: Similar as a manner adverb meaning "in this way"; it differs by being demonstrative rather than interrogative, and by belonging to formal, slightly bookish prose.
  • as: Similar in introducing clauses of manner ("as I said"); it differs by expressing comparison or simultaneity rather than asking for a method, and it cannot stand alone in a question.
  • why: Similar as a wh-adverb of circumstance; it differs by asking for a cause or motive, whereas how asks for manner or means — though colloquial how come slides into the territory of why.
  • what ... like: Similar in asking about a state or quality ("What was it like?"); it differs by seeking description and comparison, while how seeks evaluation or degree.
  • wherein: Similar as a formal relative adverb; it differs by meaning "in which" and by being confined to legal, archaic and self-consciously elevated prose.
  • howsoever: Similar in meaning "in whatever way"; it differs by being archaic and emphatic, and by introducing concession rather than a question.
  • in what way / by what means: Phrasal paraphrases of interrogative how; they differ by being explicit and formal, whereas how is the unmarked, single-word default.
  • the way: Similar in fused-relative function ("the way he did it"); it differs by being a noun phrase with no wh-movement, and it is the standard substitute where how is barred after an antecedent.
  • howdy: Similar only in origin, being a contraction of "how do ye"; it differs by functioning solely as a greeting, with no interrogative force left.
