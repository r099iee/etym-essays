• Dutch: the standard West Germanic language of the Netherlands and of official Belgium, of which Flemish dialects form the southern branch; differs because Dutch names the whole national language and its supra-regional norm, whereas Flemish foregrounds the Belgian regional varieties and a sub-national identity.
  • Netherlandic: an academic synonym for "Dutch" in its broad sense, covering the language of the Low Countries; differs because it is a scholarly, geographically neutral term and does not carry the Belgian cultural and political associations of Flemish.
  • Belgian Dutch: the variety of standard Dutch used in Belgium; nearly interchangeable with the looser sense of Flemish, but more precise and more neutral, since it names the standard register rather than the dialects or the ethnic community.
  • Vlaams: the Dutch-language autonym for Flemish; identical in reference, but it belongs to Dutch discourse and can carry stronger identity politics, and it often distinguishes the dialects from standard Dutch more sharply than English usage does.
  • Fleming: the noun for a person from Flanders; similar in referring to the same population, but it is personal and countable ("a Fleming, the Flemings"), whereas Flemish is chiefly an adjective or a mass noun for the language.
  • Flemish-speaking: a descriptive compound for the Dutch-speaking part of Belgium; similar in scope, but explicitly linguistic and bureaucratic, avoiding the ethnic and art-historical overtones of Flemish.
  • Brabantian: the dialect group of Antwerp, Brussels and Leuven; similar because it is a core component of Belgian Dutch, but narrower, naming one regional variety rather than the whole Flemish complex.
  • West Flemish: the coastal dialect of Bruges and Kortrijk; similar as a Flemish variety, but more specific and markedly divergent, often unintelligible to standard-Dutch speakers.
  • Low Franconian: the West Germanic branch to which Dutch and Flemish belong; similar as a classificatory label, but it is a comparative-linguistic term covering Dutch, Afrikaans and their dialects, not a cultural or regional name.
  • Walloon: the Romance language and regional identity of southern Belgium; similar as a Belgian regional-linguistic label, but it is Romance rather than Germanic and stands as the customary counterpart and political foil to Flemish.
  • Francophone: a speaker of French, especially in Belgium; not a synonym but the habitual contrast term, since the historical Flemish Movement defined itself against the French-speaking elite.
  • Teutonic: an older learned word for "Germanic"; similar as a broad ancestral label, but archaic and now largely literary, and it never denotes the modern Flemish language or region.
