- Late 18th c. (1779) — Coined in French as phonologie by Abbé Gabriel Batteux, used loosely for the study of vocal speech sounds and vocal physiology.
- Early 19th c. (1815–1830s) — Adopted into English, primarily functioning as a synonym for physical phonetics, elocution, and orthoepy.
- Late 19th c. — Utilized by Neogrammarian philologists to document historical sound shifts (Lautgesetze) in the historical evolution of Indo-European languages.
- 1920s–1930s — Radically redefined by Nikolai Trubetzkoy and Roman Jakobson of the Prague Linguistic Circle, permanently separating functional, psychological phonology from physical phonetics.
- 1960s — Transformed into a generative mentalist framework by Noam Chomsky and Morris Halle (The Sound Pattern of English), introducing distinctive binary features and ordered rule derivations.
- Late 20th–21st c. — Expanded into sign language cherology (Deaf sign phonology), Optimality Theory (Prince & Smolensky), and digital NLP speech recognition models.
