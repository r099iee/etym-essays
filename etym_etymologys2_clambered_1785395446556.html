CHAIN for CLAMBERED: 1. PIE *glei- "to stick, smear, be sticky, cling" (Pokorny *gley-; a root of stickiness and adhesion) — reconstructed, c. 4500–3500 BCE. 2. Proto-Germanic *klimbaną "to climb, cling" (a nasalized intensive of the stick-root; the earliest climbers "stuck" to the tree with arms and knees) — reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–100 CE. 3. Old English climban "to climb" (attested from the 8th c.). 4. Middle English climben "to climb" (12th–13th c.). 5. Middle English clambren "to climb with effort, scramble, climb repeatedly" (c. 1300; a frequentative of climben, formed with the -er/-ren suffix from Old English -rian, its vowel taken from the strong past stem clamb). 6. Modern English clamber "to climb awkwardly, using hands and feet" (verb and noun; the settled form of the 16th–18th c.). 7. CLAMBERED "climbed awkwardly" (past tense; regular weak -ed formation). NOTE ON THE DEBATED STAGE: the link of the whole climbing family to PIE *glei- is standard (the Germanic *klimban- is accepted as a nasalized derivation of the stick/cling root), but Greek gloios "glutinous oil, gum" is only a probable, not certain, cognate. No secure Indo-Iranian reflex of *glei- is established in the climbing sense; none is invented. Latin clay and German Klei 'marsh clay' are the same root's clay-branch, not ancestors of clamber.
