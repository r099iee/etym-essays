- 1901 to 1910 - Boris Bugaev adopts the pseudonym *Andrei Bely* (often transcribed *Biely* in Western Europe); leads the young "Argonauts" and the Second Generation of Russian Symbolists, publishing his revolutionary musical "Symphonies" and landmark theoretical essays on rhythm and verse.
- 1913 to 1920s - Writes and publishes his masterwork *Petersburg*, hailed by Russian contemporaries as an apocalyptic triumph capturing the doomed splendor of Tsarist imperial power; lives in Switzerland studying Anthroposophy with Rudolf Steiner before returning to revolutionary Russia.
- 1930s to 1950s - Following Bely's death in Moscow in 1934, his works are suppressed and marginalized under Stalinist Socialist Realism; in the West, early émigrés and the 1959 John Cournos translation introduce *Petersburg* to English readers under the transliteration *Biely*.
- 1960s to 1980s - Rocketed into global literary prominence when Vladimir Nabokov publicly ranks *Petersburg* alongside *Ulysses*, *The Metamorphosis*, and *In Search of Lost Time*; Western scholars re-evaluate Biely as a central genius of European modernism.
- 1990s to 21st Century - Following the collapse of the Soviet Union, Bely's complete oeuvre is reclaimed and celebrated across Russia; internationally, his works are intensely studied in comparative literature, narratology, and urban modernist aesthetics.
