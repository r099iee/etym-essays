• Refractivity: Similar because both terms quantify the capacity or tendency of light waves to undergo refraction; differs because refractivity is the modern standard physical term measuring the refracting power of a material substance or medium (e.g., the specific refractivity of a chemical solution or optical glass), whereas refrangibility historically describes the intrinsic disposition or susceptibility of the light ray itself to be bent.
  • Diffractability: Similar because both describe the deviation of light waves from a straight-line trajectory; differs fundamentally because diffractability refers to the spreading and bending of waves around physical obstacles or through narrow slits due to wave interference, whereas refrangibility concerns the bending of rays as they cross an interface between two transparent media of different refractive indices.
  • Dispersibility: Similar because both concepts are intimately linked to the separation of composite light into spectral components; differs because dispersibility is a broad property denoting how readily a substance or system disperses particles or distributes energy throughout a medium, while refrangibility specifically refers to the angular refraction that produces chromatic dispersion in optical prisms.
  • Reflectivity: Similar because both describe fundamental optical behaviors at the boundary between two media; differs sharply because reflectivity measures the fraction of radiant energy that bounces back into the original medium without penetrating the surface, whereas refrangibility governs the light that penetrates the boundary and is transmitted obliquely with an altered trajectory.
  • Bendability: Similar because both describe the potential to deviate from a straight alignment; differs because bendability is a colloquial, mechanical Germanic term describing the physical ductility or flexibility of solid materials (such as wire or sheet metal), utterly lacking scientific precision or optical relevance.
