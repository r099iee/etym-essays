Abounding is an adjective and the present participle of the verb abound, signifying existing in great numbers, plentiful, rich, or overflowing with a particular quality, resource, or entity. It describes an active, surging plenitude—not merely static sufficiency, but a vigorous, teeming surplus that fills an environment or character to overflowing.

In the source passage from Jonathan Swift's Gulliver's Travels, Gulliver writes with biting, sarcastic irony of the impossible societal transformations he had expected his book to effect in seven short months: he desired to know ‘when party and faction were extinguished; judges learned and upright; pleaders honest and modest... the female Yahoos abounding in virtue, honour, truth, and good sense; courts and levees of great ministers thoroughly weeded and swept.’ Swift’s use of abounding here is masterfully rhetorical: by pairing a word that denotes overwhelming natural abundance with virtues that corrupted human nature completely lacks, he sharpens the satirical contrast between utopian fantasy and wretched human reality.

Core Meaning and Grammatical Versatility: Grammatically, abounding functions both as an attributive or predicative adjective (‘an abounding harvest’, ‘a heart abounding in love’) and as a verbal participle governing prepositions such as in or with (‘abounding in wildlife’, ‘waters abounding with fish’). Etymologically rooted in the surging motion of ocean waves (Latin unda via abundāre), the word retains this dynamic sense of liquid surplus cresting beyond normal boundaries.

All Primary, Secondary, and Domain-Specific Meanings:

  • Richly Teeming with Physical Organisms or Resources (Ecological & Physical Sense): Existing in lavish quantity throughout a landscape or habitat (‘a rainforest abounding in rare orchids and bird species’). The semantic bridge connects the swelling of ocean waves to the teeming fertility of the biological natural world.
  • Overflowing with Moral, Spiritual, or Emotional Qualities (Ethical & Psychological Sense): Fully endowed with virtues, enthusiasm, goodwill, or spiritual grace (‘abounding in gratitude’, ‘abounding with joy and optimism’). The bridge translates the physical overflow of water into an internal, psychological state where feeling cannot be contained within the heart.
  • Theological Fullness and Divine Grace (Scriptural & Canonical Register): In Christian theology and English biblical translations, abounding serves as the quintessential descriptor of God's unmerited, infinite favor and mercy (as in Romans 5:20: ‘where sin abounded, grace did much more abound’). The bridge elevates physical plenitude to metaphysical infinity, where divine grace exceeds human transgression.
  • Intellectual and Creative Fertility (Literary & Epistemological Sense): Characterized by a relentless generation of ideas, wit, or scholarly insights (‘a mind abounding in creative inventions’, ‘a philosophical essay abounding in brilliant paradoxes’). The bridge links hydraulic surplus to cognitive and artistic productivity.
  • Economic and Material Wealth (Commercial & Agricultural Register): Signifying expansive prosperity, lush agricultural output, or liquid financial capital (‘an economy abounding in capital investment and employment opportunities’). The bridge equates the overflowing river with the flourishing harvest and thriving marketplace.
  • Ironic and Satirical Contrast (Rhetorical & Literary Nuance): Applied deliberately to impossible ideals or glaring deficits, as in Swift’s prose, where human beings are sarcastically imagined as ‘abounding in virtue’. The bridge exploits the grandiloquent elevation of the word to highlight the tragic or comic absence of the very quality mentioned.

Register and Connotation: Abounding is an elevated, resonant, and predominantly positive term. It avoids the clinical sterility of numerous or the utilitarian plainness of plentiful, carrying instead an expansive, celebratory warmth. When used in literary prose or philosophical discourse, it lends majesty and emotional amplitude to descriptions of generosity, nature, and moral excellence.
