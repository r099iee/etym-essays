• Dwells on: Similar in being a mind returning repeatedly to a subject; differs because dwelling is neutral and could equally be pleasant rumination, while brooding is sullen — a low, compulsive heat with none of the light of enjoyment.
  • Ponders: Similar in being deep thought; differs because pondering is analytical and almost administrative — one ponders a problem — whereas one broods over a wound, a wrong, or a hunger.
  • Muses: Similar in being absorbed reflection; differs because musing is a leisured, often pleasurable drifting of the mind, the opposite of brooding's involuntary, unhappy pressure.
  • Meditates: Similar in concentrated, sustained inner attention; differs because meditation is deliberate, disciplined, and usually serene, while brooding is compulsive, passive, and coloured by distress.
  • Contemplates: Similar in regarding something at length; differs because to contemplate is to view or weigh a matter, often calmly or from a distance, whereas one broods on a thing that has already wounded one.
  • Rankles: Similar in capturing a hidden, continuing grievance; differs because rankling describes the irritation itself — the sore that stays sore — while brooding is the person sitting on the nest, incubating the feeling.
  • Smolders: Similar in describing a slow, hidden fire of feeling; differs because smoldering is the emotion burning beneath the surface without action, while brooding is the act of sitting on and feeding that fire in the mind.
  • Frets: Similar in being anxious, restless inner disturbance; differs because fretting is agitated, fussy worry about small things, outward in its nervousness, while brooding is heavy, silent, and concentrated.
  • Worries: Similar in being uneasy thought about something; differs because worrying is future-directed anxiety about what may happen, while brooding is retrospective sullenness about what is or was.
  • Obsesses over: Similar in a thought returning again and again without relief; differs because obsession has an object one is fixated on and an often irrational intensity, while brooding is a gloomier, slower affair — a sitting on the feeling rather than a chasing of it.
  • Sulks: Similar in a silent, withdrawn bad mood; differs because sulking is a visible, childish withdrawal meant to be noticed, while brooding can be a genuine private heaviness of thought with no display.
