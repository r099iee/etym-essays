- 16th c. (Onomatopoeic / Sound-symbolic emergence) - Emerged in early Modern English as *jerk* / *yerk*, meaning to whip, lash, or strike with a switch, and to move with a sudden, sharp thrust or kick.
- 17th–18th c. (Kinetic & mechanical generalization) - Generalized to any sudden, abrupt pull, twitch, or short start in mechanical apparatuses and human limbs.
- 19th c. (Culinary convergence & athletics) - Converged with South American Quechua *ch'arki* to denote dried jerked beef; also emerged as a technical term in weightlifting.
- Early 20th c. (American slang & Soda fountains) - Coined for "soda jerkers" operating soda fountain levers, and shifted into ubiquitous American pejorative slang for an obnoxious or foolish person.
- Late 20th c.–Present (Physics & Aerospace kinematics) - Formally standardized in physics and mechanical engineering as the third time-derivative of position ($d^3x/dt^3$).
