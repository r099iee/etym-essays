• Baffled: Similar because it means stopped or defeated by a puzzle; differs because baffled suggests being halted at a single barrier that defeats the attempt, while flummoxed is the whole mind knocked into a heap — broader, looser, more comic.
  • Bewildered: Similar because it denotes confused disorientation; differs because bewildered means lost and directionless in an unfamiliar landscape, while flummoxed means beaten down by something absurd or impenetrable rather than genuinely lost.
  • Perplexed: Similar because it means puzzled; differs because perplexed is the intellectual, Latinate default that still admits of thought, while flummoxed is blunter, physical, and slangy — the mind jostled, not merely occupied.
  • Nonplussed: Similar because it is the nearest in meaning — utterly at a loss; differs because nonplussed (literally ‘no more’) is bookish and Latinate, whereas flummoxed is colloquial and auditory, a word that sounds like its own collapse.
  • Confounded: Similar because it means thrown into confusion or defeat; differs because confounded carries an old-fashioned imprecatory force (“confound it!”) and hints at shame or ruin, while flummoxed is purely comic and harmless.
  • Mystified: Similar because it means puzzled by something obscure; differs because mystified suggests an air of wonder at something inexplicable, while flummoxed is a blunt, everyday loss of composure.
  • Stumped: Similar because it means completely at a loss, stopped short; differs because stumped (from a tree-stump image) means brought to a halt as if blocked, while flummoxed means knocked sideways into disorder — halt vs. collapse.
  • Discombobulated: Similar because it is a slangy, comic word for total confusion; differs because discombobulated (an American invention) emphasises being thrown into disorder of one's whole composure, while flummoxed is British, older, and focuses on being defeated by a puzzle.
