- Medieval plain carrier - the word enters English as the plain, functional name for a small bag of leather or stout cloth, carried by travelers, messengers, and laborers; it is purely practical, with no special social coloring.
- 18th-19th century schoolroom - the satchel becomes the schoolchild's emblem - the bag for books, slates, and lunch, slung on one shoulder - acquiring its studious, everyday, warm register and its place in scenes of childhood and departure.
- 20th-century nostalgia - as two-strap backpacks and rigid briefcases take over, the satchel turns old-fashioned; it gains a soft period glow, the flavor of inkwells, slate pencils, and boys setting out for school.
- Contemporary revival - the fashion world resurrects the single-strap, top-flap "satchel bag" as retro-chic, while in prose it remains the literary word for a child's or traveler's shoulder bag - warm, unpretentious, and lightly antiquated.
