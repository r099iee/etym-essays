• forge blocks: Similar because both are heavy bases for metalwork; differs because forge block is generic and may include swage blocks, while anvil implies the classic horned form.
  • swage blocks: Similar because both shape metal by resistance; differs because swage blocks are perforated cubes with grooves, while anvils have flat face and horn.
  • stakes: Similar because both are small anvil-like supports for fine work; differs because stakes are slender uprights held in a bench plate, not massive freestanding blocks.
  • mandrels: Similar because both shape metal around or upon them; differs because mandrels are rods for forming rings and tubes, while anvils are flat beating surfaces.
  • dies: Similar because both shape material by pressure; differs because dies are paired moulds in presses, while anvils work with free hammer blows.
  • incudes (Latinate plural): Similar because it is the technical synonym; differs because incus is anatomical or archaeological register, while anvil is everyday craft register.
  • workbenches: Similar because both provide a stable work surface; differs because benches support general work, while anvils specifically absorb hammering.
  • lasts: Similar because cobbler's lasts act as foot-anvils; differs because lasts are foot-shaped for shoes, not flat for metal.
  • anvils vs hammers: Similar because both belong to the forge; differs because hammer is active striker, anvil is passive receiver.
  • braziers: Similar because both belong to smithing equipment; differs because braziers hold fire, while anvils hold work.
