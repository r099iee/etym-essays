• Medicine: Similar because both mean the healing of bodily ailment and the medical art; differs because medicine is the modern standard term, while physic is archaic/historical.
  • Medication: Similar because both denote a substance taken to treat illness; differs because medication is clinical and modern, while physic is literary and old-fashioned.
  • Remedy: Similar because both mean a cure for illness; differs because remedy can be any solution (legal, social, technical), while physic is strictly medical/herbal.
  • Cure: Similar because both denote something that restores health; differs because cure names the successful result, while physic names the preparation or art itself.
  • Treatment: Similar because both denote the management of illness; differs because treatment is modern and clinical, while physic implies the older art of healing.
  • Therapy: Similar because both denote healing practice; differs because therapy can be non-medical (speech, art, physio-), while physic is specifically bodily medicine.
  • Healing: Similar because both denote the restoration of health; differs because healing is the process/result, while physic is the means (the art or the dose).
  • Drug: Similar because both denote a medicinal substance; differs because drug is a broad modern term (also narcotic/recreational), while physic is a specific old medical dose.
  • Potion: Similar because both denote a liquid medicine; differs because potion evokes magic, fantasy, or romance, while physic is plain, archaic medicine.
  • Tonic: Similar because both denote a restorative medicine; differs because tonic is specifically strengthening/invigorating, while physic could be a purging dose.
  • Elixir: Similar because both denote a medicinal preparation; differs because elixir implies a magical or alchemical life-giving liquid, while physic is ordinary medicine.
  • Nostrum: Similar because both denote a medicine; differs because nostrum is a quack remedy of secret composition, while physic is a respectable learned art.
  • Panacea: Similar because both denote a healing substance; differs because panacea is a cure-all (often figurative), while physic is a specific, fallible treatment.
  • Cathartic: Similar because both can mean a purging medicine; differs because cathartic is specific to bowel cleansing (and, figuratively, emotional release), whereas physic covered all general medicine.
  • Purgative: Similar because both include the laxative sense; differs because purgative names only the cleansing agent, while physic is the whole healing art.
  • Laxative: Similar because both can mean a bowel-moving medicine; differs because laxative is the modern clinical term, while physic's laxative sense was only one of its uses.
  • Aperient: Similar because both are gentle purging medicines; differs because aperient is a rare technical term, while physic is general archaic medicine.
  • Emetic: Similar because both are bodily-ejecting medicines; differs because emetic induces vomiting, while physic was not restricted to one effect.
