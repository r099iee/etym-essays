• Scuffle: Similar because both mean a brief disorderly physical struggle; differs because a scuffle stresses shuffling feet, confusion, and accident (a scuffle in the crowd), while a tussle stresses two parties actively grappling with each other.
  • Skirmish: Similar because both are small-scale engagements short of full battle; differs because a skirmish is military (patrols exchanging fire, debating skirmishes), while a tussle is bodily and close, fought with hands rather than weapons.
  • Brawl: Similar because both are noisy, unruly physical fights; differs because a brawl is bigger, drunker, and uglier (a bar-room brawl), while a tussle stays small, brief, and comparatively good-natured.
  • Scrap: Similar because both name scrappy, informal fights with no great malice; differs because a scrap is street-tough slang (a playground scrap), while a tussle is softer and more acceptable in print.
  • Grapple: Similar because both picture opponents locked together wrestling; differs because grapple names the gripping technique itself (grappling on the mat), while a tussle is the whole little episode of struggle.
  • Struggle: Similar because both mean striving against resistance; differs because a struggle can be long, lone, and serious (a struggle with illness), while a tussle is short, social, and seldom grave.
  • Wrangle: Similar because both cover heated contests that may be verbal or physical; differs because a wrangle leans verbal and quarrelsome (wrangling over the bill), while a tussle leans bodily and playful.
  • Squabble: Similar because both are petty, small-scale disputes; differs because a squabble is noisy bickering over trifles (a family squabble), while a tussle involves actual grappling or a genuine contest.
  • Fray: Similar because both can mean a bout of fighting or contest; differs because fray is literary and often larger (eager for the fray, above the fray), while a tussle is deliberately downscaled and domestic.
  • Tussle vs tousle (parent word): Similar because tousle means to rumple or handle roughly, the very action of a tussle; differs because tousle describes what happens to hair or clothes (tousled locks), while tussle names the mutual struggle itself.
