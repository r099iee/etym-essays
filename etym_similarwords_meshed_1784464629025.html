• Interwoven: Similar because both describe things laced together; differs because interwoven stresses orderly textile construction with warp and weft, while meshed stresses the open net-structure and the locking or catching of parts.
  • Entangled: Similar because both describe things caught together; differs because entangled implies an unwanted snarl or trap, while meshed can be deliberate, clean, and even mechanical (as of gear teeth).
  • Enmeshed: Similar because both come from the same net-image and mean caught in a network; differs because enmeshed is almost always negative and trapping, while meshed also names a benign interlacing or a working mechanical engagement.
  • Interlaced: Similar because both describe weaving or lacing things together; differs because interlaced is decorative braiding, looser and prettier, while meshed is functional interlocking with open spaces between the strands.
  • Woven: Similar because both are fabric-verbs describing threads combined; differs because woven implies a closed cloth with warp and weft, while mesh is open-work — holes are the point.
  • Knit: Similar because both join threads or parts together; differs because knitting makes tight loops of fabric with needles, while meshing makes open grids and, in the mechanical sense, interlocking teeth.
  • Locked: Similar because both describe parts joined so they act together; differs because locked means fixed and immovable, while meshed (of gears) means connected so that motion transmits — engagement, not arrest.
  • Fused: Similar because both describe parts united into one working thing; differs because fusion melts or bonds permanently, destroying the separate identity of the parts, while meshing keeps parts distinct but interlaced.
  • Gear-connected / geared: Similar because both describe machinery that runs together; differs because geared is the broader mechanical term for fitting with gears, while meshed specifically names the moment the teeth of two gears engage.
  • Netted: Similar because both share the net-image and mean caught in or made like a net; differs because netted is the older, plainer word for catching with a net, while meshed emphasizes the interlaced structure and has carried the word into machinery and abstraction.
  • Interlocked: Similar because both describe parts joined so they engage each other; differs because interlocked stresses the dovetailing or overlapping of shapes, while meshed keeps the net-and-open-space image and the gear-tooth engagement.
