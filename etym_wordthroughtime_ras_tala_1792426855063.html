- [Vedic Antiquity: c. 1500–800 BCE] — The noun *Rasā* appears in the *Ṛgveda* as a mythic, cosmic subterranean river surrounding the earth and flowing through dark nether spaces; *tala* denoted a flat surface or foundation — [perception: primordial cosmic watercourse encircling the subterranean foundations of creation]
- [Epic Period: c. 500 BCE–200 CE] — Codified in the *Rāmāyaṇa* and *Mahābhārata* as the terrifying subterranean abyss penetrated by divine weaponry (e.g., Rāma's testing arrow piercing the seven trees into Rasātala) and visited by gods and heroes — [perception: awe-inspiring subterranean battleground and ultimate cosmic threshold beneath the earth]
- [Classical Purāṇic Era: c. 300–1200 CE] — Systematized across the *Purāṇas* as the sixth of the seven opulent subterranean paradise realms (*pātālas*), characterized by jeweled palaces, radiant serpent lords, and heroic titans living in luxury — [perception: opulent subterranean paradise realm of serpent power, hidden wealth, and titan kingdoms]
- [Medieval to Modern Era: 13th–21st c.] — Secularized and metaphorically extended across modern Indo-Aryan languages (Hindi, Bengali, Marathi) as a common literary and colloquial idiom for total ruin, the abyss, and catastrophic moral or systemic collapse (*rasātala meṃ jānā*) — [perception: universal literary idiom for the lowest depths, catastrophic downfall, or utter destruction]
