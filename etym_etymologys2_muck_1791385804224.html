Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*meug- / *meuk-
"slimy, slippery; mucus, discharge"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*mūk- / *mugjan-
"dung, heap of manure, wet filth"
↓
Old Norse (c. 900 CE)
myki (noun) / moka (verb)
"cow dung" / "to shovel manure"
↓
Middle English (c. 1250 CE)
muk / mucke
"manure, stable dung, wet filth"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550 CE)
muck
"dung; worldly vanity, filthy lucre"
↓
Modern English (18th c.–present)
muck
"manure; slimy mud; unpalatable slop; poker discards"

Etymological Note: Borrowed into Middle English from Old Norse myki, displacing native Old English scearn and meox (which survives in mixen, a dunghill). The verb muck arose from the Scandinavian practice of shoveling dung (Old Norse moka).
