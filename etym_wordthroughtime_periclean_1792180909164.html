- 5th c. BCE — Coined in Greek as Períkleios (Περίκλειος), describing policies, buildings, or factions loyal to Periklês; Thucydides recorded that under him Athens was "in name a democracy, but in reality ruled by its first citizen."
- Roman Imperial Era — Revered by Plutarch, Cicero, and Quintilian as the supreme classical model of gravitas, sublime oratory, and magnificent civic patronage.
- Renaissance & Enlightenment — Celebrated by early modern political theorists and humanists who rediscovered Thucydides and hailed the Athenian golden age as the historical cradle of civic virtue.
- 19th c. — Adjective Periclean becomes firmly established in Victorian English historiography (George Grote, John Stuart Mill, Will Durant) to designate the 5th-century BCE Athenian cultural peak, while also applied metaphorically to other national literary revivals.
- 20th–21st c. — Re-evaluated in modern critical historiography, feminist scholarship, and postcolonial studies to encompass both the democratic-artistic glory and the underlying exploitation of allied tribute, chattel slavery, and domestic subjugation of women.
