Linear derivation chain for SLACKLY (root word one of two): R — PIE *(s)lēg- (to be lax, loose, to hang without tension) — the root of laxity and looseness. → E — Proto-Germanic *slakaz (loose, lax, slack; sluggish) — the adjective of looseness. → D — Old English slæc (loose, lax, negligent, remiss; of a rope, without strain). → C — Middle English slak (loose, lax, slack) — the same adjective continued. → B — English slack (adjective: not taut, loose; noun: the loose part of a line) + the adverbial suffix -ly. → A — slackly (adverb: in a loose, limp, un-taut manner) ◄ TARGET. Note: The chain *(s)lēg- → *slakaz → slæc → slak → slack → slackly is secure. The related Latin adjective laxus ("loose"), which enters English as lax, relax, and laxative, is a collateral branch of the same PIE root, not part of the English slack lineage.
Linear derivation chain for GLISTENED (root word two of two): R — PIE *ghel- (to shine, gleam; bright; yellow, green) — the great Indo-European root of shining and its colours. → E — Proto-Germanic *glis- (to gleam, to glitter) — a derived extension of the shining root; → *glisēn / *glisnōn (to glisten). → D — Old English glisnian (to glitter, gleam, glisten) — the frequentative verb of gleaming. → C — Middle English glisnen / glisten (to glisten, shine) — the verb continued. → B — English glisten (verb: to shine with a soft, reflected, wet luster). → A — glistened (past tense of glisten: shone with a sparkling or wet luster) ◄ TARGET. Note: The chain is secure. The same PIE root *ghel- produced Proto-Germanic *gulþą ("gold," whence English gold), Sanskrit hári ("yellow, green, golden," whence Hindi harā, "green"), and many English light-words including gleam, glow, glare, and glass — all collateral branches of the one ancient root of shining.
