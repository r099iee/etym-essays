• Cut back: Similar because it is the everyday equivalent of spending or doing less; differs because cut back is plain and neutral, while retrench is formal and dignified, implying principled economy.
  • Economise: Similar because both mean to spend less and manage frugally; differs because economise stresses thrifty management within means, while retrench stresses active cutting of what already exists.
  • Curtail: Similar because both cut something short of its former extent; differs because curtail applies to anything shortened (speeches, powers, leaves), while retrench centres on expenditure and establishment.
  • Reduce: Similar because both make something smaller; differs because reduce is the bare general verb, while retrench adds the motive of necessity and the approval of prudence.
  • Slash: Similar because both cut budgets fiercely; differs because slash is violent, sudden, journalistic cutting, while retrench is deliberate, systematic, almost surgical.
  • Pare: Similar because both shave away excess bit by bit; differs because pare stresses thin gradual shavings (pare spending to the bone), while retrench can mean large structural cuts.
  • Prune: Similar because both cut back overgrowth to restore health; differs because prune keeps the gardening metaphor visible and hopeful, while retrench keeps the accountant's ledger.
  • Downsize: Similar because both shrink establishments to fit harder times; differs because downsize is modern corporate jargon for cutting staff, while retrench is older and wider, covering any expenditure.
  • Abridge: Similar because both shorten what was longer; differs because abridge belongs to books and rights (an abridged edition), while retrench belongs to budgets and ways of living.
