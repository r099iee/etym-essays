↓
PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*weyd-
"to see; to know"
↓
Latin (3rd c. BCE)
vidēre
"to see"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
providēre
"to foresee, look ahead"
pro- "before" + videre
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
prūdēns
"wise, foresightful"
contracted from providens
"foreseeing"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
prūdentia
"foresight, wisdom"
↓
Latin (1st c. CE)
imprūdentia
"lack of foresight, folly"
in- "not" + prudentia
↓
English (16th c.)
imprudence
"lack of caution or foresight;
rashness"

Note: prudens is a contraction of providens
'foreseeing'; the shift from 'foreseeing'
to 'wise' is a well-attested semantic
development.
