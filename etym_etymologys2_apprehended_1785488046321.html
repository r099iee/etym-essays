Linear derivation chain for APPREHENDED (single root word):

*gʰed- "to take, to seize" (Proto-Indo-European, Pokorny) → Latin *hendō "to seize, grasp" (a reconstructed simplex, attested only inside compounds) → Latin prehendō / prendō "to lay hold of, grasp, seize" (prae- "before" + *hendō; fully attested) → Latin apprehendō "to take hold of, to seize, to arrest, to grasp mentally" (ad- "towards" + prehendō; fully attested) → Latin apprehensus "seized, grasped" (perfect passive participle) → English apprehend "to seize, to perceive, to grasp mentally" (late 14th c., borrowed from Latin; the wider word family was reinforced through Old French, e.g. apprendre "to learn") → APPREHENDED (past tense and past participle — the exact form of this card).

Note on the family: the same Latin stem *hendō yields comprehendere (English comprehend, comprehensive), reprehendere (English reprehend, and via Latin reprehēnsio the noun reprimand), and prehensiō (English prehension, prehensile). The abstract noun apprehension, "a seizing; perception; fearful anticipation", is a parallel formation on apprehensus; the anxiety sense is a later medieval development, not part of the original chain.
