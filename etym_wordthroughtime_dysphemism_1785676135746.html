- [1884 · scholarly coinage] - Coined in English by analogy with 'euphemism' from Greek dys- ('bad') + phēmē ('speech'); born a technical term naming the harsh opposite of softening language.
- [Early 20th century · linguistic term] - Adopted by philologists and lexicographers to classify deliberate taboo substitutions ('croaked' for 'died'); confined to academic registers.
- [20th century · rhetoric and taboo studies] - Spreads through stylistics and the study of taboo, insult, and political speech; 'dysphemistic' widens its reach.
- [Modern · neutral label for aggression] - A neutral, technical label in linguistics; it names language deliberately engineered to shock or demean without itself carrying moral judgement.
