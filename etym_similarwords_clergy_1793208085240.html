• Priesthood: Similar because both name the ordained ministry set apart for sacred service; differs because priesthood stresses the sacramental office itself (especially Catholic and Orthodox), while clergy stresses the collective body of office-holders.
  • Ministry: Similar because both denote those called to preach and pastor; differs because ministry stresses the work and function of service (favoured by Protestants), while clergy stresses the order of persons (with Catholic overtones).
  • Clerics: Similar because both designate ordained members as opposed to laypeople; differs because clerics is the countable plural of individuals, while clergy is the collective singular of the body.
  • Ecclesiastics: Similar because both refer to church officers and the churchly order; differs because ecclesiastics is bookish and formal (church documents, history), while clergy is the standard everyday collective.
  • Priestcraft: Similar because both concern the priestly order and its power; differs because priestcraft is hostile — the alleged self-serving cunning of priests — while clergy itself is neutral.
  • Laity: Similar because clergy and laity are the complementary halves of the church, defined against each other; differs because the laity are precisely those who are NOT ordained, the ordinary faithful.
  • Monks: Similar because both are religious vocations living under vows and discipline; differs because monks belong to enclosed orders devoted to prayer and work, while the clergy serve parishes and dioceses in pastoral ministry.
  • Rabbinate: Similar because both are authoritative teaching orders interpreting a sacred law; differs because the rabbinate belongs to Judaism with no sacramental priesthood, while clergy is properly a Christian ordained order.
  • Ulema: Similar because both are learned religious establishments wielding moral and legal authority; differs because the ulema are Islamic scholars of the law with no ordination, while the clergy are consecrated by holy orders.
