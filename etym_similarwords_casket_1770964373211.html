• coffin: Similar (in North American usage) because both name a burial container; differs because coffin is the blunt, tapered, functional burial box, while casket in the funeral trade is the rectangular, cushioned, ornamented euphemism—and in its historical, British sense casket is a small jewel box, not a burial container at all.
  • chest: Similar because both are storage containers; differs because a chest is large, heavy, and utilitarian for bulk goods (linens, armor, tools), while a casket is small, delicate, and ornamental for valuables.
  • coffer: Similar because both are boxes for holding treasure; differs because a coffer is a heavily reinforced, lockable strongbox for money, gold, and government reserves, prioritizing security and strength, whereas a casket is a lighter decorative jewel-box prioritizing elegance.
  • reliquary: Similar because both are receptacles for precious or venerated things; differs because a reliquary is a sacred vessel for saints' relics or bones, carrying an explicit religious and devotional purpose, while a casket holds secular valuables.
  • case: Similar because both denote a container from the same Latin family (capsa); differs because a case is a plain, functional box or covering of any size (a pencil case, a watch case), lacking the smallness, craftsmanship, and jewel-guarding purpose of a casket.
  • trinket box / jewelry box: Similar because both are small decorative containers for valuables; differs because these modern names are plain and descriptive, whereas casket carries archaic, literary, and romantic weight and in America now collides with the funerary sense.
  • box: Similar because both name a container; differs because box is the completely generic word for any rectangular container, while casket specifies a small, precious, or ritual container with emotional and ceremonial associations.
  • cassette: Similar because both descend from the same Latin diminutive root and mean "little box"; differs because cassette in modern English means a sealed cartridge (audio cassette, film cassette), a mechanical object, not an ornamental or burial receptacle.
  • urn: Similar because both can hold the remains of the dead; differs because an urn holds cremated ashes in a vessel (and is also a classical vase for water or wine), while a casket holds an entire body in a burial container.
