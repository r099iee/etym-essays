• David Zeisberger: similar because he was the Moravian missionary whose diaries and reports form the core of Loskiel's history, and he spent decades among the Lenape and other peoples; differs because Zeisberger was a field missionary and linguist who left his own extensive journals and a Delaware grammar, whereas Loskiel was the armchair compiler and bishop who edited him.
  • John Heckewelder: similar because he was another Moravian missionary in the Ohio Country who wrote an influential ethnographic account of Native manners and customs; differs because Heckewelder's Account of the History, Manners, and Customs of the Indian Nations (1819) was his own first-hand testimony, while Loskiel's book is a synthesis of others' reports.
  • August Gottlieb Spangenberg: similar because he was a Moravian bishop and organiser of the American missions whose papers lie behind much of the record; differs because Spangenberg was a founder and administrator who wrote biography and theology, not a systematic history of the missions.
  • Christian Ignatius Latrobe: similar because his English translation of 1794 created the “Loskiel” known to Anglophone readers; differs because Latrobe was a translator, composer and Church official, not the author of the observations, and his name rarely appears in citations of the content.
  • John Eliot: similar because he was an earlier Protestant missionary whose writings on “praying Indians” shaped European ideas about indigenous conversion; differs because Eliot worked in seventeenth-century Massachusetts, translated the Bible into Algonquin, and his fame rests on direct missionary labour rather than on compiling a continental archive.
  • David Brainerd: similar because his published journal gave eighteenth-century readers an intimate Protestant account of mission work among Native Americans; differs because Brainerd's record is introspective, personal and devotional, whereas Loskiel's is institutional, chronological and documentary.
  • Junípero Serra: similar because he is the name most associated with Spanish Franciscan missions in North America and with the conversion and regimentation of indigenous communities; differs because Serra was a founder of missions in California and is remembered through the mission system itself, not through a compiled history of another province's workers.
  • Henry Rowe Schoolcraft: similar because his nineteenth-century compilations preserved a mass of ethnographic and linguistic material about Native peoples; differs because Schoolcraft worked later, drew on his own Indian agency experience, and wrote for a national audience rather than for a church readership.
  • William Graham Sumner: similar because he is the twentieth-century author who quotes Loskiel in the card's passage and uses him as an authority; differs because Sumner was a sociologist deploying the quotation for a theory of social selection, not a missionary historian transmitting the archive.
  • Loskiel's History: similar because it is the work rather than the man, and it is what most citations actually mean; differs because it names a three-part book published at Leipzig in 1789 and London in 1794, with its own textual history and reception.
  • Moravian: similar because it identifies the Church whose missions Loskiel chronicled and whose bishop he became; differs because it is a denominational adjective covering thousands of people and institutions, not an individual or a text.
  • Unitas Fratrum: similar because it is the formal Latin name of the Moravian Church to which Loskiel belonged; differs because it is the corporate body's official designation, used in ecclesiastical and treaty documents, and carries none of the bibliographic associations of his name.
