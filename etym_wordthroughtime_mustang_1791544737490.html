- Medieval Castile (c. 13th c.) - Spanish 'mesteño': an unclaimed stray animal belonging legally to the ranchers' guild ('Mesta') or the Crown.
- 16th-17th Century New Spain - Spanish horses escape into northern Mexico and the American Southwest, becoming feral 'caballos mesteños'.
- Early 19th Century Frontier - Anglo-American trappers and explorers adopt the term as 'mustang', shifting the meaning from 'unclaimed property' to 'wild, free horse of the plains'.
- Mid-20th Century Military Aviation (1940s) - The P-51 Mustang fighter plane becomes a decisive military weapon in World War II.
- 1960s to Present - Ford Mustang launches the American 'pony car' phenomenon; U.S. military 'mustang officers'; federal legal protection under the 1971 Wild Horse Act.
