*ret- (Proto-Indo-European, "to run, roll, turn"; a root of motion, also the source of Latin rota "wheel" and the Sanskrit ratha "chariot," literally "the runner") → Latin rota ("wheel," attested from the 3rd–2nd c. BCE) → Latin rotundus ("wheel-shaped, round," adjective formed from rota with the suffix -undus, attested 3rd–2nd c. BCE; Plautus uses rotundus of a round figure) → English ROTUND ("round, plump," direct loanword from Latin, attested from the early 18th century). Note on parallel forms: English round is NOT a descendant of this loanword but a sibling — it descends from Latin rotundus through Old French reont/rotont (from Vulgar Latin *retundus), entering English in the 13th–14th century. The direct Latin loan rotund (early 18th c.) and the Italian-derived rotunda (a round building, from Italian rotonda, feminine of rotondo, 17th–18th c.) complete the family. The PIE root *ret- "to run, roll, turn" is securely attested by the wheel-words of Latin (rota), Germanic (Old English rāð “wheel,” roð “ride”), and Indo-Iranian (Sanskrit ratha), though the full root shape is sometimes reconstructed as *reth₂- and is not debated in essentials.
