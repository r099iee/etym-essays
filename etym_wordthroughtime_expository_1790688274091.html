- Theological commentary (1600s–1700s) - expository means verse-by-verse sermon exposition; learned, reverent, church-bound.
- Rhetorical mode (1800s) - Blair, Whately and Bain fix expository as one of four discourses; becomes composition-class jargon for informing.
- Schoolroom staple (1900s) - expository essay and expository writing dominate US/UK exams; connotation turns diligent but dry.
- Film and narrative critique (late 20th c.) - expository dialogue/scene means necessary info-dump; often mildly pejorative, clunky telling vs. showing.
- Contemporary literacy science (21st c.) - expository text structures taught as reading skill; neutral-technical term for informational genre.
