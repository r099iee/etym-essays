• incline: Similar because both name any slanting surface; differs because incline is the general, abstract term for deviation from the level, whereas slope implies graded ground of the terrain, potentially gentle enough to walk — incline can name a road or a mathematical line where slope names the land itself.
  • grade: Similar because both describe a measured slope; differs because grade is a gradient expressed quantitatively, used of roads and railways (a steep grade, a grade crossing), whereas slope is the visible shape of the land itself, not its measured value.
  • gradient: Similar because both denote the steepness of a surface; differs because gradient is the mathematical or technical measure of rate of inclination, while slope in everyday use names the inclined surface directly.
  • scarp: Similar because both are sloping landforms; differs because a scarp is a steep slope produced by faulting or erosion, abrupt and often rocky, whereas a slope is a gradation — it may descend as gently as it can be walked.
  • cliff: Similar because both are landforms rising or falling from level ground; differs because a cliff is a near-vertical rock face, a drop, whereas a slope is a gradated incline that carries the eye — and the road — smoothly downward.
  • descent: Similar because both can name downward-sloping ground; differs because descent emphasises the act or direction of going down (and its abstraction, lineage), whereas slope names the fixed shape of the ground itself, without the sense of movement.
  • declivity: Similar because both name downward-sloping ground; differs because declivity is the formal, technical antonym of acclivity (an ascending slope), rare in speech, whereas slope is the everyday word in every register.
  • declination: Similar because both involve deviation from a level line; differs because declination is chiefly astronomical or surveying jargon for angular deviation, whereas slope is the plain word for an inclined surface.
  • ramp: Similar because both are inclined surfaces; differs because a ramp is a constructed incline, usually for vehicles or the disabled, with a deliberate function, whereas a slope is natural ground inclined by terrain.
  • hillside: Similar because both name inclined ground; differs because a hillside is a specific slope that forms the side of a hill, whereas a slope is any graded surface, on a hill, a roof, a graph, or a ski run.
