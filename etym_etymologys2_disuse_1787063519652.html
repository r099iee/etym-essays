Note on uncertainty: the origin of Latin ūtī is unknown — no convincing Indo-European cognates have been established, and handbooks mark its etymology as unsettled. The chain therefore begins at attested Latin rather than at a reconstructed PIE root, per zero-guesswork. The prefix dis-, by contrast, has a solid chain back to PIE *dwís "twice, in two", the sense "apart" arising from the idea of division into two parts. The verb disuse was modelled on Old French desuser (from des- + user).
