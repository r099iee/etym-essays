Proto-Germanic *klumpa- "a compact, heavy mass, lump, or block" (reconstructed; an expressive Germanic formation belonging to the sound-symbolic kl- family of mass-words that also produced clod, clot, cluster, and clog) → Middle Low German klump(e) "lump, mass, block" and Middle Dutch clumpe "lump, mass; wooden clog" (continental Germanic, medieval period) → Middle English clompe / clumpe "lump, compact mass" (borrowed from the continental Germanic dialects in the 14th c., the age of Hanseatic trade) → English clump "a dense cluster of growing things; a lump of earth or material" (17th c.; the sense of a clump of trees extends the old mass-sense from inert matter to living growth) → Modern English clump (noun) "a dense cluster of trees or plants; a lump of earth", and clump (verb) "to walk heavily with a dull thud" (verb attested 19th c.) — the target clump, in the noun sense of a dense, sheltering cluster of trees. DEBATE / NUANCE: The reconstruction of Proto-Germanic *klumpa- is secure within the Germanic family, but the family itself is expressive — built on the sound-symbolic consonant cluster kl- suggesting weight and cohesion — so no further Indo-European ancestor is reconstructable; the word is a Germanic coinage rather than a PIE inheritance. The English form was borrowed from the continental Low German / Middle Dutch area, not directly from Old English, whose related clyster ("cluster") and clott / clodd supplied neighbouring members of the same family.
