- 14th–15th centuries: Snuffing a candle primarily means trimming the burnt wick—practical, domestic, associated with better light rather than darkness.
- 16th–17th centuries: Sense of fully extinguishing the flame strengthens; snuffers as tools enter common inventory; literary use of the sudden dark begins.
- 18th–19th centuries: Metaphorical "snuff out" for ending life or enterprise spreads; British slang "snuffed it" for death hardens the fatalistic tone.
- Early–mid 20th century: Crime fiction and colloquial speech keep the kill/die senses lively; literal candle sense remains available but less central as electric light replaces candles.
- Late 20th–present: "Snuffed out" is still dramatic for hopes, rebellions, and lives; "snuff" compounds (e.g. sensational media senses) show extreme extensions; everyday perception mixes household relic with hard slang finality.
