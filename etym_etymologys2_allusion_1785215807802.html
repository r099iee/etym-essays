PIE *leid- "to play, sport, jest" (proposed reconstructed root, c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE; the deeper origin of Latin lūdere is debated among specialists, and the root has no securely attested reflexes outside Italic) → Latin lūdere "to play, sport, mock, jest" (attested 3rd–2nd c. BCE onward; built on lūdus "game, play, school") → Latin allūdere (adlūdere) "to play toward, to jest at, to touch upon lightly" (classical Latin; prefix ad- "toward, at" + lūdere) → Latin allūsiō, genitive allūsiōnis "a playing with, a jesting reference, wordplay" (Late Latin; a rhetorical term of indirection) → French allusion "a playful or indirect reference" (15th–16th c.) → English ALLUSION "a playful or indirect reference" (attested 1540s, chiefly via Sir Thomas Elyot's lexicography and Latin/French sources) → semantic narrowing: "pun, wordplay" (16th c.) → "a covert or indirect reference to a person, text, or event" (17th c., with Dryden and Addison; fixed by c. 1700)
