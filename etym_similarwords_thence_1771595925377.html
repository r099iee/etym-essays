• Hence: Similar because it is the direct twin of thence, built on the same demonstrative machinery and meaning "from that place, source, or consequence"; differs because hence means "from this (place or statement)," pointing back at the speaker's own position, while thence points outward at something just stated or understood — keeping the reader at a distance.
  • Whence: Similar because it completes the demonstrative trio (hence, thence, whence) and means "from which place, source, or cause"; differs because whence is interrogative or relative ("whence came this?"), whereas thence is purely deictic, pointing at a known place or statement.
  • Therefore: Similar because it introduces a conclusion as following from what precedes; differs because therefore names the relation of consequence directly and is fully at home in ordinary modern prose, where thence has retreated to formal and legalistic registers.
  • Consequently: Similar because it marks a result that follows from a cause; differs because consequently is the purely logical connective of result, fully modern and neutral, while thence adds the archaic spatial image of departing from a place.
  • Therefrom: Similar because it means "from that," being thence's even rarer twin; differs because therefrom is chiefly legal and technical, while thence, though formal, retains a wider (if still bookish) use.
  • Thereupon: Similar because it links a consequence to what precedes; differs because thereupon means "immediately after that, in consequence of that act," stressing sequence in time, whereas thence stresses departure from a point of origin.
  • From there: Similar because it is the plain modern translation of thence's spatial sense; differs because from there is conversational and concrete, drained of the logical and temporal colouring that thence carries.
  • So: Similar because it can introduce a consequence ("it is his conclusion, so..."); differs because so is a bare, all-purpose connective, while thence is a demonstrative adverb that keeps the reader oriented by pointing back at a specific ground.
  • Thus: Similar because it marks a consequence or manner of proceeding; differs because thus means "in this way, in this manner," describing how something is done, while thence describes whence — the point of origin of a motion or conclusion.
  • Ergo: Similar because it introduces a logical conclusion; differs because ergo is a learned, Latinate, often jocular equivalent of "therefore," while thence is a native English word with a spatial-visual core.
