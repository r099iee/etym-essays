• Water-wagon / water-waggon: Similar because it is another horse-drawn water carrier; differs because wagon is larger and four-wheeled, while cart was smaller and often two-wheeled.
  • Water-bowser / water-tanker: Similar because it does the identical street and site job today; differs because bowser/tanker is motorised and steel, while water-cart was horse-drawn and wooden.
  • Sprinkler-cart / street-sprinkler: Similar because it names the dust-laying function; differs because it stresses the sprinkler fitting, while water-cart stresses the load.
  • Barrel-cart / butt-cart: Similar because many water-carts were literally barrels on wheels; differs because barrel-cart can carry beer or oil, while water-cart specifies water.
  • Dust-cart: Similar because it was the sister parish vehicle doing refuse rounds; differs because it carried away dust and ashes, while water-cart brought water to lay dust.
  • Dung-cart / manure-cart: Similar because it is the same farm chassis with a different load; differs because dung is fertiliser, water is for drinking and damping.
  • Fire-engine (hand water-cart): Similar because early engines were wheeled water reservoirs; differs because fire-engines pumped jets, while water-carts sprinkled or delivered.
  • Standpipe / water-plug: Similar because carts filled from plugs through trapdoors; differs because plug is the fixed main fitting, while cart is the mobile distributor.
  • Milk-cart / milk-float: Similar because it delivered liquid door to door by horse; differs because milk is a food sale round, while water was municipal supply.
  • Tank-truck / water-truck (modern): Similar because it is the direct motor descendant; differs because truck is automotive and large-scale, while cart is horse-drawn and parish-scale.
