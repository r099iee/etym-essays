Derivation chain for ODOACER (an East Germanic compound of two root words; chains given for each root in isolation, then the compound and its Latinization): ROOT 1 — *audaz "wealth": 1. Proto-Indo-European *h2ew- "to enjoy, consume" (reconstructed root; an extended form *h2ewd- produces the wealth-family), c. 4th-3rd millennium BCE. 2. Proto-Germanic *audaz "wealth, riches, fortune, possessions" (reconstructed; attested in Gothic auda- "wealth" in compounds, Old Norse audr "riches, fortune, fate", Old English ead "prosperity, happiness" — the beloved name element of Edward, Edmund, Edgar, Edith — Old High German ot "possession"), c. 2nd-1st millennium BCE. 3. East Germanic *auda- "wealth" (the name element; attested in Germanic royal onomastics), 5th c. CE. ROOT 2 — *wakraz "watchful": 1. Proto-Indo-European *weg- "to be strong, be lively, be awake" (reconstructed root; reflexes include Latin vegere "to be lively", vigere "to thrive", Sanskrit vaja- "strength"), c. 4th-3rd millennium BCE. 2. Proto-Germanic *wakraz / *wakr- "awake, watchful, alert" (reconstructed; attested in Gothic wakan "to watch", Old Norse vakr "alert, bold", Old English wacor/waeccer "watchful" — modern English watch and wake belong to the same family — Old High German wahhen "to watch", wackar "awake"), c. 2nd-1st millennium BCE. 3. East Germanic *wakr- "watchful" (the name element), 5th c. CE. THE COMPOUND AND ITS LATINIZATION: 4. East Germanic *Audawakraz "wealth-watchful, one who guards his wealth" (reconstructed two-element compound), 5th c. CE. 5. Latinized Odoacer / Odovacar / Odovaker / Audovacar (the Latin and Greek sources show these variants; e.g., the contemporary chronicle of Marcellinus comes, Jordanes' Getica, Eugippius' Vita Severini; Greek sources have Odoachos), 5th-6th c. CE. 6. English ODOACER (standard modern English form, fixed by modern historiography), 18th-21st c. CE. Note on the debate: the analysis *auda- "wealth" + *wakr- "watchful" is the standard scholarly reading, but the first element has been read alternatively as *otha- "ancestral property, inheritance" (the o-graded cognate) in some older analyses; the two readings differ little in meaning. The tribal identity is also debated: the sources call Odoacer a Scirian (Sciri), a Rugian, or a Herulian; his mother's people were probably Scirian or Turcilingian. The Latinization stage is secure; the reconstructed Proto-Germanic compounds are scholarly reconstructions, not attested forms.
