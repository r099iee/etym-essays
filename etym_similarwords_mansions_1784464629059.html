• Houses: Similar because both are dwellings; differs because house is the general word for any building in which people live, while a mansion is specifically large, grand, and impressive.
  • Palaces: Similar because both are grand, imposing buildings; differs because a palace is royal and ceremonial, the seat of a monarch or court, while a mansion is a great private residence.
  • Manors: Similar because both are grand residences; differs because a manor implies a landed estate and its associated lands and privileges, while a mansion may stand alone without land.
  • Residences: Similar because both are places of dwelling; differs because residence is formal but not necessarily large, while a mansion is large and grand.
  • Abodes: Similar because both are dwelling-places; differs because abode is literary and general, with no implication of scale, while a mansion is specifically vast and impressive.
  • Halls: Similar because both can be grand dwellings or buildings; differs because a hall may be a single great room or a public building, while a mansion is a whole grand house.
  • Villas: Similar because both are large houses; differs because a villa suggests a suburban or country house with grounds, while a mansion stresses urban or estate grandeur.
  • Dwellings: Similar because both are places where people live; differs because dwelling is a neutral, general term for any home, while a mansion is the grand, high-end member of the set.
  • Stately homes: Similar because both name great houses; differs because "stately home" is a formal British estate term with specific historical and architectural frames, while a mansion is the general word for a large impressive house.
