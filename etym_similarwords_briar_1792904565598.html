• Bramble: Similar because both name thorny scrambling shrubs that bar the path; differs because bramble usually means blackberry canes and their fruit, while briar is the wider word for any thorn scrub and for pipe wood.
  • Thorn: Similar because both wound with a sharp plant point; differs because a thorn is the single spike itself, while a briar is the whole shrub or stem bearing many such spikes.
  • Thicket: Similar because both block passage with dense growth; differs because a thicket can be thornless coppice or bamboo, while briar always implies prickles.
  • Brier: Similar because it is the same word in alternate spelling; differs because brier is the preferred spelling for the pipe and the rose in some dictionaries, while briar dominates for the wild tangle in Tolkien’s sentence.
  • Hedge: Similar because both are lines of woody growth bounding fields; differs because a hedge is planted and trimmed by people, while briar is wild growth that must be fought.
  • Scrub: Similar because both cover waste ground with low woody plants; differs because scrub is ecological and neutral, while briar is felt on the skin, sharp and personal.
  • Bush: Similar because both name low woody plants; differs because bush is the plain general word, while briar narrows to the armed and tangled kind.
  • Nettle: Similar because both sting the careless walker; differs because nettles burn chemically with fine hairs, while briars tear mechanically with woody prickles.
