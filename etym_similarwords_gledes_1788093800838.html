• Embers: Similar because both are the modern and the archaic words for the same thing — the glowing remains of a fire; differs because ember is the standard living word, while gledes is archaic and literary, and always stresses the glow.
  • Coals: Similar because both name burning or glowing fuel; differs because coal is the broader word (fuel, lump, live or dead), while a glede is specifically a live, glowing piece.
  • Cinders: Similar because both are small burning or burnt pieces; differs because cinders are partly burnt, often dead or dying remains, while gledes are alive with glow and heat.
  • Clinkers: Similar because both name resistant fire residues; differs because clinkers are the fused, glassy, mostly dead lumps of a furnace, while gledes are glowing and can rekindle.
  • Firebrands: Similar because both are pieces of burning material; differs because a firebrand is a whole burning stick carried as a weapon or torch, while a glede is an ember left in place.
  • Brands: Similar because both name burning pieces of wood; differs because brand (now archaic) is the larger burning stick, while glede is the small glowing coal.
  • Sparks: Similar because both are bright particles of fire; differs because sparks are tiny, brief and fly through the air, while gledes are larger, sustained and lie in the hearth.
  • Ashes: Similar because both are what a fire leaves behind; differs because ashes are dead, grey and cold, while gledes are the opposite — still glowing with life.
  • Glow: Similar because both derive from the same "glow" root-family and share the sense of warm light; differs because glow is the light itself, not the burning coal that sheds it — and glow is a verb/noun of light, not of fire-matter.
  • Tinder: Similar because both are fire material; differs because tinder is dry matter ready to catch fire, while gledes are already burning matter that can light it.
