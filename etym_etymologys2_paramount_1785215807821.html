ROOT 1 — PIE *per- "through, forward, across, over" (c. 4500–3000 BCE), the source of Latin per "through, by, over."
│
└──▶ Latin per "through, by, over" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE).
────▶ Old French / Anglo-Norman par "by, through" (9th–12th c.) — the Romance reflex of Latin per.
────────▶ Anglo-Norman phrase par amont "by the upward [way], above, at the top" (12th–13th c.) — par + amont "upward, above."
────────────▶ Anglo-Norman paramont "above, over all" (13th c.) — fused from the phrase.

ROOT 2 — PIE *men- / *mon- "to project, stand out, rise" (c. 4500–3000 BCE), the root of eminence and height, source of Latin mōns "mountain."
│
└──▶ Latin mōns, genitive montis "mountain" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE).
────▶ Old French a mont / amont "upward, above" (10th–12th c.) — from Latin ad montem, "to the mountain," i.e., up the slope.
────────▶ Anglo-Norman phrase par a mont "by the upward way, above" (12th–13th c.) — par + amont.
────────────▶ Anglo-Norman paramont "above, over all" (13th c.).
────────────────▶ Middle English paramount "supreme, chief" (first attested 15th c.), noun and adjective; in feudal law "lord paramount" = the tenant-in-chief holding directly from the Crown.
────────────────────▶ Modern English PARAMOUNT "more important than anything else; supreme in rank or authority" (sense generalised from the 16th–17th c.).

Note on debate: the derivation of Latin mōns "mountain" from a PIE root is debated: the traditional source is *men- / *mon- "to project, stand out, rise," but some scholars (e.g. de Vaan) note that the formation is not perfectly regular and leave the ultimate root uncertain. The Romance, Anglo-Norman, and English stages, however, are fully attested and uncontroversial.
