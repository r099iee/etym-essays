• Dehydration: similar because both are loss of water; differs because dehydration is the clinical, bodily term — reversible water deficit in an organism — while desiccation means drying to actual dryness, often of non-living or environmental material.
  • Drying: similar because both are the removal of moisture; differs because drying is the plain, everyday word for any loss of wetness, while desiccation is the scientific term for thorough, often deliberate drying.
  • Evaporation: similar because both remove water; differs because evaporation names the physical process of liquid turning to vapour — which may be partial and natural — while desiccation names the end-state dryness of a whole thing.
  • Aridification: similar because both describe a region becoming drier; differs because aridification is the modern climate-science term for a long-term climatic trend, while desiccation can be a single industrial or geological process.
  • Withering: similar because both describe drying up; differs because withering is organic and intimate — a plant, a body, a hope shrivelling — while desiccation is impersonal and technical.
  • Parching: similar because both mean drying by heat; differs because parching is poetic and conveys scorching sun (parched lips), and rarely describes laboratory or industrial process.
  • Exsiccation: similar because it is the learned doublet of desiccation, from the same Latin verb; differs because exsiccation is now rare, archaic-technical, virtually confined to old chemistry and medicine.
  • Drought: similar because both belong to prolonged dryness; differs because drought is the weather event — a period without rain — while desiccation is the resulting process or state.
  • Preservation (related contrast): similar because desiccation is often used for preservation (dried foods, mummies); differs because preservation names the purpose and outcome, and may be achieved by cold, salt, or smoke rather than dryness.
