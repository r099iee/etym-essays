• Nanny: Similar because both care for and supervise a child; differs because a nanny need not breastfeed, so the wet-nurse's milk-giving bodily role is absent.
  • Nursemaid: Similar because both attend a young charge; differs because nursemaid is a more generic childcare role emphasising physical care, without the lactation that defines a wet-nurse.
  • Foster-mother: Similar because both mother another's child; differs because foster-mother implies legal or social fostering and broader parental substitution, not specifically suckling.
  • Dry-nurse: Similar as the historical counterpart who minds an infant; differs because it explicitly excludes breastfeeding, making the pair a strict contrast on the milk-giving function.
  • Milk donor: Similar because both provide breast milk to another's child; differs because a milk donor supplies expressed milk through medical channels, while a wet-nurse feeds the child directly and cares for it.
  • Childminder: Similar because both look after a child in a household for pay; differs because a childminder provides general day-care without nursing or lactation.
  • Governess: Similar because both are household women involved in a child's upbringing; differs because a governess teaches and supervises older children, not infant feeding.
