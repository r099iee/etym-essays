Root 1 — MUSH:

Old English (c. 1000)
māscan
"to mash, mix, crush (esp. malt with water)"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
mash
"mashed malt; a crushed or pulpy mass"
↓
Modern English (17th c.)
mush
"a soft, pulpy mass; soft food"

Debate: Old English māscan is itself of uncertain origin (possibly echoic); mush is a dialectal variant of mash, first attested meaning soft food.

Root 2 — RICKETY:

Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE)
ῥάχις
"the spine, backbone"
↓
Ancient Greek (2nd c. CE)
ῥαχίτης
"inflammation of the spine"
↓
New Latin (17th c.)
rachitis
"the disease rickets"
↓
English (17th c.)
rickets
"a disease of bone softening in children"
↓
Modern English (17th c.)
rickety
"suffering from rickets; shaky, unsteady"

Debate: origin debated — possibly from Greek rhachitis (via New Latin rachitis), or from an English dialectal word; the spelling rickets may reflect folk etymology.
