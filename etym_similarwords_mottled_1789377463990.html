• Dappled: Similar because both describe surfaces marked with irregular spots, patches, or blotches; differs because dappled typically evokes rounded, soft patches created specifically by shifting sunlight filtering through leaves or clouds, or the natural circular spots on horses and deer, whereas mottled often describes sharper, blotchier, darker, or more variegated mineral, amphibian, or skin discolorations.
  • Speckled: Similar because both involve non-uniform coloring; differs because speckled refers specifically to tiny, distinct, fine flecks, dots, or sprinkles of color (like the tiny spots on a bird's egg or freckles on human skin), whereas mottled involves larger, irregular, merging patches and blotches.
  • Variegated: Similar because both describe having multiple colors; differs because variegated is a formal botanical and aesthetic term frequently describing distinct, organized zones or streaks of color (such as green and white striped leaves in hostas), whereas mottled is organic, chaotic, and blotchy.
  • Spotted: Similar because both indicate the presence of marks of different color; differs because spotted suggests discrete, well-defined, and often circular spots (like those of a leopard or Dalmatian), whereas mottled marks are irregular, bleeding into one another, and asymmetrical.
  • Flecke / Flecked: Similar because both describe small color irregularities; differs because flecked denotes tiny, isolated specks or particle-like fragments scattered across a solid background, whereas mottled describes the comprehensive, continuous blotched texture of an entire surface.
  • Brindled / Brindle: Similar because both describe animal coats with mixed color patterns; differs because brindled specifically designates tawny, brownish animal coats marked with subtle darker streaks or bands (as seen in certain dogs, cattle, or cats), whereas mottled involves blotches and spots of any color.
  • Pied: Similar because both describe animals or objects displaying patches of two or more colors; differs because pied (derived from the magpie) strictly denotes bold, contrasting, large patches of two distinct colors (typically black and white, as in a piebald horse), whereas mottled involves complex, multi-tonal, smaller blotches.
