PIE *spend- "to pour a libation, to make an offering" (reconstructed; c. 4500–2500 BCE) → Greek spondē "libation; (pl.) truce, treaty" (attested from Homer, 8th c. BCE) — sibling branch showing the ritual-to-covenant shift → Latin spondēre "to promise solemnly, to pledge oneself, to answer for another" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE) → Latin dēspondēre "to promise away, to give up, to resign" (dē- "away, down" + spondēre; attested 1st c. BCE) → Latin idiom dēspondēre animum "to give up one's spirit/heart, to lose heart, to despair" (attested 2nd c. BCE–1st c. CE, e.g. Terence, Seneca) → English despond (verb, "to lose heart," 17th c., learned borrowing) → DESPONDING (present participle/adjective) ◄ TARGET Note: the PIE root *spend- is reconstructed on the strength of Greek spondē and Latin spondēre; the unity of the root is generally accepted but not free of debate. English comes directly from Latin, not through Greek; Greek shows the parallel religious development (libation to treaty), which illuminates why Latin spondēre means "to pledge.
