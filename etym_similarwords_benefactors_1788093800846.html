• Donors: Similar because both give money or goods to a person or cause; differs because donor is the neutral, everyday fundraising word for a gift of any size, while benefactor implies large, remembered, transformative generosity.
  • Patrons: Similar because both support people or institutions, often financially; differs because patron emphasizes ongoing support of artists, writers or businesses (and "customer" in commerce), while benefactor stresses a one-way gift of help or rescue.
  • Sponsors: Similar because both provide money for an activity, event or person; differs because sponsor is commercial and contractual — sponsorship buys advertising or association — while benefaction is gift-giving without return.
  • Philanthropists: Similar because both give large sums for the public good; differs because philanthropist is a grand, systematic word for someone devoted to improving humanity, often through foundations, while benefactor can be a single act or a smaller scale.
  • Backers: Similar because both supply money or support to a person or project; differs because backer is informal and commercial (backing a venture or candidate), with no moral halo, while benefactor is formal and altruistic.
  • Supporters: Similar because both help a person or cause; differs because supporter is the broadest, weakest word — moral, vocal or financial help of any degree — while benefactor is specifically a material helper of significant degree.
  • Contributors: Similar because both give money to a fund or cause; differs because contributor covers any participant in a collection, often small and anonymous, while benefactor implies a principal, remembered giver.
  • Angels (informal): Similar because an "angel" (as in angel investor) rescues a person or project with money at a critical moment; differs because angel is colloquial and investment-flavoured, while benefactor is formal and selfless in tone.
  • Champions: Similar because both actively promote a person or cause; differs because champion fights or advocates publicly, often without money, while benefactor gives material aid, often quietly.
  • Well-wishers: Similar because both are friendly to a person or cause; differs because a well-wisher only wishes well, while a benefactor actually does good — the difference between sentiment and substance.
  • Saviours / rescuers: Similar because both deliver people from harm, the oldest sense of benefactor; differs because saviour is dramatic and life-saving (often religious), while benefactor is secular and usually financial.
  • Benefactresses: Similar because identical in meaning, feminine form; differs because it is now rare and often avoided as unnecessary and dated.
