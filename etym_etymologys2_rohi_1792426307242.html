Note on phonetic and morphological derivation: Indo-Europeanists (Mayrhofer, Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindoarischen) trace Sanskrit róhita- directly to PIE *h₁rewdʰ- ("red"). In Vedic phonology, the Proto-Indo-Iranian dental/aspirate clusters developed into Indo-Aryan voiced aspirate -dh- and subsequently shifted intervocalically in certain dialects to -h- (hence róhita- alongside archaic lōhita- with r/l dialectal variation). The feminine nominal formation róhiṇī was formed by the possessive/characterizing suffix -in- in feminine grade -inī (with retroflex nasalization -n- → -ṇ- caused by the preceding r- per the classic Sanskrit nati rule). Originally denoting any female creature possessing a reddish hide (specifically cows), it was assigned to the radiant red giant Aldebaran because of its distinctively fiery, copper-red visual magnitude.
