• Axe: Similar because both are bladed chopping and splitting tools with a cutting bit mounted on a handle; differs because an axe typically possesses a longer handle designed for two-handed swinging and heavy felling, whereas a hatchet is designed specifically for nimble, one-handed operation.
  • Tomahawk: Similar because both are lightweight, single-handed axes of similar dimensions; differs because a tomahawk originated as an indigenous North American multi-purpose weapon and tool featuring a straight, friction-fit slip handle and often a pipe bowl or spike, whereas a hatchet is traditionally an English/European woodworking tool with a pinned or wedged head.
  • Cleaver: Similar because both are heavy, single-handed bladed implements used for forceful chopping; differs because a cleaver has a broad, rectangular, thin blade primarily used in butchery and culinary preparation to chop through meat and bone, lacking the hammer poll and wedge geometry of a hatchet.
  • Adze: Similar because both are handheld woodworking tools used to shape and dress timber; differs because an adze has its cutting edge set perpendicular to the handle (like a hoe) rather than parallel, designed for gouging and smoothing flat timber surfaces.
  • Billhook: Similar because both are traditional handheld forestry and hedging tools; differs because a billhook features a curved hook at the blade's tip used for lopping small green branches and coppicing, lacking the heavy chopping wedge of a hatchet.
  • Machete: Similar because both are handheld outdoor cutting tools; differs because a machete is a long, flexible, flat-bladed sword-like tool ideal for clearing brush and vegetation, whereas a hatchet possesses a thick wedge head built for splitting dense wood.
