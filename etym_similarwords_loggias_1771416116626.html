• Gallery: Similar because both are long, covered spaces attached to a building; differs because a gallery need not be open to the outside at all (art galleries, upper corridors), while a loggia is by definition open on one or more sides to the air.
  • Veranda: Similar because both are roofed, open-sided platforms for sitting out; differs because a veranda is usually a light wooden porch projecting beyond a house, whereas a loggia is monumental and set within the building’s structure.
  • Porch: Similar because both are sheltered transitional spaces at a building’s edge; differs because a porch is a small covered entrance at a door, while a loggia is a full arcaded gallery, often running along an entire courtyard or facade.
  • Colonnade: Similar because both involve a row of columns supporting a roof; differs because a colonnade names only the column row itself and need not form an enclosed or habitable space, while a loggia is the room those columns help define.
  • Arcade: Similar because both are roofed passages carried on arches or columns; differs because an arcade is usually a covered walkway for shops or circulation, fully integrated into a building or street, whereas a loggia emphasizes openness to garden or courtyard and a residential or palatial character.
  • Balcony: Similar because both are elevated outdoor spaces attached to a building; differs because a balcony projects outward from a wall and is open on all sides, while a loggia is recessed within the structure and roofed, open on just one face.
  • Portico: Similar because both are columned, roofed entries to a building; differs because a portico is specifically an entrance porch at the front of a temple or mansion, while a loggia may run along the sides and function as a living gallery rather than an entry.
