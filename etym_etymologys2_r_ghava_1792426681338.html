Note on morphological formation: In Pāṇinian Sanskrit grammar (Aṣṭādhyāyī 4.1.92 ff.), Rāghava is formed from the proper noun Raghu by adding the secondary patronymic suffix (taddhita-pratyaya) -a (technically aṇ). The addition of this suffix triggers two regular morphophonemic operations: (1) initial syllable vowel lengthening or vr̥ddhi, changing short a- to long ā- (ra- → rā-); and (2) guṇa strengthening and semi-vocalic substitution of the final stem vowel -u before the vowel suffix, converting -u to -av-. Hence, Raghu + a regularly yields the patronymic substantive Rāghava ("son or descendant of Raghu").
