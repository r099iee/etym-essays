• Medal: Similar in being a stamped token of metal bearing a portrait or emblem; differs because a medal is the smaller, honourific object awarded or struck to commemorate a person or event, whereas a medallion in the decorative sense is a framed round or oval motif - and in the numismatic sense is simply a larger medal.
  • Roundel: Similar in being a circular ornament or panel; differs because a roundel is the plain geometric term for any circular disk or charge, with no implication of figural content or honour, whereas a medallion is framed, emblem-bearing, and ceremonial.
  • Plaque: Similar in being a flat framed ornamental tablet; differs because a plaque is typically rectangular, hung or mounted singly to commemorate a person or event, while a medallion is round or oval and often repeated as a pattern.
  • Rosette: Similar in being a circular decorative ornament; differs because a rosette is specifically rose-shaped or radially petalled, a floral motif, whereas a medallion encloses any design - dragon, portrait, scene - within its frame.
  • Cartouche: Similar in being an enclosed ornamental frame containing a design; differs because a cartouche is oval or scroll-shaped, often containing an inscription or coat of arms, and belongs to architecture and heraldry, while a medallion keeps the coin-and-medal ancestry and the numismatic link.
  • Cameo: Similar in being a framed circular or oval device with a raised design; differs because a cameo is specifically a gem or shell carved in relief and worn as jewellery, whereas a medallion is any framed roundel in any material, metal, plaster, or textile.
  • Emblem: Similar in being a symbolic design with meaning; differs because an emblem is the meaning-bearing symbol itself, independent of shape or frame, while a medallion is the framed shape in which an emblem sits.
  • Token: Similar in being a small metal piece with a design; differs because a token is a coin-like piece used as currency, voucher, or symbol, while a medallion is larger, commemorative, or purely decorative.
  • Boss: Similar in being a raised circular ornament; differs because a boss is a plain protruding knob or stud, usually undecorated and architectural, while a medallion is flat, framed, and filled with a design.
  • Coin: Similar in being a round stamped metal object; differs because a coin is currency issued for exchange and of standard small size, while a medallion is struck for commemoration or decoration, larger, and not legal tender.
