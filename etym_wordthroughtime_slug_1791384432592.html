- Late 14th to 15th Century - [Lazy person, idler] - First recorded in Middle English (*slugge*) to describe a slow, indolent, torpid person; closely related to Scandinavian dialect terms for sluggishness and drooping.
- Early 16th Century - [The shell-less mollusc] - Transferred metaphorically to the terrestrial gastropod mollusc on account of its creeping, leisurely locomotion and seemingly limp, shell-less body, rapidly becoming its primary biological name.
- 17th to 18th Century - [Rough lead projectile & metal scrap] - Transferred in ballistics to heavy, cylindrical or irregularly cut pieces of lead fired from firearms, whose dense, lumpy appearance resembled the fleshy body of the mollusc.
- 19th Century - [Linotype bar & shot of liquor] - Adopted in hot-metal printing for an entire line of type cast in lead, and in American colloquial slang for a heavy, fiery gulp of liquor that hits the throat like a bullet.
- 20th to 21st Century - [Journalistic slug, web URL identifier & physics unit] - Formalized in newspaper offices for story codes, in physics for the gravitational mass unit, and in web software development for clean, human-readable URL endpoints.
