• thickset: Similar because it also describes a compact, heavy, strongly built frame; differs because thickset leans toward the sheer heaviness of the build while stocky stresses the whole short, well-proportioned figure — and of the pair, stocky is the more vivid, colloquial, body-focused word.
  • heavyset: Similar because it names a solid, stocky, strongly built body; differs because heavyset is a plain Americanism for physical density that lacks stocky's suggestion of neat proportion and its faintly dignified, stump-like firmness.
  • stout: Similar because it suggests breadth and solidity of build; differs because stout points to bulk and portliness — soft weight — while stocky means firm, dense muscle; a stocky man need not be stout, and a stout man need not be compact.
  • squat: Similar because it conveys shortness and breadth; differs because squat suggests being excessively low or ungainly, sometimes of objects, with a contemptuous edge, while stocky keeps a better proportion and a neutral-to-favourable tone.
  • burly: Similar because it conveys bodily strength and a heavy frame; differs because burly adds sheer size — a big, strong man — while stocky says small but strong; the two are opposites of scale.
  • sturdy: Similar because it means strong and robustly built; differs because sturdy is about robustness generally — of objects, furniture, morale — and lacks the specific short-and-broad, stump-like geometry that stocky names.
  • robust: Similar because it describes a strong, healthy, hardy constitution; differs because robust is a broader, more abstract term for healthiness and toughness, while stocky is a concrete visual description of a compact physical frame.
  • compact: Similar because it conveys density and tightness of build; differs because compact is a general word for anything small and densely arranged, while stocky is specifically about the human or animal body and carries strength and sturdiness.
  • chunky: Similar because it describes a thick, solid, shortish shape; differs because chunky is informal and can suggest softness or awkward chunkiness, while stocky implies firm, dense, well-built muscle.
  • dumpy: Similar because it describes a short, thick figure; differs because dumpy is unflattering, suggesting a stumpy, ungainly, ill-proportioned body, while stocky is neutral-to-favourable and implies a solid, strong build.
  • beefy: Similar because it describes a large, strong, muscular body; differs because beefy emphasises bulk and muscular flesh at any height, while stocky stresses shortness combined with density and solidity.
  • brawny: Similar because it names muscular strength; differs because brawny focuses on the development of muscles — big arms and shoulders — while stocky describes the overall short, thick, well-planted frame.
  • well-knit: Similar because it describes a compactly and strongly built body; differs because well-knit stresses the harmonious joining of the parts — a neat, firm integration — while stocky foregrounds shortness, breadth, and density.
  • solid: Similar because it conveys a substantial, firmly built body; differs because solid is a general adjective of firmness and mass that can apply to objects and arguments, while stocky is the precise word for the short, broad, weighty human or animal frame.
