PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*sprengh-
"to move swiftly,
spring, hurry"
(cf. Greek spérkhesthai "to hurry")
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*springaną
"to spring up, burst
forth, leap, rise"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
springan
"to leap, burst forth,
rise, come into being"
↓
Old English (strong verb, singular past)
sprang
"leapt, burst forth,
arose, issued"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
sprang
"sprang, leapt up,
arose, originated"
↓
Modern English (past tense, 16th c.)
sprang
"sprang, leapt, burst
forth, originated"

Note: the deeper Indo-European origin of *springaną is debated; *sprengh- is proposed on the strength of Greek spérkhesthai "to hurry, be eager," but some scholars regard the Germanic verb as of obscure or sound-symbolic origin. The Germanic form itself, with its widespread cognates (Old Norse springa, German springen, Dutch springen), is certain.
