• Framework: Similar because both refer to an underlying structure that supports something built upon it; differs because framework is a broad, abstract term for any supporting structure, conceptual model, or scaffolding, whereas armature specifically implies an internal skeletal support (in art), an electromagnetic coil (in engineering), or protective biological armor.
  • Skeleton: Similar because both provide internal structural support to prevent collapse; differs because skeleton strictly denotes the biological bony system of an organism or an empty building frame, whereas armature is an artificial or specialized apparatus used in sculpting, mechanics, and linguistic theory.
  • Scaffolding: Similar because both provide support during the construction of a work; differs because scaffolding is temporary and erected externally around a building or project to be removed once complete, whereas an armature remains embedded permanently inside a sculpture or system as its core support.
  • Chassis: Similar because both serve as base structural mountings for functional components; differs because chassis refers specifically to the load-bearing frame of vehicles or electronic appliances, lacking the artistic, biological, and semiotic connotations of armature.
  • Bracing: Similar because both provide structural reinforcement against mechanical stress; differs because bracing refers to external diagonal struts or cables added for stabilization, rather than the primary internal skeleton or electromagnetic core.
  • Armor: Similar because both share the same Latin etymology and can denote protective outer coverings; differs because armor denotes external protective suits or plates against combat weapons, whereas armature has evolved into internal structural skeletons and electromagnetic rotors.
  • Substructure: Similar because both form the underlying basis of an edifice or system; differs because substructure refers strictly to the foundational base or masonry below ground level, while armature describes the intricate three-dimensional internal matrix.
