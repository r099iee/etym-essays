• Vellum: Similar because both are prepared animal-skin writing surfaces; differs because vellum is properly fine calfskin (the premium grade), while parchment covers sheepskin and goatskin of every quality.
  • Deeds: Similar because the source sentence parchments ARE deeds — conveyances of land; differs because deeds names the legal act and instrument, while parchments names them by their historic material, with antiquarian colour.
  • Charters: Similar because both are solemn written grants of rights, often on skin with seals; differs because charters are foundational grants (liberties, corporations, colonies), while parchments is the general mass of title-documents.
  • Scrolls: Similar because both are ancient skin or papyrus writings, rolled and sealed; differs because scrolls stresses the roll form (Torah, Dead Sea writings), while parchments stresses the skin material and especially legal use.
  • Manuscripts: Similar because medieval manuscripts were overwhelmingly written on parchment; differs because manuscript stresses handwriting versus print (any material), while parchment stresses the skin versus paper.
  • Muniments: Similar because both mean the title-deeds evidencing ownership of estates; differs because muniments is the lawyer precise term for defensive title papers, while parchments is the literary collective.
  • Archives: Similar because both denote hoarded old documents of record; differs because archives is the institutional repository and its holdings generally, while parchments evokes the private chest of family deeds.
  • Papyrus: Similar because both are pre-paper writing surfaces of the ancient world; differs because papyrus is laminated Egyptian reed (brittle, roll-based), while parchment is stretched animal skin (tough, codex-friendly).
  • Foolscap: Similar because both are traditional names for legal writing paper by size and use; differs because foolscap is rag or wood-pulp paper in a set size, while parchment is animal skin with far greater age and prestige.
