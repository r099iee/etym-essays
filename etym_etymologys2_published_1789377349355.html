PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*pelh₁- / *pleh₁-
"to fill; fullness, multitude, people"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*poplos / *poblikos
"army, people, belonging to the people"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
populus -> pūblicus
"the people; belonging to the state, open to the public"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
pūblicāre
"to make public property, confiscate for the state; divulge, disclose, make known to all"
↓
Old French (c. 12th–13th c.)
publier
"to make public, proclaim, declare openly"
↓
Middle English (c. 1350–1400)
publisshen
"to proclaim, make known to the people, announce publicly"
Note: Middle English adopted the verb by attaching the inchoative suffix -isshen (reflecting the Old French extended stem -iss- from Latin -iscere) to the root publi-, parallel to flourish, nourish, and vanish.
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
publish (past participle: published)
"issued in print for public distribution, officially promulgated"
