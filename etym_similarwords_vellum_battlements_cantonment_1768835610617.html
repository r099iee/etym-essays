• Parchment: Similar in being prepared animal skin used for writing; differs because parchment is the generic term (usually sheep- or goatskin), while vellum strictly denotes the finer, smoother calfskin product.
  • Papyrus: Similar in being an early writing material; differs because papyrus is plant-based — strips of pressed reed from ancient Egypt — whereas vellum is animal skin, a substance of flesh and hide rather than of the field.
  • Paper: Similar in being a writing surface; differs because paper is a sheet formed from macerated plant fibre, cheap, ubiquitous, and disposable, while vellum is the durable, dynastic membrane of the law.
  • Parapet: Similar in being a low wall along a roof or defensive wall-top; differs because a parapet is solid and unbroken, whereas battlements are the notched version with merlons and crenel gaps for shooting through.
  • Rampart: Similar in being a defensive fortification; differs because a rampart is the broad earthen or stone bank around a fortification, while battlements are the notched wall-top built upon such defences for fighting.
  • Crenellation: Similar in describing the notched parapet of a castle; differs because crenellation is the abstract noun for the pattern of merlons and crenels, while battlements are the physical wall-top itself.
  • Barracks: Similar in being military lodging; differs because barracks is the building itself, while cantonment is the whole permanent military station or district that surrounds it.
  • Camp: Similar in being a place where troops are lodged; differs because a camp is temporary and tented, whereas a cantonment is a permanent station with fixed buildings.
  • Garrison: Similar in belonging to military quartering; differs because garrison denotes the troops stationed in a place (or the fort they occupy), while cantonment is the district they are allotted as their corner.
  • Canton: Similar in descending from the same corner-district root; differs because a canton is a territorial subdivision (as in the Swiss cantons), while a cantonment is specifically the military quarter or station — the troops' corner of the map.
