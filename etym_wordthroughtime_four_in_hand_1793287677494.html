- Late 18th c. — the sporting hitch: gentlemen begin driving two pairs as one team for speed and show on the new turnpikes.
- Regency — the golden road: the Four-Horse Club and the mail coaches make four-in-hand the emblem of pace, nerve, and fashion before railways arrive.
- 1820s — Cobbett's hero: country fame belongs to the amateur whip who can hold four mouths together down a Surrey hill.
- 1856 — the club gives the name: the Four-in-Hand Driving Club formalises the sport, with meets, dinners, and a dress code.
- 1860s — the knot: clubmen loop their neckcloths like their reins, and hosiers sell the four-in-hand tie to everyone.
- Today — heritage and haberdashery: coaching survives as display sport while the knot survives on millions of necks, the horses remembered only in the name.
