PIE *seh2g- "to track down, to scent out, to perceive keenly" (the root of the hound on the trail; earliest reconstructable stage) to Latin sagīre "to scent, to perceive keenly" (2nd-1st c. BCE) to Latin sagāx (gen. sagācis) "keen-scented, shrewd" (sagīre + agentive suffix -āx; classical Latin, 1st c. BCE) to Latin sagācitās "keenness of perception, shrewdness" (1st c. BCE) to Old French sagacité (learned form; 16th c.) to English sagacity "keenness of perception, shrewdness" (16th c.) to English sagacious "having keen perception, shrewd" (taken directly from Latin sagāx; early 17th c.) to English sagaciously "in a keen-scented, shrewd manner" (sagacious + adverbial suffix -ly; 17th c.; the rootWord). Note: the Germanic branch of the same root produced Proto-Germanic *sōkijaną "to seek" (Gothic sōkjan, Old English sēcan, whence English seek and beseech; Old Norse rannsaka, whence English ransack). No secure Sanskrit reflex of *seh2g- is established; Sanskrit saj- "to cling" is a different root and is therefore omitted. The related noun presage descends from Latin praesagium "a foreboding", from praesagīre "to scent beforehand".
