1. PIE root *per- "to go through, to cross, to pass over" (reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE) — the same root as Latin per "through," Greek perán "across, beyond," Sanskrit pārayati "carries over." 2. Proto-Germanic *faranan "to go, to travel, to fare" (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE), from *per-; attested in Old English as faran "to travel, go" (> Modern English fare, forth, ford). 3. Proto-Germanic causative *farjanan "to make go, to cause to travel, to convey" (reconstructed; cognate Old Norse ferja "to ferry, to convey by boat"), formed with the causative suffix *-jan- from *faranan. 4. Old English ferian "to carry, convey, transport" (attested 9th–11th c., e.g., in the Old English Orosius and in glosses; also ferigean). 5. Middle English ferien / feri(en) "to carry, convey, ferry (especially across water)" (attested c. 1200–1500, e.g., Layamon's Brut, c. 1205, and later Middle English texts). 6. Early Modern / Modern English ferry (verb) "to convey by boat, especially across a short stretch of water" (attested from the 14th c. onward; the noun ferry "a vessel for such crossings" from the 15th c.). 7. FERRIED — the regular past tense and past participle of ferry, formed with -ed (respelled -ied after consonant + y), attested from the 15th century onward. ◄ TARGET Debated/nuanced note: the exact PIE root form and vowel grade are reconstructed on comparative evidence, not directly attested; the causative *farjanan is a standard Germanic formation, but its precise pre-Germanic ablaut is debated. The specialization to "convey by boat" is already visible in Old Norse ferja and developed in English independently by the Middle English period.
