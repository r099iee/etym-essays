• Plane trees: Similar because in American English "sycamore" actually names a plane tree (Platanus), so the two share a genus; differs because plane tree is the precise botanical group name (Platanaceae), while sycamore is the common English name, and in Britain sycamore names a maple, not a plane.
  • Maples: Similar because the British sycamore is itself a maple (Acer) with similarly broad, lobed leaves; differs because maple is the wider genus with paired samara fruits and sugary sap, and the word maple never names a plane tree or a fig.
  • Sycomore figs: Similar because they are the original biblical referent of the word; differs because a sycomore fig is a tropical fig-tree (Ficus), botanically unrelated to maples and planes, and produces fig fruit rather than samaras or seed-balls.
  • Cottonwoods: Similar because both are tall, fast-growing riverbank trees with pale trunks and broad shade; differs because cottonwoods are poplars (Populus) with fluttering leaves and cottony seeds, not planes or maples.
  • London planes: Similar because they are hybrid plane trees, the urban cousins of American sycamores; differs because London plane is a specific hybrid cultivar (Platanus x acerifolia) famed for city planting, not a general common noun.
