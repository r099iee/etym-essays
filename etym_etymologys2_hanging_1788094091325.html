NOTE: PIE *ḱenk- “to hang” is a standard reconstruction on the Germanic evidence (Gothic hahan, Old Norse hanga, Old High German hāhan). Its further cognates are debated: Latin cūnctārī “to delay” and Sanskrit śaṅkate “to doubt, hesitate” are sometimes read as the same root via “to hang back, hesitate,” but not all scholars accept these links. Within Old English two verbs coexisted — strong hōn (transitive) and weak hangian (intransitive); Middle English merged them as hangen, and modern English later split hanged (of executed persons) from hung (of things) under eighteenth-century prescriptive grammar.
