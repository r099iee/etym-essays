Primary Definition and Morphological Identity
The strong, irregular past-tense verb WORE represents the preterite indicative form of the polysemous English verb wear. It encompasses a multifaceted spectrum of core semantic meanings across English grammar, ranging from the physical carrying of clothing, adornments, or demeanor upon one's person, to the physical deterioration or erosion of materials through continuous frictional contact, and crucially, the gradual, unhurried, or exhausting passage of time.

Temporal Progression Sense (Time Wearing On / Wearing to Night)
In temporal and literary descriptive registers, wore specifically denotes the slow, inexorable, and often fatigue-laden passage of hours, seasons, or natural transitions:

  • Gradual Transition of Time: In expressions such as "as evening wore to black night" or "the day wore on," the verb describes time slipping away incrementally, minute by minute, without sudden disruption. It carries an evocative mood of quiet persistence, waiting, weariness, or atmospheric melancholy.
  • Atmospheric and Psychological Weight: This temporal sense implies that time exerts a perceptible physical and psychological friction upon human consciousness; the hours are not merely passing, but actively exhausting the energy, vigilance, or hope of those enduring them.

The Somatic and Vestimentary Senses (Garments, Adornments, and Expressions)
The most familiar everyday application of wore relates to bodily bearing and attire:

  • Clothing and Protective Gear: Carrying garments, footwear, armor, jewelry, or functional equipment on the human body (e.g., "he wore a heavy woolen cloak and steel chainmail").
  • Facial Expressions and Demeanor: Displaying a visible psychological state, countenance, or mood upon one's face or posture (e.g., "she wore an enigmatic smile," "the general wore a solemn, grim expression").
  • Hair and Personal Grooming: Maintaining a specific hairstyle, beard, or cosmetic appearance (e.g., "he wore his hair braided in the traditional manner").

Mechanical, Frictional, and Physical Erosion Senses
In physics, mechanical engineering, and geology, wore describes the physical reduction, alteration, or exhaustion of substances caused by sustained friction:

  • Frictional Abrasion: Mechanical components, stone steps, or leather soles that have suffered material loss through repetitive rubbing are said to have worn down (e.g., "centuries of pilgrim footsteps wore deep grooves into the cathedral stones").
  • Erosion and Hydraulic Weathering: River currents, ocean waves, or wind-borne sands that carve valleys and smooth boulders (e.g., "the relentless mountain torrent wore a deep canyon through the limestone ridge").
  • Depletion of Patience and Energy: Figuratively, sustained stress, conflict, or illness that exhausts human endurance (e.g., "the relentless siege wore down the defenders' resolve").

Nautical Maneuvering Sense (Wearing Ship)
In traditional sailing and naval seamanship, wore serves as the past tense of the nautical verb wear (to turn a sailing ship's bow away from the wind and bring the stern through the wind to change tacks, contrasted with tacking where the bow passes through the wind). A naval captain who wore ship executed a deliberate, wide leeward tactical turn.
