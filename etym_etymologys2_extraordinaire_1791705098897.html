extra + ordo (ordinis)

Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
extraordinarius
"out of the common order, uncommon,"
"irregular, exceptional"
↓
Old French (c. 14th c.)
extraordinaire
"unusual, exceptional, remarkable"
↓
Early Modern French (c. 17th c.)
ambassadeur extraordinaire
"diplomatic envoy on special mission"
↓
Modern English (c. 1850s–present)
extraordinaire
"outstanding, consummate, preeminent;"
"used postpositively after a noun"

Note: A direct re-borrowing of French extraordinaire in its native spelling and postpositive syntax. Ultimately derives from Latin extraordinarius (extra 'outside' + ordo 'regular order'). Distinct in English from the fully nativized prepositive adjective extraordinary.
