• Mallarmé: Similar because Stéphane Mallarmé and Charles Baudelaire are the foundational masterminds of French Symbolism who elevated the musicality, symbolic density, and spiritual mystery of poetic language; differs because Baudelaire immersed his poetry in the visceral sensory shock, urban decay, and passionate erotic anguish of metropolitan Paris, whereas Mallarmé retreated into cold hermetic abstraction, intellectual purity, and the metaphysical silence of the white page.
  • Rimbaud: Similar because Arthur Rimbaud and Baudelaire were revolutionary visionaries who broke completely with bourgeois morality and sought to transfigure human perception through radical poetic alchemy; differs because Baudelaire retained an unyielding commitment to classical prosodic form, elegant dandyism, and traditional Alexandrine balance, whereas Rimbaud violently disrupted syntax, embraced ecstatic wildness, and abandoned literature entirely at age twenty.
  • Verlaine: Similar because Paul Verlaine and Baudelaire were masters of French lyrical melancholy, exploring spiritual remorse, Parisian bohemian suffering, and the musicality of human sadness; differs because Verlaine favored delicate, understated, fluid, and song-like intimacy ("De la musique avant toute chose"), whereas Baudelaire constructed architectural, sonorous, and intellectually rigorous monuments of metaphysical despair.
  • Poe: Similar because Edgar Allan Poe was Baudelaire's supreme intellectual soulmate and literary idol, whose works Baudelaire translated into French for over a decade, both sharing a fascination with morbid psychology, formal craftsmanship, and the aesthetics of dark beauty; differs because Poe operated within American Gothic romance, deductive mystery, and grotesque macabre tales, whereas Baudelaire translated these themes into intimate urban lyric poetry and the sensory texture of modern metropolitan alienation.
  • Huysmans: Similar because Joris-Karl Huysmans drew directly from Baudelaire's aesthetic theories to create the decadent protagonist Jean des Esseintes in *À rebours*, worshiping artificiality and sensory synesthesia; differs because Huysmans worked primarily as a prose novelist and art critic documenting the neuroses of the fin-de-siècle, whereas Baudelaire was first and foremost a lyric poet whose verses established the modern poetic canon.
  • Gautier: Similar because Théophile Gautier was Baudelaire's revered mentor—to whom *Les Fleurs du mal* was famously dedicated as the "impeccable poet"—and a fellow champion of "Art for Art's Sake" (*l'art pour l'art*); differs because Gautier pursued plastic, visual, and emotionally detached Parnassian perfection inspired by classical sculpture, whereas Baudelaire infused his verses with agonizing psychological torment, Christian guilt, and raw existential despair.
