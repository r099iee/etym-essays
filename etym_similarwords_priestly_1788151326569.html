• sacerdotal: similar because both mean "of priests"; differs because "sacerdotal" is the formal theological and Latin-derived term (Latin sacerdos, "priest-sacrificer") and stresses the sacrificial function.
  • clerical: similar because both relate to the clergy; differs because "clerical" also means office-secretarial (from clerk) and covers deacons and church workers, not priests alone.
  • hieratic: similar because both suggest the sacred style of holy men; differs because "hieratic" also names the Egyptian cursive script and implies stiff, ceremonial formality.
  • ministerial: similar because both pertain to Christian religious service; differs because "ministerial" extends to pastors of any rank and to government ministers.
  • pastoral: similar because both belong to church leadership; differs because "pastoral" stresses the shepherd's care, with rural and landscape imagery, "priestly" the sanctuary and sacrifice.
  • ecclesiastical: similar because both concern the Church; differs because "ecclesiastical" describes institutions, buildings, and law, "priestly" the person and office.
  • pontifical: similar because both describe high sacred office; differs because "pontifical" refers to bishops and popes and, figuratively, means pompously dogmatic.
  • monastic: similar because both belong to dedicated religious life; differs because "monastic" concerns the monastery and vows, not the ordained priesthood as such.
  • spiritual: similar because both evoke the sacred; differs because "spiritual" is broad and personal, "priestly" institutional and ordained.
