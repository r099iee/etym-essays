• neigh: Similar because both are the vocalizations of a horse, covering the equine cry in general; differs because neigh is the louder, longer, far-carrying call that may signal alarm or summons, whereas whinny is softer, higher-pitched, and more intimate — the greeting rather than the alarm.
  • whicker: Similar because both name a soft, gentle equine sound of greeting or affection; differs because whicker is quieter still, a throaty lip-rolled murmur (typically a dam's response to her foal), while a whinny is a clearly voiced, trilled, higher cry.
  • nicker: Similar because both describe the horse's soft contact call; differs because nicker is the low, breathy, murmuring variant made with lips closed, whereas a whinny is more open, pitched, and musical.
  • snort: Similar because both are sounds a horse makes; differs because a snort is a sharp percussive blast of air through the nostrils signaling alarm or irritation, with no vocal trill, whereas a whinny is a voiced, gentle greeting.
  • bray: Similar because both are the cries of a large domestic equid; differs because bray is the harsh, raucous, unmusical cry of a donkey — loud and grating — while whinny is soft, high, and musical.
  • whine: Similar because both descend from the same onomatopoeic root meaning "to make a high, drawn-out sound"; differs because whine now belongs to humans and machines, naming a complaining or plaintive tone, and has lost all equine association.
  • whimper: Similar because both are soft, high-pitched, plaintive vocalizations; differs because a whimper is a sound of pain, fear, or pleading in humans and animals, whereas a whinny is a sound of eager greeting and pleasure.
  • titter: Similar because both can describe a high-pitched, half-suppressed laugh; differs because titter is strictly a human nervous giggle, while the laugh sense of whinny is deliberately equine and mocking in flavor.
  • giggle: Similar because both name a light, high, repeated vocalization; differs because giggle is wholly human and childish, while whinny retains the horse-sound core even in its transferred laugh use.
  • snicker: Similar because both can describe a suppressed, nasal laugh; differs because snicker implies mockery and is fully human, whereas whinny starts from the horse's call and only secondarily means a laugh.
