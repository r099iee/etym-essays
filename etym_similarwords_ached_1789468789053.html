• Throbbed: Similar because both denote continuous, rhythmic bodily sensations; differs because throbbed emphasizes a pulsating, vascular beat or surging tempo (like a swollen pulse), whereas ached conveys a heavy, unmoving, dull ache.
  • Pained: Similar because both signify experiencing hurt; differs because pained often refers to emotional distress, moral discomfort, or acute twinges, whereas ached suggests a prolonged, deep-seated physical or romantic yearning.
  • Hurt: Similar because both encompass general physical and emotional suffering; differs because hurt is a broad umbrella term that can denote momentary, superficial, or sharp injuries, whereas ached strictly implies a lingering, deep dullness.
  • Yearned: Similar because both convey intense longing for someone or something distant; differs because yearned focuses purely on the aspirational desire of the soul, while ached emphasizes the visceral, heavy somatic weight of that yearning in the chest or body.
  • Pined: Similar because both involve prolonged longing; differs because pined emphasizes wasting away, languishing, or losing vitality due to unrequited love or grief.
  • Smarted: Similar because both describe bodily discomfort; differs because smarted denotes a stinging, sharp, superficial, or burning sensation (such as a slap or antiseptic on a wound) rather than a deep, dull ache.
  • Strained: Similar because both occur after excessive physical or mental exertion; differs because strained highlights the structural tension or overextension of ligaments and faculties rather than the resulting throbbing soreness.
