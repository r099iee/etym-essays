• Ridge: Similar because both name a raised, elongated part of a mountain or landscape. Differs because a ridge is a sharp crest or spine of rock, while a shoulder is a rounded, sloping flank.
  • Spur: Similar because both name a projecting part of a mountain mass (the context's "mountain-spur"). Differs because a spur is a lateral ridge branching off a main range, sharper and more defined; a shoulder is the broad sloping mass where the mountain rounds off.
  • Brow: Similar because both name the rounded upper part of a hill or slope ("the brow of the hill"). Differs because brow stresses the very top edge, like a forehead; a shoulder is the flank below the summit.
  • Crest: Similar because both name the top of a rise. Differs because crest is the highest line or peak; shoulder is the sloping part below it.
  • Flank: Similar because both name the side of a mountain or of a body. Differs because flank is the plain side face, with no sense of roundness or of load-bearing projection.
  • Slope: Similar because both name inclined ground. Differs because slope is a generic angle of incline; shoulder implies a rounded, distinct projection.
  • Promontory / Headland: Similar because both name a projection of land. Differs because these are coastal features jutting into the sea; a shoulder belongs to a mountain or hill.
  • Bluff: Similar because both name a projecting landform. Differs because a bluff is a steep, cliff-like bank; a shoulder is rounded and gradual.
  • Hillock / Knoll: Similar because both name raised ground. Differs because they are free-standing small hills; a shoulder is part of a larger mass.
  • To assume (responsibility): Similar because both mean taking on a duty ("shoulder the blame" ≈ "assume the blame"). Differs because assume is formal and abstract, with no physical image; shoulder keeps the bodily metaphor of carrying weight.
  • To bear / To carry: Similar because both mean supporting weight, literal or figurative. Differs because bear/carry are general and may be literal or abstract; shoulder specifically means taking weight onto oneself, literal or moral.
  • To undertake: Similar because both mean taking on a task. Differs because undertake is task-oriented and neutral; shoulder implies heavy, burdensome responsibility.
  • To jostle / To shove: Similar because both mean pushing. Differs because jostle/shove are generic pushing with any part of the body; shoulder specifies pushing with the shoulder and often implies forcing a passage.
  • To push aside: Similar because both describe forcing through a crowd. Differs because push aside is neutral; shouldering is a distinctive, deliberate use of the body.
