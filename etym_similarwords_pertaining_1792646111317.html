• relating: Similar because both verbs/participles describe an intellectual or conceptual connection between two entities; differs because relating is broader, more conversational, and can refer to interpersonal empathy, emotional connection, or the narrative act of recounting a story (e.g., "relating a tale"), whereas pertaining is formal, analytical, and strictly denotes institutional relevance or legal belonging.
  • appertaining: Similar because both share the exact Latin etymological root (ad + pertinēre) and denote belonging or relevance; differs because appertaining is an elevated, archaic legal double-compound used almost exclusively in statutory law, property conveyances, and formal charters (e.g., "all privileges appertaining thereto"), whereas pertaining is widely used across academic, grammatical, and general educated prose.
  • concerning: Similar because both function prepositionally to designate the subject matter of an inquiry, discussion, or document; differs because concerning simply marks the general topic ("a book concerning gardening"), whereas pertaining implies a tighter, more direct structural or legal connection of relevance.
  • regarding: Similar because both point to the subject being referenced; differs because regarding is a standard business and epistolary connector ("regarding your invoice") that sets up a topic without implying intrinsic legal attachment or morphological relation.
  • belonging: Similar because both indicate possession, membership, or proper placement; differs because belonging emphasizes physical possession, personal identity, or emotional inclusion (e.g., "belonging to a family", "a feeling of belonging"), whereas pertaining denotes functional, legal, or thematic relevance.
  • applying: Similar because both indicate relevance or operational validity to a particular case; differs because applying suggests active implementation, enforcement, or practical deployment (e.g., "rules applying to minors"), while pertaining denotes the underlying factual or conceptual relationship itself.
  • relevant: Similar because both denote that something has a bearing upon the topic at hand; differs because relevant is a qualitative evaluative adjective that admits of degree (something can be "highly relevant" or "barely relevant"), whereas pertaining is a relational participle establishing categorical connection.
  • germane: Similar because both designate appropriate relevance; differs because germane (from Latin germen, "sprout, kin") specifically evaluates intellectual appropriateness, fitness, and direct bearing on an argumentative thesis, often carrying an evaluative philosophical tone.
  • apropos: Similar because both link an observation to an ongoing theme; differs because apropos (from French à propos, "to the purpose") is an adverb or preposition marking opportunistic, timely, or conversational relevance.
  • attaching: Similar in legal contexts where rights or liabilities become connected to a property or office; differs because attaching emphasizes the procedural, mechanical, or physical act of joining or affixing, whereas pertaining describes the established state of inherent belonging.
