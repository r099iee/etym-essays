Chain for RIBALDRY (Germanic → Romance → English; the base is Germanic, the ultimate PIE link is speculative): Stage 1 — (Speculative) PIE *wer- (2) "to turn, bend" → Proto-Germanic *wribanan "to rub." Etymonline marks this deeper link as "possibly"; the German word for "to rub" is reiben. The ultimate PIE connection is uncertain and must be labelled speculative rather than asserted. Stage 2 — Proto-West Germanic *wrīb- / *rib- "to rub" — with the euphemistic venery sense "to be wanton, to be in heat" (the "rub" → "copulate" metaphor is common across languages; Old High German riban is glossed "to be wanton," literally "to rub"). The attested Germanic base. Stage 3 — Frankish *rīban "to rub, to be wanton" → Old French riber "to be wanton, to sleep around, to dally amorously" (the Frankish-influenced Old French of the post-Carolingian period; Frankish supplies the verb to French). Stage 4 — Old French ribaut / ribalt "rogue, scoundrel, lewd lover" (12th–13th c.; noun and adjective, formed from riber with the depreciative agent-suffix *-ald / -aut, cognate with Germanic *-wald "ruler"); also Old French ribauderie "debauchery, licentiousness, bawdy speech." Stage 5 — Middle English ribaud "rascal, ruffian" (mid-13th c.; also attested as a surname from the late 12th c.), and ribaudrie "debauchery, licentiousness, coarse language" (late 14th c., from Old French ribauderie). Final node — English RIBALDRY (the modern spelling of Middle English ribaudrie; the noun settles by the 16th c. as "coarse, irreverent, jeering talk or behaviour; vulgar mockery." The adjective ribald is attested from c. 1500.) Note on the debate: the deeper PIE stage is speculative. The attested chain is secure from Proto-West Germanic *wrīb-/*rib- onward (Old High German riban, German reiben "to rub"), through Frankish into Old French riber → ribaut/ribalt → ribauderie, and into Middle English ribaudrie. The suffix *-ald/*-aut is the Germanic depreciative element seen in other French words; some scholars instead connect the Old French noun to a Latin source, but the Germanic route through the Franks is the mainstream view. No stages are invented between the attested Germanic base and English.
