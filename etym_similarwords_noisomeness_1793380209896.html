• Foulness: Similar because both denote an offensive, unclean state that repulses the physical senses; differs because foulness is an Anglo-Saxon Germanic term describing tangible dirt, moral corruption, or physical filth, whereas noisomeness specifically emphasizes noxious, nauseating exhalations and pestilential harm.
  • Noxiousness: Similar because both characterize elements that are injurious, poisonous, or deleterious to physical health; differs because noxiousness is a cooler, more clinical and scientific term focusing on chemical or biological toxicity, while noisomeness foregrounds intense sensory revulsion and sickening stench.
  • Fetidness: Similar because both describe unbearable, rotting odors; differs because fetidness is strictly confined to the olfactory domain of decaying biological matter, lacking the broader moral, spiritual, and critical allegorical dimensions of noisomeness.
  • Malodorousness: Similar because both refer to unpleasant odors; differs because malodorousness is an objective, formal Latinate descriptor that merely registers a bad smell without conveying the suffocating disgust, toxicity, or moral outrage inherent in noisomeness.
  • Rankness: Similar because both convey offensive sensory intensity; differs because rankness frequently implies gross overgrowth, excessive coarseness, or rancid oiliness in plants and animal flesh, whereas noisomeness emphasizes pestilence, decay, and sickness.
  • Mephitism: Similar because both refer to poisonous, suffocating atmospheric vapors arising from the earth or decaying matter; differs because mephitism is an archaic chemical and geological term for deadly subterraneous gas emissions, lacking the rhetorical and literary versatility of noisomeness.
  • Pestilentiality: Similar because both link sensory foulness with infectious disease; differs because pestilentiality highlights epidemic contagion and lethal transmission, whereas noisomeness focuses on the offensive sensory experience and disgust generated by the corrupt source.
  • Repulsiveness: Similar because both describe qualities that drive people back in aversion; differs because repulsiveness is a broad psychological category that can apply to visual ugliness or rude manners, whereas noisomeness is rooted in visceral, nauseating, and suffocating physical corruption.
  • Loathsomeness: Similar because both denote an extreme degree of disgust and abhorrence; differs because loathsomeness focuses on the subjective psychological feeling of revulsion felt by the observer, while noisomeness denotes the objective, active emission of offensive, sickening vapors or conduct.
