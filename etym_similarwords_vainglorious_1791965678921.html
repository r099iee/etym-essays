• boastful: Shares the core of loud self-praise; boastful describes the talk alone and can be almost boyish, whereas vainglorious adds the judgement that the glory claimed is empty and that the speaker is in love with his own image.
  • conceited: Both accuse excessive self-regard; conceited concerns the private opinion a person holds of himself, while vainglorious concerns the public demand for admiration and the hollowness of what is being admired.
  • vain: The parent word, and broader: vanity can be about appearance, dress or charm, and can be a mild foible; vainglorious is specifically about claimed merit and is always a grave condemnation.
  • arrogant: Both imply an inflated sense of superiority; arrogant focuses on contempt for others and the assumption of rank, vainglorious on the hunger for glory and the emptiness of the grounds for it.
  • pompous: Describes inflated manner and self-important delivery; it is a matter of style and can be merely tiresome, whereas vainglorious is a moral charge about the worth of the person's claims.
  • swaggering: Captures the physical, performative side of self-display; swaggering is behavioural and often youthful, while vainglorious is verbal, ceremonial and typically middle-aged or institutional.
  • self-aggrandizing: The modern analytic equivalent, used of people who inflate their power and record; it is cooler and more sociological, whereas vainglorious carries open contempt and a theological memory of sin.
  • bombastic: Applies only to inflated language; a bombastic speaker may be harmless, but a vainglorious one is accused of falsifying his own worth.
  • vaunting: An archaic-literary adjective for exulting in one's own deeds; it shares the public self-exaltation but lacks the crucial idea of emptiness, and it can even imply genuine triumphs.
  • ostentatious: About conspicuous display of wealth or status; it concerns show rather than glory, and it does not necessarily accuse anyone of lying about merit.
  • narcissistic: A clinical and psychological term for self-absorption; vainglorious is a moral and rhetorical judgement, not a diagnosis, and it is aimed at public boasting rather than inner self-love.
