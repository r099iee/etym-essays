• Weakness: Similar because both denote a point of vulnerability or failing in a person; differs because weakness is the broad, neutral word for any lack of strength, moral or physical, with no suggestion of charm, whereas foible always names a small, almost endearing failing.
  • Flaw: Similar because both denote an imperfection in a person or thing; differs because flaw is broader and more neutral — it can be structural or tragic (a fatal flaw) and applies to objects and arguments — whereas foible is always minor, personal, and affectionate.
  • Failing: Similar because both denote a shortcoming of character; differs because failing can be serious — any falling short, from a small lapse to a grave dereliction — whereas foible is by definition small and forgivable.
  • Fault: Similar because both denote a defect or imperfection; differs because fault carries a stronger sense of blame and responsibility (it is someone's fault), whereas foible is a weakness observed with indulgence, not blame.
  • Vice: Similar because both can denote a bad habit or failing of character; differs because vice is a settled, habitual immorality — serious, blameworthy, sometimes depraved — while foible is a minor, endearing weakness that never rises to the level of vice.
  • Shortcoming: Similar because both denote a failure to be perfect; differs because shortcoming stresses practical inadequacy — falling short of a standard of performance — while foible stresses the personal, charming quirk of character rather than a failure of ability.
  • Defect: Similar because both denote an imperfection; differs because defect is a structural or physical imperfection, cold and objective, often technical, whereas foible is a warm, human, minor weakness of temperament.
  • Imperfection: Similar because both denote something less than perfect; differs because imperfection is a general, neutral term applicable to anything, while foible specifically describes the small human weaknesses observed with tolerance and humor.
  • Frailty: Similar because both denote human weakness; differs because frailty is the word for human weakness in general — with a biblical, tragic, and universal ring ("frailty, thy name is woman") — whereas foible is the light, amused word for the specific little weaknesses one smiles at.
  • Quirk: Similar because both denote a peculiarity of behavior or manner; differs because quirk stresses eccentricity of manner with no moral content at all, while foible implies a small moral or temperamental weakness that is observed with indulgence.
  • Eccentricity: Similar because both denote a departure from the ordinary; differs because eccentricity stresses oddness of behavior or opinion, celebrated or pitied but morally neutral, while foible implies a minor weakness that is forgiven rather than merely noted.
  • Idiosyncrasy: Similar because both denote an individual peculiarity; differs because idiosyncrasy is a distinctive mannerism or habit unique to a person, neutral or even admirable, whereas foible carries the idea of a small weakness to be smiled at.
  • Peculiarity: Similar because both denote something distinctive to a person; differs because peculiarity is a neutral mark of individuality, while foible specifically names the weak or flawed side of that individuality.
  • Infirmity: Similar because both can denote a weakness; differs because infirmity is physical or constitutional frailty, often of the body or of resolve in old age, with no comic warmth, whereas foible is a small, endearing fault of character.
