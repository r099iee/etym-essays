• Ferumbras: Similar because it is an exact thematic, morphological, and chivalric cognate borne by other prominent Took Thains (such as Ferumbras II and III), both meaning "iron-arm" / "fierce-arm" in medieval romance nomenclature; differs because Ferumbras derives from the French chanson de geste tradition (Fierabras), whereas Isumbras incorporates the native Middle English Germanic stem isen ("iron") and references the homiletic Saint Eustace-style romance.
  • Thain: Similar because Isumbras is virtually synonymous with the office of Shire Thain, having been borne by four distinct holders of the title; differs because Thain (from Old English thegn) is a formal constitutional and military title designating the sovereign's representative in the Shire, whereas Isumbras is the specific personal and baptismal name of individual rulers.
  • knight-errant: Similar because the historical namesake, Sir Isumbras, was the quintessential medieval knight-errant wandering in search of redemption, suffering, and chivalric combat; differs because a knight-errant is a solitary, landless wanderer undertaking perilous quests, whereas Tolkien's Isumbrases were wealthy, landed Shire patricians presiding over vast agricultural estates from their ancestral smials.
  • patriarch: Similar because Isumbras I established the grand dynasty of Took Thains, serving as the venerated founding father of the clan's political dominance; differs because patriarch is a universal anthropological term for the male head of a tribe, family, or religious hierarchy, whereas Isumbras is a specific literary name carrying deep English romance associations.
  • paladin: Similar because both terms evoke the high chivalric atmosphere of the Middle Ages and the valiant knights of legend; differs because a paladin is a peer of Charlemagne's court bound by strict vows of holy warfare, whereas the historical romance hero Sir Isumbras underwent a journey of severe Christian penance and poverty before regaining his estate.
  • dynast: Similar because the name Isumbras was systematically passed down through multiple generations (I through IV) to maintain hereditary authority over the Shire; differs because dynast is an abstract political science term denoting any hereditary monarch or ruler, whereas Isumbras carries rich literary, onomastic, and philological specificity.
  • chieftain: Similar because the Thain functioned effectively as the military leader and clan chief of the Shire folk in times of peril; differs because chieftain implies a primitive or tribal social structure, whereas the Shire under Thain Isumbras was an advanced, peaceful, agrarian constitutional society.
