• Spilled: Similar because both are the past-participle and past-tense forms of the verb spill, designating liquid or matter that has accidentally escaped its container; differs because spilled is the regularized weak form favored in contemporary American English and technical reporting, whereas spilt is the traditional irregular British and Commonwealth form preserved in frozen proverbs and poetic literature.
  • Shed: Similar because both denote fluid (especially blood or tears) flowing forth or falling from a container or living body; differs because shed is often associated with organic discharge, deliberate sacrifice, or natural casting off (as a snake sheds skin or soldiers shed blood for a cause), whereas spilt emphasizes unintentional, clumsy, or wasteful physical escape.
  • Scattered: Similar because both involve substances dispersed across a surface in disorder; differs because scattered applies predominantly to dry, discrete individual objects, seeds, or particles thrown across a wide area in separate directions, whereas spilt typically applies to continuous fluids or mass granular materials escaping from a single vessel.
  • Poured: Similar because both describe the movement of liquid emerging from a receptacle onto a surface; differs because poured denotes a deliberate, controlled, and intentional act of decanting liquid into another vessel or measure, whereas spilt is strictly accidental, uncontrolled, and characterized by mishap.
  • Leaked: Similar because both designate fluids escaping confinement against the handler's intention; differs because leaked emphasizes a slow, gradual, and persistent seepage through a fissure, crack, hole, or loose seal over an extended duration, whereas spilt typically implies a sudden, momentary overturning or tipping over of the vessel.
  • Overflowed: Similar because both involve liquid extending beyond the physical limits of its container; differs because overflowed occurs when liquid volume exceeds the container's physical capacity or surges over the rim while the vessel remains upright, whereas spilt usually results from the tilting, dropping, bumping, or breaching of the receptacle itself.
  • Squandered: Similar because both metaphorically represent valuable resources that are lost, wasted, and rendered utterly unrecoverable; differs because squandered refers to the reckless, foolish, or extravagant expenditure of intangible or fungible assets (such as money, time, opportunities, or talent) through poor judgment, whereas spilt primarily anchors in physical fluid loss.
