• Newspaper: Similar because both are periodical publications of current news; differs because newspaper is the general everyday term without any official authority, while a gazette is (especially) the formal government record.
  • Journal: Similar because both are periodic records of events; differs because journal stresses periodicity and can be personal, learned, or literary, but lacks the state-authoritative role of a gazette.
  • Bulletin: Similar because both can be official notices of news; differs because a bulletin is a single short announcement, while a gazette is a regular publication compiling such announcements.
  • Chronicle: Similar because both are written records of events; differs because a chronicle is retrospective history arranged in sequence, while a gazette is a live, official, in-the-moment record.
  • Circular: Similar because both may be official notices distributed widely; differs because a circular is a one-off document sent to a group, while a gazette is a recurring state publication.
  • Organ: Similar because both can name a publication voicing an institution; differs because organ usually means a newspaper representing a political party or movement, while a gazette represents the state itself.
  • News-sheet: Similar because both are early printed news publications; differs because news-sheet is a historical, generic term, while gazette (after 1665) acquired the official-record sense.
  • Periodical: Similar because both are regularly issued publications; differs because periodical is the broad, neutral superordinate term covering magazines and journals, without gazette's official connotations.
