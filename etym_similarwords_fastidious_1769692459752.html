• Meticulous: Similar because both name extreme care over detail; differs because meticulous is purely positive and painstaking, while fastidious retains a hint of squeamishness and difficulty in pleasing.
  • Particular: Similar because both describe exacting standards; differs because particular is neutral and everyday, while fastidious is more intense and formal.
  • Fussy: Similar because both describe petty exactness; differs because fussy is informal, trivial, and slightly childish, while fastidious is formal and dignified.
  • Finicky: Similar because both describe hard-to-please fussiness; differs because finicky is colloquial and suggests fuss over trifles, while fastidious can imply refined or principled standards.
  • Picky: Similar because both mean selectively hard to please; differs because picky is casual slang, while fastidious is formal and carries cultural refinement.
  • Choosy: Similar because both describe selectivity; differs because choosy is friendly and neutral, while fastidious is critical and exacting.
  • Pernickety (UK) / Persnickety (US): Similar because both mean over-particular about small things; differs because pernickety is folksy and disapproving, while fastidious can be admiring.
  • Exacting: Similar because both describe demanding high standards; differs because exacting focuses on the demands made of others or tasks, while fastidious focuses on the person's taste and aversion.
  • Scrupulous: Similar because both describe careful attention to correctness; differs because scrupulous emphasizes moral conscience and accuracy, while fastidious emphasizes taste and refinement.
  • Demanding: Similar because both imply high standards; differs because demanding stresses the burden placed on others, while fastidious stresses the person's own discernment.
  • Squeamish: Similar because both name easy disgust at the coarse or unclean; differs because squeamish is a physical-aversive reaction (nausea), while fastidious is an aesthetic-intellectual standard.
  • Over-refined: Similar because both suggest excessive delicacy of taste; differs because over-refined implies artificial excess, while fastidious can describe genuine precision.
  • Nice (in the older "fastidious, precise" sense): Similar because both once meant "finicky, hard to please"; differs because nice has long since drifted to "pleasant," while fastidious kept its exacting core.
