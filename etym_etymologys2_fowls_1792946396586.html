PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*plew-
‘to fly, flow, float’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*fuglaz
‘bird’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
fugol
‘bird, fowl’
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
foul, fowel
‘bird, domestic fowl’
↓
Modern English (current, plural)
fowls
‘domestic birds’

Note: the leap from PIE *plew- to Germanic *fuglaz is the leading hypothesis rather than a proven chain, since no exact cognate survives outside Germanic; some scholars leave *fuglaz without a deeper etymology, and the connection should be read as probable, not certain.
