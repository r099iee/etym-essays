Note: a deliberate 20th-century coinage with no PIE, Latin or French ancestor - zero-guesswork requires the chain to stop in 1906-1907. Coined/used by scholar Brander Matthews (1906, American Character) and popularised by humorist Frank Gelett Burgess (1907 dinner for the American Booksellers' Association; Are You a Bromide? mock jacket; defined 1914 in Burgess Unabridged as "a flamboyant advertisement; fulsome praise; a sound like a publisher"). Etymonline calls it "probably derisively imitative" - echoing blurt, blubber, babble - but the imitative element is conjecture, not a documented ancestor.
