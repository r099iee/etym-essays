• Stalactite: Similar because both are tapering cones hanging from an overhead surface and are formed by slow dripping growth; differs because a stalactite is mineral (calcium carbonate) on a cave ceiling built over centuries, while an icicle is frozen water, brief and seasonal.
  • Ice dagger / Ice spear: Similar because both name long, pointed ice; differs because these are literary emphatic coinages for single impressive spikes, while icicle is the plain generic term, usually plural and domestic.
  • Frost: Similar because both are ice in winter scenes; differs because frost is a thin deposit formed from vapour directly on surfaces, while an icicle is built from dripping liquid water; frost covers, icicles hang.
  • Rime: Similar because both are accretions of ice; differs because rime is feathery ice deposited from freezing fog or wind-driven droplets on windward surfaces, not the drip-freeze growth of icicles.
  • Hoarfrost: Similar because both belong to hard frost weather; differs because hoarfrost is the white crystalline coating from vapour freezing (dew frozen solid), while icicles require liquid meltwater and a drip edge.
  • Glacier: Similar because both are masses of ice and the words share a root (Old Norse jǫkull); differs because a glacier is a huge moving river of compacted snow, while an icicle is a small static drip-built spike.
  • Ice shard / Ice splinter: Similar because both are fragments of ice; differs because shards come from breaking larger ice and have irregular sharp edges, while icicles grow whole from drips and taper to a point.
  • Needle ice: Similar because both form thin, pointed ice; differs because needle ice (pipkrake) grows upward out of saturated soil on freezing nights, while icicles hang downward from eaves, ledges, and branches.
  • Ice pendant / Ice stalactite: Similar because both describe hanging ice columns; differs because these are descriptive technical or literary synonyms for a single large icicle, while icicle is the everyday word for the whole class.
  • Drip: Similar because the first water that freezes begins as a drip before growing into an icicle; differs because a drip is the falling drop or tap-leak itself, while an icicle is the frozen accumulation hanging where drips froze.
