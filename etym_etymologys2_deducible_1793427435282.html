Note: Formed from Latin prefix dē- ("down, away from") and the verbal root dūcere ("to lead", from PIE *dewk-), with suffix -ible (from Latin -ibilis, expressing passive capability). In Classical Latin, dēdūcere originally meant physically leading troops, conducting a bride to her husband's house, or drawing out a thread of wool; its philosophical sense of logically deriving conclusions from premises was developed in Late Latin and scholastic philosophy before passing into English.
