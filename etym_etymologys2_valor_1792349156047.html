Note on historical debate & orthography: Etymologists and philologists highlight the fascinating semantic evolution from Latin valēre (which strictly meant physical health and efficacy, preserved in valid, convalesce, and the greeting valē "be well") to Late Latin valor (commercial value and intrinsic moral merit), before French feudal chivalry crystallized the word around martial bravery. In English orthography, the variant valour was standard in Anglo-Norman and British English, while the un-u'ed form valor was established in American English by Noah Webster (1828) based directly on classical Latin spelling.
