- [16th-17th century neutral adjective] - [Enters meaning simply 'of or pertaining to the theatre'; connotation neutral and technical — the stage as an institution]
- [18th century showy and artificial] - [Gains pejorative force applied to behaviour: calculated for display, mannered, insincere; the stage becomes the emblem of contrivance — acting for an audience]
- [19th century art criticism] - [Ruskin makes it a severe artistic reproach: art made for the sake of being seen, to win applause, rather than faithfully recording nature; grouped with 'affected' and 'false']
- [20th-21st century double life] - ['Theatrical' in drama remains a neutral technical adjective, but applied to people or behaviour it still means exaggerating for effect — the word slides between admiration for craft and censure for insincerity]
