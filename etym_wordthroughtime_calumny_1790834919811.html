- c. 1400s-1500s (Late Middle English): Calumny / calumnie entered from French and Latin as a term for false legal accusation, judicial fraud, and malicious deception.
- c. 1550s-1700s (Renaissance & Enlightenment): Expanded into moral philosophy, literature, and court politics as the quintessential weapon of the envious against the virtuous - Central theme in Shakespeare, Bacon, and Addison.
- c. 1800s-1900s: Maintained in high political rhetoric, historical analyses of smear campaigns, and literary treatises on public honor.
- c. 2000s-Present: Elevated, formal term for calculated character assassination, disinformation, and malicious smear campaigns in politics and public life.
