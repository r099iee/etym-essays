• Nanjing: Similar because it is the identical city, the same referent under modern pinyin romanization; differs because it is the contemporary official form, while "Nanking" is the older transcription used in historical and literary texts.
  • Beijing (Peking): Similar because it is a fellow historic Chinese capital and the natural counterpart in the "Northern/Southern Capital" pair; differs because it denotes the northern seat of imperial and now national government, while Nanking is the southern, culturally romantic capital.
  • Capital: Similar because Nanking was the capital of numerous regimes and its name literally contains the morpheme "capital" (京); differs because "capital" is a generic functional noun for any seat of government, while Nanking is a specific proper noun with dynastic and cultural associations.
  • Metropolis: Similar because Nanking is a vast, historic urban centre; differs because "metropolis" is a neutral, descriptive term for any great city, stripped of Nanking's specific history, grandeur, and southern literary identity.
  • Municipality: Similar because Nanking is today an administratively defined city; differs because it is a bureaucratic, legal term for an urban administrative unit, with none of the cultural resonance of the name.
  • Jinling (金陵): Similar because it is an older poetic name for the same city, widely used in classical literature; differs because it is a literary-historical designation meaning "Golden Hill", carrying the aura of the ancient kingdom rather than the formal "Southern Capital" title.
  • Jiankang (建康): Similar because it is the historical name of the city as capital of the Eastern Jin and Southern Dynasties; differs because it refers specifically to the medieval capital era, while Nanking/Nanjing is the name of the modern and late-imperial city.
  • Seat of government: Similar because Nanking has repeatedly been the seat of Chinese governments; differs because it is a functional phrase with no proper-noun identity, emotion, or history.
  • Shanghai: Similar because it is another great, wealthy metropolis of the same lower-Yangtze region; differs because Shanghai is a treaty-port commercial hub of modern prominence, while Nanking is the ancient political and cultural capital of the south.
