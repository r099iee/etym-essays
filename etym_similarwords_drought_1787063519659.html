• dryness: Similar because both name a condition of being without moisture; differs because "dryness" is the general state of anything being dry, while "drought" is a sustained meteorological and agricultural phenomenon.
  • dearth: Similar because both name a lack or shortage; differs because "dearth" is a scarcity of anything at all (food, money, ideas), while drought is specifically prolonged dryness or lack of rain.
  • shortage: Similar because both name a deficiency; differs because "shortage" is any insufficiency, whereas drought is the natural climatic shortage of water.
  • famine: Similar because both cause crop failure and hunger and often occur together; differs because famine is the resulting food scarcity and starvation, while drought is the climatic cause.
  • aridity: Similar because both describe extreme dryness; differs because aridity is the permanent climatic condition of a dry region (an arid climate), whereas drought is an abnormal, temporary deviation toward dryness.
  • dry spell: Similar because both name a period without rain; differs because a dry spell is a short, casual term for a brief dry period, while drought suggests prolonged, damaging dryness.
  • water scarcity / water shortage: Similar because both name a shortage of water; differs because these are resource-management terms that may result from drought but also from overuse, pollution, or poor supply.
  • thirst: Similar because both evoke the need for water; differs because thirst is the bodily sensation of a living being, while drought is an environmental condition.
