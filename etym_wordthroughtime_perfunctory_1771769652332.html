- [16th century performed through] - [From the Latin for 'performing'; first means done to get it over with, of superficial attention to form]
- [17th-18th century minimal effort] - [Settles as an adjective for duty gone through at minimum intensity — obligation discharged without heart]
- [19th century critical force] - [Becomes quietly damning in criticism: a perfunctory nod, glance, or task reveals the actor's indifference]
- [20th century to present institutional use] - [Describes checkbox compliance and routine duty; still pejorative, the opposite of absorbed, thorough work]
