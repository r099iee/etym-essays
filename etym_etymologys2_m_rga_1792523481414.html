Note on historical debate: Sanskrit grammarians (Pāṇini, Aṣṭādhyāyī 3.2.1 and Uṇādisūtras) derive mārga as a *vṛddhi* patronymic/relational formation from mṛga ("wild deer / beast"), literally meaning "the trace, slot, or trail left by a wild animal." Modern comparative linguists (such as Manfred Mayrhofer in Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindoarischen) correlate Sanskrit mṛg- / mṛgáyati ("to hunt, chase") with PIE root *merǵ- ("boundary, border, track"), cognate with Latin margo ("edge, margin, border") and Old English mearc ("mark, boundary"). A minority debate among traditional commentators considered whether mārga derived directly from the root mṛj- ("to cleanse, wipe, polish, purify"), interpreting *mārga* as "the purified or refined path," but Pāṇinian phonology and semantic trajectory overwhelmingly confirm the hunting derivation from *mṛg-*.
