• Half-open: Similar because both describe a door between open and shut; differs because half-open is a blunt report of position, while ajar suggests being caught mid-turn, precarious and provisional.
  • Cracked open: Similar because both mean only slightly open; differs because cracked open implies a mere sliver, often of a window, while ajar suggests a slightly wider, hinge-held gap.
  • Slightly open: Similar because both state a small opening; differs because slightly open is the neutral modern paraphrase, while ajar adds the note of being on the turn and possibly closing.
  • Unlatched: Similar because both can describe a door that is not fully shut; differs because unlatched describes the fastener (the latch), while ajar describes the position of the panel — a door may be ajar yet latched.
  • Unlocked: Similar because both suggest an opening one could push through; differs because unlocked concerns the lock only and says nothing about how far the door stands open.
  • Open: Similar because both are states of a door; differs because open is the absolute full position, while ajar is deliberately partial — neither open nor shut.
  • Shut: Similar because both are states of a door; differs because shut is the absolute closed position, while ajar straddles open and shut.
  • Agape: Similar because both are postpositive adverbs describing an opening; differs because agape means wide open (of mouths or doors), while ajar means only slightly open.
  • Gaping: Similar because both describe an unattended opening; differs because gaping is wide and noticeable, while ajar is narrow and easily missed.
  • On the jar: Similar because it is the older form of the same word; differs because on the jar is now obsolete dialectal usage, while ajar is the modern standard form.
