Earliest SECURE stage: Latin indulgēre
"to be kind to, yield to, grant favor to, pardon"
(attested 1st c. BCE; Plautus, Cicero)

↓

Latin indulgentia
"kindness, clemency, remission of penalty, imperial favor"
(attested 1st c. BCE)

↓

Old French indulgence
"pardon, remission, grace" (13th c.)

↓

Middle English indulgence
"remission of sin-penalty; divine grace; leniency"
(late 14th c.)

↓

Modern English INDULGENCE
"gratification, leniency, ecclesiastical remission, luxury treat"

DEBATE NOTE: No secure PIE etymon for Latin indulgēre is established. The old folk-etymology connecting it to dulcis "sweet" (in- + dulcis) is rejected by modern scholarship; a hypothesis deriving it from in- + an otherwise lost verb *dulgēre is unverifiable. The chain above therefore begins at the earliest securely attested form (Latin indulgēre) rather than at a reconstructed root.
