• Foremost: Similar because both mean first in rank or importance; differs because foremost is fully standard in modern English and describes position at the front or head, while chiefest is archaic-literary and insists on absolute primacy.
  • Principal: Similar because both mean main or most important; differs because principal is neutral, formal and standard (the principal cause), while chiefest is emphatic and archaic, and is never a noun (whereas principal is).
  • Pre-eminent: Similar because both denote standing above all others; differs because pre-eminent is Latinate, formal and current, and stresses acknowledged superiority in a field, while chiefest stresses rank and primacy in a more old-fashioned way.
  • Paramount: Similar because both mean of supreme importance; differs because paramount is a standard formal word (paramount concern), while chiefest is now literary-archaic and carries rhetorical, not legal or formal, weight.
  • Supreme: Similar because both denote the highest degree; differs because supreme is the normal modern word for absolute highest (and functions as noun and adjective), while chiefest is a rare, emphatic superlative of a different base word.
  • Greatest: Similar because both are superlatives used for the topmost degree; differs because greatest is the everyday superlative of great (size/degree), while chiefest is the archaic superlative of chief (rank/primacy) — Tolkien pairs them for emphasis.
  • Leading: Similar because both can mean most important or at the head; differs because leading is standard, modern and also participial (the leading candidate), while chiefest is archaic and purely superlative-adjectival.
  • Uppermost / topmost: Similar because both mean at the very top; differs because these are spatial and concrete in feel (topmost shelf), while chiefest is abstract, ranking and rhetorical.
  • Primary: Similar because both mean first in order or importance; differs because primary is scientific/formal and means first in a series, while chiefest means first in rank or esteem and has a grand, archaic ring.
