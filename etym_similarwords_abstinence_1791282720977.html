• Abstention: Similar because it also means deliberately holding back or not indulging; differs because it most often refers to declining to vote or decide, while abstinence centres on bodily appetites.
  • Temperance: Similar because it denotes moderation and self-control regarding drink and pleasure; differs because temperance allows moderate use whereas abstinence demands complete avoidance.
  • Continence: Similar because it means self-restraint, especially sexual; differs because continence stresses control of impulse in the moment, while abstinence stresses sustained non-engagement.
  • Celibacy: Similar because it involves sexual abstinence; differs because celibacy is a formal state of unmarried chastity, often vowed, not merely temporary restraint.
  • Chastity: Similar because it denotes sexual purity and restraint; differs because chastity is a virtue of purity of mind and conduct, wider and more moralised than factual non-activity.
  • sobriety: Similar because it describes freedom from intoxicants; differs because sobriety is the resulting clear-headed state, while abstinence is the act of refraining that produces it.
  • Self-denial: Similar because it means voluntarily going without desired things; differs because self-denial is broader and more ascetic, covering any comfort, not just appetite.
  • Renunciation: Similar because it means giving up pleasures or claims; differs because renunciation is a formal, often permanent repudiation, stronger and more public than abstinence.
  • Abnegation: Similar because it denotes self-denial and putting aside desire; differs because it is literary and extreme, implying denial of the self itself, not just of a substance.
  • Fasting: Similar because it involves going without food or drink; differs because fasting is time-limited and specifically alimentary, while abstinence can be indefinite and cover many objects.
  • Forbearance: Similar because it means refraining from action or retaliation; differs because forbearance concerns patience toward others, not denial of bodily appetite.
  • Teetotalism: Similar because it means total abstinence from alcohol; differs because it is a named pledge movement specific to drink, while abstinence is the general principle.
