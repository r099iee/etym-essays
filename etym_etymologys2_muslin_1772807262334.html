Derivation chain — single root (non-Indo-European origin; no PIE reconstruction applies): - Arabic wasala "to join, to connect, to be continuous," attested in classical Arabic. - → Arabic mawṣil "place of joining, junction, linking point" (the nominal/place form of wasala), attested in classical Arabic. - → al-Mawṣil "Mosul," the city of northern Mesopotamia on the River Tigris, so named as a "junction" (of trade routes and of the river crossings), attested from the Arabic conquest of the 7th c. CE. - → Arabic mawṣilī "pertaining to Mosul," the attributive form naming the fine cotton cloth traded from the city, attested in medieval Arabic textile usage. - → Old Italian mussolina "muslin" (the cloth of Mosul), attested 15th c., from the Italian name of the city (Mussolo / Mosul). - → French mousseline "muslin," attested early 17th c. (c. 1600–1610). - → English muslin "lightweight cotton cloth in a plain weave," attested 17th c. (c. 1670). - → MUSLIN "lightweight cotton cloth in a plain weave," modern English. Debated / nuanced points: - The word is a borrowing chain with no Indo-European ancestry: its terminus is Arabic, and the ultimate source is a Semitic verbal root, not PIE. No PIE reconstruction is applicable to this word. - The city name al-Mawṣil "Mosul" is derived from Arabic mawṣil "junction, joining," a reference to the city's position at the joining point of trade routes and of the river crossings of the Tigris; the connection of the city name to the verb wasala "to join" is standard in Arabic philology. - Dictionaries differ on whether English took the word directly from French mousseline or from Italian mussolina via French; both routes converge on the same Arabic etymon, and the French form mousseline is the immediate source recorded for English.
