• pays: Similar because it denotes giving money to settle a debt; differs because it is neutral and general, while foots stresses paying the whole, often for others.
  • covers: Similar because it means taking care of costs; differs because it is broader and can mean partial cover, while foots implies the entire bill.
  • settles: Similar because it means discharging a bill finally; differs because it stresses finality and agreement, not who bears the burden.
  • defrays: Similar because it means providing money for expenses; differs because it is formal and literary, while foots is colloquial and idiomatic.
  • shoulders: Similar because it means taking on a burden or cost; differs because it foregrounds moral or heavy responsibility, while foots is purely financial.
  • bears: Similar because it means carrying costs; differs because it is abstract and can be non-monetary (bears responsibility), while foots is cash-specific.
  • bankrolls: Similar because it means financing a person or venture; differs because it implies ongoing sponsorship, while foots is usually a single bill or recurring bills.
  • funds: Similar because it means supplying money; differs because it suggests providing a budget in advance, while foots suggests paying after the fact.
  • underwrites: Similar because it means guaranteeing costs; differs because it is formal financial jargon implying risk assumption, while foots is everyday idiom.
  • picks up (the tab): Similar because it is the exact idiomatic equivalent of paying for others; differs because it is American and hospitality-flavoured, while foots the bill is Anglo-American and broader.
  • meets (expenses): Similar because it means satisfying costs; differs because it is formal and implies adequacy, while foots implies the act of handing over.
