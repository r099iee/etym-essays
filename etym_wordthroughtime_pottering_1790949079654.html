- Old English to Middle English - Root verb potian / poten meant to push, poke, prod, or thrust with a stick.
- 16th to 17th Century - Addition of the frequentative suffix -er created dialectal potter / pooter, meaning to poke repeatedly, poke about in the dirt, or fumble with small objects.
- 18th Century - Semantic expansion from physical prodding to dawdling, doing little jobs slowly, or wasting time in a pleasant manner.
- 19th Century - Cemented in British Victorian and Edwardian literature (and humorists like Jerome K. Jerome and P.G. Wodehouse) as the quintessential middle-class weekend leisure pastime.
- 20th to 21st Century - Continues in modern English as a beloved idiom for cozy, restorative, self-directed domestic downtime.
