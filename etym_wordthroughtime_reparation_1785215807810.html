- 15th century repair - enters English meaning the act of restoring or repairing a damaged thing; the word is concrete, close to mending a broken object or a ravaged land.
- 16th-17th century moral amends - extends from mending things to making amends: reparation becomes the compensation, satisfaction, or restitution offered for a wrong or injury, at once practical and moral.
- 18th-19th century compensation - settles into the formal language of law, commerce, and diplomacy for making good a material loss; in the Mill episode it names the £100 pressed on the aggrieved Carlyle, a concrete act of responsibility.
- 20th-21st century historical scale - the plural reparations comes to dominate public memory: war reparations after 1918 and 1945, and demands for reparations for slavery and colonial injustice; the word now swings between private amends and vast historical reckoning.
