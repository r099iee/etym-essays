• Happening: Similar because both refer to the occurrence of events; differs because 'happening' is completely neutral and informal, whereas 'befalling' almost always connotes significant, momentous, or adverse occurrences arriving like fate.
  • Occurring: Similar because both describe events taking place; differs because 'occurring' is objective, scientific, and impersonal, whereas 'befalling' is literary, emotionally charged, and directed at a recipient who suffers or experiences the event.
  • Transpiring: Similar because both describe things coming to pass; differs because 'transpiring' often implies events becoming known after having taken place secretly or gradually over time, whereas 'befalling' emphasizes the descent of fortune or calamity onto a subject.
  • Overtaking: Similar because both denote an event coming upon someone; differs because 'overtaking' implies catching up to someone from behind or overwhelming them with sudden force, whereas 'befalling' highlights the destined or passive reception of circumstances.
  • Betiding: Similar because both are archaic/literary Germanic verbs for events happening to someone ('woe betide'); differs because 'betiding' is far more obsolete and predominantly used in subjunctive warnings or conditional curses.
  • Striking: Similar because both describe calamitous events impacting a subject; differs because 'striking' emphasizes sudden violent impact, whereas 'befalling' encompasses both sudden disasters and prolonged turns of fate.
  • Descending: Similar because both suggest motion from above; differs because 'descending' denotes literal physical downward movement or genealogical succession, while 'befalling' is metaphorical event occurrence.
