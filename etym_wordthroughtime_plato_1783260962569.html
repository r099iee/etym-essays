- Ancient Athens - Plato (c. 427-347 BC), the pupil of Socrates and teacher of Aristotle, founded the Academy and redirected Western philosophy toward the soul, the Forms, and the ideal state, and his dialogues made him the divine Plato in antiquity.
- Late antiquity and Byzantium - through Neoplatonism his name remained the summit of Greek thought, and Macaulay's pedants of Constantinople could still read and interpret his masterpieces while themselves destitute of taste and spirit.
- Renaissance recovery - recovered and translated in fifteenth-century Florence, Plato became the symbol of idealist philosophy and Platonic love, a name synonymous with the transcendent and the spiritual.
- Contemporary sense - Plato is one of the two pillars of Western philosophy (with Aristotle), and the adjective platonic (non-sexual love, from his Symposium) keeps his name in everyday speech.
