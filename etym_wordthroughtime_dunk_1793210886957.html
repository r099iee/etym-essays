- Pennsylvania German tables — the immigrant verb: dunke names dipping bread in coffee, a farmhouse ritual carried to America in the 1700s.
- 1919 — print notices it: American writers record dunk for dipping doughnuts, slightly comic, slightly foreign, entirely delicious.
- 1930s — the basketball leap: players begin thrusting the ball down through the hoop, and the table word becomes the arena word.
- 1967–1976 — banned for being too good: college basketball outlaws the dunk because one giant made it unfair, which only gilds its legend.
- 1980s–90s — slang takes flight: to dunk on someone spreads from playgrounds to politics, naming any stylish public humiliation.
- Today — kitchen and court together: biscuits engineered for dunkability share the word with slow-motion replays of hands above the rim.
