• Morasses — Similar because both name swampy ground and, figuratively, entangled situations hard to escape. Differs because morass (from Dutch moeras, "marsh") stresses confusion and overwhelming complexity, while quagmire stresses the sinking, holding terrain and the danger of struggling deeper into it.
  • Swamps — Similar because both are literal wetlands and figurative overwhelming situations. Differs because swamp as a verb means "overwhelm, flood, inundate" (swamped with work), while quagmire means "entangle so that escape is difficult"; the two images are floods versus quicksand.
  • Bogs — Similar because both are soft, wet, spongy ground. Differs because bog is the plainer, more neutral literal term (and lightly figurative in "bogged down"), while quagmire is more literary and carries the strong sense of treacherous instability and strategic disaster.
  • Predicaments — Similar because both are difficult situations. Differs because predicament is abstract, neutral, and covers any awkward fix, while quagmire specifically images a sinking, worsening trap from which withdrawal is costly.
  • Mires — Similar because both involve mud and sinking; mire is literally "deep mud, bog" and figuratively an entangling condition ("mired in debt"). Differs because mire tends to name the substance or the condition, while quagmire names the whole treacherous terrain or situation with greater dramatic force.
  • Quicksands — Similar because both are literal and figurative traps that swallow and hold. Differs because quicksand is a specific physical phenomenon (loose wet sand that sucks down) and its figurative use stresses inescapable descent, while quagmire stresses trembling boggy ground and, figuratively, entangling complexity.
  • Imbroglios — Similar because both are complicated, tangled situations. Differs because imbroglio (Italian) stresses interpersonal confusion, scandal, or misunderstanding, while quagmire stresses the sinking, inescapable structure of the situation itself.
  • Entanglements — Similar because both mean involvement in something difficult to escape. Differs because entanglement is abstract and can be neutral (legal entanglements), while quagmire is dramatic, usually pejorative, and full of the sinking image.
