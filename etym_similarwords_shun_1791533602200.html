• avoid: Similar because both involve keeping away from a person, situation, or behavior; differs because avoid is emotionally neutral and purely practical (one avoids a pothole or rush hour), whereas shun implies intentional, morally charged, or socially total rejection.
  • eschew: Similar because both signify deliberate abstention from something harmful or inappropriate; differs because eschew is an elevated, intellectual term applied almost exclusively to vices, practices, philosophies, or material indulgences, whereas shun applies forcefully to persons and social groups.
  • ostracize: Similar because both involve collective social exclusion; differs because ostracize denotes a formal or communal banishment by an entire community or institutional body, whereas shun can be practiced individually or as an intimate personal policy.
  • spurn: Similar because both express rejection; differs because spurn involves active contempt, disdainful refusal, or an overtly haughty gesture of dismissal (e.g. spurning an offer or lover), whereas shun is characterized by quiet, persistent evasion or turning away.
  • cold-shoulder: Similar because both describe socially ignoring someone; differs because cold-shoulder is an informal, colloquial idiom describing deliberate unfriendliness or social chilliness in a casual environment, lacking the deep moral or religious gravity of shun.
  • boycott: Similar because both describe organized non-participation or avoidance; differs because boycott is specifically an economic, commercial, or political tactic designed to exert financial pressure on an entity.
  • snub: Similar because both describe a deliberate slight; differs because snub refers to a sharp, sudden, and often public insult or rebuff delivered in a specific encounter, rather than a continuous, systematic avoidance.
  • evade: Similar because both involve escaping contact; differs because evade suggests cunning, stealth, or dodging an obligation, tax, or pursuer, whereas shun is an open refusal to associate.
  • disdain: Similar because both are driven by feeling above or repulsed by something; differs because disdain is an internal attitude of haughty contempt, whereas shun is the external behavioral action of avoidance.
  • steer clear of: Similar because both mean keeping at a safe distance; differs because steer clear of is an informal maritime metaphor focusing on avoiding hazard or inconvenience rather than deep moral disapproval.
