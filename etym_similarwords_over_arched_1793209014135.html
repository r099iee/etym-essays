• Arched: Similar because both describe a curved spanning shape; differs because arched simply means curved like a bow — arched backs, arched windows — while over-arched means something stands beneath the curve, sheltered and shaded by it.
  • Vaulted: Similar because both name spaces roofed by arches; differs because vaulted is architectural masonry — vaulted ceilings, vaulted crypts — while over-arched reaches beyond buildings to trees, grottoes, and eyebrows.
  • Domed: Similar because a dome is a great arch rotated into a roof over all; differs because domed implies a rounded enclosed crown like St Paul's, while over-arched implies a passage or hollow with an opening at each end.
  • Canopied: Similar because a canopy likewise roofs from above; differs because a canopy is hung fabric or foliage spread flat, while an over-arching cover curves down on both sides like a tunnel.
  • Spanned: Similar because engineers span gaps with arches and beams; differs because spanned stresses crossing from side to side, while over-arched stresses the sheltering roof felt from below.
  • Embowered: Similar because a bower is a leafy arched shelter, the woodland cousin of the over-arched gully; differs because embowered is purely poetic greenery for lovers, while over-arched also covers stone, bone, and abstract ideas.
  • Overshadowed: Similar because both darken what lies beneath; differs because overshadowed stresses gloom and dominance — overshadowed lives and talent — while over-arched keeps the shelter's tenderness alongside its shadow.
  • Enclosed: Similar because an over-arched hollow feels shut in; differs because enclosed means shut on all sides by any barrier, while over-arched names specifically the roof of arch above, open at the ends like a nave.
