• Caesar: Similar because he was Pompey's ally and then his rival, the other half of the pair that destroyed the Republic; differs because Caesar crossed the Rubicon, won the civil war and became dictator, while Pompey defended the Senate and lost.
  • Crassus: Similar because he was the third member of the First Triumvirate and a consular colleague; differs because Crassus' power rested on wealth and patronage rather than military glory, and his death at Carrhae in 53 BCE broke the alliance.
  • Sulla: Similar because Pompey began his career under Sulla and learned from him how a successful general could dominate the state; differs because Sulla seized a formal dictatorship and then retired, whereas Pompey preferred extraordinary commands within republican forms.
  • Marius: Similar because both were generals whose armies and popular support made them decisive political players; differs because Marius was a “new man” from Arpinum and a seven-time consul, while Pompey was a wealthy provincial aristocrat who never quite mastered the political game.
  • Cicero: Similar because Cicero was Pompey's political ally and championed the same cause in 63 and in 49 BCE; differs because Cicero had no army and relied on speech, whereas Pompey's authority was military and his indecision in the field proved fatal.
  • Alexander the Great: Similar because Pompey was compared to Alexander and consciously imitated him in the East; differs because Alexander conquered an empire and died at thirty-two at the height of his power, while Pompey's eastern settlement was administrative and his end was defeat and murder.
  • imperator: Similar because the title was acclaimed by soldiers to a victorious general and Pompey bore it; differs because it is an honour, not a name, and later became the imperial title par excellence.
  • triumvir: Similar because Pompey was one of three men who dominated the state; differs because the word denotes a member of a board or alliance, and covers the legally constituted Second Triumvirate as well.
  • warlord: Similar because Pompey commanded personal armies and negotiated with the Senate from a position of force; differs because the term is modern and implies the collapse of central authority, whereas Pompey insisted on republican legality.
  • admiral: Similar because Pompey's pirate command was naval and gave him control of the sea; differs because admiral is a professional rank, not a personal ascendancy over the state.
