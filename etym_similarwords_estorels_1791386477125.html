• Estérel: the standard singular French name for the same massif; differs only in number and spelling, and is the form normally used on maps.
  • Esterèu: the Provençal name for the range; differs by being the local Occitan endonym rather than the French or English exonym.
  • Massif de l'Estérel: the full official French designation; differs by explicitly naming the massif as a geological unit.
  • Estérel Massif: the common English atlas form; differs only by word order and by its cartographic register.
  • Maures: the neighbouring crystalline massif to the west, similar in rock, maquis and Riviera setting; differs because it is a separate range with its own name and higher peaks.
  • Tanneron: the smaller massif immediately to the east, similar in red volcanic scenery; differs by its position, its own protected forest and its separate toponym.
  • Corniche d'Or: the coastal road along the Estérel, similar in being a Riviera landmark; differs because it names the route, not the mountain.
  • Côte d'Azur: the wider tourist region, similar in its glamorous Mediterranean associations; differs because it is a broad coastal brand, not a specific massif.
  • Calanques: the drowned rocky inlets characteristic of the Estérel coast, similar in scenery; differs because the word names a landform type, not this place.
  • Riviera: the general international term for the coast, similar in evoking sun and sea; differs by covering a much larger area and carrying a social rather than geological meaning.
  • Estérelle: the legendary fairy associated with the massif in local folklore, similar in name and local colour; differs because it is a folkloric personage, not the place itself.
  • Esterel: the unaccented international spelling, similar to the source form; differs by dropping the acute accents and usually using the singular.
