• mummified: Similar because both preserve a corpse against decay; differs because mummified specifically denotes drying or Egyptian ritual preservation (often of wrapped, unwrapped mummies), whereas embalmed implies chemical or spice treatment that keeps the body recognisable.
  • preserved: Similar because both mean kept from decay; differs because preserved is generic — jams, relics, documents — and carries no funerary force at all.
  • pickled: Similar because both treat bodies or tissues in preservative fluids; differs because pickled is coarse, comic, or laboratory register (specimens in jars), not a reverent funeral rite.
  • cured: Similar because both arrest decay; differs because cured applies to meat, leather, or illness, never to a human corpse treated with dignity.
  • anointed: Similar because both involve applying substances to a body, often in a religious setting; differs because anointing is ritual consecration with oil, with no preservation function.
  • perfumed: Similar because both involve fragrant substances; differs because perfuming merely scents the surface and does nothing to stop decay.
  • interred / buried: Similar in funerary context; differs because burial is deposition in the ground and implies no preservation of the body at all.
  • petrified: Similar in the metaphor of being frozen in time; differs because petrified implies hardening to stone, while embalmed implies preserved softness and lingering aroma.
