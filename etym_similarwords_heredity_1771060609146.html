• inheritance: Similar because both words describe something passed down from predecessors to successors. Differs because inheritance is predominantly legal and material — money, land, rights, titles — while heredity centres on the biological and involuntary transmission of traits; inheritance can also be freely chosen (a bequest), whereas heredity cannot.
  • genetics: Similar because both concern the transmission of traits across generations. Differs because genetics is the empirical science of genes, DNA, and molecular mechanisms, while heredity names the phenomenon itself and carries older legal, literary, and figurative layers that genetics lacks.
  • lineage: Similar because both concern descent from ancestors. Differs because lineage emphasises the traceable line of descent — the family tree, its record and prestige — while heredity focuses on the transmission of traits and endowments rather than the genealogical narrative.
  • genealogy: Similar because both relate to ancestors and family connection. Differs because genealogy is the systematic study and documentation of descent, a scholarly practice, while heredity is the biological and legal fact of what is transmitted.
  • succession: Similar because both describe the passage of something from one holder to the next. Differs because succession is a formal legal and political term for order of accession, while heredity covers biological, cultural, and figurative transmission far beyond mere order of office.
  • heritage: Similar because both denote what is received from the past. Differs because heritage is typically cultural, historical, or natural treasure shared collectively, while heredity is the personal endowment transmitted to individuals, usually biologically.
  • bloodline: Similar because both link inherited qualities to kinship and ancestry. Differs because bloodline is informal and emphasises aristocratic or racial purity of descent, while heredity is neutral, scientific, and broader in scope.
  • ancestry: Similar because both point back to one's forebears. Differs because ancestry names the line of ancestors themselves, while heredity names the transmission of traits through that line; ancestry is descriptive, heredity is causal.
  • predisposition: Similar because both can explain why a trait or illness appears in a family. Differs because predisposition is narrower and probabilistic — a tendency that may or may not develop — while heredity is the general mechanism of transmission of which predisposition may be a part.
  • atavism: Similar because both involve the reappearance of ancestral traits. Differs because atavism specifically means the throwback to a remote ancestor (often pejoratively), while heredity covers all transmission, recent or remote, favourable or not.
