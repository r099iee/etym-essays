*legʷʰ- (PIE, reconstructed)
"light, having little weight"
(source also of Latin levis "light";
English light, lung, lever)

▼

levis (Latin, Classical)
"light in weight, not heavy"

▼

levāre (Latin, 1st c. BCE)
"to make light, raise, lift up, lighten"
(causative verb formed on levis)

▼

lever (Old French, from 9th c.)
"to raise, lift up"
(continuing Latin levāre)

▼

levée (Old French, 12th c.)
"a raising; the summoning of vassals;
the collection of dues and taxes"
(feminine past participle of lever used as a noun)

▼

levie / levy (Middle English, c. 1400)
"an assessment, tax collection, or
compulsory recruitment of troops"
(via Anglo-Norman administration)

▼

levy (English noun and verb)
"the act of raising troops or taxes;
the troops or money so raised"

▼

LEVIES (modern English plural)
"conscripted troops, taxes, or compulsory
contributions raised by legal or
military authority"
