Latin candēla (n.) "candle, torch; a light made of wax or tallow" (classical Latin, from candēre "to shine, glow, be white-hot"; the base is a Latin stem of shining — a deeper PIE root *kand- "to glow, shine" is often proposed but is not required for this chain) — the candle-noun that entered North Germanic as a loan → Old Norse kyndill (n.) "candle, torch" (borrowed into North Germanic from Latin candēla, attested in Old Norse; the candle kept the fire/light image alive in the same word-family) → Old Norse kynda (v.) "to kindle, to set on fire, to light" (the verb, attested in Old Norse; its exact formation is debated — see note — and it is commonly grouped with kyndill in a fire-lighting complex) → Middle English kindlen / kindel / kindle (v.) "to kindle, to light, to set on fire" (borrowed from Old Norse kynda in the northern Danelaw contact zone, from around 1200) → Modern English kindle (v.) "to set fire to, to ignite; to arouse, excite" (the standard verb, with the -n- inserted in the infinitive as with other Norse borrowings) → English KINDLING (present participle and gerund of kindle; also noun "the material that starts a fire", attested as a noun from the 16th century) — the card's target form. NOTE on the debate: the ultimate origin of the Old Norse verb kynda is not securely established. Its most obvious relative is the candle-noun kyndill, itself a Latin loan (candēla), and many dictionaries therefore treat "to kindle" as built within the candle/fire-shining complex rather than as an inherited Indo-European verb; a deeper PIE root for the shining base (*kand- "to glow") is proposed for Latin candēre, but the Norse verb need not descend directly from it. An alternative, frequently repeated but not proven, connects kynda to the Germanic "birth, beget" root (*ǵenh₁- "to give birth, beget") through the idea of "bringing fire into being"; this is attractive but is treated by careful dictionaries as speculative. What is secure is the attested path Old Norse kynda → Middle English kindlen → kindle → kindling.
