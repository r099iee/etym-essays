• Throbbing: Similar because both name a rhythmic beating, especially of the heart under strong feeling; differs because throbbing is a steady, aching pulse while palpitating is rapid and irregular, a flutter rather than a beat.
  • Pulsating: Similar because both denote repeated beating or pulsing; differs because pulsating is a regular, even rhythmic beat, as of music or light, without the agitation of a racing, uneven heart.
  • Pounding: Similar because both describe a strong heartbeat; differs because pounding is a violent, heavy hammering under exertion or rage, whereas palpitating is lighter, fluttering, and irregular.
  • Quivering: Similar because both mean trembling motion; differs because quivering is a small involuntary shake of cold or fear, of the limbs or voice, not specifically the rapid beating of the heart.
  • Trembling: Similar because both denote shaking under emotion; differs because trembling is coarse bodily shaking (hands, voice, body) rather than the heart's quick, fluttering beat.
  • Fluttering: Similar because both describe light, rapid, irregular motion; differs because fluttering is delicate and feathery (wings, a nervous heart), while palpitating is more agitated and alarming.
  • Palpable: Similar because it shares the same Latin root and the idea of touch; differs as a false friend — it means tangible or obvious, never 'throbbing.'
