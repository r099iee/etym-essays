• steadfast: Similar because both convey unshakable loyalty and constancy in purpose; differs because steadfast emphasizes emotional loyalty, moral dependability, and standing firm in an established position or relationship, whereas resolute emphasizes active intellectual determination and decisiveness in a course of action.
  • determined: Similar because both denote a settled intention to achieve a goal; differs because determined is a broader, everyday psychological term denoting focused effort, whereas resolute carries a higher moral dignity, suggesting courage and dignified refusal to waver under severe trial.
  • tenacious: Similar because both describe an unwillingness to yield; differs because tenacious evokes the physical metaphor of holding on tightly with claws or grip (often associated with stubborn persistence or clinging to an objective), whereas resolute reflects principled, dignified firmness of mind.
  • unwavering: Similar because both highlight freedom from hesitation or doubt; differs because unwavering is primarily descriptive of an unbroken line of sight, steady gaze, or consistency over time, whereas resolute describes the underlying internal fortitude that generates that consistency.
  • adamant: Similar because both denote an inflexible refusal to change a position; differs because adamant carries a more unyielding, hard-edged, and uncompromising connotation (from Greek adamas, unbreakable diamond), often appearing in negative contexts of refusal to negotiate, whereas resolute possesses positive, heroic connotations of noble resolve.
  • intransigent: Similar because both describe refusing to budge; differs because intransigent carries an overwhelmingly critical political connotation of stubborn refusal to compromise or reach practical accommodations, whereas resolute denotes admirable moral courage.
  • unyielding: Similar because both describe resistance to external bending or softening pressure; differs because unyielding emphasizes physical or behavioral rigidity, whereas resolute emphasizes an enlightened, conscious moral purpose.
  • staunch: Similar because both denote strong, dependable support; differs because staunch is most frequently paired with loyalty, belief, or friendship ("a staunch ally"), focusing on fidelity to another, whereas resolute focuses on internal self-determination.
