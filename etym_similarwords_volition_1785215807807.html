• Will: Similar because both denote the mental power to choose or decide; differs because will is the broad, everyday word for the power itself, while volition is the formal, philosophical term for the individual act or exercise of that power.
  • Choice: Similar because both involve the making of a decision; differs because choice emphasizes the selection among alternatives and its outcome, whereas volition emphasizes the inner act of willing itself rather than the item chosen.
  • Decision: Similar because both concern the settling of a course of action; differs because decision names the act or result of settling between alternatives, while volition keeps its focus on the willing faculty and the deliberate act behind the decision.
  • Desire: Similar because both precede action and motivate it; differs because desire is the passive longing or appetite that precedes the will, whereas volition is the active resolution that turns wanting into decision.
  • Intention: Similar because both are mental states directed at action; differs because intention names a plan or aim that may or may not issue in action, while volition is the executive step in which intention is converted into a willed act.
  • Determination: Similar because both imply resolve to act; differs because determination stresses firmness and persistence in holding to a course, while volition is the immediate act of willing a particular action.
  • Resolution: Similar because both denote a fixed purpose formed by the will; differs because resolution emphasizes the firm, settled character of the purpose, while volition emphasizes the faculty and act of willing itself.
  • Free will: Similar because both concern the capacity for self-determined choice; differs because free will is the grand philosophical question of whether choice is genuinely free, while volition is the neutral term for the act of willing, without committing to a theory of freedom.
  • Agency: Similar because both denote the capacity to act deliberately; differs because agency is the broader term for being a source of action and effect, while volition is specifically the exercise of the will in choosing.
  • Purpose: Similar because both involve a directedness of action; differs because purpose names the end or aim being sought, while volition names the act of willing by which the purpose is adopted.
  • Preference: Similar because both involve the mind selecting among options; differs because preference is a disposition toward one option, often unreflective, while volition is the deliberate, executive act of choosing.
  • Deliberation: Similar because both belong to the process of deciding; differs because deliberation is the weighing of reasons before the decision, while volition is the concluding act of the will that actually chooses.
  • Impulse: Similar because both can move a person to act; differs because an impulse is a sudden, involuntary urge, while volition is deliberate, conscious, and self-controlled — the very opposite of impulse.
  • Consent: Similar because both concern the will's assent; differs because consent is the will's agreement to what another proposes, usually in a formal or legal setting, while volition is the general power and act of willing one's own course.
