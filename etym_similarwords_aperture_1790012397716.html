• opening: Similar because both denote a gap that allows passage; differs because opening is the generic everyday word, while aperture is technical and stresses calibrated roundness or optical function.
  • hole: Similar because both mean an empty void in a surface; differs because hole is blunt, often irregular and accidental, while aperture is precise, often circular and functional.
  • gap: Similar because both mean a break or interval; differs because gap stresses separation between two things, while aperture stresses a hole through one thing.
  • slit: Similar because narrow opening admitting light; differs because slit is long and thin, while aperture is usually round or adjustable.
  • orifice: Similar because anatomical or technical mouth-like opening; differs because orifice is formal for bodily or tubular mouths, while aperture is broader and optical.
  • mouth: Similar because entrance to a cavity; differs because mouth is animate or container-like (mouth of cave, river), while aperture is abstract-geometric.
  • vent: Similar because opening for air release; differs because vent implies outflow and ventilation purpose, while aperture implies light or sight passage.
  • perforation: Similar because hole punched through material; differs because perforation stresses the act of piercing and often small repeated holes, while aperture stresses the resulting open area.
  • window: Similar because glazed opening for light and view; differs because window is architectural with frame and glass, while aperture can be empty and is often adjustable.
  • pupil: Similar because light-admitting eye opening controlled by iris; differs because pupil is specifically anatomical, while aperture is the general optical analogue.
  • eye: Similar because in needles, storms, and cameras the eye is a hole; differs because eye is metaphorical and animate, while aperture is technical.
  • passage: Similar because channel allowing movement through; differs because passage stresses transit route, while aperture stresses the cross-sectional opening itself.
