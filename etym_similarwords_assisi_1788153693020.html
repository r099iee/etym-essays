• Padua: Similar because an Italian town inseparable from its patron saint (St Anthony) and famous for a pilgrimage basilica. Differs because Padua grew into a great university city and industrial centre, whereas Assisi remains a small hillside shrine-town dominated by the Franciscan complex.
  • Loreto: Similar as an Italian pilgrimage town built around a single holy place (the Holy House of the Virgin). Differs because Loreto’s cult is Marian and later (15th c.), while Assisi is bound to Francis and Clare and to a whole religious order.
  • Canterbury: Similar as a European town whose cathedral makes it a pilgrim destination connected to a famous saint (Thomas Becket). Differs because Canterbury is an English cathedral city and primatial see, while Assisi is an Italian hill-town and the cradle of the mendicant Franciscans.
  • Subiaco: Similar as an Italian monastic town above a gorge, holy through one founder (St Benedict). Differs because Subiaco anchors Benedictine monasticism and the monastery library, whereas Assisi means the Franciscan movement of the open air and the street.
  • Nazareth: Similar as a small holy town whose name metonymically evokes a religious founder and his message. Differs because Nazareth is the evangelical homeland of Jesus in Galilee and carries the weight of the Gospels, whereas Assisi is tied to the medieval church and to Franciscan renewal.
