Preponderantly is the adverb of preponderant: it says that something holds true by outweighing rather than by excluding — mostly, mainly, for the greater part, because the heavier side of the scales tips the balance. Where exclusively claims everything and partly claims only some, preponderantly claims the decisive bulk.

In the source sentence the word weighs a whole folklore: the verbal art of all the Eastern and Southern Slavic territory has always been of preponderantly epic character — the claim is carefully scholarly, conceding that lyric and ritual songs exist while insisting the weight of the tradition falls on epic narrative. The adverb does the work of honest quantification in a field where counting songs is impossible.

Core weighing sense in detail: The image is the balance scale: one pan sinks because more weight lies in it, and the verdict follows the heavier pan. Preponderantly epic means epic outweighs; a preponderantly young crowd means youth outweighs; preponderant evidence means proof outweighs doubt. The word always implies a contest of portions, decided but not unanimous.

All senses, primary and secondary:

  • For the most part, mainly (primary modern sense): a preponderantly rural population, preponderantly male enrollment. The bridge is the scale: the greater weight decides the description, minorities acknowledged but outweighed.
  • By weight of evidence or argument (evidential): the data point preponderantly one way, opinion running preponderantly against. The bridge carries the physical scale into reasoning: reasons are weighed like ingots, and the heavier pan wins the sentence.
  • In greater numbers or force (demographic and military): forces preponderantly infantry, a fleet preponderantly submarines. The bridge is counting by weight: numbers become mass, and mass tips the description.
  • With decisive but not total influence (causal): a crisis preponderantly economic in origin, a style preponderantly French in influence. The bridge softens causation into weighting: many causes enter, one outweighs.
  • In older use, with literal outweighing (literal, now rare): the heavier pan weighing preponderantly down. The bridge is the original physical balance, from which all figurative weighings descend.
  • Scholarly hedging quantifier (academic register): traditions described as preponderantly this or that, as in the source sentence. The bridge is methodological caution: the scholar weighs an uncountable corpus and reports the tilt of the scales.

Register and connotation: preponderantly is formal, Latinate, and judicious — the committee room, the courtroom, and the seminar rather than the street. It flatters its user with precision and its subject with measured judgment: to say preponderantly is to refuse both exaggeration and evasion. The price is stiffness; no one says it lightly, and its very syllables tip the scale slowly, weightily, one by one.
