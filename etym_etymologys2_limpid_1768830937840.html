PIE *- (uncertain; Greek →)nymphE "bride, young woman; water-divinity" (Homeric, 8th c. BCE; of uncertain, possibly pre-Greek origin) → Latin lympha "water, water-deity" (early republican loan from Greek nymphe, attested 2nd-1st c. BCE; also spelled lumpa) → Latin limpidus "clear, transparent, pure" (derived on lympha with suffix -idus and dissimilation n → l; attested 1st c. CE, Pliny the Elder and Seneca) → Old French limpide (learned, 13th-14th c.) → Middle French limpide "clear" (16th c.) → English limpid "clear, transparent" (borrowed c. 1610, from French limpide or directly from Latin limpidus). NOTE: The derivation of limpidus from lympha, though ancient and widely accepted, is debated in modern scholarship; some handbooks derive limpidus independently within Latin, and the ultimate source of lympha (Greek nymphe, "bride, nymph") is itself of uncertain origin, so no secure PIE etymon can be stated.
