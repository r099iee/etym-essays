• Chewed: Similar because both mean bitten and worked with the teeth; differs because chewing is the general, continuous grinding of food, while gnawing is the patient wearing-away of a hard surface that actually removes matter.
  • Nibbled: Similar because both are gentle, repeated biting; differs because nibbling is small, delicate and mostly harmless, whereas gnawing is persistent and destructive, leaving grooves and hollows.
  • Bitten: Similar because both involve the teeth; differs because biting is a single decisive snap of the jaws, while gnawing is sustained, repeated attrition over time.
  • Worried: Similar because both describe an animal persistently biting and shaking something; differs because worrying is the restless tearing of prey, while gnawing is the steady, patient scraping of teeth on a surface.
  • Munched: Similar because both describe eating with the teeth; differs because munching is noisy, comfortable, everyday eating of food, while gnawing is hard, persistent scraping at something tough or inedible.
  • Chomped: Similar because both describe vigorous biting; differs because chomping is a strong, noisy bite-and-chew, usually of food, while gnawing is patient attrition on a surface.
  • Eroded: Similar because both describe gradual wearing away; differs because erosion is impersonal and natural (water, wind, time), while gnawing is the personal, tooth-driven version of the same process.
  • Corroded: Similar because both mean eaten away gradually; differs because corrosion is chemical, usually of metal, while gnawing is physical, by teeth — or, by extension, by hunger, doubt and pain.
  • Scraped: Similar because both describe removal of surface matter; differs because scraping is abrasion by a tool or friction, while gnawing is specifically by teeth.
  • Rasped: Similar because both suggest rough wearing or grating; differs because rasping is a filing, grating action with a tool, while gnawing is the mouth-and-teeth version.
  • Grazed: Similar because both describe surface wearing; differs because grazing is a light, incidental scrape, while gnawing is deep, persistent, repeated.
  • Tormented / nagged: Similar because gnawing (figurative) means a persistent torment or nagging worry; differs because these name the mental effect directly, while gnawing keeps the vivid tooth-and-surface image of erosion from within.
