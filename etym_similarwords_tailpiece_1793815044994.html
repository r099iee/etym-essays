• Colophon: Similar because both appear at or near the terminal conclusion of a printed book; differs because a colophon is an explicit textual note or imprint recording production details, publication data, printer name, typeface specifications, and printing location, whereas a tailpiece is primarily a visual illustration, decorative engraving, or typographic ornament.
  • Vignette: Similar because both describe small, unbordered or delicate decorative illustrations in fine printing; differs because a vignette can appear anywhere on a printed page (at the top, in the margins, or centered) and is characterized by soft, shading borders that fade into the paper, whereas a tailpiece specifically and exclusively occupies the foot or terminal space closing out a chapter.
  • Cul-de-lampe: Similar because both refer to terminal ornaments in classical typography; differs because cul-de-lampe is a specific French typographic term designating a pendant or triangular ornament tapering downwards like an inverted pyramid or lamp-base to fill the triangular void at the base of a page.
  • Headpiece: Similar because both are ornamental engravings designed to enhance the layout of a book chapter; differs because a headpiece is placed exclusively at the top or opening of a chapter or section, serving an introductory rather than a concluding or terminal function.
  • Epilogue: Similar because both provide closure at the conclusion of a work; differs because an epilogue is an extended narrative or dramatic textual chapter providing thematic resolution to a story, whereas a tailpiece is typically an image, woodcut, or brief aesthetic coda.
  • Postscript: Similar because both follow the conclusion of the main body; differs because a postscript (such as a P.S. in correspondence) consists of supplementary textual commentary, afterthought, or addenda rather than an illustrative ornament or structural anchor.
  • Endpiece: Similar because both designate an object or segment located at the terminal extremity; differs because endpiece is a broad, generic mechanical or structural term lacking the deep historical and artistic associations with 18th-century printmaking and violin luthiery that define tailpiece.
  • Printer's ornament: Similar because both encompass decorative embellishments used in book design; differs because printer's ornament is an overarching umbrella category including fleurons, dingbats, rules, initials, and border frames, of which the tailpiece is a specific positional sub-type.
