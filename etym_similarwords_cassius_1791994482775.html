• Brutus: Similar because Gaius Cassius Longinus and Marcus Junius Brutus were the co-leaders, brothers-in-law, and joint commanders of the senatorial conspiracy that assassinated Julius Caesar, both dying by suicide at Philippi; differs because Brutus was an idealistic Stoic philosopher motivated by ancestral republican duty and widely perceived as honorable, whereas Cassius was an experienced, pragmatic military general driven by sharp personal rivalry, strategic realism, and Epicurean skepticism.
  • Catiline (Lucius Sergius Catilina): Similar because both were Roman patricians who organized secret armed conspiracies against the ruling Roman regime; differs because Catiline was an indebted, disgraced aristocrat who attempted a violent populist coup d'état to overthrow the Republic and burn Rome (suppressed by Cicero), whereas Cassius was a high magistrate attempting to restore the traditional constitutional Republic against an autocratic dictator.
  • Iago: Similar because both are depicted in literature as consummate master manipulators who whisper grievances and strategic doubts into the ears of other men to incite lethal action; differs because Iago (in Shakespeare's Othello) is a fictional sociopath motivated by petty professional jealousy and sheer malice, whereas Cassius was an authentic historical general motivated by genuine hatred of tyranny and dedication to senatorial liberty.
  • Alcibiades: Similar because both were brilliant military strategists of classical antiquity whose political alienation led them to challenge the supreme leadership of their states; differs because Alcibiades was a flamboyant Athenian opportunist who repeatedly betrayed Athens, Sparta, and Persia to serve his personal glory, whereas Cassius remained steadfastly committed to the republican cause until his death.
  • Robespierre: Similar because both sought to defend a republican constitution against perceived tyranny through violent political elimination; differs because Robespierre directed state-sanctioned mass terror (the Reign of Terror) via bureaucratic execution tribunals, whereas Cassius engaged in targeted tyrannicide within the Senate chamber and fought conventional field battles.
  • Machiavelli: Similar because both represent ruthless political realism, psychological insight into human ambition, and an absence of sentimental moralizing; differs because Niccolò Machiavelli was a Renaissance civil servant and political theorist who analyzed power mechanics in theoretical treatises (*The Prince*), whereas Cassius was an active military commander who directly executed the assassination of an autocrat.
