• rotten: Similar because both words describe organic matter undergoing cellular breakdown and decay; differs because rotten is the broad, everyday Germanic term applicable to any decaying substance (such as soft apples, spongy wood, or autumn leaves), whereas putrid specifically emphasizes the nauseating, gas-filled, foul-smelling decomposition of animal proteins and fleshy tissues.
  • fetid: Similar because both adjectives denote an intensely offensive, suffocating odor; differs because fetid focuses exclusively on the sensory perception of the smell itself (which can emanate from stagnant marshes, sewers, or sulfur pits without decomposition), whereas putrid requires actual biological putrefaction and rotting cellular matter.
  • foul: Similar because both describe revolting, contaminated, and offensive states; differs because foul is a comprehensive, multi-register term encompassing bad weather, unfair sporting play, polluted air, or abusive language, while putrid is narrowly biological, clinical, and forensic in its core meaning.
  • stinking: Similar because both signify the emission of an offensive, malodorous scent; differs because stinking is an earthy sensory descriptor applicable to any pungent source (such as garlic, sweat, sulfur, or unwashed feet), whereas putrid specifically indicates the sickening odor of decaying corpses, necrotic flesh, or advanced biological breakdown.
  • rancid: Similar because both denote spoiled, inedible organic matter; differs because rancid describes the chemical oxidation of fats, lipids, and oils (such as stale butter, sour bacon grease, or spoiled nuts), whereas putrid describes the bacterial decomposition of proteins into noxious diamines like cadaverine and putrescine.
  • decayed: Similar because both denote structural and biological degradation over time; differs because decayed is a neutral, scientific, or dental term (decayed teeth, decayed timber, radioactive decay) lacking the visceral horror, liquid dissolution, and sickening odor of putrid.
  • decomposed: Similar because both describe post-mortem biological breakdown; differs because decomposed is an objective, clinical, and forensic descriptor used in autopsy reports and scientific literature, whereas putrid is a visceral, sensory-laden word carrying intense physical and emotional revulsion.
  • corrupt: Similar because both describe a state of degeneration from pristine integrity into foulness; differs because corrupt is predominantly used for legal, moral, spiritual, or computational breakdown, whereas putrid applies physical rot as a visceral metaphor to intensify the level of disgust.
