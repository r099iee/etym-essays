“Hilt” has always meant the handle of a sword, dagger, or bladed weapon — the grip, guard, and pommel by which the weapon is held, as distinct from the blade.
“Hilt” in medieval and heroic English was a place of craft and oath — the jewelled and engraved handle of a lordly sword, and the surface on which warriors swore their vows.
“Hilt” by the 16th–17th centuries, through fancy rapiers and dress swords, became a showcase of metalwork and fashion, while the idiom “to the hilt” (burying the blade up to the handle) gave the word its everyday sense of “completely.”
“Hilt” today is a precise, archaic-flavoured weapon word, most alive in the idiom “to the hilt” and in fantasy, where jewelled hilts still hold light and wealth.
