• Deserted: Similar because both describe places without people; differs because deserted implies people have left recently, while desolate suggests long abandonment and bleakness.
  • Uninhabited: Similar because both mean without inhabitants; differs because uninhabited is neutral and factual, while desolate carries emotional and visual bleakness.
  • Abandoned: Similar because both describe places or people left alone; differs because abandoned focuses on the act of leaving, while desolate describes the resulting state.
  • Forsaken: Similar because both mean deserted and left alone; differs because forsaken is more intense and often implies betrayal or divine abandonment.
  • Barren: Similar because both describe empty, lifeless places; differs because barren stresses the unproductiveness of soil, desolate stresses abandonment and loneliness.
  • Bleak: Similar because both describe cheerless, exposed places; differs because bleak is about exposure to weather and lack of comfort, desolate about emptiness and isolation.
  • Empty: Similar because both mean containing nothing; differs because empty is neutral and general, while desolate adds loneliness and ruin.
  • Forlorn: Similar because both describe pitiable loneliness; differs because forlorn applies chiefly to persons and moods, desolate to both places and persons.
  • Lonely: Similar because both describe being alone; differs because lonely is a common, mild emotional word for persons, while desolate is stronger, more literary, and applies naturally to landscapes.
  • Solitary: Similar because both describe being alone; differs because solitary can be chosen and peaceful (a solitary walk), while desolate is never chosen or pleasant.
  • Dreary: Similar because both describe dull, depressing places; differs because dreary suggests boredom and greyness, desolate suggests emptiness and grief.
  • Ravaged / Devastated: Similar because both describe places laid waste; differs because these imply violent destruction, while desolate describes the resulting emptiness without specifying a cause.
  • Waste: Similar because a waste is desolate land; differs because waste (noun) names the region, while desolate describes its quality.
