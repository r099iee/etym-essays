- Early 19th c. (c. 1820s) — Northern English & Scottish dialectal birth: first attested in regional dialect glossaries (Brockett, 1825) as a colloquial term for an odd figure, curious picture, or queer mechanical contrivance.
- Mid-19th c. — Industrial Revolution adoption: spread into general British and American popular vernacular to describe the bewildering array of novel steam engines, agricultural threshers, and patent factory machinery.
- Late 19th–Early 20th c. — Skepticism toward early aviation & automobiles: widely employed by the public and press as a bemused, cautionary term for early "horseless carriages," biplanes, and experimental gliders that appeared perilously fragile.
- Mid-to-Late 20th c. — Comic and cartoon icon: firmly canonized through the satirical illustrations of Heath Robinson and Rube Goldberg, as well as cinematic animation (such as Wallace & Gromit), cementing its status as an affectionate symbol of eccentric, tinkering genius.
- 21st c. — Contemporary metaphorical & nostalgic usage: applied with ironic humor to over-engineered consumer gadgets, jury-rigged DIY home repairs, or hopelessly convoluted bureaucratic and financial systems.
