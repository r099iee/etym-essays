• prayer: Similar because a litany is a form of prayer and shares its address to the divine; differs because prayer is the general act or utterance, while Litanies names one highly structured genre of prayer defined by repeated petitions and fixed responses.
  • supplication: Similar because a litany is petitionary and often penitential, pleading for mercy or help; differs because supplication stresses the humble urgency of the asking, whereas a litany stresses the form and repetition of the asking, which may be calm or even mechanical.
  • petition: Similar because both consist of requests presented in sequence; differs because a petition is a single formal request, often legal or political, while a litany is a liturgical chain of many brief requests with congregational answers.
  • invocation: Similar because litanies begin by invoking God, the saints or the angels; differs because an invocation is the opening call for aid or presence, whereas the litany is the whole extended form, including the responses that follow.
  • chant: Similar because litanies are traditionally sung or intoned, and the source speaks of them being “read or chanted”; differs because chant names the musical delivery, not the text or its structure, and a chant need not be petitionary at all.
  • rosary: Similar because both are repetitive devotional forms built on a fixed cycle of prayers; differs because the rosary is specifically a Marian devotion counted on beads and centred on the Hail Mary, while a litany is a spoken or sung dialogue of petitions and responses.
  • catalogue: Similar because the figurative litany is a long enumeration; differs because a catalogue is neutral, organised and often written, whereas a litany is spoken, repetitive and coloured by complaint or suffering.
  • recital: Similar because both involve reading out a series of items at length; differs because a recital is neutral about content, while a litany implies monotony and an emotional charge, usually of grievance.
  • refrain: Similar because the litany’s response is a refrain repeated after each petition; differs because the refrain is only the repeated line, not the whole service, and it can occur in any song or poem.
  • jeremiad: Similar because both can denote a long, wearisome recital of woes; differs because a jeremiad is a passionate denunciation or lament, full of prophetic anger, while a litany is cooler, more formulaic and defined by repetition rather than rhetoric.
