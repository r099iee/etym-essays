• Devouring: shares the idea of eating eagerly and completely; differs because it implies ferocity and speed, often with the suggestion of consuming something large, whereas tucking into is homely and cheerful.
  • Gobbling: similar in suggesting rapid, enthusiastic eating; differs because it emphasises noise and lack of manners, and is usually mildly disapproving.
  • Wolfing: shares the hearty, rapid consumption; differs because it likens the eater to a predatory animal and carries a stronger note of greed.
  • Scoffing: British informal for eating quickly and greedily; differs in being more slangy and slightly more critical than the affectionate tucking into.
  • Gorging on: similar in implying excessive eating; differs because it stresses excess to the point of illness or waste, not healthy appetite.
  • Munching: shares the focus on eating with enjoyment; differs because it describes steady, often noisy chewing rather than starting a meal with relish.
  • Nibbling: the opposite extreme, eating in small delicate bites; differs in scale, pace and register, and suggests lack of appetite.
  • Attacking (a meal): shares the energetic start; differs because it is more aggressive and less domestic in tone.
  • Digging into: the closest American equivalent; differs only in regional flavour, being standard in North America rather than marked British.
  • Getting stuck into: British informal, very close in meaning; differs because it is more colloquial and can apply to any task, not only food.
  • Polishing off: shares the hearty appetite; differs because it stresses finishing the food completely rather than beginning it.
  • Putting away: similar in treating food as something to be stowed; differs because it often implies a surprising quantity and lacks the specific image of a sit-down meal.
  • Feasting on: shares enjoyment and abundance; differs because it suggests a celebratory or luxurious meal rather than an ordinary hungry one.
  • Guzzling: similar energy but normally applied to drinking; differs because it is about liquid and carries a stronger note of excess.
  • Consuming: the neutral, formal term for eating; differs in register entirely, being clinical or legal rather than warm and idiomatic.
  • Ingesting: the technical term for taking food into the body; differs because it is scientific and emotionless, with no hint of appetite or pleasure.
