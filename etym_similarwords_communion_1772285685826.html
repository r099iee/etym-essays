• Communication: Similar because both involve an exchange between people; differs because communication is the broad transmission of information that needs no intimacy or mutuality.
  • Conversation: Similar because both involve the exchange of thoughts; differs because conversation implies spoken social exchange without spiritual or intellectual elevation.
  • Fellowship: Similar because both name a shared bond between people; differs because fellowship stresses companionship and shared status (often communal or religious) but can lack the mental fusion of communion.
  • Rapport: Similar because both describe harmonious understanding; differs because rapport is psychological and secular, lighter than the sacramental weight of communion.
  • Sharing: Similar because both involve participation in something common; differs because sharing is general and momentary, while communion implies deep, sustained mutual participation.
  • Union: Similar because both name a joining; differs because union is neutral and can be mechanical, whereas communion is an intimate, reciprocal union of minds or souls.
  • Intimacy: Similar because both involve closeness; differs because intimacy may be merely emotional or physical and need not involve a shared possession held in common.
  • Intercourse (archaic): Similar in the older sense of social dealings; differs because it has lost the elevated mutual sense and is now chiefly sexual.
  • Contact: Similar because both imply connection; differs because contact is superficial and can be one-way, lacking the depth and reciprocity of communion.
  • Association: Similar because both name being with others; differs because association is practical and external, not a union of spirit.
  • Oneness: Similar because both express merged identity; differs because oneness is a state, while communion is an act of sharing that produces it.
  • Sacrament / Eucharist: Similar because Holy Communion is the central Christian rite; differs because these name the rite or the consecrated elements themselves, while communion is the broader fellowship the rite expresses.
