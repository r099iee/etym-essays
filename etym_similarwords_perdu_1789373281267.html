• Forlorn: Similar because both characterize an abandoned, desperate, and seemingly hopeless condition; differs because forlorn emphasizes deep emotional sadness, loneliness, and pitiful desolation (such as a forlorn child or a forlorn hope), whereas perdu encompasses literal physical misplacement, tactical military concealment in ambush, and philosophical temporal dissipation.
  • Forfeit: Similar because both denote something alienated, surrendered, or lost through error, failure, or misfortune; differs because forfeit carries strong contractual, legal, or punitive connotations of a formal penalty or right surrendered as a consequence of a crime or breach, whereas perdu describes an existential, spatial, or temporal loss.
  • Squandered: Similar because both describe valuable resources, talents, or hours foolishly or wastefully dissipated; differs because squandered focuses actively and critically upon the reckless agent who threw the assets away through prodigality, whereas perdu describes the resultant existential condition of the lost time or item itself.
  • Concealed: Similar because both describe being kept hidden from public observation or placed out of view (as in the idiom to lie perdu); differs because concealed is a neutral, descriptive term for any covered or hidden object, lacking the mortal peril, desperate vanguard exposure, and tragic finality inherent in historical military *perdu*.
  • Irretrievable: Similar because both characterize things that cannot be brought back or regained; differs because irretrievable is a formal, abstract modal adjective stressing the absolute impossibility of recovery, whereas perdu within Proustian aesthetics contains the dynamic, transcendent potential for miraculous resurrection (*le temps retrouvé*).
  • Desolate: Similar because both evoke a bleak atmosphere of abandonment; differs because desolate describes barren, wind-swept physical landscapes or inconsolable souls stripped of comfort, whereas perdu encompasses concrete spatial loss, tactical ambushes, architectural voids, and temporal memory.
  • Missing: Similar because both describe the absence of a person or object from its proper place; differs because missing is a mundane, functional administrative designation (such as a missing file or missing person report), lacking the philosophical weight, moral damnation, and rich literary heritage of perdu.
