• Farm: Similar because both are agricultural holdings of land; differs because a farm usually grows crops and is comparatively small and intensive, while a ranch is a vast operation given to grazing livestock on open range.
  • Hacienda: Similar because both are large landed estates of the Spanish-speaking world; differs because hacienda is the Spanish colonial estate with a grand main house and often mixed farming, while ranch is the English name for the North American livestock spread.
  • Estancia: Similar because both are large South American cattle estates; differs because estancia is the Argentine and South American term, while ranch is the North American one, with the frontier-cowboy associations of the US West.
  • Station: Similar because both are vast livestock holdings; differs because station is the Australian and New Zealand term for a sheep or cattle run, while ranch belongs to the American and Canadian West.
  • Range: Similar because both name open grazing land used for livestock; differs because range names the open land itself (and a place in the West), while ranch names the whole operating estate, land, buildings, and stock together.
  • Spread: Similar because both are colloquial names for a large ranch or holding; differs because spread is a slangy, informal American term for a ranch or estate, while ranch is the standard, neutral word.
  • Estate: Similar because both are large holdings of land; differs because estate is a general legal term for any large landed property, while ranch specifically names a livestock-raising operation in the style of the American West.
  • Plantation: Similar because both are large agricultural estates; differs because plantation names a crop-raising estate (cotton, sugar, coffee) with a specific and dark colonial history, while ranch is a livestock spread with frontier associations.
  • Cattle ranch / cattle run: Similar because both name land given to raising cattle; differs because cattle run is the Australian usage for unfenced grazing land, while ranch is the American term for the managed estate.
  • Homestead: Similar because both are rural land holdings associated with the American frontier; differs because a homestead is a small family claim worked for subsistence, while a ranch is a large commercial livestock estate.
  • Ranchero: Similar because both belong to the ranching world; differs because ranchero is Spanish for the rancher or ranch-owner himself (and a style of music and dress), while ranch is the English word for the estate.
  • Granger's farm: Similar because both are rural agricultural properties; differs because the farmer works intensively and grows crops, while the rancher manages vast grazing land and livestock — the distinction of scale and enterprise the word ranch was built to express.
