- Coined as a scholarly period label (early 19th century) - the Romantic rediscovery of 'the age of Elizabeth' turns a queen's personal name plus the suffix -an into an adjective; first confined to historical and literary scholarship, purely chronological in tone.
- 19th-century literary canonisation - Victorian criticism extends it to the whole culture of 1558-1603: the drama of the Globe, the sonnet sequences, Hooker's prose, the 1559 religious Settlement; the word thickens with golden-age glamour.
- Modern usage - still the standard, always-capitalised label for the era and its literature, and in the technical sense for Elizabethan 'classical verse' (quantitative metres); loosely extended to anything old-fashioned or exuberantly Shakespearean; scholarly yet warmly celebratory in register.
