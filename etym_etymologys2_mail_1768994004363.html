Latin macula "a spot, a stain; also the mesh or hole of a net" (attested Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE; NO secure PIE etymology — de Vaan marks the word as of uncertain, possibly substrate origin; the senses "spot" and "mesh" may be the same word for an open cell or mark) → Late Latin macula with the "mesh" sense (the open hole of a net) → Old French maille "a mesh; a link of chain armor; a stitch" (attested 12th c.) → Middle English mail(e), mayle "armor of interlocked rings" (attested 14th c., c. 1300–1400) → Modern English mail "armor of rings or plates" (the pleonasm "chain mail" coined in the 18th c.).

EXPLICIT DISTINCTION — NOT related: "mail" the armor and "mail" the postal system are two different words. Postal "mail" ← Old French male "a bag, wallet" ← Frankish *malha ← Proto-Germanic *malhō "satchel"; it has no kinship with Latin macula. The two are homographs only.

DEBATE / NUANCE NOTE: Because no PIE ancestor for macula can be reconstructed, the chain begins at attested Latin and the gap is stated explicitly rather than guessed.
