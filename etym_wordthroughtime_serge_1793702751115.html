- Early Old French (c. 12th-13th C.) - "Serge" as a luxurious silk fabric - Connotation of high status, wealth, and exotic origin, reflecting its precious material.
- Middle English (c. 14th-15th C.) - "Serge" as a durable, often coarse, woolen or mixed fabric - Shift to practicality, utility, and widespread use, losing its exclusive luxury status and becoming associated with robust, everyday textiles.
- 17th-19th Century - "Serge" as a staple for uniforms, workwear, and sturdy civilian clothing - Strong association with resilience, formality (for tailored suits), and reliability; a fabric for the working and middle classes, as well as military and service personnel.
- 20th Century - "Serge" maintains its reputation for durability and structure in tailored garments and uniforms - Perception as a traditional, somewhat conservative fabric, reliable and enduring but perhaps less fashion-forward than newer synthetics or lighter weaves.
- Contemporary Usage - "Serge" continues to be valued for its classic aesthetic and hard-wearing qualities in high-quality suits, uniforms, and outerwear - Seen as a timeless, dependable choice for structured garments, often associated with quality craftsmanship and enduring style, particularly in wool variants.
