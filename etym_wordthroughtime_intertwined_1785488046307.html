- Physical braiding of threads and strands - coined in the mid-17th century from inter- + twine; purely concrete, describing the twisting together of cords, yarns or rope in craftwork.
- Organic botanical image - vines, roots and branches growing round one another; the sense turns natural and wholesome, of living things 'growing together'.
- Abstract extension - emotions, fates, arguments and identities braided together; by the 19th century the word is mostly figurative rather than physical.
- Modern analytical register - in academic prose it names a relation of closeness-with-distinctness: two things that cannot be cleanly separated yet are not merged; measured, neutral and even approving in tone.
