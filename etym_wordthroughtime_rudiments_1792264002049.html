- 1st c. BCE — Coined in Classical Latin as rudīmentum, describing the first rigorous drills and swordplay training given to tīrōnēs (raw military recruits), as well as elementary schooling in letters.
- 1540s — Entered Tudor English via Renaissance humanist educators (Elyot, Ascham) to designate introductory textbooks and Latin grammar primers for young schoolboys.
- 17th–18th c. — Codified in military martial arts and percussion music (Swiss fife and drum corps, British army) as the standardized kinetic sticking patterns required of snare drummers.
- 19th c. — Transformed into a foundational scientific concept in evolutionary biology by Charles Darwin and comparative embryologists, designating both undeveloped embryonic primordia and atrophied vestigial organs.
- Late 20th–21st c. — Universal pedagogical descriptor in computer science, software engineering, and technical disciplines, denoting the irreducible programmatic primitives necessary to build functional software.
