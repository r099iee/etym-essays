Derivation chain for DOLTISH:
  • PIE *dhel- "to be dim, murky, dull; to make muddy or dark" (c. 4000-3000 BCE) - the root of dimness and cloudiness; Greek preserves it as tholos "mud, murk, sediment", with tholeros "turbid, muddy" and tholoo "to stir up, make turbid".
  • Proto-Germanic *dulaz "dull, foolish, mad" (c. 500 BCE-500 CE) - built on the zero grade *dhul-, tilting the root's dimness of light into dimness of mind; the extended form *dwalaz "astray, wandering, foolish" produced Old English dwol "error, heresy, folly" and the verb behind English dwell.
  • Old English dol "dull, foolish" (attested from the 8th-9th century) - the adjective of dullness and folly.
  • English dull (13th century) "blunt; dim; slow-witted" - descending from Old English dol; the sense "slow-witted" is established by the 14th century.
  • English dolt "a dull, stupid fellow" (recorded from c. 1540) - formed from the base of dull with a -t formant, reinforced by Middle Low German dolte "stub, plug, wedge"; the word's body is the image of a blunt peg.
  • English doltish "dull, blockish, stupid" (recorded from the 1560s) - formed with the native adjective suffix -ish attached to dolt.
  • DOLTISH (the form on this card).
Debated / nuanced points: the formation of dolt is not perfectly certain - the -t formant is a Germanic trick of noun-building, and the Middle Low German dolte provides a close formal parallel, but the exact route from dull to dolt is reconstructed rather than directly attested. A separate guardrail: the Sanskrit dhuli "dust" and Hindi dhul "dust" are NOT cognates of *dhel-; they descend from the separate PIE root *dhewh2- "to smoke, give off dust or clouds" (Latin fumus "smoke"), and the two roots must not be merged. *dhel- has no securely attested Indo-Aryan reflex, so no Sanskrit or Hindi branch appears in this chain.
