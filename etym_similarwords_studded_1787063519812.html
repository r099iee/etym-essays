• Bejewelled / jeweled: Similar because both describe decoration with gems; differs because bejewelled means specifically "covered with jewels," whereas studded may use nails, bosses, or any projecting element.
  • Ornamented: Similar because both mean decorated; differs because ornamented is general and implies refined embellishment, while studded specifies discrete raised points set into the surface.
  • Dotted: Similar because both describe things scattered across a surface; differs because dots are flat marks while studs are physical projections; dotted lacks the protruding, metallic or gem-like quality.
  • Speckled: Similar because both mean marked here and there; differs because speckles are small spots or stains, not raised objects, and the effect is dappled rather than hard and decorative.
  • Adorned: Similar because both mean made more beautiful by additions; differs because adorned is broader and softer, while studded is specific, mechanical, and implies attachment of hard points.
  • Spangled: Similar because both suggest a surface covered with glinting points; differs because spangles are flat, shiny sequins, while studs are solid projecting heads.
  • Bristling: Similar because both suggest a surface thick with projecting points; differs because bristles are fine, hair-like, and usually flexible, while studs are rigid and hard; bristling also carries a hostile or defensive tone.
  • Encrusted: Similar because both mean thickly covered; differs because encrusting forms a continuous crust over the surface, whereas studding leaves clear spaces between discrete points.
  • Embossed: Similar because both involve raised decoration; differs because embossing raises the surface itself into relief, while studs are separate objects fixed on top of the surface.
