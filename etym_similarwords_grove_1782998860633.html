• Wood: Similar because both denote a mass of trees; differs because wood is the neutral general term and may be very large and wild, while a grove is small, open, and walkable.
  • Woodland: Similar because it names tree-covered ground; differs because it refers to the category of land or terrain, not a particular small, discrete stand of trees.
  • Forest: Similar because it is a tree-covered expanse; differs because it is vast, wild, and in older literature sinister, not the intimate "home" a grove offers.
  • Copse / Coppice: Similar because both are small stands of trees; differs because a copse is a practical thicket of small cut-and-regrown trees, humble and scrubby rather than pleasing and open.
  • Thicket: Similar because both are clusters of growth; differs because a thicket is dense, tangled, and impassable, while a grove is open enough to walk through and shelter in.
  • Spinney: Similar because it names a small wood or clump of trees; differs because it is a regional (chiefly British) word for a small plantation or bush-clump, less resonant than grove.
  • Brake: Similar because it names a clump of growth; differs because it denotes rough undergrowth or ferns, not stately trees.
  • Orchard: Similar because both can be planted stands of trees; differs because an orchard is strictly fruit or nut trees grown for produce, with no sacred or poetic charge.
  • Plantation: Similar because both can be deliberately planted tree groups; differs because a plantation is a commercial, large-scale planting (also of other crops), without the shade-shelter intimacy of a grove.
  • Stand: Similar because it denotes a group of trees in forestry; differs because it is technical and neutral, describing a growth unit rather than a sheltering place.
