• Astounded: Similar because both describe strong amazement; differs because being astounded implies no loss of speech or motion, while dumbfounded specifically silences and stills you.
  • Stunned: Similar because both describe being overwhelmed by shock; differs because stunned suggests a blow, physical or figurative, that briefly numbs the senses, while dumbfounded emphasizes muteness and cognitive shutdown.
  • Stupefied: Similar because both describe being dazed by shock; differs because stupefied points to the dulling of the wits, while dumbfounded stresses the loss of speech along with the bewilderment.
  • Bewildered: Similar because both describe being overwhelmed by surprise; differs because bewilderment is active mental confusion and lost bearings, whereas dumbfounded is paralysis and speechlessness.
  • Flabbergasted: Similar because both describe speechless amazement; differs because flabbergasted is a later, colloquial, almost comic word that undercuts gravity, while dumbfounded can carry a grim and solemn force.
  • Astonished: Similar because both are strong reactions of surprise; differs because astonished is the plain word for great surprise without any implication of being robbed of words or movement.
  • Confounded: Similar because both derive from the same confound element and mean utterly bewildered; differs because confounded on its own stresses confusion without the added image of muteness that dumb supplies in dumbfounded.
  • Mystified: Similar because both involve the mind failing to comprehend; differs because mystification is a puzzling bafflement that invites explanation, while dumbfounded is the paralysed, speechless shock of overwhelming surprise.
