Note on historical debate & folk etymology: The modern English spelling of sovereign and sovereignty with an internal, silent -g- represents one of the most famous examples of historical folk-etymological corruption in the English language. Historically, the word contains no etymological connection to the Latin verb regnare ("to reign") or its derived noun regnum. The original Old French and Middle English spellings were consistently soverain, soveran, and sovereynte (directly reflecting Vulgar Latin *superānus, from super, "above"). However, during the 14th to 16th centuries, English scribes and early Renaissance scholars mistakenly assumed that the word was intrinsically compounded from the native verb reign (which was spelled reigne, from Old French reigner), and consequently inserted a spurious -g- by false analogy, which permanently ossified into standard English orthography.
