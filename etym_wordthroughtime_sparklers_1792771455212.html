- 1560s — anything or anyone brilliant and glittering: the agent noun of sparkle, a literary compliment for shining things and vivacious people.
- 17th–18th c. — chiefly poetic: ornaments, eyes, and wits that ‘sparkle’ in verse and polite letters; a genteel, admiring word.
- 1906 — the hand-held firework is born: the word attaches to wire-stick pyrotechnics and begins its career as the vocabulary of celebrations.
- 20th c. — slang spreads it sideways: diamonds become ‘sparklers’, vivacious people are ‘sparklers’, while the firework dominates everyday use.
- Today — overwhelmingly the festive firework of Diwali, Bonfire Night, and weddings; warm, childlike, celebratory, with the gemstone slang kept alive by jewellers and gossip columns.
