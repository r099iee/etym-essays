- [19th century: American invention] - 'Spittoon' appears in America as the receptacle for chewing-tobacco juice; connotation is functional and practical - a fixture of saloons, hotels, and public buildings.
- [Late 19th century: the brass cuspidor era] - The spittoon becomes a polished brass or porcelain furnishing of lobbies and barrooms; connotation is still plebeian but also institutional, part of the daily furniture of public American life.
- [20th century: disappearance and exotic residue] - Chewing tobacco fades and the spittoon vanishes from public life; connotation shifts to the period-flavoured and historical - a comic relic of old saloons, or, in an aristocratic Chinese setting, a mark of fastidious household ceremony where even bodily functions have their appointed vessels.
