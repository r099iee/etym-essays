Note on etymological debate and historical cognates: The etymology of Sanskrit gandharva is one of the most famously debated topics in comparative Indo-European philology. Traditional Sanskrit grammarians derived it folk-etymologically from gandha ("scent, fragrance") + arva / dhā ("he who receives or preserves fragrance"), reflecting their fondness for sweet perfumes. In 19th-century comparative linguistics, Adalbert Kuhn famously proposed an etymological equation between Sanskrit gandharva and Greek kentauros (κένταυρος, "centaur"), postulating a shared PIE cloud/horse myth; however, modern phonologists (Mayrhofer, Beekes) reject this equation as phonologically irregular. What is universally accepted is the exact Indo-Iranian cognate: Avestan gaṇdarəwa (a monstrous yellow-heeled aquatic monster slain by the hero Kərəsāspa in the sea Vourukaša), proving that *gandʰarwás was an ancient common Indo-Iranian mythical figure associated with deep celestial waters before bifurcating into the terrifying Iranian aquatic dragon and the benevolent, beautiful Indian celestial musician.
