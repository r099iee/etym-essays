• insanity: Similar because it names unsoundness of mind and is the standard legal counterpart; differs because insanity remains a live forensic term and carries no lunar image or asylum-era flavour.
  • madness: Similar as a general word for severe mental disturbance; differs by being broader, more literary and more emotionally coloured, with no legal or administrative history behind it.
  • psychosis: Similar because it denotes a loss of contact with reality; differs as a strictly clinical term for a symptom cluster, whereas lunacy is non-clinical and would be offensive if applied to a patient.
  • dementia: Similar in older usage, when it meant madness generally; differs because modern dementia names neurodegenerative cognitive decline, a sense lunacy never acquired.
  • mania: Similar as a form of severe mental disturbance; differs because mania is a specific mood state of elevated arousal, or an obsessive enthusiasm, not a general verdict of insanity.
  • derangement: Similar as a formal term for disordered mind; differs by being gentler, more archaic and less institutional — it suggests disturbance rather than legal incapacity.
  • folly: Similar because it covers foolish behaviour; differs in force, since folly may be harmless or even endearing, while lunacy implies dangerous, incomprehensible irrationality.
  • absurdity: Similar because both can describe a ridiculous policy; differs because absurdity names the quality of ridiculousness, while lunacy imputes a state of mind to those who produce it.
  • recklessness: Similar in judging conduct as irresponsible; differs because recklessness concerns disregarded risk, whereas lunacy denies the conduct any rational basis at all.
  • craziness: Similar as an informal synonym; differs by register — craziness is colloquial and light, while lunacy is formal, weightier and historically loaded.
  • alienation: Similar in the obsolete legal-medical sense of alienatio mentis, a transfer or loss of reason; differs because modern alienation means estrangement, and the mental sense is entirely dead.
