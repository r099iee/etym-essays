- 17th century - Coined from “individual” to name the fact of being an indivisible, distinct being; originally close to mere individual existence.
- 18th century - Shifts toward the qualities that make a person or thing distinct — the sum of inner traits setting one apart — in philosophy and aesthetics.
- 19th century (Romantic and Victorian high point) - Becomes a loaded moral and political ideal: selfhood and self-expression against conformity; in Mill's On Liberty it is the fountain of improvement and the ground of human flourishing, while in his Autobiography the boy's individuality is declared “unimportant” by a father who designed him for a mission.
- 20th–21st century - Widens into everyday language for personal distinctness of style or taste, softened from Victorian moral force into mere quirk or uniqueness.
