• calmed: Similar because it means made quiet and still; differs because calmed is purely soothing without any subjugation or military overtone.
  • soothed: Similar because it means eased pain or agitation gently; differs because soothing implies comfort, while pacified can imply imposed control.
  • appeased: Similar because it means satisfied anger or demands; differs because appeasement concedes something, while pacifying may simply suppress.
  • placated: Similar because it means won over an angry party; differs because placating flatters or yields, while pacified stresses resulting quiet.
  • mollified: Similar because it means softened hostility; differs because it is formal and emotional, lacking the administrative-military pole of pacified.
  • subdued: Similar because it means brought under control by force or restraint; differs because subdued is openly coercive, while pacified euphemises coercion as peace.
  • quelled: Similar because it means crushed resistance or disturbance; differs because quelling is abrupt and forceful, while pacifying can be gradual.
  • suppressed: Similar because it means put down revolt or motion; differs because suppression keeps pressure on, while pacified claims settled calm.
  • conquered: Similar because Roman pacare meant to conquer into peace; differs because conquered admits victory, while pacified disguises it.
  • drowned: Similar because in this passage pacified literally means drowned ducks; differs because drowned names the mechanism, while pacified veils it.
  • stilled: Similar because it means made motionless and silent; differs because stilled is physical and poetic, while pacified is political-moral.
  • assuaged: Similar because it means eased grief or thirst; differs because assuagement relieves feeling, not governance or capture.
