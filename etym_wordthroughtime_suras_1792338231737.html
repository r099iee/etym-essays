- [7th c. CE / Classical Arabic era] - coined/adopted in early Arabo-Islamic revelation to designate discrete structural chapters of the newly recited Qur'an - [perception: revered with absolute awe as direct, miraculous divine speech (*i'jāz*) demarcated by sacred guidance]
- [12th–16th c. / Medieval European translation era] - entered Western scholarship via Latin translations (such as Robert of Ketton's *Lex Mahumet pseudoprophete* in 1143, rendered as *azoara* or *alura* from Arabic *al-sūrah*) - [perception: viewed through polemical anti-Islamic scholasticism as alien, non-chronological subdivisions of the Saracen scripture]
- [17th–18th c. / Early Modern Orientalism] - standardized into French (*sourate*) and English (*sura* / *surah*) through scholarly translations like George Sale's 1734 Quran - [perception: recognized objectively by Enlightenment intellectuals and philologists as the formal literary chapter units of Islamic scripture]
- [19th c. Victorian & Historical-Critical scholarship] - analyzed systematically by German and British orientalists (Nöldeke, Muir, Palmer) attempting to reconstruct chronological Meccan and Medinan revelation layers - [perception: treated as complex historical artifacts, theological documents, and masterpieces of Semitic rhymed prose]
- [20th–21st c. Global discourse] - universally standardized in English comparative literature, academic religious studies, and international journalism as *sura* (plural *suras* or *surahs*) - [perception: understood as the foundational structural, liturgical, and aesthetic chapters of Islamic religious life and world literature]
