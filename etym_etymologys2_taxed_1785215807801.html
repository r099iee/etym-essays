PIE *tag- "to touch, handle, arrange" (reconstructed root, c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE; the family of Latin tangere "to touch", Greek tetagōn "seizing, grasping", and English tact, tactile, contact, contagion, intact) → Proto-Italic *tag- "to touch, handle" → Latin tangere "to touch" (attested 3rd c. BCE onward) → Latin taxāre "to touch repeatedly; to appraise, assess, value; to charge, censure" (frequentative of tangere; attested from classical Latin onward) → Latin taxātiō, genitive taxātiōnis "a valuation, an appraisal, an assessment" (classical Latin) → Old French taxer "to assess, to impose a levy; to charge, blame" (12th–13th c.) → Anglo-Norman taxer "to assess, to impose a levy" (13th c.) → Middle English taxen "to assess a levy; to charge with a fault" (14th c., attested c. 1325) → Modern English tax (verb) "to impose a levy; to charge with a fault; to strain" (16th c. onward) → TAXED (past tense and past participle of tax) Note: the shift of taxāre from "touch repeatedly" to "assess" reflects the appraisal of goods by repeated handling; the same frequentative lies (via Old French taster) behind English taste — to "touch" a thing with the tongue was to sample and assess it.
