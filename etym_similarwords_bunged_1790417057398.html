• Chucked: Similar because both denote throwing or tossing an object casually and carelessly; differs because chucked emphasizes the throwing motion through the air, whereas bunged often carries the added nuance of thrusting something into a tight, confined space or giving someone a tip/bribe.
  • Shoved: Similar in describing forceful or unceremonious placement; differs because shoved implies aggressive, direct muscular pushing and rough friction, while bunged is more nonchalant, breezy, and colloquial.
  • Crammed: Similar in packing people or things tightly into a space; differs because crammed emphasizes overfilling a container beyond its comfortable capacity through sustained physical packing, whereas bunged highlights the arbitrary, haphazard nature of the act.
  • Plugged: Similar in the literal sense of stopping a hole with a stopper; differs because plugged is standard, formal technical terminology applicable to any hole or electronic socket, whereas bunged has specialized coopering origins and rich slang connotations.
  • Tossed: Similar in denoting light, careless throwing; differs because tossed implies a gentle, upward or graceful arc, lacking the forceful, haphazard thrust of bunged.
  • Congested: Similar in the physiological sense of blocked nasal passages; differs because congested is the formal medical and clinical term, while bunged up is intimate, informal domestic vernacular.
  • Bribed: Similar in the slang sense of passing illicit money; differs because bribed is a serious legal and moral accusation of corruption, whereas bunged is colorful, informal British street argot for tipping or slipping cash.
  • Stuffed: Similar in filling an enclosure roughly; differs because stuffed specifically focuses on compressing pliable material into an interior until it is plump and full.
