- [c. 1000–500 BCE: Vedic & Epic Dawn] — Ancient Sanskrit hydronym formed from tamas ("darkness/shadow"), naming a secluded, forest-shaded river in the Kośala kingdom — [perception: venerated forest stream whose crystal waters ironically offered ritual purity free from the mud of worldly corruption]
- [c. 500 BCE–500 CE: Classical Sanskrit Kāvya Era] — Immortalized in Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa as the holy site of the krauñca-vadha and the birth of the śloka meter; personified by dramatists like Bhavabhūti as a tender river goddess consoling the exiled Sītā — [perception: celebrated literary sanctuary and aesthetic fountainhead of poetry and tragic pathos (karuṇa-rasa)]
- [c. 500–1600 CE: Purāṇic & Medieval Pilgrimage] — Formalized in Purāṇic geography and regional Māhātmyas as a designated tīrtha for ritual ablution and an allegory for crossing the dark ocean of mundane existence — [perception: sacred threshold between civic illusion and ascetic spiritual liberation]
- [Modern Era: Philological & Contemporary Toponymy] — Identified by modern geographers with the Tons River and preserved in modern Indian languages (as Tamas / Tons) — [perception: revered historical landmark of India’s epic heritage and cultural memory of Vālmīki’s hermitage]
