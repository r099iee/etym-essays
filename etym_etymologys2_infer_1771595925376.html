PIE *bʰer- "to carry, bear, bring" (reconstructed; Pokorny *bher-; de Vaan: ferō < *bʰer-) → Latin ferre, ferō "to carry, bear, bring" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE; a suppletive verb whose perfect tulī and supine lātum come from other stems) → Latin īnferre (in- "into" + ferre) "to bring in, carry in; to bring in an argument, to adduce; to conclude" (attested from the 1st c. BCE; Cicero uses īnferre of bringing in reasons and arguments) → Late and medieval Latin logical use: inferre "to conclude, to bring in as a conclusion" (standard in the medieval schools; a conclusion is said to be inferre a premise) → Middle English inferre, infer "to bring in, import; to adduce; to conclude" (borrowed c. 15th c. from Latin īnferre) → English INFER (16th c., logical sense "to derive a conclusion by reasoning" firmly established) Note: The suppletive forms of Latin ferre (perfect tulī, supine lātum) belong to different inherited stems and are not part of the direct chain from *bʰer-. The sense "to hint, to imply" seen in some usage is a confusion of infer with imply, not a stage in the word's own derivation.
