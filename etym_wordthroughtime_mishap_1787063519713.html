- 16th c.: coined from mis- (bad) + hap (luck); means literally “bad luck”, used of serious strokes of ill fortune as well as small ones.
- 17th–18th c.: begins to slide down the scale of gravity; increasingly names small, annoying, recoverable accidents — spilt wine, torn sleeves, missed appointments.
- 19th c.: novelists use mishap for comic near-disasters and domestic irritations; it loses its tragic weight and grows almost affectionate.
- 20th–21st c.: a light word for a minor accident or unlucky blunder; “without mishap” reassuringly means “nothing went wrong”, while “training mishap” does polite institutional understatement.
