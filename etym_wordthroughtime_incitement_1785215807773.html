- 16th–18th century - “Incitement” names the act of spurring someone to action or thought; morally neutral to commendatory — a stimulus that sets the will in motion.
- 19th century (elevated prose) - Reaches a positive, ennobling peak: Mill calls Harriet Taylor's “exalted sense of truth and right” his strongest incitement — the very engine of his authorship.
- 20th century (criminal-legal narrowing) - Public discourse fixes the word in “incitement to violence”, “to hatred”, “to riot”; the commendatory sense retreats into literary prose.
- Contemporary - Dominantly legal and condemnatory in news and law, while the older noble sense survives only in formal or historical writing.
