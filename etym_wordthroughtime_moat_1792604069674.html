- [14th c.] - borrowed into Middle English from Anglo-French and Old French motte (defensive mound or earthwork hillock), undergoing metonymic transfer to denote the protective excavated trench surrounding the mound - [perception: recognized as an indispensable military earthwork essential for castle fortification]
- [15th–17th c.] - established across Europe as a hallmark of medieval and Renaissance fortress architecture, engineered with complex sluices, bastions, and stone revetments - [perception: viewed as a formidable symbol of aristocratic power, feudal isolation, and military impregnability]
- [18th–19th c.] - with the obsolescence of castle siegecraft due to gunpowder artillery, moats were drained, filled in for boulevards, or preserved as romantic landscape features with swans and water lilies - [perception: shifted from a fearsome military obstacle to a picturesque, historic relic of the feudal past]
- [Late 20th–21st c.] - popularized globally by investor Warren Buffett in corporate finance as 'economic moat' (sustainable competitive advantage), while concurrently adopted in cybersecurity (perimeter air-gapping) and zoo design (sunken barriers) - [perception: universally celebrated as an iconic metaphor for unassailable strategic defense, enduring security, and structural protection]
