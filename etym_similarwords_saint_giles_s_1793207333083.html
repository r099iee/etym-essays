• Rookery: Similar because a rookery is exactly what St Giles’s was — a dense thievish slum warren; differs because rookery is the general term for any such nest, while Saint Giles’s is the one supreme historical example.
  • Slum: Similar because both name districts of squalid overcrowded housing; differs because slum is the plain modern generic (any city, any age), while Saint Giles’s is a dated proper-name shorthand steeped in Georgian London.
  • Seven Dials: Similar because the Dials were the very heart of the St Giles rookery; differs because Seven Dials names the seven-street junction and its pillar, while Saint Giles’s names the whole parish and its moral legend.
  • Whitechapel: Similar because Whitechapel is London’s other great historic byword for poverty and menace; differs because Whitechapel belongs to the East End and the Ripper’s 1880s, while Saint Giles’s belongs to the West End and Hogarth’s 1750s.
  • Alsatia: Similar because both were lawless London sanctuaries proverbial for rogues; differs because Alsatia (Whitefriars) was a debtors’ liberty of the 17th c., while Saint Giles’s was an 18th-c. poverty rookery, not a legal asylum.
  • Skid row: Similar because both are bywords for the quarter where drink and destitution pool; differs because skid row is American and twentieth-century, while Saint Giles’s is London’s Georgian original.
  • Shantytown: Similar because both describe settlements of wretched makeshift dwellings; differs because shantytown suggests jerry-built huts on a city’s edge, while Saint Giles’s was rotting brick tenements at the metropolis’s core.
  • The stews: Similar because both carry the old London stench of vice and squalor; differs because the stews properly means the brothel quarter (Southwark), while Saint Giles’s means gin-sodden beggary more broadly.
  • Bedlam: Similar because both are London proper names turned into common nouns of horror (madness, squalor); differs because Bedlam (Bethlem hospital) gives us chaos and lunacy, while Saint Giles’s gives us dirt, gin, and rags.
