- 1940s–1950s coinage as neutral technical class - perceived as progressive, scientific, yield-raising; emblem of post-war abundance and the Green Revolution
- 1962–1967 Carson / Baker turn - perception darkens sharply; word acquires ecological dread, associated with thinning eggshells, silent springs and near-extinction of raptors
- 1970s–1980s regulatory era - perceived as legally controlled hazard; DDT bans and licensing regimes make the word bureaucratic as well as toxic
- 1990s–2010s polarised usage - industry uses it neutrally for a market sector; environmentalism uses it pejoratively for industrial farming versus organic; public hears risk
- Present - umbrella term with dual register: technical in agronomy and finance, cautionary in conservation, journalism and food culture
