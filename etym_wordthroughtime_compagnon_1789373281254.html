- Late 11th to 14th Century - Emerges in Old and Middle French as *compaignon*, initially denoting an intimate table-companion or brother-in-arms sharing bread, before evolving into a formal guild designation for a journeyman craftsman who has completed his apprenticeship.
- 16th to 18th Century - Stabilizes as a respected regional French surname across central and eastern France, while continuing in social life as a guild title for itinerant craftsmen touring the Kingdom of France on their *Tour de France*.
- 1940s to 1950s - Gains sacred political and moral stature in post-war France with General de Gaulle's creation of the *Ordre de la Libération*, honoring heroes of the Resistance as *Compagnons de la Libération*.
- Late 1970s to 1990s - The surname becomes prominent in international literary theory and academia as Antoine Compagnon publishes groundbreaking monographs on Montaigne, Proust, and structuralism, bridging elite French theory with Anglo-American scholarship.
- 2000s to Present - Cemented at the pinnacle of French cultural life as Antoine Compagnon is elected to the Collège de France and the Académie Française, where the name signifies authoritative Proustian scholarship, elegant skepticism toward ideological dogmas, and the defense of literary humanism.
