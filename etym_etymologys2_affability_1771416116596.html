Root 1: fari "to speak"
1. Proto-Indo-European *bheh2- "to speak" (reconstructed root with laryngeal *h2; attested cognates: Greek phemi "I say," phone "voice" — source of English "phone" and "phonetics"; Sanskrit bhasati "speaks"; Latin fari, fama, fas, fatum).
2. Latin fari "to speak, say, utter" (attested deponent verb; source of fama "report, rumour, fame," fatum "that which is said, fate," fas "divine utterance, right").
3. Latin affari "to speak to, address" — compound of ad- "to" + fari (attested; affatus "a speaking to, address").
4. Latin affabilis "easy to speak to, courteous, approachable" — adjective from affari with -bilis "able to be" (attested, Classical Latin; cf. Cicero).
5. Latin affabilitas "affability, courteousness, graciousness" — noun from affabilis with -tas (attested, Classical Latin).
6. Middle French affabilite "affability, courtesy" (attested 14th-15th c.).
7. English affability "courteous friendliness, easy approachability" — borrowed from Middle French (mid-16th c.).
8. AFFABILITY — present-day form.

Root 2: ad- "to, toward"
1. Proto-Indo-European *h2ed- "at, near, to" (reconstructed; attested cognates: Latin ad "to," English "at," Sanskrit adhi "over, upon").
2. Latin ad "to, toward, at" — preposition and prefix (attested; assimilates to af- before f, as in affari).
3. af- / ad- prefix in Latin affari "to speak to" -> affabilis -> affabilitas -> French affabilite -> English AFFABILITY.

Debate note: the reconstruction of PIE *bheh2- "to speak" and its laryngeal shape are standard; the exact status of Sanskrit bhasati as a direct cognate is occasionally questioned, but the family including Latin fari, Greek phemi, and English "phone" is uncontroversial.
