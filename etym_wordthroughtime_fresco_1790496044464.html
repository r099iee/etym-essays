- Proto-Germanic fresh and lively, vigorous and new; physical and approving
- Old High German frisc, new and lively; youthful and vernacular
- Medieval Italian fresco, fresh, cool, new (14th century); everyday and sensory
- Italian a fresco, on the fresh (plaster), painters' jargon; technical and workshop
- 16th-century Italian affresco cycles, Vatican Stanze and Sistine; papal, monumental, theological
- 1590s English borrowing fresco, wall-painting on wet plaster; connoisseurial and Grand Tour
- 18th-century al fresco, in the cool air, dining outside; fashionable and Italianate
- Victorian Ruskin moralises fresco, true public Christian art versus oily easel; earnest and reforming
- Modern conservation science, carbonation, giornate, salts and damp; chemical and fragile
- Modern menu fresco, fresh cheese and cool wine; appetising and authentic
