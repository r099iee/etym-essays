PIE *smēi- "to smear, rub, daub" (c. 4500–3000 BCE) → Proto-Germanic *smītaną (strong verb, class I; c. 500 BCE–200 CE) "to smear, daub; to throw, strike" → Old English smītan (strong verb, class I; 8th c. CE) "to daub, smear, defile; to smite"; past singular smāt → Middle English smiten "to strike, hit, beat" (by c. 1200; the smear sense recedes); past smot/smate from Old English smāt → Modern English smite (v.); strong past smote (archaic, biblical, literary) "to strike, hit; to affect suddenly and overwhelmingly" → Modern English smote (past tense; "struck forcefully or affected suddenly and deeply with emotion") Note: the semantic bridge from "smear, daub" to "strike" runs through "to throw, fling": a blow is something hurled at a body. The "throw" sense is preserved in Middle Dutch smiten and German schmeißen ("to throw, fling"), while English's "smear, stain" sense survives sideways in smut. The change is Germanic-internal and completed in English by c. 1200.
