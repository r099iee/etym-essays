• Unconventional: Similar because both describe people, styles, or behaviors that diverge from mainstream societal norms; differs because *unconventional* is a broad, neutral descriptor applicable to any methodology or idea, whereas *bohemian* specifically carries strong artistic, romantic, countercultural, and aesthetically eccentric connotations.
  • Nonconformist: Similar because both denote individuals who reject standard societal rules and orthodoxies; differs because *nonconformist* often implies philosophical, religious, or political defiance and resistance, whereas *bohemian* emphasizes a lifestyle rooted in artistic passion, casual indifference to material wealth, and informal living.
  • Eccentric: Similar because both characterize unusual, idiosyncratic personal habits or lifestyles; differs because *eccentric* suggests quirky, odd, or peculiar individual psychological traits, while *bohemian* identifies with a recognized artistic milieu, subculture, and aesthetic philosophy.
  • Maverick: Similar because both refer to independent thinkers who refuse to follow the herd; differs because *maverick* emphasizes solitary, aggressive, trailblazing individualism (especially in business or politics), whereas *bohemian* evokes communal artistic camaraderie, wanderlust, and poetic sensibility.
  • Avant-garde: Similar because both are intimately connected with progressive, rule-breaking artistic communities; differs because *avant-garde* specifically refers to experimental, cutting-edge artistic techniques and intellectual radicalism pushing aesthetic boundaries, whereas *bohemian* refers to the living conditions, social habits, and lifestyle of artists.
  • Czech: Similar because *Czech* is the direct modern ethno-linguistic and national designation for the people and culture of the region historically called Bohemia; differs because *Czech* is a precise political and ethnic demonym without the countercultural lifestyle or Romani historical associations that attached to *bohemian*.
