• Cushions: Similar because both are soft layers that absorb shock and pressure; differs because cushions are separate stuffed objects for sitting or reclining on, whereas pads are flatter protective or anatomical layers, often integrated into a surface or a body.
  • Mats: Similar because both are flat layers laid under something for protection or comfort; differs because mats are typically woven or rigid floor coverings, while pads are soft, yielding, cushion-like layers, and mats have no anatomical sense.
  • Soles: Similar because both are the underparts of feet; differs because the sole is the entire flat bottom of a foot or shoe, while a pad is a distinct localized cushion, and pads are characteristic of paws rather than human feet.
  • Footpads: Similar because both name the fleshy cushions on an animal's foot; differs because footpad is the more technical, zoological term, while pads is the general everyday word.
  • Paws: Similar because both relate to an animal's foot; differs because the paw is the whole foot including toes and claws, while pads are only the soft under-cushions beneath it.
  • Pillows: Similar because both are soft supports filled with yielding material; differs because pillows are domestic objects for supporting the head in bed, while pads are flatter and serve protective or functional cushioning roles.
  • Wads: Similar because both are soft, compact masses of material; differs because a wad is an irregular ball or lump, while a pad is flat and deliberately shaped.
  • Insoles: Similar because both cushion under the foot; differs because insoles are manufactured shoe components placed inside footwear, not anatomical structures.
  • Treads: Similar because both are ground-contact surfaces of feet (or footwear) that leave tracks; differs because treads are the ribbed gripping surface of a shoe or tire, not fleshy cushions.
  • Notepads: Similar because both are "pads" in the flat-stack-of-paper sense; differs because notepad narrows the meaning specifically to a block of paper, losing the cushion and anatomical senses entirely.
