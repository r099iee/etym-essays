dwellings — This is a multiple-root note, but the English word descends through a single Germanic line; the deeper PIE root is debated and is flagged below. Stage 1 — PIE (debated): *dʰeu- or *dʰwel- "to cloud, dull, confound; to blow, raise dust, darken" (the family of daze, dusk, dust; Pokorny's *dʰeuə-). NOTE: the attribution of the Germanic "delay/lead-astray" verb to this root is traditional but contested; the deeper PIE stage must be treated as a debated hypothesis. Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic: *dwaljaną "to delay, hinder, lead astray"; adjective *dwala- "dazed, confused, foolish" (reconstructed; attested indirectly in the Germanic daughters). Stage 3 — Old English: dwellan (transitive/causative) "to mislead, deceive, hinder, delay, keep back"; alongside dwelian (intransitive) "to go astray, err, wander" (attested from the 9th century). Stage 4 — Middle English: dwellen "to remain, tarry, stay, abide" (attested from the 12th century; the sense shifts from "delay/linger" toward "stay, reside"). Stage 5 — English: dwell "to live, reside" (the "reside" sense attested from the 15th century). Stage 6 — English: dwelling "a place of residence" (the gerund/noun, from Old English dwellung "a delaying, a lingering," shifting to "habitation"); plural dwellings (preserved inflection). NOTE ON THE DEBATE: the standard account connects the Germanic stem to the PIE "cloud/dull" family (via the idea of being led astray, dulled, diverted), but the deeper link is disputed; some scholars treat the Germanic verb as an independent formation. The stages from Proto-Germanic onward are secure; the PIE stage is the debated one.
