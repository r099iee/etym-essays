• Paraśurāma: Similar because both refer to the exact same legendary sixth avatar of Lord Viṣṇu; differs because Paraśurāma is a descriptive popular compound meaning "Rāma with the Axe" (*paraśu* + *rāma*), highlighting his iconic martial weapon, whereas Jāmadagnya is his formal, aristocratic Sanskrit patronymic title denoting his sacred paternal descent from sage Jamadagni.
  • Bhārgava: Similar because both are patronymic titles applicable to Paraśurāma; differs because Bhārgava is a broader ancestral designation meaning "descendant of Bhṛgu" (encompassing an entire clan of ancient sages, including Cyavana, Śukra, and Jamadagni), whereas Jāmadagnya specifically pinpoints direct sonship to Jamadagni.
  • Dāśarathi: Similar because both are prestigious epic Sanskrit patronymics applied to major avatars of Lord Viṣṇu named Rāma; differs because Dāśarathi signifies "son of Daśaratha" (referring to Rāma the prince and king of Ayodhyā, the hero of the Rāmāyaṇa), whereas Jāmadagnya signifies "son of Jamadagni" (the earlier axe-wielding avatar).
  • Balarāma: Similar because both are divine epic heroes bearing the name Rāma who wield unconventional weapons (Balarāma the plough, Jāmadagnya the axe) and are considered avataric manifestations of divine power; differs because Balarāma is the son of Vasudeva/Rohiṇī and the gentle elder brother of Kṛṣṇa, whereas Jāmadagnya is the son of Jamadagni who exterminated the warrior caste.
  • Dattātreya: Similar because both are revered as avatars of Lord Viṣṇu born as sons of celebrated Vedic sages (Dattātreya to Atri, Jāmadagnya to Jamadagni); differs because Dattātreya is a serene, wandering, non-dual ascetic who teaches spiritual detachment (the *Avadhūta*), whereas Jāmadagnya is a militant warrior-sage who waged cosmic war against tyrannical kings.
  • Karṇa: Similar because both are tragic, formidable masters of archery in the Mahābhārata connected by destiny; differs because Jāmadagnya was the supreme divine teacher who cursed Karṇa for concealing his identity, whereas Karṇa was his mortal student who suffered the consequences of that curse at Kurukṣetra.
  • Droṇa: Similar because both were Brahmins who mastered the martial sciences of warfare (*Dhanurveda*) and trained great princes; differs because Droṇa served as an appointed military preceptor in the royal court of Hastināpura, whereas Jāmadagnya operated outside all state institutions as an autonomous cosmic avenger.
  • Bhīṣma: Similar because both are immortal or long-lived (*Cirañjīvī* / *Icchā-mṛtyu*) warrior-titans who engaged in an epic, days-long duel over the princess Ambā; differs because Bhīṣma is a Kshatriya prince bound by eternal vows of celibacy and loyalty to the Kuru throne, whereas Jāmadagnya was his Brahmin preceptor who fought to enforce moral justice for a wronged woman.
