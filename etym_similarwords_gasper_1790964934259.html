• fag: Similar because both are informal British slang terms for a cigarette; differs because fag became the ubiquitous, everyday British generic slang term, whereas gasper specifically emphasizes cheap, harsh, unfiltered, strong tobacco.
  • cigarette: Similar because cigarette is the direct formal term; differs because cigarette is completely neutral, formal, and global, lacking the rough, humorous slang character of gasper.
  • smoke: Similar because both refer to tobacco products; differs because smoke is a broad, collective, informal term that can refer to cigars, pipes, or cigarettes generally (e.g., "stepping out for a smoke").
  • coffin nail: Similar because both are colorful, gritty slang for cigarettes; differs because coffin nail is a dark, ominous American and British dysphemism highlighting the lethal health hazards of smoking.
  • weed: Similar because both are colloquial terms for tobacco; differs because the weed was an old British literary slang term for tobacco in general (from the tobacco plant), whereas in modern slang weed almost exclusively denotes cannabis.
  • cigarillo: Similar because both are small combustible tobacco rolls; differs because cigarillo is wrapped in whole tobacco leaf rather than cigarette paper and is a formal Spanish-derived term.
  • roll-up: Similar because both are unfiltered and strong; differs because a roll-up is hand-rolled by the smoker using loose tobacco and rolling papers rather than factory-made.
  • snout: Similar because both are British slang associated with smoking; differs because snout is British prison and underworld cant referring to tobacco currency or rations.
  • butt: Similar because both relate to cigarettes; differs because butt specifically refers to the discarded stub or filter end of a smoked cigarette.
  • puffer: Similar because both can describe smokers or smoking devices; differs because puffer can also refer to steam locomotives, inhalers, or fish.
