- Rome, 44 BCE — a family name: Gaius Julius Caesar is murdered, and his name becomes the title of his successors.
- 4th c. — Gothic borrowing: the Goths take Latin caesar as kaisar, and the word marches east with armies and missionaries.
- 9th–10th c. — Slavonic emperor: Old Church Slavonic cesar names Christian emperors, and Bulgarian and Serbian rulers claim it.
- 1547 — Ivan takes the crown: Ivan IV is crowned tsar of all Rus, and his sons become the first tsarevichi of Moscow.
- 1797 — Paul sorts the nursery: succession laws distinguish the cesarevich heir from mere tsar's sons, though English ignores the nicety.
- 1868–1918 — Alexei, the last: the haemophiliac heir becomes the world's most pitied prince, then a victim of revolution.
- Today — tale and memory: Tsarevitch lives in wonder-tales and histories, the questing Ivan of the firebird and the lost boy of Ekaterinburg at once.
