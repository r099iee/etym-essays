• thereupon: Similar because both adverbs mark an action that closely follows upon an antecedent event; differs because thereupon places a stronger emphasis on immediate temporal and causal succession ("upon that having occurred, then..."), whereas therewith encompasses physical accompaniment, instrumental agency ("by means of that"), and additive association.
  • thereunto: Similar because both are archaic and formal pronominal adverbs compounded with there; differs because thereunto denotes direction, purpose, or reference toward an objective ("unto that", "to that end"), whereas therewith denotes accompaniment, connection, or concurrent succession.
  • forthwith: Similar because both can indicate prompt temporal action in formal narrative and legal contexts; differs because forthwith strictly means without delay, immediately, or at once as an imperative temporal urgency, whereas therewith explicitly ties the subsequent event back to an antecedent cause or accompanying circumstance.
  • herewith: Similar because both are deictic pronominal compounds ending in -with; differs because herewith uses a proximal deictic stem referring to something accompanying *this present communication* or document (e.g., "enclosed herewith"), whereas therewith uses a distal deictic stem referring back to a previously mentioned fact, object, or event.
  • thereby: Similar because both connect an action back to a preceding circumstance; differs because thereby explicitly denotes causation, consequence, or agency ("by that means", "as a direct result of which"), whereas therewith centers on co-occurrence, companionship, or sequential unfolding.
  • moreover: Similar because both can function additively to append an additional fact to an ongoing discourse; differs because moreover is a pure rhetorical connective meaning "furthermore" or "in addition", lacking the concrete instrumental ("using that") and temporal-coincidence ("and at that moment") capacities of therewith.
  • concomitantly: Similar because both capture concurrent occurrence; differs because concomitantly is a Latinate, polysyllabic scholarly adverb emphasizing simultaneous, parallel co-existence of trends or phenomena, whereas therewith is a Germanic pronominal adverb denoting immediate narrative sequence or physical attachment.
