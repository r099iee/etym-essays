• channel: Similar because both denote a navigable body of water between landmasses (the English Channel, the Bristol Channel). Differs because a channel is generally wider and often a route through a sea rather than a narrow connection between two seas, and it lacks the figurative "difficulty" sense.
  • passage: Similar because both can name a navigable waterway through or between lands (the Northwest Passage). Differs because passage is far broader (any route, voyage, or corridor) and is not specialized to narrow straits nor used for distress.
  • narrows: Similar because both denote constricted waterways, and "the Narrows" is a common local name. Differs because narrows is more colloquial and purely geographic, with no figurative "distress" extension.
  • sound: Similar because both are names for bodies of water separating land (Long Island Sound, Puget Sound). Differs because a sound is usually wider and often an inlet or estuary rather than a connection between two open seas, and it has no "difficulty" idiom.
  • inlet: Similar because both are coastal water features bounded by land. Differs because an inlet is an indentation of the sea into the land (usually a dead end), while a strait connects two bodies of water and serves as a shipping passage.
  • gulf: Similar because both are large maritime features of geopolitics (Persian Gulf, Gulf of Aden). Differs because a gulf is a broad, deep indentation of the sea, not a narrow connection, and "gulf" also means a wide separation or chasm rather than distress.
  • difficulty: Similar because "in straits" means in difficulty. Differs because difficulty is the abstract generic term, while straits is a vivid metaphor (being squeezed tight) with a distinctly literary, emphatic flavour.
  • predicament: Similar because both denote an awkward, hard-to-escape situation. Differs because predicament is neutral and often humorous or logical, while straits stresses extremity, peril, or poverty ("dire straits").
  • plight: Similar because both describe a bad situation, often of suffering. Differs because plight emphasizes misfortune and helplessness, while straits emphasizes constriction and difficulty and works naturally with adjectives like dire or desperate.
  • distress: Similar because "in straits" means in distress. Differs because distress is an emotional or physical state, while straits names the circumstantial bind that causes it.
