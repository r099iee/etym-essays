• Small talk: Similar because both are light, trivial conversation exchanged in social settings; differs because small talk is the broadest and most colloquial term, often implying unease or the mere filling of time, while pleasantries are more deliberate and courteous — remarks designed to give pleasure rather than merely to pass minutes.
  • Civilities: Similar because both are polite, conventional social gestures or remarks; differs because civilities stress adherence to the etiquette of respect — the minimum courtesies demanded by convention, which can feel stiff — while pleasantries are amiable and cheerful, meant to give pleasure, not just to observe form.
  • Courtesies: Similar because both are courteous acts or remarks; differs because courtesies is the broader term, covering any polite gesture or favour beyond the minimum, while pleasantries are specifically light, good-humoured remarks in conversation.
  • Chitchat: Similar because both are casual, inconsequential conversation; differs because chitchat is informal, gossipy, and often purposeless, while pleasantries are formal, polished, and serve the social function of smoothing transitions.
  • Banter: Similar because both are light, conversational exchange among people; differs because banter implies playful, teasing wit among intimates, with a witty or competitive edge, while pleasantries are courteous, safe, and deliberately uncontroversial remarks for any company.
  • Jests: Similar because both are light remarks made to amuse; differs because a jest is specifically a joke — a piece of humour meant to raise laughter — while a pleasantry in modern usage is a polite remark, not necessarily humorous, though the older sense of "pleasantry" as "jest" shows their kinship.
  • Witticisms: Similar because both are brief, clever remarks; differs because a witticism is a pointed, ingenious remark admired for its cleverness, while a pleasantry is a soft, courteous remark admired for its agreeableness — the one sparkles, the other smooths.
  • Pleasantness: Similar because both derive from the root of "pleasing" and share the idea of agreeableness; differs because pleasantness is the abstract quality of being pleasant or agreeable, while pleasantries are the concrete spoken remarks that produce that effect.
