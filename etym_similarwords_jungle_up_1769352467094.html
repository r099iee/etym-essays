• Camp out: Similar because both mean sleeping in the open in a temporary camp; differs because camp out is the general, neutral term for any outdoor camping, while jungle-up is the specific slang of tramps and hobos for an improvised, rough encampment.
  • Bivouac: Similar because both mean a temporary, improvised outdoor camp without tents; differs because bivouac is a military term for a night in the open, while jungle-up is the American hobo's word for settling down to rest.
  • Encamp: Similar because both mean to set up camp; differs because encamp is formal and general, often of an army or a settled group, while jungle-up is informal, historical slang for a rough vagrant camp.
  • Make camp / pitch camp: Similar because both describe establishing a camp for the night; differs because these are general and neutral, while jungle-up carries the specific flavour of hobo, tramp, or rough improvisation.
  • Bed down: Similar because both mean settling to sleep somewhere rough; differs because bed down focuses on the act of lying down to sleep anywhere (even a room), while jungle-up names the whole act of establishing an outdoor camp.
  • Doss down (UK slang): Similar because both are informal terms for sleeping in improvised quarters; differs because doss down is chiefly British and can mean sleeping anywhere rough, while jungle-up is specifically American and means camping out in the open.
  • Crash (slang): Similar because both are informal terms for sleeping somewhere temporarily; differs because crash usually means sleeping on a couch or floor indoors, while jungle-up always means camping in the open air.
  • Rough it: Similar because both mean enduring rough, improvised outdoor conditions; differs because rough it is a general idiom for enduring discomfort, while jungle-up is the specific act of setting up a makeshift outdoor camp.
  • Hole up: Similar because both mean settling in a temporary shelter or refuge; differs because hole up implies hiding away in a confined spot, while jungle-up is camping openly in the wilds near water.
  • Camp: Similar because both mean to set up a temporary outdoor home; differs because camp is neutral and general (camping grounds, campsites), while jungle-up names specifically the rough, improvised tramp encampment and its way of life.
