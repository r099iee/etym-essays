PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*wer-
"to speak, say"
↓
Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
verbum
"word, speech"
↓
Late / Medieval Latin (c. 11th c. CE)
verva
"fanciful words, talk, banter"
↓
Old French (c. 1250)
verve
"chattering, whimsical speech, caprice"
↓
Modern French (17th–18th c.)
verve
"poetic inspiration, artistic fire, vigor"
↓
English (c. 1803)
verve
"vitality, artistic spirit, vivacity"

Note: The semantic trajectory is remarkable: Latin verbum ('word') passed through Medieval Latin verva to mean 'talkativeness or witty turns of phrase'. In French, this shifted from 'fanciful speech' to 'capricious whim', then to 'poetic frenzy/artistic inspiration', before English borrowed it in the early 19th century as an indispensable term for lively creative vigor.
