- c. 1550–1650 - [Ptolemaic & Copernican Astronomy] - A technical mathematical term describing an orbital circle whose center is displaced from the Earth or Sun; viewed as an objective geometric property of celestial mechanics.
- c. 1650–1700 - [Mechanics & Geometry] - Extended to physical machinery, referring to cams and off-center axles that translate circular motion into reciprocating stroke; purely functional and mechanical in connotation.
- c. 1700–1780 - [Early Enlightenment Metaphor] - Figuratively applied to intellectual unorthodoxy and individuals whose thinking did not align with orthodox religious or social centers; initially viewed with mild philosophical skepticism.
- c. 1780–1900 - [The Golden Age of English Eccentricity] - Celebrated in British society as a distinctive badge of aristocratic liberty, intellectual independence, and endearing personal character; immortalized in literature (Sterne, Dickens) as an admirable human virtue.
- c. 1900–Present - [Modern Psychology & Popular Culture] - Differentiated strictly from mental pathology as benign, healthy nonconformity characterized by high curiosity and low stress, while retaining its precise quantitative definitions in orbital mechanics and conic geometry.
