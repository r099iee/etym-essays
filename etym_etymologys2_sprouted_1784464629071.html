Derivation chain for SPROUTED (past tense and past participle of sprout); the card's form is the weak past, so the final node is bolded as sprouted, with the verb sprout given as the base of the chain. [1] PIE root *sper- "to strew, scatter, sow" (often reconstructed with extensions, e.g. *spreu-d-, for spreading or pushing-forth motion). Attested daughters: Latin spargere "to scatter, sprinkle" (whence sparse, disperse), Greek speirō "to sow" and spóros "seed, sowing" (whence spore). DEBATED: the exact shape of the PIE root — simple *sper- or extended *spreu- / *spreud- — is uncertain; the semantic core "fling outward, strew, push forth" is agreed. [2] Proto-Germanic *spreutaną "to sprout, spring up, shoot forth" — the growth-motion extension of the strew-root within Germanic. [3] Old English sprūtan "to sprout, germinate" (attested chiefly in compounds and derivatives in the 8th–11th c. record; the simplex verb is thinly attested but its existence is securely reconstructed from the family). [4] Middle English spruten, sprouten "to bud, germinate, put forth shoots" (13th c.). [5] Modern English sprout (v.) "to put forth shoots; to develop rapidly" and (n.) "a young shoot; Brussels sprout" (15th–16th c.). [6] Modern English sprouted "past tense and past participle" — the regular weak past formed on sprout (16th c. onward). Note on the chain: the verb is native Germanic throughout, with clear West Germanic cognates (German sprießen, Dutch spruiten, Middle Low German spruten) and a North Germanic relative (Old Norse sproti "sprout, twig"). The PIE stage [1] is the only debated link, and the Germanic stage [2] onward is secure. The past sprouted is a regular, fully transparent modern formation.
