• Twice: Similar because both denote a doubling; differs because twice counts occurrences of an action (he knocked twice), whereas doubly intensifies the quality itself without counting repetitions.
  • Twofold: Similar because both mean 'double in amount or degree'; differs because twofold is the formal adjectival form (a twofold increase) that cannot itself modify a verb, while doubly is the adverbial and more idiomatic choice in emphatic speech.
  • Double: Similar as the bare adverbial form of the same root; differs because it is confined to fixed phrases of optical confusion (to see double) and cannot carry the figurative emphasis of 'doubly sure'.
  • Twofoldly: Similar because it is the rare exact adverbial synonym; differs because it is a clumsy, learned doublet seldom used, where doubly is the natural, standard adverb.
  • Redoubled: Similar because both suggest intensity increased again; differs because redoubled is an adjective or participle describing the result (a redoubled guard), while doubly is an adverb modifying the action.
  • On two counts / for two reasons: Similar because both express the grounds sense of doubly; differ because they are phrasal and explicit, spelling out the second reason, while doubly is compressed and rhetorical, folding both grounds into one word.
