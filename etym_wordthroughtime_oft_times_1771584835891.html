- [Old English oft] - 'Oft' is the native word for 'often'; 'oft-times' forms on the model of 'sometimes' and 'betimes' as a poetic compound meaning many times, frequently.
- [Middle English to Renaissance: literary staple] - 'Oft-times' flourishes in ballads, verse, and proverbial language; connotation is grave, rhythmic, and oracular - the adverb of fate and recurring sorrow rather than of casual frequency.
- [Modern usage] - 'Oft-times' survives only as an archaic or deliberately poetic form; connotation is antique, ballad-like, and formal, chosen for dignity and old-world rhythm where plain 'often' would fall flat.
