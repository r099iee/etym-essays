- 5th c. BCE - *Classical Greek παράδειγμα (parádeigma)*: Used in rhetoric, architecture, and Platonic metaphysics to denote a physical pattern, architectural blueprint, legal precedent, or divine eternal archetype.
- 4th c. CE–1500s - *Late Latin & Renaissance Grammar*: Adopted into grammatical pedagogy (*paradigma*) to designate the standardized inflection tables showing the complete set of case endings or conjugations.
- 1600s–1800s - *Early Modern English*: Used in literature and rhetoric as a formal synonym for an exemplary pattern, illustrative story, or moral model.
- 1916 - *Saussurean Structural Linguistics*: Ferdinand de Saussure introduced the 'paradigmatic axis' of language, contrasting vertical associative substitutions with horizontal syntagmatic sequences.
- 1962 - *Kuhnian Philosophy of Science*: Thomas Kuhn published *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*, permanently transforming 'paradigm' and 'paradigm shift' into worldwide intellectual household words.
- Late 20th–21st c. - *Corporate & Technological Jargon*: Ubiquitously adopted across management consulting, computer science, and tech marketing to describe revolutionary business models and software architectures.
