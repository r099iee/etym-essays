- Old English pledge-word - treowth as truth, fidelity, sworn covenant to lord or kin; grave, oath-bound, heroic perception
- Early Middle English moral truth - trouthe as both honesty and loyalty in Langland and Chaucer; ethical, allegorical, Christian perception
- c.1300 marriage specialization - bitrothen to promise in marriage by hand-plighting; contractual, familial, public perception
- Tudor-Stuart legal solemnity - betrothed as quasi-marital state with banns and breach suits; juridical, Shakespearean, biblical perception
- Victorian respectability - betrothed announced in papers, ringed, chaperoned; proper, sentimental, respectable perception
- Modern archaic-comic survival - solemn in liturgy and obituaries, arch in Wodehouse and fantasy; reverent versus playfully stuffy perception
