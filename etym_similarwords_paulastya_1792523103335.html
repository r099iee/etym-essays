• Rāvaṇa: Similar because both refer to the exact same mythic antagonist and ten-headed sovereign of Laṅkā in the Rāmāyaṇa; differs because Rāvaṇa (from Sanskrit ru, 'to roar or cause wailing') is his descriptive personal name bestowed by Shiva after he shook Mount Kailāśa, whereas Paulastya is his dignified patronymic lineage epithet emphasizing his descent from the primordial sage Pulastya.
  • Daśānana: Similar because both are frequent epithets applied to Rāvaṇa throughout classical epic poetry; differs because Daśānana (literally 'ten-faced' or 'ten-mouthed') is a physical somatic compound highlighting his terrifying supernatural multi-headed morphology, whereas Paulastya is a genealogical designation of ancestral origin.
  • Daśagrīva: Similar because both designate the lord of Laṅkā in Vālmīki's verse; differs because Daśagrīva (literally 'ten-necked') focuses specifically on the physical anatomy of his ten throats, whereas Paulastya recalls his high-born brahminical heritage and cosmological connection to Brahmā.
  • Rakṣasendra: Similar because both describe Rāvaṇa's imperial role in the narrative; differs because Rakṣasendra (from rakṣas + indra, 'lord/king of the rakshasas') denotes his political and military sovereignty over the demon race, whereas Paulastya denotes his hereditary paternal lineage.
  • Laṅkeśvara: Similar because both refer to the monarchical power of Rāvaṇa; differs because Laṅkeśvara (literally 'sovereign lord of Laṅkā') designates his territorial kingship over the island citadel of Laṅkā, whereas Paulastya traces his blood descent.
  • Vaiśravaṇa: Similar because both are patronymic titles rooted in the exact same household; differs because Vaiśravaṇa (patronymic of Viśravas) refers primarily to Kubera, the god of wealth and half-brother of Rāvaṇa, who is also a Paulastya by blood but represents righteous celestial stewardship rather than demonic destruction.
  • Pulastyan: Similar because both signify belonging to the lineage of the sage Pulastya; differs because Pulastyan is an Anglicized adjectival formation, whereas Paulastya is the authentic Sanskrit nominative patronymic substantive formed via classical vṛddhi ablaut.
  • patronymic: Similar because Paulastya is an exemplary token of this linguistic category; differs because patronymic is the generic morphological classification for any name derived from a father or paternal ancestor, whereas Paulastya is the specific proper Sanskrit designation.
  • epithet: Similar because Paulastya functions stylistically as an epic epithet; differs because an epithet is any descriptive word or phrase expressing a quality or characteristic of a person or thing, whereas Paulastya is specifically genealogical.
  • demon-king: Similar because both identify Rāvaṇa's status in popular folklore and translations; differs because demon-king is a blunt modern English compound that obscures Rāvaṇa's profound Vedic erudition and semi-divine sage ancestry inherent in Paulastya.
