Pre-Roman Alpine substrate word *larix- 'the larch' (unattested; a non-Indo-European native Alpine name — NO PIE ancestry, since the word obeys no Indo-European word-formation rule) → Greek lárix (λάριξ) 'the larch, or its resin' (early loan from the substrate, attested from the 4th c. BCE, Theophrastus) → Latin larix, laricis 'the larch' (Pliny the Elder, 1st c. AD, on Alpine timber) → Old French larice / lariçe 'larch' (medieval Romance development) → Middle English lerge / larch 'larch' (14th c., via Old French; the sibilant hardening to English -ch) → Modern English LARCH (spelling fixed by the 16th c.).

NOTE ON DEBATE: The word is agreed to be a loan from an unknown pre-Roman Alpine language; Greek and Latin are independent early loans of the same substrate word, not stages of each other; the Germanic forms (Old High German lariha → German Lärche; Dutch lork) are parallel borrowings from Latin, not ancestors of the English word; English tamarack is an Algonquian loan for the American species.
