- 1951 — Conceived and compiled by SIL linguist Richard S. Pittman as a modest 10-page mimeographed pamphlet titled Ethnologue, cataloging 40 languages to guide missionary translators.
- 1953–1965 — Expanded through 6 printed editions, growing to cover several hundred indigenous languages across the Americas and the Pacific.
- 1967–2000 — Transformed into a globally recognized academic authority under editor Barbara F. Grimes (8th through 14th editions), expanding coverage to over 6,800 languages and pioneering computerized linguistic database architecture.
- 2000s — Anchored the creation of the International Organization for Standardization's ISO 639-3 standard, adopting its three-letter codes as the universal digital language keys.
- 2010s–present — Transitioned into an annually updated digital subscription web database (`ethnologue.com`), integrating the Expanded Graded Intergenerational Disruption Scale (EGIDS) to assess language vitality for governments, NGOs, and global tech corporations.
