• Unpunished: Similar because both mean that no penalty was exacted; differs because unpunished is the plain legal fact, lacking the idiomatic sting and the buried tax metaphor of scot-free.
  • Unscathed: Similar because both mean escaping harm; differs because unscathed stresses physical or reputational survival rather than moral accountability or penalty.
  • With impunity: Similar because both describe acting without consequence; differs because with impunity is more formal and often implies systemic license to offend again, while scot-free is idiomatic and vivid.
  • Unaccountable: Similar because both imply avoiding a reckoning; differs because unaccountable is broader (evading responsibility, or not requiring justification), while scot-free specifically means escaping penalty or harm after an event.
  • Exempt: Similar because both can mean free from an obligation; differs because exempt is the neutral administrative term for being released from a duty or tax, with no connotation of wrongdoing or punishment.
  • Clear (of blame): Similar because both can mean free from responsibility; differs because clear implies vindication or exoneration, whereas scot-free implies having deserved punishment but escaped it.
  • Without consequence: Similar because both denote no penalty following an action; differs because the phrase is neutral and analytical, lacking the idiomatic, slightly archaic charge of scot-free.
  • Free: Similar because both involve liberty from something; differs because free is far broader and lacks the specific after-image of escaping a due payment or penalty.
