Arabic / Semitic Toponym & Textile Root (c. 10th–12th c.)
Ġazzah (Gaza) / qazz (raw silk)
"Palestinian trading city; raw silk or sheer woven fabric"
Note: Historically debated etymology: either a toponymic derivation from the ancient weaving port of Gaza (Arabic Ġazzah), or from Arabic qazz ('raw silk'), via Spanish or Italian maritime merchants.

Medieval Latin / Italian Maritime Trade (c. 1200–1280)
gazzatum / gazzara
"very fine, light, transparent silk fabric"
↓
Old French (c. 13th–14th c.)
gaze
"sheer, transparent silk textile or veil"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1580–1600)
gaze / gawse / gauze
"a very thin, slight, transparent fabric of silk or linen"
Note: Standardized spelling 'gauze' influenced by French pronunciation and British textile imports.

19th Century English (Medical Application) (c. 1860–1880)
