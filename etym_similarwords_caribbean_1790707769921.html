• Antillean: Similar because both refer to the West Indian island realm; differs because 'Antillean' is derived from the mythical island of *Antillia* and refers strictly to the archipelago (Greater and Lesser Antilles), whereas 'Caribbean' encompasses the islands, the vast oceanic sea itself, and the continental circum-Caribbean coastal nations (e.g., Belize, Guyana, Colombia, Venezuela).
  • West Indian: Similar because it denotes the same geographical island region; differs because 'West Indian' reflects the Eurocentric colonial misnomer arising from Columbus believing he had reached India/Asia, whereas 'Caribbean' is derived directly from the indigenous Carib people.
  • Circum-Caribbean: Similar because it covers the Caribbean sphere; differs because 'circum-Caribbean' is a technical anthropological and geopolitical term specifically designating the continental mainland coastal zones bordering the Caribbean Sea.
  • Tropical: Similar because the Caribbean lies entirely within the tropical zone; differs because 'tropical' is a broad climatic and latitudinal descriptor applied to any region between the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn worldwide.
  • Archipelagic: Similar because the Caribbean islands form an extensive archipelago; differs because 'archipelagic' is a generic geographical adjective for any group of islands, lacking the regional and cultural specificity of 'Caribbean'.
  • Equatorial: Similar because both evoke warm, sun-drenched maritime regions; differs because 'equatorial' refers strictly to the immediate vicinity of the equator (0° latitude), whereas the Caribbean lies primarily between 10°N and 25°N.
  • Maritime: Similar because the Caribbean identity is inextricably linked to the sea; differs because 'maritime' is a general adjective relating to seafaring, naval navigation, or coastal ecology anywhere on Earth.
