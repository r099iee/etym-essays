- 1610s — of considerable dimensions: coined as sizeable for ships, estates, and portions, a merchant’s word for bulk worth bargaining over.
- 17th–18th c. — commercial respectability: ledgers and wills use it for fortunes, cargoes, and legacies; bigness with a powdered wig.
- 19th c. — people and houses: novelists describe sizable appetites, sizable country houses, and sizable gentlemen, politely sizing up bodies and buildings.
- 20th c. — the abstract career: majorities, samples, investments, and literatures become sizable; social science adopts it as its favourite unit of respectable magnitude.
- Spelling split — America regularises to sizable after Webster, Britain keeps sizeable; the Atlantic spells bigness two ways.
- Today — measured approval: the formal, auditor-friendly synonym for big, granting standing without surrendering precision’s pretence.
