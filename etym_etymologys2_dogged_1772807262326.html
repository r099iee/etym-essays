Old English (c. 1050) docga "dog" ↓ Middle English (13th c.) dogge "dog" ↓ English (16th c.) dog "a dog" ↓ Middle English (14th c.) dogged "having the qualities of a dog" ↓ English (sense shift, 18th c.) dogged "stubborn, tenacious, grimly persistent" ↓ English (present) dogged
"Having or showing tenacity and grim
persistence." Debate: the origin of Old English docga is unknown; it may be a diminutive form, but no secure cognates are attested. The sense moved from "doglike" (14th c., later "brutal, cruel") to "stubborn, tenacious" by the 18th century.
