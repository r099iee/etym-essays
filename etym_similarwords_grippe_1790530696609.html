• Influenza: Similar because it is the exact medical equivalent for the viral illness; differs because influenza is the standard scientific and modern English term (derived from Italian), whereas grippe is an archaic, literary, or Continental French term.
  • Flu: Similar because it refers to the same acute viral respiratory infection; differs because flu is a modern, colloquial English truncation, whereas grippe is an old-fashioned, formal, and historic designation.
  • Catarrh: Similar because both involve respiratory inflammation, coughing, and mucus; differs because catarrh is a broad symptom descriptor for mucosal discharge rather than a specific epidemic viral disease.
  • Ague: Similar because both involve severe chills, feverish shivering, and physical weakness; differs because ague historically referred specifically to malaria or intermittent marsh fevers rather than respiratory influenza.
  • Coryza: Similar because both affect the upper respiratory tract; differs because coryza refers specifically to acute nasal inflammation or the common head cold without the profound systemic fever and prostration of grippe.
  • Malaise: Similar because both describe feelings of bodily discomfort and illness; differs because malaise is a non-specific general symptom of fatigue or unease, not a discrete viral illness.
  • Seizure: Similar because the root meaning of grippe is a sudden clutching or seizing; differs because in English medical terminology, seizure refers to a neurological or epileptic convulsion.
