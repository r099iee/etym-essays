• Eulogy: Similar because both denote a formal expression of high praise; differs because a eulogy is specifically praise of the deceased delivered at a funeral, whereas an encomium may honour the living and is not bound to mourning.
  • Panegyric: Similar because both denote a formal speech or written tribute of praise; differs because panegyric leans toward public oratory and flattery of rulers or cities, while encomium is the more general word covering both spoken and written praise.
  • Tribute: Similar because both honour a person or achievement; differs because a tribute may be a gift, a memorial, or an act of remembrance, while an encomium is always a verbal composition.
  • Compliment: Similar because both express approval or admiration of someone; differs because a compliment is small, casual, and face-to-face, whereas an encomium is a composed, literary set-piece.
  • Commendation: Similar because both denote formal approval; differs because commendation is often official and practical (a commendation for valour, a letter of commendation), while an encomium is ornamental and rhetorical.
  • Laudation: Similar because both denote the act of praising highly; differs because laudation is the general noun for praising, while encomium names the specific literary or rhetorical composition of praise.
  • Paean: Similar because both are formal tributes of joy or gratitude; differs because a paean is originally a hymn to Apollo, then any song of triumph or thanksgiving, and is more musical and celebratory than the composed encomium.
  • Homage: Similar because both express reverence and honour; differs because homage historically denoted the feudal acknowledgement of a lord and now means respectful acknowledgement generally, whereas an encomium is specifically an elaborate speech or piece of writing.
  • Testimonial: Similar because both are formal statements in someone's favour; differs because a testimonial is typically a practical attestation of character or fitness (a reference for employment), not a literary tribute.
  • Hymn of praise: Similar because both are formal, elevated tributes; differs because a hymn is sung and religious in origin, while an encomium is secular and may be spoken or written.
