- Original adjective sense - Entered English in the 15th century meaning 'depending on chance, accidental, or uncertain,' tied to events that merely happen to occur.
- Conditional sense - Shifted to mean 'dependent on circumstances or conditions,' as in something contingent upon another event, a formal and logical register.
- Military noun sense - In the 18th century, came to name a body of troops that a state contributed to a combined force, stressing disciplined, self-contained units.
- Extended noun sense - Broadened to any identifiable group forming part of a larger gathering or organization, the dominant modern meaning.
- Contemporary usage - The noun is common in organizational and political contexts (a national contingent at a summit), while the adjective survives chiefly in formal 'contingent upon' clauses.
