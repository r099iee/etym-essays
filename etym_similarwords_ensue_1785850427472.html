• Follow: Similar because both mean to come after something; differs because "follow" is the general verb for coming after in space, time, or order and can be transitive with a person as subject ("I follow you"), while "ensue" is impersonal and intransitive, used of events.
  • Result: Similar because both denote what follows from a cause; differs because "result" emphasizes the causal product and can be a noun or a verb, while "ensue" emphasizes the temporal coming-after and is a verb used of events.
  • Arise: Similar because both mean to come into being; differs because "arise" is used of problems, questions, and opportunities without any sense of sequence, while "ensue" always implies following something earlier.
  • Supervene: Similar because both mean to follow upon something; differs because "supervene" is a formal, technical word (medicine, philosophy) for something following unexpectedly upon an existing condition, while "ensue" is the ordinary follow-on verb.
  • Succeed: Similar because both mean to come after in order; differs because "succeed" takes an object ("X succeeds Y") and implies taking the place of what came before, while "ensue" has no object and implies following on, not replacement.
  • Occur: Similar because both name events taking place; differs because "occur" is neutral about sequence and is the ordinary word for any event, while "ensue" specifically marks the event as following something.
  • Happen: Similar because both name the taking place of events; differs because "happen" is the plain generic verb for any event with no implication of sequence, while "ensue" carries the "come-after" meaning.
  • Transpire: Similar because both mean to happen; differs because "transpire" in the "happen" sense is a criticized Gallicism and can also mean "become known", while "ensue" unambiguously means to follow on.
  • Eventuate: Similar because both mean to result eventually; differs because "eventuate" is formal and needs the preposition "in" ("eventuate in"), while "ensue" stands alone.
  • Issue: Similar because both can mean to proceed from a source; differs because "issue" is formal and transitive ("issued from"), while "ensue" is the everyday verb of following-on.
  • Proceed: Similar because both suggest movement onward; differs because "proceed" emphasizes going forward with an action or journey, while "ensue" emphasizes what happens next in consequence.
  • Come after: Similar because both mean to follow in time; differs because "come after" is the informal phrasal construction that usually needs an object or complement, while "ensue" is the formal intransitive verb.
