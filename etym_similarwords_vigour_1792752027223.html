• vitality: Similar because both describe life force, energetic animation, and health; differs because vitality emphasizes the underlying biological spark of life, resilience, and exuberant radiance of existence, whereas vigour focuses more directly on muscular output, dynamic exertion, physical strength, and active forcefulness.
  • stamina: Similar because both denote physical capacity to perform demanding work; differs because stamina is specifically the endurance to withstand prolonged fatigue, stress, or hardship over extended duration (defensive holding power), whereas vigour is proactive, kinetic energy and robust vitality displayed at any given moment.
  • robustness: Similar because both describe sound health and sturdy constitution; differs because robustness denotes physical sturdiness, structural resilience, and the capacity of an organism, system, or mechanism to resist breakdown under rough conditions, while vigour highlights lively motion, enthusiastic drive, and flourishing growth.
  • energy: Similar because both designate the capacity to perform active work; differs because energy is a broad, neutral physical and psychological currency that can fluctuate wildly hour by hour, whereas vigour implies a deep, ingrained, healthy biological constitution brimming with force.
  • dynamism: Similar because both describe forcefulness and vigorous activity; differs because dynamism is often applied abstractly to charismatic leadership, fast-paced personality, or rapid economic change, while vigour remains anchored in organic vitality, biological health, and rigorous execution.
  • potency: Similar because both describe inherent power to produce an effect; differs because potency focuses on chemical, medicinal, or latent strength (e.g., a potent drug or venom) that may lie dormant until administered, whereas vigour is overt, active, flourishing health in motion.
  • lustiness: Similar because both denote hearty bodily vigor and robust physical appetites; differs because lustiness carries an earthy, boisterous, and sometimes animalistic connotation of raw bodily appetite and loud exuberance, whereas vigour can be refined, intellectual, and dignified.
  • efficacy: Similar because both can describe the legal or institutional power of a law or measure; differs because efficacy measures whether an action successfully achieves its intended result, whereas the vigour of a law denotes its active operational force, enforcement strength, and uncompromised authority.
