Late Latin (c. 4th c.)
battuere
‘to beat, strike, pound’
↓
Old French (11th c.)
batre
‘to beat, strike, thresh’
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
bateren
‘to beat repeatedly, wallop’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
batter
‘to pound; siege verb and noun’
↓
Modern English (current, participle)
battering
‘repeated pounding; its damage’

Note: Latin battuere itself has a debated origin. Ancient grammarians treated it as sound-imitative, while modern scholars often suspect a Gaulish (Celtic) loan into colloquial Latin, since the classical verb for beating was verberare and battuere appears late and vulgarly. Either way the French-to-English road above is securely attested.
