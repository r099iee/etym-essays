• Slipshod: Similar because both mean careless and negligent, especially of work; differs because slipshod (from shoes worn like slippers, down at heel) emphasises low standards and hasty, sloppy execution, while slovenly emphasises dirt, untidiness, and a settled habit of neglect.
  • Untidy: Similar because both describe a lack of neatness; differs because untidy is mild and neutral (an untidy desk, no blame attached), while slovenly is strongly disapproving and implies carelessness of character, not just a moment of disorder.
  • Messy: Similar because both describe disorder; differs because messy is informal, neutral-to-affectionate, and can be temporary (a messy room after a party), while slovenly implies chronic neglect and carries censure.
  • Sloppy: Similar because both mean careless and loose; differs because sloppy stresses emotional or physical looseness (sloppy work, a sloppy kiss) and is informal, while slovenly has an old-fashioned, moral weight and a stronger suggestion of dirt.
  • Disheveled: Similar because both describe neglected appearance; differs because disheveled refers specifically to ruffled hair and rumpled clothing, often temporarily (hair blown by the wind), while slovenly is a character trait covering habits, house, and work.
  • Unkempt: Similar because both mean not cared for; differs because unkempt (from "uncombed") focuses on grooming, on rough hair and beard, while slovenly ranges over dress, home, habits, and work.
  • Slatternly: Similar because both describe a dirty, negligent person and come from the same semantic field; differs because slatternly is used specifically (and now archaically) of a dirty, slovenly woman or housekeeper, whereas slovenly is gender-neutral.
  • Negligent: Similar because both imply failure to care; differs because negligent is neutral, legal, and clinical (negligent driving, negligent manslaughter), naming a failure of duty without the image of dirt, while slovenly is vivid, personal, and about disorder rather than duty.
  • Slothful: Similar because both imply laziness; differs because slothful names indolence and inertia (the deadly sin of sloth), while slovenly names the untidy, slipshod output that laziness produces.
  • Scruffy: Similar because both describe shabby, unkempt dress; differs because scruffy is informal and can be affectionate (a scruffy dog, scruffy students), while slovenly is disapproving and implies neglect and dirt rather than mere shabbiness.
  • Grubby: Similar because both imply dirt; differs because grubby means literally dirty or grimy (grubby hands), a smaller, physical matter, while slovenly covers whole habits, house, and character.
  • Bedraggled: Similar because both describe a dishevelled, soaked, or dragged-down appearance; differs because bedraggled implies the effect of rain, mud, or a struggle (bedraggled and wet), while slovenly implies habitual neglect rather than external mishap.
  • Careless: Similar because both mean without care; differs because careless is general (a careless mistake) and can be momentary, while slovenly denotes a settled, messy neglect that shows in appearance and habit.
  • Slob (and sloblike): Similar because a slob is exactly a slovenly person; differs because slob is modern, informal, and noun-heavy (chiefly North American slang), while slovenly is the formal, adjective form of the older and more literary word.
