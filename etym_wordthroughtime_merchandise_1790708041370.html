- c. 1300–1500s: Denoted both the trading occupation of a merchant and the collective physical wares of overseas commerce - Respected trade & maritime wealth.
- 1600s–1800s: Central term in classical literature and colonial mercantilism for commercial cargo and wholesale trade - Associated with industrious middle-class enterprise and civic prosperity.
- 1900s–1970s: Re-functionalized in corporate retail as a verb and discipline ('merchandising') - Modernized into retail science, shelf placement, and window design.
- 1980s–Present: Clipped to 'merch' in pop culture, designating branded entertainment memorabilia, band apparel, and influencer products - High commercial and fandom value.
