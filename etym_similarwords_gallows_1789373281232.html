• Gibbet: Similar because both refer to upright wooden structures used in capital punishment; differs because a gallows is the apparatus upon which the prisoner is actually hanged, whereas a gibbet specifically refers to the post with an overhanging arm from which the executed criminal's body was suspended in iron chains or an iron cage after death, left hanging for weeks or months in public view as a decomposing deterrent to others.
  • Scaffold: Similar because both designate elevated wooden platforms used for public capital executions; differs because a scaffold is a broad, elevated wooden stage that can be used for various forms of execution (most notably beheading by axe or guillotine) as well as speeches, whereas a gallows is specifically the upright post-and-beam frame designed for hanging by the neck.
  • Noose: Similar because both are intimately associated with execution by hanging; differs because the noose is the running loop of knotted rope that is placed around the condemned person's neck, whereas the gallows is the heavy wooden architectural structure from which the rope is suspended.
  • Guillotine: Similar because both are mechanical instruments of state-administered capital punishment designed for public execution; differs because the guillotine is a mechanical apparatus utilizing a weighted, angled falling blade between vertical tracks to execute by rapid decapitation (popularized during the French Revolution), whereas the gallows executes through hanging, suspension, or neck fracture.
  • Cross: Similar because both are ancient wooden frameworks used by sovereign authorities for public, humiliating, and agonizing execution of condemned individuals; differs because the cross is an instrument of slow crucifiction and exposure dating from Roman antiquity, imbued with immense sacred theological significance in Christianity as the instrument of universal redemption, whereas the gallows carries predominantly profane, ignominious, and criminal associations.
  • Halter: Similar because both relate directly to the mechanics of hanging; differs because a halter is a piece of rope, cord, or noose used for leading animals or hanging criminals, focusing purely on the cordage rather than the architectural wooden apparatus of the gallows.
  • Pillory: Similar because both are wooden structures set up in public squares for state judicial punishment; differs because the pillory was a non-lethal device with holes for the head and hands designed for public humiliation, shaming, and physical peltings of petty offenders, whereas the gallows is an explicitly lethal instrument of capital execution.
