- [Old French 'tell over'] - In its current form since Middle English (from Old French reconter, 'to tell again'), 'recount' meant to relate or narrate in detail and in order - the great verb of storytelling and chronicle.
- [Historical solemnity] - In 17th and 19th century prose it became the formal pledge of the historian - 'I shall recount...' - dignified, programmatic, the promise of an ordered, connected narrative.
- [Modern coexistence] - In the 19th and 20th centuries, as the homograph 'recount' (to count again) strengthened, the two spellings settled side by side, the ballot-sense now a fixture of democratic politics.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'recount' means both to tell a story in detail and to count ballots again - a word split between chronicle and democracy, with the telling sense retaining its old dignity.
