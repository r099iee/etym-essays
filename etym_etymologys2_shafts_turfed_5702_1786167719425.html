ROOT WORD 1 of 2: SHAFTS
PIE *(s)kep- (to cut, scrape, hew) [variant reconstructions *(s)kap-, *skēp-] — earliest known stage
├── Proto-Germanic: *skaftaz (a shaved or cut rod; spear-handle, pole)
│ └── Old English: sceaft (pole, spear-shaft, shaft of an arrow)
│ └── Middle English: shaft (pole, spear, ray of light)
│ └── English: shaft → shafts (plural noun) ◄ TARGET
├── Cognates: Latin scāpus (shaft, stem, stalk) and Greek skēptron (staff → English sceptre), skaptein (to dig); both point to the cut-and-scrape root
└── Note: The reconstruction is disputed in detail — some scholars write *(s)kap-, others *(s)kep-, others *(s)kēp- (Pokorny gives *skēp-/*skep-, to cut); the s-mobile alternation and the vowel grade are debated, but the Germanic chain *skaftaz → sceaft → shaft is secure. The later sense shaft of light arises in English from the 16th century as a transfer of the spear image.

ROOT WORD 2 of 2: TURFED
Origin debated: possibly PIE *dʰer- (to hold, fasten) or a non-Indo-European substrate; not securely established — earliest stage uncertain
├── Proto-Germanic: *turbą / *turba- (turf, sod, peat)
│ ├── Old Norse: torf (turf, sod)
│ ├── Old English: turf (sod, grass surface, piece of peat)
│ │ └── Middle English: turf
│ │ └── English: turf → turfed (past participle and participial adjective) ◄ TARGET
│ ├── Old High German: zurba → German: Torf (peat)
│ ├── Old Frisian: turf; Middle Dutch: turf; Dutch: turf (peat)
├── Proposed cognate: Sanskrit darbhá (tuft of sacred grass, used in ritual) — sometimes cited as a distant relative but widely regarded as uncertain
└── Note: The Proto-Germanic *turb- has no secure Indo-European etymology; the Sanskrit darbhá link is speculative, and several authorities treat the Germanic word as possibly borrowed from a non-Indo-European substrate (the peat economies of the north). The Germanic family itself is secure: the word for cut sod and peat is attested in every Germanic language. The spelling turfed (f not v) reflects the native sound (contrast the French-derived initial v- of vat), and -ed forms the participle.
