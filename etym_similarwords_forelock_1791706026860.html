• Cowlick: Similar because both refer to a distinct, rebellious tuft or section of hair on the human head that stands apart from the surrounding hair; differs because a cowlick is a spiral whorl or stubborn patch of hair that refuses to lie flat in any direction (often occurring at the crown or hairline), whereas a forelock is specifically a long lock or tress hanging forward from the front hairline above the forehead.
  • Bangs / Fringe: Similar because both bangs (American English) and fringe (British English) describe hair worn over the forehead; differs because bangs/fringe refers to an intentionally cut, straight or styled horizontal line of hair across the brow, whereas a forelock is a natural lock, tress, or tuft, commonly applied to horses and historic masculine hairstyles.
  • Topknot: Similar because both describe a prominent tuft or bunch of hair situated at the front or crown of the head; differs because a topknot is fastened, gathered, or tied up at the top or vertex of the head (as in historical samurai or modern bun styles), whereas a forelock hangs down freely forward across the forehead and face.
  • Quiff: Similar because both describe hair originating from the front hairline above the forehead; differs because a quiff is an artificially brushed back, upward-swept, and styled modern haircut (popularized in pompadour and rockabilly culture), whereas a forelock is a natural hanging lock or equine feature.
  • Tuft: Similar because both denote a small cluster or clump of hairs, feathers, or strands growing close together; differs because tuft is a general, non-specific descriptor that can occur anywhere on the body, plants, or fabrics, whereas forelock is anatomically restricted to the frontal hairline or horse's brow.
  • Mane: Similar because both designate hair growing on the neck and head of equines and other mammals; differs because the mane encompasses the entire long growth of hair extending along the crest of the neck from poll to withers, whereas the forelock is strictly the forwardmost section of the mane falling between the ears over the forehead.
