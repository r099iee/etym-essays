- [Advisors of the Republic] - "Consul" (from the Latin for deliberating together) names the highest republican magistracy; two annually elected consuls share the supreme civil and military power.
- [Competitive office] - In Roman political history the consulship becomes the engine of competitive ambition — short tenure and mutual rivalry drive campaigns and triumphs.
- [Modern diplomatic offshoot] - The word branches into the modern "consul," a diplomatic representative abroad, while the Roman sense of the paired chief magistrates is preserved in historical prose.
