• shelf: similar because both mean a flat horizontal surface that projects; differs because shelf is usually man-made and regular (a bookshelf), while ledge names the natural or rugged projection, especially of rock.
  • ridge: similar a long narrow elevation; differs because a ridge is the crest of a rise running along its top, while a ledge is a flat shelf jutting sideways out of a vertical face.
  • outcrop: similar rock exposed at the surface; differs because an outcrop is a mass of rock rising out of the ground, while a ledge is a thin shelf projecting from a face.
  • sill: similar a flat strip below an opening; differs because sill is specifically the base of a window or door, while ledge is general and geological too.
  • projection: similar anything that juts out; differs because projection is technical and abstract, while ledge is the concrete flat shelf itself.
  • overhang: similar a part that juts out; differs because an overhang sticks out above you and is usually a ceiling or brow of rock, while a ledge is a floor you can stand on.
  • terrace: similar a raised flat shelf of land; differs because a terrace is wide, stepped, and cultivated or built, while a ledge is narrow and natural.
  • berm: similar a raised flat band along a slope; differs because a berm is a mound or ridge of earth (often built), while a ledge is rock cut into a face.
  • rim: similar the edge of an opening; differs because a rim is the lip around a top, while a ledge is a shelf along a side.
  • cornice: similar a projecting moulding; differs because a cornice is the decorative top of a building, while ledge is plain and functional.
  • step: similar a flat place to stand on a slope; differs because a step is one of a regular series of risers, while a ledge is a single irregular shelf.
  • band (geological): similar a horizontal stratum; differs because a band is a layer of colour or rock within a face, while a ledge is a layer that physically juts out.
