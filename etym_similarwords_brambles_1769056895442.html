• Briars: Similar because both are prickly wild plants of the hedgerow; differs because a briar is the thorny wild rose or heath (sweetbriar), and even the wood tobacco pipes are carved from — a rose, not a berry.
  • Thorns: Similar because both describe the sharp points that make the plants hostile; differs because 'thorns' names the points themselves, a religious and proverbial word ('crown of thorns'), while 'brambles' names the whole woody, arching bushes of the Rubus genus.
  • Undergrowth: Similar because both are low vegetation you must push through; differs because undergrowth is any plant layer beneath the trees — ferns, nettles, saplings — and need not be prickly at all.
  • Scrub: Similar because both name rough, low vegetation; differs because scrub is stunted, dry growth of poor land with no thorns and no berries, while brambles are a living fence that is both barrier and food source.
  • Blackberry canes: Similar because brambles are, in their primary sense, exactly the blackberry's thorny canes; differs because 'blackberry' names the plant by its fruit, while 'bramble' names it by its rough, prickly character and its tangled growth.
  • Hedgerow: Similar because both belong to the boundary vegetation of the countryside; differs because a hedgerow is the managed line of mixed shrubs that marks a field edge, while brambles are the wild, unmanaged tangle that grows where cultivation stops.
  • Nettles: Similar because both are stinging, scratchy wild plants that snag passers-by; differs because a nettle is a soft-leaved herb that stings by touch, while a bramble is a woody, thorned bush — the nettle stings, the bramble tears.
