- Old English / Old Norse (snear / snara, c. 800–1100 CE) - Denoted twisted strings, cords, running nooses, and physical animal traps in early Germanic hunting culture.
- Middle English (snare, c. 1150–1450 CE) - Deeply entrenched in theological and allegorical literature via Biblical translations, symbolizing the deceptive temptations of Satan ('snares of the devil').
- Early Modern English (16th–17th Century) - Expanded into legal, political, and romantic discourse for seductive wiles and rhetorical ambushes; also applied to drum acoustic strings.
- 19th Century - Adopted into medical surgery for wire loops used to excise polyps, and celebrated in romantic and fantasy literature for arachnid traps.
- Modern English - Retains full polysemy across wildlife trapping, percussion music, medical technology, and metaphorical pitfalls.
