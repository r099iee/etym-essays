Latin(attested 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
lumbus
"loin, hip, lower back"
(earlier PIE history debated — see note)
↓
Late Latin / Vulgar Latin(4th–8th c.)
*lumbea
"(the) loin-part; that of the loin"
(reconstructed; adjectival reshaping of lumbus)
↓
Old French(12th–13th c.)
loigne
"loin, hip"
(also loyne; -mb- weakens to palatal -gn-)
↓
Middle English(13th–14th c.)
loyne
"loin; lower back"
(plural loynes usual; cf. surloigne "sirloin")
↓
Modern English(16th c. – )
loins
"lower trunk; seat of strength and descent"
(plural fixed by the Bible: "gird up thy loins")

Note on the debate — the pre-Latin history of lumbus is not securely settled: a PIE root *lendh- ("loin, flank") is often cited (cf. Old Norse lend "loins" > Eng. lend), but the form and vocalism are debated, and no reconstructed ancestor is universally accepted.
