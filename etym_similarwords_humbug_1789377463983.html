• Nonsense: Similar because both dismiss statements or ideas as foolish, irrational, and without substance; differs because nonsense describes statements that lack logical coherence, intelligibility, or rational meaning (often without intentional malice), whereas humbug specifically implies deliberate pretense, hypocrisy, or fraudulent posturing.
  • Fraud: Similar because both denote intentional deception, deceitful misrepresentation, or an imposter; differs because fraud is a formal, serious legal and criminal concept involving unlawful theft, forgery, or damaging financial swindles, whereas humbug emphasizes theatrical showmanship, social hypocrisy, or harmless boasting.
  • Hypocrisy: Similar because both involve pretending to possess virtues, principles, or feelings that one does not truly hold; differs because hypocrisy focuses strictly on the moral contradiction between a person's proclaimed ideals and their actual corrupt behavior, whereas humbug encompasses a wider range of hoaxes, nonsense, and entertaining bluffs.
  • Sham: Similar because both describe something false or counterfeit masquerading as genuine; differs because sham applies widely to inanimate objects, fake institutions, or staged trials designed to mimic the real thing, whereas humbug highlights the verbal rhetoric, personality, or social performance of the deceiver.
  • Charlatanism: Similar because both characterize the deceptive practices of a fake who boasts of non-existent abilities; differs because charlatanism carries a more formal, high-register connotation specifically linked to fraudulent medical healers, quacks, or pseudo-intellectuals selling fake knowledge, whereas humbug is a robust, popular, colloquial term.
  • Claptrap: Similar because both describe empty, pretentious rhetoric intended to impress listeners; differs because claptrap specifically denotes flashy theatrical language, cheap catchphrases, or political grandstanding calculated exclusively to win cheap applause, whereas humbug encompasses outright trickery, personal deceit, and general bunkum.
  • Bunkum: Similar because both characterize empty, insincere political talk and public nonsense; differs because bunkum (or bunk, derived from a hollow 1820 congressional speech for Buncombe County) focuses specifically on legislative grandstanding and verbose political drivel, whereas humbug has a rich theatrical and Victorian literary heritage.
