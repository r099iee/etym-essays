slates (noun, plural of slate; also 3rd person singular present of the verb to slate) — Thin, flat, rectangular or shaped tiles or slabs of fine-grained, foliated metamorphic rock (derived from shale or clay through low-grade regional metamorphism), celebrated for its natural planar cleavage that allows it to be split into exceptionally smooth, durable, and impermeable sheets. In traditional architecture and building construction—such as the enchanted castle in Oscar Wilde's fairy tale The Selfish Giant—slates refers specifically to natural stone roofing shingles affixed with copper or iron nails across timber roof battens, prized across centuries for their fire resistance, longevity, and distinctive natural blue-gray, purple, or green hues.



Primary architectural and roofing sense. The predominant material meaning denotes natural metamorphic roofing tiles quarried extensively in regions like North Wales (e.g., the famous slate quarries of Bethesda and Blaenau Ffestiniog), Cornwall (Delabole slate), Vermont, and Spain. When properly split, dressed, holed, and laid in overlapping courses, roofing slates provide centuries of impermeable protection against rain, snow, and ice. In severe weather events—such as the relentless three-hour pounding of the Hail in Wilde's The Selfish Giant—brittle stone slates can be cracked, loosened, or shattered, exposing the underlying timber rafters to water damage.



Secondary sense — writing tablets and chalkboards. Historically, slates denoted portable handheld writing tablets framed in wood, composed of smooth dark slate stone, used universally by schoolchildren, clerks, and scholars with a slate pencil or chalk before the advent of cheap mass-produced paper. This material practice gave rise to the enduring English idiom a clean slate (a fresh start with all past errors, debts, or offenses erased, translating Latin tabula rasa), and the phrase wiped the slate clean.



Tertiary sense — political ballots, rosters, and scheduling (noun and verb). In electoral politics and organizational governance, a slate designates a recognized list or roster of candidates presented by a political party or faction for election on a common platform (e.g., "the reform party put forward a progressive slate"). As a verb in the third person singular (slates), it means to schedule, designate, or plan an event or person for a future appointment (e.g., "the committee slates the conference for October").



Quaternary sense — severe theatrical and literary censure (British verb). In British colloquial and journalistic idiom, to say that a reviewer or audience slates a play, book, film, or politician means to subject the work to scathing, merciless criticism or harsh public condemnation (e.g., "the theater critic ruthlessly slates the new West End musical"). The historical semantic bridge stems from nineteenth-century schoolroom practice, where unruly pupils or debtors had their names publicly written upon the disciplinary blackboard/slate for punishment.



Technical and specialized domains:



  • Geology & Petrology: A foliated metamorphic rock formed by the alteration of shale or mudstone by low-grade regional metamorphism, characterized by microscopic phyllosilicate alignment producing excellent slaty cleavage.

  • Billiards & Gaming: Precision ground slabs of heavy slate stone used as the perfectly level, warp-free bed beneath the green felt cloth of tournament billiard, snooker, and pool tables.

  • Film & Television Production: The clapperboard (traditionally made of black slate) marked with scene, take, and roll numbers held before the camera to synchronize picture and sound.

  • Electrical Engineering: Historic switchboards and insulating panels crafted from non-conductive, fireproof slate slabs in early twentieth-century power stations.
