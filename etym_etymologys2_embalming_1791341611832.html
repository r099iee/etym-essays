Proto-Semitic (c. 3000 BCE)
*baśam-
"fragrant spice, aromatic resin"
↓
Phoenician / Hebrew (c. 1200 BCE)
bāśām (בָּשָׂם)
"balsam tree, sweet perfume, spice"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 5th c. BCE)
βάλσαμον (balsamon)
"fragrant resin of the balsam tree"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
balsamum
"balsam, aromatic gum, preservative resin"
↓
Late Latin / Vulgar Latin (c. 5th c.)
*inbalsamare
"to steep or preserve in aromatic balsam"
↓
Old French (c. 12th c.)
embaumer
"to anoint with balm, preserve from decay"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.)
embaumen / enbaumen
"to preserve a corpse with fragrant herbs"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th c.)
embalming
"the preservation of dead bodies from decay"

Note: The spelling with -l- was artificially re-introduced during the Renaissance in French and English under Latinizing scholarly influence (balsamum), although spoken Middle French and Middle English had vocalized Latin -l- to -u- (baume / baum). Suffix -ing descends from Old English verbal noun suffix -ung / -ing.
