• Athena: Similar because Athena is the ancient Greek goddess of wisdom, strategic war, and crafts with whom the Romans directly equated Minerva; differs because Athena possessed a fierce, prominent civic identity as the armed patron sovereign and protectress of Athens (Parthenos/Polias), whereas Minerva was historically rooted in Italic and Etruscan guild craftwork, domestic medicine, and mental ingenuity before assuming martial and civic roles in the Capitoline Triad.
  • Sophia: Similar because both represent the profound spiritual and philosophical embodiment of ultimate wisdom and divine insight; differs because Sophia is a Judeo-Christian and Hellenistic philosophical concept representing the personified, transcendent Holy Wisdom of God or divine emanation, whereas Minerva is a concrete mythic Roman polytheistic deity with armor, civic temples, festivals, and artisanal patronages.
  • Saraswati: Similar because both are venerated as the supreme goddess of wisdom, learning, music, arts, and intellectual speech in their respective Indo-European mythic traditions; differs because Saraswati (in Hinduism) is linked with river purity, melodic arts (playing the veena), and spiritual tranquility without any martial or military defensive attributes, whereas Minerva regularly wears armor, wields a spear, and strategizes defensive warfare.
  • Thoth: Similar because both are the patron deities of wisdom, writing, scribes, medicine, and intellectual measurements; differs because Thoth is an ancient Egyptian, ibis-headed lunar deity associated with magical spells, cosmic equilibrium (Ma'at), and the weighing of souls in the underworld, whereas Minerva is a Roman solar-civic goddess linked to statecraft, artisan guilds, and defensive strategy.
  • Juno: Similar because both Juno and Minerva are female members of Rome's supreme Capitoline Triad alongside Jupiter; differs because Juno governs marriage, childbirth, female sovereignty, and the protection of the state's communal purse, whereas Minerva specifically presides over individual intellect, technical craft skills, and military strategy.
  • Bellona: Similar because both Bellona and Minerva are prominent Roman war goddesses; differs because Bellona presides over the bloody madness, frantic bloodlust, and devastating fury of battle (allied with Mars), whereas Minerva disdains berserk frenzy, governing strictly disciplined tactical defense, foresight, and strategic wisdom.
