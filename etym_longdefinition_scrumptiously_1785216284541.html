Scrumptiously is the adverb of scrumptious, meaning "in a delicious, delightful, mouth-wateringly appetizing manner", and it typically functions as an intensifying degree-adverb in front of adjectives of approval — "scrumptiously crunchable", "scrumptiously good", "scrumptiously sweet". In The Hobbit, the famished Gollum asks of his imagined meal: "Is it nice, my preciousss? Is it juicy? Is it scrumptiously crunchable?" The adverb does more than assert that the hobbit would taste fine: it claims that the very texture of eating would be a pleasure, a rich sensory gratification built into the crunch. The irony is gorgeously pointed — a starving, wretched creature savours the idea of eating with the cosy vocabulary of the nursery and the sweet-shop. Scrumptiously is an informal, warm, almost childlike word — the language of treats and feasts — and its playful indulgence in Gollum's mouth turns the scene at once tender and menacing.

Core meaning. At its centre, scrumptiously means "in a way that is supremely pleasing, especially to the taste". It is nearly always positive, colloquial, and affectionate, and it intensifies whatever adjective it modifies:

  • Gustatory sense (primary). Of food and eating: "scrumptiously delicious", "scrumptiously crunchy", "a scrumptiously rich chocolate cake". Here it means "so as to be delicious; with delicious quality".
  • Extended sense (secondary). Of anything delightful or attractive: "scrumptiously pretty", "scrumptiously comfortable", "a scrumptiously cheerful room". The bridge from food to general delight is the common metaphor by which pleasure of any kind is described as a taste — a dish, a treat, a feast for the senses.
  • Intensifying / adverbial role. As a degree-adverb (compare "wonderfully", "terribly", "awfully"), scrumptiously boosts an adjective it precedes, carrying approval in its own right: "scrumptiously good" does not merely say "good" but "good in a way that is itself a delight".

Exhaustive polysemy, register, and semantic history.

  • Register: informal, jocular, nursery-familiar. Scrumptiously is never formal or technical. It belongs to the homely language of sweets, feasts, and pampered appetites — the word of a child at a pudding, a cook praising her own work, a reviewer being affectionately extravagant. Its informality is precisely what gives it its warmth.
  • The bridge from sumptuously. The adjective scrumptious (c. 1830–1850) is generally explained as a playful corruption of sumptuous ("costly, lavish, magnificent"). The semantic shift is the interesting part: where sumptuously stresses expense, display, and grandeur ("sumptuously furnished"), scrumptiously shrinks the idea to the mouth — costly grandeur becomes a mouthful of delight, high display becomes cosy indulgence. The nursery register then domesticates the grand Latin word: scrumptious is "sumptuous" scaled down to a sweet.
  • Related family. The cluster includes scrumptious, the clipped scrummy (childish approval), the Roald Dahl superlative scrumdiddlyumptious, and possibly the 19th-century slang scrum for food. The adverb scrumptiously itself (with the adverbial suffix -ly) is attested from the mid-19th century and remains rare, which makes each literary use distinctive.
  • Literary attestation and effect. Tolkien's 1937 use is the word's most famous moment. The adverb's rarity and its warm, pampered register make Gollum's question at once comic and disturbing: the starving creature speaks of eating with a child's greedy relish, and the nursery word becomes the vocabulary of a predator. The effect is that scrumptiously carries an ironic charge whenever it appears in a hungry or needy mouth.
  • Contrasts and near-synonyms. Scrumptiously differs from deliciously (neutral and literal), lusciously (rich, juicy, sensuous sweetness), sumptuously (costly display), appetizingly (appeal to hunger), mouth-wateringly (the cliché of salivation), and exquisitely (formal refinement): scrumptiously alone is the playful, homely word of delighted indulgence.

Summary. Scrumptiously means "in a supremely delicious, delightful way" — an informal, affectionate adverb of gustatory and extended pleasure, an intensifier of approval, and a word whose odd history (a nursery corruption of a grand Latin adjective) gives it an ironic, indulgent warmth that no synonym quite matches.
