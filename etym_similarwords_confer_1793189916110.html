• Consult: Similar because both name the seeking and exchanging of counsel before deciding; differs because consult is one-directional (you consult an expert), while confer is mutual — parties bring views together as equals.
  • Deliberate: Similar because both describe careful joint weighing of a question; differs because deliberate stresses slow weighing (often of a jury), while confer stresses the meeting and exchanging itself.
  • Parley: Similar because both name conference between parties, often adversaries; differs because a parley is specifically a truce-talk with an enemy, while confer covers all joint counsel, friendly or hostile.
  • Bestow: Similar because both name the formal giving of honours and favours; differs because bestow is general ceremonial giving, while confer is institutional — degrees, titles, and rights conferred by authority.
  • Grant: Similar because both transfer benefits, rights, and favours to recipients; differs because grant is legal-administrative (licenses, petitions), while confer adds ceremony — honours laid on with witnesses.
  • Award: Similar because both attach honours and prizes to merit; differs because an award follows judgment or victory (cups, damages), while confer follows investiture — status solemnly laid upon a person.
  • Compare: Similar because Latin conferre itself meant to bring things together for comparison (hence cf.); differs because compare is the everyday matching of likenesses, while confer keeps the comparing-sense only in scholarship.
  • Converse: Similar because both involve talking together and exchanging views; differs because converse is general talk, while confer is talk aimed at a decision or judgment.
  • Accord: Similar because both can name the granting of status or rights (to accord a privilege); differs because accord stresses harmony and agreement, while confer stresses the solemn act of giving or counselling.
