lever

lever / levée

↓
Modern English (current)
levees
"morning receptions; river embankments"

Note: The word entered English twice from French: first in the 1670s as a noun for the monarchical morning reception (from French masculine infinitive le lever or feminine noun la levée, the act of rising); second in the early 1700s via French colonial Louisiana (from feminine past participle levée, "something raised up", used for artificial earthen dikes along the Mississippi). Both senses unite in Latin levāre ("to raise").
