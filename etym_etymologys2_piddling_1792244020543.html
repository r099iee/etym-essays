Proto-Germanic (Expressive / Diminutive Base) (uncertain date)
*pid- / *pud-
"small, trifling, to poke lightly"
[Compare Low German peddeln, 'to potter about']
↓
Early Modern English Frequentative Verb (c. 1520s)
piddle
"to pick at food delicately; to toy with trifles"
[Derived with frequentative suffix -le]
↓
Early Modern English Present Participle (c. 1570s)
piddling
"trifling, petty, eating squeamishly"
↓
18th c. Colloquial / Satirical Development (c. 1720s)
piddling
"paltry, insignificant in amount; dawdling"
↓
Modern English (present)
piddling
"trifling, paltry; fussing at negligible margins"
