=== ROOT WORD 1: SPIT "roasting rod" === Stage 1 — PIE *spey- "sharp point, pointed stick, spike" (reconstructed; c. 4000–3500 BCE; the root of Latin spīca "ear of grain, point" and spīna "thorn, spine", hence English spine, spica, spire; the exact shape of the reconstruction is debated — see note) Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *spituz "spit, roasting rod, skewer" (reconstructed; cf. Old High German spiz "point, spit", Middle Low German spit, Middle Dutch spit "spit, skewer") Stage 3 — Old English spitu "spit, roasting rod, pointed rod" (attested; the noun continues into Middle English) Stage 4 — Middle English spite, spete, spitte "spit, roasting rod" (12th–15th c.) Stage 5 — Early Modern English spit (16th c.) Stage 6 — Modern English spit + plural -s → spits "roasting rods" (the sense in the text) => spits (modern English plural noun) === ROOT WORD 2: SPIT "to eject saliva" (homonym — separate, unrelated word) === Stage 1 — PIE *spyēu- "to spit, eject saliva, vomit" (sound-symbolic / imitative root; c. 4000–3500 BCE; cf. Latin spuere "to spit", Greek ptýō "I spit", Sanskrit ṣṭhīvati, Lithuanian spjáuti) Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *spīwaną "to spit, vomit" and *spītaną "to spit" (reconstructed; cf. Old High German spīwan, German speien "to spit, vomit") Stage 3 — Old English spīwan "to spit, vomit" and spǣtan "to spit" (attested 8th–11th c.) Stage 4 — Middle English spiten "to spit" (12th–15th c.) Stage 5 — Early Modern English spit "to eject saliva" (16th c.) The roasting-rod noun and the saliva verb are unrelated; their identity in modern English is pure homophony, dating back to their independent Old English and Germanic ancestors. DEBATE / NUANCE: The PIE *spey- root behind the roasting rod is standard in the handbooks (connecting the Germanic *spitu- with Latin spīca and spīna), but the exact shape of the PIE reconstruction is debated, and the OED treats the Germanic *spitu- as of uncertain further origin; an honest chain marks the PIE stage as probable rather than certain. The saliva verb is secure in its PIE *spyēu- root — an imitative, onomatopoeic root with clear cognates across the family. The plural spits is a regular English inflection.
