• obstacles: Similar because both describe barriers that stand in the way of achieving an objective; differs because an obstacle is typically a discrete, external, often physical object or challenge that must be climbed over, bypassed, or destroyed (from Latin obstare "to stand against"), whereas an impediment emphasizes slowing, encumbering, or entangling foot-like progress.
  • hindrances: Similar because both denote factors that delay or obstruct activity; differs because a hindrance is a Germanic term often referring to minor, nagging, or temporary inconveniences that drag from behind (hinder), whereas impediments often denote systemic, intrinsic, or insurmountable structural barriers.
  • obstructions: Similar because both denote things that block progress; differs because an obstruction specifically implies a complete physical blockage of a passage, channel, or roadway (e.g., an arterial obstruction or blocked pipe), preventing flow altogether, whereas an impediment may simply retard or encumber movement without absolute occlusion.
  • barriers: Similar because both prevent advance; differs because a barrier is an overt boundary, wall, or fortification deliberately erected to keep people out or enforce separation, whereas impediments can be unintended side-effects, organic speech defects, or subtle legal entanglements.
  • encumbrances: Similar because both weigh down or retard an agent; differs because an encumbrance is primarily a burdensome load, financial lien, or mortgage attached to property that weighs upon ownership, focusing on weight and liability rather than procedural blockage.
  • hurdles: Similar because both represent difficulties to be overcome; differs because a hurdle is a specific, discrete jump or test in a sequence (borrowed from track and equestrian sports) meant to be leapt over, whereas impediments entangle and slow the traveler from the ground up.
  • deterrents: Similar because both inhibit action; differs because a deterrent operates psychologically through fear of consequences (discouraging someone from attempting an action in the first place), whereas an impediment is an actual structural or physical obstacle encountered during execution.
  • shackles: Similar because both trace to restraining the feet or limbs; differs because shackles are explicit iron fetters or instruments of physical captivity representing overt subjugation, whereas impediments is generalized to any form of resistance, delay, or flaw.
