- c. 1300–1400: First recorded in Middle English as *dappel* / *dapled*, primarily describing horses with coats marked with round, mottled spots (*dapple-gray*).
- c. 1400–1600: In Early Modern English, extended from horses to deer (fallow deer), cattle, and bird plumage, signifying any animal coat patterned with soft, rounded patches.
- c. 1600–1800: Shifted into poetic and descriptive landscape literature to depict shifting optical effects, particularly the interplay of sunlight and foliage casting mottled shadows on the ground.
- c. 1877: Immortalized in English Victorian aesthetics by Gerard Manley Hopkins's masterpiece *Pied Beauty* ("Glory be to God for dappled things..."), transforming the word into a cultural emblem of organic texture and multifaceted natural beauty.
- c. 1900–Present: Widely used across visual arts, photography, equestrian breeding, ecology, and evocative literary prose to describe mottled lighting, variegated patterns, and multi-toned animal coats.
