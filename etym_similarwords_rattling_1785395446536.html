• Clatter: Similar because it denotes rapid repeated hard sounds, exactly rattle's sound-field; differs because clatter is lighter and more hollow, the sound of loose objects knocking together in disorder, while rattle is tighter, more mechanical, and more evenly spaced.
  • Clank: Similar because both are percussive noises of hard things; differs because a clank is a single duller, heavier sound of metal on metal, whereas rattle is a rapid, lighter, unbroken series.
  • Clang: Similar because both belong to the striking-metal sound family; differs because a clang is one loud, resonant, prolonged ring, not a quick staccato succession.
  • Clack: Similar because of the hard, dry, percussive quality shared with rattle; differs because a clack is a single short sharp sound, or the loose chattering of a valve, not a sustained rapid series.
  • Click: Similar because both are short sharp sounds; differs because a click is a single light sound, the smallest member of the family, while a rattle is a rapid chain of many sounds.
  • Rustle: Similar because both name sounds made by things in motion; differs because rustle is soft, dry, and swishing (leaves, paper, silk), entirely lacking the hardness and sharpness of a rattle.
  • Shake: Similar because rattling often involves shaking; differs because shake names the motion itself without committing to the sound, whereas rattle names the sound and, by extension, the motion that produces it.
  • Jangle: Similar because both name discordant repeated sounds; differs because jangle is reserved for harsh, clashing, metallic noises (keys, bells), while rattle covers any hard loose repetition, from windows to engines to hail.
  • Din: Similar because both denote a loud, sustained noise; differs because din is an abstract, general word for loudness, while rattle is a concrete, imitative name for a specific rapid percussive sound.
