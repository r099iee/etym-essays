• Distinguished: Similar because both describe someone set apart as notable or eminent; differs because distinguished denotes earned renown of any kind, while distingué, the French borrowing, names a specific air of high-society, continental polish.
  • Elegant: Similar because both describe tasteful, refined beauty; differs because elegant is a broad term for graceful beauty in movement, design, or manner, while distingué insists on elite social exclusivity and worldly sophistication.
  • Refined: Similar because both describe polish and cultivated good taste; differs because refined suggests a process of removing coarseness, while distingué stresses being socially marked off from the common crowd by birth and breeding.
  • Dignified: Similar because both describe composed, stately bearing; differs because dignified emphasizes calm gravity and worthiness of respect, while distingué emphasizes an aura of aristocratic refinement and social standing.
  • Polished: Similar because both describe smooth, finished, cultivated manner; differs because polished stresses flawless social technique and manners, while distingué stresses the visible stamp of inherited breeding.
  • Cultivated: Similar because both describe refined education and taste; differs because cultivated points to deliberate self-improvement through learning and the arts, while distingué points to a conferred, almost effortless social elevation.
  • Aristocratic: Similar because both suggest the highest social rank; differs because aristocratic refers to noble birth or class itself, while distingué describes the observable refined bearing such a background is presumed to confer.
  • Patrician: Similar because both suggest elite, privileged elegance; differs because patrician names the ancient Roman (or, by extension, old-family) nobility itself, while distingué is a quality of appearance that may be cultivated or borrowed.
  • Suave: Similar because both describe smooth, self-assured refinement; differs because suave implies polished, ingratiating charm and persuasiveness in social dealings, while distingué implies dignified, reserved distinction rather than charm.
  • Sophisticated: Similar because both describe worldly refinement; differs because sophisticated implies intellectual and cultural worldliness, sometimes with a hint of cynicism, while distingué implies stately, decorous elegance.
  • Courtly: Similar because both describe refined, ceremonious manners; differs because courtly suggests the manners of a royal court and formal courtesy, while distingué is a modern air of distinction independent of any court setting.
  • Graceful: Similar because both can describe an attractive, refined bearing; differs because graceful emphasizes ease and beauty of movement, while distingué emphasizes the social stamp of superior breeding and standing.
