Hanging is the present participle of the verb hang, and as an adjective it means “suspended from above, overhanging, or pending.” In the source sentence, “This had a shingly shore under hanging banks,” it describes banks that overhang the water — steep slopes whose brows jut out above the shore so that the beach runs beneath them. That overhang sense is one of several distinct lives the word leads today.

1. Physical sense — suspended or overhanging. A hanging object is attached at its upper point and left to dangle: a hanging lamp, a hanging basket, a hanging rope. Applied to landforms, hanging means “jutting out over what lies below”: hanging cliffs, hanging rocks, hanging banks; a “hanging valley” (geology) is a tributary valley whose floor stands well above the main valley floor, often with a waterfall pouring out of its mouth; a “hanging glacier” clings to a near-vertical mountainside. The “hanging gardens” of Babylon — from Greek kremastos, “suspended” — were terraced gardens that seemed to float above the city, the most famous hanging structure of antiquity.

2. Judicial sense — execution by the noose. Hanging denotes the method of execution by strangulation or neck-break from a gallows: “death by hanging,” “a hanging offence” (a crime for which one may be hanged), “a hanging judge” (a judge notorious for passing death sentences). The noun a hanging is the execution itself — historically in England, “hanging, drawing and quartering” for treason. This sense is grimly factual in law and news, and its weight colours every other use of the word.

3. Figurative sense — undecided, in suspense. A “hanging question,” “hanging matter,” or “hanging issue” is unsettled — left suspended in the air; “to hang fire,” of a slow-burning gun or a hesitant person, yields the adjectival “hanging fire” (delayed, hesitating). A decision “hanging by a thread” is precarious, one twist away from collapse; a promise “left hanging” is unfulfilled. The image everywhere is of something held up with nothing beneath it — unresolved, vulnerable, awaiting release.

4. The grammatical rogue — hanging participle. In grammar, a “hanging participle” (more usually called a dangling modifier) is a participle whose implied subject does not match the sentence subject (“Walking home, the rain began”), a construction schoolteachers flag. The word here is purely technical: it names a syntactic element left hanging without a proper anchor.

5. Decorative sense — hangings. The plural noun hangings means wall coverings — tapestries, curtains, draperies: “the walls were hung with rich hangings.” The Bayeux Tapestry is in fact a hanging: an embroidered wall textile. The singular is rare except in compounds such as wall-hanging; the body that decides which pictures are shown in an exhibition is still called the “hanging committee.”

6. Other technical uses.

  • Mining and geology: the “hanging wall” is the rock mass above a fault plane or ore body (opposite: footwall).
  • Typography: a “hanging indent” is a paragraph whose first line stands proud while the following lines are indented; “hanging punctuation” overhangs the margin for optical alignment.
  • Butchery: a “hanging carcass” is meat suspended to age; the “hanging weight” is its weight at slaughter.
  • Electoral politics: the “hanging chad” of the 2000 U.S. presidential recount — a perforated ballot punch-out left dangling — gave the word a starring, disputed role in world headlines.
  • Horticulture: hanging baskets and hanging garden displays in everyday gardening.

7. Grammar notes. As a participle it builds progressive tenses (is hanging, was hanging); as an adjective it is attributive or predicative (a hanging lamp; the lamp is hanging); as a gerund it names the activity (the hanging of the washing; the hanging of an inmate). The form is fully regular from hang; note that hanged, not hung, is preferred for executions, though hung is standard for things — a split eighteenth-century grammarians codified.

8. The semantic bridge. One physical fact — attachment from above with nothing supporting from below — generates the whole family: landforms that overhang, people suspended on a rope, matters suspended unsettled, arguments left dangling without support, textiles suspended on walls. Hanging is, in every sense, the state of being held up and let down at once: the cliff brow held up by its own weight, the sentence held up by the neck, the question held up by nothing at all.
