Note: As a composite personal name, Russel Wallace unites two ancient ethno-linguistic traditions of the British Isles. Russel derives from Anglo-Norman French Roussel (a diminutive of Latin russus "red", originally a descriptive nickname for red-haired or ruddy individuals). Wallace derives from Anglo-Norman waleis ("Welshman, Briton"), ultimately stemming from Proto-Germanic *walhaz ("foreigner, Celt"), the same Germanic root that generated Wales, Cornwall, and Walloon. In medieval Scotland, it designated Britons of the Kingdom of Strathclyde, made legendary by Sir William Wallace.
