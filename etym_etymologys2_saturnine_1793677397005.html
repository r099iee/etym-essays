Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*seh₁-
"to sow, scatter seed"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*sa-tornos
"god of sowing, agriculture"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd–1st c. BCE)
Sāturnus
"Saturn, god of agriculture; the planet"
↓
Late / Astrological Latin (c. 4th–12th c.)
sāturnīnus
"of Saturn; gloomy, born under Saturn"
↓
Old / Middle French (c. 14th c.)
saturnin
"gloomy, taciturn, cold-natured"
↓
Middle English (c. late 14th c.)
saturnine
"born under Saturn; somber, gloomy"
↓
Modern English (present)
saturnine
"gloomy, morose, sullen; sluggish"

Note on etymological debate: Roman antiquarians (Varro) and modern Indo-Europeanists derive Sāturnus from the PIE verbal root *seh₁- ("to sow", via participle satus). However, an alternative linguistic hypothesis suggests borrowing from an Etruscan deity Satre or Satres; both traditions converge on an ancient agricultural protector who was subsequently identified with the Greek Titan Cronus.
