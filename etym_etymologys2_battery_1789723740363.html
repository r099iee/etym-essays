Note: Latin battuere is rare in literary Latin but common in Vulgar Latin and is said to be probably borrowed from Gaulish, compare Welsh bathu beat and Irish bata staff, per Etymonline and de Vaan. Pokorny proposes PIE *bhau- to strike as deeper source, also giving Old English beatan to beat. The Gaulish and PIE stages are provisional; the secure chain runs from Latin battuere through French batterie to English battery (1530s action of battering, 1550s artillery, 1748 electrical via Franklin).
