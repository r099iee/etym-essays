• Impudence: Similar because both denote shameless, brazen disrespect; differs because impudence highlights sheer nerve and lack of shame, while insolence stresses haughty contempt directed against authority.
  • Impertinence: Similar because both mean inappropriate disrespect; differs because impertinence is milder and often means intrusive or meddlesome behaviour by someone out of their sphere, while insolence is bolder and more openly defiant.
  • Audacity: Similar because both involve boldness that breaks expected bounds; differs because audacity can be admired as daring or courageous, whereas insolence is always offensive and disrespectful.
  • Effrontery: Similar because both describe unblushing rudeness; differs because effrontery emphasises the sheer shamelessness of making an unreasonable demand, while insolence emphasises contemptuous defiance toward a superior.
  • Cheek: Similar because both denote rude, forward behaviour; differs because cheek is informal, light, and often mildly comic (British register), while insolence is formal, serious, and carries a charge of insubordination.
  • Rudeness: Similar because both denote discourteous behaviour; differs because rudeness is the general term for bad manners and can be unintentional, while insolence is deliberate, contemptuous, and usually directed at authority.
  • Disrespect: Similar because both involve a failure to show proper regard; differs because disrespect is a broader attitude and can be silent, whereas insolence is active, voiced, and provocative.
  • Arrogance: Similar because both involve inflated self-importance; differs because arrogance is an internal attitude of superiority that need not be expressed, while insolence is the outward, aggressive expression of that attitude toward others.
  • Haughtiness: Similar because both connote proud condescension; differs because haughtiness is cold, distant disdain from above, while insolence is active, impudent provocation from below.
  • Presumption: Similar because both involve overstepping one's proper bounds; differs because presumption is acting as if entitled beyond one's station, while insolence is the contemptuous, often verbal defiance that goes with it.
  • Contumely: Similar because both denote insulting contempt; differs because contumely is a rare, archaic word for humiliating scorn or abuse, while insolence is the everyday term for defiant disrespect.
  • Hubris: Similar because both involve overweening pride; differs because hubris is specifically the tragic, self-destructive arrogance that defies the gods or fate in classical literature, while insolence is ordinary (if grave) social defiance.
