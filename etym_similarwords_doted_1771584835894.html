• Adored: Similar in meaning loved deeply; differs because adored retains a note of reverent worship and distance, whereas doting is familiar, sheltering, and physically indulgent.
  • Loved: Similar in meaning had deep affection for; differs because loved is the neutral, encompassing term with no sense of excess, whereas doted implies love that has crossed a boundary into unreasonableness.
  • Indulged: Similar in meaning treated with excessive leniency; differs because indulged describes the behaviour of gratifying a person's wishes, while doted names the emotion of uncritical fondness from which the indulgence flows.
  • Pampered: Similar in meaning treated with excessive care; differs because pampered focuses on luxurious, overprotective treatment, whereas doted focuses on the fierce, uncritical love itself.
  • Spoiled: Similar in meaning the child was overindulged; differs because spoiled describes the damaging effect on the child, not the emotion of the adult, while doted is the adult's uncritical love.
  • Worshipped: Similar in meaning regarded with extreme devotion; differs because worshipped brings religious awe and solemnity, far too reverent for the familiar, coddling register of doting.
  • Idolized: Similar in meaning regarded with excessive admiration; differs because idolized implies public admiration and heroic status, whereas doting is private, domestic, and sheltering.
  • Cherished: Similar in meaning held dear and protected; differs because cherished is tender, measured, and entirely positive, with no hint of the folly or loss of judgment that doted always carries.
  • Coddled: Similar in meaning treated overprotectively; differs because coddled emphasizes the pampering treatment given to the child, while doted names the unbounded affection of the adult.
  • Made much of: Similar in meaning lavished with attention; differs because made much of is a modern colloquialism for being fussed over, while doted retains its archaic flavour of love tipping into folly.
