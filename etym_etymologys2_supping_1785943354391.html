PIE (c. 4000–3000 BCE)
*seu-
"juice, moisture; to take liquid"

↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*sūpaną
"to sip, drink in small amounts"

Native path: to sip

↓
Old English (before 900)
sūpan
"to sip, drink"

↓
English (14th c.)
sup
"to take liquid by spoonfuls, sip"

Path via French: the evening meal

↓
Frankish (reconstructed)
*soppa
"bread soaked in broth, sop"

↓
Old French (12th c.)
soupe
"broth, soup"

↓
Old French (12th c.)
souper
"to take the evening meal"

↓
Middle English (14th c.)
soupen
"to eat supper"

↓
English (15th c.)
sup
"to eat dinner, take supper"

↓
English (present participle)
supping
"eating dinner; taking liquid by spoonfuls"

Note: English has two homophonous verbs sup: the native one "to sip" (Old English sūpan) and the French-derived one "to eat supper" (Old French souper, from Frankish). Both ultimately go back to the same Germanic stock of words for taking in liquid, so the chains above share one PIE root.
