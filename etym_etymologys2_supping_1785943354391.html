DERIVATION CHAIN (a) — SUP (v.1 "to sip"), one of the two verbal bases of SUPPING: PIE *seup- / *sūp- "to drink, sip, suck" (reconstructed root; cognate with Old Norse súpa "to sip", Old High German sūfan "to drink, swill", Middle Dutch supen, and the base of English sip, sop, soup, suck) [c. 4000–2500 BCE] → Proto-Germanic *sūpaną "to drink, sip" (reconstructed strong verb of class II; stem *sūp-/*sup-) [c. 500 BCE – 200 CE] → Old English sūpan "to drink, swallow, sip, taste" (attested strong verb) [attested 8th–11th c.] → Middle English suppen / soupen "to sip, take liquid in small draughts" [attested 12th–15th c.] → Early Modern English sup "to take (liquid, soup, broth) into the mouth in small quantities" [16th c.] → Modern English sup → present participle, spelled with doubled -p: sup + -ing → SUPPING DERIVATION CHAIN (b) — SUP (v.2 "to take supper"), the second verbal base of SUPPING: PIE *seup- "to drink, sip" (the same root as chain a) [c. 4000–2500 BCE] → Proto-Germanic *supō(n)- "sopped bread, liquid food" (reconstructed noun; the form behind the borrowed Latin word) [c. 500 BCE – 200 CE] → Late Latin suppa "bread soaked in liquid, sop, broth" (borrowed from Germanic) [c. 4th–6th c.] → Old French soupe "soup, sop" [attested 9th–10th c.] → Old French souper / soper "the evening meal" (substantive use of the infinitive souper "to have one's evening meal, to sup") [attested 11th c.] → Middle English souper / supper "the evening meal" (also soper) [attested 13th c.] → English noun supper "evening meal" → English verb sup "to eat the evening meal" (probably a back-formation from the noun supper, converging in form with sup v.1 "to sip") [attested 16th c.] → Modern English sup → present participle → SUPPING NOTE ON THE DEBATE / NUANCE: The two verbs sup ("to sip") and sup ("to take supper") are historically distinct in origin but have converged completely in form — a documented lexical merger. Some authorities derive the "take supper" sense directly from the Old French verb souper, others treat it as an English back-formation from the noun supper; either way it shares the same ultimate root *seup- with the "sip" verb. The precise route of the merger is not documented stage-by-stage, and both explanations are considered defensible.
