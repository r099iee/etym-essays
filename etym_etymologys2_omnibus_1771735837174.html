PIE *h₃ep- "to work, to produce, to be abundant" (traditional etymology of Latin ops and omnis; de Vaan cautions the link *op-ni- → omnis is not fully secure) ├── Italic: Latin ops, opis "power, resources, wealth, abundance" │ ├── Latin opus, operis "work" → English opus, operation, cooperate (sister) │ ├── Latin opulentus "wealthy, rich" → English opulent, opulence (sister) │ ├── Latin cōpia "abundance, plenty" (con- + ops) → English copious, copy (sister) │ └── Latin omnis "all, every" (traditionally < *op-ni-, literally "that is in full supply, complete") — DEBATED link │ └── Latin omnibus (dative/ablative plural of omnis) "for all, to all" (the third-declension plural ending -ibus) │ ├── French (voiture) omnibus "a public carriage for all" (1820s Paris, associated with Stanislas Baudry) │ ├── English omnibus "the public horse-drawn carriage" (1829) │ │ ├── English bus (by 1832, slang clipping of omnibus) → the modern motor vehicle, bus stop, autobus │ │ └── English omnibus "a volume containing several works previously published separately" (early 20th c., book trade) — the sense in this card │ └── English omnibus "a single volume gathering several titles published separately" (Modern Library edition) — OUR CARD NOTE (debate): the derivation of omnis from *h₃ep- via *op-ni- is the traditional textbook account, but de Vaan cautions that the link is not secure ("no reliable etymology"); the descent of omnibus itself (the dative/ablative plural of omnis) is not in doubt. The English word arrived first as the name of the 1829 public carriage and only later acquired the book-trade sense.
