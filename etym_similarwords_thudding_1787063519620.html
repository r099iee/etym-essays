• Pounding: Similar because both denote heavy, forceful, rhythmic blows or somatic sensations; differs because pounding emphasizes repeated, punishing kinetic strikes and hammer-like destruction, whereas thudding highlights the dull, muffled, low-frequency sound of impact.
  • Thumping: Similar because both convey dull, heavy blows; differs because thumping has a slightly higher acoustic pitch and more hollow resonance (like hitting a hollow drum or wooden box), while thudding implies greater dead weight and less rebound.
  • Banging: Similar because both involve noisy collisions; differs because banging produces a sharp, loud, sudden, and jarring noise with high-frequency reverberation, unlike the cushioned, deep acoustic profile of thudding.
  • Crashing: Similar because both describe forceful physical impacts; differs because crashing conveys shattering, violent destruction, and fragmented noise (breaking wood, glass, or metal), whereas thudding denotes impact without structural shattering.
  • Clattering: Similar because both describe objects striking together in motion; differs because clattering involves a succession of rapid, sharp, light, or hard rattling noises (such as falling dishes or armor), the antithesis of the heavy, deadened sound of thudding.
  • Smacking: Similar because both describe impacts of moving bodies; differs because smacking produces a sharp, crisp, flat report upon striking a surface (especially liquid or smooth solids), whereas thudding produces a deep, muffled resonance.
  • Bumping: Similar because both involve blunt physical contact; differs because bumping is milder, less sonorous, and often accidental or gentle, lacking the substantial mass and visceral shockwave of thudding.
  • Throbbing: Similar because both describe rhythmic internal pulsations; differs because throbbing focuses on alternating waves of pressure, vascular ache, or warmth, whereas thudding emphasizes the discrete, heavy acoustic beat of each individual pulse.
  • Drumming: Similar because both involve rapid, continuous percussion; differs because drumming implies a distinct, patterned, musical, or resonant staccato cadence.
  • Rumbling: Similar because both represent low-frequency acoustic vibrations; differs because rumbling is a continuous, rolling, prolonged sound (like thunder or earthquakes), while thudding is percussive, discrete, and shock-based.
