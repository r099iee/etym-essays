• smudge: Similar because both refer to an unsightly, blurred mark made on a surface; differs because a smudge is typically caused by friction, rubbing, or dirty fingers smearing a substance, whereas an inkblot is specifically created by liquid ink dropped or spilled in a concentrated pool.
  • stain: Similar because both describe a discoloration of a material; differs because a stain implies liquid absorption and discoloration that is difficult to wash out of fabrics or surfaces, while an inkblot describes the distinct, localized pooled shape of ink.
  • splatter: Similar because both denote an irregular liquid mark; differs because a splatter consists of multiple scattered droplets flying outward under force or impact, whereas an inkblot is a heavy, concentrated drop or puddle on paper.
  • blemish: Similar because an inkblot historically represented an imperfection; differs because a blemish is an abstract or physical flaw that impairs perfection or skin beauty, while an inkblot is an explicit mark made by the writing medium itself.
  • splotch: Similar because both describe an irregular, amorphous patch of liquid or color; differs because splotch is an informal, generic term for any uneven patch of paint, mud, or color, whereas inkblot is specifically bound to writing ink and psychological testing.
  • blot: Similar because blot is the base simplex of the compound; differs because blot can refer to any stain, moral fault, or blemish (as in "a blot on one's reputation"), whereas inkblot specifically identifies the liquid substance and is universally linked with psychological testing and paper-folding.
  • daub: Similar because both describe applying a patch of liquid pigment; differs because a daub implies a clumsy, deliberate stroke or smear with a brush or finger, whereas an inkblot typically arises from dripping, pooling, or paper folding.
  • cipher: Similar because both can figuratively represent something whose meaning must be deciphered; differs because a cipher is a deliberate code, encrypted message, or mathematical zero, while an inkblot has no encoded secret message, only whatever meaning the observer projects upon it.
