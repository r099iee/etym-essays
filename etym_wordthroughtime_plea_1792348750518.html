- [Late Latin & Carolingian Era (c. 6th–9th c.)] — placitum referred to an imperial or royal decree ('that which pleased the sovereign'), and by extension the public judicial assembly or court held under royal authority to resolve disputes
- [Anglo-Norman & Early Middle English (c. 12th–13th c.)] — imported into England as ple / plee, denoting a formal common law action, dispute, or lawsuit argued before royal magistrates (e.g., the Court of Common Pleas)
- [Late Medieval Common Law Era (c. 14th–15th c.)] — specialized technically into the formal written or oral answer entered by a defendant in response to a plaintiff's declaration or criminal indictment
- [Renaissance & Early Modern Literary Era (c. 16th–18th c.)] — broadened into general literature and political discourse to signify an excuse, pretext, or moral justification (Milton's 'necessity, the tyrant's plea')
- [Modern Forensic & Humanitarian Era (19th–21st c.)] — bifurcated into the worldwide procedural mechanism of criminal plea bargaining on the one hand, and universal humanitarian rhetoric for urgent appeals for mercy, peace, and clemency on the other
