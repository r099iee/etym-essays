• withered: Similar because both mean dried up and losing vitality, of plants or people; differs because withered emphasises the loss of life and vigor through age or neglect, while shrivelled emphasises the physical contraction and wrinkling through moisture loss.
  • wrinkled: Similar because both describe creased, folded surfaces; differs because wrinkled refers simply to the creases (as in clothes or old skin) and is neutral, while shrivelled adds the sense of shrinkage, dryness, and often decay.
  • dried up: Similar because both mean emptied of moisture; differs because dried up is casual and literal (a dried-up pond), while shrivelled is more graphic and implies the contracting, crumpling result of drying.
  • shrunken: Similar because both mean reduced in size by drawing together; differs because shrunken stresses the loss of size alone (a shrunken head, shrunken wool), while shrivelled adds wrinkles, dryness, and usually a negative, decaying tone.
  • wizened: Similar because both describe small, wrinkled, dried appearances; differs because wizened is almost exclusively used of people (a wizened old face) and suggests age, while shrivelled applies to plants, fruit, and objects as well.
  • parched: Similar because both result from extreme dryness; differs because parched describes the state of being desperately dry (parched lips, parched earth) without the contracted, wrinkled shape that shrivelled implies.
  • desiccated: Similar because both mean thoroughly dried out; differs because desiccated is the technical, neutral term for extreme dehydration, while shrivelled is the vivid, everyday word with emotional and Gothic overtones.
  • wilted: Similar because both describe plants losing moisture; differs because wilted means drooping and limp, often recoverable, while shrivelled means contracted, creased, and dry, usually past recovery.
  • seared: Similar because both can result from intense heat; differs because seared means scorched or burnt on the surface (seared flesh), while shrivelled means contracted and wrinkled by heat or dryness.
  • mummified: Similar because both describe extremely dried, preserved tissue; differs because mummified is the specific term for deliberate or natural embalming, while shrivelled is the general word for shrunk and dried matter.
