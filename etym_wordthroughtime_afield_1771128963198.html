- "In or on the field" — Originally a literal spatial sense: out in the open countryside, the battlefield, or the hunting ground, set against the safety of the hearth and home.
- "Away from home, to a distance" — The sense widened to mean departure from one's accustomed base; the idiom 'far afield' came to stress great, sometimes excessive distance, both physical and figurative.
- Contemporary Sense — Survives mainly in 'far afield' and phrases like 'go afield'; now literary and slightly archaic, evoking a range far from home, one's subject, or the point of discussion.
