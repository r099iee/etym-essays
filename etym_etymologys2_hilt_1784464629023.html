Chain for root word hilt: Proto-Germanic *heltą / *heltō "sword-handle, hilt" (reconstructed; the remoter PIE layer is debated — see note) → Old English hilt / hilte "handle of a sword" (attested from the 8th–9th c. CE; plural hiltas/hilte could name one sword's handle-gear of guard, grip, and pommel) → Middle English hilt (12th–15th c. CE) → Modern English hilt (this card) Debated note: no single, universally accepted Proto-Indo-European reconstruction is agreed for *heltą. Some scholars associate the stem with a "hold, grasp" verbal idea (in the neighbourhood of English hold and its Germanic kin), but the cognate set is thin and the deeper layer is honestly labelled uncertain; rather than inventing distant Indo-European "twins," the chain begins at Proto-Germanic, where the word is securely attested across West and North Germanic (Old High German helza, Old Norse hjalt "hilt, guard-assembly").
