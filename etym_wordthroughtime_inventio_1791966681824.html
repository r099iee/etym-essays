- 1st c. BCE — inventio is fixed as the first canon of rhetoric in Latin handbooks; it means the finding of arguments, not the creation of novelties.
- Middle Ages — the canon survives in the school curriculum and in the artes dictaminis; inventio remains a technical Latin term of the trivium.
- 16th c. — English invention shifts from "discovery" toward "thing newly devised," and the word begins to be used of machines, devices and fabricated stories.
- 17th–18th c. — patent law and natural philosophy fix the modern sense of a new contrivance; rhetoric narrows toward style and delivery, and inventio loses its central place in education.
- 19th–20th c. — invention is the ordinary word for creativity and technological novelty; composition studies revives the classical canon under the English name.
- 21st c. — Inventio survives as a specialist term in rhetorical scholarship, while everyday English "invention" means creation, device or fabrication.
