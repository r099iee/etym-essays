PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3000 BCE)
*se- + *greǵ- / *ger-
"apart, by oneself + to assemble, flock"
[Base root *ger- 'to gather, assemble into a herd']
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*sē-gregāō
"to separate from the flock or herd"
[Formed from reflexive se- + stem greg- 'flock']
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
sēgregāre
"to separate from the flock, remove, divide"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th c. CE)
sēgregātiō (stem sēgregātiōn-)
"a separating, parting, isolation"
↓
Middle French (c. 14th c.)
ségrégation
"separation, parting, division"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550s)
segregation
"the act of separating or setting apart"
[Attested in religious, legal, and natural philosophy texts]
↓
Modern English (present)
segregation
"institutional racial/social separation or genetic/physical division"
