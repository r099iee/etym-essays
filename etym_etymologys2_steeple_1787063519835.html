Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*staupilaz
"tower, steep structure"
↓
Old English (before 9th c.)
stēap
"steep, lofty, towering"
↓
Old English (before 10th c.)
stēpel
"tower, church tower"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
stepel
"church steeple, tall pointed tower"
↓
Modern English (present)
STEEPLE
"tall pointed tower of a church"

Note: The word is native Germanic, a derivative of the adjective steep ("lofty, precipitous"), which goes back to Proto-Germanic *staup- and ultimately to a PIE root associated with "to stoop, sink" or "steeper rise" — the exact PIE etymon is debated, so it is safest to present the Germanic chain only. The related word stupa (Buddhist mound) is a different, Indic borrowing and unrelated.
