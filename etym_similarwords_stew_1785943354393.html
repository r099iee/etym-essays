• Soup: Similar because both are hot dishes of food simmered in liquid; differs because a soup is predominantly thin broth with smaller, submerged ingredients, while a stew has a lower liquid ratio and large, distinct chunks.
  • Braise: Similar because both are slow, moist-heat cooking methods for tenderizing meat; differs because braising cooks large, whole cuts partially submerged after searing, while stewing uses uniform bite-sized pieces fully submerged.
  • Casserole: Similar because both are slow-cooked dishes of meat and vegetables in liquid; differs because a casserole is oven-baked in a covered dish (often with a topping), while a stew is typically simmered on the stovetop in a pot.
  • Chowder: Similar because both are thick, hearty hot dishes of solid food and liquid; differs because chowder is a creamy soup (often seafood or corn, thickened with milk or cream), while stew relies on reduced broth without dairy.
  • Fricassee: Similar because both are dishes of meat cooked slowly in liquid; differs because a fricassee is a white stew of poultry or veal cooked without browning and finished in white sauce.
  • Pot-au-feu / Boiled dinner: Similar because both involve simmering meat in liquid; differs because these boil large pieces in abundant liquid and serve them with the broth separately, while a stew reduces the liquid into a concentrated gravy.
  • Goulash: Similar because both are slow-cooked meat-and-liquid dishes; differs because goulash is the specific Hungarian paparika-flavored beef stew, while stew is the general category.
  • Steam / Simmer (cooking verbs): Similar because both describe cooking by moist heat; differs because simmering is the gentle cooking action used within a stew, while stewing is the broader method of slow cooking in a covered pot with liquid.
  • Worry / Fret (figurative): Similar because both denote anxious, agitated brooding; differs because worry and fret are general emotional verbs, while stew adds the vivid image of slow internal simmering and prolonged helpless agitation.
  • Brood: Similar because both denote dwelling on something at length in a disturbed mood; differs because brooding is quiet, inward rumination, while stewing implies restless, flustered, boiling agitation.
