- 14th c. verb, echoic — a heavy heaving or beating, chiefly of the heart or the body under stress (fear, exertion, warfare).
- 16th c. — the noun “a throb” (c. 1570s) names a single beat of the pulse; the word enters medicine and love poetry.
- 19th c. — throbbing becomes a clinical descriptor of pain; throbbing engines and machinery appear with industrialisation.
- 20th c. — throbbing moves into music (jazz, rock, electronic bass) and romance (“heartthrob”, 1927); the city and the crowd “throb with life”.
- 21st c. — standard in medical, musical, and literary registers; throb is now the default word for a deep, felt rhythm in audio and vibration.
