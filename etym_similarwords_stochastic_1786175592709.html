• Probabilistic: Similar because both characterize systems governed by chance rather than absolute certainty; differs because probabilistic is a broader umbrella term for anything involving probability, whereas stochastic specifically denotes a process, sequence, or time-evolving variable governed by probability laws.
  • Aleatory: Similar because both describe phenomena dependent on chance or randomness; differs because aleatory carries strong philosophical, legal, and artistic connotations (e.g., aleatoric music, games of dice) emphasizing pure contingency.
  • Random: Similar because both describe lack of deterministic predictability; differs because random suggests haphazardness or equal probability across outcomes, while stochastic systems often exhibit well-defined statistical structures, bounds, and probability distributions over time.
  • Indeterminate: Similar because neither outcome can be fixed beforehand; differs because indeterminate implies vagueness or lack of exact limits/specification rather than quantifiable statistical behavior.
  • Contingent: Similar because the outcome depends on uncertain variables; differs because contingent stresses conditional dependency upon specific external occurrences rather than intrinsic probabilistic time evolution.
  • Heuristic: Similar because both trace back to Greek roots concerning problem-solving and conjectural discovery; differs because heuristic refers to practical rule-of-thumb search methods rather than mathematical probability distributions.
