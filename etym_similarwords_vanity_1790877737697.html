• Conceit: Similar because both refer to excessive self-pride; differs because conceit emphasizes an exaggerated, smug opinion of one's own abilities or intellect, whereas vanity is often intensely tied to external appearance, public admiration, and social display.
  • Narcissism: Similar because both involve self-absorption; differs because narcissism is a clinical and psychoanalytic concept denoting a deep personality pathology involving grandiosity, lack of empathy, and an obsessive need for external validation.
  • Arrogance: Similar because both convey inflated self-worth; differs because arrogance manifests as overbearing, haughty behavior that actively demeans or domineers over others, whereas vanity can be anxious, needy, and desperately hungry for applause.
  • Futility: Similar because both denote pointlessness or lack of lasting result; differs because futility focuses strictly on the practical impossibility or uselessness of an action, while vanity frames emptiness within a moral, spiritual, or existential dimension.
  • Egotism: Similar because both center on the self; differs because egotism is the habit of talking constantly about oneself and viewing the world exclusively through personal self-interest, whereas vanity is preoccupation with being admired by others.
  • Vainglory: Similar because it is an exact archaic synonym combining vanity and glory; differs because vainglory specifically emphasizes boastful, ostentatious swagger and pride in empty, fleeting triumphs.
