- Middle English (c. 1200–1400): Originated as *pichen*, meaning to thrust a pointed stake into the ground to fasten tents or erect camps; later developed the sense of throwing or casting.
- 16th century: Emerged in falconry to denote the supreme towering height reached by a hunting hawk before diving; rapidly adopted into Renaissance literature to mean the highest apex of human emotion, power, and ambition.
- 18th century: Codified in physics and music theory to describe the perceived highness or lowness of acoustic tones, as well as architectural slope measurements.
- 19th–20th centuries: Expanded into British sports fields (cricket/soccer pitch), nautical/aviation rotational axes, and commercial sales presentations (*sales pitch*), while retaining its high-register literary sense for acute emotional summits (*fever pitch*, *ruthless pitch*).
