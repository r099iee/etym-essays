- [18th century philosophy of action] - [Enters English from German 'pragmatisch' via Kantian thought; a pragmatic history treats facts as they bear on consequences — a technical term of philosophy]
- [19th century practical judgment] - [Popularized by the American pragmatists (Peirce, James): truth and value are judged by what works and by results]
- [20th century political realism] - [Becomes a political epithet of praise: prudent, consequence-minded, cool; Gibbon's politics are described as 'prudent and pragmatic']
- [Present everyday sense] - [Broadly means sensible and results-based rather than theoretical; still approving, though diluted from its philosophical precision]
