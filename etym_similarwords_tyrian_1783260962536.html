• Phoenician: Similar because both refer to the ancient Semitic seafaring and trading peoples of the Levant; differs because Phoenician is the broad regional and cultural designation for the whole people and civilization, while Tyrian specifically names Tyre and its distinctive purple trade.
  • Sidonian: Similar because both name a sister Phoenician city and its people; differs because Sidonian refers to Sidon, Tyre's rival port, whereas Tyrian points to Tyre — and to the dye that made Tyre famous above its neighbours.
  • Carthaginian: Similar because both describe Phoenician colonial expansion; differs because Carthaginian refers to Carthage in North Africa, Tyre's most famous colony, whereas Tyrian names the mother city.
  • Punic: Similar because both are classical adjectives for the western Phoenicians (especially Carthage); differs because Punic is the Latin-derived term for the Carthaginian branch and its language, while Tyrian names the eastern mother city and its dye.
  • Imperial (purple): Similar because both describe the regal purple of rulers; differs because imperial purple is a political and status designation for any royal purple, while Tyrian purple names the exact ancient historical origin and the murex extraction process.
  • Royal purple / Purple: Similar because both denote the deep reddish-purple hue; differs because these are generic colour terms, whereas Tyrian (purple) is the precise historical and geographical label for the dye of Tyre.
  • Murex / Purpuraria: Similar because both are associated with the dye's source; differs because murex names the shellfish itself and purpuraria the dye-works, whereas Tyrian names the finished colour and its city of production.
