• actors: Similar because both perform roles before an audience; differs because actors work from scripts on a formal stage with a clear performer-audience divide, while mummers are masked, largely silent performers of seasonal folk plays rooted in ritual and village custom.
  • performers: Similar because both name people who present entertainment; differs because performer is the broad, neutral modern term for anyone from a musician to a magician, while mummer specifically means a masked, disguised actor of a traditional festival play.
  • masqueraders: Similar because both are people in disguise at a celebration; differs because a masquerader is a ball-goer or reveller who wears a costume chiefly for social fun, while a mummer is a performer who presents a play or procession as part of a seasonal ritual.
  • maskers: Similar because both denote people wearing masks in festive contexts; differs because masker is a general term for anyone masked (as at a masked ball), whereas mummer implies the specific English folk tradition of disguised Christmas or midwinter performance.
  • guisers: Similar because guisers are the northern English and Scottish equivalent of mummers — masked, costumed performers who visit houses and act at Halloween, Christmas, and Plough Monday; differs mainly in regional name and in a stronger association with guising (going door to door) than with a staged play.
  • mimes: Similar because both perform with the body rather than speech; differs because a mime is a professional silent artist performing refined, controlled gesture on a stage, while a mummer is an amateur masked reveller whose "silence" is an accident of disguise, not an artistic method.
  • pantomimists: Similar because both use exaggerated, wordless gesture; differs because a pantomimist is a skilled performer of expressive bodily storytelling, whereas a mummer belongs to a communal seasonal tradition and is often masked and barely disciplined in technique.
  • buskers: Similar because both entertain in public street spaces; differs because buskers are modern, often professional street performers working for immediate financial tips, while mummers are traditional masked festival performers acting within a seasonal ritual, not for casual donations.
  • players: Similar because both are old-fashioned words for actors in popular entertainment; differs because player is a general historical term for any actor, and in modern English has shifted chiefly to sports and games, while mummer remained specific to masked folk performance.
  • harlequins: Similar because both are costumed, masked, comic performers; differs because a harlequin is a specific commedia dell'arte stock character in a diamond-patterned suit with defined comic mannerisms, while mummers are a broad folk tradition of disguised seasonal players without fixed characters.
  • impersonators: Similar because both disguise identity in order to perform; differs because an impersonator imitates a specific real or famous individual, while a mummer hides behind a generic mask to play a ritual or folk role and does not aim at likeness.
  • revellers: Similar because both take part in festive street celebrations; differs because a reveller is simply a person enjoying a party, not necessarily costumed or performing, whereas a mummer is a disguised performer presenting traditional drama within the festival.
  • morris dancers: Similar because both are English folk performers appearing at seasonal festivals in costume; differs because morris dancing is a specific choreographed dance tradition with bells, sticks, and handkerchiefs, not masked drama, and morris dancers are not disguised.
