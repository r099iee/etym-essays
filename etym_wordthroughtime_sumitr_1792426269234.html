- [Vedic & Pre-Epic Era (c. 1000–500 BCE)] - the linguistic elements su- and mitra- held profound religious significance in Vedic hymns, personifying divine covenants, cosmic truth, and benevolent companionship - [perception: revered as sacred terms of cosmic peace and divine friendship]
- [Classical Sanskrit Epic Era (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)] - established in Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa as the noble second queen of Ayodhyā, mother of Lakṣmaṇa and Śatrughna, immortalized by her benediction of detachment - [perception: honored as the supreme archetype of maternal wisdom and selfless duty]
- [Medieval Bhakti Period (c. 11th–16th c. CE)] - celebrated in regional vernacular epics (Kamban's Irāmāvatāram, Tulsīdās's Rāmacaritamānasa) where she is depicted as a spiritually illuminated jñāninī who dispels the grief of the royal court - [perception: venerated as a realized saint of devotion and stoic fortitude]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era] - re-evaluated in modern literary criticism and feminist indology as the most psychologically balanced and morally resilient woman in the Rāmāyaṇa - [perception: admired as a quiet pillar of unshakeable strength, wisdom, and egoless devotion]
