- 15th c. (Middle English): borrowed from Old French "acertainer" meaning "to make certain", establishing a fact — a formal, legal-administrative term.
- 17th–18th c.: becomes a hallmark of Enlightenment empiricism — to find out for certain through observation and inquiry; carries the prestige of rigorous fact-finding.
- 19th c.: in polite Regency prose it elevates ordinary fact-finding into a forensic act, often with satirical weight (Austen's ladies "ascertaining" a gentleman's coat and horse).
- Contemporary: "ascertain" feels bookish and formal — preferred in legal, academic, and administrative writing; in everyday speech it has been largely replaced by "find out", "establish", and "determine".
