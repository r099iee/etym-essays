- [19th century: educational sense] - Built into English (from Greek heuriskein, 'to find') around discovery-based teaching: the 'heuristic method' that leads a learner to find answers for himself rather than receive them.
- [Early-mid 20th century: philosophy of science] - Generalised to any fruitful hypothesis or exploratory procedure that guides inquiry before proof is complete; an admired, almost exalted word in logic and research.
- [Mid-20th century: computing and AI] - Adopted in computer science and artificial intelligence for quick rule-of-thumb search procedures that reach a good-enough solution fast; the register turned pragmatic and engineering-like.
- [Late 20th-21st century: cognitive psychology] - Tversky and Kahneman's work recast heuristics as the mind's fast, usually useful but systematically error-prone shortcuts; the connotation gained a skeptical edge — efficiency purchased at the price of guaranteed correctness.
