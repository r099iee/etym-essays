- Classical Latin (1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE) — *recēnsiō* meant an official review, enumeration, or census inspection of Roman citizens and military forces — perceived as a solemn administrative and legal count.
- Late Antiquity & Medieval Church Latin (4th–12th c.) — Occasionally used for ecclesiastical inventories, synodical records, and checking roll-calls of clerics — perceived as an institutional auditing procedure.
- Early Modern Renaissance & Enlightenment (16th–18th c.) — Humanist scholars adapted the term to denote the rigorous critical review of classical Greek and Latin manuscripts by collating divergent witnesses — perceived as an intellectual purification of ancient wisdom.
- 19th century (Lachmannian philology) — Codified in scientific stemmatics (*recensio* vs. *emendatio*) to signify the establishment of distinct textual families and regional branches of ancient literary and scriptural works — perceived as a rigorous scientific methodology.
- 20th–21st century — Expanded into digital humanities, genetic criticism, Biblical studies, and computational stemmatics; widely understood by scholars as distinct manuscript traditions of foundational epics like the Ramayana and Gilgamesh — perceived as vital historical evidence of cultural diversity.
