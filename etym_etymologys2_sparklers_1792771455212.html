PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*sper-
‘to strew, sprinkle, scatter’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*sparkōn-
‘glowing particle, spark’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
spearca
‘spark, small glowing particle’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
sparke
‘spark; trace, small amount’
↓
Early Modern English (1560s)
sparkle + -er
‘to glitter’ + agent suffix
‘one/that which sparkles’
↓
Modern English (1906, plural)
sparklers
‘hand-held fireworks’

Note: the link between Proto-Germanic *spark- and PIE *sper- (‘to strew’) is the standard handbook connection but is not fully certain; some scholars leave the Germanic root without a deeper PIE etymology.
