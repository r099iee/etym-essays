- 16th c.: Emerged in Early Modern English from Latin *animatus*, used in theological and natural philosophy treatises to describe physical matter endowed with a soul or living breath.
- 17th c. to 18th c.: Broadened to describe lively human facial expressions, spirited conversations, and energetic artistic compositions.
- 19th c.: Frequently employed in Victorian literature and realism (e.g., Dickens, Hardy, Zola translations) to portray bustling public spaces, crowded enclosures, and market scenes.
- 20th c. to Present: Acquired a revolutionary technical meaning in cinema and digital media to designate animated cartoons, CGI simulations, and interactive user interfaces while retaining its classic literary meanings.
