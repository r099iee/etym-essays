- [Ancient ethnonym] - 'Tyrian' (from Latin Tyrius, Greek Tyrios) has named the people of Tyre since classical times, entering English as a learned adjective for the great Phoenician city.
- [Purple dye association] - Through the centuries the word carried the glamour of Tyrian purple - the famous dye extracted from murex shellfish, the colour of emperors and kings - so that 'Tyrian' meant both the city and its regal purple.
- [19th-century historical use] - In 19th-century prose (as in Macaulay) it evoked the ancient Tyrian mariners - the first civilised seafarers to reach Britain - a word of deep antiquity and Phoenician maritime enterprise.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'Tyrian' survives in historical writing and in 'Tyrian purple', the poetic, evocative adjective for the ancient city and its imperial dye.
