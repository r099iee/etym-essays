PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed)
*leyd- / *loid-
"to play, mock, jest, leap"
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*loid-ō
"to play, amuse oneself"
↓
Latin (classical; 3rd c. BCE–1st c. CE)
lūdere
"to play, frolic, sport, mock, deceive"
↓
Latin Compound (classical; 1st c. BCE)
allūdere
"to play with, joke at, touch playfully, hint at"
(composed of ad- "to, toward" + lūdere "to play")
↓
Late Latin Past Participle Stem (c. 4th–5th c.)
allūs- (from allūdere → allūsus)
"having played upon, having referred indirectly"
↓
Late Latin Derived Adjective (c. 6th c.)
allūsīvus
"playful, containing a figurative reference or hint"
(with adjectival suffix -īvus indicating tendency or function)
↓
Middle French (c. 15th–16th c.)
allusif
"making indirect hints, metaphorical"
↓
Early Modern English (late 16th c.; c. 1590s)
allusive
"containing indirect hints, figurative, referencing external texts"
