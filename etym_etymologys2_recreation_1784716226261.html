Linear derivation chain for RECREATION: Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *kerh1- (also cited *ker-), reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE: "to grow, to cause to grow; to nourish, to bring forth, to create." Stage 2 — Proto-Italic *kre-, c. 1000–500 BCE: "to grow; to make grow." Stage 3 — Latin creare, attested from the 3rd century BCE (Plautus, Terence): "to create, make, bring forth, produce, cause to grow." Stage 4 — Latin recreare, attested in Classical Latin, 1st century BCE–CE (Cicero, Pliny): re- "again" + creare; literally "to create again," hence "to restore, refresh, revive, rebuild." Stage 5 — Latin recreatio, gen. recreationis, Late Latin, attested 4th century CE (Vulgate): "refreshment, restoration, recovery." Stage 6 — Old French recreacion / recreation, 12th–14th centuries: "refreshment of spirit, amusement, relaxation." Stage 7 — Middle English recreacioun, attested c. 1390 (Chaucer): "refreshment, relaxation, diversion." Stage 8 — Modern English recreation, 16th century onward: noun, "restorative pleasant activity; leisure pursuit." Debated / nuanced note: The PIE root *kerh1- is reconstructed chiefly from Italic (and probably Greek) evidence. One claimed Greek cognate, kraino (κράινω), "to accomplish, fulfil," attested in Homer, is debated — Beekes treats the connection as uncertain. No generally accepted Indo-Iranian cognate serves the creare branch (Sanskrit krnoti "to make" belongs to the distinct root *kwer-), so the chain above is Italic-centred. These points are recorded as scholarly debate rather than resolved fact.
