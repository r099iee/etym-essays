• Baldachin: Similar because both are coverings overhead; differs because baldachin is the ceremonial canopy of state over a throne or altar, more architectural and processional than a bed curtain.
  • Tester: Similar because both cover a bed; differs because tester is the rigid wooden or fabric roof of a four-poster bed, a structural term rather than a free hanging cloth.
  • Pavilion: Similar because both are tent-like coverings; differs because a pavilion is freestanding or outdoor, not an indoor bed enclosure.
  • Awning: Similar because both are cloth coverings; differs because an awning is outdoor shade cloth, utilitarian rather than an ornamental rank marker.
  • Drapery: Similar because both are hanging cloth; differs because drapery hangs at windows or walls without the overhead, enclosing sense of a canopy.
  • Curtain: Similar because both are hanging fabrics; differs because a curtain is a screen at a window or door, lacking the overhanging roof function.
  • Marquee: Similar because both are large tent-like coverings; differs because a marquee is a freestanding outdoor tent or a theatre's projecting sign, not an indoor shelter.
  • Velarium: Similar because both are overhead cloth coverings; differs because a velarium is specifically the awning over a Roman amphitheatre, a historical technical term.
  • Tree crown / tree canopy: Similar in the natural figurative sense; differs because the "tree canopy" is the botanical layer of leaves — an extension of the word, not a cloth covering.
  • Shade / shelter: Similar because both provide cover; differs because these are generic protection words without the image of suspended ornamental cloth.
