- 1300s-1400s Anglo-Norman legal and marital entry - to furnish a bride with a dower; to settle lands and revenues upon a church or monastery; formal legal feudalism
- 1500s-1600s Reformation and Renaissance philanthropy - founding and endowing grammar schools, colleges, and hospitals; Book of Common Prayer matrimonial vow ('with all my worldly goods I thee endow')
- 1700s-1800s Enlightenment and political philosophy - nature or the Creator endowing humanity with innate reason, rights, and talents; US Declaration of Independence (1776)
- Victorian era to 1900s - aesthetic and literary projection; authors endowing characters or nature with symbolic and emotional resonance; institutional university endowment funds
- Contemporary modern usage - dominant in institutional finance (university endowments) and analytical prose (endowing ordinary reality with meaning); high-register and dignified
