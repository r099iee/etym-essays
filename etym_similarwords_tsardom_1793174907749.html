• Kingdom: Similar because both name a monarch's realm, built on the same -dom suffix; differs because kingdom is the generic Western term, while tsardom is specifically Russian and autocratic in flavour.
  • Empire: Similar because after 1721 the tsardom renamed itself the Russian Empire with the same territory and ruler; differs because empire claims multi-national grandeur and modernity, while tsardom suggests the older, Muscovite, bearded order.
  • Tsarate (rare): Similar because both denote the rule or office of a tsar; differs because tsarate, on the model of caliphate, is a rare bookish coinage, while tsardom is the established English word.
  • Autocracy: Similar because both describe unlimited one-man rule, the tsar's defining style; differs because autocracy is an abstract Greek system-word, while tsardom is a concrete place and period with domes and snow.
  • Realm: Similar because both mean a royal domain, especially a storybook one; differs because realm is general and poetic, while tsardom pins the realm to Russia and the folktale map.
  • Dominion: Similar because both name ruled territory under a sovereign; differs because dominion is legalistic and often colonial, while tsardom is personal, patrimonial, and folkloric.
  • Principality: Similar because both are pre-modern monarchical states of the Russian lands; differs because a principality is a lesser rule, like early Moscow itself, while a tsardom is the gathered, sovereign whole.
  • Khanate: Similar because both ruled the Eurasian steppe frontier and taxed the same fur-trails; differs because a khanate is Turco-Mongol and nomadic in origin, while a tsardom is Slavic, Orthodox, and settled.
