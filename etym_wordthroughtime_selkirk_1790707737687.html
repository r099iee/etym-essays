- 12th Century - Originates as a Scottish Borders toponym and royal burgh (*Selechyrche*), meaning 'church in the forest' or 'shieling kirk'.
- 16th–17th Century - Flourished as a prominent Scottish Border family surname, adopted by families throughout Fife and the Lowlands (including the Selcraig family of Lower Largo).
- Early 18th Century (1709–1719) - Shot to global celebrity when Alexander Selkirk was rescued by Captain Woodes Rogers from the uninhabited Pacific island of Más a Tierra; celebrated in London journalism (Richard Steele's *The Englishman*) and immortalized by Daniel Defoe's *Robinson Crusoe* (1719).
- 19th–21st Century - Cemented into world history and geography; the Chilean island on which Selkirk survived was officially renamed *Alejandro Selkirk Island* in 1966, while the Scottish town and surname remain celebrated emblems of maritime folklore.
