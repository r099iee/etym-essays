• Cadmean: Similar because both denote a victory that proves disastrous or ruinous to the victor; differs because Cadmean specifically invokes the myth of Cadmus and the dragon's teeth, emphasizing mutual annihilation where both victor and vanquished perish completely.
  • Costly: Similar because both indicate that an achievement involved substantial expenditure or sacrifice; differs because costly is a broad, neutral descriptor that does not necessarily imply the cost outweighed the value of the outcome.
  • Ruinous: Similar because both describe an outcome causing severe devastation; differs because ruinous can apply to any catastrophic event or failure, without requiring a nominal victory or success as its precursor.
  • Counterproductive: Similar because both involve efforts that end up undermining the agent's ultimate objective; differs because counterproductive denotes tactical inefficiency or unintended negative consequences, lacking the epic, tragic magnitude of a *Pyrrhic* triumph.
  • Self-defeating: Similar because both characterize actions where the winner harms themselves in the process; differs because self-defeating focuses on the inherent contradiction in the strategy itself rather than the sheer casualty rate of an intense contest.
  • Hollow: Similar because both characterize a victory that brings no genuine satisfaction or triumph; differs because a hollow victory lacks meaning or substance (e.g. winning against an opponent who forfeited), whereas a pyrrhic victory is defined by active, catastrophic sacrifice.
