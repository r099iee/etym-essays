“Stow” entered English from Old English meaning to place, to put — and the sense of placing things in a designated space grew around it.
“Stow” through the age of sail became nautical vocabulary: to stow cargo and sails is to arrange them tightly and safely into the ship’s available space — a word of seamen and careful packing.
“Stow” extended from ships to homes and journeys in the 18th–19th centuries: luggage stowed, provisions stowed, guests stowed into spare rooms — the orderly fitting of things and people into finite room.
“Stowed” today is the past of that deliberate placement — packed away, tidily arranged, put into their places — a practical, slightly sailor-like or housekeeperly word, as when an exhausted host gets everyone stowed before bed.
