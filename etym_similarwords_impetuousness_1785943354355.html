• Impulsiveness: Similar because both name acting without reflection on sudden urge; differs because impulsiveness is everyday psychological vocabulary for spontaneous, unreflective action of any kind, while impetuousness adds passionate force, violent speed, and dramatic flare.
  • Rashness: Similar because both denote hasty action; differs because rashness emphasizes reckless disregard of obvious danger and likely harm, while impetuousness emphasizes emotional momentum — the rush itself — regardless of danger.
  • Hastiness: Similar because both involve speed; differs because hastiness is simply doing things quickly, often under time pressure, without the passionate, fiery quality that impetuousness carries.
  • Precipitancy: Similar because both describe acting before full consideration; differs because precipitancy suggests acting so quickly as to fall headlong into error, a colder, more procedural haste, while impetuousness is warm-blooded and emotional.
  • Recklessness: Similar because both disregard consequences; differs because recklessness emphasizes indifference to risk and may be cold-blooded, while impetuousness is defined by hot, passionate speed.
  • Temerity: Similar because both involve daring disregard of prudence; differs because temerity is rash boldness that overestimates one's luck or nerve — more about audacity than about speed.
  • Vehemence: Similar because both carry intensity of feeling; differs because vehemence is intensity or forcefulness of expression (of opinions, arguments), while impetuousness is forcefulness of action.
  • Spontaneity: Similar because both involve acting without premeditation; differs because spontaneity is positively valued — natural, fresh, unforced — while impetuousness carries risk and excess.
  • Petulance: Similar because both involve uncontrolled emotion; differs because petulance is childish, peevish, sulky irritability, while impetuousness is fiery, forceful, outward-charging action.
  • Headlong action: Similar because both describe rushing forward without looking; differs because "headlong" is an adverb or adjective describing the manner of a physical dive or plunge, while impetuousness is a stable character trait.
