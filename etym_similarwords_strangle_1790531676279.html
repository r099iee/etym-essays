• throttle: Similar because both denote compressing the throat to choke off breath; differs because throttle focuses specifically on seizing the windpipe with hands or a mechanical valve, whereas strangle encompasses cords, vines, economic forces, and broad figurative eradication.
  • choke: Similar because both involve obstruction of respiration; differs because choke often describes internal blockage of the airway (e.g. choking on food) or non-fatal gasping, while strangle strictly requires external compressive constriction around the neck.
  • stifle: Similar because both denote suppressing development or air; differs because stifle emphasizes cutting off air by smothering or creating a close, oxygen-deprived environment, or gently muffling an utterance (e.g. stifle a yawn), whereas strangle is overtly violent and destructive.
  • smother: Similar because both cause death by depriving of breath; differs because smother specifically involves covering the mouth and nose (or covering a fire with ashes) to block air entry, rather than compressing the cervical column.
  • suffocate: Similar because both denote death or distress from lack of oxygen; differs because suffocate is the broad physiological umbrella term for oxygen deprivation (whether by gas, confinement, or smoke), while strangle is the specific mechanical act of neck compression.
  • suppress: Similar because both denote putting a forceful stop to an uprising, idea, or feeling; differs because suppress is an abstract, institutional, or legal term (e.g. suppress a riot, suppress evidence), lacking the violent bodily imagery of strangle.
  • quell: Similar because both denote crushing an insurrection or disturbance; differs because quell emphasizes pacifying, calming, and subduing disorder back into quietude, whereas strangle emphasizes choking the life out of something entirely.
  • asphyxiate: Similar because both denote cutting off oxygen supply; differs because asphyxiate is a formal clinical/medical term describing the biochemical state of hypoxia and carbon dioxide buildup, whereas strangle is an active, aggressive mechanical verb.
