• Roof: Similar because both belong to the top of a building; differs because roof names the whole covering surface, of which the eave is only the protruding margin — you repair a roof but trim the eaves.
  • Cornice: Similar because both are projecting edges at the top of a wall; differs because a cornice is an ornamental moulding, decorative rather than functional, and it does not necessarily shed rainwater, while the eave is a working overhang.
  • Canopy: Similar because both are overhanging shelters; differs because a canopy is a covering stretched overhead — of cloth or a light frame — tent-like and removable, while eaves are rigid, built into the roof, and permanent.
  • Soffit: Similar because both are parts of the underside of a projecting structure; differs because a soffit is the exposed undersurface of an architectural member (such as a beam or stair), not specifically the rain-shedding roof edge that eaves name.
  • Fascia: Similar because both are horizontal strips along the roof edge; differs because the fascia is the flat board fixed to the eaves to which gutters are attached — a finish rather than the overhang itself.
  • Gable: Similar because both are parts of a pitched roof; differs because the gable is the triangular wall-end of a pitched roof, whereas the eave is the horizontal overhanging edge.
  • Overhang: Similar because both name a projecting part; differs because overhang is the general term for any part that projects beyond its support, while eaves is the specific roof-edge overhang with its rain-shedding function.
  • Verandah: Similar because both appear at the edge of a house; differs because the verandah is the covered walkway beneath the eaves — a space for walking — not the roof edge itself.
