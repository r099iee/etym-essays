Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*stinkwan
"to emit odour,"
"to push, strike"
↓
Old English (c. 8th–11th c.)
stincan
"to emit smell,"
"sweet or foul"
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
stinken
"to smell foul"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
stink + -er
"one/thing that"
"stinks"
↓
English (modern)
stinker
"foul thing; rotter;"
"hard problem, flop"

Note: beyond Proto-Germanic the deeper origin is uncertain. Older dictionaries linked *stinkwan to a root meaning to push or strike, but the semantic link is thin; many scholars now treat the base as imitative of sniffing or eruption. No secure PIE root is claimed here.
