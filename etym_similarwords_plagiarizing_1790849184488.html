• Pirating: Similar because both involve the unauthorized theft of intellectual property; differs because pirating focuses on illegal commercial reproduction and distribution for profit without paying royalties, whereas plagiarizing focuses on fraudulently claiming authorship of the work.
  • Stealing: Similar because both denote unlawful taking; differs because stealing is a broad term for taking tangible property or money, whereas plagiarizing specifically concerns intellectual, rhetorical, or creative ideas passed off as one's own.
  • Copying: Similar because both involve duplicating existing material; differs because copying can be legitimate, open, educational, or mechanical, whereas plagiarizing is inherently deceptive and uncredited.
  • Appropriating: Similar because both involve taking another's cultural or intellectual output; differs because appropriating is a broader critical term that can describe adopting cultural styles, motifs, or concepts without necessarily hiding the source or committing direct textual fraud.
  • Infringing: Similar because both violate intellectual property; differs because infringing (copyright infringement) is a strict legal term relating to statutory property rights, whereas plagiarism is primarily an academic and ethical violation that can occur even with public-domain works.
  • Poaching: Similar because both describe illicit taking from someone else's domain; differs because poaching typically applies to stealing employees, hunting game illegally, or encroaching on another's territory, carrying a more predatory or opportunistic flavor.
