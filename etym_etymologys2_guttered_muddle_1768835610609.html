GUTTERED

Latin (origin uncertain)
gutta
"a drop"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
goute
"a drop"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
goutiere
"a channel for carrying off water"
↓
English (14th c.)
gutter
"a channel for carrying water;
a groove"
↓
guttered
"flickered and went out
(like a candle)"

Note: Latin gutta "a drop" is of uncertain origin. Of a candle, guttered means the melting wax runs down in channels and the flame flickers out.

muddle

muddle

Note: The origin of mud is uncertain; a borrowing from Middle Low German or Dutch mudde "mud, peat" is possible but unproven. In the 17th c. the verb shifted from "to wallow in mud" to "to confuse, make a mess of."
