• Surly: Similar because both mean bad-tempered; differs because surly suggests a menacing, hostile moroseness, while grumpy is milder, homely and even comic.
  • Sullen: Similar because both mean withdrawn and ill-humoured; differs because sullen implies resentful, brooding silence, while grumpy can be a passing, fussy mood without brooding.
  • Irritable: Similar because both mean easily annoyed; differs because irritable points toward snapped, explosive responses, while grumpy is a settled but gentle, almost affectionate grumpiness.
  • Peevish: Similar because both mean easily vexed; differs because peevish is fussier, more childish and querulous, while grumpy conveys a good-humoured, forgiving bad temper.
  • Cross: Similar because both mean out of temper; differs because cross is a milder, more matter-of-fact annoyance (British English), while grumpy adds a settled, muttering surliness.
  • Cranky: Similar because both mean irritable; differs because cranky (American English) is more eccentric and complaining, while grumpy is quieter and more withdrawn.
  • Grouchy: Similar because both are colloquial for bad-tempered; differs because grouchy emphasises the grumbling complaint (from grutch/grumble), while grumpy emphasises the silent, sullen mood.
  • Cantankerous: Similar because both are bad-tempered; differs because cantankerous is actively quarrelsome, obstinate and aggressive, while grumpy is passive and comic rather than fighting.
  • Morose: Similar because both are gloomy and ill-humoured; differs because morose is deep, heavy and brooding gloom, while grumpy is a lighter, more forgivable irritability.
  • Churlish: Similar because both are rude and surly; differs because churlish names boorish, ungracious rudeness toward others, while grumpy need not be directed at anyone.
  • Testy: Similar because both mean easily provoked; differs because testy implies being quick to snap when irritated, while grumpy is a more sustained, low-grade bad mood.
  • Crotchety: Similar because both mean irritable and odd-tempered; differs because crotchety suggests whimsical, eccentric ill humour in an old person, while grumpy is general and unremarkable.
  • Ill-tempered: Similar because both mean having a bad temper; differs because ill-tempered is the formal, neutral term, without the affectionate, comic colouring of grumpy.
  • Grum (archaic): Similar because both mean surly and morose; differs because grum is an archaic word (from the same echoic base as grumble), while grumpy is the living modern adjective.
