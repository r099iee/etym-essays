Note: French ogre was popularized by Charles Perrault in 1697. Historical linguists derive it from Latin Orcus ('Pluto, the Underworld'), which yielded Italian orco ('man-eating ogre', famously featured in Giambattista Basile's Pentamerone, 1634). An antiquated 18th-century folk etymology linking ogre to Hongrois ('Hungarians/Huns') is completely refuted by modern comparative philology.
