- [Old English 'tīnder' + 'box'] - The compound names the dry flammable substance that catches a spark — charred cloth, dried fungus or shredded bark — together with the container that holds it; the tinder-box was the standard means of kindling fire long before friction matches.
- [Pre-modern everyday object] - For centuries the tinder-box was ordinary household and wilderness hardware, holding tinder, flint and a steel striker; losing one was a genuine small calamity in daily life.
- [Early 19th century] - The friction match makes the tinder-box obsolete as living technology, and the literal sense retreats into historical and period vocabulary.
- [Modern metaphorical sense] - From the 19th century onward 'tinderbox' also names a situation or region ready to burst into flame — a powder-keg of a different substance — so the word now survives far more often as a metaphor than as a literal object.
