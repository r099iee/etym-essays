LINEAR CHAIN (SALONISTE) 1. PIE *sel- (to dwell, inhabit; settlement, dwelling, habitation) — reconstructed root; the standard, though not universally certain, link for the Germanic "hall/house" family. 2. Proto-Germanic *saliz / *salaz (house, hall, one-room dwelling; the main living hall of a homestead) — reconstructed, c. 1st millennium BCE. 3. Early Romance / Italian sala (hall, large room, big common room) — attested in medieval Italian and Spanish; borrowed from Germanic into Romance in the early medieval period (traditionally via Langobardic/Lombard or Gothic contact in Italy). 4. Italian salone (augmentative of sala; a very large hall or room) — medieval–Renaissance Italian. 5. French salon (a large reception or drawing room; later, the fashionable social gathering held in such a room; later still, the Parisian art exhibition le Salon) — 17th century onward. 6. French saloniste (salon + agentive suffix -iste; "a person who keeps a salon," prototypically a woman) — formed in the 18th–19th century as the person-noun beside the institution. 7. English saloniste (loanword: a woman who hosts a salon) — borrowed into English in the 19th–20th century as a French cultural term. MORPHOLOGICAL NOTE: the suffix -iste descends from Greek -istēs → Latin -ista → French -iste, "one who practises or is associated with X." NOTE ON DEBATE: The PIE stage is the weakest link — some scholars prefer to leave the Germanic/Romance "hall" words without a secure PIE etymon, and *sel- is only one of several proposed candidates. The Germanic-to-Romance borrowing (whence sala) is likewise debated in detail, though a Lombard/Gothic channel is the usual view. The later Romance-to-French development is well attested and uncontroversial.
