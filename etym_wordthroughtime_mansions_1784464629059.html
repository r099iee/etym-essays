“Mansion” entered English from Latin meaning a place of staying — a dwelling, a house, an abode, with the older sense of any lodging on a journey.
“Mansion” from the biblical phrase “in my Father’s house are many mansions” kept an elevated sense of dwelling-places, while secular use built the meaning of a grand, impressive residence.
“Mansion” through the 18th–19th centuries fixed on the large house of the wealthy — the grand country house, the town mansion — a word of rank, scale, and impressive architecture.
“Mansions” today means large, grand dwellings, though the older “abodes” colour still haunts elevated English; in a catalogue of a kingdom’s halls and mansions, the word names the splendid living-spaces now emptied and scoured.
