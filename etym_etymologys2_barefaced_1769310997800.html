Derivation chain for the compound BAREFACED (two independent root chains, given in isolation). CHAIN 1 — the element BARE (native Germanic):
  • PIE *bhosos (or *bhoso-) "naked, bare, uncovered" (c. 4000–3000 BCE)
  • Proto-Germanic *bazaz "naked, bare, uncovered" (c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE)
  • Old English bær "naked, bare, without covering" (c. 8th–11th c.)
  • Middle English bare "naked, uncovered, mere" (c. 12th–15th c.)
  • Modern English bare (16th c. onward)
  • BARE enters the compound barefaced (1580s) as its first element.
CHAIN 2 — the element FACE (Romance, from Latin):
  • PIE *dheh₁- "to put, place, set, make" (c. 4000 BCE) — the traditional source of Latin facere "to do, make"; the chain into "face" is via the made form or appearance.
  • Latin facere "to do, make" (c. 3rd c. BCE) and its derived noun facies "form, shape, appearance; face, countenance" (classical, 1st c. BCE; the "face" sense is attested in Latin)
  • Vulgar Latin *facia / *facia, the spoken continuation of facies (c. 4th–7th c.)
  • Old French face "face, visage, countenance, front" (c. 9th–12th c.)
  • Anglo-Norman / Middle English face "face, visage" (c. 13th c., via Norman French after 1066)
  • Modern English face (14th c. onward)
  • FACE enters the compound barefaced (1580s) as its second element.
COMPOUND ASSEMBLY: bare + face → barefaced, first attested c. 1580 in the literal sense "with the face uncovered, unmasked, beardless"; the moral sense "shameless, impudent, undisguised" follows by c. 1590–1600. Final node: barefaced (English, late 16th c.) — our word. NUANCE NOTE: The etymology of Latin facies is debated. The traditional, majority view connects facies to the root of facere (i.e. PIE *dheh₁- "to make/do"), with the semantic logic "that which is made or shaped, hence outward form, then face." Some scholars instead treat facies as of unclear or separate origin within Latin, and the deeper PIE assignment of the "bare" root *bhosos is itself not beyond dispute (Pokorny gives *bhoso-, but some regard its broader Indo-European relations as uncertain). What is well attested is the Germanic branch *bazaz → Old English bær, and the Romance branch *facia → Old French face → English face.
