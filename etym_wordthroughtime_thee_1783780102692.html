Old & Middle English Ordinary Use - Thee is the everyday objective pronoun for a single addressee, the unremarkable "you" of the language, used for friend and stranger alike in ordinary speech.
Early Modern T/V Etiquette - As the polite plural you invades the singular in the 15th-17th centuries, thee becomes charged: the pronoun of intimacy, of address to God, of love, condescension, or contempt, carrying a whole social drama of closeness and distance.
18th Century Retreat - By the 18th century thou and thee are driven out of standard speech, surviving only in scripture, liturgy, and poetry, where they gain a solemn, tender, or archaic aura.
Contemporary Sense - Today thee is archaic and poetic, preserved in fixed phrases ("woe unto thee"), in the living dialect of parts of Yorkshire, and in historic Quaker plain speech — always signalling closeness, solemnity, or period flavour.
