• Pounded: Similar because both describe heavy, repeated strikes or heart palpitations; differs because pounded implies continuous, rhythmic, hammer-like repetition and crushing force, whereas thumped emphasizes the single dull, low-frequency sound or isolated heavy impact.
  • Thudded: Similar because both represent dull, muffled impacts; differs because thudded focuses almost entirely on the dead, flat acoustic sound of a soft or heavy object landing (lacking energy or bounce), while thumped conveys greater kinetic force, deliberate striking action, or dynamic pulsation.
  • Battered: Similar because both involve striking; differs because battered denotes severe, repeated, damaging blows that disfigure, break, or bruise the target over time, whereas thumped relates to a blunt, concussive hit that may not leave visible structural destruction.
  • Beaten: Similar because both describe being struck or defeated; differs because beaten is broader and more formal, referring to rhythmic striking, continuous corporal punishment, or general defeat, while thumped is more visceral, informal, and acoustically descriptive.
  • Pummeled: Similar because both denote physical striking; differs because pummeled specifically implies a rapid, relentless barrage of punches or blows in close succession, whereas thumped can be a single, heavy, isolated blow.
  • Smacked: Similar because both indicate a sudden blow; differs because smacked denotes a sharp, crisp, stinging slap with a flat hand or object producing a high-frequency crack, contrasting with the deep, low-frequency, muffled resonance of thumped.
  • Banged: Similar because both refer to loud percussive hits; differs because banged produces a sharper, explosive, reverberating noise (like a slammed door or gunshot), whereas thumped is muffled, heavy, and flesh-or-wood-like.
  • Palpitated: Similar because both describe violent heart movement; differs because palpitated is a formal clinical or poetic term emphasizing rapid, fluttering, irregular, or trembling cardiac movement, whereas thumped emphasizes forceful, heavy, resounding cardiac pounding.
  • Knocked: Similar because both involve striking a surface; differs because knocked refers to a rap, tap, or structured signal on a door, or an accidental collision, lacking the deep, heavy bass resonance inherent in thumped.
  • Whacked: Similar because both represent vigorous blows; differs because whacked is informal and conveys a swift, sweeping, slicing strike with a stick, bat, or implement, while thumped implies a direct, heavy, blunt mass impact.
