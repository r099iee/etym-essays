Proto-Indo-European *upér "over, above" (Pokorny *uper "über, über hinaus, oberhalb"; the "above" form paired with *upó "up from under, under") → [Proto-Italic] *super "above, over" → [Latin] super "above, over, on top of" (preposition and prefix) → [Vulgar Latin] *superānus "situated above; chief, principal, supreme" (super + the adjective suffix -ānus "pertaining to") → [Old French] soverain "supreme; a supreme ruler, a king" (12th c.; the cluster super- dissimilated and weakened to sove-/sou-) → [Anglo-French] sovereyn, soverain "sovereign, supreme" (13th c.) → [Middle English] sovereyn, souerein "sovereign, supreme ruler" (13th c.) → [English] sovereign (the 15th–16th c. spelling re-cast with -ei- under the false influence of reign, though the two words are etymologically unrelated) → [English] sovereigns (plural) "kings, queens, supreme rulers; also states holding supreme power" NOTE ON THE FALSE SPELLING: English sovereign was respelled with -ei- in the 15th–16th centuries under the influence of reign (from Latin rēgnāre), but the two words are NOT related: sovereign descends from Latin super "above," reign from Latin rēgnāre "to rule." The Romance cognates — Italian sovrano, Spanish and Portuguese soberano — preserve the true shape of *superānus.
