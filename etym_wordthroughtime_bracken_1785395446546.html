- 14th century - Middle English 'braken' enters from Old Norse 'brakni', the poetic name of the fern; at first a general word for fern.
- 15th-16th centuries - the modern spelling 'bracken' settles as the ordinary plant name.
- Modern English - narrows and specializes: while 'fern' stays the broad family word, 'bracken' comes to mean specifically Pteridium aquilinum, the tall, coarse fern that spreads invasively over hillsides, moors and clearings, often standing several feet high.
- Modern register - rural, naturalistic, faintly archaic: a word of moors, fells and old woods rather than botany lectures, carrying a whole tawny, head-high, rustling landscape of its own.
