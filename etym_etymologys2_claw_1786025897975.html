PIE *gel- / extended *glō(u)-

"to form into a ball, curl, round up; ball, clump"
(reconstructed root; also source of clay, clew, clue —
the connection to "claw" is TRADITIONAL but DEBATED;
the semantic bridge would be "curled, hooked thing")

↓

Proto-Germanic *klawō

"claw, talon, nail, cleft foot"
(reconstructed; cf. Old Norse kló, Dutch klauw,
German Klaue, Swedish/Danish klo)

↓

Old English clauu / clawu (noun)

"claw, talon, nail of a finger or toe"
(attested from the 8th–9th c.)

↓

Old English clawian (verb)

"to scratch, scrape, claw"
(attested from the 9th–10th c.)

↓

Middle English clawe (noun) / clawen (verb)

"claw, talon"; "to scratch, tear, scrape,
grasp greedily, flatter"
(attested from the 13th c.)

↓

CLAW

"sharp curved nail on an animal's toe or pincer;
to scratch, tear, scrape, or desperately grasp"
(Modern English)

Note: the PIE stage is debated. Many handbooks link *glō(u)-
"ball" to claw via the curved shape, but the intermediate
evidence is thin; the Germanic chain from *klawō onward is
secure and firmly attested.
