• Handmaid: Similar because identical in origin and meaning; differs because handmaid is the shorter, older, more archaic form, while handmaiden is the fuller, more literary variant that dominates modern figurative use.
  • Maidservant: Similar because both mean a female household servant; differs because maidservant is a plain, functional term for a domestic worker, while handmaiden implies personal, close, "at-hand" attendance and carries religious and literary weight.
  • Maid: Similar because both denote a female servant; differs because maid has broadened to any female servant (and historically any young unmarried woman), losing the precision of the personal attendant.
  • Attendant: Similar because both mean a person who attends on another; differs because attendant is gender-neutral and can be a mere functionary (a parking attendant), while handmaiden is specifically female, personal, and intimate.
  • Lady-in-waiting: Similar because both denote a personal female attendant on a noblewoman; differs because a lady-in-waiting is herself an aristocratic woman serving at court, while a handmaiden is a servant of lower station.
  • Chambermaid: Similar because both are female servants; differs because a chambermaid cleans and prepares rooms, while a handmaiden attends personally to a person.
  • Servant: Similar because both denote domestic service; differs because servant is general and gender-neutral, while handmaiden is specifically female, intimate, and historical.
  • Waiting-woman: Similar because both denote a woman who waits upon a lady; differs because waiting-woman is a plain, descriptive household term, while handmaiden is literary and carries biblical weight.
  • Retainer: Similar because both denote a loyal follower attached to a household; differs because retainer usually suggests a male attendant or feudal dependent, and lacks the religious and feminine overtones.
  • Ancillary / auxiliary: Similar because both denote something subordinate and supportive; differs because ancillary and auxiliary are impersonal, technical terms for supplementary equipment, staff, or roles, while handmaiden is a vivid, personified image.
  • Subordinate: Similar because both denote lower rank or position; differs because subordinate is an abstract administrative term, while handmaiden is a concrete, human image of service.
  • Instrument / tool: Similar because both denote something used in service of another; differs because instrument and tool are wholly impersonal, while handmaiden retains the image of a person serving.
  • Helpmeet / helpmate: Similar because both denote a supportive partner in service; differs because a helpmeet is a companion-equal (originally of a wife to a husband), while a handmaiden is a subordinate servant.
  • Concubine: Similar because both are female figures attached to a household in biblical usage; differs because a concubine is a secondary wife with a sexual role, not a servant, and the word carries different moral and social weight.
