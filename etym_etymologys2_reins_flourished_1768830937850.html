Root 1 — REINS:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*ten-
"to stretch, hold"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
tenēre
"to hold"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
retinēre
"to hold back, restrain"
(re- "back" + tenēre "to hold")
↓
Old French (12th c.)
rene
"a rein"
↓
English (13th c.)
rein
"a strap for guiding a horse"
↓
English (plural form)
reins
"straps used to guide a horse"

Note: the Old French form was also written resne / reigne; the sense "rein" grew from retinēre "to hold back" — reins hold the horse back. The plural reins is the normal form.

Root 2 — FLOURISHED:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*bʰleh₃-
"to bloom, flower"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
flōs
"a flower"
↓
Latin (1st c. CE)
flōrēre
"to bloom, flourish"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
florir
"to bloom, flourish"
↓
English (13th c.)
flourish
"to bloom; to thrive"
↓
English (15th c.)
flourished
"thrived; (of a sword) waved
in a sweeping, showy way"

Note: the sense "to brandish, wave showily" (14th–15th c.) grew from the idea of blooming/adorning, via the noun flourish "an ornament".
