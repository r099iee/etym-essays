• Hanging: Similar in the shared core of being held up from above without support; differs because hanging is the neutral term for any fixed suspension and carries none of dangling's implication of free, ungoverned movement.
  • Swinging: Similar in loose motion from a fixed point; differs because swinging implies a deliberate arc from a pivot, as on a hinge or a rope's end, whereas dangling is ungoverned — attached, yet at the mercy of gravity.
  • Sagging: Similar in drooping slackness; differs because sagging suggests a downward collapse under weight or weakness, not the free lateral sway of a pendulous object.
  • Drooping: Similar in hanging down limply; differs because drooping implies limpness or fatigue bending downward, while dangling keeps the sense of loose swing and instability.
  • Suspended: Similar in being held without firm anchorage; differs because suspended implies deliberate, usually temporary arrest (a bridge or a game suspended) and carries no image of sway at all.
  • Swaying: Similar in to-and-fro motion; differs because swaying emphasizes rhythmic movement from the base and does not require hanging, whereas dangling is hanging first and moving second.
  • Trailing: Similar in hanging down loosely; differs because trailing suggests something dragged behind on the ground or in water, not suspended freely in air.
  • Pendulous: Similar in hanging loosely downward; differs because pendulous is a formal, botanical-clinical word without the comic or precarious overtones of dangling.
  • Pending: Similar in the figurative sense of an unresolved matter; differs because pending is a legal-administrative term for awaiting decision, with none of the visual image of loose suspension.
