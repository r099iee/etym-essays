• begin: Similar because both express the initial transition from inactivity or nonexistence into action; differs because begin is native Germanic, neutral in register, and used universally in everyday speech, whereas commence is Romance, elevated, and typically reserved for formal, ceremonial, or documented procedures.
  • start: Similar because both denote setting something into motion; differs because start inherently conveys sudden physical movement, impulse, or mechanical activation (e.g. starting an engine), whereas commence cannot denote mechanical ignition or involuntary physical jolts.
  • initiate: Similar because both describe bringing something into being; differs because initiate implies active causation, originating an institutional process, or introducing someone into an esoteric group, while commence focuses on the temporal threshold of opening.
  • inaugurate: Similar because both refer to an official beginning; differs because inaugurate specifically implies a formal ceremony, ritual induction, or the historic opening of a building, public era, or term of office.
  • embark: Similar because both describe entering upon a sustained undertaking; differs because embark carries a strong seafaring metaphor of setting sail, personal adventure, or risking uncertainty on a long voyage (usually constructed with on or upon).
  • launch: Similar because both mark the outset of an operation; differs because launch emphasizes forceful propulsion, public release, or commercial introduction to a marketplace (e.g. launching a product or campaign).
  • institute: Similar because both describe putting something into operation; differs because institute emphasizes the formal establishment of a system, law, custom, or investigation rather than mere chronological beginning.
  • open: Similar because both mark the first stage of an event; differs because open emphasizes the unsealing, unlocking, or inaugural greeting of an exhibition, debate, or chess match.
  • originate: Similar because both trace to the outset; differs because originate focuses on provenance, ancestry, and the initial point of genesis or invention rather than execution.
  • kick off: Similar because both describe beginning an event; differs because kick off is an informal sporting idiom suitable for casual colloquial registers, contrasting starkly with the Latinate solemnity of commence.
