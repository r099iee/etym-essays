- Classical Antiquity — Latin noun ārea designated a cleared, open threshing floor, courtyard, or vacant building site in agricultural and urban Roman contexts.
- 17th c. — Mathematical Latin and early scientific texts began adopting area for planar geometric surface extent; āreālis coined in Neo-Latin mathematical treatises.
- Early 19th c. (c. 1820s–1830s) — Entered English scientific prose to describe two-dimensional surface metrics, popularized in physics through celestial mechanics (Kepler's "areal velocity").
- Early 20th c. (1920s–1930s) — Imported into linguistics by the Italian neolinguists (Matteo Bartoli's linguistica spaziale) and the Prague Linguistic Circle (Nikolai Trubetzkoy, Roman Jakobson) to define the horizontal diffusion of language features (areal linguistics / Sprachbund).
- Mid-to-Late 20th c. — Standardized across historical linguistics, geography, and ecology (areal distribution), while simultaneously emerging in computer hardware and materials science (areal storage density).
- 21st c. — Essential technical vocabulary in contact linguistics, geographic information systems (GIS), nanotech data storage, and orbital mechanics.
