- Antiquity to Roman Era - Greek *kanistron* and Latin *canistrum* refer exclusively to woven reed baskets used for carrying bread, fruit, flowers, or sacrificial offerings in sacred civic rites.
- 15th to 16th Century - Re-enters English during the Renaissance through scholarly Latin translations as *canister*, initially designating classical wicker baskets and religious vessels of offering.
- 18th Century - Undergoes a profound material transformation during the British tea trade; transferred to airtight tinplate, lead-lined, or ceramic boxes used to store precious imported Chinese tea leaves, becoming a standard domestic fixture.
- 19th Century - Expands dramatically into military artillery ballistics ("canister shot" breaking infantry charges) and industrial pyrotechnics (cylindrical firework casings, as depicted in Oscar Wilde's 1888 fairy tale); solidifies as a pantry staple.
- 20th to 21st Century - Advances into life-safety and environmental technology, designating gas-mask respirator filter cartridges and automotive fuel-vapor emissions canisters, while remaining universally familiar in home kitchens.
