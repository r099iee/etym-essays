- **16th-17th Century (Early Attestation)** - Primarily used to describe a very small stream or brook, often in natural or pastoral settings. Connotation: Neutral, descriptive, emphasizing smallness and natural flow.
- **18th-19th Century (Literary & Scientific Expansion)** - Continued use in poetry and descriptive prose to evoke natural landscapes. Emergence of more specific hydrological understanding, especially in relation to erosion. Connotation: Poetic, evocative, also gaining a more technical, observational aspect in early scientific writing.
- **20th Century (Hydrological Specialization)** - Increasingly used in scientific contexts (e.g., soil science, geomorphology) to denote a specific type of erosional channel, distinct from sheet or gully erosion. Connotation: Precise, technical, while retaining its general descriptive use. Societal perception: Understood by the general public as a small stream, but recognized by specialists as a key element in land degradation studies.
- **21st Century (Contemporary Usage)** - Maintains its dual usage: common descriptive term for a small stream in everyday language and literature, and a specific technical term in environmental science and engineering. Connotation: Versatile, from poetic to scientific, reflecting its enduring relevance in both general and specialized discourse.
