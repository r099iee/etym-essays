- Latin desistere to stand away - neutral military and civic term for leaving off
- 15th-16th c. borrowing into Scots and English law - formal, written: desist from violence, suits; authoritative tone
- 17th-18th c. general literary use - moralists and historians use desisted for armies, sects, authors abandoning courses
- 19th c. legal fossilization - cease and desist becomes fixed formula; everyday speech shifts to stop/quit, leaving desisted bookish
- 20th-21st c. niche survival - lawyers, minutes, anthropology and irony keep desisted alive; to modern ears it sounds dignified, slightly pompous or comic
