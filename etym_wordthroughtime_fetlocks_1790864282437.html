- Late Middle English (c. 14th c.) - fitlok / fetlak / fetlock emerged as an equestrian compound denoting the tuft of hair growing on the back of a horse's lower leg.
- 15th to 16th Century - Metonymically transferred from the hair tuft to the entire lower anatomical hinge joint itself.
- 17th to 18th Century - Became standard terminology in English farriery, veterinary medicine, and classical horsemanship treatises.
- 19th Century - Widely utilized in descriptive prose and military chronicles as a standard measure of depth (e.g., "fetlock-deep in mud or blood").
- 20th to 21st Century - Retains essential status in equestrian sports, equine veterinary orthopedic surgery, and literary pastoral description.
