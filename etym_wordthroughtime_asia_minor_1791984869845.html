- 5th c. BCE — the name Asia is first applied to the Lydian coast, then to the whole continent and the Persian empire; the peninsula has no separate name.
- 2nd c. BCE — Rome creates the province of Asia in the west of the peninsula; “Asia” in Latin usually means that province, not the continent.
- 4th–5th c. CE — geographers begin to distinguish the peninsula from greater Asia by the descriptive phrase Asia Minor; it remains learned and bookish.
- 16th–17th c. — English translators and travellers adopt “Asia Minor” for the Anatolian peninsula, often in biblical and classical contexts.
- 19th c. — The term becomes standard in classical atlases, missionary geography and the new archaeology of Ephesus and Troy.
- 20th–21st c. — “Anatolia” and “Asian Turkey” displace it in general geography, while “Asia Minor” survives as the historical and archaeological name.
