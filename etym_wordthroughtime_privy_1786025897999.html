- 13th–14th c. (Middle English *prive / privy*) - Intimate, secret, & personal: Denoted that which was kept private from the public, personal secrets, or close confidential companions.
- 14th–16th c. (Late Middle / Early Modern English) - Institutional monarchical power: Cemented into royal governance denoting the monarch's closest advisory bodies (Privy Council, Privy Purse, Lord Privy Seal).
- 16th–19th c. - Euphemistic bodily sanitation: Became the standard Anglo-American vernacular term for an outhouse or latrine (the 'private place' of bodily relief).
- 20th–21st c. (Contemporary English) - Predicate cognitive idiom: Modern usage concentrates almost exclusively on the predicate construction privy to (possessing insider secret knowledge), while the noun sense survives historically or legally.
