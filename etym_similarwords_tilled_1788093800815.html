• cultivated: Similar because both mean working land to raise crops; differs because cultivated is broader and more formal — it covers the whole cycle (ploughing, sowing, weeding, tending) and has strong figurative senses (cultivated taste), while tilled is concrete and restricted to breaking and preparing the soil.
  • ploughed: Similar because both describe working the soil with a blade; differs because ploughing is the specific mechanical act of turning the furrow, whereas tilled is the general preparation of which ploughing is the first step.
  • harrowed: Similar because both are stages of soil preparation; differs because harrowing is the particular act of breaking clods and levelling after the plough — a subset of tilling.
  • worked: Similar because "worked land" means land used productively for farming; differs because worked is vague and refers to any labour, not specifically to soil preparation.
  • farmed: Similar because both imply agricultural use of land; differs because farming is the whole enterprise (including livestock), while tilled is one specific soil operation.
  • farrowed: Similar because both mean breaking ground; differs because farrowed is archaic/dialectal and even more specifically the first ploughing, while tilled is general and still current.
  • dug: Similar because both involve breaking the ground; differs because digging is manual, small-scale and unspecific (holes, trenches, gardens), while tilled implies systematic, field-scale preparation.
  • prepared (of soil): Similar because both describe making ground ready for planting; differs because prepared is generic and modern-agronomic, with none of tilled's biblical or pastoral colour.
  • tended: Similar because both involve caring for land; differs because tending implies ongoing maintenance, whereas tilled means the initial breaking and working of the soil.
