• Cohesion: Similar because both denote unity and connectedness within a system or text; differs because 'cohesion' refers to the explicit surface grammatical/syntactic ties linking sentences, whereas 'coherence' refers to the deeper semantic meaning and conceptual integrity of the whole.
  • Consistency: Similar because both involve harmony and lack of contradiction; differs because 'consistency' emphasizes uniformity and adherence to a single rule or standard over time, whereas 'coherence' emphasizes the mutual structural integration of diverse parts into a unified whole.
  • Unity: Similar because both express the condition of forming a single complete entity; differs because 'unity' describes the overarching state of oneness, whereas 'coherence' specifically highlights the logical, intelligibly connected relations between internal elements.
  • Lucidity: Similar because both contribute to ease of understanding; differs because 'lucidity' emphasizes radiant clarity, brightness, and transparency of expression, whereas 'coherence' emphasizes structural and relational soundness.
  • Congruence: Similar because both describe harmonious alignment; differs because 'congruence' emphasizes exact geometric or qualitative agreement between two corresponding entities.
  • Integrity: Similar because both denote wholeness and soundness; differs because 'integrity' often carries moral, ethical, or physical uncorruptedness, whereas 'coherence' focuses on intellectual and structural intelligibility.
