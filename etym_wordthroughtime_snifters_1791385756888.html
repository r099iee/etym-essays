- 1790s–1830s: Industrial steam mechanics - an automatic valve mounted on steam engines and hydraulic pumps that admits atmospheric air with an audible sniffing sound.
- 1840s–1890s: Saloon and pub slang - a small measure, tot, or quick swallow of strong spirits taken at a counter to revive spirits or clear the head.
- 1900s–1950s: Clubland slang and luxury glassware - stylized balloon glasses designed to swirl and sniff brandy, alongside an affectionate upper-class exclamation for drinks popularized by P.G. Wodehouse.
- 1960s–present: Gastronomic and craft brewing culture - standard technical stemware for evaluating the complex aromatics of single-cask brandies, cognacs, and high-gravity craft ales.
