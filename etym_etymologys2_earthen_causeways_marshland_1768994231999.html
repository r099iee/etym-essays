↓
PIE (reconstructed; uncertain)
*kelk-
"to be cold, hard"

calx, calcis
"lime, limestone"
↓

calciata
"paved with lime, limestone"

↓
Medieval Latin (8th–14th c.)
via calciata
"a road paved with limestone"
↓
Anglo-French (13th–15th c.)
causee
"a raised, paved road"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
causey
"a raised road"
↓
English (15th c.)
causeway
"a raised road across wet ground"
↓
English (present, plural)
causeways
"raised roads across wet ground"

Note: causeway is a re-analysis of causey + way. Causey is from Anglo-French causee, from Medieval Latin calciata, sc. via "a road surfaced with limestone", from Latin calx, calcis "lime, limestone". The PIE root *kelk- "to be cold, hard" is uncertain.
