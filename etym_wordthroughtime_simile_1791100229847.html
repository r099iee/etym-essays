- Latin grammatical term for likeness - neutral technical, similis meaning like or similar
- Medieval rhetoric textbook term - scholastic and prescriptive, simile as a figure to be classified against metaphor
- Renaissance poetic ornament - admiring, Homeric and biblical similes prized as beauty and wisdom
- Dickensian proverbial wisdom - reverent and comic, the wisdom of ancestors stored in a simile like flies in amber
- Modern schoolroom staple - pedagogical and basic, simile versus metaphor as the first figure every child learns
- Contemporary loose use for any comparison - casual, simile sometimes stretched to cover jokes, memes, and analogies
