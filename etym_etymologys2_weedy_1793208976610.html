wēod

weed
'unwanted plant'
↓
Early Modern English (15th c.)
weed + -y
'weed' + 'full of'

↓
Modern English (current)
weedy
'full of weeds; scrawny'

Second element — -y, the adjective suffix:
Old English (9th c.)
-ig
'having the quality of'
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
-y
'full of, like'
↓
Modern English (current)
-y
'characterised by'

Note: Old English wēod has no certain cognates outside English and no agreed deeper origin, so the chain honestly begins at the attested Old English stage rather than inventing a Germanic or Indo-European root.
