• Trudged: Similar because it is a heavy, effortful way of walking; differs because trudging is weary and resigned, dragging feet in exhaustion, whereas stumping is stiff and percussive, charged with irritation and protest.
  • Plodded: Similar because it is slow and heavy-footed; differs because plodding is monotonous and weariness-driven, flat-footed, with none of stumped's snapping stiffness or indignation.
  • Clumped: Similar because it is the closest sound-cousin, a heavy thudding gait; differs because clumping emphasises heavy boots and mud, while stumping suggests moving on a shortened, wooden-like stub.
  • Strode: Similar because it is a deliberate way of walking; differs because striding is confident, purposeful, and at ease — the exact opposite of stumped's grudging, protest-heavy compliance.
  • Shuffled: Similar because it is an informal walking verb; differs because shuffling is soft, reluctant, and dragging, with no anger in it, while stumping is hard, stiff, and faintly offended.
  • Limped: Similar because both can describe impaired, uneven walking; differs because limping implies pain, injury, or an actual lame leg, whereas stumping need imply no injury — only stiffness and heavy footfall.
  • Marched: Similar because it is a deliberate, emphatic walk; differs because marching is disciplined, rhythmic, and purposeful (military or protest), while stumping is clumsy, stiff, and comically indignant.
  • Stomped: Similar because it is a loud, emphatic, angered gait; differs because stomping is angry stamping, often performed to make a point or release rage, whereas stumping is the heavy clump of a walker on stubs, irritation rather than fury.
