- Old English - 'Heap' denotes both a physical pile of objects and a 'great number' - a crowd or troop of people, already stretching from mounds of matter toward the abstract idea of a large aggregate
- Middle English - The verb 'hepen' (to heap, pile up, gather into a mass) arises beside the noun, giving the past participle 'heaped'
- Later Middle English to modern - 'Heaped' describes anything gathered into a high, abundant, conspicuously unordered mass: a heaped spoonful, heaped plates, heaped coals
- Modern figurative use - Freely takes metaphorical objects: heaped praise, abuse, or criticism poured out in generous abundance; register is informal-to-neutral, typically approving of generosity or wryly amused by excess
