• echo: Similar because both describe sound returning from a surface; differs because echo names the return itself and can be a single repetition, while resound implies a space filled with loud, continuing sound and often the public impact of that sound.
  • reverberate: Similar because both involve sound bouncing repeatedly in an enclosed space; differs because reverberate is the technical, physical term for multiple reflections and is neutral in tone, whereas resound is literary and carries impressions of grandeur or publicity.
  • ring: Similar because a hall can ring with laughter just as it resounds with laughter; differs because ring is the plainer, more conversational verb and can describe a single sharp sound (a bell ringing), while resound always suggests volume and a large containing space.
  • resonate: Similar because both descend from Latin resonare and describe sound continuing in a space; differs because resonate is now chiefly technical (sympathetic vibration) or psychological (“the idea resonated with voters”), while resound remains acoustic and rhetorical.
  • peal: Similar because both are used of bells and of loud bursts of sound; differs because peal is a sudden, ringing succession, usually of bells or thunder or laughter, while resound describes the filling of a space rather than the burst itself.
  • thunder: Similar because both convey a deep, carrying noise that fills a place; differs because thunder is a specific natural sound and, as a verb, often means to move with noisy force, whereas resound is a general acoustic predicate that needs a sound as its subject or complement.
  • boom: Similar because both suggest a low, powerful sound filling the air; differs because boom stresses a single deep report and economic or figurative expansion, while resound stresses continuation and echo.
  • be celebrated: Similar because the archaic sense of resound is “to be much spoken of, to be famous”; differs because be celebrated is abstract and modern, while the resounding sense depends on the physical metaphor of a name filling a country with sound.
  • re-sound: Similar because it is spelled almost identically and also involves sound; differs because it means “to sound again” (a bell re-sounded), is pronounced /ˌriːˈsaʊnd/, and is a modern prefixation rather than the inherited verb.
