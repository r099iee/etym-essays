• Bole: Similar because both name the main woody stem of a tree; differs because bole is the literary, poetic word for the thick lower column of a tree from ground to first branches, warmer and more antique in tone than the plain, practical trunk.
  • Stem: Similar because both denote the above-ground axis of a plant; differs because stem in ordinary speech suggests the slender support of herbs and shrubs, while trunk names the massive central stem of a tree.
  • Stalk: Similar because both are plant supports; differs because a stalk is the slighter support of leaves, flowers, or fruit, and is never the main woody stem of a tree.
  • Stump: Similar because both are parts of a tree's main axis; differs because a stump is what remains after felling or lopping — a truncated trunk, not a standing tree-stem.
  • Stock: Similar because both are parts of a tree; differs because stock is the lower rooted part from which new growth springs, while trunk is the standing main stem between roots and crown.
  • Torso: Similar because both can name the main body of a person; differs because torso is the technical anatomical word, while trunk in the body sense is older and more general, and is now chiefly the tree and luggage word.
  • Chest / Box: Similar because both are storage containers; differs because a trunk is the largest and sturdiest travel box, originally hollowed from a tree-trunk, while a chest or box is a general container of any size.
  • Proboscis: Similar because both name the elephant's nose; differs because proboscis is the formal zoological term for any tubular feeding or sensing organ, while trunk is the ordinary, homely word for the elephant's.
