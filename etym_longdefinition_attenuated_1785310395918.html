ATTENUATED is the past participle of the verb attenuate, used here as an adjective meaning “reduced in force, intensity, effect, magnitude, or thickness — made thin, weak, or diluted without being destroyed.” The word describes a graded loss: something attenuated still exists, still retains its essential character and function, but operates, acts, or presents itself at diminished strength. In the neuro-cognitive context of the card — “This neuro-cognitive characteristic describes an attenuated capacity of the brain to automatically filter out familiar or previously irrelevant sensory stimuli from conscious awareness” — the adjective qualifies the brain’s sensory-gating mechanism, the system that normally filters familiar or previously irrelevant stimuli out of conscious awareness. An “attenuated capacity” is thus a filtering ability that still functions but at reduced power: routine, familiar sensory input is no longer held back as effectively as before and may intrude upon attention. The word names the failure as one of degree, not of kind — the gate is weakened, not broken.

Polysemy and the full range of senses.

  • Neuro-cognitive / psychological sense (the card’s sense): a weakened or diminished response, capacity, or processing — as in “attenuated sensory gating,” “attenuated neural response,” or “attenuated attention” — where the nervous system processes a stimulus at reduced strength.
  • Immunology and vaccinology: an “attenuated vaccine” or “attenuated virus” is a pathogen that has been weakened in the laboratory (by repeated passage, temperature adaptation, or genetic modification) so that it provokes immunity without causing full disease; the classical attenuated vaccines are those for measles, mumps, rubella, and oral polio.
  • Physics, acoustics, and engineering: attenuation is the reduction in amplitude, power, or intensity of a signal, wave, or beam as it travels through a medium — sound muted by distance or fog, an electrical signal weakening along a cable, X-rays absorbed by tissue. An “attenuated signal” is one whose strength has dropped measurably.
  • Microbiology and medicine: an attenuated micro-organism is one whose virulence has been reduced; attenuated strains are also used in research. In clinical language a patient’s “attenuated response” may mean a blunted or dampened physiological reaction, and an “attenuated form” of a condition is a milder variant of it.
  • Rhetoric and general figurative use: an “attenuated argument” is one whose force has been weakened or thinned out; an “attenuated duty” or “attenuated obligation” is one diluted to the point of near-vacuousness; “attenuated” here always implies loss of substance or strength while something recognisable remains.
  • Physical thinning (the oldest sense): to attenuate a body was to make it thin or lean; “attenuated limbs” suggests drawn-out slenderness, and in technical description a wire, fibre, or layer may be “attenuated” in the sense of drawn thin or tapering.

The historical bridge. The single semantic thread connecting all these senses is the Latin idea of thinning by drawing out. Attenuate is built on tenuis, “thin,” from the root meaning “stretched”; to attenuate something was literally to stretch it thin, to reduce its substance without annihilating it. From physical thinning, the word was extended (by the seventeenth century) to any reduction of force or importance — arguments, duties, sounds, and bodies could all be “attenuated.” The modern scientific senses then annexed the word as a precise technical term: physics and engineering use attenuation for the measurable loss of signal amplitude; immunology uses it for the controlled weakening of a pathogen. In every case the word keeps its core logic — reduction of degree, with identity preserved — which is exactly the logic the neuro-cognitive sentence exploits when it describes a filtering capacity that is weakened but still present.

Grammar, register, and collocations. Attenuated is most commonly an adjective (an attenuated form) but is also the past participle of the transitive verb attenuate (the fog attenuated the sound) and may be passive (the signal was attenuated by the cable). Its technical collocations are dense: “attenuated vaccine,” “attenuated virus,” “attenuated live vaccine,” “attenuated signal,” “attenuated wave,” “attenuation coefficient,” “attenuated response,” “attenuated capacity,” “attenuated form.” The register is formal and scientific; the word is at home in immunology, physics, acoustics, engineering, psychology, and neuroscience, and in academic prose generally. It differs from “diluted” (which implies adding a foreign element), from “debilitated” (which implies bodily or structural breakdown), and from “mitigated” (which implies an active agent softening an outcome): attenuation is a measured, often quantitative reduction from within, a thinning that leaves the thing recognisably itself.
