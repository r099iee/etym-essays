- 16th Century (c. 1540–1580) - First attested in Tudor English (Turner's *Herbal*, 1548) with an agglutinated initial *d-* prefixed to the older Middle English *affodill*; designated the wild yellow *Narcissus pseudonarcissus* of woodlands and gardens.
- Late 16th to 17th Century - Celebrated in Elizabethan pastoral verse and plays (Spenser, Shakespeare's *The Winter's Tale*); colloquial diminutives like *daffadowndilly* flourish; Robert Herrick pens his famous lyric *To Daffodils* as a meditation on mortal transience.
- 18th to Early 19th Century - Cemented in Romantic aesthetics by William Wordsworth's 1804 poem *I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud*; becomes the ultimate poetic archetype of pure natural joy, vernal rebirth, and spontaneous dance.
- Late 19th to 20th Century - Adopted alongside the traditional leek as the premier national floral emblem of Wales, celebrated on Saint David's Day; widely incorporated into Oscar Wilde's pastoral fairy tales.
- Mid-20th Century to Present - Reimagined globally as the universal philanthropic badge of hope, resilience, and survivorship by cancer research organizations (such as the Great Daffodil Appeal and Daffodil Days).
