• Lake-town: Similar because it is the ordinary English name for the very same place — Esgaroth is the Elvish name for the Lake-town; differs because it is a descriptive common-noun phrase rather than a proper name and carries none of the Elvish, archaic colouring of Esgaroth.
  • mere: Similar because both denote still water in a lowland setting, and Esgaroth's second element means “pool, mere”; differs because a mere is the body of water itself, whereas Esgaroth is a town built upon such water.
  • lagoon: Similar because both evoke shallow, sheltered water near a shore where a pile-settlement could stand; differs because lagoon is a geographical term for a shallow coastal or lake water-body, not a settlement, and has no connection to Tolkien's world.
  • reed-bed / reeds: Similar because the name's first element esgar means “reed”, making Esgaroth literally the “reed-lake”; differs because reeds are plants, not places, and the link is etymological only.
  • crannog: Similar because a crannog is a real-world dwelling built on an artificial island or on stilts within a lake — exactly the construction type of Esgaroth; differs because crannogs are small fortified homesteads of Iron-Age Scotland and Ireland, whereas Esgaroth is a large mercantile town of fiction.
  • pile-dwelling / stilt-town: Similar because both denote settlements raised on posts above water, the defining architecture of Esgaroth; differs because these are generic archaeological and architectural terms with no proper-name status and no narrative meaning.
