• Scanty: Similar in meaning barely sufficient or less than sufficient in amount; differs because scanty stresses skimpy, barely-there quantity with an apologetic colour, while scant carries a more measured, consciously rationed insufficiency.
  • Sparse: Similar in being small in amount; differs because sparse describes things thinly scattered over a space — sparse hair, a sparse population — while scant describes a shortfall in quantity or measure.
  • Meagre: Similar in being small and insufficient; differs because meagre suggests thinness and poverty, often of food or income, with a note of pity, while scant is drier and more formal.
  • Minimal: Similar in being very small in amount; differs because minimal is the neutral, technical word for the least possible amount, while scant implies that the amount falls short of what is wanted.
  • Limited: Similar in being restricted in amount; differs because limited states a bound without emotional colour, while scant carries the sense of a deliberate or regretted shortfall.
  • Skimpy: Similar in being barely enough; differs because skimpy is colloquial and mildly dismissive — a skimpy outfit, a skimpy portion — while scant is formal and measured.
  • Insufficient: Similar in being less than needed; differs because insufficient is a plain, factual statement of shortfall, whereas scant adds the connotation of being deliberately or noticeably kept short.
  • Deficient: Similar in lacking the required amount; differs because deficient suggests a lack relative to a standard — a deficient diet — while scant describes a small portion relative to expectation.
  • Slight: Similar in being small; differs because slight is a general word for smallness of degree — a slight chance — without the 'insufficiency' that scant insists on.
  • Negligible: Similar in being very small; differs because negligible stresses that the amount is too small to matter, while scant, though small, is emphatically noticed.
  • Scarce: Similar in being in short supply; differs because scarce means hard to find or obtain — scarce water, scarce resources — while scant means little in amount, not difficult to obtain.
  • Short: Similar in being less than the full amount; differs because short is the plain, blunt word for any shortfall, while scant is the slightly formal word for a portion deliberately kept brief.
