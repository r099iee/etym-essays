- 13th-14th centuries - 'Troter' enters English from Old French as the horseman's word for the brisk four-beat gait between walk and gallop; to trot is to ride a horse moving at this easy, steady pace.
- 15th-18th centuries - The word extends to people: a person trots when moving with short, quick, steady steps, and 'trot' broadens into busy routine ('on the trot'), keeping its industrious, rhythmic character.
- Contemporary sense - Trotting still means moving in short, rapid, regular steps, half-walk and half-run; it connotes efficient, steady progress under pressure rather than elegant or heroic motion.
