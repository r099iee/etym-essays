• Jasper Johns: Similar because he is the paired Pop / Neo-Dada painter of flags, targets, numbers and alphabet paintings cited with Oldenburg; differs because Johns is painterly, encaustic, restrained and semi-abstract, while Oldenburg is sculptural, soft, theatrical and overtly comic.
  • Andy Warhol: Similar because he is the central Pop artist turning mass information (soup cans, headlines, photos) into art; differs because Warhol is cool, mechanical, silkscreened repetition, while Oldenburg is handmade, tactile, and monumental-comic.
  • Roy Lichtenstein: Similar because he depicts printed information (comic dots, speech bubbles) as painting; differs because Lichtenstein stays flat and graphic, while Oldenburg makes information three-dimensional and floppy.
  • Robert Rauschenberg: Similar because he combines painting with objects and news images to depict information overload; differs because Rauschenberg is collagist and painterly, while Oldenburg isolates single objects at giant scale.
  • Wayne Thiebaud: Similar because he paints everyday food and goods with Pop warmth; differs because Thiebaud remains illusionistic painter, while Oldenburg builds literal soft objects.
  • Joseph Beuys: Similar because he uses humble materials and multiples with social theory; differs because Beuys is shamanic German Fluxus, while Oldenburg is playful American Pop.
  • Oldenburg (city): Similar because it is the identical spelling and source of the surname; differs because it denotes a Lower Saxon city and former grand duchy, not the artist.
  • House of Oldenburg: Similar because it is the dynastic family bearing the name that ruled Denmark, Norway, Greece and Russia; differs because it is a royal lineage attested from 1108, not a 20th-c. artist.
  • Oldenburger (horse): Similar because it is named for the same region and is a famous heavy sport horse; differs because it is an animal breed, not a person or artwork.
  • Concrete poet / information artist: Similar in function — artists who depict language as visual information (Emmett Williams, Jenny Holzer); differs because they work primarily with text layouts and LEDs, while Oldenburg makes letters sculptural bodies.
