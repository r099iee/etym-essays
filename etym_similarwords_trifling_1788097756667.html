• Trivial: Similar as the standard synonym for “of little importance”; differs because trivial is neutral and common, while trifling is a touch more literary and can specifically mean “very small in amount”.
  • Petty: Similar in smallness; differs because petty implies meanness or small-mindedness (petty jealousy), whereas trifling implies worthlessness of value, not of spirit.
  • Paltry: Similar in contemptible smallness; differs because paltry is stronger in contempt and mostly quantitative (a paltry sum), while trifling also covers matters, acts, and the old “toying” sense.
  • Negligible: Similar in being too small to matter; differs because negligible is technical and quantitative, while trifling is qualitative and human.
  • Insignificant / Inconsequential: Similar in unimportance; differs because these are neutral and serious words, whereas trifling carries a light dismissive colour and the verbal “trifling with” sense.
  • Minor: Similar in small degree; differs because minor is a rank-order word with no dismissiveness and no history of mockery.
  • Idle: Similar in frivolous, wasted activity (idle talk, idle hours); differs because idle is about inactivity and time-passing, never about value.
  • Frivolous: Similar in not being serious; differs because frivolous implies gaiety and irresponsibility, while trifling implies worthlessness or mockery.
  • Toying / Dallying: Similar to “trifling with” as verbs of playful, unserious handling; differs because these are purely verbs of play, with no “worthless” branch and a lighter, sexier register.
  • Fooling: Similar in deceiving or making sport of someone (the old sense of trifle); differs because fooling is playful and informal, while “trifling with” is formal and reproachful.
  • Nugatory: Similar in being of no value or effect; differs as a formal, juridical register word that never means “toying with”.
  • Measly / Picayune: Similar in contemptible smallness; differs in register — measly is colloquial, picayune chiefly American — and neither has the verbal sense.
