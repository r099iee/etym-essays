• Retainers: Similar because both are household or personal followers of a powerful figure; differs because retainers can be paid staff without criminal implication, while henchmen imply moral complicity and readiness for dirty work.
  • Thugs: Similar because both are ready for violence; differs because thugs foreground street brutality and lose the bond of personal loyalty to a particular master.
  • Goons: Similar because both are hired or loyal enforcers; differs because goons are blunter and more generic, often implying hired muscle for hire, without the lord-and-retinue structure of henchmen.
  • Minions: Similar because both are subservient followers; differs because minions stress low status and eagerness to please rather than the physical enforcement role.
  • Lackeys: Similar because both are servile underlings of a powerful figure; differs because lackeys emphasize servility, obsequiousness, and menial running-around, not violent enforcement.
  • Bodyguards: Similar because both serve a principal at close quarters; differs because bodyguards defend protectively, whereas henchmen act offensively as an assault squad.
  • Accomplices: Similar because both share in guilt; differs because accomplices name shared criminal responsibility without the boss-and-gang hierarchy the context needs.
  • Underlings / subordinates: Similar because both name people below a chief; differs because these are neutral terms of rank that carry no implication of loyalty, crime, or violence.
