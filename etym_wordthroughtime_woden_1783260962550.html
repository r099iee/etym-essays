- Anglo-Saxon pagan England - Woden was the supreme god of the old English pantheon, a deity of war, wisdom, poetry, magic, and the dead, the counterpart of Norse Odin, and his name stood at the head of the royal genealogies of several Anglo-Saxon dynasties.
- Christian conversion - after the conversion of England, Woden was diminished into a heathen idol, demonized in sermons and chronicles, and survived mainly as a fossil in the weekday name Wednesday.
- 18th-19th-century revival - antiquarian and romantic scholarship resurrected Woden as the emblem of a lost Germanic paganism, and Macaulay could invoke his temples as the badge of stubborn, savage anachronism against Christian Europe.
- Contemporary sense - Woden is the English name of the god now better known as Odin, kept alive by the weekday Wednesday and by the popular re-imagining of Norse mythology.
