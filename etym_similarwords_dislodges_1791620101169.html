• Displaces: Similar because both involve moving something out of its customary or assigned location; differs because displaces often implies a systematic, gradual, or volumetric taking of another's space (such as a body displacing water or refugees displaced by famine), whereas dislodges emphasizes overcoming resistance to forcefully knock something out of a wedged or entrenched position.
  • Ousts: Similar because both denote expelling someone from a position of control; differs because ousts is predominantly political, legal, and social (such as ousting a dictator or corporate director through vote or legal challenge), while dislodges applies equally to physical rocks, anatomical clots, and cognitive faculties.
  • Unseats: Similar because both describe knocking someone out of a position of authority; differs because unseats evokes the specific metaphor of throwing a rider from the saddle or defeating an elected parliamentary representative at the polls, whereas dislodges carries a broader physical and mechanical application.
  • Uproots: Similar because both involve violent removal from a settled foundation; differs because uproots specifically implies tearing something out by its deep roots, destroying its sustaining foundation, while dislodges focuses on knocking an object free from its resting slot or shelter.
  • Ejects: Similar because both signify throwing or forcing something out; differs because ejects implies a rapid, outward thrust or expulsion from within an enclosed mechanism or chamber (such as an aircraft ejection seat or tape cassette), whereas dislodges highlights breaking the initial grip or wedged contact.
  • Dislocates: Similar because both involve moving a physical part out of its proper alignment; differs because dislocates is primarily anatomical or logistical, referring to throwing joints out of socket or disrupting the orderly functioning of a network, whereas dislodges focuses on liberating or expelling something from its resting place.
  • Supplants: Similar because both involve one entity taking the place of another; differs because supplants emphasizes the replacement and substitution of the old by the new (such as one technology superseding another), whereas dislodges concentrates on the forceful eviction itself, regardless of what replaces it.
