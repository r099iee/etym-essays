• Radcliffe-Brown (A.R. Radcliffe-Brown): Similar because both were the founding co-architects of modern British social anthropology who established its academic supremacy in the 20th century; differs because Radcliffe-Brown championed structural-functionalism (viewing society as an objective system of social structures maintaining equilibrium) and pursued abstract comparative sociological laws, whereas Malinowski advocated bio-cultural functionalism (centered on individual biological and psychological needs) and prioritized rich, experiential, immersive fieldwork.
  • Franz Boas: Similar because both are venerated as the primary founders of modern empirical, anti-racist anthropology who destroyed nineteenth-century armchair racial hierarchies; differs because Boas founded the American four-field tradition (cultural, linguistic, archaeological, physical) rooted in historical particularism and cultural relativism, whereas Malinowski established the British tradition of intensive single-society sociological immersion and functional analysis.
  • Claude Lévi-Strauss: Similar because both were monumental twentieth-century European theorists whose writings redefined the global study of myth, kinship, and human culture; differs because Lévi-Strauss founded structuralism, analyzing universal, unconscious mental structures and binary cognitive categories through literature and myth, whereas Malinowski was an unyielding empiricist focused on lived pragmatic reality, biological drives, and daily human action.
  • Margaret Mead: Similar because both were brilliant field ethnographers and gifted literary prose stylists whose South Pacific studies challenged Western orthodoxies regarding sexuality, adolescence, and human nature; differs because Mead was a cultural determinist of the Boasian school who conducted shorter field surveys across multiple cultures focusing on socialization, whereas Malinowski pioneered multi-year linguistic immersion in a single community.
  • Sir James Frazer: Similar because Frazer's The Golden Bough was the foundational literary spark that originally seduced Malinowski into abandoning physics for anthropology; differs because Frazer was the supreme archetype of the Victorian "armchair scholar" who never conducted fieldwork, compiling third-hand colonial anecdotes to construct unilinear evolutionary timelines that Malinowski's empirical method permanently discredited.
  • E.E. Evans-Pritchard: Similar because Evans-Pritchard was Malinowski's most brilliant student at the London School of Economics, conducting legendary immersion fieldwork among the Azande and Nuer; differs because Evans-Pritchard later rejected Malinowski's biological functionalism, re-envisioning social anthropology not as a natural science of human needs, but as a humanistic art of translation, symbolism, and historical meaning.
