- [Old French 'sailor'] - In its current form since Middle English (from Old French marinier, from Latin mare 'sea'), 'mariner' has meant a sailor or seafarer, but always with an elevated, literary flavour.
- [Heroic and mercantile register] - Through the age of sail it named navigators of a grand, heroic or mercantile kind - merchant captains, ancient voyagers, the men of the Rime of the Ancient Mariner - never the plain 'sailor'.
- [19th and 20th century poetry] - In 19th-century prose and verse the word kept its archaic dignity, evoking seamanship of the grand and adventurous sort.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'mariner' is formal, literary or institutional (mariner's compass, the Mariners), a word for the seafarer of romance and history rather than the everyday rating.
