- [Classical Roman Antiquity / 3rd c. BCE–6th c. CE] - Functions in Roman jurisprudence (*stipulārī* / *stipulatio*) as the foundational verbal contract under the *ius civile*, formalized through a strict ritual question (*Spondesne?*) and answer (*Spondeo!*) - [connotation: sacred oral contract, immutable legal bond, ultimate civic solemnity]
- [Medieval Era / 11th–15th c.] - Preserved in medieval Latin legal charters, canon law treatises, and papal chancery documents to formulate obligatory contractual clauses and feudal pledges - [connotation: formal legal drafting, ecclesiastical and feudal authority]
- [Early Modern English / 1620s–1700s] - Naturalized into English from the Latin past participial stem *stipulat-*, initially restricted to specialized legal pleading, civil law courts, and international treaty negotiations - [connotation: formal juristic precision, international diplomacy, legally binding covenants]
- [18th–19th c. Enlightenment & Industrial Era] - Expands from specialized legal chambers into commercial trade, maritime shipping charters, and labor contracts - [connotation: professional business agreements, baseline transactional requirements]
- [20th c.–Present] - Enters everyday language, academic discourse, and informal negotiation, denoting any explicit insistence upon ground rules, prerequisites, or boundaries, while retaining an authoritative legalistic flavor - [connotation: asserting firm boundaries, setting clear baseline terms]
