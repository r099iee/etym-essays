• Helmet: Similar because both are protective coverings for the head; differs because helmet is the modern everyday word (from Old French, a diminutive of helme) while helm is now archaic, poetic, or deliberately old-fashioned in this sense.
  • Casque: Similar because both name metal head-armour; differs because casque (via Spanish/Italian casco) specifically denotes the close-fitting steel helmet of the 16th–17th centuries, a far narrower and more technical term.
  • Headpiece: Similar because both mean a covering for the head; differs because headpiece is informal and broad (any head covering), carrying none of helm's martial weight.
  • Bascinet / sallet / morion: Similar because all are helmets; differs because these are precise medieval/early-modern helmet types defined by their shape, while helm is the generic word for the whole class.
  • Rudder: Similar in the nautical sphere, where both are part of steering a vessel; differs because the rudder is the flat blade in the water that turns the ship, while the helm is the wheel, tiller, or steering station that controls the rudder.
  • Tiller: Similar because both are steering devices; differs because the tiller is the specific lever or bar attached to the rudder, whereas the helm is the whole steering apparatus or position.
  • Wheel: Similar because "take the wheel" matches "take the helm" as a steering-and-command metaphor; differs because the wheel is only the modern physical object, while the helm names the office and act of steering itself.
  • Command / control: Similar because "take the helm" means assume command; differs because these are abstract nouns of authority with no naval or armoured imagery attached.
