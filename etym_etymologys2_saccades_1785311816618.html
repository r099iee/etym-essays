SACCADES is a Romance loanword with a Semitic ultimate source; it has no Indo-European ancestry. The chain runs from the Semitic sack-word to the modern oculomotor noun. CHAIN — ‘sack / shake’ element: - Semitic saq (Hebrew saq, Phoenician saq) ‘sackcloth, coarse woven bag, mourning garment’ (1st millennium BCE; the ultimate source within Semitic is debated, but no PIE root is involved) → Greek σάκκος (sákkos) ‘sack, bag of coarse cloth’ (loanword from Semitic; attested in Greek from the classical period) → Latin saccus ‘sack, bag’ (loanword from Greek; attested from the 2nd–1st c. BCE) → Late/Vulgar Latin saccāre ‘to shake, jostle, toss; to thrust into a sack’ — a denominal verb formed on saccus (reconstructed for the late-antique register from Romance outcomes; not attested in standard classical texts) → Old French sachier / saquier ‘to pull, draw out, snatch, thrust violently’ (attested from the 9th–12th c.) → Middle French saccade ‘a sudden jerk, a sharp pull, a violent twitch’ — especially a tug on a horse’s reins to check or redirect it (attested from the 16th c.) → French saccade ‘the abrupt, jump-like movement of the eye during reading’ — technical coinage of the ophthalmologist Émile Javal, who described eye movements as saccades (1893) → English saccade (singular) / SACCADES (plural) ‘rapid, ballistic refixation movements of the eyes’ (borrowed from the French physiological literature in the 1890s; the compound micro-saccades follows in the 20th century) NOTE ON DEBATE: the earliest stages are partly reconstructed and partly debated. The link to Latin saccus is the standard account, but the intermediate verb saccāre is not directly attested in classical Latin and is reconstructed from Romance (French sacquer, Spanish sacudir, Italian scuotere); an alternative view derives the Old French verb from a distinct Germanic or substrate base. The sound change Latin -cc- → Old French -ch- (as in saccāre → sachier) is the expected Romance palatalisation, but its precise dating is uncertain. The transfer of the word from riding to ophthalmology is securely attributed to Émile Javal (1893), whose coinage then passed into English, German (Sakkade), and the international scientific vocabulary.
