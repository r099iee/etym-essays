• worried: similar because both mean anxiety; differs because worried is the general modern word, while fret is fussier, more repetitive and peevish — smaller-scale, nagging worry.
  • agonised: similar deep worry; differs because agonise is intense, prolonged suffering, while fret is petty and restless.
  • brooded: similar silent rumination; differs because brood is withdrawn and sullen, while fret is restless and vocal.
  • stewed: similar internal worry (stew over); differs because stew is slow, simmering resentment, while fret is active nervous fuss.
  • fussed: similar petty nervous activity; differs because fuss is behaviour (bustling, tidying, clucking), while fret is a mental-emotional state.
  • fumed: similar agitated anger/worry; differs because fume is angry (like smoke rising), while fret is anxious rather than wrathful.
  • chafed: similar the physical rubbing sense; differs because chafe is purely physical friction (or mild irritation), while fret-as-chafe is the older, more literary word for the same corrosion.
  • eroded: similar gradual wearing away; differs because erode is geological and technical, while fret is the older poetic word for the same process.
  • corroded: similar eating away; differs because corrode is chemical and technical, while fret is the homely devouring image.
  • gnawed: similar eating away at; differs because gnaw is literal animal-teeth action, while fret-as-gnaw is metaphorical corrosion or mental worry.
  • creaked: similar the Tolkien sense (of objects groaning); differs because creak is pure sound, while fret adds the animate fretfulness — wood that worries.
  • groaned: similar complaint sounds; differs because groan is a human/animal voice, while fret-of-wood is an animate metaphor.
  • complained: similar voicing discontent; differs because complain is deliberate human speech, while fret can be wordless restlessness of people or things.
