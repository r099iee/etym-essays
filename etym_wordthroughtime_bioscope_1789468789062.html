- Late 19th Century (1890s) - Coined as a cutting-edge technological trademark and scientific apparatus for projecting animate photographic life onto a screen, greeted with technological wonder and curiosity.
- Edwardian Era to 1910s - Shifted from an experimental novelty to the standard British, European, and colonial trade term for travelling motion-picture shows, projection apparatuses, and early cinema halls, carrying lively popular-entertainment connotations.
- Mid-to-Late 20th Century - Largely superseded by "cinema" and "movies" in standard British and American English, but firmly entrenched as the everyday vernacular term for a movie theatre in South African English and Indian English ("the bio"), evoking nostalgia and local cultural identity.
- 21st Century / Contemporary - Primarily an archaic, historical, and nostalgic term in global English, celebrated in film history and media archaeology as an iconic monument of early moving-image technology.
