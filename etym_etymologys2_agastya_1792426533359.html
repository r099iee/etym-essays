Note on historical and philological debate: Classical Indian grammarians and lexicographers (including the Nirukta and Pāṇinian scholiasts) derive Agastya as a tatpuruṣa compound: aga- ("mountain", literally "that which does not go", from a- privative + ga- "going") + -stya (a reduced agentive form from the root sthā- "to stand, check, stay"), interpreting the name as "the mountain-stiller" or "he who humbled and arrested the Vindhya mountains". However, modern comparative philologists (such as Manfred Mayrhofer in the Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindoarischen and F.B.J. Kuiper) note that personal names in -stya (e.g. Agastya, Pulastya) are morphologically archaic and may represent an ancient non-IE substrate borrowing adapted into Vedic phonology, or an archaic derivative of root aj- ("to drive, throw"). Nevertheless, the classical consensus of "mountain-arrester" remains the universally celebrated semantic anchor throughout Sanskrit and Tamil literary traditions.
