• peaceful: Similar because both describe an absence of conflict, disturbance, or war; differs because peaceful is an everyday Anglo-Saxon-rooted compound describing an existing state of calm or non-aggression, whereas pacific carries an elevated, formal tone implying an active tendency toward peacemaking or an intrinsic non-violent character.
  • peaceable: Similar because both describe an inclination toward peace and aversion to quarreling; differs because peaceable typically describes the agreeable, non-confrontational personal disposition of individuals or neighbors, whereas pacific is frequently applied to diplomatic policies, historical epochs, and oceans.
  • placid: Similar because both describe an undisturbed, tranquil condition; differs because placid emphasizes emotional calmness, unruffled surface stillness, or mild complacency (often suggesting a lack of strong feeling or reaction), whereas pacific focuses specifically on the absence of hostility, war, or discord.
  • serene: Similar because both evoke profound tranquility; differs because serene emphasizes radiant, clear, unclouded majesty and inner spiritual or meteorological clarity (from Latin serēnus, clear sky), whereas pacific denotes the active preservation or character of peace.
  • tranquil: Similar because both describe freedom from disturbance or agitation; differs because tranquil conveys a quiet, restful stillness of the environment, mind, or body, lacking the diplomatic, political, or moral dimensions of pacific.
  • conciliatory: Similar because both aim at avoiding or resolving conflict; differs because conciliatory specifically describes actions, gestures, or words intended to appease an offended party, disarm anger, or rebuild broken trust, whereas pacific denotes a general ongoing condition or character of peace.
  • appeasing: Similar because both seek to prevent war or conflict; differs because appeasing carries a strong modern connotation of compromising principles, making concessions, or placating an aggressive bully from a position of weakness, whereas pacific remains neutral and dignified.
  • irenic (eirenic): Similar because both relate to the promotion of peace; differs because irenic (from Greek eirēnē, peace) is a rare, scholarly term applied almost exclusively to theological discourse aimed at reconciling doctrinal differences and healing ecclesiastical schisms.
  • halcyon: Similar because both describe an idyllic state of peace and calm; differs because halcyon (derived from the myth of the kingfisher) is poetic and nostalgic, almost always applied to a remembered golden age or past season of unblemished prosperity and happiness ("halcyon days").
  • nonbelligerent: Similar because both describe not engaging in military hostility; differs because nonbelligerent is a technical, formal legal designation for a nation that does not participate in a war but may still provide political or material support to one side without declaring war.
