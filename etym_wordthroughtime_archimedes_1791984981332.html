- Antiquity — Archimedes is a Syracusan engineer and geometer known through Plutarch, Polybius and Cicero; the bath, the lever and the siege engines circulate as anecdotes before his treatises are widely read.
- Middle Ages — The name survives in Latin translations of a few works, above all On the Measurement of the Circle, and “Archimedes” becomes an authority on weights and water alongside Euclid.
- 16th–17th c. — The Basel edition of 1544, then Galileo and Stevin, turn the name into shorthand for mathematical physics; to say “Archimedes” is to invoke proof joined to experiment.
- 18th–19th c. — Schoolbooks fix the bathtub and the lever as moral lessons about discovery, and the name becomes a generic compliment for a prodigy.
- 20th–21st c. — The palimpsest, the eponymous principles and a wave of popular science make the name a brand: genius, antiquity and applied mathematics in one word.
