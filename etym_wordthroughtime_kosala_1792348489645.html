- [Late Vedic Era (c. 900–600 BCE)] — emerged in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa as an eastern frontier realm settled by Indo-Aryan clans crossing from the Sarasvatī, viewed by western Kuru-Pāñcāla orthodoxy as a rapidly developing center of Vedic ritualism
- [Epic & Classical Era (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)] — exalted in the Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata into the supreme earthly embodiment of righteous monarchical rule (Rāmarājya), dynastic honor, and golden-age prosperity under the Solar Dynasty
- [Historical Buddhist & Jain Era (c. 6th–3rd c. BCE)] — historical Mahājanapada ruled from Śrāvastī by King Prasenajit, famed as an intellectual haven where the Buddha and Mahāvīra delivered major sermons and engaged wealthy merchant patrons
- [Imperial Magadhan & Classical Hindu Era (c. 4th c. BCE–6th c. CE)] — absorbed politically into the Mauryan and Gupta empires, while preserving its identity as an indispensable sacred pilgrimage landscape centered on Ayodhyā
- [Early Medieval Epigraphic Era (c. 6th–12th c. CE)] — toponym migrated south of the Vindhyas, giving birth to Dakṣiṇa Kosala (South Kosala) in Chhattisgarh and western Odisha as an independent kingdom ruled by the Somavaṁśī and Kalacuri dynasties
- [Modern Philological & Regional Era (19th–21st c.)] — excavated and identified archaeologically at Ayodhyā and Sahet-Mahet, while serving as a modern linguistic and cultural emblem for Awadhi (Kausalī) and Sambalpuri (Kosli) regional heritage
