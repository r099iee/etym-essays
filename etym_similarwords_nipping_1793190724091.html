• Pinching: Similar because both name a small sharp squeeze of flesh between hard edges; differs because pinching stresses fingers and prolonged pressure, while nipping stresses speed, teeth or claws, and repetition.
  • Biting: Similar because both use teeth to seize flesh; differs because biting is the general and often wounding act, while nipping is its light miniature, playful or warning rather than injuring.
  • Snapping: Similar because both are quick closing bites, as of dogs or turtles; differs because snapping stresses the sudden jaw-shut sound and temper, while nipping stresses the small repeated pinch itself.
  • Stinging: Similar because both describe sharp skin pain from cold, insects, or nettles; differs because stinging names the sensation left behind, while nipping names the pincer-like action causing it.
  • Tingling: Similar because nipping frost and tingling cheeks belong to the same cold-weather feeling; differs because tingling is purely the prickling sensation, while nipping implies an outside agent doing the pinching.
  • Darting: Similar because nipping out and darting out both mean quick small movements; differs because darting stresses arrow-like speed and direction, while nipping adds domestic briskness and the errand frame.
  • Hurrying: Similar because both describe brisk going; differs because hurrying is general haste under pressure, while nipping is cheerful short-distance popping in and out.
  • Snatching: Similar because both seize quickly; differs because snatching stresses grabbing hold of a thing, while nipping stresses the smallness of the bite or the trip.
  • Sipping: Similar because nipping whisky and sipping whisky both take liquor in small mouthfuls; differs because sipping is delicate and neutral, while nipping suggests a stinging bite of spirit against the cold.
