• Flanders: Similar because both relate to the Dutch-speaking northern region of Belgium renowned for commerce and art; differs because Flanders is the entire historical principality and modern cultural region, whereas Antwerp is its largest, most prominent individual metropolis and province.
  • Bruges: Similar because both are famous historic Flemish commercial and artistic centers; differs because Bruges was the dominant textile and banking capital of the 14th century whose silting canal preserved its medieval architecture, whereas Antwerp surpassed Bruges in the 16th century to become a dynamic early modern global seaport and Baroque center.
  • Ghent: Similar because both are historic major Flemish cities; differs because Ghent was historically an industrial textile powerhouse and medieval manufacturing center, while Antwerp was an international maritime trading port, diamond capital, and financial exchange hub.
  • Amsterdam: Similar because both were preeminent trading ports and financial exchanges in the Low Countries; differs because Amsterdam rose to global primacy in the 17th century specifically after Protestant merchants fled Antwerp following the Spanish siege of 1585 and the Dutch blockade of the Scheldt.
  • Rotterdam: Similar because both are colossal modern container ports in the Rhine-Meuse-Scheldt delta; differs because Rotterdam is a predominantly modern, 20th-century reconstructed industrial port in the Netherlands, whereas Antwerp in Belgium possesses a rich 16th-century Renaissance, Baroque, and diamond heritage.
  • Brussels: Similar because both are major Belgian cities; differs because Brussels is the administrative, bilingual political capital of Belgium and the European Union, whereas Antwerp is the economic, maritime, diamond, and Flemish cultural engine.
  • Venice: Similar because both were world-dominating maritime trading republics with brilliant artistic traditions; differs because Venice dominated Mediterranean and Levantine sea routes with a maritime empire of galleys, while Antwerp was an Atlantic-facing merchant hub connecting global oceanic empires with continental Europe.
  • Genoa: Similar because both were major European banking and port centers closely tied to the Spanish Empire; differs because Genoa was an Italian Mediterranean maritime republic whose bankers financed the Spanish Crown, whereas Antwerp was the physical northern emporium where northern commodities and American bullion were exchanged.
  • Naples: Similar because both were monumental European cities representing regional art schools; differs because Naples was the southern Italian, Mediterranean classical capital renowned for dramatic Caravaggesque tenebrism and ancient ruins, serving in Ruskin's critique as the southern pole to Antwerp's northern Flemish mastery.
