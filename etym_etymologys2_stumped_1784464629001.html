STUMPED is the past tense / past participle of the verb STUMP (to walk heavily), built on the noun STUMP. One linear chain. 1. Proto-Germanic *stumpaz “blunt, mutilated, cut short” (adjective; reconstructed at the Germanic level — no secure PIE equation is generally accepted; handbooks sometimes compare it speculatively to PIE *(s)teu- “to push, knock, strike”, the stock behind stow, stun, stupid, and Latin tundere “to beat”, but the link is not firmly established). 2. West Germanic noun stock: Middle Low German stump / Middle Dutch stomp “blunt, stump”, Old High German stumbal / Middle High German stum(p)f “mutilated, blunt, truncated” (the blunt-remainder image throughout). 3. Middle English stump “the truncated base of a tree, a stub, a remaining base” (noun; attested late 13th c.). 4. Modern English stump (noun) “the remaining base of a tree, limb, tooth, or broken object”; by the early 19th c. also the three posts of a cricket wicket. 5. English stump (verb, motion) “to walk heavily and clumsily, as on a wooden leg or on residual stumps” (attested c. 1600–1610); later “to baffle, nonplus” (19th c.). 6. English stumped (past tense / past participle) — the card's exact inflected form — STUMPED.
