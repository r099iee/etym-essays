PIE (Proto-Indo-European)
*kʷel- / *kʷelh₁-
"to turn, move round, revolve"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*hrēhulaz / *hrauhilaz
"reeling implement, turning winder for thread"
↓
Old English (c. 700–1100 CE)
hrēol
"a reel for winding yarn or thread, rotary spool"
↓
Middle English (c. 1150–1450)
rele / reel
"rotary yarn winder, spool; to whirl, turn round"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500–1700)
reel
"spool for thread; lively folk dance; to stagger, sway dizzily"
↓
Modern English (standard English)
reel
"spool, winder; film spool; folk dance; to stagger dizzily"

Note: The noun reel (the physical winder) is the primary historical word. The verb to reel ("to stagger, sway from dizziness") and the dance reel (Scottish Gaelic righil) are sixteenth-century semantic derivations directly branching from the rapid whirling rotation of the textile spinning winder.
