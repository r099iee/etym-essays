• Gateway: Similar because both name an opening through a wall or enclosure that admits passage; differs because a gateway implies a gate and a controlling or defensive function, while an archway is the arch-shaped opening itself, with or without anything to shut.
  • Portal: Similar because both mean an entrance; differs because portal is grand, ceremonial, and often ornate (palaces, cathedrals), while archway is plainer and can describe a railway bridge or a garden rose-arch.
  • Doorway: Similar because both are openings in walls; differs because a doorway is domestic, small, and centred on its door, while an archway is defined by its curved top and need not have a door at all.
  • Passageway: Similar because both provide a route through something; differs because passageway names the corridor as a whole (any shape, often long and roofed), while archway names the arched opening specifically.
  • Arcade: Similar because both involve arches; differs because an arcade is a row or series of arches forming a gallery or covered walk, while an archway is a single arched passage.
  • Tunnel: Similar because both are passages through obstructions; differs because a tunnel is long, enclosed, and usually cut through earth or rock, while an archway is short, open-ended, and part of a wall or bridge.
  • Vault: Similar because both are curved structures; differs because a vault is an arched ceiling or chamber enclosing space from above, while an archway is an opening one passes through; vaults enclose, archways admit.
  • Porch / Portico: Similar because both can shelter an entrance at a wall; differs because a porch or portico is a roofed annex projecting in front of a door, while an archway is the opening in the wall itself.
  • Entrance / Entry: Similar because both are points of access; differs because these are function words naming any way in, while archway specifies the form (arched) of the opening.
  • Gate: Similar because both control passage through a boundary; differs because a gate is the movable barrier (hinged or sliding), while an archway is the structure of the opening; a gate can hang in an archway.
  • Threshold: Similar because both mark the crossing point of a boundary; differs because a threshold is the line or sill at the foot of a doorway (and figuratively the moment of change), while an archway is the whole arched opening.
