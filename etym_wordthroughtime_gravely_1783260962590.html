- 16th-century entry - gravely, the adverb of grave (from Latin gravis, heavy, weighty), meant literally in a heavy, weighty manner, and so in a serious, solemn one.
- Formal and medical specialization - over the centuries the adverb settled into two strong modern collocational families: medical and disaster language (gravely ill, gravely injured) and moral or journalistic seriousness (gravely concerned, gravely mistaken).
- Macaulay's ironic use - in the 19th century Macaulay could say the able historian gravely related the supernatural marvels of the East, the word carrying formal earnestness on its surface while the context exposes the credulity of that gravity.
- Contemporary sense - gravely remains a formal adverb meaning with weighty seriousness, distinct from gravel (stones) and from grave the burial pit, despite the visual similarity.
