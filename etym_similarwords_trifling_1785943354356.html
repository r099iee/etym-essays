• Trivial: Similar because both mean lacking importance; differs because trivial comes from Latin trivium "a crossroads, a public place," hence "commonplace," and focuses on lack of significance, while trifling carries a livelier hint of worthlessness, silliness, or mockery.
  • Insignificant: Similar because both mean having no consequence; differs because insignificant is formal and neutral, while trifling is descriptive, conversational, and faintly contemptuous.
  • Negligible: Similar because both mean too small to matter; differs because negligible is a quantitative, scientific, and statistical word — something can be neglected in an equation — while trifling applies to human affairs, money, and feelings.
  • Petty: Similar because both mean small or unimportant; differs because petty adds a sense of small-mindedness or mean-spiritedness ("petty revenge," "petty squabbles"), while trifling is about smallness of value, not smallness of soul.
  • Frivolous: Similar because both carry lack of seriousness; differs because frivolous emphasizes silly, light-hearted, or irresponsible behavior (and baseless lawsuits in legal English), while trifling emphasizes smallness of value.
  • Piddling: Similar because both mean ridiculously small; differs because piddling is informal and contemptuous, almost comic, whereas trifling can be used neutrally.
  • Inconsequential: Similar because both mean without consequence; differs because inconsequential is abstract and intellectual, about effects and results, while trifling is concrete, about the small size of a thing or amount.
  • Nugatory: Similar because both mean of no worth; differs because nugatory is a rare, formal, bookish word for "worthless, futile," while trifling is common and flexible.
  • Minor: Similar because both mean small; differs because minor is a neutral, relative scale word (a minor detail, a minor key) without the dismissive, playful undertone of trifling.
  • Unimportant: Similar because both mean lacking importance; differs because unimportant is a plain, neutral word, while trifling adds texture — smallness, silliness, or contempt — and is more expressive in literary or colloquial use.
