- Roman law — recovering by proof: Latin evincere means to overcome at law, to recover property by proving the better title.
- 15th c. — English lawyers borrow it: evict enters Scots and English as a term of art for recovering land by judgement.
- 17th–18th c. — the landlord's word: enclosures and estate clearances make eviction the engine of rural change, feared from the Highlands to Ireland.
- 19th c. — the novelist's tragedy: eviction scenes in Dickens, Gaskell, and the Irish famine press fix the evicted family on the roadside as an emblem of injustice.
- 20th c. — metaphor spreads it: games, offices, and television houses all gain evictions, while rent-control law makes the legal sense technical and procedural.
- Today — property plus metaphor: the courtroom sense rules the headlines (arrears, eviction bans), while computing quietly evicts pages from caches a billion times a day.
