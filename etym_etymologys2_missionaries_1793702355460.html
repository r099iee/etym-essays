Latin (c. 1st-2nd cent. CE)
mittere
"to send, let go, dispatch"
[Root of many English words related to sending, e.g., transmit, admit]
↓
Latin (c. 4th cent. CE)
missio
"a sending, a dispatching; a release, a dismissal"
[From past participle stem of 'mittere', 'miss-']
↓
Late Latin (c. 16th cent.)
missionarius
"one sent on a mission"
[Coined by Jesuits for members sent to evangelize]
↓
French (c. 17th cent.)
missionnaire
"missionary"
[Direct borrowing from Late Latin 'missionarius']
↓
English (c. 1600s)
missionary
"a person sent on a religious mission"
[First attested in English in early 17th century]
↓
English (Modern)
missionaries
"plural of missionary"
