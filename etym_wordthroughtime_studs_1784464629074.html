“Stud” entered English with several strands — a post or support, a horse kept for breeding — words of sturdy upright things.
“Stud” from the fastening sense came to name the small knob or fastener: the decorative button-like device with a shank that passes through an eyelet to lock — collar studs, shirt studs, cuff studs.
“Stud” in the 19th century became the gentleman’s jewellery-word — metal or jewelled fasteners for shirts and collars, small precious hardware of formal dress.
“Studs” today keeps the fastener sense: the removable decorative studs that secure a shirt front or collar in place of sewn buttons — and, magically upgraded, self-fastening diamond studs that never come undone till ordered.
