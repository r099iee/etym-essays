• ambiguity: Similar because both name a failure of precision; differs because ambiguity is the property of having two or more meanings (a property of statements), while vagueness is the property of lacking sharp outline or definition anywhere — in perception, memory, or meaning alike.
  • imprecision: Similar because both name a lack of exactness; differs because imprecision is a neutral, technical term for a failure of measurement or wording, while vagueness adds the image of something that drifts and will not hold still.
  • obscurity: Similar because both name a failure to be clearly seen or understood; differs because obscurity implies concealment in darkness or difficulty, while vagueness implies indefinite outline rather than hiddenness.
  • indistinctness: Similar because both name a failure to be clearly perceived; differs because indistinctness is a sensory failure (a blurred sound or sight), while vagueness covers mental and semantic indefiniteness too.
  • uncertainty: Similar because both name a state of doubt; differs because uncertainty is about the truth of a claim or the outcome of events, while vagueness is about the sharpness of an outline or meaning.
  • haziness: Similar because both name indistinct perception; differs because haziness suggests mist or obstruction in the medium, while vagueness suggests the object itself refuses to settle.
  • fuzziness: Similar because both name soft, blurred indistinctness; differs because fuzziness is colloquial and suggests a woolly edge, while vagueness is the more neutral, intellectual term.
  • nebulousness: Similar because both name cloudiness of outline or idea; differs because nebulousness is rarer, more literary, and literally means "cloudy" (nebulosus), while vagueness keeps its own image of wandering.
  • indefiniteness: Similar because both name a lack of fixed limits; differs because indefiniteness is chiefly a failure of measure or extent, while vagueness is a failure of definition and precision.
  • generality: Similar because both can mean not going into detail; differs because generality is a deliberate, acceptable breadth of statement, while vagueness is usually a reproach.
  • looseness: Similar because both name a lack of tight definition; differs because looseness applies to reasoning and language as "careless," while vagueness applies to outlines, memories, and perceptions as "unfixed."
  • luminous: Similar because both mean full of or giving off light; differs because luminous is the broad, standard word for any emission or reflection of light, while lucent is rarer, more poetic, and stresses a soft brilliance that diffuses edges.
  • lucid: Similar because both come from the Latin light-family (lux); differs because lucid developed the intellectual sense "clear, easily understood," while lucent remained purely luminous and never took the mental meaning.
  • radiant: Similar because both mean shining brightly; differs because radiant implies beams, strength, and direction, while lucent implies a soft, even, edge-dissolving glow.
  • brilliant: Similar because both mean intensely bright; differs because brilliant is standard and implies dazzling intensity, while lucent is literary and implies luminous softness.
  • gleaming: Similar because both mean giving off light; differs because gleaming implies reflected or intermittent light from a surface, while lucent implies light emitted or transmitted through a medium.
  • glowing: Similar because both mean emitting a steady light; differs because glowing is the plain everyday word, often of warmth (a glowing fire), while lucent is formal and purely luminous.
  • incandescent: Similar because both mean emitting light; differs because incandescent is technical, meaning light from extreme heat, while lucent is poetic light without any cause specified.
  • shining: Similar because both mean giving off or reflecting light; differs because shining is the simple, neutral word, while lucent is ornamental and suggests a diffused, hazy brilliance.
  • lambent: Similar because both name a soft, play of light; differs because lambent means light playing lightly over a surface (as a flame over oil), while lucent simply glows evenly, with no contact with a surface implied.
  • translucent: Similar because both concern light passing through a medium; differs because translucent is technical and describes the medium's property, while lucent is poetic and describes the light or glow itself.
  • effulgent: Similar because both mean shining forth brilliantly; differs because effulgent is grand and implies pouring radiance, while lucent is quieter and more diffused.
