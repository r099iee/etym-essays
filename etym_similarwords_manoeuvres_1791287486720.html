• Manoeuvres (US: maneuvers): Similar because identical meaning; differs because spelling only — oe British vs e American.
  • Tactics: Similar because both mean planned moves to win; differs because tactics stresses battlefield logic, manoeuvres stresses movement and handling.
  • Strategies: Similar because both mean high-level plans; differs because strategy is overall design, manoeuvres are the concrete moves.
  • Schemes: Similar because both mean secret plots; differs because schemes is more pejorative and underhand than neutral manoeuvres.
  • Intrigues: Similar because both mean plotting; differs because intrigues stresses romance or court conspiracy, not troop movement.
  • Ploys: Similar because both mean tricks to gain advantage; differs because a ploy is a single ruse, manoeuvres are extended operations.
  • Ruses: Similar because both mean deceptive moves; differs because ruses emphasizes deceit, manoeuvres can be honest skill.
  • Operations: Similar because both mean organised military actions; differs because operations is formal staff language, manoeuvres can be exercises or tricks.
  • Exercises: Similar because autumn manoeuvres are exercises; differs because exercises stresses training, manoeuvres stresses movement.
  • Evolutions: Similar because both mean formal troop movements; differs because evolutions is archaic drill language.
  • Stratagems: Similar because both mean artful tricks; differs because stratagem is literary and singularly cunning.
  • Wiles: Similar because both mean sly scheming; differs because wiles implies seductive cunning, not military science.
  • Deployments: Similar because both mean positioning forces; differs because deployment is logistical, manoeuvre is kinetic.
  • Feints: Similar because both mean deceptive moves; differs because a feint is a pretended attack, a manoeuvre may be real movement.
