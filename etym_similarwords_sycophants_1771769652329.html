• Flatterer: Similar because both give excessive praise; differs because flattery need not be instrumental or systemic — one may flatter casually — whereas a sycophant's praise serves a calculated advantage.
  • Toady: Similar because both denote servile fawning on the powerful; differs because toady is the colloquial peer, less classical and without the historical Athenian sting.
  • Yes-man: Similar because both denote someone subserviently agreeable; differs because yes-man emphasizes agreeing with everything a superior says, while sycophant emphasizes flattery and self-interest more broadly.
  • Bootlicker: Similar because both denote abject flattery of superiors; differs because bootlicker is slangy and contemptuous, while sycophant is formal and classical in tone.
  • Obsequious (person): Similar because both involve excessive deference; differs because obsequious names a manner or attitude of cringing politeness, not the role and motive of gaining through fawning.
  • Parasite: Similar because both live off a powerful host; differs because parasite emphasizes exploiting resources, while a sycophant's currency is verbal flattery and social service.
  • Lackey: Similar because both denote a servile attendant of the great; differs because lackey stresses menial service and following, less the art of flattery.
  • Minion: Similar because both denote a servile favourite; differs because minion emphasizes being a favoured subordinate or deputy, without the fawning-praise connotation.
  • Hanger-on: Similar because both denote someone clinging to the powerful for advantage; differs because hanger-on stresses parasitic association rather than active flattery.
  • Timeserver: Similar because both denote someone who adapts to power for advantage; differs because timeserver stresses trimming one's opinions to the prevailing wind, not specifically giving praise.
  • Informer / denouncer: Similar because both preserve the old Greek sense of the word's ancestor; differs because informer is the historical meaning of sykophantēs, now obsolete for the modern word, which means the flatterer.
  • Groveller: Similar because both denote self-abasing pursuit of favour; differs because groveller stresses the physical-metaphorical lowering of oneself rather than the strategic giving of praise.
