Compound Formation:
Early Modern English (c. 1630s–1640s)
back-gammon
"back-game (tables variant where pieces are sent back)"
↓
Modern English (standardized)

"two-player board race game"
Note: In the 18th century, antiquarians such as Thomas Pennant proposed that backgammon derived from Welsh bach ("little") + cammawn ("battle"). Philologists (including the OED and Skeat) have decisively rejected this Celtic derivation as fanciful; the compound is transparently Germanic, formed from native English back + gammon (an archaic northern and dialectal variant of game, from OE gamen). The "back" element most plausibly refers to the defining rule that struck pieces must re-enter from the back, or that pieces are borne back home.
