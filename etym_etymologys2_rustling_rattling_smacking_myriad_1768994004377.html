Root 1 — RUSTLING:

Echoic origin (18th c.; imitative)
rustle
"to make a soft, dry sound"
↓
Modern English (present participle)
rustling
"soft, dry, scratching sound"

Debate: rustle first appears in the 18th c., imitative of the sound of dry leaves or movement; no accepted PIE etymon. The later sense "to steal cattle" (19th c.) is probably a separate word.

Root 2 — RATTLING:

Middle English (14th c.)
ratelen
"to rattle"
(echoic; probably from Middle Dutch ratelen or Old Norse raðla)
↓
Modern English
rattle
"to make rapid short sharp sounds"
↓
Modern English (present participle)
rattling
"rapid series of short,
sharp sounds"

Debate: echoic/onomatopoeic; Middle English ratelen was probably borrowed from Middle Dutch ratelen or Old Norse raðla "to rattle".

Root 3 — SMACKING:

Echoic origin (Germanic; imitative)
smack
"to make a sharp sound with the
lips; to strike"
(related to Dutch smakken "to smack")
↓
Modern English (present participle)
smacking
"sharp, quick noise of lips striking"

Debate: imitative/echoic, from a Germanic root; related to Dutch smakken "to smack" and German schmatzen.

Root 4 — MYRIAD:

PIE (proposed; uncertain)
*muh₁-
"many"
(uncertain link)
↓
Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE)
μυρίος (myrios)
"countless, ten thousand"
↓
Ancient Greek (plural)
μυρίοι (myrioi)
"ten thousand"
↓
Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE)
μυριάς, μυριάδος (myrias, myriados)
"a myriad, ten thousand"
↓
Late Latin (gen. myriados)
myrias
"ten thousand, a myriad"
↓
English (16th c., plural)
myriads
"countless numbers"
↓
Modern English (16th c.)
myriad
"a countless or extremely
great number"

Debate: Greek μυρίος is of uncertain origin; the proposed PIE link *muh₁- "many" is speculative and not firmly established.
