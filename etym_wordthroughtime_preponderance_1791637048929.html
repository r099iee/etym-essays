- 1630s–1680s: Physical Mechanics and Gravitational Outweighing - Coined in Early Modern English natural philosophy to denote the literal, mechanical outbalancing of one side of a balance beam or scale over another.
- 1680s–1740s: Moral Philosophy and Decision Theory - Extended by philosophers like John Locke to describe the 'preponderance of reasons' or motives that tip the scales of human will and deliberate choice.
- Late 18th c.: Jurisprudence and the Standard of Proof - Codified in English common law as the foundational legal standard of proof in civil actions ('preponderance of evidence'), requiring a proposition to be more likely true than false.
- 19th c.: Geopolitics and Balance of Power - Integrated into international diplomacy and military strategy to analyze whether one superpower's preponderance of resources threatens the peace of Europe.
- 20th c. to Present: Gestalt Aesthetics and Statistical Demographics - Expanded into visual art theory (Arnheim) to describe the perceived visual weight of shapes, and into sociology and data science to denote numerical dominance in demographic populations.
