• Brunelleschi: Similar because Filippo Brunelleschi was Alberti's older contemporary in early Quattrocento Florence who originally invented linear perspective through empirical mirror experiments; differs because Brunelleschi was primarily a master builder and engineer who left no written treatises, whereas Alberti was a humanist scholar who codified perspective mathematically into theoretical Latin and vernacular texts.
  • Leonardo: Similar because Leonardo da Vinci was the subsequent generation's ultimate polymath who expanded optical, anatomical, and perspective theory; differs because Leonardo's writings were private, fragmented workshop notebooks exploring empirical nature and atmospheric *sfumato*, whereas Alberti wrote polished, systematic humanistic treatises structured on classical Roman models.
  • Vitruvius: Similar because both authored foundational ten-book encyclopedic treatises on architecture (*De architectura* and *De re aedificatoria*); differs because Vitruvius was an ancient Roman military engineer serving Augustus, whereas Alberti was a fifteenth-century Renaissance humanist who adapted classical principles to Christian civic humanism.
  • Palladio: Similar because Andrea Palladio was an influential Italian Renaissance architect who codified design principles in an illustrated treatise (*I quattro libri dell'architettura*); differs because Palladio worked a century later in the late Renaissance Veneto, focusing on practical villa typologies and domestic domesticity, whereas Alberti established the philosophical and monumental foundations in Florence, Rimini, and Mantua.
  • Cennini: Similar because both wrote seminal Italian treatises on painting; differs because Cennino Cennini recorded manual workshop recipes for grinding pigments and applying tempera (*Il Libro dell'Arte*), whereas Alberti articulated a humanist philosophy of painting grounded in geometry, proportion, and rhetorical composition (*istoria*).
  • Vasari: Similar because Giorgio Vasari also operated as an architect and writer who codified the Florentine artistic tradition; differs because Vasari wrote narrative biographical histories celebrating artistic personalities (*Le Vite*), whereas Alberti produced theoretical treatises establishing the mathematical, aesthetic, and structural laws governing art.
