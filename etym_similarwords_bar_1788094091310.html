• Rod: Similar because both name a long thin rigid piece of material; differs because a rod is slender and usually round and functional, while a bar may be thick, square or decorative and emphasises hardness and blocking.
  • Pole: Similar because both are long cylindrical pieces; differs because a pole is round, tapering and often of wood for support (tent pole), while a bar is more often metal and used to bar or divide.
  • Shaft: Similar because both are long straight pieces; differs because shaft (of a spear, a column, a mine) stresses length and straightness but not the blocking function of a bar.
  • Beam: Similar because both are long rigid structural pieces; differs because a beam carries load horizontally in a structure, while a bar may be a movable barrier, a unit or a device.
  • Rail: Similar because both are long bars used as barrier or support; differs because a rail is specifically a horizontal member for fencing or guidance (handrail, railway), while a bar is generic and movable.
  • Spar / Stave: Similar because both are long wooden pieces; differs because a spar is nautical (a mast or yard) and a stave is a barrel part or walking staff — neither blocks like a bar.
  • Barrier: Similar because a bar is a barrier and barriers are often bars; differs because barrier is purely functional (it bars), while "bar" also names the concrete rod and the many metonymic senses (law, pub, music).
  • Barricade: Similar because both block passage; differs because a barricade is a makeshift street fortification, plural in parts and improvised, while a bar is a single rigid piece or a permanent rail.
  • Obstacle / Obstruction / Impediment: Similar because all can be "a bar to" something; differs because these are fully abstract, while "bar" keeps the physical image of a rod laid across the way.
  • Ban / Prohibit / Forbid: Similar because "to bar" can mean to forbid or exclude; differs because bar is the least bureaucratic — it images the barrier itself — while ban, prohibit and forbid are legal-administrative verbs of rule.
  • Debar / Exclude: Similar because to debar is precisely to bar out; differs because debar is formal and legalistic, while bar is everyday and physical.
  • Pub / Tavern / Inn / Saloon: Similar because "the bar" can mean the drinking establishment; differs because "bar" names the counter first and the house only by extension (and is the modern casual word), while pub, tavern, inn and saloon are denominations of the premises themselves.
  • Measure / Beat: Similar because a musical bar is a measure of time; differs because "bar" is the written segment between bar-lines ("measure" in American usage), while the "beat" is the pulse within it.
  • Sandbank / Shoal / Spit: Similar because all are ridges of sediment in water, and a sandbar is such a ridge; differs because a spit is attached to land at one end and a shoal is simply a shallow area, while a bar crosses or closes a channel.
  • Millibar: Similar because the millibar is a subdivision of the bar (the pressure unit); differs because millibar is only the meteorological unit (0.001 bar), while "bar" also carries the whole rod-and-barrier family.
