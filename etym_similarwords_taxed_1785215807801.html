• Accused: Similar because both attribute a fault to someone; differs because accused is the neutral legal or moral default, lacking the personal pressing and assessing force of "taxed with."
  • Charged: Similar because both allege a fault; differs because charged suggests formal, often institutional or legal accusation, while "taxed with" adds the sense of pinning the fault on an individual as a debt owed.
  • Confronted: Similar because both involve facing a person with a matter; differs because confronted stresses the face-to-face encounter without necessarily carrying the moral assessment of a fault.
  • Reproached: Similar because both express blame; differs because reproached implies rebuke tinged with disappointment, while "taxed with" is a more bookish, formal pressing home of a specific charge.
  • Censured: Similar because both express formal disapproval of conduct; differs because censure is an official or public expression of disapproval, while taxing with a fault is a personal, moral accusation.
  • Rebuked: Similar because both name a sharp expression of blame; differs because rebuke is direct and often angry, while "taxed with" is colder and more formal, assessing the fault like a debt.
  • Admonished: Similar because both warn or correct a person; differs because admonish is gentle and corrective in intent, whereas to tax with a fault is to press an accusation home.
  • Burdened / Strained: Similar in the figurative sense of loading a demand onto someone; differs because burdened and strained emphasize weight or pressure, while taxed carries the fiscal image of assessment.
  • Levied: Similar in the fiscal sense of imposing a charge; differs because levy is a formal administrative verb for imposing a tax or duty, while tax is the broader, everyday word.
