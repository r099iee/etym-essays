- 18th c. French “the substantial joint” - the roast after the potages and entrées; literal, kitchen-bound, the meal’s structural anchor.
- Late 18th–early 19th c. figurative bloom - pièce de résistance for any crowning item (a collection’s gem, an evening’s triumph); witty, salon-approved.
- Early–mid-19th c. English borrowing - gourmets and travellers import it with French menus; high-status, slightly showing-off, always italicised.
- Late 19th–20th c. general connoisseurship - auction rooms, museums, reviews: the Vermeer, the aria, the Chelsea Flower Show rose; prestige intact, italics fading.
- Present mock-grandeur - food bloggers (“my bean-stew pièce de résistance”) and ironists (Durant’s lice) use it straight-faced for low subjects; the grandeur is the joke, which proves the grandeur survives.
