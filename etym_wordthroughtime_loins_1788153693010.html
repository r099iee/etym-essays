- [13th–14th c.: loyne(s) = lower back and hips, a plain body-word borrowed from French] - [Perception: neutral, anatomical, everyday (manor, cookery, romance)]
- [1611 KJV: 'loins' fixed in scripture — gird up thy loins, fruit of thy loins, strength in his loins] - [Perception: solemn, virile, genealogical; the word becomes the seat of readiness and descent]
- [17th–18th c.: literary use for vigour and begetting; Milton's bodily diction] - [Perception: dignified and strong, already bookish rather than street speech]
- [19th c.: Victorian reticence adopts 'loins' as euphemism for the genitals] - [Perception: modest, strained; the word acquires embarrassment it never had in the KJV]
- [Today: singular 'loin' = the meat cut, neutral culinary; plural 'loins' = biblical/archaic idiom and anatomy] - [Perception: two registers sundered — butcher's counter on one side, scripture on the other]
