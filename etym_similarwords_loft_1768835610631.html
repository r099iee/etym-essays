• attic: Similar because both name a room directly under the roof; differs because the attic is the enclosed, boarded storage room of a private house, while the loft is the rougher, more open, often single unpartitioned volume.
  • garret: Similar because both are small rooms under the roof; differs because the garret stresses the cramped poverty of a poor occupant — the artist's or writer's garret — while the loft stresses bareness and air rather than squalor.
  • penthouse: Similar because both are roof-top dwellings; differs because in modern usage the penthouse is the luxurious, privileged inversion of the loft, while the loft was traditionally the humble storage chamber.
  • mezzanine: Similar because both are upper intermediate spaces within a building; differs because the mezzanine is an intermediate floor between two main floors and has nothing to do with the roof.
  • gallery: Similar because both can be raised platforms or upper storeys in a church or hall; differs because the gallery is an open balcony built for display or audience, while the loft is a closed or half-closed chamber under the roof-pitch.
  • belfry: Similar because both are high chambers near a building's top; differs because the belfry is the bell-chamber of a tower, open to the bells and their sound, while the loft is a storage or living space under the roof, and the two words share no etymology.
