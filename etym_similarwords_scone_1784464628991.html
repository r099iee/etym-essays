• Biscuit: Similar because both are small quick breads; differs because an American biscuit is flaky, savoury, and leavened with baking powder and butter, while a British scone is denser, often slightly sweet, and served with jam and cream (the British "biscuit" is a different item — a crisp cookie).
  • Muffin: Similar because both are quick tea breads; differs because a muffin is baked from a wetter batter in a cup mold, while a scone is cut from a firm kneaded dough and baked flat.
  • Bannock: Similar because both are traditional griddle-baked flat breads of Scotland; differs because the bannock is a large round cake, plainer and often barley or oatmeal, while the scone is smaller, richer, and baked as individual pieces.
  • Crumpet: Similar because both are British griddle-baked breads eaten at tea; differs because a crumpet is a yeasted, spongy round with holes for melting butter, while a scone is a denser, cut quick bread.
  • Teacake: Similar because both are British baked items of the tea-table; differs because a teacake is a sweet, yeasted, often fruited roll (or a flat muffin-like bun), while a scone is firmer and not yeast-leavened.
  • Girdle cake: Similar because both are cooked on a griddle; differs because a girdle (griddle) cake is a generic term for griddle-baked items including early scones, while "scone" names the specific bread.
  • Dumpling: Similar because both are small dough-based foods; differs because dumplings are boiled or steamed, while scones are baked on a griddle or in an oven.
  • Bun: Similar because both are small round baked goods; differs because a bun is yeast-leavened and often sweet-glazed, while a scone is chemically leavened and unglazed.
  • Damper: Similar because both are simple quick breads; differs because damper is the Australian soda-and-flour bush bread baked in ashes, coarser and plainer than a scone.
