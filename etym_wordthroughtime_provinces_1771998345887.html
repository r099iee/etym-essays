- [Ancient Rome administrative divisions] - ['Provincia' names the magistrate's charge, a conquered territory governed under Roman law outside Italy]
- [Imperial era union] - [Provinces are the great bodies of Roman rule; 'the union of the provinces' is a political achievement of laws and manners]
- [Medieval church territories] - [The word carries into the Church: a 'province' is the area under an archbishop's jurisdiction]
- [16th-19th century modern divisions] - [Adopted for subdivisions of states and empires, especially in France, Canada, and colonial territories]
- [19th-20th century 'provincial' offshoot] - [The adjective 'provincial' splits off to mean narrowly local or unsophisticated, while the noun keeps its administrative dignity]
