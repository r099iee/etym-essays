- Stone base for percussion (Neolithic) - Neutral survival tool, shared passive hammering stone for flaking flint and crushing food.
- Bronze/Iron smith's block (c. 1200 BCE - 500 CE) - Respected craft core; the smith's power seat, slightly magical, at village edge.
- Medieval mythic forge centre (500-1500) - Reverent and fearful; linked to Wayland, Dunstan, and devil stories; emblem of making and punishment.
- Early modern idiom carrier (1500-1800) - Moralised; "on the anvil" and "hammer and anvil" turn the tool into a figure for trial, endurance, and policy-making.
- Industrial icon (1800-1950) - Proud proletarian emblem; London-pattern anvil standardised, celebrated on union banners as honest labour.
- Modern plural / scientific metaphor (1950-present) - Dispersed and technical; museum artefacts, thunderhead anvils, ear-bone anvils; nostalgic craft revival alongside weather and anatomy senses.
