- 14th c. — the name of the ancient philosopher becomes current in English, studied through translations and known above all as the master of logic.
- 15th–16th c. — humanists recover and print the Greek text; the name becomes central to university arts courses and to the new theory of poetry and rhetoric.
- 17th–18th c. — “Aristotle” still commands the schools, but his natural philosophy is increasingly set against the new science; “Aristotelian” begins to sound old-fashioned.
- 19th c. — historical and philological scholarship separates the historical Aristotle from the scholastic “Philosopher”; the name becomes an object of academic study.
- 20th–21st c. — the name evokes systematic philosophy, virtue ethics, logic and biology; “Aristotelian” is a live label in ethics and metaphysics, and the Rhetoric is read again in rhetorical and literary theory.
