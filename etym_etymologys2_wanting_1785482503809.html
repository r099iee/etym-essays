1. PIE *h₁wen(h₁)- (older Pokorny *we-no-) "empty, lacking, deficient" - exact laryngeal reconstruction debated (some scholars reconstruct *h₂wen-). 2. Proto-Germanic *wana- "wanting, lacking, deficient" (adjective; cf. Gothic wans, Old Saxon wan). 3. Old English wan- (prefix) "wanting, lacking" - attested in wanhope "despair" and, through Middle English wantowen (wan- + togen "disciplined"), in wanton "ill-disciplined" (lit. "lacking training"). 4. Old Norse vanr "wanting, lacking" (adjective), giving the impersonal verb vanta "it lacks (to me), there is a lack of" (exact derivational path from vanr debated). 5. Middle English wanten (borrowed from Old Norse, c. 1200) "to be lacking, to lack, be without". 6. English want (verb) "to lack, be without" (13th c.), with the noun want "deficiency, poverty"; the desire-sense "to wish for" develops c. 1706 from "to feel the want of". 7. English wanting (present participle used as adjective "lacking, deficient" and preposition "without", from the 16th c.) - fixed in the idiom "found wanting" (16th c.), modelled on the biblical weighing image (Daniel 5:27). Note the debate: the PIE laryngeal is uncertain (*h₁wen- vs *h₂wen-); the Indo-Iranian cognate Sanskrit una "deficient" is accepted by some scholars and rejected by others; and the exact morphological path from the adjective vanr to the impersonal verb vanta is not fully settled.
