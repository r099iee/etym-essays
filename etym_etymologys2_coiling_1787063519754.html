Note on nuance: the semantic leap from Latin "to gather" to English "to wind into rings" happened when the French-derived noun was applied to rope being 'gathered up' into a circular heap on shipboard — the gathered heap became the coiled ring. Shakespeare's mortal coil ("this mortal coil", Hamlet, c. 1602), meaning the bustle and turmoil of life, belongs to a SEPARATE and etymologically obscure English word coil "noise, bustle, turmoil" of uncertain origin — it is not derived from Latin colligere and must not be confused with the rope-coil line.
