- Old English (c. 700–1100) - Watchful Awareness: Originated as the root adjective *wær*, meaning cautious, aware, watchful, or on one's guard.
- Middle English (c. 1200–1450) - Suffixation to *Wary*: The Old English root *wær* was suffixed with *-y* to create *wary / warie*, competing with dialectal forms like *ware* (as in *beware*).
- 16th to 17th Century - Renaissance Prudence & Machiavellian Politics: Widely utilized in Renaissance literature, statecraft treatises, and King James Biblical prose to describe political circumspection, spiritual watchfulness against temptation, and battlefield survival.
- 18th to 19th Century - Wilderness & Maritime Lore: Prominently featured in maritime and frontier narratives (e.g., Defoe, Cooper, Melville) describing sailors navigating treacherous shoals and scouts traversing hostile territory.
- 20th to 21st Century - Everyday Commercial & Geopolitical Vigilance: Standardized across corporate finance, cybersecurity (wary of phishing attacks), and interpersonal psychology for measured caution against deception.
