• Trivikrama: Similar because both refer to the exact same fifth avatar of Lord Viṣṇu in Hindu mythology; differs because Vāmana designates the initial, diminutive guise of the young dwarf Brahmin carrying an umbrella and water pot, whereas Trivikrama ("He of Three Strides") specifically denotes the gigantic, universe-spanning cosmic form Viṣṇu assumed when striding across the earth, heavens, and netherworld.
  • Bali (Mahābali): Similar because the narrative of Vāmana is inextricably linked to King Bali as his generous, noble patron; differs because Bali is the Asura sovereign whose imperial hubris was humbled, whereas Vāmana is the divine avatāra who tested Bali's devotion and granted him eternal liberation in Sutala.
  • Airāvata: Similar because both are primordial cosmic guardian bull elephants (*Diggajas*) created to support the universe; differs because Airāvata is the pristine, multi-tusked white elephant of the East and the vehicle of Indra, whereas Vāmana guards the Southern quarter under Yama and shares his name with the dwarf avatar of Viṣṇu.
  • Diggaja: Similar because Vāmana is one of the eight Ashta-Diggajas; differs because Diggaja is the generic categorical term for any of the eight world-bearing elephants, whereas Vāmana is the specific proper name of the southern guardian.
  • Añjana: Similar because both are cited in Vālmīki's *Rāmāyaṇa* as the primordial cosmic ancestors of Ayodhyā's finest war-elephants; differs because Añjana is the ink-black guardian of the West named for collyrium, whereas Vāmana presides over the South.
  • Avatāra: Similar because Vāmana is one of the most prominent divine incarnations; differs because Avatāra is the overarching Sanskrit theological concept denoting any descent of the Supreme Being into the mortal realm, whereas Vāmana is one specific historic and theological incarnation.
  • Matsya: Similar because both are primary avatars within Viṣṇu's *Daśāvatāra*; differs because Matsya was the first avatar who appeared as a horned fish to save Manu and the Vedas from the cosmic deluge, whereas Vāmana was the fifth avatar and the first to assume a fully human form.
  • Varāha: Similar because both are incarnations of Viṣṇu who rescued the earth; differs because Varāha took the zoomorphic form of a cosmic boar who physically lifted the earth goddess Bhūmi from the primordial ocean on his tusks, whereas Vāmana reclaimed the earth through moral cleverness and cosmic strides.
  • Narasiṁha: Similar because both immediately precede each other in the avatar sequence at cosmic transitions; differs because Narasiṁha assumed a terrifying therianthropic half-man, half-lion form to violently slay the tyrant Hiraṇyakaśipu, whereas Vāmana acted with non-violent, disarming humility to redeem King Bali.
  • dwarf: Similar because dwarf is the direct English lexical translation of Sanskrit vāmana; differs because dwarf is a plain secular biological noun or Western Germanic mythological creature, lacking the rich Vaishnava theology, cosmic world-bearing elephant lore, and Sanskrit poetic school associations of Vāmana.
  • miniature: Similar because both denote an exceptionally small or reduced physical scale; differs because miniature is a general descriptive adjective applied to models, portraits, or small versions of objects, lacking the mythic agency, spiritual personification, and divine paradox of Vāmana.
  • pyknic: Similar because both characterize short, thick-set physical bodily habitus; differs because pyknic is a modern clinical and psychological typology (from Ernst Kretschmer's constitutional classifications) describing a stocky, rounded human physique, completely devoid of religious or epic significance.
