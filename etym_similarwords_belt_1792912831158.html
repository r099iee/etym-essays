• Sash: Similar because both are bands worn round the body; differs because a sash is a broad cloth band worn diagonally or at the waist for ceremony, while a belt is narrower, sturdier, and buckled for work or dress.
  • Girdle: Similar because both encircle the waist to hold garments; differs because girdle is literary or archaic with poetic and scriptural overtones, while belt is the everyday modern word.
  • Strap: Similar because both are narrow flexible bands that fasten; differs because a strap fastens baggage, watches, or machinery anywhere on the body or object, while a belt prototypically encircles the waist.
  • Waistband: Similar because both sit at the waist of garments; differs because a waistband is the sewn-in top edge of trousers or a skirt, while a belt is the separate removable band threaded through it.
  • Band: Similar because both name encircling strips; differs because band is the broad generic term from rubber bands to brass bands, while belt specifies waistwear or a geographic or mechanical strip.
  • Strip: Similar because a belt of trees is literally a strip of woodland; differs because strip stresses thinness and removal or exposure, while belt stresses binding, zoning, or encircling.
  • Zone: Similar because both mark regions like the green belt and the temperate zone; differs because zone is abstract and administrative, while belt keeps the visual image of a long band lying across the map.
  • Girth: Similar because both pass round the body of horse or rider; differs because girth is the saddle strap under a horse belly, technical and equestrian, while belt belongs to human dress and landscape.
  • Cummerbund: Similar because both are formal waistwear; differs because a cummerbund is a pleated silk sash worn with dinner jackets, while a belt is buckled leather or webbing for daily use.
