• Filthy: Similar because both mean disgustingly dirty; differs because filthy is the general word for extreme dirt and can apply to any surface, while scummy specifically describes a floating film on a liquid (or, in slang, morally contemptible people).
  • Dirty: Similar because both describe uncleanness; differs because dirty is neutral and general, while scummy is stronger, more visceral, and carries the image of a surface film.
  • Grimy: Similar because both describe surface filth; differs because grimy refers to a thin layer of soot or dirt on solid surfaces, while scummy refers to the film on liquids.
  • Foul: Similar because both mean offensively dirty or disgusting; differs because foul emphasises the bad smell or taste (foul water) and is also used of weather and foul play, while scummy keeps the image of floating waste.
  • Murky: Similar because both describe unpleasant, unclear water; differs because murky means dark, turbid, and hard to see through, while scummy means carrying a visible floating film on the surface.
  • Stagnant: Similar because both describe bad standing water; differs because stagnant refers to the water not flowing (which often causes scum), while scummy refers to the surface film itself.
  • Contaminated / Polluted: Similar because both describe water unsafe to drink; differs because contaminated and polluted are formal, technical terms for chemical or biological impurity, while scummy is the everyday, visual description.
  • Frothy: Similar because both describe a layer of foam or bubbles on a liquid; differs because frothy is clean, light, and appealing (frothy milk, froth on a beer), while scummy is dirty, foul, and repulsive.
  • Sleazy: Similar because both describe morally cheap, sordid people or places; differs because sleazy emphasises shabby, disreputable, sexually seedy shoddiness, while scummy emphasises outright contemptible worthlessness.
  • Despicable: Similar because both mean deserving of contempt; differs because despicable is formal, weighty moral condemnation, while scummy is informal, visceral, slangy invective.
  • Contemptible: Similar because both name moral worthlessness; differs because contemptible is the detached, intellectual term, while scummy brings the physical image of filth.
  • Vile: Similar because both mean morally disgusting; differs because vile is a strong, formal word for repulsive evil, while scummy is colloquial and more dismissive than horrified.
  • Sordid: Similar because both describe morally dirty, base situations; differs because sordid stresses the squalid, shameful, squalidly selfish quality, while scummy is blunter slang.
  • Seedy: Similar because both describe disreputable places; differs because seedy means shabby, rundown, and faintly criminal (a seedy hotel), while scummy is a harsher, more openly contemptuous judgement.
  • Disgusting: Similar because both provoke revulsion; differs because disgusting is a general word for anything that turns the stomach, while scummy ties the disgust to the specific image of scum.
