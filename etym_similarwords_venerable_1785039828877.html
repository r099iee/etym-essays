• Ancient: Similar because both imply great age; differs because ancient belongs to things and distant times rather than to a person one honours, and carries no reverence for wisdom.
  • August: Similar because both command deep respect; differs because august conveys imposing institutional grandeur (from the emperor's title Augustus) without venerable's fusion of age and sanctity.
  • Revered: Similar because both denote deep respect; differs because revered focuses on the feeling of the beholder, while venerable inheres in the person's age and dignity.
  • Hoary: Similar because both picture white-haired old age; differs because hoary is often faintly humorous or sentimental, where venerable is solemn and hallowed.
  • Distinguished: Similar because both mark eminence; differs because distinguished honours achievement and standing rather than age, and can apply to the young.
  • Old / aged: Similar because both report great age; differ because they are purely chronological and carry no claim of worth or reverence.
  • Hallowed: Similar because both carry sacred overtones; differs because hallowed is strictly sacred — set apart for holy use — while venerable may be merely lofty or historic.
