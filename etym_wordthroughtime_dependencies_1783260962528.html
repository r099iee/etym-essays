- [15th-century 'hanging from'] - In its current form since Middle English (from Latin dependere, 'to hang down from'), 'dependency' first meant a state of hanging from or being subordinate to something.
- [Feudal and political sense] - By the 17th and 18th centuries it became the administrative word for a territory subordinate to a governing state - a dependency of the crown - bound by allegiance yet not part of the home realm.
- [Colonial empire] - In the 18th and 19th centuries the plural 'dependencies' named the overseas possessions of the imperial powers, a word of administrative hierarchy and empire.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'dependencies' survives in historical and legal prose for subordinate territories, while the common meaning has broadened to reliance generally - drug dependency, financial dependency - the imperial sense now chiefly historical.
