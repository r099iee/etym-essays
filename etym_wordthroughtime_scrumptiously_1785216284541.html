- [c. 1830–1850: a jocular coinage] - scrumptious appears as slangy, playful approval, probably a fanciful shortening of sumptuous; it is informal from birth and charged with relish rather than grandeur — a word that pats the stomach and winks.
- [Victorian era: the nursery register] - the word settles into the homely language of treats, puddings, and children's delight; “scrumptious!” is a child's cry at a sweet, cozy and affectionate, the cosy diminution of the costly idea it mimics.
- [1937: literary irony] - Tolkien puts the rare adverb into Gollum's mouth — “Is it scrumptiously crunchable?” — so that a famished, starving creature savours the idea of eating with a gourmand's nursery word; the adverb's indulgent warmth is suddenly servant to hungry menace.
- [Contemporary sense] - scrumptiously remains a colloquial, approving adverb of gustatory delight (“scrumptiously crunchy”), informal and warm, almost always spoken with a smile; the form stays rare, and its effect is playful, pampered indulgence rather than measured praise.
