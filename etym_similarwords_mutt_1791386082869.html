• Mongrel: Similar because both denote a dog of mixed or indeterminate breed; differs because mongrel is an older, native English term (derived from *among*) that historically carried harsher, more contemptuous overtones of racial or physical impurity, whereas mutt is casual, warm, and affectionate.
  • Cur: Similar because both can describe an unpedigreed canine; differs because cur is intensely pejorative, denoting an aggressive, cowardly, ill-tempered, or diseased stray dog (or a despicable, treacherous man), whereas mutt is friendly and harmless.
  • Crossbreed: Similar because both have parents of different breeds; differs because crossbreed is a formal, neutral biological term, frequently describing the intentional mating of two distinct purebred dogs (such as a Cockapoo), while mutt implies accidental or multigenerational mixing.
  • Pooch: Similar because both are casual, affectionate terms for a dog; differs because pooch is an uncritical pet name applicable to any dog regardless of breed or pedigree, with no reference to intelligence or foolishness.
  • Simpleton: Similar because in its human sense mutt denotes a foolish person; differs because simpleton is an older, formal, literary noun specifically diagnosing intellectual deficiency or childish naivety, lacking mutt's streetwise American slang flavor.
  • Chump: Similar because both are informal American insults for an easily duped or foolish person; differs because chump specifically stresses gullibility, being swindled, or being made a sucker of.
  • Boob: Similar because both describe an awkward, foolish blunderer; differs because boob emphasizes buffoonery, loud ignorance, or clumsiness.
  • Blockhead: Similar because both denote a dense, unintelligent individual; differs because blockhead specifically evokes a head made of wood (unreceptive to learning), whereas mutt traces back to a sheep's head.
  • Stray: Similar because many mutts are found as strays; differs because stray describes a homeless, lost, or feral domestic animal, regardless of its genealogical breed status.
  • Dunderhead: Similar because both brand someone as dim-witted; differs because dunderhead is an older, quaint British-origin term for a slow-witted dunce.
