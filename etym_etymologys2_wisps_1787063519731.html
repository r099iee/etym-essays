Old Norse (pre-13th c.)
visp
“small bundle of hay or straw”
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
wisp / wips
“handful or bundle of hay, straw, twigs”
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
wisp
“thin streak of smoke, vapour, hair”
↓
Modern English (19th–21st c.)
WISPS
“plural: thin delicate strands or trails of vapour, hair, smoke”
NOTE: The pre-Norse origin is debated; no secure PIE etymon is attested. Some scholars connect the root to Germanic words for “twist” or “bend”, but the connection is unproven.
