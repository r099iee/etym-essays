*PIE* (c. 3000 BCE)
*epi- / *op- + *ka- / *skai-
"toward, over" + "dark, dirty, left-hand, ill-omened"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
obscēnus / obscaenus
"ill-omened, repulsive, offensive, foul, unchaste"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. CE)
obscēnitās
"foulness, repulsiveness, lewdness, indecency"
↓
Middle French (c. 16th c.)
obscénité
"foul speech, gross indecency, lewdness"
↓
Modern English (late 16th c.–present)
obscenity
"lewdness, gross indecency, repulsive offence"
Note: Latin 'obscēnus' traditionally parsed as 'ob-' + 'caenum' (filth) or 'scaevus' (left-handed/ill-omened).
