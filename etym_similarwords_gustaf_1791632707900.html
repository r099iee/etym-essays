• Gustav: Similar because it is the exact same historical proper name sharing identical Germanic roots; differs because Gustav is the standard modern German, Austrian, and international spelling, whereas Gustaf is the traditional royal and formal orthography preserved in Sweden.
  • Gustavus: Similar because it designates the identical historical name; differs because Gustavus is the Latinized form used in Early Modern European historiography, university records, and treaties (e.g. Gustavus Adolphus).
  • Gautstafr: Similar because it is the direct Old Norse progenitor meaning "pillar of the Geats"; differs because it represents an archaic medieval Scandinavian pagan compound rather than a contemporary Christian personal name.
  • Gösta: Similar because it is derived from the same root in Swedish; differs because Gösta is a traditional Swedish hypocoristic or vernacular pet form, carrying an informal, folksy domestic register compared to the regal dignity of Gustaf.
  • Augustus: Similar because both historically functioned as premier European regal names associated with state authority; differs because Augustus is Latin in origin (from augere, "to increase, venerable"), completely distinct in etymology and linguistic lineage.
  • Staffan: Similar because it shares the phonetic presence of the "staff/stave" element in popular Swedish naming folklore; differs because Staffan is the Scandinavian vernacular evolution of the Greek biblical name Stephen (Stephanos, "crown/garland").
  • Britschian: Similar because it directly invokes the intellectual legacy of Gustaf Britsch in art education; differs because it is an academic relational adjective rather than a personal proper name.
