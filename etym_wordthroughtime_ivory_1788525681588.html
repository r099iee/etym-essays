- [c. 1200-1300 / Middle English] - ivori / yvory - [borrowed from Old French yvoire to denote the precious luxury material for religious reliquaries, devotional triptychs, and royal chessmen imported from Africa and the Orient]
- [14th-16th c. / Late Medieval & Renaissance] - ivory - [entrenched in Biblical poetry (Song of Solomon: "tower of ivory") and courtly love poetry as the supreme blazon for female beauty and radiant skin]
- [17th-18th c. / Enlightenment] - ivory - [widely applied to elite domestic goods: billiard balls, miniature portrait backings, cutlery handles, and piano keys]
- [19th c. / Victorian Era] - ivory - [central to Aesthetic and Decadent literature (Oscar Wilde, Walter Pater, Gautier) for romantic pallor and artistic perfection; Sainte-Beuve coined "tour d'ivoire" (ivory tower) for poetic detachment]
- [20th-21st c. / Modern Era] - ivory - [marked by anti-poaching wildlife treaties (CITES international trade ban), shifting primarily to colour terminology, piano slang ("tickling the ivories"), and academic critique ("ivory tower")]
