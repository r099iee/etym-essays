• Chios: Similar because both represent the exact same Aegean island; differs because Chios (Χίος) is the authentic ancient and modern Greek endonym and the standard contemporary international geographic name, whereas Scio is the historical Genoese, Italian, and 19th-century diplomatic/art-historical form.
  • Chio: Similar because both are Western Romance adaptations of the Greek island's name; differs because Chio is an intermediate spelling used in early French and Italian texts without the palatalizing initial s-, whereas Scio was the formal Genoese administrative and Venetian cartographic spelling.
  • Sakız: Similar because both designate the identical Aegean island; differs because Sakız (or Sakız Adası) is the Ottoman and modern Turkish name, meaning literally "Mastic Island" or "Gum Island," highlighting its economic crop rather than its Hellenic toponym.
  • Lesbos: Similar because both are large north-eastern Aegean Greek islands situated close to the Anatolian coast with illustrious classical literary heritage; differs because Lesbos is famed for Sappho, olive cultivation, and its capital Mytilene, whereas Scio is defined by the unique mastic resin of its southern villages and the 1822 tragedy.
  • Samos: Similar because both are neighboring eastern Aegean islands deeply involved in the Greek War of Independence; differs because Samos successfully repelled Ottoman seaborne invasions and maintained semi-independent autonomy, whereas Scio suffered near-total civilian extermination and enslavement.
  • Candia: Similar because both are historical Italian/Venetian names for major Greek territories (Candia being the Italian name for Crete and its capital Heraklion); differs because Candia was a Venetian fortress colony for nearly five centuries, whereas Scio was the sovereign domain of the Genoese Giustiniani family.
