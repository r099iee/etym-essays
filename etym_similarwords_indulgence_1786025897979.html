• Gratification: Similar because both refer to the satisfaction of desires and the experience of pleasure; differs because gratification names the psychological feeling of fulfillment, whereas indulgence names the act of yielding and relaxing discipline to obtain it.
  • Leniency: Similar because both involve soft treatment and tolerance toward faults or desires; differs because leniency is specifically mercy or mildness in judging others, while indulgence more often means actively granting pleasures or favors.
  • Pampering: Similar because both describe treating someone or oneself with special care and luxury; differs because pampering is informal and bodily, while indulgence carries theological, legal, and ethical weight.
  • Coddling: Similar because both describe over-protecting and yielding to someone; differs because coddling has a strong negative implication of weakening the recipient, while indulgence can be neutral or even admiring.
  • Hedonism: Similar because both involve the pursuit of pleasure; differs because hedonism is a systematic philosophy that makes pleasure the chief good, while indulgence refers to individual acts of yielding.
  • Extravagance: Similar because both involve excess and luxury; differs because extravagance emphasizes spending beyond reasonable financial limits, while indulgence emphasizes satisfying emotional or sensory appetites.
  • Dispensation: Similar because both involve official exemption from rules or punishment; differs because dispensation is a formal administrative release from an obligation, whereas indulgence relates to spiritual penance or personal favor.
  • Forbearance: Similar because both describe patient tolerance or restraint; differs because forbearance is refraining from enforcing a right or punishment, while indulgence is actively granting a pleasure or exemption.
  • Perk / Treat: Similar because both name something pleasant given to oneself as a reward; differs because perk is an entitlement attached to a role or job, while indulgence is a voluntary yielding to appetite.
  • Permissiveness: Similar because both denote tolerance of behavior that others would restrain; differs because permissiveness is a general policy or attitude, while indulgence highlights specific acts of granting.
