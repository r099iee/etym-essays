• Surround: Similar because both close round a person or place on all sides; differs because surround is the general military and spatial word, while hem adds the stitched-border image of tight domestic enclosure.
  • Enclose: Similar because both shut something within bounds; differs because enclose stresses walls, fences, and formal boundaries, while hem stresses living or pressing edges that creep inward.
  • Encircle: Similar because both make a ring round the target; differs because encircle is geometric and often grand (hills, arms, wreaths), while hem is needle-small and inescapably snug.
  • Envelop: Similar because both wrap and swallow movement; differs because envelop folds like an envelope or fog, soft and total, while hem stitches edge to edge, firm and finished.
  • Trap: Similar because hemming in traps the traveller; differs because trap names the snare and its violence outright, while hem hides menace inside housewifely sewing.
  • Besiege: Similar because both cut off escape by surrounding; differs because besiege is prolonged warfare with armies and walls, while hem can be done in a moment by trees or crowds.
  • Edge: Similar because hems and edges both border cloth and land; differs because edge is the general boundary line, while hem is the folded sewn finish, labour made visible.
  • Border: Similar because both bound and trim; differs because border is broad and often decorative (borders of flowers, nations), while hem is narrow, functional, and holding against fray.
  • Confine: Similar because both restrict freedom of movement; differs because confine is formal and institutional (confined to barracks, to bed), while hem is vivid and physical, branches and bodies pressing close.
