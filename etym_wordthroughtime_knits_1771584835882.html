- [Old English cnyttan: to tie in a knot] - 'Knit' means to fasten or tie by knots; connotation is practical and manual - the work of string, rope, and binding.
- [14th century: the fabric craft] - The verb acquires its modern textile sense of forming fabric by interlocking loops of yarn; connotation becomes domestic, feminine, and homey - the patient craft of sweaters and stockings.
- [Figurative modern usage] - 'Knits' extends to the drawing together of brows in a frown ('knits his brows') and to the close joining of people ('a tightly knit community'); connotation is now both craft-literal and figurative, describing anything bound or gathered tightly together.
