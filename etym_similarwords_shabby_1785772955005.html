• Threadbare: Similar because both describe textiles worn thin by long use; differs because threadbare is tightly fabric-focused (visible threads), while shabby covers whole outfits, rooms, and also mean behaviour.
  • Tattered: Similar because both describe worn-out clothing; differs because tattered stresses tears and ragged edges, while shabby can mean faded, stained, or ill-kept without dramatic rips.
  • Seedy: Similar because both describe run-down, disreputable appearance of places or people; differs because seedy carries a stronger hint of moral seediness or sleaze, while shabby is the default for ordinary wear and petty unkindness.
  • Dilapidated: Similar for neglected buildings and structures; differs in scale and technical feel (falling into ruin), while shabby is often milder and more about social appearance than structural collapse.
  • Despicable: Similar because both can condemn behaviour; differs because despicable marks extreme moral vileness, while shabby marks smaller meanness — stingy, disloyal, underhanded — contemptible but not necessarily monstrous.
  • Mean: Similar because both can describe ungenerous behaviour; differs because mean is broader and more direct (cruel, nasty), while shabby's moral sense is more about low, cheap, dishonourable conduct.
  • Frayed / Faded / Worn: Similar because all describe physical decline of fabric; differs as narrowly physical words without shabby's extension to honour and morality.
  • Grubby: Similar because both suggest uncleanness and neglect; differs because grubby is about dirt specifically, while shabby is about wear and upkeep and extends to character.
  • Spruce / Smart: Contrast terms — similar as opposite-pole words for appearance and conduct; differs because they mean neat, well-kept, and honourable, the direct antonyms of shabby.
