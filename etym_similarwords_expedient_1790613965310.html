• convenient: Similar because it means suited to comfort or needs; differs because convenient stresses ease and lack of trouble, while expedient stresses advantage toward a goal, often with moral risk.
  • advantageous: Similar because it means giving benefit; differs because advantageous is openly positive, while expedient can imply benefit at the cost of principle.
  • pragmatic: Similar because it means practical rather than idealistic; differs because pragmatic praises realism as philosophy, while expedient can criticize short-termism.
  • opportune: Similar because it means well-timed for purpose; differs because opportune stresses fortunate timing, while expedient stresses calculated usefulness.
  • stopgap: Similar because as noun it means temporary makeshift remedy; differs because stopgap is frankly provisional and humble, while expedient can sound statesmanlike.
  • contrivance: Similar because it means an ingenious device or plan; differs because contrivance stresses ingenuity and artificiality, while expedient stresses fitness to the end.
  • resort: Similar because it means recourse to a means in difficulty; differs because resort (as in last resort) stresses desperation, while expedient stresses calculation.
  • stratagem: Similar because it means a clever scheme; differs because stratagem implies trickery in conflict, while expedient need not deceive.
  • makeshift: Similar because it means improvised substitute; differs because makeshift is colloquial and openly shabby, while expedient is formal and can dignify the shabby.
