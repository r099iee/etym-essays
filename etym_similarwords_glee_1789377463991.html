• Mirth: Similar because both denote genuine cheerfulness, amusement, and high spirits; differs because mirth is characteristically accompanied by laughter, convivial humor, and social banter in a festive gathering, whereas glee emphasizes exuberant, radiant, and often childlike triumph or personal delight.
  • Delight: Similar because both describe a state of intense pleasure and happiness; differs because delight is a broad, refined emotional reaction that can be quiet, aesthetic, and contemplative (such as taking delight in a painting), whereas glee is energetic, outwardly demonstrative, and boisterous.
  • Jubilation: Similar because both express vocal and visible celebration of success; differs because jubilation is typically a collective, public demonstration of triumph following a major historical or military victory, whereas glee is more intimate, spontaneous, and personal.
  • Exultation: Similar because both involve triumphant joy over an achievement; differs because exultation carries a dignified, elevated, and sometimes solemn tone of victorious pride, whereas glee possesses an informal, mischievous, or childlike flavor.
  • Schadenfreude: Similar because both can describe taking pleasure in the misfortune of others; differs because Schadenfreude is a formal philosophical and psychological loanword strictly restricted to malicious pleasure at another's plight, whereas glee is primarily positive and innocent, only acquiring malicious nuance when modified as "malicious glee".
  • Gaiety: Similar because both evoke lighthearted cheerfulness and festive spirits; differs because gaiety suggests a social atmosphere of elegance, social charm, and festive decorum (as in a Parisian salon), whereas glee is raw, spontaneous, and uninhibited.
  • Merriment: Similar because both describe active, boisterous fun; differs because merriment emphasizes communal feasting, drinking, joking, and tavern revelry, whereas glee focuses on the vivid spark of joyous emotion within the individual.
