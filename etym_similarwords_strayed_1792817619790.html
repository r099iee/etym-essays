• Wandered: Similar because both mean moving without a fixed course; differs because wander is neutral roaming (often pleasant), while stray always implies leaving a proper place — wandering has no lost home, straying does.
  • Drifted: Similar because both are slow unintentional movement off course; differs because drift is passive (carried by wind, water, or inattention), while stray keeps the ghost of feet — even a drifting hand is imagined walking.
  • Roamed: Similar because both range beyond boundaries; differs because roam is free and often joyful (roaming the hills), while stray is anxious — someone, somewhere, wants the stray back.
  • Rambled: Similar because both meander pleasantly off the direct line; differs because ramble is the walker's and talker's holiday (rambling roses, rambling speeches), while strayed confesses a path abandoned.
  • Meandered: Similar because both wind indirectly toward no clear goal; differs because meander follows graceful curves (rivers, conversations), while stray has left the course altogether — curves versus departure.
  • Deviated: Similar because both leave the expected line; differs because deviate is formal and often deliberate (deviating from procedure), while stray is usually accidental and always literary-human.
  • Erred: Similar because both fail to keep the right way, morally or otherwise; differs because err is abstract wrongness (to err is human), while stray paints the wandering feet — doctrine versus journey.
  • Digressed: Similar because both leave the main subject; differs because digress belongs to discourse (the speaker digresses), while stray covers feet, flocks, hands, eyes, and souls alike.
  • Veered: Similar because both turn aside from a course; differs because veer is a sharp sudden swerve (the car veered, the debate veered), while stray is a gradual unnoticed drift — swerve versus seep.
