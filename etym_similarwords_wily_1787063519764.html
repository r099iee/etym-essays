• Cunning: Similar because both denote cleverness used to deceive or gain advantage; differs because cunning is broader and more neutral (it can describe an animal's survival skill and even “cunning workmanship”), while wily more strongly implies sly scheming and personal trickery.
  • Crafty: Similar because both mean skilled in deception and stratagem; differs because crafty foregrounds the practical skill and workmanship of the trick, while wily stresses the sly, underhanded quality of the mind behind it.
  • Sly: Similar because both imply sneaky, covert cleverness; differs because sly is narrower, stressing secrecy, quietness and low-key deception, whereas wily can describe grand, long-term strategic scheming.
  • Guileful: Similar because both mean full of deceitful cunning; differs because guileful is more formal and more purely negative (guile = deceit), while wily can carry a hint of admired cleverness.
  • Shrewd: Similar because both describe sharp practical judgment; differs because shrewd is morally neutral to positive (astute business sense), while wily implies manipulation and advantage at others' expense.
  • Foxy: Similar because both evoke animal-like cunning, the fox being the classic emblem of wiles; differs because foxy is colloquial and can also mean attractive in slang, a sense wily never has.
  • Tricky: Similar because both relate to tricks and deception; differs because tricky more often describes situations or tasks (a tricky problem), while wily describes a person's character or disposition.
  • Artful: Similar because both imply skill in achieving ends; differs because artful can be admiring and refined and also means “done with artistic skill”, while wily is narrower and more suspect.
  • Devious: Similar because both mean not straightforward, taking indirect routes to gain advantage; differs because devious stresses the crookedness of the path taken, while wily stresses the cunning mind that plans it.
  • Scheming: Similar because both imply planning to achieve hidden advantage; differs because scheming emphasizes the active planning of plots, while wily emphasizes the innate sly character rather than a specific plot.
  • Astute: Similar because both denote sharp mental acuity; differs because astute is entirely positive (perceptive, discerning) with no necessary deceit, while wily implies manipulative cunning.
