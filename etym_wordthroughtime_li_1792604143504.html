- [Vedic & Late Vedic Era (c. 1000–600 BCE)] - emerged in the Atharvaveda, Brāhmaṇas, and early Buddhist texts (Pali sāli) to distinguish elite transplanted winter wetland rice from fast-growing upland monsoon rice (vrīhi) - [perception: revered as a precious aristocratic grain requiring advanced irrigation, associated with royal abundance and religious purity]
- [Classical Sanskrit Golden Age (c. 300 BCE–700 CE)] - systematically codified in the monumental Ayurvedic compendia (Caraka and Suśruta Saṃhitā) as the sovereign grain (dhānya-śreṣṭha), and celebrated in classical Kāvya poetry (Kālidāsa) as the supreme aesthetic symbol of the autumn and winter harvest - [perception: considered the absolute benchmark of dietary health, tridoṣic balance, and courtly poetic beauty]
- [Medieval Agrarian Society & Inscriptions (c. 800–1600 CE)] - utilized throughout epigraphic copper-plate land grants across northern and southern India as the primary unit of agrarian taxation, temple revenue, and royal endowment - [perception: recognized as the foundational economic engine and fiscal gold-standard of pre-colonial Indian kingdoms]
- [Modern Philological, Agricultural & Ethnobotanical Era (19th–21st c.)] - preserved in modern Indo-Aryan languages (e.g. Bengali śāli dhān, Hindi sālī, Odia sāḷi) to designate traditional indigenous winter paddy, and studied by agrarian scientists and geneticists conserving heritage indigenous rice strains - [perception: valued as an ancient treasure of agricultural biodiversity and timeless Ayurvedic wisdom]
