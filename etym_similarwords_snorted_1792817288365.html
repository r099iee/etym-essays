• Sneered: Similar because both voice contempt aloud in reported speech; differs because a sneer curls the lip into a visible expression of scorn, while a snort blasts it through the nose as sound.
  • Scoffed: Similar because both dismiss a claim as absurd; differs because scoff is chiefly verbal mockery (jeering words), while snort is a bodily noise that can dismiss without any words at all.
  • Huffed: Similar because both are audible exhalations of displeasure; differs because a huff is a sulky, wounded puff (storming off in a huff), while a snort is sharper and more aggressive — contempt rather than hurt feelings.
  • Snickered: Similar because both signal amusement escaping control; differs because a snicker is a sly half-suppressed giggle, while a snort is louder, coarser, and can carry contempt as well as mirth.
  • Sniggered: Similar because both are nasal-tinted laughs at someone's expense; differs because snigger (British spelling and flavour) stresses disrespectful mockery shared with others, while snort can erupt from one person alone, even unwillingly.
  • Chortled: Similar because both are joyous throaty bursts between laugh and snort; differs because a chortle (Carroll's blend of chuckle and snort) is warm and gleeful, while a snort of laughter is harsher and often derisive.
  • Guffawed: Similar because both are loud involuntary eruptions of mirth; differs because a guffaw is deep belly laughter from the throat, while a snort is routed through the nose and reads as cruder and less generous.
  • Sniffed: Similar because both are meaningful noises made with the nose; differs because a sniff draws air in (disdain inhaled, tears held back), while a snort drives air out — opposite plumbings, cousin attitudes.
  • Spluttered: Similar because both are explosive inarticulate outbursts; differs because splutter sprays confusion and indignation (often with saliva and half-words), while snort is a single clean nasal blast of scorn or mirth.
