• Sobriquet: Similar because both are words or phrases applied to a person to characterize or identify them; differs because a sobriquet is a personal nickname or byname often used in place of the real name, while an epithet is an attributive word or phrase that names a quality and may be purely descriptive.
  • Nickname: Similar because both are familiar labels fastened on a person; differs because a nickname is informal, often affectionate or mocking, and usually replaces the name, while an epithet is a characterizing tag that can be formal, poetic, or honorific and need not stand in for the name.
  • Title: Similar because both are designations attached to a name; differs because a title is an official designation of rank, office, or nobility, while an epithet is a descriptive tag expressing a quality and carries no office.
  • Description: Similar because both characterize a person or thing; differs because a description is a neutral, objective, usually fuller account, whereas an epithet is a brief, pointedly interpretive tag that selects and judges a single quality.
  • Attribute: Similar because both denote a quality regarded as belonging to a person or thing; differs because an attribute is any inherent characteristic, while an epithet is that characteristic expressed as a word or phrase attached to a name.
  • Appellation: Similar because both are names or designations applied to a person; differs because an appellation is simply a name by which someone is known, while an epithet specifically characterizes and interprets the bearer.
  • Designation: Similar because both are words that identify or label someone; differs because a designation is neutral and often official, while an epithet is interpretive and carries rhetorical force.
  • Label: Similar because both are words attached to a person or thing; differs because a label is the general, informal modern word for any classifying tag, while an epithet is a literary or rhetorical characterizing phrase, more formal and classical.
  • Moniker: Similar because both are names applied to a person; differs because a moniker is slang for a nickname or alias, while an epithet belongs to rhetoric and poetry and expresses a characteristic quality.
  • Cognomen: Similar because both are additional names given to a person; differs because a cognomen is specifically the third, hereditary or distinguishing name in the Roman system, while an epithet is descriptive and need not be inherited or legal.
  • Insult: Similar because both can be hostile labels directed at someone; differs because an insult is openly abusive in intent, while an epithet may be neutral or even honorific and only darkens into abuse in hostile phrases such as "racial epithets."
  • Slur: Similar because both can be derogatory labels for a person or group; differs because a slur is a specifically abusive and stigmatizing epithet, while the wider word epithet ranges from praise through neutrality to insult.
  • Abuse / term of abuse: Similar because the plural "epithets" often means hostile labels; differs because abuse is the general act or body of offensive language, while an epithet is a single characterizing word, and the abusive sense of epithet is only one of its meanings.
  • Metaphor: Similar because both are figures that characterize by transfer of a quality; differs because a metaphor is an explicit or implicit comparison, while an epithet need only attach a quality and need not work by comparison.
  • Byword: Similar because both can be labels for which a person or thing is known; differs because a byword denotes a thing proverbially associated with a quality, while an epithet is the attributive word or phrase naming that quality directly.
