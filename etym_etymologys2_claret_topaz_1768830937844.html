PIE *kelh- / *kle- "to call, shout, resound" (reconstructed; also the source of Latin clamare "to shout," calare "to proclaim," clarus "clear, bright, famous," and English low, loud) → Latin clarus "clear, bright, brilliant; loud, distinct; famous" (attested 3rd c. BCE onwards; the sense grew from "audibly clear, resounding" to "visually clear, bright" to "famous") → Latin clarum (neuter adjective used as noun; base of the Romance word) → Old French cler (later clair) "clear, bright" + diminutive suffix -et → Old French claret "a light, clear red wine" (13th c.; literally "the clear little wine") → Middle English claret "a clear or light red wine" (14th c.) → Modern English claret "the red wine of Bordeaux; a deep red colour like that of red wine" (the colour sense develops in the 18th-19th c.). NOTE: The chain is secure; the only nuance is that the English colour-sense is a later transfer from the wine-name, and the modern commercial sense is fixed on Bordeaux wines from the 15th-18th c. onwards.
