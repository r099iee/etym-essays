• Home: Similar because both denote the place where one lives; differs because 'home' is the warm, everyday word with deep emotional and domestic associations, while 'abode' is formal and literary, without the same warmth.
  • Dwelling: Similar because both are formal nouns for a residence; differs because 'dwelling' is a plainer, more neutral legal/building term, while 'abode' leans slightly more literary and implies the act of abiding.
  • Residence: Similar because both denote a place where one lives, often in official language; differs because 'residence' is the formal, administrative term, while 'abode' is more poetic and less bureaucratic.
  • Habitation: Similar because both denote a place of dwelling; differs because 'habitation' emphasizes the fact of inhabiting or is used of wild creatures and settlements, more scientific or formal than 'abode'.
  • House: Similar because both denote a building for living; differs because 'house' is a purely physical building, neutral and everyday, whereas 'abode' names a place of dwelling and carries a settled, dignified tone.
  • Lodging / Lodgings: Similar because both denote a place where one stays; differs because 'lodging' suggests temporary shelter or rented rooms, while 'abode' implies a permanent, settled home.
  • Domicile: Similar because both denote a legal home; differs because 'domicile' is a strictly legal/fiscal term for one's permanent legal residence, while 'abode' is broader and more literary.
