Note on Celtic and Hellenic cognates: The Proto-Indo-European root *h₃erdʰ- ("high, steep") produced cognates across multiple Western Indo-European branches. In Celtic, it yielded Old Irish ard ("high, great, noble") and the Gaulish place-name element Arduenna (referring to the heavily forested, steep highlands of the Ardennes, source of Shakespeare's Forest of Arden). In Hellenic, it developed into Greek orthós (ὀρθός, "straight, upright, correct", source of orthodoxy and orthopedic). In Indo-Iranian, it yielded Sanskrit ūrdhvá- ("rising upward, erect, high"). English arduous directly preserves the Latin topographical development of this vertical root.
