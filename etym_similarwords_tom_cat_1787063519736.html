• Tom: Similar because it is the short form of the same word, naming the male cat; differs because it is clipped, informal, and also serves as a generic male marker in compounds (tom-tit, tom-turkey).
  • Male cat: Similar because it names the same animal; differs because it is a plain descriptive phrase without the cultural baggage of roaming, yowling, and territorial fighting that tom-cat carries.
  • Gib / Gib-cat: Similar because both name a male cat; differs because a gib is specifically a castrated (neutered) male, whereas the tom-cat is the entire, unneutered male.
  • Queen: Similar because both name an adult domestic cat by sex and role; differs because the queen is the female cat (especially one with or seeking kittens), the reproductive opposite of the tom.
  • Mouser: Similar because both name a cat by function rather than breed; differs because a mouser is defined by its job of catching mice, not by its sex, and may be of either sex.
  • Feline: Similar because a tom-cat is a feline; differs because feline is the formal, scientific, gender-neutral word for any member of the cat family.
  • Moggy / Moggie: Similar because both are informal everyday words for a cat; differs because a moggy is any ordinary non-pedigree cat of either sex, while a tom-cat is specifically the male.
  • Tabby: Similar as an everyday cat word; differs because a tabby is a cat of a particular striped coat pattern, of either sex, whereas tom-cat specifies sex, not appearance.
  • Kitten: Similar as a domestic cat word; differs because a kitten is the young of either sex, whereas the tom-cat is the adult male.
