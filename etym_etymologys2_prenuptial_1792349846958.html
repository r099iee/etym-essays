Note on morphological compounding: While both prae- and nuptialis are classical Latin elements, the specific compound adjective prenuptial is a modern 19th-century English formation created to supplement the traditional legal term antenuptial (from Latin ante, "before"). Philologists note that the bridal root *(s)newbʰ- specifically denoted the cultural act of veiled modesty: in Roman ritual, the bride took the flame-colored veil (flammeum) when giving herself in marriage, which gave rise to the asymmetric Roman idiom vir ducit uxorem ("the man leads the wife") versus femina nubit viro ("the woman veils herself for the husband").
