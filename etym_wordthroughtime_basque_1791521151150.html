- 17th c. — English Basque enters through French and Spanish as an ethnonym for the people of the western Pyrenees, with little linguistic content.
- 18th–19th c. — the language sense consolidates as philologists recognize Basque as a pre-Indo-European isolate; the word becomes a byword for linguistic strangeness.
- 19th c. — Romantic nationalism and the fashion trade add two new associations: Basque as a political identity and basque as a fitted bodice.
- 20th c. — the Franco dictatorship, exile, ETA and the autonomy statute make Basque a charged political adjective in news reports.
- Late 20th–21st c. — language revitalization, euskara batua, the Guggenheim and Basque gastronomy turn the word into a cultural and culinary brand as well as an ethnic label.
