Note: The Greek word boúbalos initially referred to a North African species of antelope before Greco-Roman zoologists transferred the designation to large wild bovines (the European wisent and the Asian water buffalo). English originally borrowed the term as buffe leather in the mid-sixteenth century, which was swiftly shortened via ellipsis to the bare monosyllable buff. From this single material node, English generated one of the most prolific metaphorical branching networks in Indo-European lexicography.
