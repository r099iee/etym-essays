— CONSTITUENT ROOT 2: JEWRY —
Biblical Hebrew (c. 10th c. BCE)
Yəhūdī (יְהוּדִי)
‘Judean, of the tribe or land of Judah’
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 3rd c. BCE)
Ioudaîos (Ἰουδαῖος)
‘Judean, Jewish’
↓
Classical Latin (1st c. BCE)
Iūdaeus
‘Judean, Jewish person’
↓
Old French (c. 11th–12th c.)
juerie / juierie
‘Jewish quarter, Jewish population’
↓
Anglo-Norman / Middle English (c. 13th c.)
Iuwerie / Jewerie
‘Jewish district, Jews collectively’
↓
Toponymic Compound in London Records (late 13th c.)
la Vielle Iuwerie
‘the former Jewish quarter of London’
↓
Early Modern to Present English (current)
Old Jewry
‘historic street and former Jewish quarter in London’

Note: In medieval Anglo-Norman municipal rolls, the street was recorded as Vetus Iudaismus and la Vielle Iuwerie. The modifier Old was applied following the 1290 Edict of Expulsion to mark the historical, evacuated district where the Great Synagogue had previously flourished.
