• features: Similar because both refer to the prominent, identifying parts of a human face (eyes, nose, mouth); differs because features is the everyday, neutral term for facial components, whereas lineaments specifically emphasizes the linear contours, structural outlines, and refined artistic proportions of the face.
  • contours: Similar because both denote the outline and curving boundary edges of a physical form; differs because contours is an engineering, topographical, and artistic term applicable to terrain, sculptures, and arbitrary shapes, while lineaments focuses predominantly on faces, living anatomy, or the essential structural traits of concepts.
  • outlines: Similar because both signify the exterior enclosing boundary lines of an object; differs because outlines is a plain, functional term describing basic silhouette or a preliminary summary, lacking the expressive, psychological, and intimate depth associated with lineaments.
  • physiognomy: Similar because both relate to the study and appearance of facial characteristics; differs because physiognomy denotes the total facial cast viewed specifically as an indicator of internal character, ethnic origin, or psychological temperament, whereas lineaments refers to the physical lines and contours themselves.
  • traits: Similar because both refer to distinguishing characteristics; differs because traits applies overwhelmingly to internal psychological habits, behavioral tendencies, or inherited genetic markers, while lineaments denotes visual outlines and structural contours.
  • profiles: Similar because both relate to facial outlines; differs because profiles specifically denotes a side view of the face or a concise biographical sketch, whereas lineaments encompasses the full three-dimensional facial architecture and expressive features.
  • hallmarks: Similar because both serve as distinctive markers of identity; differs because hallmarks (derived from goldsmith stamps) refers to distinctive quality stamps, signature traits, or typical indicators of excellence, lacking any physical anatomical meaning.
  • visages: Similar because both refer to human countenances; differs because visage denotes the face as a whole in terms of its general emotional expression, countenance, or mood, whereas lineaments breaks down the countenance into its constituent linear forms.
