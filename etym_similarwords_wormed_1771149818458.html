• Wriggled: Similar because both involve sinuous, twisting movement; differs because wriggled is purely physical squirming, while wormed carries the metaphorical stealth and persistence of working one's way into something.
  • Insinuated: Similar because both mean entering a situation or mind gradually; differs because insinuate focuses on introducing an idea or suspicion subtly, while wormed stresses bodily persistence in extracting a specific piece of information.
  • Extricated: Similar because both get something out of a difficult tangle; differs because extricate implies careful, often heroic, surgical rescue, while wormed is sly, spineless, and devious.
  • Burrowed: Similar because both involve digging in gradually and deeply; differs because burrowed is physical and neutral (a badger burrowing), while wormed is social and implies manipulating or deceiving people along the way.
  • Infiltrated: Similar because both involve entering a place or group stealthily; differs because infiltrated is often systematic, professional, or espionage-related, while wormed is personal, opportunistic, and contemptuous in tone.
  • Pried: Similar because both obtain information by persistent intrusion; differs because prying is direct, nosy probing of another's affairs, while worming works sideways and deceptively rather than intrusively.
  • Wheedled: Similar because both obtain something by persistent soft pressure; differs because wheedling uses flattery and coaxing openly, while worming is stealthier and more subterranean.
  • Coaxed: Similar because both get a person to reveal something gradually; differs because coaxing is gentle and affectionate, while worming is manipulative and somewhat contemptuous of the person being worked on.
  • Ferreted out: Similar because both find hidden information by patient searching; differs because ferreting is investigative diligence, while worming implies slyness toward a person who is guarding the secret.
  • Elicited: Similar because both draw something out of a person; differs because elicited is neutral and clinical (a response, a reaction), while wormed is vivid, sneaky, and morally shaded.
  • Wrung: Similar because both extract something under pressure; differs because wringing implies force or distress (wringing a confession), while worming implies patient, slippery deception.
