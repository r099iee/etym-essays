• entranced: Similar because both describe being carried into a state of overwhelming delight or fascination; differs because entranced stresses a passive, trance-like, hypnotic absorption that freezes attention, while enraptured stresses the active, joyful sweep of emotion.
  • delighted: Similar because both describe great pleasure; differs because delighted is a lighter, fully conscious pleasure that leaves reason intact, while enraptured is far more intense and momentarily overrides rational awareness.
  • captivated: Similar because both describe being strongly charmed; differs because captivated describes attention being held and won (as by a captor or a spell), while enraptured describes joy flooding the whole emotional state rather than mere attention.
  • spellbound: Similar because both describe being overwhelmed by fascination; differs because spellbound implies being frozen by magic-like wonder, whereas enraptured emphasizes rapture and ecstatic pleasure rather than stillness.
  • ecstatic: Similar because both name a peak of emotional intensity; differs because ecstatic often implies an outward, visible, even frantic explosion of joy, while enraptured is a deeper, more inward being carried away.
  • transported: Similar because both carry the image of being carried off by emotion; differs because transported is more archaic and general (and also means "banished, deported"), while enraptured is specifically about delight.
  • ravished: Similar because both share the Latin root of seizing and carrying away; differs because ravished is now loaded with sexual-violence and archaic overtones, while enraptured is pure delight.
  • charmed: Similar because both describe being pleasantly won over; differs because charmed is light, conscious, and polite, while enraptured is deep and overwhelming.
  • thrilled: Similar because both describe intense excitement and pleasure; differs because thrilled suggests a sudden thrill or frisson (from "to pierce"), while enraptured suggests a sustained state of being carried away.
  • elated: Similar because both describe a high, joyful emotional state; differs because elated is a buoyant, lifted happiness (from Latin "carried up"), while enraptured is a rapt, absorbed transport.
  • mesmerized: Similar because both describe total absorption; differs because mesmerized is a hypnotic, passive fixation (from Mesmer's magnetism), while enraptured is an active joy.
