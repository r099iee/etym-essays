[Component 1: shaggy]
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*skok- / *skek-
"to shake, toss, rough hair"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*skaggwaz / *skaggwō
"tuft of hair, beard, rough wool"
↓
Old English / Old Norse (c. 8th–10th c.)
sceacga / skegg
"rough matted hair, beard"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.)
schagge + -y
"rough, hairy, covered with tufted wool"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th c.)
shaggy
"having thick, coarse, tangled hair"

[Component 2: maned]
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*mon- / *moni-
"neck, nape of the neck"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*manō
"mane, neck hair of a horse"
↓
Old English (c. 9th c.)
manu
"mane of an animal"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.)
mane + -ed
"having a mane"
↓
Modern English Compound (18th–19th c.)
shaggy-maned
"having a thick, unkempt, or coarse mane"
