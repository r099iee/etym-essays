- Sixteenth century - Gypsies is a legal and ethnographic label for itinerant people believed to be Egyptians; connotation suspicious, vagrant, foreign.
- Seventeenth and eighteenth centuries - The word becomes the ordinary ethnic name, tied to fortune-telling, music, horse dealing, and petty theft in popular imagination.
- Nineteenth century - Romantic literature turns the figure into a symbol of freedom and mystery, while racial science and vagrancy laws keep the hostile side alive.
- Twentieth century - Gypsies becomes increasingly pejorative; the verb to gyp is openly offensive; Nazi persecution and the Porajmos deepen the word's stigma.
- Twenty-first century - Widely avoided in official and media usage in favour of Roma; survives in historical quotations, organization names, and artistic genres such as Gypsy jazz.
