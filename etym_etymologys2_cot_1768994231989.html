COT (the light portable bed) — a loan from Hindi, with a separate native homonym "cot" (hut). Complete derivation chain for each in isolation. CHAIN 1 (the light bed — the word on this card): 1. Substrate root *khaṭṭ- "bedstead, frame of a light woven bed" — no secure PIE ancestry; widely taken to be a substrate item (Dravidian or Munda) or a very old cultural Wanderwort of the Indian subcontinent (reconstructed as a source; the non-Indo-European origin is secure in the handbooks, though the precise donor family is debated). 2. Sanskrit khaṭvā (खट्वा) "bedstead, cot, couch on a frame" (attested in epic and classical literature, c. 1st millennium BCE). 3. Middle Indo-Aryan / Prakrit *khaṭṭā "bedstead" (attested at the Prakrit stage). 4. Hindi–Urdu khaṭ (खाट) "light Indian bedstead, cot — a frame strung with rope or woven webbing" (attested in the modern vernacular; sisters include Bengali khāṭ, Odia khāṭa, Gujarati khāṭ). 5. English cot "light portable bed" — borrowed in the Anglo-Indian usage of the East India Company's world from Hindi khaṭ in the 17th century (attested): the rootWord on this card, COT. 6. Sense developments: American English cot "folding field or army bed" (standard 20th-century sense); British English cot "baby's crib" (19th-century transferred sense) → cot death (SIDS), cot bed, cot sheet; compounds camp cot, hospital cot, folding cot. Parallel colonial borrowing: Portuguese took the same word as cata / catre (16th c.) → Brazilian Portuguese catre "a rough, poor bed." CHAIN 2 (the native homonym "cot" = hut/shelter — NOT the word on this card): 1. Proto-Germanic *kutan- "small dwelling, hut, shed, shelter" (reconstructed; no secure PIE root). 2. Old English cot / cote "hut, cottage, shelter, small dwelling" (attested from the 8th–9th c. CE). 3. Middle English cot "small house, cottage, shelter" (attested from the 12th c. CE). 4. English cot / cote "a small shelter, especially for sheep or birds" — as in sheepcot and dovecote, and the second element of cottage (from Old French cot + -age). This "cot" shares only the spelling with the Indian loan and is a separate lexeme.
