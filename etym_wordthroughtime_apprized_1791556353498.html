- 16th to 17th Century: Valuation, pricing, and Scots legal execution - Primarily perceived as a legal and commercial term for assessing the monetary worth of debtor property, confiscated maritime vessels, or probate estates.
- Late 17th to 18th Century: Epistemic notification and diplomatic intelligence - Emerged in formal statecraft, epistolary correspondence, and philosophical discourse as a refined term for making someone cognizant of crucial facts.
- 19th Century: Victorian administrative, naval, and literary ubiquity - Widely employed in classic prose (Austen, Melville, Dickens) for polite notification, with "apprize" and "apprise" used interchangeably across both British and American English.
- 20th to 21st Century: Archaic/variant spelling division and elevated formal register - Modern style guides codified "apprise" for notification and "appraise" for valuation, leaving "apprized" as a dignified, historical, or legalistic variant signaling high-register erudition.
