- Late 14th c. — inciting incident and opportune moment: enters Middle English from Old French as a noun denoting an external occurrence, pretext, or favorable window of opportunity.
- 15th c. — moral cause and stumbling block: employed in theological and legal discourse for a circumstance that induces a person to sin, stumble, or act foolishly.
- Late 15th to 16th c. — verbal functional shift: grammaticalized into a transitive verb meaning 'to cause, effect, or bring about' through an inciting incident.
- 17th to 18th c. — philosophical and formal staple: becomes a cornerstone of rationalist philosophy (occasionalism) and high Augustan literature, conveying gentle or dignified causation without vulgar physical force.
- 19th c. to present — dual life of formal verb and ceremonial noun: while the verb remains an elevated synonym for 'to cause' in literary and judicial prose, the noun dominates everyday speech for celebratory events, anniversaries, and rare occurrences.
