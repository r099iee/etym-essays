• Uncertain: Similar because both describe something not sure or not secure; differs because uncertain is broader and weaker — it can mean merely unknown — and lacks precarious's sense of dependence on another's will or of imminent collapse.
  • Insecure: Similar because both describe lack of stability; differs because insecure stresses structural or psychological wobbliness (a regime, a ladder, a person's confidence), while precarious emphasizes revocable tenure and dangerous fragility.
  • Unstable: Similar because both describe things liable to change or collapse; differs because unstable stresses tendency to shift or topple (chemistry, emotion, structures), while precarious stresses being held only by favour or chance.
  • Perilous: Similar because both describe serious risk; differs because perilous emphasizes active, immediate danger, while precarious situations may be quiet yet fragile — a tension that could break without an active threat.
  • Dangerous: Similar because both imply risk of harm; differs because dangerous is broad and concrete, describing actual hazards, while precarious focuses on insecure holding and dependence rather than on the threat itself.
  • Fragile: Similar because both describe things easily broken or lost; differs because fragile is material or emotional brittleness, while precarious retains the legal-legal overtone of being held only by permission.
  • Ephemeral: Similar because both involve impermanence; differs because ephemeral stresses brief duration (lasting a day), while precarious stresses revocability and insecurity rather than shortness of life.
  • Unsteady: Similar because both describe lack of firmness; differs because unsteady is physical and concrete (a hand, a gait, a ladder), while precarious extends to tenure, health, employment, and existential states.
  • Shaky: Similar because both describe instability; differs because shaky is informal and physical (a shaky table, shaky evidence), while precarious is more formal and carries dependence on another's will.
  • Vulnerable: Similar because both describe exposure to harm; differs because vulnerable stresses openness to attack or injury, while precarious stresses insecure holding that may be lost or collapse.
  • Risky: Similar because both involve exposure to loss; differs because risky emphasizes the chance of a bad outcome in a venture, while precarious emphasizes the fragility and dependence of the position itself.
  • Dicey: Similar because both describe uncertain, chancy situations; differs because dicey is informal slang for risky uncertainty, while precarious is the formal word carrying dependence and fragility.
  • Tentative: Similar because both suggest something not firmly established; differs because tentative describes hesitation or provisionality (a tentative agreement), while precarious describes insecure holding with real risk of loss.
  • Contingent: Similar because both describe dependence on circumstances; differs because contingent is a neutral logical or legal term (dependent on a condition), while precarious adds the danger of collapse.
  • Tenuous: Similar because both describe something weak or flimsy; differs because tenuous stresses slightness of connection or basis (a tenuous link), while precarious stresses insecurity and risk of falling or being revoked.
  • Chancy: Similar because both describe uncertain outcomes; differs because chancy is informal and focuses on luck, while precarious focuses on insecure, revocable holding.
