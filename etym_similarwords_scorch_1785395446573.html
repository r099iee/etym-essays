• Singe: Similar because both burn a surface with heat; differs because singe is a brief, light contact with flame that only blackens the tips, often trivial and unintentional, while scorch implies discolouration and shrivelling of the whole outer layer.
  • Sear: Similar because both dry or wither by sudden intense heat; differs because sear stresses quick browning and colour (as in cooking) rather than blackening, with less deep surface damage than scorch.
  • Char: Similar because both burn and blacken something; differs because char implies deeper carbonisation and a smouldering blackness that reaches into the substance, while scorch stays at the surface.
  • Burn: Similar as the general word for destruction by fire; differs because burn implies nothing superficial and can consume entirely, while scorch is limited to the outer layer of the object.
  • Blaze: Similar because both are words of intense heat; differs because blaze describes the fierce burning of the flame itself, not its effect on a surface, whereas scorch names the seared surface left behind.
  • Parch: Similar because both emphasise dryness; differs because parch implies dryness without necessarily any fire at all — the sun or drought parches — while scorch carries the image of heat applied directly to a surface.
  • Shrivel: Similar because both name destruction by heat and are natural partners; differs because shrivel folds and deforms the whole body, while scorch sears and blackens the surface without changing its shape.
  • Scald: Similar because both burn something; differs because scald is specifically burning by hot liquid or steam, while scorch is burning by dry heat.
