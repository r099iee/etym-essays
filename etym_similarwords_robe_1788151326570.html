• Gown: Similar because both name a long, loose outer garment worn on formal or informal occasions; differs because gown emphasises the specific garment of a graduate, judge, or surgeon (and, for women, an evening dress), whereas robe stresses envelopment and official investiture and never means a woman’s evening dress in British usage.
  • Vestment: Similar because both denote liturgical garments; differs because vestment is a restricted technical term for clergy’s ritual clothing, while robe covers everything from bathrobe to coronation robe.
  • Cassock: Similar as an ankle-length clerical garment; differs by being institution-specific (clergy, choir) and fitted at the shoulders, while robe is generic and looser.
  • Cloak: Similar because both are loose outer coverings that envelop the wearer; differs because a cloak is sleeveless and generally worn for warmth or concealment over other clothes, whereas a robe carries rank and ceremony and may be sleeved.
  • Mantle: Similar because it can name a loose enveloping outer garment and, figuratively, a covering or inherited responsibility; differs in that mantle today is overwhelmingly figurative (“the mantle of leadership”), while robe keeps a strong literal and ceremonial presence.
  • Habit: Similar because monastic and riding habits are robe-like costumes; differs because habit specifically implies the rule-governed uniform of a religious order or a riding outfit, with no general application.
  • Dressing gown / bathrobe: Similar because in North American use these are the ordinary referents of unqualified robe; differ as precise synonyms that lack the ceremonial spectrum of the full word.
  • Dress: Similar in the older general sense of “outer garment, apparel”; differs because modern dress means women’s frocks or clothing generally, losing the flowing form and official weight of robe.
  • Stole: Similar as a liturgical vestment; differs as a narrow band worn over the shoulders rather than a full enveloping garment.
  • Surplice: Similar as a white ecclesiastical overgarment; differs as a specific, relatively fitted linen vestment of choir and clergy, not a general robe.
  • Alb: Similar as a priestly white robe; differs as the specific under-vestment worn at Mass, while robe is the generic term.
  • Kaftan: Similar as a long loose enveloping garment of Eastern origin; differs in ethnic specificity and in lacking all official, judicial, or academic connotation.
