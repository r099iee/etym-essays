First element — way:
Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*weǵʰ-
‘to go, carry, move’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*wegaz
‘way, road, journey’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
weġ
‘path, road, course’
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
wey
‘road, path, manner’

Second element — farer, from fare:
Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*per-
‘to go forth, cross over’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*faraną
‘to go, travel, sail’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
faran + -ere
‘to journey’ + doer suffix
‘one who journeys’
↓
Middle English (15th c.)
weifarere
‘one who fares along the way’
↓
Modern English (plural)
wayfarers
‘travellers on foot’
