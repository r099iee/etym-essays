• Waverley: Similar because it is the first Scott novel and names the whole series; differs because Waverley is Hanoverian-reconciled, while Redgauntlet is intransigent Jacobite.
  • Rob Roy: Similar because it is another Scott Border-Jacobite novel with clan hero; differs because Rob Roy is Highland outlaw, Redgauntlet Lowland laird.
  • Ivanhoe: Similar because it is Scott's most famous chivalric novel; differs because Ivanhoe is medieval England, Redgauntlet 18th-century Scotland.
  • Guy Mannering: Similar because it shares Solway smuggling and Border setting; differs because its theme is inheritance, not Jacobitism.
  • The Heart of Midlothian: Similar because it is a late Scott masterpiece of Scots law and loyalty; differs because its heroine Jeanie Deans is humble Presbyterian, not Jacobite laird.
  • Bride of Lammermoor: Similar because it is a tragic Border tale; differs because its tragedy is domestic, while Redgauntlet's is political.
  • Bonnie Prince Charlie (Charles Edward Stuart): Similar because he is the Cause Redgauntlet serves; differs because Charlie is historical prince, Redgauntlet fictional adherent.
  • Darsie Latimer: Similar because he is the novel's young hero and Redgauntlet heir; differs because Darsie is vacillating youth, Sir Edward resolved maturity.
  • Wandering Willie: Similar because his Tale is the novel's inset masterpiece; differs because Willie is blind fiddler-narrator, not laird.
  • Jacobite: Similar because Redgauntlet embodies Jacobitism; differs because Jacobite is the general partisan, Redgauntlet one specific character.
