• Inventive: Similar because both describe the ability to create new things; differs because inventive focuses on producing new devices or ideas, while ingenious stresses the brilliant cleverness and elegance of the design itself.
  • Clever: Similar because both praise mental quickness; differs because clever is broader and can be mildly cunning or glib, while ingenious is unreservedly admiring and implies original brilliance.
  • Creative: Similar because both involve producing something new; differs because creative applies to artistic imagination and self-expression, while ingenious applies to solving problems and constructing elegant solutions.
  • Original: Similar because both imply something new and non-derivative; differs because original describes the novelty of the thing itself, while ingenious describes the cleverness behind it.
  • Resourceful: Similar because both describe solving problems well; differs because resourceful means making clever use of what is available under constraint, while ingenious implies a deeper inventive brilliance.
  • Brilliant: Similar because both signal exceptional intelligence; differs because brilliant can describe general intellect or dazzling results, while ingenious is specific to inventive cleverness.
  • Astute: Similar because both describe sharp mental ability; differs because astute is shrewd judgement and insight into situations, while ingenious is constructive inventiveness.
  • Shrewd: Similar because both describe quick, effective thinking; differs because shrewd has a worldly, often calculating cast, while ingenious is purely admiring.
  • Witty: Similar because both involve quick, surprising mental turns; differs because witty is specifically verbal humour, while ingenious covers ideas and devices generally.
  • Deft: Similar because both describe skilled execution; differs because deft stresses manual or practical nimbleness, while ingenious stresses clever conception.
  • Imaginative: Similar because both involve forming new ideas; differs because imaginative is broad creative fantasy, while ingenious is problem-solving cleverness.
  • Innovative: Similar because both involve new departures; differs because innovative describes introducing change (often institutional or commercial), while ingenious describes the cleverness of a particular solution.
  • Gifted / Talented: Similar because both praise inborn ability; differs because gifted and talented refer generally to natural aptitude, while ingenious singles out inventive cleverness.
  • Ingenuous: NOT a synonym — ingenuous means innocent, open, and candid; the two are confused because of near-identical spelling, but ingenious praises cleverness while ingenuous praises frankness.
