- [c. 1350–1500: Middle English 'flappen'] - Onomatopoeic root imitating loose slapping or fluttering motions.
- [c. 1590–1680: Early Modern English 'flappy'] - Used to describe loose, pendulous, flapping flesh, ears, or sails.
- [c. 1690–1750: 18th Century 'flabby'] - Emerged as a phonetic lenition (-pp- to -bb-), specifically denoting soft, untoned, spongy flesh or deflated objects.
- [c. 1800–1900: Victorian Era] - Broadened into moral and literary criticism denoting weak will, undisciplined character, and bloated writing.
- [c. 1900–Present: Modern English] - Standard term for lacking muscle tone, deflated balls, bureaucratic inefficiency, or flat low-acid wine.
