• heir: Similar because both receive property after death; differs because an heir inherits by law or blood, while a legatee takes only by being named in a will.
  • beneficiary: Similar because both receive benefits from a will or trust; differs because beneficiary is broader (trusts, insurance, pensions) while legatee is will-specific and formal.
  • devisee: Similar because both take under a will; differs because traditionally a devisee takes land (real property) while a legatee takes money or goods.
  • inheritor: Similar because both come into property from the dead; differs because inheritor is general and informal, legatee is technical probate term.
  • legate: Similar because shares the same root and spelling; differs because a legate is a diplomatic envoy, not a will-beneficiary — a classic false friend.
  • recipient: Similar because both receive something; differs because recipient is generic, legatee specifies testamentary receipt.
  • donee: Similar because both receive a gift; differs because a donee receives an inter vivos gift, a legatee receives a testamentary gift.
  • assignee: Similar because Dickens lists it alongside legatee and both take transferred rights; differs because an assignee takes by assignment during life, a legatee by will after death.
  • successor: Similar because both follow another in entitlement; differs because successor implies office or position, legatee implies property.
  • legatee-at-law / next of kin: Similar because both may take the residue; differs because next of kin take on intestacy, legatees take by will.
  • trust beneficiary: Similar because both benefit from a dead person's bounty; differs because trust beneficiaries take through trustees, legatees take directly under a will.
