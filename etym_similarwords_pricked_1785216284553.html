• perked up: Similar because both describe ears (or a head) set suddenly upright with attention; differs because perked up is softer and more cheerful — a brightening — with none of the sharpness or edge of alarm carried by pricked.
  • cocked: Similar because both describe angling the ears or head toward a sound; differs because cocked suggests a deliberate, conscious positioning — a controlled tilt, with the flavour of firearms — while pricked up is the involuntary, animal-quick reflex.
  • strained: Similar because both describe trying hard to hear; differs because strained names the effort of attention itself, while pricked up names the sharp, sudden reflex of the ear standing erect.
  • raised: Similar because both describe lifting the ears; differs because raised is neutral and literal, with no idiomatic snap, suddenness, or note of sharpened alertness.
  • listened: Similar because both involve attending to a sound; differs because listening is the sustained act of hearing, while pricking up one's ears is the single instantaneous gesture that begins it.
  • pierced: Similar because both derive from the act of making a sharp point enter; differs because pierced is purely physical (a needle, a bullet) and has none of the idiomatic gesture of "pricking up one's ears."
  • goaded: Similar because both can mean urged or incited; differs because goaded implies being driven by external pressure (a goad is a pointed stick), while pricked in its inciting sense is the lighter, sharper nudge — and "pricked up" is about ears, not action.
