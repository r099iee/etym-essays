Note on compounding, phonology, and manuscript orthography: The proper name Śarabhaṅga is a classical exocentric (bahuvrīhi) compound composed of śarabha ("mythical beast of supreme power") and aṅga ("limb, body"), joined by regular Sanskrit vowel sandhi (-a + a- → -ā- / -a- shortening in proper names: śarabha-aṅga → śarabhaṅga). Indo-Europeanists connect śarabha to PIE *ḱer- ("horn, horned animal"), cognate with Latin cervus ("deer"). In traditional Devanāgarī and Bengali manuscript traditions, the guttural class-nasal ṅ immediately preceding the voiced guttural stop g is written with an anusvāra or class-nasal ligature (-ṅga), which in certain Western scholarly transliterations and critical editions was typographically rendered as Śarabhan̄ga or divided at the morpheme boundary as the stem Śarabhan-.
