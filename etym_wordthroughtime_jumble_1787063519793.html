- c. 1500 - 'jumble' first appears meaning to move about confusedly and noisily - things tumbling and jostling together
- 1520s - transitive use - to mix things up in disorder - 'jumbled' becomes the word for a heap that has lost its order
- 17th c. - noun use flourishes - a jumble is a confused mixture; separately, 'jumble' names a thin crisp sweet cake, a domestic dead end
- 19th c. - British 'jumble sale' is born from the heap of donated second-hand things - jumble acquires a warm, thrifty, parish-life flavour
- 20th c. - jumble moves into minds and news - 'a jumble of memories,' 'a jumble of rumours'; the American Jumble word puzzle (1954) makes it a household name
- 21st c. - jumble stays the friendly, mild word for disorder - a muddle, not a catastrophe - and Tolkien's 'jumble of smithereens' still shows its oldest, most violent edge
