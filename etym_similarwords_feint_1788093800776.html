• Feign: Similar because it is the verb of the same family (Old French feindre), meaning to pretend or counterfeit; differs because feign is the act of pretending in general while feint is the specific staged action or attack.
  • Pretence (pretense): Similar because both name a false appearance designed to deceive; differs because pretence is any show or claim of something false, while feint keeps the tactical, spatial sense of a staged manoeuvre.
  • Sham: Similar because both denote something false passed off as real; differs because sham stresses the worthless counterfeit itself (a sham document, a sham marriage), while feint is the performed motion of deception.
  • Ruse: Similar because both are calculated tricks to deceive; differs because a ruse is a verbal or positional stratagem of any kind, while a feint is specifically a movement that simulates an attack.
  • Stratagem: Similar because both are planned deceptions; differs because stratagem is the grand, general scheme of deception, of which the feint is a single executed move.
  • Ploy: Similar because both are tactical moves to gain advantage; differs because ploy is informal and broad (any manoeuvre, verbal or not), while feint implies simulated force.
  • Gambit: Similar because both are opening moves that risk something for advantage; differs because gambit is chess-like and intellectual (an opening sacrifice), while feint is physical and military.
  • Bluff: Similar because both are deceptions of intention; differs because a bluff is a boast of strength made with words or manner, while a feint is an acted threat in space.
  • Diversion: Similar because both draw attention elsewhere; differs because diversion is the effect or the secondary action that attracts attention, while feint names the deceptive movement itself.
  • Decoy: Similar because both lure an opponent into reacting wrongly; differs because a decoy is an object or person used as bait, while a feint is a performed action by the attacker.
  • Dummy: Similar because a 'dummy pass' or 'dummy attack' is exactly a sports feint; differs because dummy is informal and chiefly British sporting slang, without military or literary weight.
  • Subterfuge: Similar because both are deceitful devices; differs because subterfuge is any underhand scheme (word, evasion, concealment), while feint is the open performance of a false move.
  • Artifice: Similar because both are cleverly contrived deceits; differs because artifice emphasises skill in contrivance (also positive in art), while feint emphasises the staged false strike.
  • Faint (spelling-twin caution): Similar because it is the historical doublet of feint, sharing the same French participle; differs completely in meaning - faint means weak, dim, or a loss of consciousness, and confusing the two in writing is a classic howler.
