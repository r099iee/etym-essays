• Bronco: Similar because both refer to untamed, fiery horses of the American West; differs because bronco (from Spanish bronco, 'rough, wild') specifically denotes an unbroken, bucking horse prone to throwing riders in a rodeo, whereas mustang refers to the free-roaming feral breed itself.
  • Feral Horse: Similar because both scientifically designate domestic horses that have escaped captivity and adapted to life in the wild; differs because feral horse is a neutral, universal zoological descriptor, while mustang carries rich historical, geographic, and cultural connotations specific to the American West.
  • Cayuse: Similar because both designate small, hardy Western horses associated with frontier history; differs because cayuse originally referred specifically to the Indian pony bred by the Cayuse people of the Pacific Northwest, later becoming a general Western slang term for an ordinary ranch horse.
  • Stallion: Similar because both can evoke masculine equine power and wild spirit; differs because stallion strictly denotes an uncastrated adult male horse of any domestic or wild breed, whereas mustang describes a specific feral population regardless of sex or age.
  • Steed: Similar because both refer to horses imbued with nobility, strength, or spirit; differs because steed is an archaic, elevated literary term for a spirited riding horse or knight's mount, whereas mustang denotes a rugged, uncurbed feral animal of the plains.
  • Colt: Similar because both describe horses of active, spirited energy; differs because a colt is specifically a young male horse under the age of four, whereas mustang designates the breed and feral lifestyle rather than age.
  • Maverick (Figurative): Similar because both metaphorically describe fiercely independent individuals who reject conformity and wander outside the herd; differs because maverick derives from unbranded Texas cattle (Samuel Maverick) and emphasizes political or intellectual dissent, whereas mustang emphasizes raw, unbridled vitality and resistance to domestication.
