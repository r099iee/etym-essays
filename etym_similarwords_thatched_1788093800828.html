• roofed: Similar because both mean having a roof; differs because roofed is generic for any roofing material, while thatched specifies straw or reeds.
  • covered: Similar because both mean having a covering; differs because covered is totally generic and says nothing about roofing.
  • tiled: Similar because both are roof-covering adjectives; differs because tiled names fired clay or stone units, the urban rival of thatch.
  • slated: Similar because both describe roof coverings; differs because slate is stone, quarried and heavy, the prestige roofing of the modern era.
  • shingled: Similar because both describe roofs of overlapping layers; differs because shingles are wooden boards or asphalt units, not plant material.
  • straw-covered: Similar because both involve straw; differs because straw-covered is loose and generic (litter, stalls), while thatched implies the engineered craft of roof covering.
  • reed-roofed: Similar because both name plant-material roofing; differs because reed-roofed is a technical subset naming the material, while thatched also covers straw, sedge and heather.
  • bushy: Similar because both describe thick abundant hair; differs because bushy implies spreading and unkempt, while thatched implies a dense, close mat.
  • shaggy: Similar because both describe thick rough hair; differs because shaggy implies long, rough and untidy locks, while thatch is a compact mat.
  • hirsute: Similar because both mean hairy; differs because hirsute is formal and technical and usually of body hair, while thatched is jocular and of head hair.
  • mop-haired: Similar because both describe a thick head of hair; differs because mop implies an unruly round mass, while thatch implies a flat dense covering.
