• Middle-class: Similar because both refer to the intermediate socio-economic stratum between working labor and elite aristocracy; differs because middle-class is predominantly neutral and sociological, whereas bourgeois carries heavy ideological, political, or pejorative cultural baggage.
  • Philistine: Similar because both critique individuals indifferent or hostile to artistic and intellectual refinement; differs because philistine centers purely on anti-intellectual vulgarity and lack of aesthetic culture, regardless of class background.
  • Materialistic: Similar because both describe an obsession with physical possessions, financial wealth, and comfort; differs because materialistic is a broad philosophical or psychological trait not limited to historical urban townspeople or socioeconomic structures.
  • Conventional: Similar because both denote adherence to standard social norms and respectable behavior; differs because conventional lacks the economic dimension of capital ownership and property interests intrinsic to bourgeois.
  • Capitalist: Similar because both denote beneficiaries and owners within market-driven economies; differs because capitalist is an economic/financial role (investor/owner of capital), while bourgeois encompasses cultural habits, class morality, lifestyle, and worldviews.
  • Provincial: Similar because both can connote narrow-mindedness and limited horizons; differs because provincial specifically reflects geographic isolation from metropolitan culture, whereas bourgeois is fundamentally urban in origin.
  • Petite-bourgeois (Petty-bourgeois): Similar because both describe non-aristocratic property holders; differs because petty-bourgeois specifically denotes small shopkeepers, artisans, and lower-middle-class clerks who aspire to bourgeois status but lack substantial capital.
  • Suburban: Similar because both describe comfortable, safe, domestic consumer lifestyle; differs because suburban specifies spatial/geographic residential patterns and modern zoning rather than historical socioeconomic class formation.
