• Kind: Similar because both name a category of people or things; differs because kind is neutral and gentle, whereas ilk in modern English carries a sharp, dismissive edge.
  • Sort: Similar because both denote a rough category; differs because sort is informal everyday vocabulary, while ilk is literary, archaic, or stylistically marked.
  • Type: Similar because both classify; differs because type is neutral, analytical, and near-technical, while ilk implies a shared faction or disreputable group.
  • Class: Similar because both group people; differs because class suggests social or economic strata, whereas ilk groups by character, behaviour, or allegiance rather than by rank.
  • Breed: Similar because both label a type of person; differs because breed uses a biological metaphor (a rare breed) and can be admiring, while ilk is contemptuous and factional.
  • Stripe: Similar because both label a political or moral category; differs because stripe derives from the military uniform's rank stripes (politicians of every stripe), while ilk derives from Scottish estate titles.
  • Persuasion: Similar because both name a category of belief; differs because persuasion emphasizes religious or political conviction and is respectful, while ilk dismisses the group as a mere breed.
  • Stamp / Kidney / Feather: Similar because all are idiomatic synonyms for "kind, sort" (of the same stamp, of the same kidney, birds of a feather); differs because all three are more colourful and rarer than ilk, which is the most current of the set.
