Linear derivation chains for WOBBLY (two morphemes: root 'wobble' + adjectival suffix '-y'). CHAIN 1 — root 'wobble' (expressive / imitative; no PIE ancestry): 1. North Sea Germanic expressive base *wab- 'loose, side-to-side motion' (sound-symbolic syllable; no reconstructible PIE root — the family is expressive, not regular-inherited). 2. Low German wabbeln 'to shake, quiver, move unsteadily' (cognate; also Dutch wabbelen, West Frisian wabbelje). 3. English wabble (early form, 16th c.) → wobble 'to move unsteadily from side to side' (mid-17th c.). 4. wobble + -y → WOBBLY (adjective, first recorded 1851). Note: the same expressive family *wab- / *waf- produced Old English wafian 'to wave the hands, be restless' (source of wave), Old Norse vafra 'to flicker, hover, move unsteadily' (source of waver), and dialectal English waffle 'to waver, vacillate'. Parallel echoic forms elsewhere (e.g. German wabbeln) are convergent coinages, not descendants of any common PIE root. CHAIN 2 — suffix '-y' (Indo-European): 1. PIE *-ikos — adjectival suffix 'having the nature of, full of' (sister reflexes Greek -ικός -ikós, Latin -icus as in publicus, Sanskrit -ika). 2. Proto-Germanic *-īgaz — adjectival suffix 'possessing, prone to'. 3. Old English -ig — adjectival suffix (mihtig 'mighty', hālig 'holy', wīndig 'windy'). 4. English -y / -ie — adjectival suffix (windy, jumpy, rocky). 5. wobble + -y → WOBBLY.
