• Spells: Similar in magical formulas that work change; differs because spells is the general term for any magic-working, while incantations specifies the spoken or chanted formula.
  • Charms: Similar in magical words or objects; differs because charms are protective trinkets or light spells, while incantations are ceremonial spoken formulas, and cantraps are mischievous folk charms.
  • Invocations: Similar in spoken magical formulas; differs because invocations specifically summon a deity or spirit to attend, while incantations work directly through sound to alter reality.
  • Enchantments: Similar in magic worked by words; differs because enchantments (from Latin cantare, to sing) emphasize the bewitching or binding of a person or object, while incantations emphasize the ritual utterance itself.
  • Formulas: Similar in set verbal patterns; differs because formulas is neutral and scientific (chemical or mathematical), while incantations is the magical, chanted register of the same word.
  • Mantras: Similar in repeated sacred utterance; differs because mantras are the chanted sacred formulas of South Asian tradition (Sanskrit man-tra, from man, to think), while incantations belongs to Western magical and liturgical tradition.
  • Tricks: Similar in the small, clever feats of the cantrap; differs because tricks is wholly secular sleight or deception, while cantraps are folk charms believed to work magic.
  • Hexes: Similar in mischievous or harmful spells; differs because hexes are curses laid on a victim (from German Hexe, witch), while cantraps are small, often merely troublesome folk charms.
  • Glyphs: Similar in carved or inscribed symbols; differs because glyphs is the general term for any carved mark (from Greek glyphein, to carve), while runes are specifically the ancient Germanic alphabetic letters, each with a name and a mystery.
  • Sigils: Similar in symbolic marks of power; differs because sigils are individually designed occult seals (from Latin sigillum, a little seal), while runes are letters of a fixed historical alphabet.
  • Sorceries: Similar in the art of magic; differs because sorceries is the whole dark or powerful discipline (from Latin sors, lot, fate), while thaumaturgies emphasizes the production of wonders or miracles as a trained art.
  • Miracles: Similar in marvellous acts; differs because miracles are supernatural interventions attributed to divine power, while thaumaturgies are wonders worked by human art or saintly skill.
  • Wizardry: Similar in the practice of magic; differs because wizardry is the general craft of the wizard (from wise), while thaumaturgies is the elevated, near-clinical term for wonder-working.
  • Conjurations: Similar in magical operations; differs because conjurations summon or command spirits through formulas, while thaumaturgies perform wonders without necessarily invoking a being.
  • Magic / Magics: Similar in the manipulation of the supernatural; differs because magic is the general term, while each of the four card words names a specific medium or register of magic.
  • Abracadabra: Similar in a word believed to work magic when spoken; differs because abracadabra is a specific nonsense charm word, while incantations are structured ceremonial formulas.
