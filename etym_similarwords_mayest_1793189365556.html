• Canst: Similar because it is the same archaic thou-grammar applied to the neighbouring modal of ability; differs because canst (thou canst) stresses power and knowing-how, while mayest stresses permission and possibility.
  • Mightest: Similar because it is the past-tense twin of the same verb in thou-form; differs because mightest is remoter — weaker permission, dimmer possibility, cast further into past or unreality — while mayest is present and nearer.
  • Shalt: Similar because both are archaic thou-modals of ordinance and prophecy; differs because shalt (thou shalt) commands or foretells with authority, while mayest permits or admits with gentleness.
  • Shouldst: Similar because both guide conduct in old moral diction; differs because shouldst (thou shouldst) binds with duty and advice, while mayest frees with leave and hope.
  • Oughtest: Similar because it likewise governs a thou subject with moral force; differs because oughtest is obligation pure and simple, while mayest never obliges — it allows.
  • Wouldst: Similar because both are courteous old modals of will and wish; differs because wouldst (thou wouldst) speaks of willingness and desire, while mayest speaks of leave and chance.
  • Couldst: Similar because both open hypothetical doors for thou; differs because couldst is past or conditional ability, while mayest is present permission or possibility.
  • Dost: Similar because it shares the -st archaic second-person dress and the biblical register; differs because dost is the plain auxiliary of doing, while mayest is a modal colouring of permission.
