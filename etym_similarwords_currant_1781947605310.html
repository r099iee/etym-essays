• Raisin: Similar because both are dried grapes; differs because a raisin is any dried grape, while a currant is specifically the tiny, seedless, dark Corinth type, smaller and sharper-tasting.
  • Sultana: Similar because it is a small dried grape; differs because the sultana is pale, larger, and milder, while traditional currant buns specify the small dark Corinth fruit.
  • Zante currant: Similar because both name the Corinth dried grape; differs because "Zante currant" is the trade-specific name emphasizing the Zakynthos origin, while plain "currant" is the general kitchen word.
  • Blackcurrant: Similar because it shares the currant name; differs because it is a fresh tart berry of the Ribes genus, a different plant that borrowed the name by resemblance and is used for juice, jams, and cordials.
  • Redcurrant / whitecurrant: Similar because they are Ribes berries bearing the transferred name; differs because they are pale and less pungent than blackcurrants, used fresh for jellies and garnishes.
  • Gooseberry: Similar because it is a close botanical relative of the currants within Ribes; differs because it is larger, often spiny, and never confused with the dried-grape sense.
  • Dried fruit: Similar because currants are a variety of dried fruit; differs because the term is generic, covering apricots, figs, dates, and all raisins, with none of the Corinth specificity.
  • Fruit bun / spice bun: Similar because they name the bakery product currants appear in; differs because they are generic modern labels that lose the historical precision of the small Corinth fruit.
  • Corinth: Similar because currant derives from this place-name; differs because Corinth is the city and region of origin, not the fruit itself, and is used historically rather than in the bakery.
