Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *kap- "to grasp, hold, seize" (reconstructed; the notional period conventionally placed c. 4500–3000 BCE). This is the deep root of the whole capere family. Stage 2 — Latin capere "to take, seize, capture, contain; to understand" (attested from the 3rd century BCE; one of the most productive verbs of the language, e.g. in Plautus and Cicero). Stage 3 — Latin suscipere "to take up, undertake, support, receive, admit, acknowledge" (attested from the 1st century BCE). Formed as sub- "up from under, under" + capere "to take," with the regular Latin assimilation of the prefix-final consonant before c-: sub-cipere > suscipere. Stage 4 — Latin susceptus "taken up, received" (perfect passive participle of suscipere; the stem suscept- underlies the abstract formations). Stage 5 — Late Latin susceptibilis "capable of receiving, able to be sustained or received" (Christian and late-antique Latin, roughly the 4th–6th centuries CE). Stage 6 — Medieval Latin susceptibilitas "capability of being affected or impressed" (scholastic and scientific Latin, roughly the 11th–14th centuries). Stage 7 — English susceptibility "openness or liability to be affected by impressions or influences; state of being easily affected" (the adjective susceptible is attested c. 1600; the noun susceptibility first attested in the 1640s). Final node: SUSCEPTIBILITY. Notes on debate and nuance: - The prefix sub- continues Proto-Indo-European *upo "under, from below," a separate root line that contributes only the directional sense at the Latin compounding stage; it is not part of the *kap- root proper. - The often-cited connection of Germanic have / Old English habban to *kap- is debated by specialists and is not needed for this word; the secure path is Latin capere → suscipere. - The semantic shift from physical "taking up" to abstract "openness to being affected" is a classic metaphorization of passive potential: suscipere already had the senses "receive, admit" in classical Latin, and the abstract noun simply systematizes them. - Do not force every cap-/cep-/cip- lookalike into *kap-; the chain above is the verified linear path for susceptibility.
