Single linear chain from the earliest reconstructable form to the present rootWord tapestries: 1. (Old) Iranian *tapiš-, beside the verbal root *tap- (1st millennium BCE) — "a woven cover, carpet"; "to weave, twist, spin" (the base of Persian tāftan, "to weave"). No Proto-Indo-European ancestry is securely reconstructed; Beekes treats Greek τάπης as an oriental Wanderwort, a culture-loan from Persia, so the chain begins here, not at a PIE root. 2. Ancient Greek τάπης (tápēs), genitive τάπητος (5th century BCE) — "carpet, rug, tapestry, hangings." A loanword taken from the Iranian weaving-trade word during the period of Persian cultural exchange. 3. Latin tapēte / tapētum (1st century BCE/CE) — "carpet, coverlet, tapestry." Taken from Greek by Latin authors for oriental hangings. 4. Old French tapis (from Vulgar Latin *tapitum / *tappetum, 10th–11th centuries) — "carpet"; from it the verb tapisser, "to cover with carpet or hang with tapestry," and the agent-noun tapissier, "maker of wall-hangings." 5. Old French tapisserie (by the 13th century) — "the craft or work of the tapissier; hangings, tapestry." Passed into Anglo-Norman as tapisserie. 6. Middle English tapisserye (15th century) — "tapestry." Modern English tapestry /ˈtæpɪstri/, and the plural tapestries /ˈtæpɪstriz/ — the exact word form of this card. Note on nuance/debate: the word is a chain of cultural borrowings, not an inheritance. The Iranian reconstruction *tapiš- is the commonly cited base, but the exact Iranian word form is not directly attested, and Beekes classifies τάπης as an oriental Wanderwort; what is certain is the Greek → Latin → French → English line, every step of which is documented. The English plural form used here is simply the regular plural of the 16th-century established English noun.
