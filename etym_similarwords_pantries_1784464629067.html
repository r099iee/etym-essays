• Larders: Similar because both are household food-storage rooms; differs because a larder classically holds meat, dairy, and cold provisions and is kept cool, while a pantry holds bread, dry goods, tableware, and serving gear at living-floor level.
  • Cellars: Similar because both are rooms for keeping provisions in bulk; differs because a cellar is underground, cool, and for long-term stock, while a pantry is above ground, near the kitchen, and for daily access.
  • Cupboards: Similar because both are places to keep food and ware; differs because a cupboard is a single piece of furniture or a built-in cabinet, while a pantry is a whole room or closet.
  • Kitchens: Similar because both are food-centred rooms; differs because a kitchen is where food is cooked, while a pantry is where it is stored and staged before cooking or serving.
  • Sculleries: Similar because both are service rooms beside the kitchen; differs because a scullery is for washing up, dirty work, and rough preparation, while a pantry is for clean storage.
  • Storerooms: Similar because both are rooms for keeping supplies; differs because a storeroom is generic and may hold anything, while a pantry is specifically for food and tableware.
  • Pantry cupboards: Similar because they perform the modern pantry role; differs because they are furniture units rather than rooms, a late domestic downsizing of the walk-in pantry.
