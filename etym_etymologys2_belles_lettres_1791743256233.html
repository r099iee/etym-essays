Derivation Chain 1: belles (feminine plural of French beau / bel)
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*dew- / *dwen-
"to show favor, respect, revere; good, capable"
↓
Old Latin (c. 4th c. BCE)
benulus
"goodly, pleasing, charming (diminutive of bonus)"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
bellus
"handsome, pretty, charming, lovely, fine"
↓
Gallo-Romance / Old French (c. 11th c.)
bel / bele
"beautiful, fair, agreeable to sight and mind"
↓
Middle French (c. 14th–16th c.)
belle / belles
"fair, fine, elegant, aesthetically refined (fem. pl.)"

Derivation Chain 2: lettres (plural of French lettre)
Etruscan / Italic substrate (c. 6th c. BCE)
*litera / *littera
"inscribed glyph, alphabetic mark, writing"

Note: Likely adapted from Greek διφθέρα (diphtherā, 'prepared skin, parchment sheet') via a Sabellic/Etruscan d-to-l consonant shift, mirroring Latin lacruma from Greek dakryma.


↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
littera (sing.) / litterae (pl.)
"alphabetic letter; in plural: writings, literature, learning"
↓
Old French (c. 12th c.)
lettre / lettres
"epistle, written character, scholarship, book-learning"
↓
Early Modern French synthesis (c. 1660s)
belles-lettres
"fine literature, polite humanities, elegant compositions"
↓
English borrowing (c. 1710)
belles lettres
"aesthetic, imaginative literature and elegant essays"
