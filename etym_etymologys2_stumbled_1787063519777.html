*stam-
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed; debated)
"to be stunned, stun; to stammer" (possibly related)
↓
stumra
Old Norse (attested in Norse literature)
"to stumble, stagger, trip"
↓
stumblen
Middle English (attested 14th c., e.g. Wyclif)
"to trip, miss one's footing, stagger"
↓
stumble
English (from 14th c.)
"to trip while moving; to falter; to meet by chance"
↓
STUMBLED
English (past tense and participle)
"tripped or lost one's footing; faltered"

Note on debate: The origin of “stumble” is not certain. The OED and Etymonline regard a Scandinavian source as probable (compare Norwegian dialectal stumla, stumra, “to stumble, trip”), and some scholars connect the base to a Germanic root also seen in “stammer” (speech-stumbling), but no PIE root can be securely reconstructed, and there are no solid cognates outside North Germanic and English. The Middle English attestation (14th c., “stumblen”) is secure.
