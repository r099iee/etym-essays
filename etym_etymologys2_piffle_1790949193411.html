Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*pew- / *pū-
"to blow, puff, swell with air (echoic)"
↓
Middle English Dialectal (c. 1400 CE)
piffe / puffe
"a light gust, puff of breath, slight sound"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1650 CE)
piff
"expression of contempt or slight sound"
↓
19th Century British English (c. 1840 CE)
piffle (piff + -le frequentative)
"to act feebly, speak nonsense"
↓
Modern English (c. 1880 CE–present)
piffle
"trivial, foolish, or nonsensical talk"
