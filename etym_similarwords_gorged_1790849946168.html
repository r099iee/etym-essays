• Glutted: Similar because both describe being filled to extreme excess; differs because glutted often emphasizes market oversupply or economic flooding (a glut of goods) or the deadening, numb satiety of greed, whereas gorged evokes a more vivid, visceral swelling in the throat and belly.
  • Surfeited: Similar because both involve over-indulgence and excess; differs because surfeited emphasizes the resulting state of nausea, disgust, or weary boredom from having too much of something, whereas gorged highlights the physical fullness and distension itself.
  • Satiated: Similar because both indicate that an appetite has been satisfied; differs because satiated is a neutral or positive state of being fully and pleasantly satisfied without implying gross excess, discomfort, or gluttony.
  • Engorged: Similar because both derive from the same root and describe being filled with fluid; differs because engorged is predominantly used in medical, anatomical, and biological contexts to describe congested, expanded tissues or blood vessels.
  • Stuffed: Similar because both describe being filled to maximum capacity; differs because stuffed is a casual, common descriptor that lacks the visceral intensity, literary richness, and heraldic dimensions of gorged.
  • Bloated: Similar because both denote an uncomfortably swollen state; differs because bloated emphasizes unhealthy retention of gas, water, or decay, carrying a sickly or grotesque connotation compared to the rich indulgence of gorged.
