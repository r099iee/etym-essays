[Root 1: STOCK (Proto-Indo-European *steug- / *steg-)]
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*steug- or *(s)teg-
"to push, stick, strike, pierce; pointed peg"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*stukkaz
"tree trunk, stump, stick, block"
↓
Old English (c. 8th–11th c.)
stocc
"tree trunk, log, stump; wooden block"
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
stok / stock
"trunk, stem; family ancestry; store of goods"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th–17th c.)
stock
"accumulated fund, capital, commercial shares"

[Root 2: BROKER (Anglo-Norman brocour / Old French brochier)]
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*bʰreug-
"to make use of, enjoy; break, pierce"
↓
Vulgar Latin / Medieval Latin (c. 6th–9th c.)
*broccare
"to prick, pierce, tap a wine cask"
↓
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 12th–13th c.)
brochier / brocour
"tapster who taps casks; wine retailer; middleman"
Note: Derives from Old French brocour ('wine retailer, broker'), originating from brochier ('to broach, pierce a cask to draw wine'), extending to any agent who acts as a middleman.
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.)
brocour / broker
"agent, middleman in buying and selling"

[Compound Synthesis]
Early Modern English (London Financial Revolution) (c. 1690s)
stock + broker
"an intermediary who buys and sells joint-stock shares"
↓
Modern English (present)
STOCKBROKER
