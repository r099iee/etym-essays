• fortune: Similar because both refer to the unforeseen course of events shaping human life; differs because fortune often implies a personified cosmic force, destiny, or significant wealth/status, carrying an aura of grandeur or fate, whereas chance is more neutral, mechanical, and fortuitous.
  • luck: Similar because both denote favorable or unfavorable occurrences beyond one's control; differs because luck is an everyday, informal Germanic word strongly associated with personal outcomes (good luck vs. bad luck), whereas chance encompasses formal statistical probability, causal randomness, and adjectival circumstance.
  • hazard: Similar because both originate from ancient gambling games and involve uncertainty; differs because hazard has shifted almost exclusively toward negative danger, peril, and exposure to risk, whereas chance can equally signify a positive, welcome opportunity.
  • accident: Similar because both describe events that occur without intention or design; differs because accident typically denotes an isolated, sudden mishap or physical collision causing damage, whereas chance refers to the broader, abstract principle of randomness or fortunate serendipity.
  • coincidence: Similar because both involve unplanned occurrences; differs because a coincidence specifically requires two or more independent, causally unrelated events to correspond in a striking, unexpected manner, whereas chance is the underlying indeterminacy itself.
  • opportunity: Similar because a chance can mean an opening to act; differs because opportunity emphasizes a favorable, advantageous window of time that invites decisive constructive action, whereas chance highlights the randomness and uncertainty of the opening.
  • serendipity: Similar because both involve accidental occurrences; differs because serendipity specifically denotes the faculty of making fortunate, delightful discoveries purely by accident while looking for something else, carrying a uniquely joyful connotation.
  • contingency: Similar because both refer to events that are not guaranteed or inevitable; differs because contingency is a formal, philosophical, or administrative term for an event that depends upon uncertain future conditions and for which preparations must be made (contingency planning).
