• twinge: Similar because both name a sudden, brief, sharp pain; differs because a twinge is slighter and more fleeting, and rarely carries pang's full emotional depth or fixed collocations like “the pangs of hunger”.
  • spasm: Similar because both name a sudden intense contraction of pain; differs because a spasm stresses involuntary muscular action and may last long, whereas a pang is short and point-like.
  • stab: Similar because both name a sudden piercing pain; differs because a stab is more violent, localized, and physical, with none of pang's easy crossing into moral feeling.
  • throe: Similar because both name a violent spasm of pain; differs because a throe is prolonged and severe, associated with childbirth and the death-agony, while a pang is brief.
  • ache: Similar because both name felt pain; differs because an ache is dull, continuous, and diffuse — the exact opposite of pang's sharp, brief, piercing suddenness.
  • qualm: Similar because both name a sudden pang of misgiving; differs because a qualm is restricted to scruples and the conscience, while pang ranges freely over physical and emotional pain.
