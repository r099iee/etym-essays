• Elaborating: Similar because both develop a subject in detail; differs because elaborating is the constructive labour of working a point out structurally, while expatiating is expansive, free-roaming discourse across the whole topic.
  • Dilating: Similar because both mean expanding at length upon a topic; differs because dilating (dilate upon) is a genuine synonym but now rare in this sense and lacks the image of free movement that expatiating carries.
  • Enlarging: Similar because both imply speaking at length upon a subject; differs because enlarging upon pictures the growth of the subject itself rather than the speaker's roaming freely across it.
  • Digressing: Similar because both involve ranging beyond the immediate point; differs because digressing wanders away from the subject into irrelevancies — a deviation to be excused — while expatiating stays on the subject, covering it exhaustively and well.
  • Discoursing: Similar because both mean speaking formally at length; differs because discoursing is formal and reasoned but not necessarily expansive or eager, whereas expatiating implies roaming with freedom, relish, and tireless enthusiasm.
  • Rhapsodizing: Similar because both mean speaking at length with enthusiasm; differs because rhapsodizing adds effusive, emotional transport that does not fit an intellectual catalogue, while expatiating keeps an orderly, comprehensive range.
  • Expanding: Similar because both imply treating a topic at greater breadth; differs because expanding is neutral and general, lacking expatiating's spatial image of wandering out of bounds.
  • Amplifying: Similar because both mean enlarging a statement; differs because amplifying stresses adding force or detail to a point, while expatiating stresses ranging widely over the whole subject.
  • Holding forth: Similar because both mean speaking at length; differs because holding forth is colloquial and suggests a one-sided, slightly self-important lecture, while expatiating is formal and implies genuine comprehensive treatment.
  • Prolix / verbose (adjectives): Similar because both imply over-long speech; differs because prolix and verbose carry blame — tediously wordy — while expatiating is neutral or even admiring, naming expansive, eager treatment rather than mere wordiness.
