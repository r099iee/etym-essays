- 16th Century (Physical Posture) - Describing the literal act of sitting or standing with legs wide apart over an object (e.g., a horse, a fence). - Neutral, descriptive.
- 17th-18th Century (Geographical/Spatial) - Extended to describe objects or places situated across a boundary or feature (e.g., a town straddling a river). - Objective, descriptive.
- 19th Century (Metaphorical Indecision/Neutrality) - Developed the sense of taking an equivocal or non-committal position, often to avoid offense or maintain balance (e.g., straddling the fence). - Can carry connotations of caution, diplomacy, or indecisiveness.
- 20th Century (Technical/Financial) - Adopted into specialized fields, notably finance, to describe strategies covering multiple outcomes (e.g., a straddle option). - Technical, precise, domain-specific.
- Contemporary (Broad Application) - Continues to be used across all these senses, from physical action to abstract concepts of spanning, balancing, or encompassing diverse elements. - Versatile, widely understood in various contexts.
