• Foals: Similar because both denote young horses; differs because foal is an ungendered general term for any equine offspring under one year of age (male or female), whereas colts specifically denotes young *male* equines up to four years old.
  • Fillies: Similar because both refer to young horses under four years old; differs because fillies are young *female* horses, whereas colts are young *male* horses.
  • Yearlings: Similar because both describe young equines; differs because yearling is strictly an age classification (an animal between one and two years old regardless of sex), whereas colt specifies sex (male) and spans from foalhood up to four years.
  • Stallions: Similar because both are intact male horses; differs because a stallion is a fully mature, adult male horse (typically four years or older) used for breeding, whereas a colt is a juvenile, immature male horse.
  • Geldings: Similar because both are male horses; differs because a gelding has been castrated, whereas a colt is typically intact (uncastrated).
  • Novices / Rookies: Similar because when used metaphorically, all describe raw newcomers; differs because colt emphasizes spirited exuberance, youthful friskiness, and physical vitality, whereas novice and rookie focus on lack of skill or professional tenure.
