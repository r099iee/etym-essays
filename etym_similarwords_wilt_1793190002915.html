• Droop: Similar because both describe hanging limp from weakness or thirst; differs because droop is the general posture word for heads, eyelids, and flowers while wilt stresses loss of freshness through water loss or flagging energy.
  • Wither: Similar because both mean to shrivel and lose life; differs because wither is final and desiccating — dead and dry — while wilt may be temporary and recoverable with water or rest.
  • Fade: Similar because both describe flowers and strength declining; differs because fade stresses colour and light draining away while wilt stresses structure sagging and going limp.
  • Sag: Similar because both picture downward collapse under weight or weakness; differs because sag is physical sinking of shelves, ropes, and spirits while wilt adds the botanical or vital loss of freshness.
  • Flag: Similar because flagging energies and wilting enthusiasm both fail by degrees; differs because flag stresses pace slackening (the conversation flagged) while wilt stresses freshness collapsing.
  • Languish: Similar because both describe pining weakness without vigour; differs because languish is slow romantic decline, often from neglect or longing, while wilt is quicker visible collapse.
  • Waste away: Similar because both describe vitality draining out of living things; differs because waste away is gradual bodily consumption by illness while wilt is the sudden limpness of thirst or heat.
  • Shalt: Similar because it is the exact grammatical twin — archaic second person of shall as wilt is of will; differs because shalt expresses obligation or destiny while wilt expresses wish and volition.
  • Weaken: Similar because wilting strength is weakening strength; differs because weaken is the broad neutral term for all loss of force while wilt keeps its vivid plant picture.
