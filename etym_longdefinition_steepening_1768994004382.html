steepening — becoming steeper; that is, growing more sharply angled, more nearly vertical, or more severe in slope. The form is the present participle of the verb to steepen, an “-en” verb meaning “to make or become steep” (the transitive “to make steep” and the intransitive “to become steep” coexist). In the specific literary moment on this card the word describes the cave floor: “The floor was steepening, as if it had been the bed of a torrent.” The progressive aspect is the entire point — this is not a static description of a slope but a process unfolding in time, the ground tilting ever more severely beneath the walkers’ feet. The nuance is geological and almost menacing: the participle converts the cave from a passive setting into an active force, a channel still shaped by the ancient violence of water, whose steepening gradient drags the travellers downward into the dark.

An exhaustive definition must register the word’s full range across literal, technical, and figurative registers.

  • Literal sense — an incline growing more severe: A road, path, roof, bank, or cliff “steepens” as it becomes more nearly vertical. In geomorphology and geology the term is standard for slopes steepening with distance or through erosion, and in surveying and civil engineering for gradients being made more severe (“the embankment was steepened to improve the view”).
  • The progressive/participle sense — change in progress: The form steepening foregrounds process rather than state: a “steepening slope” is a slope that is getting steeper even as it is observed. This is the sense on the card, where the cave floor steepens live under the travellers’ feet, and it is the sense preferred in scientific prose, where a graph, curve, or gradient is said to be “steepening” as its slope increases over time.
  • Figurative sense — anything increasing in intensity or rate: By extension from the image of a rising curve, steepen describes any trajectory becoming more severe: steepening costs, a steepening learning curve, a steepening path of conflict, a steepening descent into crisis. The metaphoric bridge is the graph: the steepness of a line plots the rate of change, so a steepening line is change accelerating.
  • Modern technical jargon — the “steepening yield curve”: In finance, “a steepening yield curve” is one in which the gap between short-term and long-term interest rates widens — long rates rise faster than short rates. “Steepening” here is precise jargon with a specific directional meaning, the exact opposite of a “flattening” or “inverted” curve, and the word is used as noun, adjective, and participle (“a steepener,” “steepening trades”). This is the word’s most prominent modern technical life and a vivid example of a spatial metaphor exported into an abstract quantitative domain.
  • Antonymic pair and the morphology of change: The word implies its opposite — flattening — and belongs to the large family of English “-en” inchoatives and causatives: deepen, lengthen, broaden, heighten, harden, sharpen, widen. These convert adjectives into verbs of becoming, and steepen is a late, fully productive member of that family.

In synonym boundaries the word is precise about change. Sloping is the neutral static term for any inclined surface, steep or gentle, and carries no sense of alteration; inclining is formal and again static; rising and ascending describe direction rather than the sharpness of angle; deepening is the vertical analogue (downward rather than angular); escalating and intensifying are the general figurative words for increase, losing the specific image of a slope. Steepening alone insists on the angle — a sharpening of the incline itself, whether of rock underfoot, a road ahead, or a curve on a chart. Collocations confirm the range: a steepening slope, a steepening gradient, the slope was steepening, the curve is steepening, a steepening yield curve, steepening inflation expectations. On the card, the participle does the work of a whole clause of rising tension: the ground is not merely steep — it is becoming steeper as the travellers watch, the cave floor tilting away beneath their feet like a river bed resuming its ancient fall.
