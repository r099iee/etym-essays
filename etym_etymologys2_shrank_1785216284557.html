Proto-Indo-European *sker- “to turn, bend” (proposed but NOT secure: the OED judges the ultimate origin of shrink uncertain and treats it as probably an expressive, perhaps echoic Germanic formation; the link to *sker- is often repeated but cannot be demonstrated, and no Indo-Aryan or Romance cognates are securely attested) │ └── Proto-Germanic *skrinkwaną “to contract, shrivel, wither” (reconstructed; attested reflexes: Middle Dutch schrinken “to shrink”, Middle Low German schrinken “to shrink”; probably related via an ablaut variant *skremp-/*skrump- to German schrumpfen “to shrivel, shrink”) └── Old English scrincan “to shrivel, contract, wither; to draw back” (strong verb, class III; attested in the earliest Old English records, 8th–11th c., with the paradigm scrincan — scranc — scruncon — (ge)scruncen) └── Middle English shrinken / schrinken “to shrink, contract” (12th c. onward); singular preterite shrānc → shrank(e) survives as the past tense; plural preterite scruncon is levelled away; past participle shrunk and participial adjective shrunken descend from (ge)scruncen └── English shrank = the regular survival of the Old English singular preterite scranc (strong past tense of shrink) └── Modern English: shrank Debated stage: the pre-Germanic origin of *skrinkwaną is uncertain; the OED and most handbooks treat shrink as of expressive/echoic origin within Germanic, with the skr- cluster mimicking a wrinkling, rasping contraction. The frequently repeated link to PIE *sker- “to turn, bend” is suggestive but not secure and must be flagged as a hypothesis, not a fact. The Germanic-to-English chain above is fully attested.
