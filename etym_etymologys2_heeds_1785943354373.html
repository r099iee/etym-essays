Single chain for HEEDS (3rd person singular of heed):
  • Reconstructed Proto-Indo-European *kadh- — "to guard, protect, cover" (Pokorny's reconstruction; c. 4500–3000 BCE; status debated).
  • Proto-Germanic *hōdijaną — "to guard, protect, take care of" (verb on the stem *hōd-; c. 500 BCE–200 CE).
  • Old English hēdan — "to heed, take care of, guard, observe" (attested from the 8th c.).
  • Middle English heden — "to heed, guard, regard, take care" (12th–14th c.).
  • Modern English heed — "to pay careful attention to, take notice of" (the guarding sense giving way to the attentive sense from the 15th c. onward).
  • HEEDS — the regular third-person singular present, in -s (the exact form of this card; bold target).
Notes on nuance and debate: the PIE ancestor is given as *kadh- "to guard, protect" in Pokorny's Indo-European dictionary, but the reconstruction is not universally accepted — some scholars treat Germanic *hōd- as an isolated formation without secure Indo-European cognates. The Germanic family is well attested in the middle stages: cognates include Old High German huoten and modern German hüten "to watch over, guard," Old Saxon hōdian, and Gothic hōda (a deposit, what is guarded). The English form shows the regular development of Germanic *ō to Old English ē and the loss of the verb's final syllables.
