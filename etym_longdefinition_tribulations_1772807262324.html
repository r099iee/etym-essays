Tribulations (plural of tribulation) are causes of great trouble or suffering — prolonged, grinding trials that test endurance and spirit. The word comes, through French, from the Latin tribulatio, a 'threshing': literally, the crushing pressure that separates grain from husk, transferred to the experience of being ground down by affliction. A single tribulation is one crushing trial; the plural tribulations names a whole series of oppressive hardships.

IN THE PASSAGE

When the rescued girl says she thinks her tribulations are at last coming to an end, the plural noun gathers years of kidnapping, coercion, fear of beating, forced silence, and the false identity of being sold as a debtor's child. The word does not name a single mishap; it frames a prolonged series of crushing trials that have tested her endurance and her spirit. In her brief confession after young Feng pays for her, the tone is weary relief — hope that the threshing is over — rather than triumphant joy. The literary weight of tribulations elevates her private ordeal into the language of severe, almost biblical testing.

PRIMARY SENSES

  • Severe trials and suffering: a state or series of great affliction ('through all his tribulations, he never lost faith') — the dominant modern sense.
  • Theological sense: in scripture and religious writing, tribulation is the season of trial by which the faithful are tested and refined (Matthew 13:21, 'when tribulation or persecution ariseth'), and the end-time tribulation is the final great testing of the church.
  • Literary / elevated: prolonged oppressive pressure, as opposed to a single momentary misfortune; the word is formal, often plural, and implies grinding, repeated suffering.

HOW THE SENSES BRIDGE

  • The Latin source tribulum was a heavy threshing sledge studded with stones or teeth, dragged over grain to rub the husks free.
  • Tribulare 'to press, oppress, afflict' carried the literal image of crushing, and tribulatio named the state of being crushed or ground down.
  • Church Latin used the word for affliction as divine testing — the believer is 'threshed' as grain is threshed, so that the chaff of sin may be separated from the grain of faith. From that religious sense, the word passed into general English as the ordinary elevated name for severe suffering.

REGISTER AND COLLOCATIONS

  • Collocations: trials and tribulations (a fixed binominal), through all one's tribulations, endure / suffer / bear tribulations, the tribulations of war / exile / poverty.
  • Register is literary, religious, or elevated narrative. It appears in scripture-influenced English and in fiction when a character's misery is cast as a season of testing rather than a run of bad luck.
  • The singular tribulation can name one great affliction or a generalized condition of suffering; the plural tribulations emphasizes a series of separate crushing events.

BOUNDARIES WITH NEAR-SYNONYMS

  • Compared with troubles, tribulations is formal, heavier, and implies prolonged grinding pressure rather than everyday difficulty.
  • Compared with hardships, it stresses spiritual and emotional testing rather than mere material difficulty.
  • Compared with trials, it is plural and oppressive by nature — a trial can be a single test or a legal proceeding, while tribulations are repeated.
  • Compared with afflictions, it emphasizes the sequence and the crushing pressure rather than individual blows.
