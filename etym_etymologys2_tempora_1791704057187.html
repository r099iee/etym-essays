Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*temh₁- (or *ten-)
"to cut, divide, carve out;"
"to stretch, extend"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*tempos-
"section of duration, span, measure of time"
↓
Old Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
tempos (stem *tempos- / *tempes-)
"period of time, season"
↓
Classical Latin (rhotacism & vowel shift) (c. 1st c. BCE)
tempus (singular), tempora (plural)
"times, seasons, eras; temples of the head"
↓
Renaissance / Early Modern English citation (c. 16th c.)
tempora
"the times, historical circumstances, current age"

Note: Linguists debate whether Latin tempus derives from PIE *temh₁- ('to cut', conceiving time as a carved segment, identical to the root of Greek temnein 'to cut' and temenos) or PIE *ten- ('to stretch, extend', conceiving time as linear span). The plural tempora reflects regular Latin neuter s-stem phonology: intervocalic *tempos-a underwent rhotacism (-s- > -r-) and vowel weakening to produce tempora. The anatomical sense ('temples of the head') either reflects the place where gray hairs mark time, or a distinct homonym from *temh₁- meaning 'the thin, sliced spot' of the cranium.
