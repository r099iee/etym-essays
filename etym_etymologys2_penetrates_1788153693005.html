PIE(reconstructed; debated)
*pen- / *pṉ̥-
"in, inside; provisions"
(proposed root of Latin penus; not securely attested outside Italic)
↓
Latin(2nd c. BCE; Plautus)
penus
"inmost part of a house; stores, provisions"
↓
Latin(2nd c. BCE)
penitus
"inward, deep within; thoroughly"
↓
Latin(2nd c. BCE; Ennius)
penetrāre
"to put within, enter, pierce"
↓
Latin(classical)
penetrātus
"entered, pierced"
↓
English(16th c.; 1530s)
penetrate
"to pierce, enter into"
↓
Modern English(3rd person singular present)
penetrates
"enters, pierces, permeates"

Note: The ultimate origin of Latin penus is disputed — some connect it to a PIE stem for "food, provisions" (cf. Greek pēnis "food-..."), others treat it as Italic-internal; the chain within Latin and English is secure.
