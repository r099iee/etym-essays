Note on historical semantic divergence: Philologists (Ernout-Meillet, Michiel de Vaan, OED) document how Latin flōridus split into two major semantic streams in Western Europe. One stream adhered to the concrete biological vitality of blooming petals and flushed blood, producing the physical medical sense of a ruddy, flushed complexion (and later the clinical sense of a fully blooming disease state). The second stream developed metaphorically through Roman rhetorical education (Cicero, Quintilian), where rhetorical figures and ornamental tropes were termed flores rhetorici ("flowers of rhetoric"). Writers whose style was heavily adorned with these figures were classified as writing in the genus floridum (florid style), which passed into English as florid and floridly to denote ornate, flowery verbal, artistic, and musical ornamentation.
