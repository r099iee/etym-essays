• Face: Similar because both name the front of the head; differs because face is the ordinary, neutral, universal word, while visage is formal, literary, and frames the face as an object of sight and reading.
  • Countenance: Similar because both name the face as an expression of mood or character; differs because countenance emphasizes the look as revealing inner feeling or composure (to keep one's countenance), while visage emphasizes the face as something seen and read.
  • Features: Similar because both concern the visible parts of the face; differs because features names the individual parts (eyes, nose, mouth) rather than the whole, while visage is the whole face at a glance.
  • Physiognomy: Similar because both treat the face as legible evidence of character; differs because physiognomy is a semi-scientific term for the art of reading character from features, while visage is simply the seen face.
  • Mien: Similar because both describe outward bearing and appearance; differs because mien covers the whole demeanor (posture, air, manner), while visage is specifically the face.
  • Aspect: Similar because both can mean the outward appearance of a thing; differs because aspect is far broader (an aspect of a problem, an aspect of the sky) while visage is tied to the human face.
  • Front: Similar because both name the face as the outward-presented surface; differs because front is used of buildings, battle lines, and faces alike and is unliterary, while visage is poetic and face-specific.
  • Mug: Similar because both name the face; differs because mug is crude, comic slang for an ugly or grotesque face, the opposite of visage's dignity.
  • Phiz: Similar because both mean the face (from "physiognomy"); differs because phiz is jocular 18th-century slang, while visage is elevated and serious.
  • Puss: Similar because both name the face; differs because puss is playful, affectionate slang, while visage is stately.
  • Kisser: Similar because both name the face; differs because kisser is vulgar slang for the mouth-area, while visage is noble and whole-face.
  • Lineaments: Similar because both describe the features and outlines of the face; differs because lineaments is archaic and emphasizes the facial outline or structure, while visage is the whole presented face.
