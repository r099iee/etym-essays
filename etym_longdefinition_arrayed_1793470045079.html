Arrayed is the past tense and past participle of the transitive verb array, signifying drawn up in disciplined order of battle, systematically marshaled into opposing or cooperative ranks, or adorned and clothed in splendid, ceremonial vestments. Originating as a hybrid of Germanic martial culture and Romance administrative grammar—descending from Frankish *garaidjan (‘to arrange, prepare, equip for action’) latinized in Gallo-Roman speech as *arrēdāre and passing through Old French arreier—the word possesses a dual semantic soul. On one hand, it describes the tactical ordering of an army poised for combat; on the other, it denotes the aesthetic ordering of garments, gems, and regalia for public ceremony. Both branches share a common conceptual root: the conscious transformation of raw, disorganized elements into a unified, disciplined, and formidable presentation.

In the source passage—from Samuel Johnson’s essay on the veneration of antiquity in The Rambler (No. 154): “He therefore who would maintain the cause of contemporary excellence against that of elder time, must have almost every class of men arrayed against him,” the participle is deployed in its military and adversarial sense. Johnson depicts the intellectual world as an imposing battlefield: anyone audacious enough to champion modern achievements against the entrenched prestige of ancient masters must confront a vast, organized host. The opposing classes of men—critics, pedagogues, antiquarians, and readers—are not merely indifferent or privately skeptical; they are arrayed like disciplined battalions standing shoulder-to-shoulder in active, fortified opposition.

Comprehensive exploration of primary, secondary, and modern technical senses:

  • Marshaled in military formation or battle order (primary martial sense): Drawn up, positioned, and organized into tactical ranks, squadrons, or battle lines (e.g., ‘the legions were arrayed along the crest of the ridge’). The bridge links the Germanic root of readiness for horse-travel and combat with the tactical discipline required to deploy troops effectively.
  • Organized in collective opposition or alignment (figurative sociopolitical sense): Grouped, unified, or positioned into opposing factions, legal parties, or social coalitions (e.g., ‘public opinion was firmly arrayed against the proposed tax’). As in Johnson’s essay, this metaphorically casts political and philosophical debates as warfare between opposing armies.
  • Clothed, adorned, or attired in ceremonial finery (sartorial/aesthetic sense): Dressed in rich, elaborate, or symbolic garments (e.g., ‘the king was arrayed in robes of scarlet and ermine’). Widely celebrated in biblical prose (e.g., Matthew 6:29: ‘even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these’). The bridge connects equipping a warrior with protective armor to outfitting a dignitary with magnificent regalia.
  • Systematically ordered in a grid, matrix, or series (scientific and mathematical sense): Arranged in a disciplined physical or conceptual structure of rows and columns (e.g., ‘sensors arrayed across the desert floor’, ‘data arrayed in a two-dimensional matrix’). The bridge abstracts military lines into mathematical tables and computational data structures.
  • Formed into an empanelled jury (archaic legal sense): In English common law, to ‘array’ a jury meant to draw up the list or panel of jurors in order of selection (whence the legal challenge ‘to the array’). The bridge treats the jury roll as an orderly muster of citizens called to civic duty.

Register, tone, and stylistic resonance: Arrayed is a word of high aesthetic dignity and rhetorical strength. When applied to conflict or debate, it evokes an overwhelming sense of organized, formidable opposition—far more potent than merely saying people ‘disagree’ or ‘stand against.’ It implies deliberate strategy, disciplined unity, and formidable numbers. In its sartorial applications, it carries majestic, biblical grandeur, suggesting clothing that is not merely worn for warmth, but presented as a radiant statement of majesty, identity, and triumph.
