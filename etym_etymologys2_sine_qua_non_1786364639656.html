=== DERIVATION CHAIN: SINE === *só-n-i
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
"apart, separately, by oneself"
↓
*seni / *sani
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
"without, apart from, separately"
↓
sine
Latin (c. 300 BCE)
"without, apart from (governing ablative)"
↓
sine
English (in Latinate phrase) (c. 1600 CE–present)
"without"

=== DERIVATION CHAIN: QUA === *kʷo- / *kʷi-
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
"who, which, what (relative/interrogative stem)"
↓
*kʷāi / *kʷād
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
"which, by which (feminine singular)"
↓
quā (ablative fem. of quī / quae / quod)
Latin (c. 300 BCE)
"which (feminine ablative singular after 'sine')"
↓
qua
English (in Latinate phrase) (c. 1600 CE–present)
"which (referring back to feminine 'condicio')"

=== DERIVATION CHAIN: NON === *ne / *nē
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
"not (negative particle)"
↓
*ne oenum (literally *ne oinom)
Old Latin (c. 500 BCE)
"not one, not a single thing"
↓
nōn
Classical Latin (c. 200 BCE)
"not, by no means"
↓
non
English (in Latinate phrase) (c. 1600 CE–present)
"not"

=== COMPLETE COMPOSITE PHRASE === condicio sine qua non
Late Latin / Medieval Jurisprudence (c. 1200 CE)
"a condition without which [the effect is] not [possible]"
↓
sine qua non
Modern English (c. 1600 CE–present)
"an indispensable condition or prerequisite"
