• Pale: Similar because both mean lacking colour, especially of the face or light; differs because pale is the general, neutral word (fair skin, light shade), while pallid implies an unhealthy, striking bloodlessness and is more literary.
  • Wan: Similar because both describe a pale, sickly, exhausted look; differs because wan implies weakness, fatigue or unwellness specifically of the face, while pallid can also apply to light, colour and abstract quality.
  • Ashen: Similar because both denote a pale, greyish colour of the face; differs because ashen specifically evokes ashes and the grey-white look of shock or near-death, harsher and greyer than pallid.
  • Sallow: Similar because both describe an unhealthy-looking, yellow-pale complexion; differs because sallow is specifically a sickly yellowish tinge (of skin), while pallid is whiter and can be used of light and art.
  • Colourless: Similar because both mean lacking colour; differs because colourless is a plain, factual word (and can mean dull or neutral in personality), while pallid always carries an unhealthy or feeble overtone.
  • Bloodless: Similar because both suggest a face drained of blood and, figuratively, a lack of vitality; differs because bloodless is more extreme and can mean literally without blood or, figuratively, utterly lifeless and cold.
  • Bland: Similar because both can describe something lacking strength or interest; differs because bland applies to food, taste or personality as mild and uninteresting, while pallid is about visual or qualitative paleness and keeps a faintly physical sense.
  • Feeble: Similar because both describe weakness in figurative use (a pallid or feeble effort); differs because feeble is purely about lack of strength and covers physical, moral or logical weakness, while pallid keeps its image of washed-out paleness.
