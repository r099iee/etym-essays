• Stoat: Similar because both name the very same animal (Mustela erminea); differs because stoat is the plain biological name, carrying none of the regal, heraldic, or judicial associations of ermine — one speaks of an ermine robe, not a stoat robe.
  • Weasel: Similar because the stoat is a weasel-family carnivore; differs because weasel names the smaller, closely related species and carries connotations of slyness, while ermine names the stoat's white winter fur and its symbolism.
  • Pelt / fur: Similar because both name the animal skin used as material; differs because pelt and fur are generic trade words, while ermine names one specific, historically prized white fur.
  • Sable: Similar because both are costly furs used for robes and linings; differs because sable is a dark, almost black fur connoting somber, mature wealth, while ermine's white signifies purity and sovereignty.
  • Mink: Similar because both are costly furs; differs because mink suggests modern glamour and twentieth-century affluence, while ermine is the historical emblem of royal and judicial authority.
  • Chinchilla: Similar because both are ultra-luxury furs; differs because chinchilla implies extreme softness and delicate luxury, while ermine specifically symbolizes unblemished state power and incorruptibility.
  • Miniver: Similar because both are white furs historically used for royal and judicial robes; differs because miniver is a plain white fur (often of squirrel) without the black ermine spots and without the full heraldic and bestiary symbolism.
  • Vair: Similar because both are heraldic furs used in armory; differs because vair is a bluish-grey squirrel fur with a distinctive bell-shaped pattern, while ermine is the white fur with black spots.
  • Robes: Similar because ermine metonymically denotes the robes trimmed with it; differs because robes names the garment itself, while ermine names the fur and the dignity it symbolizes.
  • Tincture: Similar because ermine functions as one of the two principal heraldic furs, a subclass of tincture; differs because tincture is the general technical term for any armorial colour or fur, while ermine names this specific white-and-black fur.
  • Luxury fur (generic): Similar because all these terms belong to the vocabulary of luxury fur; differs because ermine alone carries the moral meaning of spotless purity and the legal meaning of judicial office.
