- Vedic & Brāhmaṇa Period (c. 1200–600 BCE) — Rooted in early sacrificial liturgy, where Vedic Brāhmaṇas and Śrautasūtras concluded ritual prescriptions with precise formulas declaring the specific ritual fruit (phala) attained by the sacrificer (yajamāna) who correctly executes the rite.
- Epic & Classical Purāṇic Period (c. 400 BCE–800 CE) — Formalized into a standard textual colophon across the Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa, and major Purāṇas, expanding from priestly sacrifice to universal oral reception, guaranteeing health, progeny, wealth, and liberation (mokṣa) to anyone who reads, hears, or recites the epic.
- Medieval Bhakti & Tantric Era (c. 800–1700 CE) — Popularized across devotional hymnology (stotras like the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma, Lalitā Sahasranāma, and vernacular hymns like the Hanumān Cālīsā), functioning as an egalitarian spiritual guarantee accessible to all devotees regardless of caste, gender, or formal Vedic initiation.
- 19th–20th Century (Colonial Indology & Structural Hermeneutics) — Analyzed by Western Indologists and structural folklorists as an essential genre marker and sociolinguistic mechanism that motivated active text preservation, memorization, and oral dissemination.
- 21st Century (Contemporary Global Hinduism) — Universally retained in daily domestic prayers, temple liturgies, and digital devotional audio recordings as a sacred, reassuring closing contemplation on the transformative power of holy sound.
