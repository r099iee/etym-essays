• Nonentities: Similar because both characterize persons or things of no consequence, influence, or importance; differs because nonentity is primarily social and psychological, describing a person who lacks personality, presence, or achievement, whereas nullity carries a rigorous legal, technical, and institutional foundation.
  • Ciphers: Similar because both refer to people who count for nothing; differs because cipher is a mathematical and numerical metaphor (a zero) highlighting that a person is a mere placeholder serving another's will, while nullities emphasizes an absolute lack of legal or practical force.
  • Nobodies: Similar because both dismiss individuals as socially insignificant; differs because nobody is an informal, colloquial, and often dismissive social insult, whereas nullities operates in an elevated, formal, literary, or legal register.
  • Invalids: Similar because both relate to something lacking operational validity; differs because invalid (in legal contexts) describes a status that has been overturned or made void, whereas nullities represents the plural abstract substantives that possess no life ab initio.
  • Voids: Similar because both denote total absence or emptiness; differs because void typically evokes physical spatial emptiness, cosmic vacuums, or emotional despair, whereas nullities characterizes specific acts, instruments, or ciphers.
  • Futilities: Similar because both describe things that produce no practical result; differs because futilities focuses on useless, wasted efforts and actions doomed to failure, while nullities focuses on things that lack legal standing or existential power.
  • Nothings: Similar because both convey complete lack of substance; differs because nothings is an everyday generic Germanic noun, whereas nullities is a formal Latinate term carrying constitutional, metaphysical, or legal gravity.
