• Treatises: Similar because both denote systematic, formal written expositions methodically examining the principles of a specific subject; differs because a treatise is a general Western academic term without sacred canonical status or binding normative authority, whereas a śāstra traditionally carries cosmic and societal obligation.
  • Canons: Similar because both represent an authoritative body of sacred texts, rules, or standards accepted as universally binding; differs because canon refers broadly to the accepted collection of scriptures or literary works, while a śāstra is specifically an instructional manual or operational textbook within a discipline.
  • Manuals: Similar because both provide practical, rule-based instructions and guidelines for executing tasks or mastering a discipline; differs because manual implies a mundane, utilitarian handbook, lacking the profound metaphysical, ethical, and spiritual prestige inherent to a śāstra.
  • Jurisprudence: Similar because both encompass systematic philosophies of law and societal order (specifically *Dharmaśāstras*); differs because jurisprudence is restricted strictly to legal theory and civil/criminal courts, whereas śāstras encompass arts, drama, architecture, love, statecraft, and metaphysics.
  • Scriptures: Similar because both are venerated as sacred literature embodying ancestral wisdom; differs because scripture usually denotes direct divine revelation (*Śruti* in Hinduism), whereas śāstras belong primarily to humanly composed systematic codes (*Smṛti*).
  • Compendiums: Similar because both gather and organize vast bodies of specialized information into unified textual structures; differs because a compendium is a neutral compilation or summary, whereas a śāstra is an active, normative code dictating prescribed conduct.
  • Statutes: Similar because both establish formal rules and legal obligations for society; differs because statutes are formal legislative enactments passed by modern civil governments, whereas śāstras are moral-religious codes developed through scholastic consensus.
  • Doctrines: Similar because both represent formulated principles and tenets of belief taught as authoritative; differs because a doctrine is a theoretical belief or ideological stance, while a śāstra is the entire methodological apparatus, textbook, and science underpinning it.
  • Disciplines: Similar because both designate organized branches of learning and rigorous study; differs because a discipline is the abstract field of inquiry itself, whereas a śāstra is the concrete textual manual and codified literature embodying that field.
  • Codes of Law: Similar because both provide comprehensive, structured rules governing human behavior and judicial penalties; differs because codes of law focus exclusively on legal mandates, whereas the corpus of śāstras extends to fine arts, architecture, grammar, and spiritual emancipation.
