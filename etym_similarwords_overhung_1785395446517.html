• overhanging: Similar because both belong to the verb overhang and share the same meaning of projecting or hanging above; differs because overhanging is the present participle, describing the active ongoing fact of the branches still projecting, while overhung is the past and participial form, describing a completed state, the canopy already in place.
  • projecting: Similar because both denote something that extends outward or forward over something else; differs because projecting is the neutral, technical, dimensionally exact word of architecture and engineering, with no connotation of shade, threat, or adornment, whereas overhung specifically implies covering a space below.
  • jutting: Similar because both describe something that sticks out prominently over what is beneath; differs because jutting stresses abrupt, angular protrusion, a jutting chin, a jutting rock, without the sense of roofing or shading that overhung carries.
  • protruding: Similar because both mean extending beyond a surface; differs because protruding is the plain, general verb used of any projection, anatomical, mechanical, or geological, with no implication of a sheltered space beneath.
  • extended: Similar because both can describe something stretched out over a surface; differs because extended is far broader, to stretch, widen, offer, continue, and does not of itself imply elevation above or shading of what lies below.
  • looming: Similar because both can describe something large and overhanging that threatens or dominates; differs because looming stresses the perception of indistinct, menacing size rising or approaching, a fog, a ship, a danger, while overhung describes the actual completed geometry of a canopy above.
  • beetling: Similar because both describe brows, cliffs, and crags that project over and overhang something; differs because beetling is the archaic, literary, dramatic word, a beetling cliff, beetling brows, that stresses the menace of the jutting mass, while overhung is the plainer spatial term.
  • covered: Similar because both can mean having something over or on top; differs because covered is the completely general word, a covered dish, covered in mud, and implies contact or flush coverage, while overhung specifically requires projection from above with space left beneath.
  • roofed: Similar because both describe something sheltered by a structure above; differs because roofed implies an artificial or architectural roof designed to shelter, whereas overhung describes a natural or accidental canopy of rock, branch, or cloud.
  • sheltered: Similar because both can describe protection from above; differs because sheltered emphasises the effect, protected and screened from wind or weather, rather than the mechanism, and includes shelter by walls or hills, whereas overhung specifies a projecting mass hanging over from above.
  • shaded: Similar because both can mean cast into shadow by something above; differs because shaded is narrower, naming only the effect of intercepting light, while overhung describes the full spatial arrangement, projection, cover, space beneath, of which shadow is one result.
  • shrouded: Similar because both can mean covered over; differs because shrouded implies a concealing cloth-like layer, to shroud being to wrap, veil, hide, and suggests concealment, whereas overhung is open spatial description without an implication of hiding.
  • impending: Similar because both can describe a threat hanging over something; differs because impending belongs wholly to the figurative sphere, a danger or event about to happen, while overhung is primarily physical and spatial, with the figurative sense only a secondary extension.
  • suspended: Similar because both involve something held above a space below; differs because suspended stresses hanging by a support, a suspended bridge, suspended animation, with nothing necessarily above the thing hanging, while overhung always implies a canopy or mass extending over something else.
