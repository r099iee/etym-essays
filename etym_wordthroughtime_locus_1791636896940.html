- 1610s–1680s: Classical Rhetoric and Academic Commonplaces - Borrowed directly from Renaissance Latin into English scholarship to denote a *locus communis* (a rhetorical topic, commonplace, or authoritative textual passage).
- 1710s–1790s: Mathematical Geometry - Codified in Newtonian and Enlightenment geometry as the technical term for the complete set or path of points satisfying a given algebraic or spatial rule.
- 19th c.: Jurisprudence and Legal Maxims - Standardized in British and American law through Latin doctrinal phrases such as *locus standi* (standing to sue) and *locus delicti* (the scene of the crime).
- Early 20th c.: Mendelian Genetics - Adopted by Thomas Hunt Morgan and the Columbia fly room geneticists to denote the specific, physically mappable linear address of a gene upon a chromosome.
- Mid-20th c. to Present: Psychology and Contemporary Theory - Popularized by Julian Rotter in 1954 through 'locus of control' in personality theory, and adopted widely in philosophy, art theory, and sociology to designate the central matrix of institutional power and perception.
