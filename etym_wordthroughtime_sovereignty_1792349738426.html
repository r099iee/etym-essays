- [14th c. (Late Middle English & Anglo-Norman)] — entered English from Old French *soveraineté*, denoting the supreme royal supremacy, feudal lordship, and unmatched personal power of a monarch — [perception: revered as a divinely ordained royal prerogative and personal apex of feudal hierarchy]
- [16th–17th c. (Renaissance & Westphalian Era)] — codified by political theorists (notably Jean Bodin and Thomas Hobbes) and ratified by the Peace of Westphalia (1648) into an institutional doctrine of absolute territorial state jurisdiction — [perception: celebrated as the fundamental legal shield safeguarding modern states against imperial and papist overreach]
- [18th c. (Age of Enlightenment & Democratic Revolutions)] — revolutionary shift pioneered by Jean-Jacques Rousseau and the American and French Revolutions from monarchical sovereignty to "popular sovereignty" (sovereignty residing inherently in the governed citizens) — [perception: championed as the sacred egalitarian foundation of democracy, constitutional law, and civil liberty]
- [19th–20th c. (Imperialism, Decolonization & International Law)] — functioned as the definitive benchmark for full membership in the League of Nations and the United Nations, fought for by anti-colonial liberation movements worldwide — [perception: viewed as the non-negotiable right of self-determination for sovereign nations, yet challenged by international human rights frameworks]
- [21st c. (Digital, Cyber & Indigenous Frontiers)] — expanded dynamically into indigenous rights ("tribal sovereignty"), bioethics ("bodily sovereignty"), and digital geopolitics ("data sovereignty" and "technological sovereignty") — [perception: recognized as an evolving, multi-tiered concept balancing global interconnectedness with localized autonomous control]
