• Despondently: Similar because both describe acting in low spirits; differs because despondently implies the complete abandonment of hope and a loss of the will to act, while dejectedly describes visible, drooping sadness that may be temporary and humbling rather than despairing.
  • Disconsolately: Similar because both describe deep unhappiness; differs because disconsolately emphasises inconsolability — the person cannot be comforted — while dejectedly suggests a quieter, more physical lowness that comfort might reach.
  • Gloomily: Similar because both describe a dark, low mood; differs because gloomily carries sullen, brooding darkness and a pessimistic cast, while dejectedly is more about visible drooping discouragement than dark brooding.
  • Mournfully: Similar because both describe a sorrowful manner; differs because mournfully implies grief for a specific loss, with overtones of lament, while dejectedly is a more general low-spiritedness without ritual grief.
  • Sadly: Similar because both describe doing something with unhappiness; differs because sadly is the broad, neutral everyday word covering every shade of unhappiness, while dejectedly is narrower, more literary, and specifically signals the physical posture of being cast down.
  • Sorrowfully: Similar because both describe acting with unhappiness; differs because sorrowfully attaches the feeling to a defined sorrow or regret, while dejectedly describes a general deflation of spirits after disappointment.
  • Forlornly: Similar because both describe a downcast manner; differs because forlornly adds the loneliness and abandonment of being bereft or forsaken, while dejectedly need not imply isolation — the person may simply be cast down among friends.
  • Wistfully: Similar because both describe a soft, subdued manner; differs because wistfully implies longing and nostalgia for something absent, with a gentle, yearning sadness, while dejectedly is the heavier, more collapsed sadness of defeat or disappointment.
  • Morosely: Similar because both describe a low-spirited way of behaving; differs because morosely adds sullenness, irritability, and a sour withdrawal from others, while dejectedly is passive and drooping rather than hostile or grumpy.
  • Downcast: Similar because both are built on the same "cast down" image and describe the lowered posture of low spirits; differs because downcast is principally an adjective and most specifically describes lowered eyes or gaze, while dejectedly is an adverb covering the whole demeanour.
  • Glumly: Similar because both describe a dreary, low mood; differs because glumly suggests a quiet, moody, unsmiling grimness with a touch of sulk, while dejectedly describes the visible collapse of spirit without sulkiness.
  • Woefully: Similar because both describe doing something in a distressed way; differs because woefully has a stronger flavour of wretchedness and pity, often used with mild irony ("woefully inadequate"), while dejectedly stays squarely in personal sadness.
  • Heavily: Similar because both describe slow, burdened movement; differs because heavily is a general adverb of weight and burden usable of physical objects, while dejectedly specifically names the emotional cause of the heaviness.
  • Despairingly: Similar because both describe the collapse of hope; differs because despairingly names the loss of all hope itself, an extremity, while dejectedly describes a lesser, more visible lowness that still implies possible recovery.
