• Moldy / Mouldy: Similar because both describe the smell or state of fungal decay; differs because moldy implies visible mould growth, whereas musty names the smell of damp decay without requiring visible mould.
  • Stale: Similar because both describe air or food that has gone flat and old; differs because stale stresses exhaustion and flatness (as of a closed room or old bread), whereas musty adds the damp, sour note of organic decomposition.
  • Fusty: Similar because both mean stale-smelling; differs because fusty (from Old French fusté, "smelling of the cask") leans toward the wine-cask and musty air of a close room, whereas musty leans toward damp cloth and old paper.
  • Damp: Similar because both are associated with wetness and enclosed spaces; differs because damp describes the wetness itself (a damp room), whereas musty describes the smell the wetness produces.
  • Dank: Similar because both describe unpleasant, wet, enclosed spaces; differs because dank stresses the cold unpleasant wetness of a place, whereas musty stresses the smell of decay.
  • Rank: Similar because both describe strong unpleasant smells; differs because rank is stronger and can be foul or offensive (rank vegetation), whereas musty is a milder, earthier staleness.
  • Sour: Similar because both describe an acid, unpleasant smell or taste; differs because sour is the general word for acidic smells (milk, wine, breath), whereas musty names specifically the damp-mold staleness.
  • Earthy: Similar because both describe a soil-like, organic smell; differs because earthy can be neutral or pleasant (good earthy soil), whereas musty is always the smell of damp decay.
  • Rusty (of smell): Similar because both describe the stale smell of age; differs because rusty (of a smell) is a metallic, metallic-stale note, whereas musty is the organic damp note.
  • Close (of air): Similar because both describe stuffy, unventilated air; differs because close describes the air's lack of freshness without specifying the odour, whereas musty names the characteristic smell.
  • Outdated: Similar in the figurative sense of being past usefulness; differs because outdated is a neutral, modern judgement about currency, whereas musty adds the contemptuous image of stale, airless age.
  • Obsolete: Similar because both describe what is no longer in use; differs because obsolete is a technical, neutral word for a word or thing no longer current, whereas musty is a pejorative about staleness.
  • Archaic: Similar because both describe what belongs to an earlier age; differs because archaic can be reverent (archaic beauty) or neutral, whereas musty always implies decay and neglect.
  • Antiquated: Similar because both describe what is old-fashioned and out of date; differs because antiquated stresses being old-fashioned in design or custom, whereas musty stresses staleness and airlessness.
  • Outmoded: Similar because both describe what is no longer current; differs because outmoded is a neutral judgement about fashion, whereas musty carries the sensory image of damp decay.
  • Passé: Similar because both describe what is no longer fashionable; differs because passé is a light, social word for being behind the times, whereas musty is heavier and sensory.
  • Hoary: Similar because both describe age and antiquity; differs because hoary (from "grey-haired") suggests ancientness and venerable age, whereas musty suggests decay and staleness.
  • Superannuated: Similar because both describe what is outdated by age; differs because superannuated is a formal, bureaucratic word for being retired by age, whereas musty is sensory and pejorative.
  • Old: Similar because both describe age; differs because old is completely neutral, whereas musty specifies the smell of damp age and, figuratively, the staleness of unrefreshed ideas.
