- 700s BCE Archaic Greek myth (Hesiod) - named the venomous, multi-headed swamp serpent of Lerna slain by Heracles; mythological and terrifying
- 1st c. BCE-400 CE Roman & Medieval Christian allegory - symbol of insurmountable perils in Roman poetry and multiplying heresies/sins in medieval bestiaries; moral and theological
- 1500s-1600s Renaissance political polemics - applied by Shakespeare, Spenser, and Milton to the "many-headed multitude", civil war, and popular uprisings; political and dramatic
- 1740-1815 Counter-Revolution & Linnaean taxonomy - adopted by anti-revolutionary polemicists (Burke, Tolstoy's Anna Pavlovna) for the "hydra of revolution" alongside Linnaeus's 1758 naming of the regenerative freshwater polyp genus Hydra; ideological and scientific
- 1900s-present Modern systemic metaphor - standard descriptor for decentralized criminal networks, sprawling bureaucracies, cyber threats, and biological immortality research; geopolitical and systemic
