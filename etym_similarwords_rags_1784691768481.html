• Tatters: Similar because it denotes torn, ragged cloth; differs because "tatters" emphasises hanging and trailing strips and is almost synonymous with ragged clothing, while "rags" keeps a rougher, more tactile edge and a wider transferred range.
  • Shreds: Similar because it denotes torn pieces of material; differs because "shreds" suggests smaller, narrower torn pieces, while rags are larger, coarser strips of cloth.
  • Scraps: Similar because it denotes small detached remnants; differs because "scraps" can be any small fragments of cloth or anything else, while "rags" specifically retains the roughness and torn texture of cloth.
  • Fragments: Similar because it denotes broken pieces of something; differs because "fragments" is the colourless, technical word for broken parts of anything, without the texture or poverty of rags.
  • Clout: Similar because it is an old English word for a piece of cloth; differs because "clout" is a patch or piece of cloth lacking rags' connotation of poverty, tattered clothing, and shredding.
  • Ribbons: Similar because both can describe material torn into hanging strips ("torn to ribbons"); differs because "ribbons" suggests neat, narrow, decorative strips, while rags are coarse, ragged, and worn.
  • Wisps: Similar because both can describe wisp-like, shred-like fragments of cloud or mist; differs because "wisps" are fine, light, trailing strands without the rough texture or poverty of rags.
  • Tattered: Similar because it is the adjectival form of the same ragged condition; differs because "tattered" is an adjective describing torn condition, while "rags" is a noun for the torn cloth itself.
  • Threadbare: Similar because it describes worn-out cloth; differs because "threadbare" means worn thin so the threads show, while rags are torn and ragged rather than merely thin.
