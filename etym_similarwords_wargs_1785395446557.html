• Wolves: Similar because both are large, dangerous pack predators, and wargs are wolves in Tolkien's fiction; differs because wolves are natural animals acting on instinct, while wargs are supernatural in size and malice, with intelligence and coordinated organization.
  • Dire wolves: Similar because both are huge, formidable wolves; differs because dire wolves were a real but extinct canid — wholly natural — whereas wargs are fictional, evil, and quasi-sentient, creatures of legend rather than of the fossil record.
  • Werewolves: Similar because both blur the line between wolf and intelligence; differs because werewolves are humans transformed into wolves (or into wolf-humans), while wargs are wolves by nature and are never human.
  • Hounds: Similar because both are hunting creatures used to pursue prey; differs because hounds are domestic dogs serving humans, while wargs are wild, malevolent beasts organized into a military species that serves evil masters.
  • Worgs: Similar because worgs are the Dungeons & Dragons respelling of the same word and creature type; differs because worgs are a separate, game-mechanics species in tabletop settings, while wargs retain Tolkien's older, more legendary character.
  • Hellhounds: Similar because both are infernal, terrifying wolf-like beasts of evil; differs because hellhounds belong to Christian and classical mythology as guardians of the underworld, while wargs are creatures of Middle-earth's natural-but-cursed wilderness.
  • Lycanthropes: Similar because both name wolf-like beings in fiction; differs because lycanthropes are shapeshifters — humans who become wolves — while wargs are wolves, never humans, and the word carries no transformation at all.
  • Outlaws: Similar because the English word outlaw and warg share the ancient Germanic root sense of "the accursed one cast out of society", and Old Norse vargr literally meant outlaw; differs because outlaw in modern English names a fugitive from human law, while warg names a wolf — the two are etymological cousins, not synonyms.
