- Old English — the soldier waistband: belt names the leather girdle of warriors and monks, practical gear for sword, purse, and tunic.
- Medieval — status on the hips: knights take the belt as the badge of rank, and to gird a youth with a belt is to make him a man of standing.
- Early modern — the strap as punisher: the belt becomes the schoolroom and household rod, and to belt someone is to strike them with it.
- 18th–19th c. — industry borrows the loop: millwrights name the endless leather loop driving machinery a belt, and geography names farmland strips like the corn belt.
- 20th c. — safety and rank: seat belts save lives in cars and planes, while judo and karate turn coloured belts into world-famous grades of mastery.
- Today — waist, land, and machine at once: fashion belts, green belts, asteroid belts, and conveyor belts share the word without confusion, each a band doing the work of binding.
