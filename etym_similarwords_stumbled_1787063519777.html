• Tripped: Similar because both mean to catch the foot and lose balance; differs because “tripped” focuses on the obstacle catching the foot and is a single discrete event, while “stumbled” can also describe continued unsteady movement.
  • Staggered: Similar because both describe unsteady movement; differs because staggering implies reeling from weakness, drink, or a blow — greater instability and swaying — and has no “chance discovery” sense.
  • Tottered: Similar because both mean unsteady movement; differs because “tottered” suggests teetering on the verge of collapse, with frail or unsteady steps, and is also used of buildings or systems about to fall.
  • Faltered: Similar because both mean losing steadiness; differs because “faltered” is more about hesitation, failing strength, or weakening resolve (of a voice, heart, or effort) and is often purely figurative.
  • Floundered: Similar because both mean moving or proceeding clumsily; differs because “floundered” implies struggling helplessly — often literally in water or mud — and in figurative use suggests thrashing about without progress.
  • Lurched: Similar because both describe sudden unsteady movement; differs because “lurched” implies a sharp, abrupt jerk (of a person, vehicle, or ship) rather than a foot catching an obstacle.
  • Blundered: Similar because both can mean moving clumsily or making a mistake; differs because “blundered” emphasises a heavy, foolish error and never carries the positive “found by luck” sense.
  • Wavered: Similar because both mean losing steadiness; differs because “wavered” describes trembling indecision or unsteadiness from doubt rather than a physical foot-catching trip.
  • Erred: Similar because both can describe making a mistake; differs because “erred” is purely intellectual or moral, with no physical tripping image at all.
  • Happened upon / chanced upon: Similar because all express accidental discovery; differs because these phrasal forms are the only ones used for fortunate meetings, and they lack the connotation of an initial slip.
