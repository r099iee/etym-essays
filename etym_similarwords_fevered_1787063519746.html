• feverish: Similar because both mean affected by or resembling a fever; differs because "feverish" is the standard, everyday adjective, while "fevered" is the more literary, emphatic participle implying something actively heated to excess.
  • frenzied: Similar because both describe wild, uncontrolled activity or excitement; differs because "frenzied" implies madness and chaos, while "fevered" implies heat and intensity without the loss of sanity.
  • frantic: Similar because both describe agitated, uncontrolled states; differs because "frantic" is about panicky haste, whereas "fevered" is about burning, driven intensity.
  • agitated: Similar because both describe disturbed, restless states; differs because "agitated" is clinical and general, while "fevered" adds the image of bodily heat.
  • excited: Similar because both can describe heightened emotion; differs because "excited" is neutral and often positive, while "fevered" implies excess, near-illness, and a loss of calm.
  • hectic: Similar because both describe busy, feverish activity; differs because "hectic" is used of schedules and scenes, while "fevered" attaches to minds, dreams, and bodies.
  • febrile: Similar because both are medical adjectives meaning feverish; differs because "febrile" is the formal clinical term, while "fevered" is the common literary word.
  • flushed: Similar because both suggest heat and redness of the body; differs because "flushed" describes the appearance (red cheeks) without the fever's systemic agitation.
  • burning: Similar because both evoke intense heat; differs because "burning" is literal or figurative heat of passion, while "fevered" ties the heat to illness or uncontrollable excitation.
  • delirious: Similar because both can describe fevered mental states; differs because "delirious" means actively hallucinating or raving, a stronger and more specific symptom.
  • manic: Similar because both describe abnormally high energy; differs because "manic" is a clinical term for a specific state, while "fevered" is a general image of agitation.
