- 12th – 13th Century (Plantagenet Era): Royal legislative enactments and jury inquests - Originated under Henry II (e.g. Assize of Clarendon, 1166) as royal decrees establishing standard trial procedures and grand jury presentments.
- 13th – 16th Century: Statutory regulations of prices, weights, and measures - Expanded to denote royal ordinances regulating basic commodities, most famously the Assize of Bread and Ale.
- 17th – 19th Century (Victorian Era): The premier county criminal and civil circuit courts - Epitomized the highest tier of provincial English justice, where traveling High Court judges tried serious felonies and grand jury indictments.
- 20th Century (Post-1971): Abolished and preserved in historical and legal literature - Replaced by the Courts Act 1971 with the unified Crown Court system, surviving today as a historical and legal concept.
