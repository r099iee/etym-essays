• Protuberant: Similar because both describe physical features that bulge or project outward beyond the surrounding surface; differs because protuberant is a formal, descriptive term referring to any outward swelling or prominence regardless of shape, whereas bulbous specifically emphasizes a spherical, swollen, onion-like roundedness.
  • Swollen: Similar because both denote an increase in volume or rounded distension; differs because swollen typically implies pathological inflammation, fluid accumulation, injury, or temporary enlargement, whereas bulbous describes an inherent or permanent structural shape.
  • Globular: Similar because both describe round, three-dimensional forms; differs because globular implies a relatively pure, symmetrical spherical shape (like a droplet or globe), whereas bulbous often suggests an uneven, heavy, bottom-weighted, or grotesque roundness.
  • Convex: Similar because both describe curved outward surfaces; differs because convex is a precise geometric and optical term referring to uniform outward curvature, lacking the organic, heavy, or grotesque connotations of bulbous.
  • Pug-nosed / Tuberous: Similar because both describe knobby, rounded organic protrusions; differs because tuberous specifically resembles a potato or root tuber (rough, irregular, knotty), whereas bulbous is smoother and more uniformly rounded like an onion or flower bulb.
  • Puffy: Similar because both convey soft, enlarged roundness; differs because puffy indicates soft, spongy, air-filled, or waterlogged swelling (such as tired eyes or quilted fabric), whereas bulbous suggests solid, substantial, structural mass and protrusion.
  • Distended: Similar because both denote outward physical expansion; differs because distended implies abnormal stretching or bloating from internal pressure (such as a distended stomach), whereas bulbous refers to the general geometric contour of an object.
  • Tumid: Similar because both describe bulging, swollen structures; differs because tumid is an elevated literary or medical term denoting tense, congested swelling, or pompous rhetorical style, whereas bulbous is widely used in anatomy, botany, and visual descriptions.
