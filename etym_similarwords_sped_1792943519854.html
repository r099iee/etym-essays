• Hastened: Similar because both mean hurried to completion; differs because hastened stresses urging forward, while sped stresses the swift motion itself, already done.
  • Raced: Similar because both describe rapid past going; differs because raced implies contest or top effort, while sped may be smooth and purposeful.
  • Dashed: Similar because both are sudden swift past movements; differs because dashed is abrupt and short, while sped can cover a whole journey.
  • Hurtled: Similar because both fling through space fast; differs because hurtled is violent and barely controlled, while sped is directed and dutiful.
  • Galloped: Similar because ballad riders do both; differs because galloped names the horse's gait precisely, while sped names any swift completion.
  • Flew: Similar because both say time and travellers passed rapidly; differs because flew is general and soaring, while sped keeps the old flavour of mission and errand.
  • Rushed: Similar because both hurry; differs because rushed often implies careless haste, while sped sounds efficient and fated.
  • Hurried: Similar because both make haste; differs because hurried can sound anxious and breathless, while sped sounds swift and sure.
