- Latin aggravare - To make heavy, weigh down (*ad-* "to" + *gravis* "heavy").
- Old French agrever (10th–12th c.) - To burden, distress, afflict physically or mentally.
- Middle English agreven (14th c.) - To cause grief to, afflict with hardship or injustice.
- 16th–17th Century - Evolution of passive participle aggrieved to describe people feeling targeted by unfair decisions.
- 18th–19th Century - Formalization in English common law ("aggrieved party").
- Modern / Contemporary usage - Widespread use for wounded entitlement, political resentment, and personal indignation.
