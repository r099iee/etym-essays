• Satire: Similar because both are comic genres that ridicule human foolishness, vanity, and social corruption; differs because satire has a deliberate moral and intellectual purpose, using wit, irony, and sarcasm to expose and reform vices, whereas farce prioritizes pure ludicrous amusement, physical slapstick, and absurd kinetic chaos for their own sake.
  • Parody: Similar because both employ exaggeration and humor to provoke laughter; differs because parody must mimic and exaggerate the specific stylistic idiosyncrasies of a known artist, text, or genre (e.g., a parody of a gothic novel), whereas farce creates independent, situational comedy driven by frantic pacing and ridiculous predicaments.
  • Travesty: Similar because both represent absurd debasements of serious matters; differs because a travesty specifically involves a grotesque, distorted, and insulting misrepresentation of something inherently solemn, holy, or just (e.g., a travesty of justice), carrying bitter indignity, whereas farce can be delightful, lighthearted, and playful theater.
  • Burlesque: Similar because both use broad humor, parody, and caricature to mock serious subjects; differs because burlesque historically relies on incongruity of register—treating serious subjects with absurd vulgarity or treating trivial subjects with mock-heroic solemnity—and later evolved into musical stage variety, whereas farce is structured around tight situational plots and mistaken identities.
  • Slapstick: Similar because both rely on boisterous, energetic physical comedy and sight gags; differs because slapstick specifically refers to physical violence—pratfalls, slipping on banana peels, pie-throwing, colliding with ladders—as an isolated comedic device, whereas farce is a complete dramatic structure that incorporates slapstick within an intricate web of mistaken identities.
  • Mockery: Similar because both expose human behavior to laughter and derision; differs because mockery is a direct, often cruel act of scorn, contempt, or personal ridicule (e.g., making a mockery of someone's faith), whereas farce is primarily an expansive comedic narrative or structural pretense.
  • Sham: Similar because both refer to hollow, false pretenses that conceal an underlying lack of substance; differs because a sham is a deceptive fraud designed to trick or swindle people (e.g., a sham marriage or business), whereas a farce describes the ridiculous, disorganized, and laughably transparent absurdity of the situation.
  • Caricature: Similar because both produce laughter through grotesque exaggeration; differs because caricature is a static portrait or literary sketch that exaggerates specific physical or behavioral features, while farce is a dynamic, kinetic dramatic performance in continuous motion.
