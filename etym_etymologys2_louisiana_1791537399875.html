Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*ḱlew- + *weyk-
"to hear, famous" + "to conquer"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*Hlūdawīgaz
"famous warrior, renowned in battle"
↓
Old Frankish (c. 5th c. CE)
*Hlōdawīg
"famous warrior / King Clovis I"
↓
Late Latin (c. 6th–9th c. CE)
Ludovicus / Clodovicus
"Latinized Germanic personal name"
↓
Old French (c. 11th c. CE)
Looïs / Louis
"French royal given name"
↓
Early Modern French (April 9, 1682)
La Louisiane
"territory belonging to King Louis XIV"
↓
Modern English (18th c. CE)
Louisiana
"U.S. state; former French territory"

Note: Coined in April 1682 by French explorer René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle, by attaching the Latinate territory-forming suffix -ana / -iane to the royal name of King Louis XIV of France (*Louis le Grand*). The given name Louis is the direct French development of Frankish *Hlōdawīg, which also gave rise to the name Clovis and German Ludwig.
