• Conquered: Similar because both denote victory and the reduction of a people to subjection; differs because "conquered" can be momentary (the winning of a battle) whereas "subjugated" implies the lasting yoke of sustained control.
  • Subdued: Similar because both describe being brought under control; differs because "subdued" is milder and can mean merely quieted or pacified (or, of a person, restrained in manner), while "subjugated" means wholly dominated.
  • Subordinated: Similar because both describe being placed in a lower position; differs because subordination is a graded relation of rank (one thing lower than another within a hierarchy), not necessarily the total conquest of subjugation.
  • Dominated: Similar because both mean controlled or ruled by another; differs because domination can be informal, structural, or even psychological, while subjugation usually implies force or conquest.
  • Oppressed: Similar because both describe a people held down by power; differs because oppression stresses unjust, burdensome rule and may be economic or social, whereas subjugation stresses military or political conquest.
  • Enslaved: Similar because both describe total loss of freedom; differs because enslavement is the legal institution of chattel slavery, while subjugation describes the political domination of a whole people who are not necessarily owned.
  • Vanquished: Similar because both describe being decisively defeated; differs because "vanquished" emphasizes defeat in combat and can be complete at a single stroke, while subjugation emphasizes the control that follows.
  • Defeated: Similar because both describe the loser of a conflict; differs because defeat is a single outcome and does not of itself imply lasting dominion.
  • Overcome: Similar because both mean prevailed over; differs because "overcome" can be used of obstacles, emotions or sickness, and need not involve a people at all.
  • Overwhelmed: Similar because both suggest being crushed by superior force; differs because "overwhelmed" implies being swamped or inundated, often briefly, rather than formally yoked and held.
  • Crushed / Quelled: Similar because both describe harsh suppression; differs because they name the act of suppression itself and are more violent and momentary than the enduring state of subjugation.
  • Subjected: Similar because both derive from being placed under (sub) a power; differs because "subjected" is less intense and is often used of individuals or single acts ("subjected to questioning"), not of the total conquest of peoples.
  • Submissive / Subdued (adj.): Similar because both describe a yielding attitude; differs because "submissive" is a temperament or posture chosen or habitual, while "subjugated" is a condition imposed from outside.
  • Subservient: Similar because both describe being made to serve another; differs because subservience is a servile role within a hierarchy, whereas subjugation is domination by conquest.
  • Tyrannized: Similar because both describe cruel rule; differs because tyranny stresses the arbitrary cruelty of the ruler, while subjugation stresses the loss of freedom of the ruled.
  • Subdued (colonial register): Similar because both were used of imperial conquest in 18th–19th-century prose; differs because "subdued" in that register often softened or euphemized what "subjugated" named plainly.
