Note on morphological compounding: Latin ēmānāre is compounded from the prefix ē- (the phonetic variant of ex- occurring before voiced consonants, from PIE *eǵʰs, "out of, forth") + the verb mānāre ("to flow, trickle, seep"), rooted in Proto-Indo-European *meh₂- ("wet, damp, to flow"). English back-formed the verb emanate in the late eighteenth century from the earlier noun emanation (borrowed in the 1560s), affixing the native Germanic participial suffix -ing (from Old English -ende).
