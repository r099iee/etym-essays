• abruptly: Similar in sudden, short brevity; differs because it stresses unexpected onset, while curtly implies a deliberate cutting-short that communicates dismissal.
  • brusquely: Similar in a brief, off-putting manner; differs because it adds roughness and lack of courtesy, whereas curtly's core is clipped brevity itself — the word cut to its stump.
  • tersely: Similar in economical brevity; differs because it is efficiently concise without rudeness, while curtly carries the edge of impatience and disdain.
  • shortly: Similar in brief, quick manner; differs because it is neutral and often merely temporal ("shortly after"), while curtly is pointedly, rudely brief in manner.
  • peremptorily: Similar in speech that cuts off further discussion; differs because it asserts commanding authority, while curtly's brevity is dismissive rather than commanding.
  • snappishly: Similar in sharp, short speech; differs because it adds irritability and a bite in the tone, while curtly is cool and clipped rather than angry.
  • testily: Similar in short, impatient speech; differs because it adds ill-tempered touchiness, a peevish edge that curtly's dry brevity lacks.
  • gruffly: Similar in rough, unfriendly brevity; differs because it suggests a hoarse, bluff, or surly voice, while curtly is about the length and trimming of the words themselves.
  • bluntly: Similar in speaking without softening; differs because bluntness is about frankness and the refusal to spare feelings, while curtness is about brevity and the refusal to spend words.
  • laconically: Similar in using very few words; differs because it is the classical, dignified economy of the Spartan, while curtly carries the modern edge of rudeness and dismissal.
  • succinctly: Similar in being brief and to the point; differs because succinctness is admired concision, complete and even elegant, while curtly is clipped and communicative of impatience.
