• Cowardly: Similar because it denotes a lack of courage when facing danger or hardship; differs because cowardly can be a sympathetic human weakness of fear, whereas dastardly implies active, malicious treachery executed while avoiding risk.
  • Craven: Similar because it highlights abject, contemptible lack of spirit; differs because craven emphasizes slavish, groveling surrender to fear, whereas dastardly emphasizes the underhanded, deceitful nature of the deed itself.
  • Treacherous: Similar because both involve betrayal of trust and faith; differs because treacherous focuses specifically on the breach of loyalty or hidden danger, without necessarily highlighting personal cowardice as the primary motive.
  • Pusillanimous: Similar because it condemns small-minded, timid avoidance of duty; differs because pusillanimous is a formal, intellectual Latinate term emphasizing petty faint-heartedness, lacking the vivid, villainous punch of dastardly.
  • Nefarious: Similar because it describes wicked and villainous actions; differs because nefarious emphasizes flagrant, criminal wickedness and sinfulness without requiring that the act be sneakily cowardly.
  • Underhanded: Similar because it refers to secretive, deceptive behavior; differs because underhanded focuses on sneakiness, cheating, and lack of transparency, whereas dastardly carries a far heavier emotional charge of moral contempt and base desertion.
  • Ignoble: Similar because it denotes conduct lacking honor, dignity, or chivalry; differs because ignoble is broader and milder, indicating a lack of noble principles rather than actively malicious, sneaky betrayal.
