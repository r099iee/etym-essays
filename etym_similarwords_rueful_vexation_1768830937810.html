• Remorseful: Similar in feeling sorrow over one's actions; differs because remorseful implies moral guilt and torment of conscience, while rueful keeps a wry, self-aware humour without self-pity.
  • Regretful: Similar in wishing things had been otherwise; differs because regretful is a neutral wishing, lacking the dry, faintly humorous twist of rueful.
  • Penitent: Similar in expressing sorrow for wrongdoing; differs because penitent carries solemn religious or moral weight, whereas rueful is light, secular, and self-deprecating.
  • Sorrowful: Similar in feeling grief; differs because sorrowful is heavy and unrelieved sadness, while rueful is regret tempered by wry self-awareness.
  • Apologetic: Similar in acknowledging a fault; differs because apologetic is directed at another person and seeks pardon, whereas rueful is inward, directed at one's own folly.
  • Chagrined: Similar in distress from disappointment; differs because chagrined stems from humiliated pride or embarrassment, while rueful is gentler and more self-accepting.
  • Melancholy: Similar in a sorrowful mood; differs because melancholy is a pervasive sadness without the knowing smile of rueful.
  • Wry: Similar in a dry, humorous twist; differs because wry describes the tone or expression itself, whereas rueful names the underlying regret.
  • Self-reproachful: Similar in blaming oneself; differs because self-reproachful is severe inward criticism, while rueful forgives itself with a smile.
  • Lugubrious (near-opposite): Similar in mournfulness; differs because lugubrious is exaggerated, theatrical grief, whereas rueful is quiet and ironic.
  • Annoyance: Similar in the disturbance of calm; differs because annoyance is a mild, passing irritation, while vexation carries the shaking, jolting image of its Latin root vexāre.
  • Irritation: Similar in a state of being disturbed; differs because irritation is a surface pricking of the temper, whereas vexation is a deeper, rattling disturbance of the spirit.
  • Chagrin: Similar in a vexed, embarrassed state; differs because chagrin is tied to wounded pride, while vexation can be pure bother without humiliation.
  • Worry: Similar in a troubled mental state; differs because worry is anxious dwelling on the future, while vexation is irritation at a present nuisance.
  • Distress: Similar in physical or mental affliction; differs because distress is more severe and often physical, while vexation is annoyance with a jolting edge.
  • Harassment: Similar in the archaic physical sense of vexation; differs because harassment is the act of persecuting someone, whereas vexation is the resulting feeling or its cause.
  • Exasperation: Similar in intense irritation; differs because exasperation implies being pushed past patience, while vexation emphasises the shaking disturbance of composure.
  • Pique: Similar in a feeling of irritation; differs because pique is pride-based resentment, while vexation is general and jolting annoyance.
  • Disquiet: Similar in disturbance of inner calm; differs because disquiet is a vague anxiety, whereas vexation is a sharper, more pointed irritation.
