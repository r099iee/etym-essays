• Echoic: Similar because both describe words that echo the sounds they denote; differs because echoic is the shorter, chiefly technical synonym preferred in linguistics, while onomatopoeic is the standard literary and general term.
  • Imitative: Similar because both describe copying a sound or model; differs because imitative is fully general — it applies to art, behaviour, and animals — whereas onomatopoeic is restricted to language and specifically to sound-imitative word formation.
  • Mimetic: Similar because both describe imitation; differs because mimetic is used broadly of art and literature that imitate life, while onomatopoeic is narrowly about words imitating sounds.
  • Phonaesthetic: Similar because both concern the relation between sound and meaning; differs because phonaesthetic names the broader category of pleasant/unpleasant sound associations that need not imitate anything, while onomatopoeic names direct acoustic imitation.
  • Sound-symbolic: Similar because both involve sounds standing for meanings; differs because sound symbolism includes arbitrary correlations of sound and sense (e.g. the i/oo contrast for small/large), while onomatopoeic requires an actual imitation of the referent's sound.
  • Echo-word: Similar because both name a word formed by imitation; differs because echo-word is colloquial and can include reduplicated forms (chit-chat, ping-pong) that are not pure sound imitation, while onomatopoeic is the precise term for imitative formation.
  • Onomatopoetic: Similar because it is a variant spelling of the same word; differs only in form — onomatopoetic and onomatopœic are alternative spellings of onomatopoeic, with onomatopoeic the modern standard.
  • Iconic: Similar because both describe signs that resemble their referents; differs because iconic is the semiotic term for any resemblance-based sign (pictures, diagrams, gestures), while onomatopoeic applies only to sound-resembling words.
