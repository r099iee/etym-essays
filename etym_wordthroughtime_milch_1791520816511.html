- 8th century, Old High German - miluh names the everyday dairy liquid and the nurturing secretion; it is a plain household word with no figurative burden.
- 12th–14th century, Middle High German - milch settles into the modern consonant shape; courtly and religious verse use milk as an image of purity and gentle nourishment.
- 16th century, Reformation - Luther's Bible fixes Milch in the phrase “ein Land, darin Milch und Honig fließt”, and the Epistle of Peter's “pure milk” gives the word a spiritual sense of elementary doctrine.
- 18th century, Enlightenment and Storm and Stress - the word acquires a sentimental literary colour; Schiller's Wilhelm Tell (1804) gives the language the line “Die Milch der frommen Denkart”, making milk a figure for soft-hearted mildness.
- 19th century, industrial dairying - condensed milk, milk powder and urban dairy distribution turn Milch into a mass commodity and a public-health issue; Milchmädchen and Milchmädchenrechnung enter the idiom stock.
- late 19th–20th century - Milchstraße becomes the ordinary German name for the galaxy; Milchgesicht and Milchbart are used as barrack-room insults for inexperienced recruits.
- 20th century, consumer culture - Sonnenmilch, Reinigungsmilch and Körpermilch extend the word to cosmetics and lotions, driven purely by the image of a white emulsion.
- 21st century - Hafermilch, Sojamilch and Mandelmilch make the word a battleground over labelling and dairy protection; “milk” is now as much a marketing category as a secretion.
