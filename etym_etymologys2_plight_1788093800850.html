*plek-
"to plait, weave, fold"
↓

plicāre
"to fold"
↓

pleit

↓

↓

plit
"condition, state"
↓
English (c. 1550)
PLIGHT
"bad condition, difficult state"
(spelling crossed with the native word below)

Chain 2 — the native word (spelling and meaning contaminant)
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*plehti-
"duty, obligation, danger"
↓
Old English (pre-1100)
pliht
"danger, peril; pledge, duty"
↓
English (survives in "plight one's troth")
plight
"pledge, promise"

Note: The modern word is the French word spelled with the native word's -gh-; the "bad condition" meaning absorbed the native word's "peril". The Proto-Germanic *plehti- (cf. German Pflicht "duty", Old High German pfliht) has no secure PIE reconstruction: some scholars link it to a root "to be engaged, bound", others to *plek- "to fold" itself (an obligation as a "bond"); the origin is marked uncertain in the standard dictionaries.
