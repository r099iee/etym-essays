Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
grandis
"full-grown, large, great, grand"
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 6th c. CE)
*adgrandīre
"to make larger, enlarge"
↓
Old French (c. 12th c.)
agrandir
"to increase, augment, elevate"
↓
Middle French (c. 16th c.)
agrandissement
"enlargement, advancement,"
"increase in power or dignity"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1650s)
aggrandize + -ment
"advancement of power/honor"
↓
Modern English (present)
aggrandizement
"act of increasing power/wealth;"
"ruthless self-elevation"

Note: The ultimate Proto-Indo-European origin of Latin grandis remains uncertain; older proposals linking it to *gʷʰrendʰ- ('to swell') have been rejected by modern historical linguists like Michiel de Vaan due to insurmountable phonetic and semantic difficulties. English adopted the verb aggrandize in the 17th century under French influence, appending the suffix -ment to match French agrandissement.
