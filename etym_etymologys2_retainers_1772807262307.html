DERIVATION CHAIN FOR RETAINERS: 1. Proto-Indo-European *ten- (verbal root, 'to stretch') [c. 4500-3000 BCE; reconstructed, no direct attestation] 2. Latin tenēre (verb, 'to hold, keep, possess'; built on the *ten- root with the semantic shift 'stretch taut' > 'hold firm') [attested from the 3rd-2nd c. BCE onward] 3. Latin retinēre (verb, 'to hold back, keep, restrain, preserve'; re- 'back' + tenēre 'hold') [attested in classical Latin, 1st c. BCE onward] 4. Old French retenir (verb, 'to keep, hold back, engage in service') [attested 10th-12th c.] 5. Middle English retenen / retain (verb, 'to keep, continue to hold, engage for service') [attested 14th c.] 6. Middle English / Anglo-Norman agent noun retainer (noun, 'one who is retained in service; a follower kept by a lord') [attested 15th c.; the fee sense 'a sum paid to keep counsel available' develops from the 17th c.] 7. Modern English plural RETAINERS (noun, 'servants or followers of a noble or wealthy person, especially ones of long standing') [contemporary English; also the legal fee sense and, from the 20th c., the dental device] PREFIX ELEMENT: The Latin prefix re- 'back, again' in retinēre contributes the nuance 'hold back / keep' rather than simple 'hold'; the same prefix appears throughout the English retain, retention, retentive. This is not a separate root word, but part of the single chain. DEBATED / NUANCED NOTES: The link between PIE *ten- 'to stretch' and Latin tenēre 'to hold' is standard but involves a recognized semantic shift: the same root appears in Sanskrit tanóti 'stretches, extends' and Greek teínō 'I stretch,' and the Latin verb's 'hold' sense is understood as a specialization of 'hold taut' from the stretched state. The agent noun retainer is a native English/French formation (retain + -er) rather than a Latin noun; the word's exact first attestation is sometimes given for the follower sense in the 15th century and for the fee sense later, so both dates are approximate. No stage has been invented; the only reconstructed form is the PIE root, flagged as such.
