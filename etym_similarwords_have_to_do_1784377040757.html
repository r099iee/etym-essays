• Relate to: Similar because both assert a connection between two things; differs because relate to is a single verb with a more formal, analytical register, while have to do with is the colloquial idiom of the same relation.
  • Concern: Similar because both mean "to be about, to be relevant to"; differs because concern is more formal and can be used transitively ("this concerns you"), while have to do with is fixed and conversational.
  • Pertain to: Similar because both mean "to be connected with or relevant to"; differs because pertain to is formal and legalistic, while have to do with is everyday.
  • Bear on: Similar because both mean "to be relevant to a matter"; differs because bear on stresses the weight or significance of the relevance, while have to do with merely asserts connection.
  • Be connected with: Similar because both assert a link between things; differs because be connected with is more neutral and less idiomatic, without the possessive history of have to do with.
  • Be associated with: Similar because both mean "to be linked with"; differs because be associated with is more formal and often implies a mental or statistical link, while have to do with implies practical concern or dealings.
  • Deal with: Similar because both involve engagement with a matter or person; differs because deal with stresses active handling of a task, while have to do with stresses mere relation or connection.
  • Have dealings with: Similar because both name association with a person; differs because have dealings with is specifically about transactions or social contact, while have to do with covers relevance and relation too.
  • Be involved with / in: Similar because both mean "to have a part or connection"; differs because be involved implies active participation, while have to do with can be purely about relevance.
  • Touch on: Similar because both mean "to be connected with a topic"; differs because touch on means to mention briefly, while have to do with asserts a standing relation.
  • Apply to: Similar because both mean "to be relevant to"; differs because apply to is formal and often legal or rule-based, while have to do with is general and conversational.
  • Be the business of: Similar because both relate to someone's concern; differs because be the business of is more emphatic about ownership of the matter, while have to do with simply asserts connection.
