• Torpor: similar because both name a state of deep inactivity; differs because torpor is more physical and often animal or hibernal, and can be a protective dormancy, while lethargy points to the mind and to a pathological dulling.
  • Lassitude: similar because both express exhaustion of body and mind; differs because lassitude is gentler and more poetic, a weariness without clinical drowsiness, usually self-acknowledged rather than indicting.
  • Languor: similar because both describe slow, heavy stillness; differs because languor is often pleasant and voluptuous (the languor of a summer evening), while lethargy is negative and unresponsive.
  • Apathy: similar because both name a failure of engagement; differs because apathy is exclusively emotional indifference — feeling nothing — while lethargy includes drowsiness and a slowed body, not just cool feelings.
  • Inertia: similar because both describe resistance to motion or change; differs because inertia is native to physics and institutions (inertia of habit, of a committee), carrying no implication of sleep or forgetfulness.
  • Stupor: similar because both are states of diminished awareness; differs because stupor is far deeper — near-unconsciousness, medically alarming — whereas lethargy can be mild.
  • Somnolence: similar because both are clinical drowsiness; differs because somnolence is the neutral medical word for sleepiness, without the Greek depth or the moral rebuke of lethargy.
  • Indolence: similar because both imply an unwillingness to exert oneself; differs because indolence is a volitional habit of laziness or love of ease, while lethargy is involuntary or imposed.
  • Sluggishness: similar because both mean slow-moving; differs because sluggishness is plain, everyday, and physical (sluggish traffic, sluggish sales), lacking lethargy's implication of a dulled inner life.
  • Ennui: similar because both are states of listlessness; differs because ennui is an intellectual, fashionable disenchantment — a restlessness bred of too much leisure — whereas lethargy is a heaviness that leaves one helplessly still.
