• Dejected: Similar because both describe low spirits after a setback; differs because dejected stresses the downcast look and mood without implying that hope itself has been abandoned.
  • Depressed: Similar because both denote pervasive low mood; differs because depressed can be clinical or medical and need not involve the surrender of expectation that despondent carries.
  • Discouraged: Similar because both describe flagging spirit; differs because discouraged is milder and more temporary, sapping energy without extinguishing the will to try again.
  • Hopeless: Similar because both involve loss of expectation; differs because hopeless is absolute and states that no recovery seems possible, while despondent still allows someone who has merely stopped fighting for now.
  • Despairing: Similar because both approach the surrender of hope; differs because despairing is more intense and actively anguished, while despondent is quiet and resigned.
  • Dispirited: Similar because both describe loss of courage and spirit; differs because dispirited is lighter and usually temporary, while despondent implies deeper, longer resignation.
  • Forlorn: Similar because both convey abandonment and low spirits; differs because forlorn stresses loneliness and being forsaken, not specifically the loss of hope that despondent foregrounds.
  • Downcast: Similar because both describe low mood; differs because downcast is visual and transient (eyes lowered after a rebuff), lacking the psychological depth and persistence of despondent.
