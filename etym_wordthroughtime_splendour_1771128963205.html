- 15th-16th century - Enters English via Old French meaning brilliant brightness and radiant light; chiefly literal (sun, stars, jewels) with religious overtones of divine glory.
- 17th-18th century - Broadens from light to social grandeur: the pomp of courts, ceremonies, and rich display; connotes magnificent dignity befitting rank.
- 19th century - Romantic and imperial era: applied to palaces, nature, and exotic locales; increasingly tied to material wealth and theatrical, audience-demanding show.
- Contemporary - Signals striking visual grandeur and impressiveness; an elevated, formal register that carries awe but often a hint of performative excess.
