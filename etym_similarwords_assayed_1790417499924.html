• Tested: Similar because both involve examining something under specified conditions to evaluate its properties; differs because tested is a general, everyday term applied to machinery, hypotheses, or students, whereas assayed specifically connotes precise qualitative/quantitative chemical analysis or elevated metaphorical evaluation of intrinsic value.
  • Analyzed: Similar because both denote breaking down a whole into parts to determine composition; differs because analyzed is an intellectual and systemic breakdown without the specific historical resonance of metallurgical purity, trial by fire, or weighing value.
  • Evaluated: Similar because both involve determining worth, significance, or condition; differs because evaluated is an abstract, administrative, or cognitive appraisal, whereas assayed carries the visceral imagery of physical testing, refining, or chemical measurement.
  • Appraised: Similar because both involve estimating value or worth; differs because appraised focuses heavily on monetary, commercial, or market estimation (e.g., appraising real estate or jewelry), whereas assayed assesses intrinsic composition, purity, or moral content.
  • Vetted: Similar because both involve thorough examination for suitability or authenticity; differs because vetted specifically implies background checking, security clearance, or screening of candidates for potential disqualifications, rather than measuring compositional quality.
  • Attempted: Similar in the archaic/literary sense of trying to accomplish a goal; differs because attempted is modern and neutral, whereas assayed in this sense is archaic, poetic, and evokes the metaphor of weighing one's strength against a heavy task.
