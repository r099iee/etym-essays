PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*werh₁- / *wer-
"to speak, say, declare"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*werβom
"word, utterance"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
verbum
"a word, utterance, verb"
↓
Late / Medieval Latin (c. 14th–15th c.)
verbatim
"word for word"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1580)
verbatim
"in exactly the same words"
↓
English (modern)
verbatim
"word for word, in identical terms"

Note: Classical Latin expressed "word for word" using phrases like ad verbum or verbum e verbo. The adverb verbatim is a Late/Medieval Latin scholastic formation built on verbum using the distributive and modal adverbial suffix -atim (modeled after classical adverbs like literatim "letter by letter", gradatim "step by step", and nominatim "by name").
