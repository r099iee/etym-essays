Egyptian (Old Kingdom, c. 2600–2500 BCE)
hb (ḥīb)
“ibis — the sacred bird of Thoth”

↓
Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE, Herodotus)
ἶβις (îbis)
“ibis (the Egyptian wading bird)”

↓
Latin (1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
ībis
“ibis”

↓
Middle English (1398, Trevisa)
ibis
“ibis — a bird of Egypt”

↓
Modern English (16th c.–present)
ibises
“plural — more than one ibis”

↓
Modern English
IBISES

Note: Egyptian hb is a native Egyptian word of the sacred bird; no deeper Afro-Asiatic reconstruction is safe to state. The Greek and Latin forms are straightforward loans of it. The word entered Middle English as bookish natural history (the earliest English record is in John Trevisa’s 1398 translation of Bartholomew the Englishman), and the plural “ibises” is a regular English formation.
