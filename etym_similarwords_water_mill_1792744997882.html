• windmill: Similar because both are traditional mechanical mills harnessing renewable natural forces through rotating drive shafts to grind grain or pump water; differs because a windmill relies on unpredictable, fluctuating aerodynamic air currents striking rotating outdoor sails, whereas a water-mill harnesses the steady, gravity-driven kinetic energy of flowing river water.
  • tide mill: Similar because both utilize the power of water to turn machinery; differs because a tide mill captures coastal seawater during incoming high tides in an impounded basin and discharges it during low tide, operating intermittently on lunar tidal schedules rather than continuous freshwater river currents.
  • gristmill: Similar because both are agricultural processing facilities dedicated to grinding grain into meal and flour; differs because gristmill designates the facility strictly by its functional purpose (grinding grist), regardless of whether it is powered by water, wind, horses, steam, or electricity, whereas water-mill designates the installation specifically by its hydraulic power source.
  • sawmill: Similar because early lumber sawmills were frequently powered by hydraulic water wheels; differs because a sawmill uses its mechanical power to reciprocate or rotate sawblades for cutting timber into planks, whereas a generic water-mill historically denoted a grain-grinding flour mill unless specifically modified.
  • hydroelectric plant: Similar because both convert the potential and kinetic energy of moving water into useful physical work; differs because a hydroelectric plant channels high-pressure water through enclosed steel penstocks and modern impulse/reaction turbines to generate alternating electrical current for regional power grids, rather than utilizing an open wooden wheel to directly drive mechanical milling stones.
