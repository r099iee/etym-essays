• Besieged: Similar because both mean surrounded by hostile pressure; differs because besieged implies a defined attacker and a defined position under deliberate blockade, while beset is vaguer — pressures from all sides at once, often without a visible source.
  • Beleaguered: Similar because both name exhaustion under sustained pressure; differs because beleaguered still carries the imagery of a besieged camp, while beset is quieter and more inward, describing the mind ringed by pressures.
  • Assailed: Similar because both mean attacked or pressed upon; differs because assailed is more violent and sudden, a direct strike, whereas beset suggests persistence rather than impact.
  • Assaulted: Similar because both mean physically or verbally attacked; differs because assaulted is a sudden, forceful attack, while beset describes slow, encircling pressure from many sides.
  • Attacked: Similar because both mean set upon; differs because attacked is a general, active, often physical assault, while beset implies being ringed and worn down rather than struck.
  • Harassed: Similar because both mean repeatedly troubled; differs because harassed describes petty, repeated annoyances, often trivial, while beset describes heavier, encircling pressure.
  • Harried: Similar because both mean pressed hard; differs because harried implies an active pursuer chasing, while beset implies being already surrounded and static.
  • Haunted: Similar because both mean persistently troubled; differs because haunted implies recurrent supernatural or personal spectres, while beset is impersonal and diffuse — pressures without a face.
  • Plagued: Similar because both mean afflicted persistently; differs because plagued uses a disease metaphor (a plague of troubles), while beset uses the encirclement metaphor of a surrounding force.
  • Tormented: Similar because both mean made to suffer; differs because tormented implies active, deliberate infliction of pain, while beset is persistent pressure rather than deliberate cruelty.
  • Troubled: Similar because both mean affected by difficulties; differs because troubled is the general, mild word for disturbance, while beset adds the image of being hemmed in on every side.
  • Hemmed in: Similar because both mean surrounded; differs because hemmed in is purely spatial (boxed in, constrained), while beset adds hostile pressure and the wearing-down of persistence.
  • Surrounded: Similar because both mean ringed about; differs because surrounded is a neutral spatial fact, while beset implies that the surrounding forces are hostile or burdensome.
  • Encircled: Similar because both mean enclosed in a ring; differs because encircled is spatial and often neutral, while beset carries the military and psychological charge of being pressed upon.
  • Encompassed: Similar because both mean enclosed on all sides; differs because encompassed can mean merely contained or included, while beset always implies pressure and hostility.
  • Overtaken: Similar because both mean overwhelmed by circumstances; differs because overtaken suggests being caught up with by a moving force (night overtakes, misfortune overtakes), while beset suggests being already ringed and stationary.
  • Dogged: Similar because both mean persistently pursued or troubled; differs because dogged implies a follower at the heels (a problem dogs you), while beset implies surrounding from every side at once.
  • Pressed: Similar because both mean subjected to pressure; differs because pressed is general and often temporary, while beset names the sustained, many-sided encirclement by pressures.
