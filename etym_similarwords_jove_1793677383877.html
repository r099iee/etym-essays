• Jupiter: Similar because both refer to the exact same supreme Roman sky god and celestial sovereign; differs because Jupiter represents the direct nominative compound form (Iovis pater), functioning as the standard theological and astronomical designation, whereas Jove derives from the classical oblique stem (Iovis / Iove), favored in poetic verse, dramatic tragedy, and conversational exclamations.
  • Zeus: Similar because both represent the supreme sovereign, thunder-wielding patriarch of classical Greco-Roman mythology; differs because Zeus belongs strictly to the Hellenic cultural, religious, and literary tradition, whereas Jove embodies Roman civic ideology, military triumphs, and legal sovereignty (pax deorum).
  • Sky-god: Similar because both denote a supreme celestial monarch presiding over the heavens; differs because Sky-god is an abstract comparative anthropological category encompassing deities across various world religions, whereas Jove is a distinct historical Roman anthropomorphic divinity with a specific civic cult.
  • Thunderer: Similar because both evoke the divine entity who casts lightning bolts from the clouds; differs because Thunderer (Latin Tonans) is a descriptive cult epithet emphasizing the terrifying meteorological weapon of the god, while Jove encompasses the deity's complete multifaceted identity as father, judge, and cosmic protector.
  • Optimus Maximus: Similar because both invoke the chief Roman deity; differs because Optimus Maximus ("Best and Greatest") is the formal sacral and political cult title of Jupiter consecrated on Rome's Capitoline Hill, whereas Jove is an adaptable poetic, astrological, and vernacular name.
  • Deus: Similar because both stem from the common Proto-Indo-European root for light, day, and divine being (*dyeu-); differs because Deus is a generic common noun meaning "god" or the Christian "God," whereas Jove is a specific proper mythological name and celestial entity.
  • Jovian: Similar because both share the identical lexical root and mythological reference; differs because Jovian is an adjectival derivative applied in astronomy and astrophysics to the physical properties of the planet Jupiter (e.g., Jovian atmosphere, Jovian moons), while Jove is the proper noun denoting the sovereign celestial entity itself.
