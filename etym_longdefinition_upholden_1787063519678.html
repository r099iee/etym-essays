upholden is an archaic past participle of the verb to uphold (modern: upheld), meaning "upheld, supported, maintained, kept up, held aloft." It survives today almost exclusively in literary, poetic, biblical, and archaising contexts — precisely the register in which Tolkien places the dwarven song: "His crown shall be upholden."

Primary meaning: upheld / maintained.

  • As a passive participle, upholden means "kept up, sustained, supported": a crown held in place, a standard held aloft, an institution maintained.
  • It corresponds exactly to the modern upheld but uses the older strong-form participial ending -en (the pattern of holden, gotten, shaken), which the regularised form "upheld" has lost.
  • It almost always appears in passive or perfect constructions: "shall be upholden," "have upholden," rarely as an ordinary attributive adjective.

Word-formation.

  • A compound of the adverb/preposition up + the archaic past participle holden.
  • Middle English holden was the normal past participle of the verb "to hold": "I have holden," "he was holden of all" (= regarded by all).
  • The participial ending -en descends from the Old English strong-verb pattern (healden, past participle of healdan).

Usage history and register.

  • In the 16th–17th centuries upholden was an ordinary, if somewhat formal, participle, used in the Geneva Bible and by Shakespeare's contemporaries.
  • The King James Bible (1611) and the Book of Common Prayer systematically preserve -en participles (holden, gotten, shorn), giving them a liturgical halo.
  • From the 18th century the regular "upheld" took over in ordinary prose; upholden became a consciously archaic and poetic form.
  • Today it is used in hymnody, historical fiction, pastiche, and fantasy to evoke dignity and antiquity — Tolkien's use in the dwarves' song is a deliberate archaism for grandeur.

Semantic range.

  • Physical support: something held up so it does not fall ("the roof is upholden by beams").
  • Metaphorical support: honour, law, justice, or faith "upholden" = maintained and defended.
  • Ecclesiastical/political: a crown, throne, or kingdom "upholden" = sustained in power.
  • Abstract maintenance: rights, standards, and promises "upholden" = kept and honoured.

Grammar and learner notes.

  • Do not use upholden in everyday modern prose; use upheld.
  • Recognise it as a stylistic marker: encountering "upholden" signals elevated, archaic, or poetic diction.
  • It belongs to the dwindled family of -en participles: holden, gotten (US), shaken, forsaken, beholden (the last still current in the transferred sense "indebted, obliged").
  • In Tolkien's song the word is a prophecy: the lost king's crown "shall be upholden" — sustained in place — as his kingdom is restored.
