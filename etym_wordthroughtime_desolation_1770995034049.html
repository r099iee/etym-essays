- [14th-15th c.] - From Old French desolacion / Latin desolare (de- + solus "alone"); meant laying waste, the ruin of lands wrought by war or disaster.
- [16th-17th c. extension] - Broadened from physical ruin to emotional ruin: loneliness, abandonment, and the grief of being left utterly alone.
- [18th-19th c.] - The emotional sense strengthened; "desolation" increasingly named profound existential emptiness and hopelessness, often in religious and Romantic writing.
- [Contemporary Sense] - Still swings between devastated wasteland and overwhelming grief; in modern usage it leans toward intense emotional emptiness and abandonment.
