• bag: Similar because both are general containers carried to hold and transport personal items; differs because bag is the hypernym of unlimited shape, material, and carrying method, while satchel specifically denotes a single-strap, top-flap, small-to-medium carrying case.
  • backpack: Similar because both are load-bearing bags for carrying books and supplies on a journey; differs because a backpack is worn on the back with two shoulder straps and suggests hiking or sport, whereas a satchel hangs on one shoulder and reads urban, studious, or period-flavored.
  • rucksack: Similar because both are fabric bags carried by straps for outdoor loads; differs because rucksack (from German) is a large back-mounted pack for mountaineering, not a small flap-closed shoulder bag for books.
  • knapsack: Similar because both are small strap-carried bags for a traveler's supplies; differs because knapsack is typically worn on the back, round and rucksack-like, while a satchel is shoulder-hung with a flat body and top flap.
  • haversack: Similar because both are single-strap shoulder bags; differs because haversack is the military-outdoor canvas bag for rations and field gear, with no school or flap-convention.
  • briefcase: Similar because both are rectangular carriers for papers and documents; differs because a briefcase is rigid, hand-carried by a top handle, and professional-business in register, while a satchel is softer, shoulder-worn, and student or traveler tinted.
  • valise: Similar because both are pieces of small-to-medium luggage for travel; differs because a valise is a hand-carried, soft-sided suitcase, not worn over the shoulder in the schoolboy manner.
  • portmanteau: Similar because both are traveler's bags for clothing and belongings; differs because a portmanteau is a larger hinged hand luggage item, and the word also names a linguistic blend — neither sense matches the book-toting shoulder satchel.
  • messenger bag: Similar because both are single-strap, crossbody flap bags; differs because messenger bag is the modern urban-professional term (couriers, commuters), while satchel is the older, schoolroom-and-heritage word.
  • shoulder bag / handbag: Similar because both are bags carried over the shoulder or by hand for personal items; differs because these modern terms are broad and often feminine or zipped-closed, whereas a satchel is defined by its flap, strap, and book-carrying purpose.
  • pouch: Similar because both are small containers for personal goods; differs because a pouch is a soft, small, drawstring or pocket-like holder, far smaller and less load-bearing than a satchel.
  • pannier: Similar because both are strap-attached carriers for journey loads; differs because a pannier attaches in pairs to a saddle or bicycle and is not a personal shoulder bag.
