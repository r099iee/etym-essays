• Plodded: Similar because both describe slow, heavy, laborious walking; differs because plodded stresses the monotonous, rhythmic, flat-footed heaviness, while trudged emphasises weary effort across distance or rough ground.
  • Slogged: Similar because both describe hard, effortful walking through difficult conditions (mud, rain, snow); differs because slogged is more informal, harder, and often implies striking or splashing through obstacles, while trudged is the more neutral and literary term.
  • Tramped: Similar because both involve long, weary walking; differs because tramped implies a heavier, more pounding tread (and can mean to travel on foot generally), while trudged is specifically about slowness and fatigue.
  • Lumbered: Similar because both describe heavy, awkward movement; differs because lumbered suggests a ponderous, ungainly bulk (of a large body or machine), not necessarily fatigue, whereas trudged implies a tired person on foot.
  • Marched: Similar because both describe walking in a purposeful direction; differs because marched is brisk, disciplined, and vigorous, the exact opposite of the weary slowness of trudged.
  • Dragged: Similar because both imply moving with great effort; differs because dragged usually describes pulling something (or oneself) with difficulty and resistance, while trudged describes the walking itself.
