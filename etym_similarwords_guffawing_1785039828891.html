• Roar: Similar because both are loud, deep laughter; differs because a roar can be joyful or triumphant and does not necessarily imply coarseness, while a guffaw is specifically sudden, coarse, and unselfconscious.
  • Belly-laugh: Similar because both are deep, wholehearted laughter from the body; differs because a belly-laugh is genial and warm, while a guffaw is coarse, unrestrained, and often indifferent to others.
  • Cackle: Similar because both are loud, unrestrained laughter; differs because a cackle is shrill, harsh, broken, and hen-like, while a guffaw is deep, open, and belly-driven.
  • Chortle: Similar because both are distinctive laughter words; differs because a chortle is gleeful but half-suppressed and self-satisfied, while a guffaw bursts out openly, loudly, and coarsely.
  • Chuckle: Similar because both are forms of laughter; differs because a chuckle is low, soft, and inward, almost silent, while a guffaw is the loudest and most public of laughs.
  • Snicker / Snigger: Similar because both are forms of derisive laughter; differs because snickering is suppressed, secretive, and sneering, while a guffaw is open, coarse, and unashamed.
  • Laugh: Similar because both name vocal amusement; differs because laugh is the neutral, generic term for any expression of amusement, while guffaw names the specific subset that is sudden, loud, and coarse.
  • Snort: Similar because both are explosive, imitative sounds of amusement; differs because a snort is the brief explosive expulsion of air through the nose, while a guffaw is a prolonged, open-mouthed, belly-driven peal.
