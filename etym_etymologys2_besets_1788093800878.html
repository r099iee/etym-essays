PIE (c. 4500–3500 BCE)
*sed-
“to sit, be seated”
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – c. 100 CE)
*setjaną
“to set, cause to sit”
(causative of *sitjaną “to sit”)
↓
Old English (8th–9th c.)
settan
“to set, place, put, fix”
↓
Old English (c. 10th c.)
besettan
“to set round about, surround,”
“to occupy, garrison,”
“to cover, adorn”
(prefix be- “about, around” + settan)
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
besetten
“to surround, hem in, besiege,”
“to assail, press hard upon”
↓
Modern English (16th c. – present)
besets
“surrounds and assails on every side”
(3rd person sg. present of beset)

Note: The prefix is Old English be-, bi- “by, about, around”, from Proto-Germanic *bi. Beset is the old strong-verb pattern in miniature: the modern present, past and past participle are all identical (beset).
