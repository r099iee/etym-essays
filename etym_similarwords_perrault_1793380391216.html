• Boileau (Nicolas Boileau-Despréaux): Similar because both were towering literary giants of seventeenth-century France and influential members of the Académie Française; differs because Boileau was Perrault's chief ideological antagonist and fierce leader of the 'Ancients', insisting that classical Greece and Rome established the timeless, immutable laws of poetic excellence that modern writers could only hope to imitate.
  • Fontenelle (Bernard Le Bovier de Fontenelle): Similar because both were key intellectual allies in championing the Moderns during the Querelle, applying Cartesian rationalism to cultural debates; differs because Fontenelle was primarily a philosopher, scientist, and essayist (*Conversations on the Plurality of Worlds*) focused on astronomy and epistemology, whereas Perrault focused on literary criticism, courtly poetry, and folklore.
  • Zoilus: Similar because both are cited in literary criticism as detractors who attacked Homer and classical antiquity; differs because Zoilus attacked Homer out of personal, pedantic envy and cynical fault-finding, whereas Perrault acted as a sophisticated court intellectual and academician advocating a philosophical doctrine of cultural and historical progress.
  • Iconoclast: Similar because both challenge and seek to dismantle long-revered traditional canons and authorities; differs because an iconoclast often attacks institutions from the outside through radical, subversive destruction, whereas Perrault operated from the very heart of the royal establishment as an elite civil servant and royal academician.
  • Modernist: Similar because both assert the creative supremacy and autonomy of the present over the past; differs because modernist broadly designates the twentieth-century avant-garde artistic movements that fractured traditional forms, while Perrault represented the seventeenth-century neoclassical belief in polite, refined courtly progress and rational clarity.
  • Brothers Grimm (Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm): Similar because both collected and published foundational European fairy tales that shaped global childhood imagination (such as Cinderella and Sleeping Beauty); differs because the Brothers Grimm were nineteenth-century German Romantic philologists who sought to preserve authentic, earthy oral peasant traditions, whereas Perrault adapted folk tales into witty, moralistic literary prose designed for aristocratic Parisian salons.
  • Hans Christian Andersen: Similar because both are celebrated pioneers of literary fairy tales beloved across the world; differs because Andersen composed largely original, melancholic, and deeply personal Romantic tales, whereas Perrault adapted existing French and European folk motifs into structured, didactic courtly allegories.
