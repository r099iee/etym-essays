• Pincers: Similar because both denote grasping, two-jawed mechanical tools or crustacean claws; differs because pincers is the formal, standard term derived from French (pincer), whereas nippers has an Anglo-Germanic root emphasizing sharp cutting and snapping bites.
  • Claws: Similar because both refer to animal appendages used for grasping; differs because claw is a broader term encompassing the sharp curved nails of mammals and birds (e.g., eagle claws, tiger claws) as well as crustacean pincers.
  • Mandibles: Similar because both refer to biting insect mouthparts; differs because mandible is a formal, scientific anatomical term for jawbones in vertebrates and cutting mouthparts in arthropods.
  • Chelae: Similar because it is the exact scientific term for pincer claws in crustaceans and arachnids; differs because chelae is strictly technical Latinate biology, lacking any tool or colloquial slang senses.
  • Pliers: Similar because both are handheld levered gripping tools; differs because pliers typically feature flat, serrated gripping jaws designed for holding objects, whereas nippers feature sharp edged jaws designed for cutting or biting.
  • Youngsters: Similar because both refer to children in casual speech; differs because youngsters is a neutral, standard English collective noun, while nippers is colorful British working-class slang with naval roots.
  • Urchins: Similar because both denote lively, small street children; differs because urchin implies scruffiness, poverty, mischief, or homelessness.
