- Anglo-Saxon England - Mercia, from Old English Mierce (border people), named the kingdom of the English Midlands, and in the eighth century it was the most powerful of the Heptarchy, ruled by kings who styled themselves overlords of Britain.
- Danish conquest and decline - after the ninth-century Viking conquests Mercia lost its supremacy to Wessex, and the name passed from a living kingdom into a historical and ecclesiastical term.
- 18th-19th-century historiography - Macaulay used Mercia as the emblem of the unconverted, inward-looking England that still performed savage rites, a kingdom identified by its position on the border rather than by a people.
- Contemporary sense - Mercia survives in history books, in the name of the West Midlands police force, and in popular historical fiction as a byword for the Anglo-Saxon borderland kingdom facing Wales.
