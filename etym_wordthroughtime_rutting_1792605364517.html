- [16th–17th c. Early Modern English] - expanded into poetic descriptions of nature, pastoral literature, and Elizabethan drama (Shakespeare), increasingly applied as a vivid metaphor for coarse, animalistic human lust and unchecked carnal appetite - [perception: recognized as an earthy, evocative metaphor comparing wild human passion to beasts in heat]
- [18th–19th c. Enlightenment & Victorian Era] - formalized in natural history compendia, colonial wildlife journals (describing Indian elephants and African big game), and sporting literature - [perception: accepted as an objective biological term for ungulate reproductive physiology, while retaining an aura of untamed wildness]
- [20th c. Ethology & Wildlife Ecology] - established as the rigorous scientific designation for the seasonal reproductive cycle of male cervids and large mammals, differentiated biochemically from female estrus - [perception: firmly anchored in mammalian behavioral biology, endocrinology, and conservation science]
- [21st c. Contemporary Era] - retains its dual life: an essential, respected scientific term in wildlife ecology and nature documentaries, alongside a visceral, informal colloquialism for aggressive male libido and primal sexual swagger - [perception: evokes raw biological compulsion, untamed animal power, and visceral masculinity]
