Note: the second element is Proto-Germanic *mundō “hand, protection,” giving Old High German munt. The first element is usually hros “horse,” though some older sources derive it from *hrōþiz “fame.” The medieval Latin rosa munda “pure rose” and rosa mundi “rose of the world” are folk etymologies, not the historical derivation. The exact Old High German form of the compound is uncertain; the name is securely attested through the sixth-century Lombard queen in Latin sources.
