- [Antiquity to Early Middle Ages] — Developed from primitive goat-skin bags into large, wood-framed double-chambered bellows in Roman, Celtic, and Anglo-Saxon smithies — [perception: sacred instrument of the mythical smith (Hephaestus, Weyland), indispensable for smelting weapons, armor, and agricultural ironmongery]
- [High to Late Middle Ages] — Water-powered bellows introduced into Cistercian bloomeries and early blast furnaces, vastly scaling up iron production across Western Europe — [perception: monumental symbol of medieval hydraulic engineering and proto-industrial mastery over natural forces]
- [18th–19th c. Industrial Revolution] — Replaced in heavy industrial smelting works by steam-driven blowing engines and rotary cast-iron fans, remaining the beloved hallmark of rural village blacksmiths and farriers — [perception: nostalgic emblem of pastoral self-sufficiency, manual artisan skill, and quaint village heritage]
- [20th–21st c. High Fantasy & Living History] — Canonized in Tolkien's Shire and living-history historic sites as the quintessential moral benchmark of wholesome, human-scale, non-polluting machinery — [perception: idealized icon of pastoral craftsmanship held in stark moral opposition to dehumanizing, smokestack-driven mechanization]
