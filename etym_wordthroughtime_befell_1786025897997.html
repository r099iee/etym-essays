- 9th–11th c. (Old English *befeallan*) - Physical falling upon & deprivation: Denoted literally tumbling into, falling onto, or being suddenly bereft, stripped, or deprived of life or inheritance.
- 12th–14th c. (Middle English *bifallen*) - Narrative occurrence & impersonal fortune: Broadened into the classic chronicle formula "it bifel that..." ("it so happened that"), describing the turns of wheel of fortune.
- 15th–17th c. (Early Modern English *befall/befell*) - Tragic gravity & Shakespearean drama: Gravitated decisively toward ominous occurrences, dramatic downfalls, fatal curses, and royal tragedies in Elizabethan theater and the King James Bible.
- 18th–21st c. (Modern English) - High-literary & solemn register: Preserved almost exclusively in historical retellings, epic literature, and solemn inquiries concerning tragic fates and mysterious disappearances.
