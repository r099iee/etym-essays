• China: Similar because it also denotes fine porcelain ware, especially tableware; differs because it is a mass or collective term for the ware and its origin, less technical than porcelain.
  • Ceramics: Similar because it covers fired clay wares including porcelain; differs because it is the broader superordinate, including earthenware and stoneware that are not vitrified or translucent.
  • Stoneware: Similar because it is a high-fired, durable ceramic; differs because stoneware is opaque, coarser and non-translucent, while porcelain is glassy and translucent.
  • Earthenware: Similar because it is a fired clay pottery; differs because earthenware is low-fired, porous and opaque, the opposite of vitrified porcelain in prestige and technique.
  • Pottery: Similar because it denotes fired clay vessels generally; differs because pottery usually implies coarser, everyday or artisanal wares rather than fine translucent porcelain.
  • Crockery: Similar because it denotes table dishes collectively; differs because it is an everyday domestic term for plates and cups of any ceramic, without the luxury connotation.
  • Tableware: Similar because porcelain vessels are a kind of tableware; differs because tableware includes metal, glass and all ceramics, not specifically porcelain.
  • Delftware: Similar because it is a fine decorated tin-glazed earthenware often collected like porcelain; differs because it is not true porcelain, being opaque earthenware with tin enamel.
  • Bone china: Similar because it is a translucent white English porcelain-like body; differs because it contains bone ash and is technically a soft-paste variant, prized separately from hard-paste porcelain.
  • Celadon: Similar because it is a celebrated Chinese high-fired glazed ware; differs because celadon denotes a grey-green glaze family that may be stoneware or porcelain, defined by colour not body.
  • Figurines: Similar because many collected porcelains are small figures; differs because figurine denotes form, which may be in any material, while porcelain denotes material.
  • Curios: Similar because porcelains are often collected as curios; differs because curio denotes rarity and curiosity value generally, not ceramic identity.
