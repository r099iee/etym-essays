- 1630s - "canny" is a Scottish and northern English adjective formed from "can" (to know how) with -y, first recorded meaning "knowing, wise", built on the old Germanic sense of "can" as knowing how to do something.
- 18th century - it developed the practical northern senses "careful, skilful, clever, frugal, thrifty", and by the early 19th century, partly under the influence of Sir Walter Scott's novels, "cautious, wary, shrewd" — a canny businessman never risks more than he must.
- Modern English - in Scots the older, warmer senses survive: a canny place is safe and lucky, a canny person good-natured and safe to deal with, and the word's opposite, "uncanny", carries its whole history in reverse — from "not safe to deal with" to "weird, eerie".
