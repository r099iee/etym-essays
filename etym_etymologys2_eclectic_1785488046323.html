Linear derivation chain for ECLECTIC (single root word):

*leǵ- "to collect, to gather, to pick up; to speak, to tell" (Proto-Indo-European) → Greek légein "to gather, to collect; to count, to tell, to say" (the root shifted from gathering objects to gathering words) → Greek ek-légein "to pick out from a mass, to select" (ek- "out" + légein) → Greek ek-lektós "chosen, select" (verbal adjective, "picked out") → Greek ek-lektikós "selective, choosing from various systems" (adjective in -ikos; used of the Eclectic philosophers of the 2nd c. AD, e.g. in Diogenes Laertius and the medical writers) → French éclectique (18th c., learned borrowing in philosophy) → English eclectic (1683, borrowed from French and Neo-Latin) → ECLECTIC (the exact form of this card).

Note on the family: the same PIE root *leǵ- underlies the Latin verb legere "to gather, to choose, to read", giving English select, collect, elect, neglect, intellect, legion, legend, lecture, and legible — a separate but cognate branch of the same ancient root. There is no established Sanskrit or Hindi cognate of *leǵ- in the "collect, speak" sense, so the Indic branch is not part of this chain.
