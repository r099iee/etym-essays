- Early medieval chronicle tradition - Alboin enters written history through Paul the Deacon's History of the Lombards as the fierce war-leader who in 568 led his people across the Alps into Italy, and his ferocious reputation (the skull-cup legend) made him the archetype of the barbarian warlord.
- 18th-19th-century historiography - Macaulay and Gibbon folded him into the romantic roll-call of Germanic dynasty-founders, dignified as one of the chiefs who founded Teutonic dynasties and ranked among the zealous Christians, softening the old savagery into a vanished Teutonic world.
- Contemporary sense - Alboin remains a historical byword for the Lombard migration itself and for the barbarian founder-king, remembered less for conversion than for the descent into Italy that gave the Lombards their kingdom.
