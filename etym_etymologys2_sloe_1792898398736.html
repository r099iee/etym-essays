Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*slaihwō
‘sloe, blackthorn fruit’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
slā
‘sloe, blackthorn berry’
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
slo
‘sloe, wild plum’
↓
Modern English (current)
sloe
‘blackthorn fruit’

Note: Germanic *slaihwō is securely reconstructed from Old English slā, Old High German slēha, and modern German Schlehe, but no certain deeper Indo-European root is established: older links to colour words for blue or to Slavic sliva, plum, remain debated, and the Oxford English Dictionary leaves the remoter origin open rather than forcing a PIE ancestor.
