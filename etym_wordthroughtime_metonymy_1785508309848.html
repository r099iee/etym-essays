- [Greek rhetorical term (μετωνυμία)] - 'a change of name', a classical figure codified among the tropes by Greek grammarians and Roman rhetoricians; scholarly and technical from the start.
- [English borrowing (1547)] - enters English as rhetoric is rebuilt on classical foundations; a learned, ornamental figure used and named by poets and critics.
- [Cognitive-linguistic revaluation (20th c.)] - Lakoff and Johnson recast metonymy as a fundamental mechanism of everyday thought, not a literary ornament — the word's intellectual prestige rises sharply.
- [Modern analytical byword] - 'by powerful metonymy', 'the White House said' — metonymy now names the invisible machinery by which institutions, objects and places speak for people; precise, learned, never conversational.
