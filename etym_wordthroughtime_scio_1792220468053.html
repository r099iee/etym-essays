- Archaic & Classical Antiquity (c. 8th–4th c. BCE) — Known in Ancient Greek as Khíos (Χίος), celebrated as the legendary home of Homer's rhapsodes, a formidable naval power, and exporter of prized Chian wine.
- Medieval Era (1346–1566) — Governed by the Genoese mercantile charter company of the Giustiniani (the Maona); Italianized in Mediterranean navigation charts, bills of lading, and diplomatic treatises as Scio.
- Ottoman Era (1566–1822) — Enjoyed unique autonomous privileges and low taxation as an imperial Ottoman mastic monopoly, thriving as one of the wealthiest islands in the Mediterranean.
- April 1822 — The Massacre of Scio shocks Western civilization, transforming the Italianate name into an international symbol of martyred Greece and civilian atrocities.
- 1824 — Eugène Delacroix exhibits his landmark painting The Massacre of Scio at the Paris Salon, permanently inscribing Scio into the canon of French Romantic art and the Louvre.
- 20th–21st c. — Largely replaced in modern geopolitical and cartographic usage by the Greek transliteration Chios, while Scio remains the standard historical and art-historical designation for Delacroix's painting and the 1822 event.
