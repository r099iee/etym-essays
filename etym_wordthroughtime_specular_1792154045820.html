- 1st c. CE — Coined in Classical Latin as speculāris to designate mirrors and transparent crystalline gypsum (lapis speculāris) used for imperial Roman windowpanes and greenhouses.
- 16th–17th c. — Entered Early Modern English (Bacon, Donne, Milton) in literary, philosophical, and astrological prose to describe mirrors, looking-glasses, and far-seeing observation vantage points ("specular mounts").
- 18th–19th c. — Adopted into formal physics, mineralogy, and astronomical telescope manufacturing (specular hematite, speculum metal reflectors).
- 20th c. — Defined the cornerstone dichotomy of optical physics and photographic lighting: specular reflection (direct, coherent) versus diffuse reflection (scattered, Lambertian).
- Late 20th–21st c. — Found ubiquitous application in 3D computer graphics, CGI, video game engines, and physically based rendering (PBR) as specular maps and shader highlights.
