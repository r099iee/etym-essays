• Agitated: Similar because both describe a disturbed, uneasy state; differs because agitated is broader and stronger — general physical and emotional arousal — with no necessary implication of social pressure or the comic flutter of flustered.
  • Confused: Similar because both involve a disordered mind; differs because confused is purely cognitive disorientation, which can be calm or clinical, whereas flustered is hot, visible, short-term agitation caused by pressure.
  • Rattled: Similar because both mean knocked off balance; differs because rattled implies being shaken by a specific shock or threat, with a harder, more unsettling edge than flustered.
  • Flurried: Similar because both describe hasty, excited agitation and share a 'flurry/flutter' family resemblance; differs because flurried stresses the rush and swirl of activity around a person, while flustered is the inner ruffling of composure.
  • Unnerved: Similar because both mean a loss of confidence or composure; differs because unnerved is stronger and suggests being drained of courage, whereas flustered is a lighter, social, momentary confusion.
  • Disconcerted: Similar because both describe being thrown off balance; differs because disconcerted is more formal and implies a disturbing of calm or expectations, without the comic register of flustered.
  • Unsettled: Similar because both describe loss of composure; differs because unsettled is vaguer and more lasting — a general unease — not the immediate hot flutter of flustered.
  • Fazed: Similar because both mean disturbed or disconcerted; differs because fazed is usually negated ("not fazed") and implies being bothered by a difficulty, with a cooler register than flustered.
  • Ruffled: Similar because both mean a slight disturbance of calm; differs because ruffled stresses a light, surface disruption of composure (like ruffled feathers), gentler than the flustered state of hurried confusion.
  • Flummoxed: Similar because both describe being confused; differs because flummoxed is baffled, bewildered puzzlement at something hard to understand, with no necessary agitation or haste.
  • Befuddled: Similar because both describe a muddled state, historically including drink; differs because befuddled suggests dull, stupefied confusion, often from drink, whereas flustered is an active, hot, nervous flutter.
  • Discombobulated: Similar because both are colloquial words for confused agitation; differs because discombobulated is a humorous American coinage stressing total muddle, more extravagant than the ordinary flustered.
  • Panicked: Similar because both involve agitation under pressure; differs because panicked is a stronger, fear-driven state of near-loss of control, far more intense than the mild comic flutter of flustered.
  • Frazzled: Similar because both describe a worn, nervous state; differs because frazzled stresses exhaustion, burnout, and long strain, while flustered is a short-lived moment of ruffled haste.
  • In a tizzy / het up / worked up: Similar because these colloquial phrases all describe nervous agitation; differs because they are idioms emphasizing excitement or anger more than the specific blushing, fumbling confusion of flustered.
