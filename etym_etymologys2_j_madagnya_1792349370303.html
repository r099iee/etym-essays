Note on historical debate: Traditional Sanskrit grammarians (such as Pāṇini in the Aṣṭādhyāyī 4.1.99) analyze Jāmadagnya as a regular patronymic (*gotrāpatya*) formed from Jamadagni by applying the yañ affix with initial vowel strengthening (*vṛddhi*). Philologists debate the exact archaic verbal morphology of the base Jamad-: Yāska in the Nirukta (7.24) and classical commentators interpret jamat as an archaic participial form of jam- ("to eat, chew, consume"), hence "he whose sacrificial fire devours offerings voraciously." Others connect jam- to an archaic variant of gam- ("to go, move"), signifying "he who moves with fiery energy." Both interpretations converge upon the brilliant, devouring solar fire characteristic of the Bhārgava priestly clan.
