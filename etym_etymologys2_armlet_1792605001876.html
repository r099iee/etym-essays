Note on morphology and cross-linguistic parallels: The formation of armlet represents a quintessential Early Modern English native-Romance morphological hybrid. The lexical base is Germanic arm (descending from PIE *h₂er-, "to join"), combined with the Anglo-Norman double diminutive suffix -let (derived from Old French -el + -et, ultimately from Latin -ellus + -ittus). This morphological pattern was highly productive in Elizabethan and Jacobean English for coining terms for bodily ornaments and natural miniatures, yielding parallel formations like ringlet, wristlet, droplet, and streamlet. In parallel fashion, the secondary geographical meaning of armlet ("a small branch of water") arose through the diminutive compounding of the ancient metaphorical sense of an "arm of the sea," already attested in Old English.
