- 14th century (Middle English): Coined as *blod-hond* / *blodhound* to designate a specialized breed of large hound (the St. Hubert hound) bred for aristocratic hunting of deer and wild boar, prized for pure aristocratic pedigree ('hound of pure blood') and olfactory tracking.
- 15th to 16th century: Deployed extensively along the Scottish borders (as 'sleuth-hounds') and across feudal estates for hunting down thieves, poachers, and fugitives, cementing the word's association with criminal pursuit.
- 17th to 18th century: Transitioned into vivid literary and political metaphor to describe relentless persecutors, tyrannical debt collectors, and zealously aggressive bailiffs.
- 19th century: Solidified in Victorian detective fiction (e.g., Arthur Conan Doyle's Sherlock Holmes motifs) and American popular culture as the archetypal forensic tracker and indefatigable investigative symbol.
- 20th to 21st century: Flourished in journalism, corporate finance, and technology to denote relentless forensic accountants, investigative reporters, and aggressive tracking software.
