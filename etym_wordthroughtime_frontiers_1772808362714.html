- Military border of a state - From the 15th century frontiers names the fighting edge of a kingdom — the zone where armies stand and enemies threaten — rather than a peaceful surveyed line.
- Imperial and colonial expansion - In the 18th-19th centuries, especially in American English, the frontier becomes the advancing edge of settlement, the moving border between civilisation and 'empty' land.
- Metaphorical extension - In the 20th century frontier is transferred to knowledge and achievement ('the frontiers of science'), the farthest limit of human reach.
- Modern diplomatic contrast - Today frontiers survives in historical and military prose for imperial edges, while diplomacy prefers borders; the plural retains the sense of a ring of watched horizons.
