PIE *kh₂eyd- 'to cut, hew, strike' (reconstructed; zero-grade *kh₂id-, also written *kayd-) → Proto-Italic *kaid- 'to cut' → Latin caedō, caedere, cecīdī, caesum 'to cut, hew, strike, kill' (attested from the 3rd century BCE) → dē- 'down, away, off' + caedere = dēcīdere 'to cut off, cut down; (figurative) to settle, determine, decide' (1st century BCE; the cutting of a knot resolves a dispute) → dēcīsus 'settled, decided' (perfect passive participle) → Late Latin dēcīsīvus 'decisive' (4th century CE) → Middle French décisif 'decisive' (15th–16th century) → English decisive (c. 1610). Note on the debate: the PIE root is usually grouped with Sanskrit chid- 'to cut off, split' (chinátti 'he cuts off', Hindi chhed 'hole, cut'), supporting *kh₂eyd-; but some scholars assign the Sanskrit verb to *skeyd- 'to split' instead. The Germanic *hawwan- behind English hew has also been proposed as a cognate, but it may continue a distinct *kow- root, and the equation is debated.
