• Starving: Similar because both mean suffering extreme hunger; differs because starving is now the ordinary, blunt, everyday word for the same extremity, while famished is literary and formal, with a hint of old-fashioned epic gravity.
  • Hungry: Similar because both describe the desire for food; differs because hungry is the neutral, unmarked, everyday word for appetite of any intensity, while famished insists on an extreme degree - real or comic.
  • Ravenous: Similar because both describe intense hunger; differs because ravenous stresses furious, wolfish appetite and the desire to devour, while famished stresses the emptiness and want that hunger has caused.
  • Starved: Similar because both are past participles meaning reduced to hunger; differs because starved can also mean kept without food as a deliberate act (the prisoner was starved), while famished describes the state of the sufferer rather than the act of the starver.
  • Peckish: Similar because both describe hunger; differs because peckish is a mild, slightly fussy, informal hunger before a meal, the opposite of the extremity famished describes.
  • Voracious: Similar because both suggest a strong appetite; differs because voracious means having a greedy, insatiable capacity for eating, an appetite rather than a need, while famished is the condition of having too little food.
  • Insatiable: Similar because both describe appetites that cannot be satisfied; differs because insatiable is about appetite that is never filled, while famished is about want that has already emptied the body.
  • Underfed: Similar because both describe a body without enough food; differs because underfed is clinical and suggests a chronic, steady deficiency, while famished suggests an acute, extreme hunger.
  • Malnourished: Similar because both describe want of food; differs because malnourished is the medical word for a diet lacking proper nutrients, while famished is the everyday word for acute hunger.
  • Hollow: Similar because both can describe the emptiness hunger leaves; differs because hollow is chiefly a metaphor for the feeling (a hollow in the stomach), while famished names the actual condition of extreme hunger.
  • Empty: Similar because both describe the feeling of hunger; differs because empty is a general metaphor for the stomach's state and lacks the severity, history, and literary weight of famished.
