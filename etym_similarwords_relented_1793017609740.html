• Softened: Similar because both describe hardness giving way to gentleness; differs because softened is general and gradual, while relented marks a decisive yielding after stern resistance.
  • Yielded: Similar because both name giving way under pressure or pleading; differs because yielded stresses surrender of position, while relented stresses the melting of severity itself.
  • Eased: Similar because both describe pressure growing less; differs because eased can be slight and mechanical, while relented implies a willed turning from harshness to mercy.
  • Abated: Similar because both are said of storms, fevers, and furies growing weaker; differs because abated is impersonal and quantitative, while relented personifies, granting even weather a will.
  • Subsided: Similar because both describe waters, winds, and angers settling down; differs because subsided pictures sinking to a lower level, while relented pictures a heart changing.
  • Showed mercy: Similar because mercy is the fruit of relenting toward offenders; differs because showed mercy names the gracious act, while relented names the inner softening that permits it.
  • Forgave: Similar because forgiveness often follows relenting; differs because forgave cancels the offence itself, while relented only slackens the punishment or the pursuit.
  • Acquiesced: Similar because both end resistance to a plea or demand; differs because acquiesced is cool quiet consent, while relented is warm, the thaw after frost.
  • Mellowed: Similar because both describe harshness ripening into gentleness with time; differs because mellowed is slow seasonal ripening, while relented is a turning point, dated and felt.
