- [Military honorific] - "Augustus" begins as a Latin honorific meaning "venerable, majestic"; the Roman Senate confers it on Octavian in 27 BC, making it the first emperor's name.
- [Imperial title] - The word becomes the sacral senior title of emperors, ranking above "Caesar"; "Augustan" extends it to the age and its literature as a period of greatness.
- [Modern proper-name sense] - Today "Augustus" denotes the first emperor and, by extension, the Augustan age of peace and culture; the title's sacral force survives only historically.
