• Jumped: Similar because both describe sudden off-ground motion; differs because jumped is the everyday general verb, while sprang emphasizes elastic energy released from rest.
  • Leaped / leapt: Similar because both describe springing into the air; differs because leap is slightly more literary and general, while sprang stresses a sudden explosive burst.
  • Bounded: Similar because both involve vigorous forward motion; differs because bounded suggests repeated or graceful strides, while sprang is one sudden leap.
  • Bolted: Similar because both are sudden, fast motion; differs because bolted implies running away or an animal dash, not an upward leap from rest.
  • Burst: Similar because both express sudden forceful onset; differs because burst stresses breaking out of containment, while sprang stresses the jump itself.
  • Shot up: Similar because both describe very sudden upward motion; differs because shot up suggests projectile-like movement without the image of legs and springing.
  • Dashed: Similar because both are quick sudden movement; differs because dashed is horizontal running, not a leap into the air.
  • Started up: Similar because both describe rising suddenly, often with surprise; differs because started up may be a startled jerk without a full leap, while sprang is a real bound.
  • Rose: Similar because both mean moving upward; differs because rose can be slow, gradual, or formal, with none of the explosive energy of sprang.
  • Sprung: Similar because it is the same verb family; differs because in careful traditional usage sprung is the past participle ("has sprung"), while sprang is the simple past of a completed leap.
