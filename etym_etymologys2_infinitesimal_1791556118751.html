Proto-Indo-European (c. 4500 BCE)
*ne- + *dʰeygʷ-
"not" + "to stick, fasten, fix"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*fīgnis / *fīnis
"boundary mark, dividing post, border"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
finis
"boundary, limit, border, end"
↓
Classical Latin (1st c. BCE)
infinitus
"boundless, limitless, endless, infinite"
↓
New / Modern Latin (1668)
infinitesimus
"infinitely small, infinite-th"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1705–1710)
infinitesimal
"immeasurably small; approaching zero"

Note: Coined in mathematical Latin by German-Danish mathematician Nicholas Mercator in his Logarithmotechnia (1668) as infinitesimus, deliberately coined by blending infinitus ('infinite') with the ordinal numeral suffix -esimus (as in centesimus 'hundredth', millesimus 'thousandth') to literally denote the 'infinite-th' part. English added the adjectival suffix -al.
