Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*legh-
"to lie down, recline"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*lēgaz
"low, lying flat, short"
↓
Old Norse (c. 9th c.)
lágr
"low, short, humble"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
lah / low
"not high, humble in rank"
↓
Middle English (c. 1350)
lowelich / lowly
"humble, meek, modest in rank"
↓
Modern English (c. 16th c.)
lowly
"humble, modest; low in rank"

Note: Borrowed into Middle English from Old Norse lágr (displacing Old English niþer/nēah in this spatial sense), compounded with the native Germanic suffix -ly (OE -līc).
