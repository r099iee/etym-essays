• Homage: Similar because both involve formal, reverent acknowledgment of superior authority or feudal fealty; differs because homage emphasizes public allegiance, loyal honor, and honorary tribute, whereas obeisance emphasizes the physical bow, curtsy, or psychological posture of submissive deference.
  • Deference: Similar because both denote yielding respectfully to the judgment, rank, or wishes of another; differs because deference is an internal cognitive and courteous attitude of polite submission, whereas obeisance typically manifests as an overt physical act, formal prostration, or institutionalized subservience.
  • Genuflection: Similar because both refer to physical gestures of profound reverence; differs because genuflection specifically refers to bending one or both knees (often in a liturgical, religious context), whereas obeisance encompasses a broader range of bodily bows, inclining of the head, prostrations, or abstract obedience.
  • Kowtow: Similar because both involve submissive bodily gestures of submission before authority; differs because kowtow specifically derives from the Chinese practice of kneeling and touching the forehead to the ground, and in modern English strongly connotes groveling, servile, or obsequious subservience, while obeisance retains a dignity of formal or traditional protocol.
  • Reverence: Similar because both signify deep respect and awe toward high rank or sacredness; differs because reverence is a profound emotional state of veneration, while obeisance is the concrete external manifestation or institutional obligation of showing obedience.
  • Salaam: Similar because both denote a formal ceremonial greeting involving a deep physical bow; differs because salaam is rooted specifically in Islamic and Middle Eastern/South Asian cultural greeting practices with the palm to the forehead, whereas obeisance is general Anglo-Norman feudal/formal terminology.
  • Curtsy: Similar because both are physical gestures of deference to superiors; differs because curtsy is a gendered, specific bending of the knees with a lowering of the body by women/girls in formal etiquette, whereas obeisance is universal, formal, and often deeper.
  • Subservience: Similar because both describe an attitude of subordinating oneself to another; differs because subservience carries a negative, pejorative connotation of uncritical, slavish compliance and loss of individual agency, whereas obeisance can be an honorable civic or ceremonial observance.
