- 1530s - *Early Modern English & Scholastic Latin*: Borrowed via Middle French *incongruité* from Late Latin *incongruitas*, initially used in scholastic logic and grammar to designate ungrammatical phrasing, logical inconsistency, or impropriety in formal argumentation.
- 1600s–1700s - *Restoration & Enlightenment Drama*: Broadened into social critique and dramatic theory to describe mismatched behaviors, inappropriate manners, or unseemly violations of social decorum and aristocratic etiquette.
- Late 1700s–1800s - *German Idealism & Aesthetic Philosophy*: Formalized by Immanuel Kant and Arthur Schopenhauer into the 'Incongruity Theory' of humor, establishing incongruity as the foundational cognitive trigger of comedy, wit, and laughter.
- 20th c. - *Modernist & Surrealist Art*: Embraced affirmatively by avant-garde artistic movements (Dadaism, Surrealism, Absurdist theater) as a deliberate creative tool to disrupt bourgeois rationalism and unlock subconscious associations.
- Contemporary Era - *Cognitive Science & Linguistics*: Used to analyze schema violations, cognitive processing delays, semantic frame clashes, and humor generation algorithms in artificial intelligence.
