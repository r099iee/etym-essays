• Weary: Similar because both describe tiredness of body or spirit; differs because weary is honest fatigue that rest can cure, while jaded is dulled appetite that rest alone cannot restore.
  • World-weary: Similar because both name exhaustion born of too much experience; differs because world-weary stresses sorrowful wisdom about life, while jaded stresses blunted pleasure and boredom.
  • Cynical: Similar because both describe a loss of idealism after disappointment; differs because cynical is active distrust of motives, while jaded is passive inability to feel excitement.
  • Blasé: Similar because both mean unimpressed by things that thrill others; differs because blasé is fashionable French nonchalance, worn almost proudly, while jaded admits a genuine dulling one might wish to cure.
  • Bored: Similar because both describe flatness of feeling toward present stimuli; differs because bored is momentary and situational, while jaded is a settled condition built over long overexposure.
  • Sated: Similar because both follow too much of a good thing; differs because sated is neutral fullness after enough, while jaded is sour fullness after too much.
  • Burned out: Similar because both name emotional exhaustion from prolonged strain; differs because burned out is clinical collapse needing recovery, while jaded is milder, often aesthetic or social.
  • Disillusioned: Similar because both follow the loss of a rosy view; differs because disillusioned stresses shattered beliefs about something specific, while jaded stresses generalised dullness of response.
