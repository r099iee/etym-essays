• Impervious: Similar because both denote resistance to being affected; differs because impervious is the general near-synonym for being unable to be penetrated or affected (impervious to criticism), while unimpressionable specifically names resistance to emotional or intellectual impressions, to being shaped by experience.
  • Unemotional: Similar because both suggest a lack of strong feeling; differs because unemotional states the bare absence of emotion but says nothing about influence, whereas unimpressionable means not shaped by external impressions at all.
  • Impassive: Similar because both describe an unmoved outward bearing; differs because impassive describes an outward blankness that may conceal inner feeling, while the unimpressionable person is genuinely unaffected, not merely concealed.
  • Stoical: Similar because both suggest calm, unswayed endurance; differs because stoical names the virtue of calmly bearing suffering, presupposing pain that is endured, while the unimpressionable person may feel no suffering at all.
  • Callous: Similar because both suggest hardness of feeling; differs because callous adds a moral note of hardness toward others' distress, implying cruelty or neglect, which unimpressionable does not assert.
  • Insusceptible: Similar because both name the state of not being open to influence; differs because insusceptible is more mechanical and usually prepositional (insusceptible to suggestion), lacking the image of the mind that will not take a stamp.
  • Unmoved: Similar because both imply not being swayed; differs because unmoved is usually tied to a single occasion (unmoved by the plea), while unimpressionable names a permanent disposition.
  • Thick-skinned: Similar because both suggest immunity to emotional effect; differs because thick-skinned is colloquial and implies ordinary insults and criticism rolling off, while unimpressionable is formal and concerned with the shaping of character by impressions.
  • Hardened: Similar because both suggest a resistance that has been acquired; differs because hardened implies a hardening over time through exposure or experience, while unimpressionable is the privative absence of the capacity to be impressed.
  • Insensitive: Similar because both suggest a lack of responsiveness; differs because insensitive usually implies a failure of tact or empathy toward others, while unimpressionable is neutral and concerned with influence upon the self.
