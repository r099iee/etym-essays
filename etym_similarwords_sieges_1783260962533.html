• Blockade: Similar because both cut a place off from supply and communication; differs because blockade emphasizes stopping supplies and traffic (often by naval power) without necessarily involving storming, bombardment, or the reduction of the place.
  • Encirclement: Similar because both are a surrounding position of troops; differs because encirclement describes the tactical geometry of surrounding the enemy, whereas siege includes the full sustained programme of investment — mining, bombardment, sapping, and blockade.
  • Investment: Similar because both name the formal surrounding of a fortress; differs because investment is a specialized, largely archaic term of military science for the act of besieging, while siege is the broad, epic word everyone knows.
  • Assault: Similar because both are military operations against a fortress; differs because assault is the sudden, violent storming at the climax of operations, while siege is the prolonged period of investment, blockade, and attrition that precedes it.
  • Bombardment: Similar because both involve sustained attack on a defended place; differs because bombardment is the artillery pummelling itself and can occur without surrounding or cutting off, whereas siege is the full encircling operation of which bombardment is only one phase.
  • Besiegement: Similar because both name the act of besieging; differs because besiegement is the rarer abstract noun for the state or act of being besieged, whereas sieges are the concrete operations themselves.
  • Leaguer: Similar because both denote a military camp or blockade laid before a town; differs because leaguer is archaic (from Dutch leger "camp") for the siege-camp or the act of beleaguering, whereas siege is the standard modern word.
  • Quarantine (figurative): Similar because both isolate and cut a place or population off from the outside; differs because quarantine is a sanitary measure to prevent disease spread, not a hostile military reduction, though both rest on the image of enforced cutting-off.
