ZEALOUS — single chain. PIE *yes- 'to boil, seethe, foam' (traditional reconstruction, accepted by the major etymological dictionaries; see note below) -> Ancient Greek ζέω (zéō) 'to boil, seethe' (8th-5th c. BCE, Homer and classical usage) -> Ancient Greek ζῆλος (zēlos) 'ardour, eager rivalry, emulation, jealousy, zeal' (5th c. BCE onwards) -> Latin zēlus 'zeal, emulation, envy' (late Latin biblical usage; also zēlōsus 'full of zeal', Church Fathers, 4th-5th c. CE) -> Old French zel 'zeal' and zelos/gelos 'zealous, jealous' (12th-13th c.) -> Middle English zele 'zeal' (14th c., from Old French zel) and English zealous 'full of zeal' (16th c., formed from Late Latin zēlōsus + native suffix -ous) -> Modern English zealous. NOTE ON THE CHAIN: The Indo-European root is NOT entirely secure. The traditional textbook account derives zēlos from PIE *yes- 'to boil, seethe' through the Greek verb zéō, and this is the derivation adopted by the major etymological dictionaries. Modern scholarship (Robert Beekes and others) is more cautious, noting the deeper origin may be a Pre-Greek or substrate source; an old but unproven attempt links the same root with Sanskrit yáśas- 'glory, renown'. The chain from Greek zēlos onward, however, is fully attested.
