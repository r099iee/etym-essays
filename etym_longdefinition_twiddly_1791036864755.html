Twiddly is an informal, chiefly British adjective meaning fiddly, fussy and over-intricate in a small-scale way, or characterized by small fidgety, fiddling movements or ornament. It describes things that look or feel as if someone has been twiddling — turning, twisting, fiddling — at them: twiddly patterns, twiddly bits, twiddly details, or a twiddly gesture with foot or fingers. The tone is lightly dismissive, affectionate or comic rather than neutral.

The core image in the source context — "He did a sort of twiddly on the turf with his foot" — captures the kinetic sense: a small shuffling, pawing, scribbling movement on the ground, done from embarrassment, impatience or idleness. It is not a kick or stamp but a nervous rub, a noughts-and-crosses doodle with the toe. Extended to objects, the same fussiness becomes visual: a twiddly gate, hedge, handwriting, or melody is full of little curls, turns and unnecessary flourishes.

  • Fidgety / fiddling movement (primary in context): Small, nervous, shuffling manipulation. A twiddly movement of the fingers, a twiddly doodle with the foot. Implies embarrassment, boredom or indecision rather than purpose. The bridge from verb twiddle is direct: that which twiddles or results from twiddling.
  • Fussy, over-ornamented design: Covered in small, busy, finicky decoration. A twiddly Victorian fireplace, twiddly ironwork, twiddly embroidery. Not grandly ornate but busily pretty in a way that irritates modernist taste. Implies excess of small detail over clear form.
  • Intricate and hard to handle: Near-synonym of fiddly. A twiddly little knob, a twiddly form, twiddly instructions. Smallness plus awkwardness: needs patience, good eyesight, small fingers. Common in DIY, electronics, admin.
  • Musical ornament (informal): A twiddly bit or passage is a fast, showy, ornamental run — scales, trills, grace notes — often felt to be technically clever but musically empty. Twiddly guitar solos, twiddly Baroque embellishments. Rock and folk critics use it pejoratively against prog or shred.
  • Trivial, niggling: Extended sense: petty, piddling. Twiddly regulations, twiddly objections. The fuss is bureaucratic rather than visual, but the same impatience applies.

In use twiddly is always informal and evaluative. It praises dexterity only ironically; normally it complains: too fussy, too fiddly, too pleased with itself. A craftsperson might say apologetically "I know it's a bit twiddly" meaning overdone. A reviewer might write "annoyingly twiddly" meaning self-indulgent. Unlike intricate, which can admire complexity, or delicate, which admires fineness, twiddly withholds admiration. It suggests wasted effort on smallness.

Grammatically it is a regular gradable adjective: twiddlier, twiddliest, very twiddly, a bit twiddly. It collocates with bit, pattern, detail, doodle, movement, solo, knob, fiddly and often appears with sort of, a bit of, little hedging, as in the source. Register is conversational British; American English prefers fiddly, fussy, noodly. It is never formal, never technical, and often comic — Wodehouse uses it to make an embarrassed foot-shuffle sound both precise and absurd.
