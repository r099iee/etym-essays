Chain for root word caverns: PIE *ḱeuH- / *keu- "to swell; vault, hole, hollow" (reconstructed; no written attestation; c. 4500–3500 BCE) → Latin cavus "hollow, concave" (adjective; attested 2nd–1st c. BCE) → Latin cavāre "to hollow out, excavate" (verb; 1st c. BCE) → Latin caverna "a hollow place, cave, grotto" (noun; place-suffix -erna built on cavus; attested 1st c. BCE) → Old French caverne "cave, cavern" (by the 11th c. CE) → Middle English caverne / cavern (borrowed late 14th c. CE) → Modern English cavern, plural caverns (this card) Debated note: the deeper connection of the Latin cavus family to PIE is reconstructed as *ḱeuH- / *keu- with the paired senses "to swell" and "to bend, arch, hollow"; the association of convex swelling and concave hollow as two faces of curved space is widely repeated in handbooks (Pokorny) but not universally accepted — the root's precise shape and the direction of the swell/hollow split remain debated. English cave is a separate medieval borrowing from the same Latin family (via Old French cave), not an internal derivative of cavern.
