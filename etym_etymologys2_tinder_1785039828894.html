Single root word: TINDER. The chain below follows the Germanic lineage; the deeper pre-Germanic ancestry is genuinely unknown and is flagged at the top. Proto-Germanic *tundijaną "to kindle, set alight" (verb) / *tundrą "that which is kindled, tinder" (noun) — (reconstructed; ~2nd c. BCE–1st c. CE; no secure Indo-European etymon is attested for this family; a proposed link to PIE *dendh-/*dhundh- "to burn, blaze" is DISPUTED and unproven) → Old English tynder "tinder, kindling, flammable material for catching sparks" (noun; recorded from c. 9th–10th c.), alongside the verb tendan "to kindle, set on fire" → Middle English tendre / tyndre / tinder / tyndir "tinder, flammable material for catching sparks" (noun; 13th–14th c.; the -n- forms show assimilation of the earlier cluster, cf. the verb tenden/teend/tind "to set fire to") → Early Modern English tinder "material that readily takes fire from flint sparks" (15th–17th c.; tinder-box recorded 1520s, figurative "anything easily inflamed" 1590s) → Modern English TINDER (noun; also figurative "anything that inflames", and from 2012 the dating app Tinder). Note on debate: the Proto-Germanic *tund- root ("to kindle") has clear Germanic cognates — Gothic tandjan, Old Norse tenda/tendra, Swedish tända, German zünden — but NO certain PIE ancestor. Scholars have proposed PIE *dendh-/*dhundh- "to burn, blaze," but OED and Etymonline class the family as "of unknown origin"; the link must therefore be labeled conjectural rather than attested.
