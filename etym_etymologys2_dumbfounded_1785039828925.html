DUMBFOUNDED is a 17th-century blend of two independent root words, DUMB and CONFOUND. Each element has its own complete derivation chain, followed by the blend that unites them. CHAIN 1 — DUMB (the "mute" element): PIE root *dheu- (1) "dust, mist, vapour, smoke" (reconstructed; with the extended form *dheubh- "confusion, stupefaction, dizziness" — the image of a mind clouded as if by fog; the derivation is generally accepted but debated by some scholars) → Proto-Germanic *dumbaz (adj.) "dumb, dull, mute" (reconstructed) → Old English dumb (adj.) "mute, silent, unable to speak" (attested from the Anglo-Saxon period) → Middle English dumb (adj.) "speechless, unable to speak" (12th–15th c.) → Modern English dumb (adj.) "mute, silent" (the sense active when the compound was formed in 1650; the additional sense "stupid" dates from 1823 under the influence of German dumm) CHAIN 2 — CONFOUND (the "bewilder" element): PIE root *gheu- "to pour" (reconstructed; with the nasalised extended form *gheu-d- "to pour, spill") → Latin fundere (v.) "to pour, melt, cast" (attested in the classical period) → Latin confundere (v.) "to pour together, mingle, perplex" (com- "together" + fundere "to pour"; already figurative in classical Latin, "to throw the mind into disorder") → Old French confondre (v.) "to crush, ruin, disgrace, throw into disorder" (attested from the 11th century) → Middle English confounden (v.) "to overthrow, stupefy, confuse" (c. 1300) → Modern English confound (v.) "to perplex, disconcert, bewilder" (the element used in the 1650 blend) THE BLEND: dumb + confound → dumbfound (v.) "to strike dumb with confusion or perplexity" (1650s) → dumbfounded (adj.) "greatly astonished, struck speechless with surprise" (1680, past-participle form) → Modern English DUMBFOUNDED Note on debate: the derivation of Germanic *dumbaz from PIE *dheu- (1) "dust, mist" is widely accepted but not certain — the semantic link runs through "clouded, dulled, stupefied" to "mute," and some scholars treat *dumbaz as an independent Germanic formation with no secure PIE connection. The extended form *dheubh- is speculative. The sense "stupid" of dumb (1823) postdates the compound's formation and is irrelevant to dumbfound's original meaning. Latin confundere, Old French confondre, and Middle English confounden form a fully attested chain; the only uncertainty is the PIE level.
