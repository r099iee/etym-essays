- Late Latin lampreda (compound of lambere "to lick" + petra "stone") - Name given to the fish for clinging to river rocks.
- Old French lamproie (12th c.) - Delicacy of medieval French nobility and church elites.
- Middle English lamprey / lampreie (13th–14th c.) - High-status feast food; famously linked to King Henry I's fatal feast in 1135.
- Early Modern English - Traditional presentation in potted pies and royal presentation dishes (e.g. City of Gloucester lamprey pie sent to monarchs).
- 19th–20th Century - Zoological classification as primitive jawless fish; rise of metaphorical usage for parasitic human behaviors.
- Modern / Contemporary usage - Retained in historical literature, fantasy worldbuilding (Game of Thrones lamprey pie), and invasive species ecology.
