Thracian / Illyrian (Paleo-Balkan) personal name, reconstructed as *Belisari- — the first element *bel(i)- "white, bright" is connected with PIE *bʰel- "to shine, flash, be white" (cf. Baltic baltas/balts "white", Slavic *bělъ "white", Greek phalos "white, gleaming", Latin fulgeo "to flash"); the second element *sari- "spear" is connected with the Macedonian/Thracian sárissa "pike" (Greek σάρισσα, a non-Greek loanword). Both elements are reconstructions; the Paleo-Balkan languages are only fragmentarily attested (see debate note) ↓ Ancient Greek Βελισάριος (Belisários) — the name as borne by the general (c. 505–565 AD); attested in Procopius and other 6th-century Byzantine historians ↓ Latin Belisarius — the Latinised form used in Western historical writing ↓ Belisarius — the modern English historical/literary form ◄ TARGET DEBATED / NUANCED: Because the Thracian and Illyrian languages survive only in scattered glosses and inscriptions, the internal analysis of the name is genuinely uncertain. The compound reading "*beli- 'white' + *sari- 'spear'" is the most widely repeated account, but several scholars treat the name as effectively unanalysable given the sparse evidence, and no ancient source glosses its meaning. An older popular guess connecting the second element with a hypothetical "tsar/chief" has no evidential support and is generally abandoned. What is secure: the name is not Greek or Latin in origin, it is a native Balkan personal name, and it reached English in its Latinised form.
