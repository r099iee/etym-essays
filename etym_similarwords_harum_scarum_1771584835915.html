• Reckless: Similar because both describe a person heedless of danger and consequence; differs because reckless emphasises a dangerous lack of care for consequences as a sober, serious quality, whereas harum-scarum is lighter and flightier — a kinetic, comical dashing rather than a considered risk.
  • Impetuous: Similar because both describe acting headlong, without reflection; differs because impetuous stresses the rush of emotion into action — one acts impetuously without thinking — while harum-scarum pictures erratic, zigzag, hare-like motion and carries a comic, half-mocking register.
  • Hare-brained: Similar because both share the hare image and the judgemental humour, literally "with the brain of a hare"; differs because hare-brained stresses folly and fancifulness — a flighty, foolish, silly plan or person — while harum-scarum stresses reckless, headlong energy and can be used as noun, adjective, or adverb.
  • Tearaway: Similar because both name a wild, unruly young person; differs because tearaway is the modern British word for a delinquent or rowdy youth and is concrete and current, whereas harum-scarum is dated, literary, and comic — a bouncing, rhyming picture rather than a social label.
  • Scapegrace: Similar because both name a reckless, dissolute person with roguish charm; differs because scapegrace (literally "one who escapes grace") emphasises dissolute misbehaviour and roguishness without the hare-like motion or the comic jingle, and is chiefly a noun, while harum-scarum also runs as adjective and adverb.
  • Zany: Similar because both denote an eccentric, comically foolish person; differs because zany comes from the commedia dell'arte clown Zanni and stresses comical eccentricity and buffoonery, whereas harum-scarum stresses headlong, flighty recklessness and a whirling, kinetic movement.
  • Wild: Similar because both describe uncontrolled, unruly behaviour; differs because wild is a broad, neutral, fully standard word covering every kind of unrulliness, while harum-scarum is a specific, dated, rhyming compound that pictures a person who dashes about heedless of scares.
  • Helter-skelter: Similar because both are rhyming reduplications of disorderly, headlong motion; differs because helter-skelter is chiefly an adverb and adjective of chaotic haste (things "fall helter-skelter"), whereas harum-scarum is a noun for a person as well as an adjective and adverb, and adds the scare/hare imagery of panic.
  • Giddy: Similar because both convey flighty, unsteady, dizzy-headed behaviour; differs because giddy centres on dizziness and light-headed frivolity in the person himself, while harum-scarum centres on reckless outward motion and its comic effect on others.
  • Feckless: Similar because both suggest an inability to be trusted with responsibility; differs because feckless is a moral-psychological judgement of weakness, uselessness, and lack of initiative, wholly uncomic and static, whereas harum-scarum is dynamic, affectionate, and full of kinetic energy.
