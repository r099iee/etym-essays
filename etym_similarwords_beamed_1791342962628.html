• Grinned: Similar because it involves a wide, toothy smile; differs because grinning can often be mischievous, impish, wry, or self-conscious, whereas beaming is universally radiant, sunny, and devoid of cynical mockery.
  • Smiled: Similar because it expresses pleasure or amusement through facial expression; differs because smiling is the general, basic term which can be faint, polite, or reserved, whereas beaming is an expansive, glowing, high-intensity expression.
  • Radiated: Similar because both words draw on the metaphor of emitting rays of light or heat; differs because radiate can apply invisibly to abstract qualities (radiating confidence, radiating hostility) or physical radiation, whereas beaming specifically centers on the visual smile or light shafts.
  • Glowed: Similar because it suggests light and warmth emanating from the face; differs because glowing emphasizes soft, steady, diffuse warmth (often associated with health, modesty, or inner tranquility) rather than the outward, active projection of beaming.
  • Shone: Similar because it denotes brightness and luminosity; differs because shone is an intransitive state of illumination (her eyes shone) rather than the direct, deliberate communicative warmth of beaming upon an acquaintance.
  • Smirked: Similar because it is an intentional facial smile; differs completely because smirking is conceited, smug, contemptuous, or malicious, the exact moral opposite of genial beaming.
  • Simpered: Similar because it denotes an obvious smile; differs because simpering implies an affected, coy, silly, or ingratiating smile lacking genuine warmth.
  • Broadcast: Similar because it involves transmitting electronic media signals; differs because broadcasting disseminates signals in all directions to a general public, whereas beaming directs a focused transmission along a narrow azimuth toward a specific recipient.
  • Transmitted: Similar because it means sending data or electromagnetic energy across space; differs because transmitting is a sterile, technical engineering term without the visual or emotional connotations of beaming.
  • Lighted up (Lit up): Similar because it describes a sudden illumination of the face with joy; differs because lighting up highlights the momentary transition from neutral to joyful, while beaming describes the sustained radiant state.
  • Grinned from ear to ear: Similar because it describes maximum smile width; differs because it is an informal colloquial idiom, whereas beamed is a single, elegant literary verb.
  • Sparkled: Similar because it conveys joyful liveliness; differs because sparkling centers on fleeting, glittering flashes of light, especially in the eyes, rather than a broad, sunny facial smile.
