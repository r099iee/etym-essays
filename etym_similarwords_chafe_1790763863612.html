• Fret: Similar because both describe feeling restless, annoyed, or worn down; differs because 'fret' emphasizes continuous, anxious worry and inward distress, whereas 'chafe' emphasizes active impatience and indignation against an external restriction or friction.
  • Abrade: Similar because both mean to wear away a surface through physical friction; differs because 'abrade' is a technical, physical term for scraping or eroding surfaces without any psychological or thermal connotations.
  • Gall: Similar because both denote physical rubbing raw and emotional irritation; differs because 'gall' emphasizes deep, bitter resentment, humiliation, or bile-like vexation, whereas 'chafe' emphasizes restless agitation against constraint.
  • Bristle: Similar because both describe reacting with irritation or indignation to an external stimulus; differs because 'bristle' emphasizes an aggressive, defensive posturing (like an animal raising its fur/quills) in response to a perceived insult.
  • Irritate: Similar because both involve causing physical inflammation or mental annoyance; differs because 'irritate' is a broad, generalized term, whereas 'chafe' specifically evokes the sensation of continuous rubbing or restriction.
  • Rankle: Similar because both refer to lingering irritation; differs because 'rankle' describes a painful grievance or bitter memory that festers deeply over time like an infected wound.
  • Rub: Similar because both involve surface contact and movement; differs because 'rub' is a neutral physical action that does not necessarily cause damage, heat, wear, or emotional frustration.
