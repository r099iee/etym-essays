Derivation chain for LOGGIAS: 1. Proto-Indo-European *leubh- — “to peel off, strip off” (reconstructed; the base of a family of leaf- and foliage-words; the connection to the architecture-word runs through the notion of a shelter woven from leaves). 2. Proto-Germanic *laubą — “leaf, foliage” (reconstructed), and the derived *laubjō / *laubjōn — “a shelter made of foliage; an arbor; a covered porch” (reconstructed; cf. Old High German louba “porch, gallery, arbor”). 3. Frankish *laubja — “arbor, covered walkway, porch” (reconstructed; Frankish of the 5th–9th centuries, transmitted to northern Italian and Gallo-Romance speech). 4. Old Italian / northern Italian loia — “covered walkway, gallery, loggia” (attested c. 1300; the vulgar development of *laubja, with the sound changes *au- > *o- and *-b- > -g- / -i-). 5. Italian loggia — “covered gallery open on one side; loggia” (attested by the 14th–15th century, the form fixed in Renaissance architectural usage). 6. English loggia — “open-sided roofed gallery” (borrowed from Italian in the mid-18th century, c. 1755). 7. English plural loggias — “such galleries” (19th century onward). Final node: LOGGIAS. Note on the debate: the Germanic-to-Italian path is generally accepted (the word is a well-known Frankish loan in Italian), but the precise phonological development *laubja → loggia involves regular vulgar changes (diphthong smoothing and consonant voicing) that are reconstructed rather than directly attested at every step. The ultimate PIE link (*leubh- “to peel, strip”) is secure for the Germanic leaf-words but the connection to the architecture-word is indirect and depends on the reconstructed sense “arbor, shelter of leaves.”
