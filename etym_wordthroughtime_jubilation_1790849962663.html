- 14th c.: Entered Middle English from Old French *jubilation* and Late Latin *iubilatio*, used initially in liturgical and biblical contexts to describe holy rejoicing and praise.
- 15th c. to 17th c.: Broadened across Early Modern English to encompass military victories, royal coronations, and public civic holidays.
- 18th c. to 19th c.: Frequently used in Romantic literature and historical chronicles to portray mass public euphoria and revolutionary fervor.
- 20th c. to Present: Broadly applied across news media, sports reporting, psychology, and critical theory (e.g., Roland Barthes) to describe moments of triumphant release, communal celebration, and aesthetic bliss.
