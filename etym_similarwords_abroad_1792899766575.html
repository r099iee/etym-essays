• Overseas: Similar because both mean across the sea in foreign lands; differs because overseas stresses the sea-crossing itself and suits trade and deployment, while abroad is the broader everyday word for any foreign stay.
  • In foreign parts: Similar because both mean in countries other than one’s own; differs because in foreign parts is quaint and slightly comic, while abroad is neutral and current.
  • Travelling: Similar because both describe being away from home ground; differs because travelling names the motion itself, while abroad names the foreignness of the destination.
  • Away: Similar because both mark absence from home; differs because away is vague about distance and needs a complement, while abroad declares foreignness outright.
  • Out of the country: Similar because both state foreign absence plainly; differs because out of the country is administrative and flat, while abroad carries a breath of romance and adventure.
  • At large: Similar because both describe something loose in the wider world, as in rumour abroad; differs because at large suits escaped prisoners and general truths, while abroad keeps its traveller’s flavour.
  • Out and about: Similar because both cover being out in the world rather than indoors; differs because out and about is cheerful local bustle, while abroad in this sense is older and more literary.
  • Expatriate: Similar because both describe living outside one’s native land; differs because expatriate is a formal noun or adjective naming the person’s status, while abroad is an adverb naming where they are.
