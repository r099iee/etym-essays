Hebrew Citadel-Hill - Sion begins as Ṣiyyōn, the narrow southeastern ridge of Jerusalem, the Jebusite stronghold taken by David — a plain topographical name meaning "the dry height," suited to a fortress.
Biblical Sanctification - As the site of the Ark and the Temple, Zion climbs from "citadel-hill" to "sanctuary of God," and then by synecdoche to the whole holy city and the dwelling-place of the divine presence.
Scriptural and Poetic English - English Bibles prefer Zion, while the Vulgate-derived Sion survives in liturgy and in Milton's "Sion Hill," where it stands as the biblical, Judaeo-Christian rival to pagan Helicon as a source of inspiration.
Contemporary Sense - Today Sion is an archaic variant of Zion, used in poetic and liturgical contexts, while Zion carries the full symbolic load — sanctuary, Church, heavenly Jerusalem, and (in modern times) national homeland.
