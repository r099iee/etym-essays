Ancient Greek (c. 6th c. BCE)
βάρβαρος (bárbaros)
"foreign, strange, babbling"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
barbarus
"foreign, uncivilized, wild, savage"
[Crossed with Latin prāvus, 'crooked, depraved']
↓
Late Latin / Vulgar Latin (c. 5th–7th c. CE)
*bravus / brabus
"wild, fierce, daring, bold"
↓
Old Italian (c. 13th c.)
bravo
"brave, daring, bold; cutthroat"
↓
Renaissance Italian / Spanish (c. 16th c.)
bravata / bravada
"boast, threat, swaggering bluster"
↓
Elizabethan English Adaptation (c. 1580s–1590s)
bravado
"boastful threat, swaggering display"
[Anglicized with faux-Spanish -ado suffix]
↓
Modern English (present)
bravado
"swaggering display of false or boastful courage"
