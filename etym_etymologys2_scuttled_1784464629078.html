Middle English (15th c.)
scutlen
"to run with quick,
hasty steps"
(probable frequentative

uncertain)

↓
English (16th c.)
scuttle
"to run with short,
quick steps; scurry"

↓
English (17th c.)
scuttled
"past tense of scuttle;
ran off hurriedly"

Debate note: The origin of scutlen is unknown: it is probably a frequentative of an earlier scut "to dart, run" and may be related to scud (also of obscure origin). This word is distinct from scuttle "to sink a ship by cutting a hole", which comes from Old French escoutille "hatchway".
