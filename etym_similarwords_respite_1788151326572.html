• Rest: Similar because both name the cessation of effort; differs in that rest is a positive recharge (sleep, repose), while respite is a negative relief — it is defined by the burden it interrupts.
  • Break: Similar as an informal pause in work or activity; differs in register (everyday speech) and in lacking the overtones of mercy, strain, or menace that respite carries.
  • Reprieve: Similar because both mean the temporary suspension of a doom, penalty, or sentence; differs in that reprieve is almost exclusively legal and doom-related, while respite covers every kind of relief from least to greatest.
  • Relief: Similar because both describe the easing of a burden; differs in that relief names the felt easing itself (permanent or temporary) or aid given, while respite names the interval during which the burden pauses.
  • Lull: Similar as a temporary quiet interval in noise, wind, or activity; differs in being limited to a trailing-off of force or motion (a lull in the battle), while respite can be actively granted or taken.
  • Breather: Similar as an informal short pause for recovery; differs sharply in register (colloquial, bodily) and in being typically brief and voluntary, with no hint of danger or justice.
  • Intermission / recess: Similar as scheduled pauses in a play, court session, or school day; differ by being institutional and fixed in length, while respite is open-ended and motivated by need.
  • Truce / armistice: Similar because both suspend hostilities and give fighters a respite; differ in being mutual, negotiated, and formal agreements between combatants, while respite may be unilateral and applies to weather and illness as much as war.
  • Postponement / deferral: Similar as the legal or practical putting-off of a payment, judgment, or event; differ in being neutral scheduling terms with none of the compassionate, relief-bearing colour of respite.
  • Moratorium: Similar as a formal suspension of an activity or obligation; differs in scale (institutional, often statutory) and in lacking the personal, bodily dimension of a respite.
  • Holiday: Similar in the older English sense of a holy day or authorised pause from labour; differs in that holiday is now festive and secular, while respite remains serious and relief-oriented.
