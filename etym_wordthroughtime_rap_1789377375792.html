- 14th Century (Late Middle English) - Emerges as an onomatopoeic echoic verb (*rappen*) denoting a quick, smart blow or percussive strike, closely akin to Scandinavian and Low German forms.
- 16th to 17th Century (Early Modern English) - Expands from physical impact into speech acts, meaning to utter suddenly or vehemently ("to rap out an oath"); established for door knocking in domestic architecture.
- 18th Century (Augustan Era) - Applied in Ireland to debased, counterfeit copper halfpence, establishing the popular idioms "not worth a rap" and "I don't care a rap."
- 19th Century (Victorian Era) - Becomes famous in educational corporal punishment ("a rap on the knuckles") and in spiritualist séances where ghosts supposedly communicated via mysterious "raps" on wooden tables.
- Mid-20th Century - Enters American underworld slang as a criminal charge or prison sentence ("take the rap", "rap sheet"); concurrently evolves in Black urban vernacular to mean fluent, persuasive conversation.
- Late 20th to 21st Century - Revolutionized with the global rise of hip-hop music; "rap" becomes the dominant worldwide musical and poetic art form, celebrating rhythmic rhymed lyricism over backing beats.
