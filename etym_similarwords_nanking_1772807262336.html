• Nanjing: Similar because both name exactly the same city; differs because Nanjing is the standard modern pinyin spelling, while Nanking is the older Postal Map romanization used in classic translations and period prose.
  • Peking / Beijing: Similar because both are major Chinese capital-city names in English with parallel romanization histories; differs because they name the 'northern capital', the contrasting pole to Nanking's southern seat.
  • Jiangnan: Similar because both refer to the southern Chinese region; differs because Jiangnan names the broad 'south of the Yangtze' cultural area, not the single city and its proper-name pedigree.
  • The southern capital: Similar because both gloss the Chinese morphemes 南 'south' and 京 'capital'; differs because that phrase is a transparent translation that loses the proper-name force and period flavour of 'Nanking'.
  • Nankeen: Similar because both derive from the same place-name and share the 'Nank-' core; differs because nankeen names the buff-coloured cotton cloth traded from the city, a product label, not the city itself.
  • Canton: Similar because it is another major Chinese city known in English by a non-pinyin romanization; differs because Canton (Guangzhou) is a different city, and its English form follows a different romanization tradition.
