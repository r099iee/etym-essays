- Early 1600s anatomical adjective - neutral learned description meaning two-footed, used by physicians and naturalists for birds and men
- Late 1600s–1700s natural-history noun - classificatory label contrasting bipeds with quadrupeds and multipeds in systems of animals
- 19th century evolutionary noun - charged with new meaning after Darwin: biped becomes shorthand for man’s animal place and upright destiny, both proud and humbling
- Early 20th century palaeoanthropological term - technical celebration of bipedalism as the hallmark of hominins, tied to footprints, pelvises, and upright prestige
- Mid-20th century satirical / misanthropic use - ironic reduction of man to “the marauding biped,” “featherless biped,” voicing ecological guilt and anti-humanism
- Late 20th–21st century robotics use - neutral-optimistic extension to two-legged machines, where biped means an engineering achievement rather than a biological insult
