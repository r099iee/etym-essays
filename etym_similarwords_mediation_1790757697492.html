• Arbitration: Similar because both are alternative dispute resolution methods involving a neutral third party; differs because an arbitrator acts like a judge with the power to issue a legally binding decision (an arbitral award), whereas a mediator merely facilitates negotiation and cannot force a settlement.
  • Intercession: Similar because both involve intervening on behalf of another; differs because intercession typically carries a strong religious, spiritual, or deferential tone (e.g., praying for someone or asking a superior for mercy on someone's behalf), whereas mediation is an impartial, structural, or bilateral process.
  • Intervention: Similar because both involve stepping into an ongoing situation; differs because intervention can be unilateral, coercive, forceful, or uninvited (e.g., military intervention), while mediation requires neutrality and usually the mutual consent of the parties.
  • Reconciliation: Similar because both aim to resolve discord; differs because reconciliation is the ultimate state of emotional harmony and restored relationship, whereas mediation is the procedural mechanism or technique used to pursue that resolution.
  • Negotiation: Similar because both involve discussions aimed at reaching an agreement; differs because direct negotiation occurs solely between the disputing parties themselves without an outside neutral third party, whereas mediation explicitly introduces an intermediary.
  • Liaison: Similar because both involve communication between distinct entities; differs because a liaison is a regular conduit, coordinator, or representative ensuring ongoing communication between allied departments, lacking the conflict-resolution focus of mediation.
