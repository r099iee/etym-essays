- 8th–6th c. BCE — Attested in archaic epic cycles (such as the lost Thebaid) as Oedipus's devoted daughter, maintaining ancestral piety within the cursed Labdacid line.
- 441 BCE — Immortalized in classical Athens by Sophocles' tragedy Antigone, shifting perception forever into the supreme archetype of civil disobedience, divine unwritten law, and moral conscience against tyranny.
- Hellenistic & Roman Era — Reinterpreted in Roman drama (Seneca's Phoenissae) and Stoic ethics as a model of unyielding fortitude amidst cosmic and political catastrophe.
- 19th c. — Elevated by German Idealism (especially G.W.F. Hegel) into the pinnacle of dramatic art, representing the dialectical collision between familial duty and civic law.
- Mid-20th c. — Adopted worldwide as an explicit anti-fascist, anti-authoritarian protest symbol, notably through Jean Anouilh's 1944 play in Nazi-occupied Paris and Bertolt Brecht's 1948 adaptation.
- Contemporary Era — Extensively engaged in feminist philosophy, legal jurisprudence, and human rights theory as the foundational voice of individual conscience confronting state violence.
