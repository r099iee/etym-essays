Latin area —
level open space, open ground; threshing floor
(1st century BCE; origin uncertain — no secure PIE etymon, cf. de Vaan)

↓

Old French aire —
open space, threshing floor;
(falconry) the nest or lair of a bird of prey
(medieval)

↓

Anglo-French aire / eyre —
eagle’s nest
(falconry term imported into English)

↓

Middle English eyry / aire / ayrie —
the nest of a bird of prey, especially an eagle
(15th century)

↓

English eyrie —
nest of a bird of prey; lofty, isolated perch
(19th-century respelling, folk-associated with air and aerial; American form aerie)

↓

EYRIE —
the present word

Notes on debate: Latin area has no secure Indo-European etymology; a traditional link to the verb arēre “to be dry” (the threshing floor as the dried, beaten ground) is often repeated but remains debated. The word is NOT related to Latin aer / Greek aēr “air”; the “airy” flavour is a 19th-century respelling, not etymology.
