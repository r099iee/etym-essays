• Aquilegia: Similar because both refer to the exact same genus of flowering plants in the family Ranunculaceae; differs because aquilegia is the formal Latin scientific and taxonomic genus name (derived from *aquila*, "eagle", referring to the talon-like curved petal spurs), whereas columbine is the beloved English common and literary vernacular name (derived from *columba*, "dove").
  • Granny's Bonnet: Similar because both are traditional English folk names for the common European wildflower *Aquilegia vulgaris*; differs because granny's bonnet is an informal, rustic dialectal name specifically describing the bell-like, hooded shape of the flower's ruffled sepals, whereas columbine is the standard literary and botanical designation carrying classical theatrical and artistic associations.
  • Harebell: Similar because both are delicate, nodding, blue-flowered perennials celebrated in British woodland folklore and cottage gardens; differs because the harebell (*Campanula rotundifolia*, the bluebell of Scotland) belongs to the bellflower family (Campanulaceae) and bears simple, papery, bell-shaped blossoms without spurs, whereas columbine belongs to Ranunculaceae and has complex five-spurred floral architecture.
  • Delphinium: Similar because both are towering, elegant perennials of the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae) that bear spurred blossoms pollinated by bumblebees; differs because delphinium flowers are arranged in dense, vertical, spike-like racemes with a single backward-pointing spur (resembling a dolphin), whereas columbines feature solitary, nodding blossoms with five distinct curving spurs.
  • Monkshood: Similar because both are hardy herbaceous perennials of the family Ranunculaceae with distinctively modified floral structures; differs because monkshood (*Aconitum*) produces a large, helmet-like upper sepal (resembling a monastic cowl) and contains deadly neurotoxins (aconitine), whereas columbine features open, dove-like spurs and is non-lethal in garden settings.
  • Bleeding-heart: Similar because both are old-fashioned, shade-tolerant cottage garden perennials with uniquely shaped, nodding blossoms; differs because bleeding-heart (*Lamprocapnos spectabilis*, formerly *Dicentra*) belongs to the poppy family (Papaveraceae) and bears horizontal sprays of distinctively flattened, heart-shaped pink flowers from which a white "drop" dangles.
