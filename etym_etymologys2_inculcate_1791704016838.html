Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*kóls- / *kel-
"heel, hind foot"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*kalk-
"heel"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
calx (gen. calcis)
"the human heel"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 2nd c. BCE)
calcare
"to tread upon, trample with the heel,"
"press down by walking"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
inculcare
"to tread into, stamp down with the heel;"
"press upon, force into the mind"
↓
Classical Latin (participle) (c. 1st c. BCE)
inculcātus
"trodden in, driven in forcefully"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1530–1540)
inculcate
"to instill firmly by persistent repetition"

Note: Latin calx ('heel') is distinct from the homonym calx ('limestone, chalk', source of calcium and calculate). The compound inculcare displays regular Latin vowel weakening (apophony) where short root -a- in calcare shifts to short -u- before a dark l followed by a consonant (-lc-).
