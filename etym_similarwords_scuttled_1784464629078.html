• Scampered: Similar because it is the nearest synonym — quick, light, short-stepped running, often of small animals and children; differs because scampered is lighter and more playful or skittish, whereas scuttled is busier and slightly more anxious or undignified.
  • Scurried: Similar because it names the same hasty, small-footed movement, especially of mice and insects; differs because scurried stresses a busy, mousy, almost furtive bustle, while scuttled often carries a faint comic or animal connotation and slightly longer sweeping steps.
  • Dashed: Similar because both are quick movements of departure; differs because dashed is faster and more headlong, with no implication of short steps or low posture.
  • Darted: Similar because both are sudden, quick movements; differs because darted implies one abrupt lunge or a weaving series of swift turns, not a continuous run of small steps.
  • Hurried: Similar because both involve moving quickly; differs because hurried is the general word with no information about step length, posture, or undignified quality.
  • Bustled: Similar because both suggest busy, purposeful little movements; differs because bustled implies energetic, officious activity and often a swishing of garments, whereas scuttled is more about quick, low, hasty travel.
  • Scooted: Similar because both are informal quick movements; differs because scooted suggests a quick sideways or sliding motion (as of a chair or a small body), lighter and more casual than the anxious haste of scuttled.
  • Trotted: Similar because both are steady small-step paces; differs because trotted is a rhythmic four-beat gait of animals or an even human jog, without the urgency or disorder of scuttled.
  • Skittered: Similar because both describe light, quick, uneven movement, often of insects or small animals across a surface; differs because skittered emphasizes an erratic, skimming, flickering motion rather than a directed short-step run.
  • Bolted: Similar because both are rapid departures; differs because bolted is a violent, full-speed flight, far more forceful and panicked than the modest haste of scuttled.
  • Sprinted: Similar because both are running actions; differs because sprinted is a long-strided, athletic, top-speed run, the precise opposite of short, low, undignified steps.
  • Rushed: Similar because both are hurried movement; differs because rushed stresses urgency and speed without the specific short-step, low-to-the-ground texture.
  • Fled: Similar because both can describe retreat; differs because fled implies escape from danger, while scuttled is often social or comic retreat.
  • Hightailed it: Similar because both are informal rapid departures; differs because hightailed it is American slang for a fast getaway, far more emphatic than scuttled.
