• Herons: Similar because both are large, long-legged, long-necked wading birds of wetlands; differs because herons have slender dagger bills, fly with necks folded in an S-curve, and are primarily fish-eaters, while storks have heavy bills, fly with necks extended, and take a far broader diet.
  • Cranes: Similar because both are tall, long-necked, long-legged birds, graceful in flight with extended necks; differs because cranes belong to a different family, are grassland birds famous for dancing and trumpeting, and flock in V-formation, while storks are waders of wetlands and marshes.
  • Ibis: Similar because both are wading birds of the order Pelecaniformes; differs because ibises are smaller, colonial, and unmistakable for their long, down-curved bills, whereas storks have long, straight, heavy bills.
  • Flamingoes: Similar because both are large, long-necked, long-legged birds of shallow water; differs because flamingoes are pink, filter-feed on shrimp with hooked bills, and stand in vast flocks, while storks are white, black, or grey, feed on prey, and walk stiffly.
  • Eagles: Similar because both are large birds that soar at height and nest high (on cliffs, crags, and towers); differs because eagles are birds of prey with hooked beaks and talons that hunt live quarry, while storks are waders that feed in wetlands and carry no raptorial force - the very confusion that makes Bilbo's slip funny.
  • Swans: Similar because both are large white birds associated with nesting near human settlements and with myth and folklore; differs because swans are waterfowl with webbed feet and long curved necks that swim, while storks are wading birds that walk stiffly and nest on roofs and chimneys.
  • Pelicans: Similar because both are large white birds of the order Pelecaniformes with heavy bills; differs because pelicans are aquatic birds with a huge throat pouch used for scooping fish, while storks lack the pouch and wade rather than swim.
  • Kites: Similar because both are large soaring birds of the heights seen circling over fields; differs because kites are raptors with forked tails that hunt or scavenge, while storks are waders that only pass over land between feeding grounds.
  • Cormorants: Similar because both are large dark birds of the water's edge; differs because cormorants are diving, fish-catching swimmers that sit low in the water, while storks wade, rarely swim, and nest on elevated structures.
  • Starlings: Similar because both are birds (homophone-adjacent names, easy to confuse in a slip); differs because starlings are small, dark, iridescent songbirds that flock in murmurations, while storks are huge, long-legged wading birds - the slip between them would never happen in careful speech.
