PIE *peth₂- "to spread out, extend, be wide" (reconstructed) → Greek petannymi (πετάννυμι) "I spread out, unfold, open" (root present, attested in Greek from Homer onward) → Greek patane (πατάνη) "a flat dish, plate, platter" (a thing spread flat; the exact pre-form is debated, but the Greek dish-word is the traditional source) → Latin patina "a broad shallow pan or dish, stew-pan" (attested in classical Latin) → Italian patina "shallow dish" (inherited) → "the film or tarnish formed on a metal or earthenware vessel" (semantic shift recorded by the 16th c.) → English patina "the green film on bronze" (borrowed from Italian, recorded 1765; reinforced by French patine in the 18th–19th c.) → English PATINA "a film on the surface of old bronze or copper; a surface quality acquired through age" (modern) DEBATED / NUANCED STAGES: (1) The Greek intermediate patane is the traditional link between the spreading root and Latin patina, but the exact Greek form and derivation are not fully certain; the semantic bridge is "that which is spread flat" → "flat dish". (2) The pivotal Italian shift from "shallow dish" to "film/tarnish on a vessel" is the key event; its precise route (whether through the glaze on earthenware or the oxide on metal) is debated. (3) ANTI-MERGE GUARD: patina must not be connected to PIE *pet- "to fly" (the root of English feather, pen, and Greek petein "to fly"); that root is distinct. (4) Greek petalon "leaf, thin plate" (English petal) and Latin pandere / patere "to spread" (English expand, patent) are collateral descendants of *peth₂-, not ancestors of patina.
