• Hill: Similar because both name raised ground. Differs because a hill is larger, natural and often named; a mound is smaller, often artificial, and usually unnamed.
  • Hillock: Similar because both name a small hill. Differs because hillock is purely natural and neutral; mound may be artificial and carries the heap/burial/earthwork associations.
  • Knoll: Similar because both name a small rounded rise. Differs because knoll is a gentle natural hilltop, pleasant and neutral; mound implies heaped material, often man-made.
  • Hummock: Similar because both name a small rounded elevation. Differs because hummock is used of soft or boggy ground (and of ice); mound is of heaped earth or any piled matter.
  • Heap / Pile: Similar because both name material accumulated in a mass ("a mound of gold" ≈ "a heap of gold"). Differs because heap/pile are generic accumulations of anything; mound implies a rounded, often earth-like or intentional mass, and carries topographical weight.
  • Bank: Similar because both name raised earth ("a bank of earth", "a grassy bank"). Differs because bank is long, low and linear — a raised edge or slope; mound is a rounded, more compact mass.
  • Embankment: Similar because both name an artificial bank of earth. Differs because embankment is an engineering term for a long raised roadway or dyke; mound is general and rounder.
  • Barrow: Similar because both name a raised burial mound. Differs because barrow is the technical-archaeological word specifically for a grave-mound; mound is general and may have no burial at all.
  • Tumulus: Similar because both name an ancient burial mound. Differs because tumulus is the formal archaeological, Latin-derived term; mound is the everyday English word.
  • Cairn: Similar because both name a raised heap, often marking a burial or route. Differs because a cairn is made of stones, piled by hand; a mound is of earth and often rounder and larger.
  • Motte: Similar because both name an artificial earthen mound. Differs because motte is the precise medieval-fortification term for the castle mound; mound is the general word.
  • Rise / Elevation: Similar because both name higher ground. Differs because rise/elevation are abstract topographic terms with no shape or origin implied; mound is concrete, rounded, often man-made.
  • Dune: Similar because both name a mound of loose material. Differs because a dune is specifically wind-built sand; a mound is earth or any material, usually not wind-built.
  • Tump: Similar because both are dialect words for a small mound or hillock. Differs because tump is regional (south-western England) and rare; mound is standard English.
  • Berm: Similar because both name a raised bank of earth. Differs because a berm is a narrow flat ledge or strip in engineering and fortification; a mound is a rounded heap.
  • Rampart: Similar because both name a defensive earthwork. Differs because a rampart is a long fortified wall of earth, part of a fortification system; mound is general and not necessarily defensive.
