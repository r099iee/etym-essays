- 13th–18th c. — Originates in the Franco-Italian alpine borderlands as a locative surname derived from Cesana/Cézanne, designating families tracing origins to the Piedmontese alpine pass.
- 1860s–1880s — Paul Cézanne faces relentless public mockery and Salon rejections in Paris; conservative critics brand him a lunatic and crude amateur, forcing his retreat into solitary labor in Aix-en-Provence.
- 1895 — Parisian art dealer Ambroise Vollard mounts Cézanne's first major solo exhibition; progressive painters, critics, and collectors suddenly recognize his revolutionary spatial genius.
- 1907 — The posthumous Cézanne retrospective at the Salon d'Automne in Paris stuns the avant-garde, directly inspiring Picasso and Braque to invent Cubism; Cézanne is hailed as the father of modern art.
- Mid–Late 20th c. — Celebrated in philosophy (Merleau-Ponty) and formalist art criticism as the pivotal watershed dividing classical perspective from 20th-century modernist visual consciousness.
- 21st c. — Permanently enthroned as one of the supreme masters in human history, with his masterworks commanding legendary status in world museums and cultural memory.
