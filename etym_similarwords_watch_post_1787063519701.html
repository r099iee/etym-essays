• watchtower: Similar because both are places from which a watch is kept; differs because a watchtower is a physical structure or building, while a watch-post is a position or station that may have no structure at all.
  • observation post: Similar because both mean a position for observing the enemy or terrain; differs because "observation post" is modern military jargon (often abbreviated OP), while "watch-post" is older, literary, and more general.
  • sentry post: Similar because both are fixed stations for guards; differs because a sentry post implies guarding access to a place, while a watch-post emphasizes watching and reporting.
  • guard post: Similar because both are places where a guard stands duty; differs because "guard post" stresses protection of people or property, whereas a watch-post stresses surveillance of a wider area.
  • lookout: Similar because both denote a place (or person) that observes for danger; differs because "lookout" can also mean the person themselves ("a lookout on the mast"), while "watch-post" names only the place.
  • outpost: Similar because both are detached observation stations; differs because an outpost is pushed far forward into hostile or remote territory and implies isolation, while a watch-post may sit close to camp.
  • vantage point: Similar because both are positions chosen for good viewing; differs because a vantage point need not involve guarding or military purpose, while a watch-post is by definition a post of watch.
  • perch / roost: Similar because both can mean a high resting place for observing; differs because perch and roost are informal and chiefly about birds or casual seating, lacking the military duty of a watch-post.
  • post: Similar because watch-post is literally a kind of post; differs because "post" alone is far broader (any place of duty, employment, or station), while "watch-post" narrows it to watching and guarding.
  • piquet post: Similar because both are military guard positions; differs because "piquet" (picquet) is a specific term for an outlying picket of troops, while a watch-post may be a single sentry's station.
  • watch-house: Similar because both belong to the watch-and-guard vocabulary; differs because a watch-house is the building in which the watch or guard is lodged, while a watch-post is the open station where duty is done.
