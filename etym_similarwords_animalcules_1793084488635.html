• Microorganisms: Similar because both name living things too small to see unaided; differs because microorganisms is the modern technical umbrella (bacteria, viruses, fungi), while animalcules is the historical, animal-flavoured word of the early microscopists.
  • Microbes: Similar because both denote minute life revealed by lenses; differs because microbes is short, clinical, and germ-conscious, while animalcules is long, learned, and wondering.
  • Infusoria: Similar because both were the period names for the tiny swimmers in hay infusions and stagnant water; differs because infusoria names them by habitat (infusions), while animalcules names them by size (little animals).
  • Protozoa: Similar because both cover single-celled animal-like life; differs because protozoa is a precise modern taxonomic group, while animalcules lumped everything motile and minute together.
  • Bacteria: Similar because bacteria were among Leeuwenhoek's animalcules; differs because bacteria names one specific prokaryotic domain, while animalcules was a pre-taxonomic grab-bag of all tiny life.
  • Foraminifera: Similar because their shells literally build the limestone of the source sentence; differs because foraminifera are one order of shelled marine protists, while animalcules is the whole vanished world of the minute.
  • Mites: Similar because both are barely-visible living specks that startled early observers; differs because mites are true tiny arthropods (cheese mites, dust mites), visible as specks, while animalcules proper need the microscope.
  • Plankton: Similar because both are multitudes of tiny drifting life with continent-scale effects; differs because plankton is the ecological term for aquatic drifters of all sizes, while animalcules stresses the microscopic individuals under the lens.
  • Germs: Similar because both are invisible living things with outsized consequences; differs because germs means disease-agents and carries dread, while animalcules carries wonder — marvels first, menaces only later.
