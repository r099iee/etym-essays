ABIDING denotes enduring, lasting, and steadfast — continuing without fading over a long time. Most often it modifies feelings, memories, principles, and attachments: abiding love, abiding interest, abiding memory, abiding respect, abiding faith. To call love abiding is to say it does not merely flare and pass but remains, waits out difficulty, and persists through change. The word carries a dignified, slightly literary and biblical gravity absent from plain lasting or permanent.

The primary modern sense is adjectival permanence of the inward. An abiding love for poetry and opera, as in the source sentence, means a lifelong, faithful devotion exceptional in its constancy. An abiding memory means the dead remain present in mind. An abiding principle means a conviction that survives pressure and fashion. In each case ABIDING frames endurance as loyalty rather than inertia: what abides chooses to stay, not merely happens to remain. This is why obituaries, wedding vows, and memorials favour it over durable or stable, which sound mechanical.

  • Enduring affection or devotion: abiding love, abiding friendship, abiding passion — emotional bonds that persist for years or life.
  • Lasting interest or enthusiasm: abiding interest in birds, abiding love of opera — curiosity that does not wane after novelty.
  • Persistent memory or impression: abiding memory, abiding image, abiding influence — what remains vivid long after the event.
  • Steadfast principle or value: abiding principles, abiding faith, abiding respect for law — moral commitments held through trial.
  • Legal and administrative sense: abiding citizen, abiding place of residence — now rare except in Scots law and older statutes for one who dwells continuously in a place.
  • Verbal base (abide): to remain, to dwell (abide with me), to endure or tolerate (cannot abide cruelty), to submit to (abide by rules) — the finite verb survives in formal and idiomatic pockets that illuminate the adjective.

Secondary nuances distinguish ABIDING from synonyms. Lasting and enduring are neutral about agency; abiding suggests faithful waiting, a willingness to stay. Permanent and perpetual stress unchangeability, often of states or offices; abiding stresses lived continuity of feeling. Lifelong quantifies duration; abiding qualifies texture — patient, quiet, unshowy. Deep stresses intensity at a moment; abiding stresses extension across moments. A deep love can be brief; an abiding love by definition is not. Cannot abide inverts the sense: what one cannot abide is what one cannot endure to remain near, revealing the spatial root — to abide is to dwell beside.

Domain usages confirm this. In religion, abiding carries Johannine resonance — Abide in me — denoting mystical indwelling of believer and Christ, hence abiding peace as spiritual fruit. In law, to abide by an award or decision means to stand by and obey it after waiting out process; Scots abiding-place preserves the old dwell sense. In immigration and census history, abiding citizen denotes continuous residence. In literary criticism, abiding has become a stock epithet for reputations that survive neglect — abiding influence of Baker, abiding power of the sonnet — bridging personal devotion and canonicity. The grammatical bridge is the present participle: abiding literally means currently abiding, still dwelling, still waiting, hence still true.

Metaphorically, ABIDING spatialises time as residence. Feelings are lodgers: fleeting passions visit, abiding loves take up residence. This is why the word collocates with home diction — abide with me, abiding place, no abiding city — and why it comforts in mourning: to say memory abides is to say the dead still dwell with us. Grasping abide as wait plus remain plus dwell unlocks every adjectival use from opera to obituary.
