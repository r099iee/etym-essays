Dainty is an adjective (and less commonly a noun) meaning delicately small, pretty, and neatly refined, and by extension fastidiously particular about small refinements, especially in taste, manners, or appetite.

In its most common modern sense, dainty describes something pleasing precisely because it is small, delicate, and exquisitely finished: dainty hands, dainty ankles, dainty lace, dainty china, dainty sandwiches, dainty footsteps. The emphasis is on lightness, neatness, and fragile prettiness rather than grandeur or robustness. A dainty object is one that must be handled carefully; a dainty person moves or eats with controlled grace.

  • Delicately small and pretty (core physical sense): Neat, petite, and attractively fragile in appearance or construction — e.g., a dainty necklace, dainty wildflowers, a dainty figure. Implies aesthetic pleasure in fineness of scale and workmanship.
  • Delicately delicious (food sense): Choice, delicious in a light refined way, served in small portions — e.g., dainty morsels, dainties for tea. This is the oldest surviving sense, directly inherited from Middle English deynte meaning a choice delicacy.
  • Fastidious and fussy (character sense): Excessively particular, hard to please, squeamish about coarseness — e.g., a dainty eater, dainty manners, dainty conscience. Here the delicacy lies in the person judging, not the object judged. Can be admiring (refined, discriminating) or mildly critical (affected, squeamish, effete).
  • Requiring delicate handling (extended sense): Needing tact, care, or precision — e.g., a dainty operation, a dainty topic. Less common, shading toward delicate.

As a noun, now somewhat literary or archaic, a dainty (plural dainties) means a choice delicacy to eat or more broadly any small pretty thing: Christmas dainties, dainties from the bakery. Shakespeare and the King James Bible use this noun sense freely.

Polysemy bridges are transparent once the history is known. The food sense came first: what is rare and excellent is served in small exquisite portions. Small exquisite portions suggest small exquisite form generally, hence the pretty sense. People who habitually demand such rarity and fineness are themselves hard to please, hence the fastidious sense. Affectation then gives the modern ironic edge: to call a large footballer dainty for refusing mud is to accuse him of incongruous fussiness.

  • Register and connotation: Generally positive or affectionate for objects (charming, elegant), ambivalent for people — admiring when it means graceful and refined, gently mocking when it means fussy, prim, or effeminate. Your context — You whites are really too dainty — uses the critical sense: overly fastidious, squeamish, soft.
  • Domain usages: In fashion and jewellery, dainty is a term of art for fine minimal chains and small pendants. In horticulture, dainty describes small-flowered cultivars. In etiquette writing, dainty manners means restrained, neat, ladylike conduct.
  • Collocations: dainty fingers, dainty feet, dainty lace, dainty china, dainty bite, dainty morsel, dainty step, dainty eater, dainty manners, dainty work.

In short, dainty condenses worth, rarity, deliciousness, smallness, prettiness, and fussiness into a single light word — a word that itself, fittingly, feels small and neat in the mouth.
