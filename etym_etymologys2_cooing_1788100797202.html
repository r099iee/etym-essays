English (1660s — echoic)
coo
“to make the low murmuring sound of a dove”

(An imitative coinage from the bird’s own call — a sound-word is born, not inherited, so no Indo-European ancestry exists to trace.)

↓
English (late 18th c.)
coo — extended sense
“to speak softly, fondly, lovingly”

↓
English (18th–19th c.)
cooing
“present participle — softly murmuring; uttering endearments”

↓
Modern English (present)
COOING

Note: The earliest recorded English forms are the verb and noun “coo” in the 1660s, in bird-lore contexts. The exact decade in which the word first applied to human speech is not precisely fixed; by the later 18th century the transferred sense is well established in sentimental prose and verse.
