- Old English — the fourth part: feorðing names any quarter share, of pennies, lands, or loaves, the surveyor's fraction.
- 13th c. — the clipped coin: pennies cut along the cross make farthings for small market change, round or ragged as the knife allows.
- Tudor–Stuart — the royal farthing: hammered and then milled farthings in silver and copper, farmed out to patentees and cursed as often as spent.
- 1860 — the bronze wren: the round bronze farthing with Britannia, later the wren, becomes the beloved small change of buns, tolls, and collection plates.
- 1961 — demonetised: inflation kills the farthing's purchasing power and the mint withdraws it, leaving the word to idiom and nostalgia.
- Today — proverb and museum: not worth a farthing survives in books and speeches while the coins themselves rest in trays and tales.
