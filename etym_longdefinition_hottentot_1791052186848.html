Hottentot is an outdated, now offensive exonym formerly used by Europeans for the Khoikhoi, the pastoral Khoe-speaking peoples of southwestern Africa (present-day South Africa and Namibia), and by extension for mixed Khoi-San peoples. In contemporary use it is regarded as a slur and should be avoided except in historical quotation; the preferred terms are Khoikhoi, Khoekhoe, Nama, or Khoe-San.

Historical meaning: From the mid-17th to mid-20th century, Hottentot in travel, colonial and anthropological writing meant a member of the cattle-herding native peoples around the Cape. Example: every Hottentot is a gentleman of leisure in the source reflects early-20th-century stereotype of supposed idleness — that Khoi herders would not do sustained wage-labour for deferred reward. Nineteenth-century literature also created stock figures: the Hottentot Venus (exhibited Khoikhoi woman Sarah Baartman), Hottentot chief, Hottentot kraal. The word carried evolutionist ranking: primitive, click-speaking, pastoral, placed low on supposed civilisational ladder.

  • 1. Ethnonym (obsolete/offensive): A Khoikhoi person. Now replaced. To use it of a living person causes offence in South Africa and Namibia and is classed with other apartheid-era slurs.
  • 2. Generic insult for savage/crude person: Historical transferred use: Don't behave like a Hottentot, meaning don't be ignorant, noisy, gluttonous, ill-mannered. Recorded in 19th-early 20th c. dictionaries. Entirely obsolete today except as evidence of racism.
  • 3. Linguistic / anthropological label: Hottentot language (now Khoekhoe, Nama language), Hottentot clicks. Still found in old books but superseded by Khoekhoe, Khoe languages.
  • 4. Biological relics: Hottentot fig (Carpobrotus edulis, now sour fig), Hottentot teal (duck), Hottentot cabbage. Common names under revision.

Why offensive: It was never a self-name; it mocks Khoikhoi speech as stammering gibberish, imposed by Dutch colonists who dispossessed its bearers. It accumulated dehumanising pseudo-science (exhibition, anatomical myth-making). Its use signals colonial contempt even when speaker intends neutral description, as arguably in the source passage which treats Hottentot as ordinary plural like Australian. Modern readers must recognise that neutrality as dated ignorance, not acceptable present usage.

Usage guidance: Do not apply Hottentot to people today. In study of older texts, gloss it: Hottentot [now offensive; Khoikhoi]. When the Anki context says every Hottentot is a gentleman of leisure, understand it as early economics illustrating supposed improvidence and leisure preference, using period terminology that must not be repeated outside quotation. For exams or essays, name the peoples Khoikhoi pastoralists and note the historical exonym.

Polysemy bridge: All senses flow from COLONIAL MISHEARING → PEOPLE LABEL → CONTEMPTUOUS TYPE. The click-mimicry origin (stammer) already contains dismissal (they don't speak, they stutter), making pejoration almost inevitable: from incomprehensible sound to incomprehensible people to byword for crudity.
