• hills: Similar because both are raised elevations of ground; differs because hills are larger natural landforms, whereas mounds are smaller, often rounded or man-made heaps.
  • hillocks: Similar because both are small raised mounds of earth; differs because hillocks are always landforms and never heaps of loose material like treasure.
  • heaps: Similar because both mean large piles or accumulations; differs because heaps emphasise disordered, often shapeless piles of material and never mean an elevation of the ground.
  • piles: Similar because both mean stacks or accumulations of things; differs because piles are neat or careless stacks with no sense of earthworks or landforms.
  • banks: Similar because both can be raised ridges of earth; differs because banks are long, sloped ridges (of a river or road), not rounded heaps.
  • ridges: Similar because both are raised lines of ground; differs because ridges are long and crested, whereas mounds are round and isolated.
  • barrows: Similar because both are raised mounds of earth, often burial mounds; differs because barrows specifically denote ancient burial tumuli and carry a funerary, archaeological register.
  • tumuli: Similar because both are burial mounds; differs because tumuli is the formal Latin-derived archaeological term for ancient grave mounds.
  • cairns: Similar because both are heaps, often raised over graves; differs because cairns are specifically made of piled stones, not earth.
  • dunes: Similar because both are rounded heaps of loose material; differs because dunes are wind-formed sand hills, natural and shifting.
  • mottes: Similar because both are man-made defensive earthworks; differs because motte is the precise medieval term for the mound supporting a castle keep.
  • hoards: Similar because both mean large stored accumulations of treasure; differs because a hoard is a secret or guarded store of valuables, while mounds are visible heaps.
