- [15th c. Middle English] boistous — "rough, coarse, clumsy, fierce"; applied to cloth, manners, force; perception largely negative or physically harsh, not festive.
- [16th–17th c.] Form boisterous spreads — strong association with wild weather, rough seas, violent storms; human uses still imply uncouth strength or turbulence.
- [18th–19th c.] Social softening — increasingly describes loud, high-spirited, disorderly but often cheerful behavior (parties, boys, country company); weather sense remains available; perception can be amused tolerance or mild reproof.
- [20th c.] Fixed dual modern profile — classroom/children/crowd exuberance vs stormy elements; schoolbook vocabulary word; generally less menacing than rowdy in many contexts.
- [21st c.] Current perception — lively, noisy, unrestrained energy; often affectionate in family and sports writing; still capable of criticism when noise violates norms; weather uses sound slightly literary.
