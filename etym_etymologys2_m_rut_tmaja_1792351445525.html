Note on etymological debate regarding Vedic marut: Historical linguists (such as Manfred Mayrhofer in the Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindoarischen and Julius Pokorny) have proposed two distinct etymological origins for Sanskrit marút: (1) Derivation from PIE *mer- ("to gleam, sparkle, flash"), pointing to the radiant golden armor, lightning bolts, and shining weapons of the storm gods in the Ṛgveda (cognate with Greek marmaírein, "to sparkle, flash"); or (2) Derivation from PIE *mer- ("to rub, crush, grind, pound"), reflecting the violent destructive nature of storm gales that uproot forests and grind stones (cognate with Sanskrit mṛṇāti "crushes", Latin mortārium "mortar", and Russian molot "hammer"). Both semantic dimensions converge historically in the dual character of the storm deities: radiant bringers of rain and terrifying destroyers of obstacles.
