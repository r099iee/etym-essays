Derivation chain for reproachful — the word is a single English formation (reproach + the suffix -ful), so two separate chains follow: the chain for the base "reproach" and the chain for the suffix "-ful." Chain for the base reproach: 1. Proto-Indo-European *pro- "before, in front, forward" (c. 4500–3000 BCE) — the great "forward" root, surviving as Sanskrit prá, Greek πρό (pró), and Germanic *furai (English "fore" and "for"). 2. Latin prō "before, forward, in front of" (attested from the earliest Latin texts, c. 3rd c. BCE). 3. Latin prope "near" (from *pro + the enclitic -kʷe/-pe, literally "and-before," hence "close ahead, near"; attested by the 3rd–2nd c. BCE). 4. Vulgar Latin *repropāre "to bring (a fault) back near (to someone)" (re- "back, again" + prope "near"; reconstructed, late Roman period) — the stage that created the moral sense. 5. Old French reprochier "to upbraid, blame, accuse" (attested from the 11th c.); Old French reproche "a blame, a reproach" (noun). 6. Middle English reprochen (verb) and reproche (noun) (borrowed via Anglo-Norman, c. 1300–1400). 7. English reproach (noun and verb). 8. English reproachful "full of reproach" (base + the native suffix -ful; attested by the 16th c.). Supplementary chain for the suffix -ful (a second root of the formation): 1. Proto-Indo-European *pelh₁- "to fill" (c. 4500–3000 BCE) → adjectival *pl̥h₁nós "full." 2. Proto-Germanic *fullaz "full" → suffix *-fullaz "-ful, full of" (c. 500 BCE–500 CE). 3. Old English -ful "full of" (attested from the earliest Old English, c. 8th c.) — a highly productive native suffix. 4. English suffix -ful, added to reproach to form reproachful (16th c.). Note on debate and nuance: the Vulgar Latin base *repropāre is reconstructed rather than attested in classical Latin; the semantic leap from "bring near again" to "blame" happened in the spoken Latin of late antiquity and is first recorded in Old French. Italian rimprovero "reproach" is a false friend — it descends from re- + improperāre (root *probrum "a disgrace"), not from prope — and must not be merged with this chain.
