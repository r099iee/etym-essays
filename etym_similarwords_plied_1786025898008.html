• Practiced: Similar because both describe performing a profession, trade, or skill over time; differs because 'practiced' emphasizes skill development, habit, or professional qualification, whereas 'plied' emphasizes the physical, continuous, and industrious labor of wielding the craft.
  • Wielded: Similar because both involve using or operating a tool, weapon, or instrument; differs because 'wielded' emphasizes the display of physical power, control, or authority, while 'plied' denotes steady, repetitive, hardworking application.
  • Exercised: Similar because both denote putting a skill or ability into active use; differs because 'exercised' is more abstract and formal (e.g., exercising rights or judgment) rather than manual, physical toil.
  • Pressed: Similar because both can mean persistently urging or presenting something to someone; differs because 'pressed' implies direct psychological coercion or insistence, whereas 'plied' conveys continuous, overwhelming showering or supplying (as with food, drinks, or questions).
  • Navigated: Similar because both relate to moving along a body of water or course; differs because 'navigated' focuses on charting and guiding the direction, whereas 'plied' emphasizes repetitive, regular transit back and forth along a fixed route.
  • Pursued: Similar because both describe engaging in a career, path, or line of work; differs because 'pursued' focuses on chasing an ambition or goal over time rather than the immediate physical exertion of work.
