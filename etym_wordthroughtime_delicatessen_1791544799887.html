- Mid-18th c. – Francophile luxury import in Germany: Borrowed from French *délicatesse* into German as *Delikatesse* to denote rare, expensive, exotic culinary dainties enjoyed by aristocratic tables.
- Early 19th c. – Commercial specialization: Plural *Delikatessen* became associated with specialized German food emporiums (*Delikatessenhandlungen*) purveying fine cheeses, cured meats, and foreign culinary imports to the rising bourgeoisie.
- Late 19th c. – American immigrant transplantation: Brought to American metropolitan centers (especially New York, Chicago, and Milwaukee) by German and German-Jewish immigrants, establishing *delicatessens* as community hubs featuring pastrami, corned beef, rye bread, and smoked fish.
- Mid-20th c. – Urban cultural icon: Clipped colloquially to *deli*, evolving into a beloved staple of American urban life, celebrated in literature and cinema as the quintessential casual lunch counter and purveyor of oversized sandwiches.
- Late 20th c. to Present – Globalized and supermarket fixture: Genericized into standard retail vocabulary, denoting any dedicated supermarket counter purveying sliced meats, artisan cheeses, and prepared salads, alongside preserved traditional heritage shops.
