• evoked: Similar because both mean bringing something forth into awareness; differs because evoked summons up dormant memories, moods, or imaginative associations by indirect resonance and suggestion, whereas elicited draws out concrete facts, explicit responses, or overt reactions through direct, purposeful prompting.
  • extracted: Similar because both involve pulling information out of someone; differs because extracted implies force, pressure, or compulsion (as in coercive interrogation), whereas elicited stresses gentle, artful coaxing without aggression.
  • educed: Similar because both mean drawing out what is latent; differs because educed is a formal, chiefly logical or philosophical term for inferring principles from premises or evidence, while elicited centers on provoking an immediate verbal, behavioral, or physiological response.
  • provoked: Similar because both mean causing a reaction; differs because provoked implies stirring irritation, anger, hostility, or defiance through deliberate irritation, whereas elicited is neutral to constructive, drawing forth without antagonism.
  • drew out: Similar because both describe obtaining information through questioning; differs because drew out is the plain, untechnical phrasal expression, while elicited is the more formal, Latinate term implying deliberate craft.
  • coaxed: Similar because both imply obtaining something through gentle persuasion; differs because coaxed focuses on the manner of persuasion (flattery, pleading, gentle pressure) regardless of what is obtained, whereas elicited focuses on the fact that a specific response was drawn out by a stimulus or question.
  • extorted: Similar because both involve obtaining something from someone against their initial willingness; differs because extorted means obtaining by threats, violence, or abuse of power, whereas elicited is achieved by skillful, non-coercive questioning.
  • occasioned: Similar because both mean causing something to come about; differs because occasioned means caused or brought about incidentally and impersonally (an event occasions an effect), while elicited implies an intentional agent drawing a specific response out of a specific source.
  • drew: Similar because both appear in the sense "obtain a reaction"; differs because plain drew is broader and often unintentional (a performance draws applause), while elicited stresses deliberate design and the prior concealment of the response.
