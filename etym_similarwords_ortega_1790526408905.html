• Unamuno: Similar as a preeminent 20th-century Spanish intellectual, philosopher, and essayist who probed the existential soul of Spain and wrote extensively on Don Quixote; differs because Miguel de Unamuno represented passionate, agonizing Christian existentialism, religious paradox, and tragic faith (The Tragic Sense of Life), whereas Ortega championed a secular, cosmopolitan, ratiovitalist, and analytical European intellectual framework.
  • Nietzsche: Similar in critiquing bourgeois complacency, herd mentality, and dry scholastic rationalism in favor of vital, dynamic life-affirmation; differs because Friedrich Nietzsche pursued an aggressive, iconoclastic anti-egalitarian dismantling of traditional Christian morality, whereas Ortega sought to integrate reason with living vitality (*vital reason*) to preserve liberal democratic civilization through enlightened cultural leadership.
  • Tocqueville: Similar as a perceptive political thinker who diagnosed the vulnerabilities of democratic society and warned against the tyranny of the majority and conformism; differs because Alexis de Tocqueville operated within 19th-century political sociology and empirical observation of American civic institutions, while Ortega analyzed 20th-century psychological mass alienation, industrialization, and technocratic specialization.
  • Sartre: Similar as a prominent public philosopher and literary essayist who examined human freedom, situated existence, and social engagement; differs because Jean-Paul Sartre grounded his philosophy in French atheistic existential phenomenology and Marxist political action ("existence precedes essence"), while Ortega developed ratiovitalism and perspectivism rooted in historical circumstance.
  • Santayana: Similar as a brilliant Spanish-born philosopher and prose stylist who attained worldwide renown for reflections on aesthetics and culture; differs because George Santayana lived and taught in the United States and England, developing a detached naturalist skepticism, whereas Ortega remained deeply engaged in Spanish political journalism, education, and institutional reform.
  • Spengler: Similar as an early 20th-century civilizational theorist investigating the crisis and destiny of Western culture; differs because Oswald Spengler proposed a rigid, deterministic, morphological decline of civilizations (The Decline of the West), whereas Ortega maintained an open, vitalist philosophy emphasizing human historical responsibility and intellectual regeneration.
