PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*gʷhedh-
"to ask, pray, entreat, beg"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*bedō / *bidą
"prayer, request, religious petition"
↓
Old English (c. 700–1050)
gebed
"prayer, supplication to God"
Note: Plural 'gebedu'. Cognate with Old High German 'beta' ('prayer') and German 'bitten' ('to request').
↓
Middle English (c. 1200–1400)
bede / beed
"a prayer; perforated ball on a rosary used to count prayers"
Note: Metonymic shift: 'counting one's prayers' on a string of beads transferred the word from the prayer to the physical ball itself.
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550–1600)
bead + -y → beady
"resembling small beads; small, round, bright, and gleaming"
