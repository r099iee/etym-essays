• Fields: Similar because both are open agricultural land; differs because a field may hold any crop or use, while a meadow is specifically grassland kept for hay or pasture.
  • Pastures: Similar because both are grazing land; differs because pasture stresses the grazing use, while meadow is the broader low-lying grassland defined by mowing and harvest as well as grazing.
  • Lawns: Similar because both are areas of grass; differs because a lawn is ornamental, mown short, and owned by a garden, while a meadow is longer, lush, and productive of hay.
  • Moors: Similar because both are open land; differs because a moor is high, open heath of heather and peat, while a meadow is low, green, watered, and lush.
  • Prairies: Similar because both are grassland; differs because a prairie is the vast North American tall- and short-grass ecosystem, while a meadow is a small European lowland hay-field.
  • Water-meadows: Similar because they are meadows beside rivers; differs because a water-meadow is the specialized, deliberately flooded river pasture, while meadow is the general term.
  • Meads: Similar because mead is the same word in older poetic dress; differs because mead is archaic, literary, and usually singular, while meadows is the modern plural of everyday speech.
