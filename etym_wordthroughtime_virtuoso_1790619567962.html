- Renaissance Italian virtù as excellence - virtuoso means worthy, virtuous, skilled in arts and arms; connotation is moral-aesthetic admiration for universal man
- 17th-century English import as connoisseur - a virtuoso collects coins, shells and experiments; perception shifts to learned curiosity, then satirised as dilettante hoarder
- Late 18th–19th-century musical specialisation - Paganini and Liszt fix virtuoso as transcendent soloist; societal perception splits between worshipful frenzy and moral suspicion of empty display
- Contemporary broadened master - from classical to jazz, rock, sport and coding, means dazzling performer of any craft; now largely admiring with ironic edge when craft overshadows feeling
