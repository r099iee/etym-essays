• Mettis: Similar because it is the late Latin name of the same city and the direct etymon of the modern form; differs because it belongs to Roman administrative vocabulary and appears only in ancient and medieval Latin sources.
  • Divodurum: Similar because it denotes the same settlement in the pre-Roman and early Roman period; differs because it is the Gaulish name, meaning “divine fortress”, and was replaced rather than continued by Mettis.
  • Mediomatrici: Similar because the tribe gave the city its later name and its territory defined the region; differs because it names a people, not a place, and its own meaning is disputed.
  • Messin: Similar because it is the French demonym for an inhabitant of Metz and shares the same stem; differs because it is a gentilic adjective or noun for a person, not the city's name, and is unknown to English readers.
  • Lorraine: Similar because Metz was the capital of the modern administrative region of Lorraine and the two names are constantly paired; differs because Lorraine is a much larger province or region, with Nancy as its historic ducal capital.
  • Moselle: Similar because the river and the département define Metz's setting and often appear in apposition to it; differs because the name denotes the waterway and the administrative unit, not the city.
  • Alsace-Lorraine: Similar because Metz was one of the principal cities of the imperial territory created in 1871; differs because the compound denotes a political entity of 1871–1918 rather than the city itself.
  • Trier (Trèves): Similar because it is another ancient Roman city on the Moselle, with a Latin name and a comparable frontier history; differs because it lies in Germany, takes its name from the Treveri, and was never French.
