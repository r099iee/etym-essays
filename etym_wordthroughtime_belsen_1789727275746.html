- Medieval hamlet 1235 onward - Bellenhusen at the houses of Bello, a quiet heath farming settlement; perception neutral, local, administrative.
- Barracks and POW camp 1940-1943 - Stalag XI-C for Soviet prisoners brings mass death to the heath; name still geographic, but locals associate it with guarded compounds and typhus.
- Concentration camp 1943-1945 and liberation April 1945 - Bergen-Belsen holds 60,000 starving inmates, Anne Frank dies, British cameras film corpses; Belsen becomes world shorthand for Nazi crime, connotation of shock, shame and mourning.
- Post-war emblem 1945-1960s - Belsen trial hangs the Beast of Belsen, memorial rises, writers freely write of Belsens of prisons, farms and oil slicks; perception moral-amplifier, protest shorthand for absolute evil.
- Contested metaphor 1970s-present - Auschwitz displaces Belsen as central Holocaust symbol, survivors and guides call generic Belsen comparisons trivialising; Belsen of floating oil now heard as potentially offensive, usage retreats to literal history unless deliberate provocation is intended.
