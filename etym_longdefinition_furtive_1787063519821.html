FURTIVE is an adjective meaning secretive, sly, or stealthy — done in a quiet, sneaking way, as if trying to avoid being noticed or to hide something. The classic image is the furtive glance: a quick, sideways look that betrays guilt or concealment. The word carries an inescapable suggestion of bad conscience: furtive behaviour is not merely quiet but thievish, evasive, or underhanded, as if the person has something to hide.

In The Hobbit, Tolkien writes that the dwarves "met no sign of any living thing, only furtive shadows that fled from the approach of their torches": the shadows in the tunnels of the Lonely Mountain are furtive because they slip away secretly and elusively, like guilty creatures avoiding the light. The adjective transfers human sneakiness to shadow itself, giving the passage its eerie, creeping atmosphere.

Core meaning: Stealthy, secretive, sly; done or acting so as to escape notice.

  • Primary sense (stealthy action): Done in a secret, sly manner to avoid observation or detection. Most common with glance, look, movement, gesture, step. For example, "he took a furtive sip from his flask".
  • Secondary sense (evasive character): Of a person: shy, secretive, shifty, given to concealment; arousing distrust. For example, "the man's furtive manner made the guard suspicious".
  • Literal thievish sense: The etymological sense, now largely archaic: inclined to steal, thievish, of the nature of a thief. This survives mainly in allusion and in older literature — a "furtive hand" is a thieving hand.
  • Applied to things: Of animals, sounds, movements, and even abstractions: quiet, elusive, gliding, as if fleeing detection. A "furtive rustling", "furtive steps", "furtive shadows".

The furtive glance and collocation: The most famous collocation of the word is furtive glance — a glance stolen sideways, quickly, and guiltily. Other strong partners are look, eye, step, movement, approach, manner, behaviour. The word works because it names both the outward action (quiet, quick, evasive) and the inner state it betrays (guilt, fear, shame, bad faith). A furtive act is not only hidden; it is hidden because it would not bear scrutiny.

Connotations and register: Furtive is almost always pejorative or at least suspicious. Unlike stealthy (which can be neutral or admiring — a hunter is stealthy), furtive implies guilt, shame, or dishonesty: one is furtive about something one wants hidden. It belongs to a formal-to-literary register; in casual speech people say sneaky or shifty. Its thievish etymology colours every modern use, which is why it suggests more than mere quietness — it suggests a thief's nervousness about the light.

Grammatical notes: Furtive is a regular adjective, gradable in principle though rarely compared ("more furtive"). The adverb is furtively ("he glanced furtively about"), and the noun is furtiveness. There is no verb. It frequently pairs with nouns of perception and motion: glance, look, eye, step, movement, approach, shadow.

Related semantic fields: secrecy, stealth, sneakiness, guilt, concealment, evasion, thievery, suspicion.

In the context of the card: "only furtive shadows that fled from the approach of their torches" — the adjective invests the moving shadows with stealth and guilty intent, so that the tunnels seem inhabited by hidden, watching presences. The furtiveness is precisely what makes the passage sinister: things that flee the light so secretively may be hostile, and the dwarves' torches reveal a world that does not want to be seen.
