• Mirror-like: Similar because both describe surfaces that reflect crisp, clear images; differs because mirror-like is an informal, descriptive everyday compound, whereas specular is the precise, formal scientific and photographic term defined by the law of reflection ($ heta_i = heta_r$).
  • Reflective: Similar because both describe the property of casting back light, heat, or sound; differs because reflective is an overarching term encompassing both direct mirror reflections and diffuse scattering, whereas specular specifically isolates directional, unscattered mirror reflection.
  • Lustrous: Similar because both convey a brilliant, shining surface appearance; differs because lustrous describes a soft, glowing, or iridescent sheen resulting from subsurface scattering (as in pearls or silk), whereas specular denotes a hard, unscattered surface reflection of the light source itself.
  • Glinting: Similar because both involve sharp, bright points of reflected light; differs because glinting implies a momentary, flickering, or intermittent flash of light produced by motion, whereas specular is a permanent physical and optical property of a smooth surface.
  • Diffuse: Similar because both are fundamental classifications of light reflection in physics; differs because diffuse is the direct functional opposite of specular, describing rough surfaces that scatter incoming light uniformly across all angles (Lambertian scattering).
  • Matte: Similar because both describe surface finishing and light interaction; differs because matte is the antithesis of specular, designating a dull, non-reflective, textured finish engineered specifically to eliminate glare and specular highlights.
  • Lambertian: Similar because both are mathematical models of surface reflection in optics and computer graphics; differs because Lambertian surfaces reflect light with equal apparent brightness in all viewing directions regardless of the observer's position, whereas specular reflection is highly directional.
  • Catoptric: Similar because both pertain to the reflection of light; differs because catoptric is an overarching branch term relating to optical systems that utilize mirrors to focus light (such as reflecting telescopes, contrasted with dioptric refracting systems), whereas specular describes the physical quality of the reflection itself.
  • Polished: Similar because polished materials frequently exhibit specular reflection; differs because polished refers to the physical mechanical process of smoothing or burnishing a material's surface, whereas specular refers to the resulting optical behavior of the light waves.
