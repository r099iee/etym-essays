• Trembling: Similar because it denotes fine involuntary shaking from fear, cold, or age; differs because trembling can be delicate and even tender, while quaking is coarser, more violent, and more abject.
  • Shaking: Similar because it covers all back-and-forth vibration of body or object; differs because shaking can be voluntary and vigorous (shaking rugs, shaking with laughter), while quaking is always helpless and fear-driven.
  • Shuddering: Similar because it means convulsive shaking from horror or cold; differs because a shudder is usually a single wave passing through the body, while quaking is continuous and sustained.
  • Shivering: Similar because it names rapid tremor from cold or fear; differs because shivering defaults to cold and teeth, while quaking defaults to dread and knees, a coarser whole-body event.
  • Quivering: Similar because it captures fine rapid vibration of voice or lip; differs because quivering is small-scale and can be poignant or eager, while quaking is larger and always fearful.
  • Cowering: Similar because it describes the cringing posture of fear; differs because cowering names the shrinking posture rather than the vibration, the curl where quaking is the tremor.
  • Quailing: Similar because it means flinching and failing in courage before danger; differs because quailing is literary and inward (the spirit shrinking), while quaking is the outward visible shake.
