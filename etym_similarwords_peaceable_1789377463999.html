• Peaceful: Similar because both denote an absence of conflict and disturbance; differs because peaceful primarily describes a prevailing state, quiet environment, or tranquil condition (e.g., a peaceful village or peaceful silence), whereas peaceable describes an internal character trait, disposition, or conscious policy of avoiding strife.
  • Amicable: Similar because both convey friendliness and absence of quarrel; differs because amicable is typically applied to specific interactions, transactions, treaties, or settlements between parties (e.g., an amicable divorce or amicable agreement), whereas peaceable describes an individual's enduring personality or temperamental aversion to strife.
  • Conciliatory: Similar because both seek to prevent or resolve hostility; differs because conciliatory refers to active overtures, soothing gestures, or concessions made to appease an offended opponent, whereas peaceable is an abiding, habitual state of non-aggression that does not provoke conflict in the first place.
  • Placid: Similar because both indicate an undisturbed, calm demeanor; differs because placid emphasizes an unruffled, smooth emotional surface that can sometimes border on bovine complacency or emotional indifference, whereas peaceable represents a principled moral inclination toward harmony.
  • Irenic: Similar because both champion peace over contention; differs because irenic is a scholarly, theological, and intellectual term specifically describing an approach to theological or philosophical debate that seeks common ground, whereas peaceable is an everyday descriptor of human temper and civic conduct.
  • Nonbelligerent: Similar because both denote an absence of active fighting; differs because nonbelligerent is a technical, formal geopolitical term designating a nation that does not participate in an ongoing armed conflict, lacking the moral warmth and personal gentleness of a peaceable disposition.
