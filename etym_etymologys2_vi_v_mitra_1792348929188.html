ROOT 1: viśva (विश्व)
PIE (reconstructed)
*wéyḱ-wo- / *wiḱ-wo-
"all, whole, entire; of the clan/community"
(derived from *weyḱ- "clan, village, settlement")
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (reconstructed)
*wíśwas
"all, whole, every"
(cognate with Avestan vīspa- "all, every")
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1500 BCE; Rigveda)
viśva
"all, whole, every, universal, cosmic"

ROOT 2: mitra (मित्र)
PIE (reconstructed)
*mey- / *mi-tro-
"to bind, unite; friendly covenant, contract"
(formed with instrumental/agentive suffix *-trom)
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (reconstructed)
*mitrám
"covenant, treaty, pact, friend; deity of treaties"
(source of Avestan Miθra- / Mithra)
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1500 BCE; Rigveda)
mitra
"friend, companion, ally; the solar deity Mitra"

SYNTHESIS & COMPOUND MORPHOLOGY:
Vedic Sanskrit (Rigvedic tatpuruṣa compound)
viśva + mitra → Viśvāmitra
"Universal Friend; Friend to All Creation"
(lengthening of final -a to -ā governed by Pāṇini 6.3.130)
↓
Classical Sanskrit (Epics & Puranas)
Viśvāmitra (विश्वामित्र)
"name of the great Brahmarshi and preceptor of Rama"
↓
Modern Indological Loan / English (19th c.–present)
Viśvāmitra
"the legendary Vedic sage and preceptor in Hindu literature"

Note on morphological rule: The lengthening of the connecting vowel from viśva to viśvā- is an archaic Vedic sandhi feature formally codified in classical Sanskrit grammar by Pāṇini in Aṣṭādhyāyī 6.3.130 (mitre carṣau), which specifies that when the word mitra follows viśva to designate a sage (ṛṣi), the terminal vowel of viśva obligatorily lengthens to ā.
