- [14th century: needlework ornament] - 'Embroider' enters English meaning to ornament fabric with needlework; connotation is craft and luxury - the painstaking labour of the needle.
- [16th-18th century: the aristocratic art] - Embroidery becomes the badge of elite domestic opulence - court costume, ecclesiastical vestments, household linen; the word also reaches metaphorically, to 'embroider a tale' being to embellish it with invented detail; connotation is at once luxurious and artful.
- [Contemporary sense] - 'Embroidered' still means decorated with patterns of thread, and still colours speech as a metaphor for embellishment; connotation is opulent and precise - needlework as a permanent, labour-intensive record of luxury, and a hint of added fiction when applied to words.
