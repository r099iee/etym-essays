Lineage 1 — the limb (OE earm):

PIE (c. 4500–3500 BCE)
*h₂er-
“to fit, join, put together”

↓
PIE (derivative) (reconstructed)
*h₂ermos
“that which is joined;
arm, shoulder”

↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*armaz
“arm”

↓
Old English (attested from the 8th c.)
earm
“arm (of the body);
arm of the sea”

↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
arm
“arm; anything
resembling an arm”

↓
Modern English
ARMS
“upper limbs; projecting
ridges of a mountain”


Lineage 2 — weapons (Latin arma):

PIE (c. 4500–3500 BCE)
*h₂er-
“to fit, join”

↓
Latin (attested from the 3rd c. BCE)
arma (n. pl.)
“weapons, tools of war,
equipment”

↓
Old French (10th–12th c.)
armes
“weapons, armour”

↓
Anglo-Norman / Middle English (c. 1300)
armes
“weapons; heraldic bearings”

↓
Modern English
ARMS
“weapons; coat of arms”

Note: The two lineages are distinct words that converge on the identical form “arms”. Both descend from the same PIE root *h₂er- “to fit, join”, one through Germanic (the limb) and one through Latin (the equipment).
