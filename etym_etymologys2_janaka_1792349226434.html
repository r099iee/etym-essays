Note on historical debate: Philologists and Indologists (such as Arthur Anthony Macdonell, Michael Witzel, and H.C. Raychaudhuri) have long analyzed whether Janaka was initially an individual proper name that later became an honorific royal title, or whether it began as a functional title ("progenitor/patron of the people") adopted by the kings of Videha. The consensus acknowledges both: while individual historical kings such as Janaka Vaideha of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad and Sīradhvaja Janaka of the Rāmāyaṇa were distinct historical/epic figures, the term evolved into a dynastic surname for the rulers of Mithilā, parallel to 'Pharaoh' in Egypt or 'Caesar' in Rome.
