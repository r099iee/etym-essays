- [15th-16th century commerce and bookkeeping] - [Enters with the vocabulary of trade meaning a fall in the estimated worth of goods, currency, or stock; connotation neutral and financial — the language of ledgers]
- [18th century fiscal exactness] - [Used precisely in monetary theory of the day about the debased value of coin and paper money; connotation technical, a markdown stated by an accountant]
- [19th century art-critical metaphor] - [Ruskin presses the ledger-word into criticism: 'direct depreciation of the works of living artists' — a cool, clinical devaluation drained of malice, and therefore more damning than abuse; the artist is not insulted but devalued]
- [20th-21st century twin lives] - [Standard accounting term for spreading an asset's cost over its useful life; metaphorically still used of reputations and standing being marked down in print — a word that is at once a technical formality and a quiet insult]
