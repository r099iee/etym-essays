• Vesicle: Similar because a vesicle is a small fluid-filled blister on the skin (medical term); differs because vesicle is the precise dermatological label for a small (<5 mm) blister, while blister is the everyday general word for any size.
  • Bulla: Similar because a bulla is a large blister (medical term); differs because bulla specifically means a blister over ~5 mm and is clinical jargon, while blister is the lay word.
  • Pustule: Similar because a pustule is a raised, blister-like skin elevation; differs because a pustule contains pus (infected), while a blister normally contains clear serum.
  • Bubble: Similar because a bubble is a raised pocket of gas/liquid in a surface or liquid, the same image as a blister; differs because bubble is the general word for any gas-filled sphere (and can be pleasant), while blister implies a damaging or painful swelling.
  • Bleb: Similar because a bleb is a small fluid- or air-filled swelling (medical/technical); differs because bleb is used mainly of tissue (e.g. lung blebs) and is obscure outside medicine.
  • Weal/welt: Similar because both are raised skin swellings from irritation; differs because a weal or welt is a flat ridge of inflamed skin (from a blow or allergy), not a fluid-filled bubble.
  • Wart: Similar because both are raised bumps on the skin; differs because a wart is a hard, virus-caused growth of skin, not a fluid-filled blister.
  • Blain: Similar because blain is an archaic/medical word for a blister or sore (chilblain); differs because blain is now dialectal/rare, while blister is standard.
  • Blister pack (compound sense): Similar because the packaging sense shares the same name and image; differs because blister pack is a manufactured plastic-and-card package, far from the skin-lesion core sense.
