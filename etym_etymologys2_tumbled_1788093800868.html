PIE (prehistoric; reconstructed)
*tūm- / *tum-
"to turn, whirl, reel"
↓
West Germanic (prehistoric; reconstructed)
*tūmolōn / *tumalōn
"to turn round, reel, tumble"
↓
Old English (10th–11th c.)
tumbian
"to dance, leap, tumble"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
tumblen
"to dance, perform acrobatics; to fall head over heels"
↓
Modern English (16th c.)
tumble
"to fall suddenly; to roll about"
↓
Modern English (present)
TUMBLED
"fell suddenly and helplessly; rolled over; (of rocks) worn and scattered"

Note: there is no securely attested PIE root for this word family; the base *tūm-/*tum- is a Germanic reconstruction, and the deeper connection is debated — some scholars posit an expressive (sound-symbolic) formation, others relate it to Germanic words for reeling and dizziness (German taumeln "to stagger, reel", Dutch tuimelen). The Old French verb tomber "to fall", and the tumbril cart, may belong to the same Germanic base, but their history is itself disputed (an alternative Latin origin from tumba "tomb" has been proposed). No stage in the English line itself is genuinely uncertain.
