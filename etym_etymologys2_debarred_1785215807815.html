Linear chain for DEBARRED (from the earliest reconstructable stage; note: the ultimate origin of the root barra lies in a substrate/Celtic loan and is not securely PIE): Stage 1 — Non-PIE substrate (Vulgar Latin item): *barra ("a bar, a cross-beam, a barrier, a stake"). Attested only in the vulgar register of late Latin; the word's ultimate origin is uncertain and is generally held to be a Gaulish/Celtic or other pre-Roman substrate loan (a word for a point, a spike, or a wooden pole). No PIE ancestor can be securely reconstructed; any deeper form is unknown and conjectural. Stage 2 — Old French: barre ("a bar, a beam; the bar of a gate; the bar of a court") — continued from Vulgar Latin *barra through the Gallo-Romance vernacular. Stage 3 — Old French: debarrer ("to bar out, to shut out with a bar"), a prefixed verb formed of de- ("away, from") + barrer ("to bar, to close with a bar"), the whole meaning "to remove/keep out by means of a bar." Stage 4 — Anglo-Norman / Middle English: debarren ("to shut out, to exclude, to preclude"), borrowed into English from Old French/Anglo-Norman in the 14th–15th centuries. Stage 5 — Modern English: debar ("to exclude, shut out, prohibit"); past tense and past participle DEBARRED; gerund debarring; noun debarment. (Sister branches from the same stage include English bar "a rod, a barrier; the legal bar; a counter" and bar "to forbid," and French barreau "the legal profession," all from the same Old French barre.) Note on debate: no PIE ancestry can be securely reconstructed for *barra. The word entered late Latin's vulgar register from an unknown, likely Celtic (Gaulish) source, so the derivation is treated as a loan/substrate item rather than an Indo-European inheritance. Beyond the substrate identification, the deeper origin of *barra is unknown and should not be guessed.
