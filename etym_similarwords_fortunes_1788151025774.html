• Luck: Similar as the basic noun of chance; differs because luck is a single stroke or general serendipity, whereas fortunes is the accumulated, developing condition of a life or enterprise.
  • Chance: Similar as pure contingency; differs because chance is impersonal and static (an event’s probability), whereas fortunes narrates how chance plays out over time.
  • Fate / Destiny: Similar as words for what lies ahead of a person; differ because fate and destiny imply necessity, decree or providence, while fortunes implies hazard open to the turns of events.
  • Circumstances: Similar in naming the condition of one’s affairs; differs because circumstances is factual and neutral, while fortunes adds the colour of luck and change.
  • Prospects: Similar as projections of one’s future condition; differs because prospects look forward from the present, while fortunes looks both backward and forward over an unfolding history.
  • Wealth / Property: Similar in the monetary sense; differ because wealth and property are static stocks of goods, while fortunes is dynamic — it rises, falls, is made and lost.
  • Weal / Welfare: Similar as terms for the good of one’s condition; differ as formal or archaic words for well-being that carry no sense of chance or reversal.
  • Lot: Similar as the share of life allotted to one ("one’s lot"); differs because lot is passive and fixed by assignment, whereas fortunes is active, variable and luck-driven.
  • Vicissitudes: Similar as the ups and downs of affairs; differs in register — vicissitudes is the grand Latinate term for turning points, while fortunes is plainer and covers prosperity as well as reversal.
