- [1st–5th c. CE / Roman Britain] - site guarded by the Roman Saxon Shore fort of *Portus Adurni* (Portchester Castle) at the head of the harbor - [perception: viewed as a strategic frontier bastion defending the Solent against Frankish and Saxon maritime raiders]
- [c. 501–10th c. / Anglo-Saxon Era] - recorded in the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle* as *Portesmuþa*, associated in early chronicles with an eponymous Saxon chieftain *Port* who landed there - [perception: understood as a vital coastal estuary and river mouth providing shelter to raiding longships]
- [1194 / Angevin Era] - granted a royal charter of incorporation by King Richard the Lionheart, who established a permanent naval station, docks, and annual fair - [perception: valued by the English Crown as the supreme strategic embarkation port for military campaigns to Normandy and Aquitaine]
- [15th–16th c. / Tudor Dynasty] - transformed by Henry VII (who built the world's first dry dock in 1495) and Henry VIII into the permanent headquarters of the Royal Navy - [perception: hailed as the fortified maritime shield of England against French and Spanish invasion fleets]
- [18th–19th c. / Pax Britannica] - modernized into the world's greatest naval dockyard and an industrial powerhouse, home to HMS *Victory* and pioneer of mechanized assembly lines - [perception: celebrated as the beating heart of the British Empire's global naval dominion and industrial might]
- [1940–1945 / Second World War] - heavily devastated by Nazi aerial bombing in the Portsmouth Blitz due to its naval docks, later serving as the primary embarkation staging point for D-Day (Operation Overlord) - [perception: revered as a heroic, war-scarred bastion of national survival and liberation]
- [Post-War to 21st c. / Modern Era] - rebuilt as a vibrant commercial ferry port, naval surface base, and university city, retaining its historic identity as the home of the Royal Navy - [perception: recognized internationally as a world-class maritime heritage destination and premier naval station]
