Semitic root *ḥ-r-b "to be dry, waste, desolate" (Hebrew ḥārab "to be dried up, desolated"; noun ḥorbā "waste, ruin"; Arabic ḫariba "to be ruined, deserted" and ḫarāb "ruin, waste"). → Biblical Hebrew proper noun Ḥōrēḇ (חֹרֵב) "dry place, waste, desert" — the name of the mountain of God (Exodus 3, 17; 1 Kings 19; Deuteronomy; c. 9th–6th c. BCE strata). → Ancient Greek Χωρήβ Chōrēb (Septuagint transliteration, 3rd c. BCE). → Latin Horeb and the variant Oreb (Jerome's Vulgate, c. 405 CE). → Miltonic English OREB (Paradise Lost I.7, 1667), the Vulgate-derived form without the initial H, alongside the standard English Horeb. Debated / nuanced: the derivation from ḥ-r-b "to be dry" is not seriously disputed and fits the desert setting; the name is a transparent substantive, "dry place." The only nuance is orthographic: the Hebrew ḥeth (ח) was rendered ch- (Chōrēb) in Greek, h- in the standard Latin/English Horeb, but was lost in the variant Oreb, which reflects a Latin spelling without the initial aspiration that Milton adopted for the meter and sound of his verse. The alternative name Sinai (q.v.) is a separate, differently derived name of the same mountain.
