- Early 14th Century: Denoted physical prowess, martial strength, and fierce bodily hardiness in battle or labor - Connoted heroic knightly power, sturdy peasant constitution, and intense physical resilience.
- 15th to 16th Century: Broadened to natural growth, vegetable life, and medicinal vitality (the vigorous sap of trees, vigorous herbs) - Associated with flourishing health, organic abundance, and the verdant power of nature.
- 17th to 18th Century: Applied extensively to the intellect, rhetoric, and literary style (vigorous reasoning, vigorous imagination, vigorous satire) - Celebrated as the hallmark of manly, disciplined, and persuasive neoclassical prose, contrasting sharply with effeminate or diffuse verbiage.
- 19th Century: Expanded into political campaigning, imperial governance, and industrial expansion (a vigorous administration, vigorous foreign policy) - Carried strong connotations of assertiveness, decisive imperial will, and productive enterprise.
- 20th Century: Standardized in physiological and athletic science (vigorous exercise, vigorous training) and legal advocacy (vigorous defense) - Evokes disciplined cardiovascular fitness, legal tenacity, and uncompromising constitutional activism.
- 21st Century: Frequently used in economic reporting, biomedical immunology, and corporate strategy - Signifies rapid recovery, competitive aggression, and robust, adaptive capacity.
