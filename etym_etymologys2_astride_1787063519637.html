ROOT 1: Prefix a- (from on / in) Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE) *en "in, on, into"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE) *ana "on, upon, at"
↓
Old English (c. 800) an / on "on, in, onto"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200) a- (proclitic prefix) "in a state or position of"
↓

ROOT 2: Base stem stride Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE) *streydʰ- / *strey- "to stretch, spread, walk wide"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE) *strīdaną "to strive, step broadly, stride"
↓
Old English (c. 9th c.) strīdan / stride "to straddle, take long steps"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.) striden / stride "a long step; to straddle"
↓
COMPOUND FORMATION: Early Modern English (c. 1590s) ASTRIDE "with legs straddled on each side"
