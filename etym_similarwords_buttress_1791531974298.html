• Support: The general word for holding something up; it is broader and can be temporary or personal, whereas a buttress is a substantial external structure.
  • Prop: A rigid object placed under or against something to hold it; it is usually temporary and smaller, while a buttress is built in and permanent.
  • Shore: A beam or post set obliquely to hold up a wall or ship; it is similar in function but implies a temporary or nautical remedy.
  • Brace: A piece of timber or metal that stiffens a framework; it resists movement rather than carrying weight, and is lighter than a buttress.
  • Stay: A rope, rod or strut that holds something in position; it is tension-based and slighter than masonry.
  • Strut: A compression member in a frame; it is an engineering term, precise and usually internal.
  • Pier: A vertical support between arches or openings; it is similar masonry but is free-standing rather than a thickening of the wall.
  • Abutment: The structure that takes the thrust of a bridge or arch at its end; it is more specific and usually larger.
  • Pilaster: A shallow rectangular column attached to a wall; it may be decorative and need not resist thrust.
  • Counterfort: A buttress hidden in an earth bank or retaining wall; it is a technical synonym with a narrower application.
  • Reinforcement: Material added to strengthen a structure; it is generic and modern, often internal (steel bars), unlike external masonry.
  • Mainstay: The chief support of a person or institution; it is figurative and personal, with a nautical origin.
  • Pillar: A tall vertical support, often free-standing; it is similar in strength but not in the specialised function of resisting lateral thrust.
  • Bolster: To strengthen or improve, often with encouragement; it is lighter and more abstract than buttress.
  • Underpin: To support from below, literally or figuratively; it acts vertically, whereas a buttress acts sideways against thrust.
  • Strengthen: The plain verb for making stronger; it is neutral, while buttress implies external reinforcement against pressure.
  • Reinforce: To add strength or numbers; it is broader and common in military and psychological contexts.
  • Corroborate: To confirm a claim with evidence; it is purely evidential and cannot describe masonry.
  • Substantiate: To provide evidence or substance for an assertion; it is formal and abstract, without the structural image.
