• Logic: Similar because both involve structured reasoning and the evaluation of propositions; differs because logic focuses on formal rules of valid inference and consistency, whereas dialectics embraces contradiction, dialogic inquiry, and dynamic developmental tension.
  • Rhetoric: Similar because both are ancient arts of verbal discourse; differs because rhetoric aims to persuade, charm, or move an audience regardless of absolute truth, whereas dialectics seeks philosophical illumination and truth through critical cross-examination.
  • Polemic: Similar because both involve contentious or opposing viewpoints; differs because a polemic is an aggressive, one-sided verbal attack or passionate defense of a stance without any cooperative desire for mutual synthesis or objective discovery.
  • Disputation: Similar because both denote structured academic debates on conflicting theses; differs because disputation is often an institutionalized, ritualized scholastic exercise, whereas dialectics is a broad epistemological and ontological framework.
  • Casuistry: Similar because both resolve complex ethical or theoretical tensions; differs because casuistry applies general moral rules to specific practical cases through case-based reasoning, often carrying pejorative connotations of specious quibbling.
