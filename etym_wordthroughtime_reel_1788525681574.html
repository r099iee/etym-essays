- [Old English (c. 700–1100)] - hrēol named the domestic Saxon wooden rotary winder for winding yarn into skeins - [connotation: practical hearthside spinning and textile craftsmanship]
- [Middle English (c. 1150–1500)] - rele / reel maintained its textile spool definition and developed the physical verb meaning to whirl or rotate - [connotation: domestic labor and circular physical rotation]
- [Late 16th c.] - reel expanded into the verb "to reel" (stagger, sway drunkenly or dizzily) and named the lively Scottish folk dance (Gaelic righil) - [connotation: physical instability, festive energy, and high-speed Celtic dancing]
- [18th–19th c.] - invention of the mechanical fishing reel and industrial sewing bobbins; Oscar Wilde used it in The Nightingale and the Rose for domestic silk winding - [connotation: delicate domestic craft, outdoor angling sport, and tactile gentility]
- [20th c.] - cinematography adopted reel as the universal spool and unit of motion picture film (newsreels, film reels, highlight reels) - [connotation: cinema culture, media documentation, and visual storytelling]
- [21st c.] - digital social media platforms transformed reel into short-form algorithmic video clips (Instagram Reels) - [connotation: fast-paced digital content, mobile entertainment, and social media connectivity]
