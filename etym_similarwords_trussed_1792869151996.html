• Bound: Similar because both mean tied fast with cord; differs because bound is general and still current, while trussed is tighter, neater, and faintly old-fashioned, a bundle made competent.
  • Tied: Similar because both secure with knots; differs because tied is the plain everyday word for laces and strings, while trussed implies a whole body or pack comprehensively mastered.
  • Lashed: Similar because both bind firmly, often for travel or storm; differs because lashed stresses violent strapping against motion, cargo lashed on deck, while trussed stresses neat compacting into a carryable whole.
  • Strapped: Similar because both fasten with bands for carrying; differs because strapped names the means, leather or webbing straps, while trussed names the finished bundle, whatever the cord.
  • Bundled: Similar because both gather loose things into a pack; differs because bundled is hurried and soft, clothes bundled into a case, while trussed is deliberate and hard, cord hauled taut and knotted.
  • Packed: Similar because trussed packs are packed packs; differs because packed fills the inside, clothes in a case, while trussed masters the outside, straps ruling the bundle's shape.
  • Swaddled: Similar because both wrap and bind close; differs because swaddled is tender, babies and the injured wrapped soft, while trussed is workmanlike, cord and skewer rather than flannel.
  • Pinioned: Similar because both bind limbs or wings fast; differs because pinioned is literary and martial, arms pinioned behind backs, while trussed is kitchen and campsite, fowls and packs.
