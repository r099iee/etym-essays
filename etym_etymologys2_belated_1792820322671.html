PIE (reconstructed)
*lēd-
‘slow, weary, to linger’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*lataz
‘slow, sluggish, idle’
↓
Old English (9th c.)
læt
‘slow, tardy, negligent’
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
late
‘after the proper time; slow’
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1600, Shakespeare)
belated
‘overtaken by night; delayed’
be- + late + -ed
↓
Modern English (current)
belated
‘arriving too late; overdue’

Note: the PIE root behind Germanic *lataz is sparsely attested outside Germanic and is debated; Pokorny proposed *lēd-, to leave off or be weary, but some scholars leave Old English læt without a certain deeper root. The English-internal chain from læt onward is fully secure.
