- Early 17th c. (c. 1610s–1650s) — Early Modern theological & metaphysical coining: formed in English prose by affixing -ude to Latin infinitus; primarily used in theological treatises to articulate the boundless, unsearchable nature of God and divine attributes.
- Late 17th–18th c. — Enlightenment natural philosophy: adopted by Newtonian natural philosophers and essayists (such as Samuel Clarke and John Locke) to ponder the boundless extension of physical space and absolute time in a newly decentered universe.
- 19th c. — Romantic Sublime & Victorian literature: elevated into an aesthetic keyword by poets (Wordsworth, Shelley) and novelists (Melville, Carlyle), denoting emotional awe before oceanic expanses, mountain heights, or endless urban avenues lost in twilight.
- 20th–21st c. — Poetic, speculative, and contemporary literary register: maintained as an exquisite stylistic alternative to mathematical infinity, especially in science fiction, philosophical prose, and evocative descriptions of psychological depth, starry skies, or sprawling architecture.
