Chain for the prefix ex- ("out of"):

  • Proto-Indo-European *h1eghs "out, out of, from" (reconstructed; cognates across the family).
  • Latin ex "out of, from" (attested from the earliest Latin records, c. 6th century BCE; also Greek ek/ex, Old Irish ess-, Slavic iz).
  • Latin prefix ex- (as in excludere, exire); continued as the English prefix ex-.
  • English ex- + aptation → exaptation (coined by Gould & Vrba, 1982).

Chain for -aptation ("a fitting"):

  • Proto-Indo-European *h2ep- "to grasp, reach, fasten, fit" (reconstructed; Sanskrit ap- "to reach, obtain," Greek haptein "to fasten, touch, kindle").
  • Latin apiscor (present of apisci) "to reach, attain" (attested 2nd–1st century BCE).
  • Latin aptus "fitted, joined, suited" — past participle of the same verb (attested 1st century BCE).
  • Latin aptare "to fit, adjust" (attested 1st century BCE); Late Latin adaptare "to fit to" (attested 2nd century CE).
  • Neo-Latin adaptatio "a fitting to" (scholarly Latin); via French adaptation to English adaptation (17th century).
  • English aptation — Gould & Vrba's umbrella term for any fit (1982), back-formed from adaptation.
  • ex- + aptation → exaptation (coined 1982, Gould & Vrba, Paleobiology 8:4).

Note: the coinage is fully documented (a single 1982 paper); the reconstruction *h2ep- is standard (LIV, de Vaan), while the laryngeal details of *h1eghs remain debated among Indo-Europeanists.
