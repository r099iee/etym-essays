• Adviser / Advisor: Similar because both name a person who gives advice; differs because adviser is neutral and transactional, naming a professional role without implying personal trust or shared deliberation, whereas counsellor implies a standing relationship of closeness and trust.
  • Consultant: Similar because both supply expert guidance; differs because a consultant is an external, typically paid specialist confined to a defined area of expertise, while a counsellor implies a continuing relationship of trust.
  • Mentor: Similar because both guide another's judgment; differs because a mentor develops a person by example and encouragement over time, while a counsellor is summoned into a specific deliberation and shares responsibility for the decision.
  • Counsel: Similar because both share the same root and the same vocabulary of advice; differs because counsel is the advice itself or the body of legal advisers, while the counsellor is the individual person who gives it.
  • Advocate: Similar because both may appear in legal settings; differs because an advocate pleads a client's cause in court, while a counsellor gives advice and guidance rather than pleading.
  • Attorney / lawyer: Similar because both may render legal counsel; differs because attorney and lawyer are titles of the legal profession (with power to act for a client), while counsellor is broader and applies to advice in any field, with “counsellor-at-law” as the specifically legal form.
  • Confidant / confidante: Similar because both denote a trusted person; differs because a confidant is trusted with secrets and private feelings, while a counsellor is trusted with decisions and guidance.
  • Guide: Similar because both lead and direct another; differs because a guide shows the way (physically or figuratively) without necessarily deliberating with the guided, while a counsellor enters into the deliberation itself.
  • Guru: Similar because both are authoritative sources of guidance; differs because guru has a spiritual, Eastern, and often uncritical flavour, while counsellor is a secular, practical, and professional word.
  • Right hand / right-hand man: Similar because both denote an indispensable trusted assistant; differs because a right hand is a helper who executes, while a counsellor advises and shares in judgment.
  • Therapist: Similar because both may appear in modern professional guidance; differs because a therapist treats emotional or psychological distress in a clinical setting, while a counsellor advises on decisions and conduct, though the two increasingly overlap (counselling psychology).
  • Oracle: Similar because both are sources of authoritative judgment; differs because an oracle is supernaturally inspired and consulted for predictions, while a counsellor is a human adviser giving reasoned advice — the comparison in Mill's “almost infallible counsellor” is deliberately between the two.
