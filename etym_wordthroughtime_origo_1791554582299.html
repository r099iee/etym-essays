- Classical Roman Antiquity: Tangible rising, fountainhead, and ancestral lineage - Encompassed the rising sun, the spring of a river, and noble birth; Cato named his foundational Roman history Origines.
- Late Antiquity & Middle Ages: Encyclopedic etymology and cosmological beginning - Isidore of Seville used Origines as the alternate title for his masterwork on the linguistic and cosmic roots of civilization.
- Early Modern & Enlightenment: Historiographical, legal, and philological usage - Denoted primal national or legal origins in jurisprudence, cartography, and humanist scholarship.
- 19th to 21st Century: Specialized biological, anatomical, and lexicographical citation - Retained in anatomy (muscle origo vs. insertio), international conflict of laws (domicile of origo), and dictionary etymology entries.
