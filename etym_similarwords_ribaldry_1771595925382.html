• Bawdry: Similar because it is sexually explicit or indecent humour; differs because bawdry is often playful, earthy, and even affectionate — Chaucer's fabliaux are bawdy — whereas ribaldry is deliberately irreverent and jeering, contemptuous rather than fond.
  • Vulgarity: Similar because it is coarseness and bad taste; differs because vulgarity is the broadest term and may be wholly unconscious, whereas ribaldry is a deliberate, contemptuous debasement of speech aimed at something.
  • Profanity: Similar because it is improper speech; differs because profanity concerns forbidden words as such — swearing, taking holy things in vain — while ribaldry is not a matter of vocabulary but of a debased, mocking attitude.
  • Obscenity: Similar because it is indecent speech; differs because obscenity is defined by indecent or sexually explicit content, whereas ribaldry is defined by jeering irreverence, which may or may not be sexual.
  • Derision: Similar because it is contemptuous mockery; differs because derision names targeted ridicule generally, without the defining low-life register that makes ribaldry the scoundrel's tongue.
  • Mockery: Similar because it is ridicule of something; differs because mockery is neutral as to register — it can be witty or gentle — whereas ribaldry is always coarse and low.
  • Sarcasm: Similar because it is cutting, scornful speech; differs because sarcasm is verbal irony with a bite, often clever and pointed, whereas ribaldry is blunt, coarse, gutter-level derision without irony's refinement.
  • Satire: Similar because it attacks follies and vices; differs because satire corrects through wit and has a moral aim, whereas ribaldry merely degrades — satire is ribaldry's respectable cousin.
  • Raillery: Similar because it is playful teasing; differs because raillery is good-humoured, jesting mockery among equals, whereas ribaldry is contemptuous and degrading, aimed at dragging its object down.
  • Scurrility: Similar because it is foul-mouthed abuse; differs because scurrility is abusive or defamatory language in general — the language of the buffoon — while ribaldry emphasises coarse, irreverent jesting and mockery.
