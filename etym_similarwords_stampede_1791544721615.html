• Rout: Similar because both involve a panicked, disorganized mass flight from danger; differs because a rout specifically denotes the catastrophic, disorderly retreat or total defeat of a military army or fighting force, whereas a stampede is driven by blind animal or crowd terror.
  • Surge: Similar because both describe a sudden, powerful, forward-moving rush of a collective body; differs because a surge is often purposeful, steady, and wave-like (and can apply to water, electricity, or organized crowds) without the chaotic, terror-driven trampling of a stampede.
  • Flight: Similar because both describe an urgent attempt to escape an immediate hazard; differs because flight is a general, neutral term that can be individual or organized, whereas a stampede is inherently collective, chaotic, and headlong.
  • Crush: Similar because both refer to dangerous, high-density human crowd phenomena that can cause physical harm; differs because a crush emphasizes severe compressive pressure and lack of physical space in a stationary crowd, whereas a stampede emphasizes rapid, uncontrolled forward movement and momentum.
  • Rush: Similar because both involve rapid, urgent collective motion toward or away from an objective; differs because a rush can be intentional, cheerful, or orderly (such as a gold rush or holiday shopping rush), lacking the blind terror and destructive chaos typical of a stampede.
  • Exodus: Similar because both describe a mass departure from a location; differs because an exodus is a prolonged, deliberate, large-scale migration of a people over time, rather than an instantaneous, frantic sprint.
  • Bandwagon (Figurative): Similar because both describe people collectively joining a popular movement in overwhelming numbers; differs because bandwagon emphasizes opportunistic conformism and desire to join a winning side, whereas a figurative stampede implies sudden, uncritical, panicked momentum.
