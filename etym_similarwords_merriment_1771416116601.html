• Mirth: Similar because both denote cheerful amusement and laughter; differs because mirth is lighter, wittier, and can be restrained or silent, while merriment is noisier, more festive, and requires an audience.
  • Glee: Similar because both denote high-spirited delight; differs because glee is a sudden, sometimes childlike or slightly malicious burst of delight, while merriment is sustained, shared, and social.
  • Hilarity: Similar because both denote loud amusement; differs because hilarity is extreme, boisterous, near-uncontrollable laughter, while merriment is milder, warmer, and more communal.
  • Joy: Similar because both denote happiness; differs because joy is a profound internal state that can be felt alone in silence, while merriment is outward, noisy, and social.
  • Happiness: Similar because both denote a positive emotional state; differs because happiness is a broad, sustained condition of well-being and contentment, while merriment is a specific, festive, shared amusement.
  • Gaiety: Similar because both denote bright, playful cheer; differs because gaiety is lighter and can be a general quality of cheerfulness, while merriment is more festal, more audible, and more collective.
  • Jollity: Similar because both denote cheerful good spirits; differs because jollity emphasizes a hearty, festive, often boozy good cheer, while merriment is broader and need not imply eating or drinking.
  • Festivity: Similar because both are bound up with celebration; differs because festivity names the celebratory occasion or atmosphere itself, while merriment names the joyful amusement that fills it.
  • Revelry: Similar because both denote lively group celebration; differs because revelry emphasizes boisterous feasting, drinking, and carousing, often at night, while merriment is warmer and less excessive.
  • Amusement: Similar because both denote enjoyment and entertainment; differs because amusement is a general and often passive reaction to something funny, while merriment is active, social, and festive.
  • Conviviality: Similar because both denote the friendly cheer of a social gathering; differs because conviviality emphasizes sociability and companionship, while merriment emphasizes laughter and high spirits.
  • Cheerfulness: Similar because both denote high spirits; differs because cheerfulness is a steady, light disposition or mood, while merriment is an active, often noisy outbreak of shared joy.
  • Laughter: Similar because merriment is expressed in laughter; differs because laughter names the physical sound itself, while merriment names the festive state of mind that produces it.
  • Frolic: Similar because both denote playful high spirits; differs because frolic emphasizes carefree play and movement, while merriment emphasizes shared amusement and celebration.
