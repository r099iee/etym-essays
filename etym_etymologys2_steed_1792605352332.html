Note on semantic specialization: English steed and stud are morphological twins derived from the Proto-Germanic collective noun *stōdą ("a place where horses stand together for breeding", from PIE *steh₂-). In Old English, stōd denoted the horse farm or herd, while stēda (formed with an agentive suffix causing i-mutation of vowel ō to ē) designated the virile stallion heading that stud. During the high feudal era, the word crossed over from the manor paddock to the tournament lists and the battlefield, because chivalric shock tactics required uncastrated stallions for their physical mass and combat aggression. Over several centuries, steed became thoroughly romanticized, while stud retained the original breeding and equine establishment senses.
