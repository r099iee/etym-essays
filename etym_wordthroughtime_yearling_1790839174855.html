- 9th–11th c. (Old English) - Recorded as *gēarling* / *ġēarlic*, denoting an animal one year old; essential for Anglo-Saxon herd valuation and tithing.
- 12th–15th c. (Middle English) - Solidified as *yerling* / *yeerlyng* in sheep-farming, wool-trade documents, and manor court rolls across medieval England.
- 16th–18th c. (Early Modern English) - Expanded into formal cattle ranching and equestrian breeding treatises as a vital milestone in livestock development.
- 19th c. (Victorian Equine Trade & Racing) - Canonized in Thoroughbred racing rules (where all Northern Hemisphere horses celebrate their official birthday on January 1, turning into yearlings).
- 20th–21st c. (Contemporary Global English) - High-stakes commercial term in multimillion-dollar Thoroughbred yearling sales (Keeneland, Tattersalls) and wildlife biology.
