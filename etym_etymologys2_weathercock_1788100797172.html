"to blow"
↓

*wedrą
"wind, weather; storm"
↓

weder

↓

weder, wedir, wether, weather

↓

weather

Chain 3 — the compound
↓
Middle English (by the 15th c.)
wedercok, weather-cocke
"cock-shaped wind vane on a steeple"
↓
Modern English (16th c. onward)
weathercock
"pivoted wind-direction vane, usu. cock-shaped; a fickle person"
↓
English (this card)
weathercock

Note: the origin of Old English cocc is uncertain — the OED treats it as probably imitative of the bird's call; Old Norse kokkr and Old French coq are formally similar but no Germanic–Romance link is established. The PIE root *we- / *h₂weh₁- 'to blow' also underlies wind and the Latin ventus. The compound's first recorded date varies across dictionaries (14th–15th centuries); its parts are each documented by c. 1000 CE.
