pesky means annoyingly troublesome, irritatingly persistent, or vexatiously trivial — describing a person, animal, insect, task, or detail that will not go away and that provokes impatience out of all proportion to its importance. It is mildly contemptuous but fundamentally comic and colloquial rather than hateful: a pesky fly, pesky weeds, pesky bills, pesky questions, a pesky younger brother, a pesky ornament that gets in the way.

Your sentence uses the classic dismissive construction: prose style dismissed as a peripheral discipline, a pesky ornament which gets in the way of reality. Here pesky performs ideological work — it makes style sound like a mosquito, trivial yet buzzing, something busy executives rightly leave to secretaries. The book's whole argument is that this dismissal is wrong: information style is not peripheral but central. Grasping the sneer in pesky is thus essential to grasping the passage.

Core sense today is persistent minor annoyance. Pesky implies repetition and evasiveness: flies return when swatted, weeds regrow, pop-ups reappear, coughs linger, regulations entangle. Unlike dangerous or serious, it denies importance; unlike boring, it implies active irritation. You can be bored once, but something pesky nags.

  • Insects and animals: pesky mosquitoes, flies, wasps, pigeons, squirrels — creatures that intrude, bite, steal, chatter. The prototypical use; often affectionate-exasperated.
  • Weeds, colds, pains, glitches: pesky dandelions, pesky cough, pesky backache, pesky software bug — bodily or domestic irritants that resist cure and return.
  • People, especially children and subordinates: pesky kid, pesky neighbour, pesky reporter, pesky salesman — someone importunate with questions, demands, interruptions. Usually fond rather than vicious; adults call children pesky when half-amused.
  • Tasks and paperwork: pesky forms, details, bills, emails, chores — trivial but unavoidable jobs that clog the day. Your pesky ornament belongs here metaphorically: style treated as fussy extra work.
  • Abstract obstacles: pesky doubts, pesky facts, pesky technicalities, pesky qualifications — small objections that spoil a grand theory. Lawyers and scholars constantly swat pesky counterexamples.
  • Attributive7916 dismissive use: a pesky liberal, pesky regulation, pesky conscience — political slang that shrinks opponents to nuisances, avoiding substantive rebuttal.

Syntax is almost always attributive adjective before noun: a pesky fly, those pesky kids. Predicative use (the fly is pesky) is possible but less idiomatic. Comparative and superlative are regular: peskier, peskiest, though often avoided in formal prose. Intensifiers cluster: damned pesky, pesky little, pesky old. Register is informal American, now universal in spoken English, journalism, blogs, and children's books. It would be out of place in statutes or solemn obituaries precisely because it trivializes. Collocates: pesky fly, mosquito, wasp, weed, cold, cough, kid, brother, neighbour, detail, problem, bill, law, varmint.

Connotation is key: pesky expresses the speaker's impatience more than the object's nature. Calling style a pesky ornament reveals the executive worldview that reality is hard stuff and words are decoration. The word's very cuteness — with its -sky jingle like frisky, husky, pesky — undercuts its complaint, admitting the trouble is small. Hence skilled writers use it ironically, as here, quoting the dismissive voice in order to refute it.
