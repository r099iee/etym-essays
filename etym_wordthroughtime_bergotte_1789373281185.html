- [1913] - Debuts in Marcel Proust's Du côté de chez Swann as the narrator's revered literary idol and master of lyrical French prose - [connotation: enchanted aesthetic wisdom, pure artistic idealism, stylistic perfection]
- [1919] - Deepened in À l'ombre des jeunes filles en fleurs as Marcel meets the physical author, exposing the stark divide between social vulgarity and literary genius - [connotation: cognitive dissonance, vanity, the dual nature of the creative artist]
- [1923] - Featured in the post-mortem publication of La Prisonnière in his iconic death scene before Vermeer's View of Delft - [connotation: aesthetic martyrdom, humility before true genius, artistic immortality]
- [Mid-to-Late 20th c.] - Canonised in modern literary theory and philosophy of art (Beckett, Benjamin, Deleuze, Genette) as the supreme archetype of the Proustian artist - [connotation: philosophical benchmark, the yellow wall of artistic duty]
- [21st c.] - Invoked universally in literary discourse to describe the transcendence of artistic creation over mortal fragility - [connotation: quintessential literary master, timeless monument to the written word]
