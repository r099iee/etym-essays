- Pre-1498 - Known to indigenous Arawak- and Carib-speaking populations as *Iëre* (or *Kairi*), meaning 'Land of the Hummingbird' or simply 'Island'.
- 1498–18th Century - Renamed *La Isla de la Santísima Trinidad* by Christopher Columbus on his third voyage, functioning as a sparsely populated Spanish frontier territory characterized by cacao farming and Christian missions.
- 1797–19th Century - Captured by the British Empire; transformed into a major sugar and plantation colony reliant on enslaved African labor and, following abolition in 1834, massive influxes of Indian indentured laborers.
- 20th–21st Century - Gained independence in 1962 (and became a republic in 1976 united with Tobago), establishing itself as an economic and cultural hub renowned for petroleum, steel, calypso, steelpan, and literature (e.g., Nobel laureate V. S. Naipaul).
