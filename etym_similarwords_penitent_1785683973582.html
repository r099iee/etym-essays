• Repentant: Similar because both mean feeling sorrow for wrongdoing; differs because it is the closest moral twin — more ethical and less sacramental, naming a change of mind about sin without necessarily implying the discipline of penance.
  • Contrite: Similar because both mean sorrowful for one's sins; differs because it stresses the crushing of the heart — a more intense, inward, private grief — a broken person, not a kneeling, working one.
  • Remorseful: Similar because both mean painfully regretful for one's deeds; differs because it is secular and often self-focused, describing the gnawing pang of conscience rather than any outward act of atonement.
  • Rueful: Similar because both express regret; differs because it is lighter and wistful, tinged with regret for a lost opportunity or a small mishap rather than for sin.
  • Apologetic: Similar because both express regret to another person; differs because it is social and perfunctory — the register of the drawing-room — while penitent is sacramental, performed before God with the resolve of penance.
  • Sorry: Similar because both express regret; differs because sorry is the ordinary, weakened everyday word covering everything from a spilled cup to a grave fault, stripped of the religious frame and the machinery of penance that penitent carries.
  • Shamefaced: Similar because both name the demeanour of a wrongdoer; differs because shamefaced stresses visible embarrassment and lowered countenance, while penitent stresses active, performed sorrow and the resolve to amend.
  • Compunctious: Similar because both name pangs of conscience; differs because compunctious is rare, literary, and purely inward, naming the sting of conscience with no suggestion of religious practice, whereas penitent implies the whole sacramental economy of confession and penance.
  • Atoning: Similar because both imply making amends for wrong; differs because atoning emphasizes the act of reparation itself, while penitent emphasizes the inner state of sorrow that seeks and performs it.
  • Regretful: Similar because both mean feeling sorrow about something done; differs because regretful is general, mild, and secular, applicable to any disappointment, while penitent is deep, religious, and bound to wrongdoing and its remedy.
