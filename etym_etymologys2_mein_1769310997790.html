Origin uncertain (leading theory — Celtic base): Gaulish/Celtic *min(o)- “muzzle, snout, beak, brow, face” (pre-Roman, reconstructed; cf. Breton min “beak, snout”, Welsh min “edge, lip, brink”) → Old French mine “appearance, look, countenance, facial expression” (12th c.; origin itself debated — possibly a clipping of Old French demener “to conduct oneself”, or from the Celtic base, or connected by older scholars with Latin minārī “to threaten, to drive cattle with shouts”) → Middle English mene/miene “appearance, aspect, look” (1515; borrowed from Middle French) → English mein / mein ◄ TARGET (variant spelling of the standard mien, 16th c.).

Note on the debated stage: every stage before English is contested. The OED labels French mine of uncertain origin; the Celtic and demener hypotheses are the two leading accounts, and the older Latin minārī connection is now largely abandoned. The word is not known to descend from Proto-Indo-European by any secure chain.
