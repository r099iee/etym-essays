- 1540s — coined in English from lunatic + -cy, at first simply “the condition of being a lunatic.”
- 16th–17th c. — becomes a legal and medical category, with the moon-madness theory still audible inside the word.
- 18th–19th c. — the age of asylums makes “lunacy” the bureaucratic term of the system: commissions, certificates and the Lunacy Act of 1845.
- Late 19th–20th c. — psychiatry rejects the word; “mental illness” and “mental disorder” take over, and “lunacy” survives in legal archaism and journalism.
- 20th–21st c. — chiefly figurative: reckless folly, “sheer lunacy,” now widely considered offensive when applied to people rather than plans.
