• Van: Similar because it is the same word clipped - the front of an army or of an enterprise; differs because van is shorter, chiefly idiom-fixed ('to lead the van'), and less weighty in figurative use.
  • Avant-garde: Similar because it is the same French compound re-borrowed - the leading edge of a movement; differs because avant-garde is confined to art and culture and implies deliberate radicalism, while vanguard ranges over armies, politics, science, and business.
  • Advance guard: Similar because it is the plain modern military term for troops that go before the army; differs because it is exclusively tactical and never figurative.
  • Forefront: Similar because both denote the front position of any endeavour; differs because forefront is positional only ('at the forefront'), lacks the military body-of-men sense, and cannot mean troops.
  • Front line / front: Similar because both denote the position of first contact with the enemy or the issue; differs because the front is the whole zone of contact, not the leading edge moving ahead of the main force.
  • Spearhead: Similar because both name the leading, most exposed element of an attack or movement; differs because spearhead stresses the sharp, piercing, pointed character and is usually singular and abstract ('the spearhead of the campaign').
  • Cutting edge / leading edge: Similar because both name the furthest point of technological or intellectual advance; differs because they describe technology and ideas, not organised groups, and are metaphors of blades and wings rather than armies.
  • Trailblazer: Similar because both denote someone who goes first and opens a way for others; differs because trailblazer is an individual metaphor (blazing a forest trail), personal and laudatory, never a formation.
  • Pioneer: Similar because both name those who enter new ground first; differs because the pioneer works alone or in small parties and settles new territory, while the vanguard is the front of an organised force.
  • Forerunner / precursor: Similar because both denote what comes before something else; differs because they imply an omen or earlier version of what follows (the forerunner of X), not the leading element of the same body.
  • Leader: Similar because both name those ahead of the rest; differs because leadership is about command and influence, while the vanguard is about position and exposure - one can lead without being in the vanguard, and vice versa.
  • Head / point: Similar because an army's 'head' or 'point' is its front; differs because these are generic positional words in military idiom, without the figurative movement sense of vanguard.
  • Rearguard (antonym): Similar because it is the mirror-formation of the same march order; differs because it guards the rear and retreats last - its presence is the exact opposite of the exposed, honour-laden front.
