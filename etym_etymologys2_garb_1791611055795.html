Note: English borrowed garb via Middle French from Italian garbo ("grace, pleasing shape"), which historical linguists trace to a Germanic source, likely Lombardic or Old High German *garawi ("equipment, adornment, preparation"), from Proto-Germanic *garwaz ("ready, prepared"). This makes garb a double cousin of native Old English gearu ("ready") and modern English gear. A completely unrelated heraldic English homophone garb (meaning a sheaf of wheat) derives from Old French garbe / gerbe from Frankish *garba.
