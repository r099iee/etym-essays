• Torc: Similar because it is the same object — the Celtic neck-ring of twisted metal; differs because "torc" is the modern archaeological spelling modelled on the Celtic form and preferred in excavation reports, while "torque" is the older English form and also names the physics sense of rotational force.
  • Necklace: Similar because both are ornamental bands worn about the neck; differs because a necklace is the generic term for any ornamental hanging chain or band, while a torque is specifically a rigid, massive, often twisted metal ring of the ancient Celtic type, engineered rather than dangled.
  • Collar: Similar because both are neck-encircling ornaments; differs because "collar" in jewellery is a snug modern band worn at the throat (and in dress is an item of clothing, in zoology a marking), whereas the torque is a historical metal neck-ring of the Iron Age.
  • Chain: Similar because both can be worn about the neck as metal ornaments; differs because a chain is a linked, flexible sequence of loops, whereas a torque is a solid, rigid band, often twisted and open at the throat.
  • Choker: Similar because both are close-fitting neck ornaments; differs because a choker is a tight, often elegant modern necklace worn high on the throat, while a torque is a heavier, ancient, ceremonial metal ring.
  • Carcanet: Similar because both name ornamental neck-wear; differs because a carcanet is a jeweled necklace or collar of Renaissance fashion, whereas a torque is the twisted metal neck-ring of Iron-Age Celts.
  • Gorget: Similar because both concern the throat region; differs because a gorget is a piece of armour protecting the throat (later a decorative collar), defensive equipment, while a torque is an ornament of rank rather than protection.
  • Ring: Similar because both are circular metal objects, and a torque is in essence a great neck-ring; differs because "ring" denotes the finger-ring or any small circular band, and lacks the torque's archaeological specificity and size.
  • Twist / Rotation (physics senses): Similar because torque in mechanics is a twisting or rotational effort, and both words invoke turning; differs because "twist" is the everyday word for a turning deformation, while "torque" is the precise engineering measure of rotational force (force × lever arm), with units of newton-metres.
  • Moment (of force): Similar because "moment" and "torque" both name the rotational effect of a force; differs because "moment" is the broader physical term for the tendency to produce rotation or bending about a point, while "torque" is the everyday engineering word for twisting force about an axis — in mechanics the two are the same quantity.
