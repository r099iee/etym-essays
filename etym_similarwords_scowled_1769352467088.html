• Glower: Similar because it is a hostile, angry look from beneath drawn brows; differs because glower stresses an intense, fixed, menacing stare, while scowl centres on the whole frowning configuration of the face.
  • Frown: Similar because both involve wrinkling the forehead and drawing the brows together; differs because frown can express concentration, worry, or puzzlement, whereas scowl is specifically angry or bad-tempered displeasure.
  • Glare: Similar because both are hostile, aggressive looks; differs because glare is a fierce, wide-eyed, piercing stare, while scowl keeps the eyes narrowed behind lowered brows.
  • Grimace: Similar because both are distorted facial expressions of displeasure; differs because grimace involves twisting the whole face, often in pain or disgust, whereas scowl is the specific frown of anger.
  • Lower / Lour (the brows): Similar because lowering the brows is the physical core of a scowl; differs because lower names only the brow-drop gesture and can also refer to darkening skies, while scowl describes the full angry expression.
  • Pout: Similar because both are sullen expressions of displeasure; differs because pout pushes the lips forward in childish or petulant sulkiness, while scowl is harder, hostile, and more aggressive.
  • Sulk: Similar because both convey sullen, withdrawn ill-humour; differs because sulk is a prolonged mood of resentful silence, while scowl is a momentary visible facial expression.
  • Mope: Similar because both suggest gloomy discontent; differs because mope is passive, dejected listlessness rather than the active, angry frown of a scowl.
  • Look daggers: Similar because both express intense hostility through the eyes; differs because "looking daggers" is a colourful idiom of murderous staring, while scowl is the ordinary word for an angry frown.
  • Knit (one's brows): Similar because knitting the brows produces the same wrinkled forehead and drawn-down eyebrows; differs because knitting the brows can result from deep thought or worry, not necessarily anger.
