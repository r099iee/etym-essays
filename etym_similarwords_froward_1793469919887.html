• Obstinate: Similar because both denote unyielding adherence to a position despite reason or entreaty; differs because obstinate emphasizes fixed, immovable determination (‘standing firm’), whereas froward stresses active, perverse contrariness and deliberate defiance (‘turning away’).
  • Perverse: Similar because both describe a disposition to contradict reason, goodness, or common sense; differs because perverse focuses on psychological distortion and delight in doing the wrong thing, while froward carries a distinctively archaic, biblical flavor of rebellion against righteous guidance.
  • Refractory: Similar because both denote unruliness and resistance to management or control; differs because refractory is commonly used for unresponsive materials, bodily conditions, or patients resisting medical treatment, whereas froward is strictly moral, personal, and spiritual.
  • Intractable: Similar because both describe persons, situations, or problems that cannot be easily guided or manipulated; differs because intractable is formal, analytical, and cold, whereas froward is emotionally charged, evoking personal petulance and moral fault.
  • Wayward: Similar because both trace back historically to spatial deviation from the right path (‘away-ward’); differs because wayward suggests erratic, capricious wandering and unpredictability, whereas froward denotes deliberate, hostile contrariness.
  • Contumacious: Similar because both describe willful resistance to authority; differs because contumacious is a technical legal and ecclesiastical term signifying formal contempt of court or episcopal orders, while froward is a general character trait.
  • Recalcitrant: Similar because both denote kicking against authority or restraint; differs because recalcitrant (literally ‘kicking back with the heels’) highlights kinetic, stubborn refusal to cooperate with a system, whereas froward suggests habitual moral and intellectual contrariness.
  • Cantankerous: Similar because both involve difficult, quarrelsome behavior; differs because cantankerous stresses bad temper, grumpiness, and noisy complaints, while froward emphasizes philosophical and moral contrariness.
  • Untoward: Similar because both share an etymological root in directionality (*toward* vs. *fromward*); differs because untoward has shifted in modern usage to mean improper, unfortunate, or inconvenient (e.g., ‘an untoward incident’), whereas froward remains tied to active, perverse resistance.
