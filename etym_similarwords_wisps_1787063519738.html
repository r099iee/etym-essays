• Streaks: Similar because both name thin lines or traces of something; differs because a streak emphasizes linearity and color, while a wisp emphasizes thinness, filminess, and lightness.
  • Trails: Similar because both are elongated faint traces; differs because a trail implies a path left behind by motion, while a wisp is a loose drifting portion that may not mark a path at all.
  • Tendrils: Similar because both are thin curling strands; differs because a tendril implies curling, clinging growth (of plants or hair), while a wisp is more evanescent and loosely dispersed.
  • Puffs: Similar because both are small amounts of smoke or air; differs because a puff is rounder, discrete, and burst-like, while a wisp is thin, drawn-out, and drifting.
  • Fragments: Similar because both are small portions of a whole; differs because a fragment implies a broken-off piece of something solid, while a wisp is an insubstantial film or trace.
  • Tufts: Similar because both are small gatherings of hair, grass, or fibre; differs because a tuft is more solid, clumped, and rooted, while a wisp is airy and thin.
  • Shreds: Similar because both are thin pieces; differs because a shred implies tearing from cloth, paper, or other material, while a wisp of smoke or hair is softer and unbounded.
  • Will-o'-the-wisp: Similar because it shares the word and the idea of something elusive and deceptive; differs because it is a fixed folkloric compound naming a marsh-light or deceptive illusion, not a synonym for a mere streak of smoke.
