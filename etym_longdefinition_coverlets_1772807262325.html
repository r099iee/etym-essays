In the episode from Honglou meng (The Dream of the Red Chamber) in which Dai-yu first enters the Jia household, Xi-feng has already sent across "some lilac-coloured hangings, brocade quilts, satin coverlets and the like" for Dai-yu's bedding. The plural noun coverlets names the ornamental upper layers of a bed — the finished cloth that lies over the sheets or blankets and completes the visible surface of the bed. In this inventory the word functions as part of a catalogue of textile luxury: hangings for walls or curtains, quilts for warmth, satin coverlets for display. The satin fabric is the decisive clue: coverlets may be plain, but the collocation "satin coverlets" marks them as decorative, status-bearing objects rather than utilitarian sleeping gear. Contextually, the coverlets help construct Dai-yu's new material environment — status made tangible as colour, fabric, and layered softness — rather than merely stating that she has something to sleep under. Core definition. A coverlet is a bedspread, especially a decorative one: a light outer bed covering, usually not reaching the floor, that is spread over the bed when it is not in use and may be removed or turned down at night. In modern retail and interior design, a coverlet is typically a thin, quilted or woven top layer used alone in warm weather or as the visible finishing layer above sheets and a duvet. It is generally lighter than a quilt, less bulky than a comforter, and designed to cover the top of the bed rather than hang to the floor. Polysemy and secondary senses (with their historical bridges):
  • Primary textile sense: the decorative outer covering of a bed; a bedspread. This is the sense in the novel, where satin coverlets are listed among Dai-yu's bedding.
  • Historical / inventory sense (14th–19th centuries): in wills, probate inventories, and domestic fiction, "coverlet(s)" is a standard word for the upper bed-cloth, often quilted, embroidered, or hand-woven, listed alongside sheets, blankets, hangings, and counterpanes. The bridge: because the word names a bed-covering by function ("cover + bed") rather than by material, it travelled easily through changes in bedding technology.
  • Modern retail sense: a specific lightweight bed-cover product, sold as "coverlet" to distinguish it from heavier quilts, comforters, and duvets, often paired with matching shams and a bed skirt. The bridge: the same functional naming survives in a market that now classifies bedding by weight and layering.
  • Figurative / literary extension: the word transfers to any thin covering layer over something — "a coverlet of snow," "a coverlet of leaves," "a coverlet of silence." The bridge: the image of a cloth lying over a bed is generalised to anything that lies over and conceals or protects.
  • Dialectal and historical near-synonymy: in older and British usage "coverlet" approaches "counterpane" and "bedspread," the three being interchangeable in many period inventories.
Boundary comparisons with near-synonyms:
  • bedspread: the modern everyday word for the outer decorative layer of a bed; it typically covers the pillows and hangs lower, and it lacks the literary tinge of coverlet.
  • quilt: a padded, stitched sandwich of two layers of fabric with filling between; a coverlet is generally lighter and need not be padded or quilted.
  • duvet / comforter: thick, filled bed covers usually placed inside a removable cover (duvet) or sold as a single bulky piece (comforter); coverlets are thinner and used over, or without, such pieces.
  • counterpane: the historical and literary near-synonym, now largely archaic; it was used interchangeably with coverlet in older household lists.
  • blanket: primarily a functional warmth layer, often woollen and plain; a coverlet is primarily decorative and lighter.
  • throw: a small decorative blanket for draping on a bed or sofa; a coverlet is a full bed-cover, not a drape.
  • afghan: a knitted or crocheted decorative throw; a coverlet is woven or quilted rather than made in the traditional afghan technique.
Register and collocations. Coverlet is standard to slightly literary; it survives most naturally in descriptive prose, household fiction, hotel and linen-trade vocabulary, and antique-collecting contexts. Common collocations include satin coverlet, silk coverlet, embroidered coverlet, lace coverlet, hand-woven coverlet, quilted coverlet, "pull up the coverlet," and listing phrases such as "quilts, hangings, and coverlets." Because it is a countable noun, the plural coverlets is natural when items are enumerated, exactly as in the novel's inventory of bedding sent for Dai-yu. In everyday modern speech, bedspread, duvet, or comforter is more common; coverlet signals either a lighter decorative product or a deliberately literary, period-flavoured register. The word's own formation — cover + lit ("bed") — keeps it transparently a "bed-cover," which is why, across seven centuries, its meaning has shifted so little while the objects it names have changed so much.
