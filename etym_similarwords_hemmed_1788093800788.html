• Surrounded: Similar because it means enclosed on all sides; differs because it is the neutral general word, with no suggestion of tightness or restraint, while "hemmed" implies a closed ring that leaves little room to move.
  • Encircled: Similar because it means formed in a ring around; differs because it emphasizes the circle's geometry rather than the restriction of movement, and lacks the idea of a containing boundary.
  • Enclosed: Similar because it means shut in; differs because it is static and structural (an enclosed garden, enclosed by walls), not necessarily hostile and not implying an active ring of enemies.
  • Ringed: Similar because it means circled about; differs because it is visual and neutral — "ringed by hills" — while "hemmed" carries the pressure and menace of confinement.
  • Girdled: Similar because it means surrounded as with a belt; differs because it is a literary, narrow word emphasizing a band around the middle, and is rarely used of people or armies.
  • Enveloped: Similar because it means covered or wrapped on all sides; differs because it suggests complete physical covering (mist, darkness, fire), not a perimeter of enemies around a clear space.
  • Penned (in): Similar because it means confined within barriers; differs because it evokes animals in pens, emphasizing helplessness and the physical barrier, while "hemmed" can be purely human or metaphorical.
  • Boxed (in): Similar because it means trapped so movement is impossible; differs because it is colloquial and everyday (boxed in by traffic, by a corner), with no military or archaic weight.
  • Fenced (in): Similar because it means enclosed by barriers; differs because it suggests literal fences or rules explicitly written, rather than the closing of a surrounding ring.
  • Confined: Similar because it means restricted within bounds; differs because it is neutral and static, describing the state of being limited rather than the act of a surrounding ring.
  • Trapped: Similar because it means unable to escape; differs because it focuses on the victim's predicament and the mechanism of capture, not on the surrounding formation of enemies.
  • Besieged: Similar because it means surrounded by enemies; differs because it implies a sustained military siege with attack, isolation and the intention to capture or starve, far heavier than mere hemming-in.
  • Edged / Bordered / Trimmed: Similar because they name the sewing sense of hemmed — finished with a border; differs because they cover only the garment sense, entirely losing the enclosing, confining meaning of the word.
