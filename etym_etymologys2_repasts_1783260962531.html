Stage 1 — PIE *peh₂- "to protect, guard, tend (flocks); by extension to feed, pasture" (the root of the shepherd's care. DEBATED: some accounts reconstruct a separate root for "to graze"; the connection of Latin pāscō to *peh₂- "protect" is standard (de Vaan) but the semantic bridge — guarding by feeding — is a matter of interpretation. Cognates: Greek patéomai "to eat, feed upon", Germanic *fōd- giving English food, fodder, feed, foster). Stage 2 — Latin pāscō, pāscere "to feed, graze, pasture" — literally to put animals out to graze, an inchoative verb centred on the herder's daily task. Stage 3 — Late Latin repāscere "to feed again, to nourish anew, to restore with food" — re- "again" + pāscere (attested in Late Latin). Stage 4 — Latin repastus "fed again" — past participle of repāscere, substantivised as a noun meaning "a feeding, a meal, a refection" (Late Latin). Stage 5 — Old French repast "meal, food, feast" (noun), alongside the verb repaistre "to feed, to eat" (both from the same Late Latin base; Modern French repas "meal"). Stage 6 — Middle English repast "a meal, a taking of food" (14th century, via Anglo-Norman) → Modern English repast "a meal". Stage 7 — Modern English repasts, plural, the exact form preserved in Macaulay's sentence → FINAL NODE: REPASTS.
