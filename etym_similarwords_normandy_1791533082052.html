• Normandie: The French name of the same region; identical in reference, differing only in language and spelling. English Normandy is an anglicisation of the Old French form.
  • Neustria: The Merovingian and Carolingian name for the north-western kingdom between the Loire and the Scheldt, out of which Normandy was carved; similar as a geographical frame, but it is earlier, larger and political rather than ethnically named.
  • Northmen / Norsemen: The people from whom the province takes its name; similar as the source of the toponym, but they are a population, not a place, and the terms cover all Scandinavian raiders and settlers.
  • Norman: The adjective and demonym; identical in root, differing in grammatical function — Norman describes a person or thing, Normandy the territory.
  • Duchy of Normandy: The medieval polity; similar in territory, but it is a jurisdiction and a title, and it included lands (in England and the Channel Islands) outside the modern region.
  • Brittany: The neighbouring north-western peninsula, also named for settlers (Britons); similar as a Celtic-Sea province with a strong regional identity, but its settlers came from Britain, not Scandinavia, and its language is Celtic.
  • Anjou: The neighbouring county to the south; similar as a feudal state of the Loire region, but it was the rival, not the ally, of Normandy in the twelfth century.
  • Picardy: The neighbouring region to the east; similar in landscape and history, but it has no Norse foundation and its name comes from the Picards, a medieval nickname.
  • Île-de-France: The royal domain around Paris; similar as a northern French region, but it is the political centre against which Normandy defined itself.
  • Anglo-Norman: The cross-Channel world created by the conquest; similar as a historical formation rooted in Normandy, but it denotes the mixed realm and its language, not the province.
  • Normandy format: The diplomatic framework; similar only in name, which commemorates the D-Day anniversary rather than the province itself.
