• Oregano: Similar because both belong to the identical genus (*Origanum*) within the mint family (Lamiaceae) and share similar growth habits with opposite oval leaves; differs because oregano (*Origanum vulgare*) has a sharp, pungent, peppery taste dominated by carvacrol and thymol suited for robust tomato sauces, whereas marjoram (*Origanum majorana*) is distinctly sweeter, milder, and more delicate with citrus-floral notes.
  • Wild Marjoram: Similar because it is the traditional English folk name for common oregano (*Origanum vulgare*); differs because wild marjoram is a hardy perennial native to British grasslands with pink-purple flowers and a coarser, woodier scent, whereas cultivated marjoram is a tender Mediterranean subshrub with tight knotted green bracts and sweet foliage.
  • Thyme: Similar because both are woody Mediterranean herbs in the mint family with tiny leaves widely utilized in European savory cooking; differs because thyme (*Thymus vulgaris*) possesses a sharply medicinal, pungent, earthy aroma rich in thymol, whereas marjoram provides a softer, sweeter, more floral-citrus flavor profile.
  • Basil: Similar because both are aromatic annual or tender perennial herbs in the Lamiaceae family that thrive in summer cottage gardens and pair exquisitely with summer vegetables; differs because basil (*Ocimum basilicum*) has large, tender, glossy green leaves with an unmistakable anise-and-clove perfume, whereas marjoram has tiny, downy gray-green foliage with a sweet, piney-citrus bouquet.
  • Savory: Similar because both are aromatic culinary herbs in the mint family traditionally incorporated into sausages, bean dishes, and roasted meats; differs because savory (especially winter savory, *Satureja montana*) has a biting, peppery, resinous punch, whereas marjoram is gentle, sweet, and soothing.
  • Sage: Similar because both are classic culinary herbs with downy, soft foliage used to flavor poultry stuffings and pork; differs because sage (*Salvia officinalis*) has long, velvety, pungent, camphoraceous leaves that can easily overwhelm delicate ingredients, whereas marjoram harmonizes subtly without dominating other flavors.
