[Root Word 1: CLOVE]
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*klow- / *klāw-
"nail, pin, peg, bar"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE)
clāvus
"iron nail, peg, spike"
↓
Late / Vulgar Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
clāvellus
"small nail, tack"
↓
Old French (c. 1150–1250)
clou (de girofle)
"nail (of clove tree); aromatic dried spice bud shaped like a nail"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300–1400)
clowe / clow
"dried aromatic spice bud of the clove tree"
Note: The phonetic shift from /u/ to /v/ arose from the French plural clous and Latinized forms.
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
clove
"clove spice; pungent, spicy clove fragrance"

↓ [Combined with Root Word 2]

[Root Word 2: PINK]
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–1st c. CE)
*pink- / *pikk-
"to prick, point, peck, pierce, make small holes"
↓
Middle Dutch / Low German (c. 1200–1400)
pinken / pinck
"to prick, notch, slit, or make small; tiny, pointed"
↓
Middle English (c. 1350–1450)
pynken / pinken
"to pierce, cut with small slits, scallop edges of fabric"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1560–1580)
pink
"flower of the genus Dianthus with notched, fringed petals"
Note: Named because the serrated petals appear as if notched or cut with pinking scissors.
↓
Early Modern English Compound (c. 1570–1600)
clove-pink
"fragrant Dianthus caryophyllus flower with notched petals and clove scent"
↓
Modern English (present)
CLOVE-PINK
