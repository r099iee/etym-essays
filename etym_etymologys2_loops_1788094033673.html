NOTE: The origin of loop is uncertain and debated. The OED records two main proposals: (1) a Celtic source — Irish lúb, Scottish Gaelic lùb "a bend, a loop, a fold" (the English word appears in the 14th century, a period of strong Irish-Scottish contact); (2) a Scandinavian source — Old Norse hlaup "a leap, a run", with the sense developing from "a running knot" (a knot that runs along a rope). No PIE root can be reconstructed for either candidate, so the chain begins at the first attested English stage. The verb is a natural derivation from the noun, first recorded in the 19th century; "loop the loop" follows in 1903, and the computing sense from the 1940s.
