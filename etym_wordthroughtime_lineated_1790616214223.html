- 17th-c. natural-history sense - marked with fine lines or streaks, neutral descriptive epithet for plants, shells, birds
- 18th-19th c. general literary sense - lined, furrowed, or ruled, applied to faces, pages, maps, connotation of careful observation
- Early 20th-c. taxonomic fixation - lineated as fixed English for Latin lineatus in bird and fish names, scientific prestige sense
- Late 20th-c. prosodic and composition sense - lineated verse versus prose paragraph, pedagogical sense where line-breaks notate breath and voice
- Contemporary media sense - consecutive lineated notation as culturally contingent print technology contrasted with screen display that can dramatize intonation spatially
