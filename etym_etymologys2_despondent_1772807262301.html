Single root word (DESPONDENT). Linear chain from the reconstructed PIE root to the English adjective. → PIE *spend- 'to make a libation, to perform a solemn rite, to pledge' (also reconstructed as *spondeio-; the root of ritual pouring-and-promising; source also of Greek spondē 'libation' and spondeios 'libation-chant', whence English spondee) → Proto-Italic *spondejo- 'to pledge, to promise solemnly' (the Italic development of the root) → Latin spondēre 'to promise solemnly, to pledge oneself' (attested in early Latin; conjugated spondeo, spopondi, sponsum; source also of sponsus 'one pledged, betrothed', sponsor 'surety', respondēre 'to promise back, answer') → Latin dēspondēre 'to promise away, to give up, to resign, to lose heart' (de- 'away' + spondēre; literally 'to pledge something away'; figurative animam dēspondēre 'to give up one's spirit/hope') → Latin dēspondēns / dēspondentem 'giving up, losing heart' (present participle on the -ent- stem) → English despondent 'in low spirits from loss of hope or courage' (late 17th c., borrowed from Latin dēspondent-) → DESPONDENT 'in low spirits from loss of hope or courage; having pledged away one's hope' (Modern English; with despond v., despondency n., despondently adv.) Note: the reconstruction *spend- for the libation root is the standard view in modern handbooks (Pokorny, LIV), and the connection of Latin spondēre to that ritual-pouring root is widely accepted; the precise shape of the root is reconstructed with varying detail, but the chain itself is not seriously disputed.
