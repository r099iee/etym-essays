- 15th c. appeasement - warm courtly sense: to calm wrath and settle quarrels
- 16th–17th c. general calming - broadened to storms, consciences and passions, still benevolent
- 18th c. imperial turn - chilling shift to military-administrative subjugation of provinces and frontiers
- 19th c. anthropological euphemism - detached technical use for stupefied or drowned animals, as here with ducks gently held till pacified
- 20th c. counter-insurgency - sinister bureaucratic sense of pacified villages and pacification ratios
- Present - split perception: tender when applied to babies and nerves, suspect when applied to peoples, darkly ironic when applied to prey
