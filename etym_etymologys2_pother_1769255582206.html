POTHER has no PIE ancestor. The OED classifies it as of uncertain, most probably imitative (echoic) origin in early-modern English; no ancient or foreign stage is attested, so no earlier link is invented here.
→ (c. 1580s) Early Modern English pudder / pudher "a choking cloud of dust or smoke; a noisy commotion" — the earliest attested shape, probably echoic
→ (c. 1590–1610s) pother (a variant reshaping of pudder) "a choking cloud of dust, smoke, or steam; a fuss, bustle, or commotion"
→ (early 17th c.) English pother, noun "commotion, bustle, fuss; a state of agitation," and verb "to harass, perplex, bewilder; to bustle about"
→ (present day) English pother ◄ TARGET

DEBATE / NUANCE: Origin unknown. The earliest attested form is pudder, and whether pother derives from it by sound-change or is an independent variant is unclear. A Continental loan has been proposed — Middle Low German / Dutch pudderen "to splash, kick up water or dust" — and remains the most plausible external source, but it is unproven. Influence from English potter "to poke about, trifle" (and, in modern sound, bother) is also suggested. No stage before the 16th century is attested; do not assume one.
