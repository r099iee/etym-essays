PIE *bʰuH- "to become, be, grow" (reconstructed, c. 4500–3500 BCE)
→ Ancient Greek phúō "to bring forth, produce, grow" (reflex of *bʰuH-; attested from Homer)
→ Greek physis "nature, growth, the natural constitution of a thing" (attested from the 6th–5th c. BCE)
→ Greek physikē (epistēmē), feminine of physikós "of nature": "the knowledge/science of nature; by extension the medical art" (attested from Aristotle, 4th c. BCE)
→ Latin physica "the study of nature; medicine" (attested 1st c. BCE–CE)
→ Old French fisique / physique "the study of nature; the medical art" (attested from the 12th c.)
→ Middle English fisik / phisik "the art of healing; natural science; a medicine, esp. a purgative" (attested c. 1300)
→ English Physic "the art of healing; a medicinal potion, esp. a cathartic" ◄ TARGET (present day)

DEBATE / NUANCE: No stage is seriously disputed; *bʰuH- is one of the best-attested PIE roots (Sanskrit bhū, bhávati "he becomes"; Latin fuī "I was"; Slavic byti "to be"). Nuance: English physics is the same Greek physikē re-borrowed/rebracketed in the 18th century as a distinct noun for the science of matter and energy, splitting it from the medical physic — a shared ancestor, not a derivation of physic from physics.
