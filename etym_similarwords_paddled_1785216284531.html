• rowed: Similar because both verbs mean propelling a boat through water by muscular strokes; differs because rowing uses long oars locked in oarlocks, with the rower facing backward and pulling with the whole body in deliberate, splashing strokes, whereas paddling uses a short broad blade in free, small, quiet strokes in a smaller craft.
  • sculled: Similar because both are quiet, practised ways of moving a small boat by hand; differs because sculling works a single oar over the stern in a figure-of-eight motion requiring balance, whereas paddling drives a broad-bladed paddle with free, alternating strokes.
  • punted: Similar because both propel a flat-bottomed boat by hand power; differs because punting thrusts a long pole against the river bottom, possible only in shallow, slow water, whereas paddling is self-contained blade work with no ground contact.
  • sailed: Similar because both move a boat across water; differs because sailing is driven by wind acting on sails and rigging, with no muscular stroke at all, whereas paddling is pure human muscle power applied through a blade.
  • drifted: Similar because both describe a boat moving across water; differs because drifting is passive motion carried by current or wind with no propulsion, whereas paddled is deliberate, self-generated forward motion.
  • waded: Similar because it shares the childlike, playful register and the same root sense of short repeated dipping strokes; differs because wading is barefoot walking through shallow water with no boat and no implement, whereas paddled is propelling a craft with a blade.
  • dog-paddled: Similar because both name a simple, primitive stroking motion through water; differs because dog-paddling is a swimming stroke that propels the body itself by pawing with hands and feet, whereas paddling moves a separate boat by means of a paddle.
