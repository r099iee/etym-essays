• Panentheistic: Similar because it also links God and the world; differs because panentheism holds that the universe is in God while God also transcends the universe — there is a "beyond" — whereas pantheism asserts strict identity, God and the universe being the very same thing with nothing outside.
  • Animistic: Similar because it sees spirit in nature; differs because animism attributes distinct, individual souls or spirits to particular things (this tree, that river, these stones), while pantheism holds that the whole cosmos is one single divine substance, not a collection of many little spirits.
  • Polytheistic: Similar because it is also a non-monotheistic framework with divine presence in the world; differs because polytheism affirms many distinct personal gods (Zeus, Odin, the Hindu deities), while pantheism denies personified gods altogether in favour of one impersonal divine all.
  • Monotheistic: Similar because it affirms one God; differs because monotheism holds that God is a personal being distinct from, and creator of, the world, while pantheism identifies God with the world itself and rejects the creator-creature split.
  • Deistic: Similar because both stand outside orthodox theism; differs because the deist holds that God created the world as a clockwork and then stands apart from it, while the pantheist holds that God never stands apart — God is the world, always present in it.
  • Theistic: Similar because it affirms divinity; differs because ordinary theism asserts a transcendent personal God who is other than creation, which pantheism explicitly rejects by making God immanent and identical with the cosmos.
  • Atheistic: Similar because both are often attacked together as denials of a personal God; differs because atheism denies any god at all, while pantheism affirms a divine reality — just not a separate, personal one — so a pantheist is theist and "atheist" in different definitions at once.
  • Hylozoistic: Similar because both hold that all of matter is alive or animated; differs because hylozoism concerns life in matter (all things are alive), without necessarily asserting that the whole is divine, whereas pantheism asserts the divinity of the whole.
  • Panpsychistic: Similar because both see mind or spirit pervading reality; differs because panpsychism holds that mentality is a property of all things, which may be held without any claim about God, while pantheism makes the stronger claim that the whole is God.
  • Numinous: Similar because both describe a sense of the sacred in the world; differs because "numinous" (Rudolf Otto) names the felt experience of the holy — which can accompany theism too — while "pantheistic" names a doctrine of divine-world identity, not merely a feeling.
  • Immanent: Similar because pantheism is an immanentist view — God present within the world; differs because "immanent" is a general philosophical adjective for anything present within the world (not only God), while "pantheistic" specifically asserts God's identity with the world.
  • Pagan: Similar in that both stand against Abrahamic orthodoxy and value nature; differs because "pagan" is a broad, often derogatory umbrella for pre-Christian or earth-based religions (usually polytheistic and ritualistic), while "pantheistic" is a precise philosophical position that need involve no ritual, myth, or many gods at all.
  • Transcendent (opposite): Similar only in that the two are constantly paired in theology; differs because the transcendent is God beyond the world, the exact opposite of the pantheistic immanence, and the contrast is essential to defining both.
  • Naturalistic / secular-spiritual: Similar because both find value and awe in nature without a personal God; differs because "naturalistic" makes no claim about divinity, while a pantheistic view asserts that nature is itself divine.
