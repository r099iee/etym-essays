Stage 1 — Proto-Germanic *krokaz "a hook, a bent instrument" (reconstructed; its ultimate origin is unknown — no securely attested PIE ancestor, and both OED and Etymonline treat the deeper etymology as obscure; a sound-symbolic origin for a bent shape is plausible. Cognates: Old Norse krókr "hook, bend", Old High German krahho "hook", Middle Dutch croec "bent thing"). Stage 2 — Old Low Franconian (Frankish) *krok "hook" (the West Germanic form that passed into Gallo-Romance). Stage 3 — Old French croc "hook, fang, tusk" (9th–12th centuries; source of the diminutive crochet "little hook", giving English crochet and crotchet). Stage 4 — Old French encrochier "to catch with a hook, to seize, fasten on, grab hold of" — en- "in, into" + croc "hook" (12th century). Stage 5 — Old French encrochement "seizure, grasp" (noun formed with -ment). Stage 6 — Anglo-Norman / Middle English encrochen "to seize, acquire unlawfully, extend one's hold" (13th century). Stage 7 — Modern English encroach "to advance gradually and stealthily beyond proper limits" (16th century); the noun encroachment "the act or result of encroaching" (15th–16th century, formed with the suffix -ment). Stage 8 — Modern English encroachments, plural, the exact form preserved in Macaulay's sentence → FINAL NODE: ENCROACHMENTS.
