- [Vedic & Early Epic Period (c. 800–500 BCE)] - emerged initially as an ancient Indo-Aryan gentilic toponymic designation denoting a "princess of the Kosala kingdom", formalizing diplomatic marital bonds between the Ikṣvāku kings of Ayodhyā and the ruling house of Kosala - [perception: viewed as a prestigious dynastic title and royal signifier of territorial alliance]
- [Classical Sanskrit Epic Era (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)] - immortalized by sage Vālmīki in the core Rāmāyaṇa as King Daśaratha's senior queen and mother of Rāma, serving as the dramatic emotional anchor of the Ayodhyākāṇḍa - [perception: revered as the ultimate portrait of maternal grief (śoka) harmonized with royal dignity and devotion to duty (dharma)]
- [Puranic & Classical Sanskrit Literature (c. 300–1200 CE)] - integrated into major Purāṇas and classical court dramas (Kālidāsa's Raghuvaṃśa, Bhavabhūti's Uttararāmacarita) where she is depicted as the venerable Queen Dowager counseling reconciliation and blessing royal lineage - [perception: venerated as a sacred matriarch possessing prophetic wisdom and unassailable moral purity]
- [Medieval Bhakti Movement (c. 1200–1700 CE)] - elevated by saint-poets like Tulsīdās (Rāmacaritamānasa) and Kamban (Irāmāvatāram) into the supreme theological embodiment of vātsalya-bhāva (parental devotion to God), sung in cradle lullabies and morning hymns across India - [perception: worshipped as the ideal divine mother whose unconditional love cradles the Supreme Lord of the universe]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era (20th–21st c.)] - celebrated daily through the ubiquitous temple broadcast of the Venkateswara Suprabhātam ("Kausalyā prajā pūrvā..."), while being analyzed by modern feminist scholars as an enduring study of the precarious political status of women in ancient polygynous courts - [perception: universally honored as a symbol of maternal fortitude, spiritual grace, and deep cultural heritage]
