- Old English (c. 1000) - Grindel-stan as smith's and miller's tool - practical, essential, neutral
- Medieval - Village grindstone for scythes and knives - communal, artisanal, familiar
- 1500s - Nose to the grindstone as drudgery idiom - moral, weary, proverbial
- Industrial era - Giant Sheffield wheels, factory grinding - dangerous, working-class, industrial
- 1843 Dickens - Hand at the grindstone for squeezing employer - condemnatory, capitalist critique
- Present day - Museum tool but living idiom (back to the grind) - nostalgic literal, clichéd figurative
