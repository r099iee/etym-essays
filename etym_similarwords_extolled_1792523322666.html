• lauded: Similar because both words express public, honorable commendation; differs because lauded often highlights formal or institutional praise (such as awards, ceremonies, or official commendations), whereas extolled conveys a higher degree of passionate, enthusiastic, and expansive vocal adulation.
  • praised: Similar because both denote expressing approval and admiration; differs because praised is the basic, neutral, everyday term suitable for small daily deeds (e.g., praising a child for good manners), while extolled represents an elevated, literary register reserved for extraordinary virtues, masterpieces, or monumental heroism.
  • exalted: Similar because both words trace back to the physical metaphor of raising someone high; differs because exalted emphasizes the resulting elevated status, dignity, rank, or ecstatic emotional state of the subject, whereas extolled focuses on the verbal act of eulogizing.
  • eulogized: Similar because both describe praising someone's virtues eloquently; differs because eulogized is formally associated with funerary orations or formal tributes delivered after death or retirement, whereas extolled applies freely to living persons, ideas, and abstract virtues.
  • magnified: Similar because both involve enlarging a person's reputation; differs because magnified specifically means making something appear greater or grander than it is (or exalting God in scriptural English, as in the Magnificat), while extolled centers on enthusiastic verbal speech and celebration.
  • glorified: Similar because both involve raising someone to high honor; differs because glorified often implies investing the subject with divine splendor, halos, or unmerited romanticism, whereas extolled is a conscious oratorical commendation of proven virtues.
  • acclaimed: Similar because both convey widespread public enthusiasm; differs because acclaimed emphasizes noisy, collective approval by a large audience or the general public (e.g., a critically acclaimed film), whereas extolled can be performed by a single eloquent speaker, seer, or critic.
  • commended: Similar because both express approval; differs because commended has an official, restrained, and hierarchical flavor (e.g., a superior officer commending a soldier for duty), lacking the ardent, soaring enthusiasm inherent in extolled.
  • panegyrized: Similar because both describe formal, elaborate praise; differs because panegyrized refers to the delivery of a set, formal rhetorical speech (a panegyric) composed according to strict classical rules, whereas extolled is a broader, more natural expressive verb.
  • celebrated: Similar because both denote honoring an achievement; differs because celebrated frequently functions as an established descriptive adjective of renown (e.g., a celebrated artist) or denotes festive public observance, whereas extolled is the active verbal declaration of praise.
  • vaunted: Similar because both involve speaking highly of virtues or powers; differs because vaunted is almost always pejorative, implying boastful, arrogant, or unjustified promotion that is destined to fail (e.g., their vaunted military superiority crumbled).
  • applauded: Similar because both denote positive reception; differs because applauded primarily evokes physical clapping or democratic consensus, whereas extolled evokes majestic linguistic elevation and rhetorical reverence.
  • rhapsodized: Similar because both involve passionate, effusive expression of praise; differs because rhapsodized suggests an unconstrained, emotional, and sometimes disjointed outpouring of personal delight, whereas extolled maintains intellectual dignity and moral gravitas.
