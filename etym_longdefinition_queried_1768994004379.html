queried — asked a question about something, usually in order to seek information, to test the truth of a claim, or to express doubt. In this specific literary moment, "queried" does not merely report that Chivers asked a question; it is an authorial speech tag that dramatizes the act of interrogation inside the cave's oppressive, almost geological silence. The word carries a careful, probing, mildly sceptical register: the speaker is not making small talk but actively testing a hypothesis, trying to pierce the "cryptic silence" with human logic and inquiry. Where a plainer tag like "asked" would merely register a request for information, "queried" suggests that the question is itself a tool — an instrument of verification — aimed at identifying the source of the mechanical sound. The tone is therefore alert, controlled, and slightly formal, the voice of a man who treats the unknown as a problem to be solved rather than a mystery to be feared.

The core sense of to query is "to ask, to inquire", but the word's distinctive shading is doubt and verification: you query precisely those things whose truth you doubt, whereas you ask those things you simply do not know. This double character — asking plus testing — is encoded in the word's history (the Latin imperative quaere, "seek!", used as a marginal note telling a reader to look something up). Modern senses radiate from this core:

  • Speech-tag sense (this card): "queried" as a verb of saying meaning "asked, put a question" — standard in narrative prose, where it carries a probing, slightly formal flavour between "asked" and "questioned".
  • Doubt / challenge sense: to query a statement, a figure, or a claim is to call it in question, to doubt and probe its truth ("he queried the validity of the data"). This is the sense dominant in academic, legal, and journalistic usage.
  • Information-technology jargon: a query is a formal, structured request for information executed against a database or search system (SQL query, to query a database, query string); to query a system is to interrogate it for verification. This is the word's sharpest modern form — the instrumental act of testing exactly what one doubts, made mechanical.
  • Publishing / commerce sense: a query letter is a professional inquiry (a writer's or agent's pitch); "query" in business English is a question or request for information, the vocabulary of the desk.
  • Noun senses: a query is a question or doubt ("a query arose"), and historically a query was the question mark (?) placed in a margin to flag a doubtful passage — the ancestor of the modern punctuation mark's name in some traditions.

Register and boundaries: "queried" is standard, measured, and slightly formal; it sits between "inquired" (polite, information-seeking) and "questioned" (challenging, cross-examining). Its near-synonyms divide the field by tone and intent — asked (neutral), inquired (polite), questioned (challenging), interrogated (hostile, systematic), demanded (peremptory), challenged (confrontational), probed (searching deeply), petitioned (formal request). The past tense queried is the regular -ed form of the verb query, which entered English in the sixteenth century from the Latin marginal note quaere. In the cave, the single word "queried" before "Chivers" compresses an entire epistemology: a question that is also an experiment, a tool of verification aimed at the unknown.
