Note: the Greek verb ballein "to throw" is customarily derived from a PIE root *gʷel(h₁)- "to throw, strike", but the attribution is debated (Beekes allows a possible Pre-Greek origin for ballō), so the chain begins from *per-, whose Greek reflex pará is secure. The Vulgar Latin stage *paraulare is reconstructed from the Romance verbs (French parler, Occitan paraular); the fuller spelling parabolare is documented in Merovingian texts. The decisive semantic shift — parabola, originally "parable, comparison", coming to mean "word, speech" — happened in Christian Latin, where the Gospel parable stood for the uttered word of God; it survives today in French parole, Italian parola, Spanish palabra "word" (and in English "parole"). English also has the direct doublet "parable" (14th c.) and the older short form "parle" (15th c.) beside parley.
