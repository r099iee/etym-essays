• Conglomeration: Similar because both denote an extensive collection or mass composed of varied, heterogeneous elements; differs because a conglomeration usually emphasizes the striking mismatch, odd variety, or bizarre juxtaposition of its constituent parts, whereas an agglomeration emphasizes the physical clustering, cohesive bulk, or spatial gathering of the pieces.
  • Aggregation: Similar because both refer to a total formed by gathering distinct units together; differs because an aggregation is often an abstract, systematic, or statistical sum of discrete items (e.g., aggregate data), while an agglomeration carries a tactile, physical, or geographical connotation of clustered physical mass.
  • Accumulation: Similar because both describe a growing pile or quantity of things brought together; differs because accumulation focuses primarily on the chronological process of gradual buildup or hoarding over time, whereas agglomeration emphasizes the resulting clustered spatial structure or cohesive mass.
  • Conurbation: Similar because both are key geographical terms describing coalescing urban settlements; differs because conurbation is exclusively an urban planning term denoting the polycentric coalescence of several historically equal cities into an urban corridor, while agglomeration applies universally across materials science, geology, cognition, and geography.
  • Cluster: Similar because both describe multiple individual entities grouped closely together in space; differs because a cluster usually denotes a smaller, looser, and more distinct arrangement of separate objects (e.g., a cluster of stars or grapes), whereas an agglomeration forms a denser, more consolidated, and massive whole.
  • Amalgamation: Similar because both describe bringing disparate components into a unified state; differs because amalgamation implies a complete chemical, institutional, or metallurgical blending where separate entities dissolve their boundaries and merge into a homogeneous composite, whereas in an agglomeration individual parts retain their physical identity.
  • Hodgepodge: Similar because both refer to an unorganized collection of diverse items; differs because hodgepodge is an informal, somewhat derogatory term emphasizing chaotic messiness and poor taste, while agglomeration is an objective, formal, and technical descriptor.
