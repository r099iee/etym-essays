- Old English to Middle English: Strictly used in agrarian and pastoral contexts to distinguish nursing livestock and human infants from those adapted to solid feed.
- 16th–17th Century: Expanded into dramatic and poetic literature to depict innocent, vulnerable orphans and overly attached, childish suitors.
- 18th–19th Century: Common in moral treatises and romantic poetry to symbolize primitive innocence or spiritual dependency.
- 20th–21st Century: Adopted into psychoanalytic and post-structuralist vocabulary to diagnose adult emotional fixation, oral neurosis, and unrefined, demanding literary styles.
