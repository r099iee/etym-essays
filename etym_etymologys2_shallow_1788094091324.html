Proto-Germanic (reconstructed; c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE)
*skalda-
"shallow, of little depth"
↓
Old English (attested 8th–11th c.)
sceald
"shallow, not deep"
↓
Middle English (c. 1400)
schalowe
"not deep, of little depth"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–)
SHALLOW
"of little depth; superficial"

NOTE: The origin is genuinely uncertain and debated. Middle English schalowe is generally believed to be connected with Old English sceald “shallow,” which survives independently as SHOAL (“sandbank, shoal water”); behind sceald lies a reconstructed Germanic *skalda-, but no secure pre-Germanic (PIE) root can be established, and the Germanic cognates (e.g. Middle Low German schal) are sparse and disputed. Dictionaries also observe that the vowel development sceald → schalowe is phonologically irregular, so the link — though probable — is not fully explained; some authorities treat SHALLOW as an English formation of obscure origin.
