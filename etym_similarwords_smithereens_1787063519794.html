• fragments: Similar because both mean pieces of something broken; differs because fragments is neutral, technical, and countable ("a fragment"), while smithereens is colloquial, plural-only, and stresses very small, scattered bits.
  • pieces: Similar because both mean parts of a broken thing; differs because pieces covers any size from huge slabs down to dust and is the plain everyday word, while smithereens always means tiny fragments from violent breaking.
  • shards: Similar because both mean broken pieces; differs because shards specifically names sharp-edged fragments of glass, pottery, or stone, while smithereens can be any material and stresses smallness over sharpness.
  • splinters: Similar because both mean thin, sharp fragments; differs because splinters are elongated pieces of wood, bone, or glass, while smithereens are irregular tiny bits of anything.
  • shivers: Similar because "shivered to smithereens" links them, both meaning small pieces; differs because shivers is now rare for fragments (it mainly means trembles) and suggests thin slivers, while smithereens is the everyday word for bits.
  • bits: Similar because both mean small pieces; differs because bits is the mildest possible word (you can have "bits and pieces" without any violence), while smithereens always implies violent shattering.
  • rubble: Similar because both are the debris of destruction; differs because rubble is the mass of broken stone and masonry left after collapse, often large chunks, while smithereens are the tiny flying pieces of the moment of breaking.
  • dust / atoms: Similar because both describe the ultimate small outcome of smashing; differ because dust and atoms mean the pieces have become powder or almost nothing at all, while smithereens are still visible little fragments.
  • bits and bobs / odds and ends: Similar because both are plural colloquialisms for small pieces; differ because these are cosy words for ordinary small objects, with none of smithereens' violence and shattering.
