• Sterile: Similar because both mean unable to produce; differs because sterile is clinical and also means germ-free, while barren is more poetic and environmental.
  • Infertile: Similar because both describe failure to conceive or grow; differs because infertile is the modern, neutral medical word, while barren now sounds harsh and literary when applied to people.
  • Unfruitful: Similar because both mean bearing no fruit; differs because unfruitful is almost wholly figurative and rarer in modern use.
  • Unproductive: Similar because both mean yielding nothing; differs because unproductive is a neutral economic or administrative word, lacking barren's bleak emotional colour.
  • Fruitless: Similar because both describe efforts that yield nothing; differs because fruitless applies only to efforts, searches and attempts — never to land or people.
  • Arid: Similar because both describe dry, unproductive land; differs because arid specifically means lacking water (a climate term), while barren means lacking fertility.
  • Bare: Similar because both describe exposed, empty surfaces; differs because bare simply means uncovered, with no implication of sterility or waste.
  • Bleak: Similar because both describe cheerless, exposed places; differs because bleak is chiefly about weather, exposure and mood, not fertility.
  • Desolate: Similar because both describe empty, lifeless places; differs because desolate stresses abandonment and loneliness, while barren stresses unproductiveness.
  • Empty: Similar because both describe absence of contents; differs because empty is neutral and general, with no suggestion of sterility or ruin.
  • Fallow: Similar because both describe unproductive land; differs because fallow is deliberately rested farmland — temporary and planned — not inherently sterile.
  • Waste: Similar because a waste is barren land; differs because waste (noun) names the region itself, while barren describes its quality — and waste also means refuse.
