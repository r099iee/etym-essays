- Roman colony - Arles descends from the Roman colony Arelate on the Rhône, a leading city of Gallia Narbonensis and in late antiquity a seat of the Praetorian Prefecture of Gaul and of the Visigothic kings.
- Late-antique Christian court - grouped by Macaulay with Paris, Toledo, and Ravenna, Arles stood for the Romanized, Christianized south into which the Germanic invaders assimilated, the antithesis of heathen Britain.
- Middle Ages and after - the name passed through the medieval Kingdom of Arles (10th-14th centuries) into modern memory as a Provençal city of Roman ruins and the Alyscamps.
- Contemporary sense - Arles is a living French city and UNESCO site, best known to the general public through Van Gogh, who painted its sunlit streets and Rhône nights, and through its Roman amphitheatre.
