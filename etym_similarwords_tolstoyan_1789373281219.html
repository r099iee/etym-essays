• Dostoevskian: Similar because both characterize monumental 19th-century Russian literary titans whose profound psychological insight revolutionized world literature; differs because Tolstoyan emphasizes daytime lucidity, panoramic social breadth, objective physical reality, and moral rationalism, whereas Dostoevskian evokes nocturnal spiritual crises, feverish existential torment, criminal psychology, claustrophobic urban squalor, and irrational metaphysical frenzy.
  • Proustian: Similar because both denote monumental literary works of staggering length that exhaustively dissect human society, aristocratic circles, and temporal consciousness; differs because Proustian focuses introspectively on involuntary memory, high-bourgeois salon aesthetics, and the subjective sensory texture of lost time, whereas Tolstoyan commands macroscopic historical canvases, physical outdoor vitality, and sweeping ethical clarity.
  • Balzacian: Similar because both describe an insatiable narrative appetite for cataloging every layer and stratum of society; differs because Balzacian highlights urban ambition, financial obsession, rapacious social climbing, and grotesque satirical caricature in 19th-century Paris, whereas Tolstoyan seeks spiritual authenticity, moral redemption, and the pastoral verities of nature.
  • Epic: Similar because both designate colossal artistic scale, vast temporal spans, and sprawling casts of characters; differs because epic is an ancient, neutral genre classification frequently dealing in mythical heroism and grand national legends, whereas Tolstoyan grounds its immense scope in tangible, everyday psychological realism and unvarnished historical scrutiny.
  • Gandhian: Similar because both champion militant nonviolent resistance, moral purity, agrarian simplicity, and manual labor; differs because Gandhian developed into an organized mass political movement for national liberation and institutional statecraft, whereas Tolstoyan remained fundamentally Christian anarchist, rejecting all formal state apparatuses, taxation, and organized political machinery.
  • Pacifist: Similar because both advocate the total cessation of warfare and physical violence; differs because pacifist is a broad political or philosophical opposition to armed conflict, whereas Tolstoyan anchors nonviolence strictly in the radical moral teachings of Jesus Christ and absolute non-resistance to evil by force.
  • Ascetic: Similar because both describe the rigorous renunciation of worldly luxury, sensual pleasure, and material self-indulgence; differs because ascetic often suggests monastic withdrawal, religious self-mortification, and solitary contemplation, whereas Tolstoyan asceticism is actively grounded in communal manual labor, shoemaking, plowing, and solidarity with the working peasantry.
