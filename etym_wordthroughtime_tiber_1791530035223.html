- 3rd century BCE - Tiberis is simply the river of Rome and the boundary of the city's territory; the connotation is local, practical and religious.
- 1st century BCE - Virgil, Horace and Livy make the Tiber a mythic and patriotic river, home of the god Tiberinus and witness to Rome's founding; it becomes a symbol of the city's destiny.
- 1st-4th century CE - the river is an engineering problem and an imperial artery; floods, sewers, harbours and the grain supply give it a busy, utilitarian presence in Roman life.
- Middle Ages - with Rome reduced and the papacy ascendant, the Tiber becomes the pope's river; pilgrim routes, bridges and the Castel Sant'Angelo tie it to the Christian city.
- 17th-18th century - the Grand Tour and classical scholarship fix "Tiber" as a learned, poetic name; vedute and Latin quotations make it a backdrop for ruins and nostalgia.
- 19th century - the flood of 1870 prompts the great embankments; "crossing the Tiber" spreads as an idiom for conversion to Roman Catholicism, giving the river a confessional meaning.
- 20th century - the Tiber is tamed, polluted and rediscovered; it appears in guidebooks, novels and films as Rome's ordinary, slightly melancholy river.
- 21st century - the name survives in hydrology, heritage tourism, religious journalism and popular culture, where "Tiber" can evoke Rome, the papacy or even a science-fiction empire.
