• snail: Similar because both are terrestrial gastropod molluscs with soft, unsegmented bodies, slime-producing glands, and sensory tentacles; differs because a snail possesses a prominent, coiled external calcified shell into which it can fully retract for defense against predators and drought, whereas a slug has evolved shell reduction or complete shell loss.
  • sluggard: Similar because both share the same historical Germanic etymological root signifying indolence, torpor, and slow movement; differs because 'sluggard' refers exclusively and pejoratively to an habitually lazy, unproductive human being, whereas 'slug' primarily designates the mollusc, projectile, or technical artifact.
  • bullet: Similar because both designate heavy metal projectiles launched from firearms; differs because 'bullet' is the standard, universal term for any pointed, cylindrical projectile fired from rifles and handguns, whereas a 'slug' specifically denotes a heavy, solid cylinder of lead fired from a shotgun or an unrefined, improvised lump of metal.
  • token: Similar because both are circular metal discs utilized in coin-operated machines; differs because a token is an officially minted, authorized medium of exchange for transit or amusement arcades, whereas a 'slug' in this context is an illicit, counterfeit blank manufactured to defraud the machine.
  • draught: Similar because both describe an amount of liquid ingested in a single continuous swallowing action; differs because 'draught' (draft) is a traditional, neutral term for any drink, whereas a 'slug' refers colloquially and vividly to a heavy, fiery gulp of strong distilled spirits.
  • swatch: Similar because both serve as concise working labels or sample identifiers in production workflows; differs because a swatch is a physical piece of fabric, material, or color sample, whereas an editorial 'slug' is a textual label guiding a manuscript through typesetting and publication.
