• Brotherhoods: Similar because both denote organized communities or bonds of men united by common oaths, mutual loyalty, and shared goals; differs because brotherhood is a native Germanic term that encompasses both formal organizations and informal, emotional feelings of deep solidarity, whereas fraternities specifically denotes formal, institutionalized corporate bodies such as collegiate chapters or historical sodalities.
  • Confraternities: Similar because both trace to the Latin root frater and describe sworn fraternal associations; differs because confraternities specifically denotes historical, lay Catholic religious sodalities dedicated to prayer, charitable works, and patron saint devotions, whereas fraternities encompasses secular collegiate, tribal, and professional societies.
  • Sodalities: Similar because both refer to organized associations or fellowships formed for a shared purpose; differs because sodality (from Latin sodalis "companion") is a formal theological or sociological term describing voluntary associations within a larger church or civic structure, lacking the specific collegiate and male-kinship resonances of fraternities.
  • Guilds: Similar because medieval craft guilds operated on fraternal oaths and mutual-aid insurance systems; differs because guilds were primarily economic and commercial cartels focused on controlling trade monopolies, apprenticeship standards, and commodity pricing, whereas fraternities prioritize social, ritual, collegiate, or civic bonding.
  • Lodges: Similar because fraternal orders like the Freemasons and Odd Fellows assemble in local chapter branches known as lodges; differs because lodge originally designated the physical meeting hall or shelter of stonemasons, referring metonymically to the meeting unit rather than the overarching concept of sworn brotherhood.
  • Orders: Similar because both designate hierarchical, sworn organizations governed by internal rules (e.g., chivalric or monastic orders); differs because an order typically requires strict vows of obedience, religious consecration, or royal investiture (such as the Knights Templar or Order of the Garter), whereas fraternities are self-governing collegiate, mutual-aid, or social fellowships.
  • Fellowships: Similar because both describe associations of people joined by common interests or camaraderie; differs because fellowship is a broader, gender-neutral term frequently applied to academic research grants, church congregations, or friendly gatherings without the secret oaths and chapter systems of fraternities.
  • Sororities: Similar because both are collegiate Greek-letter social organizations structured with secret rituals, national chapters, and residential housing; differs because sororities (from Latin soror "sister") are explicitly female organizations established in the mid-nineteenth century to provide women with collegiate institutions parallel to male fraternities.
  • Cabals: Similar because both can involve secret assemblies and exclusive cliques; differs because a cabal carries a sinister, pejorative connotation of a small conspiratorial junta secretly plotting political intrigue or subversion, whereas fraternities are institutionalized civic, student, or cultural societies.
  • Associations: Similar because both represent groups of people united for a collective purpose; differs because association is an expansive, formal legal term applying to any corporation, partnership, or trade union without the emotional brotherhood, ritual initiation, or kinship symbolism of fraternities.
