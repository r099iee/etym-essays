• Lakṣmaṇa: Similar because both refer to the exact same heroic prince and epic protagonist in the Ramayana; differs because Lakṣmaṇa is his primary personal given name (meaning "endowed with auspicious marks"), whereas Saumitri is an elevated matronymic epithet specifically highlighting his maternal descent from Queen Sumitrā.
  • Rāmānuja: Similar because both identify Lakṣmaṇa through his fraternal bond with Rāma; differs because Rāmānuja literally means "the younger brother of Rāma" (highlighting junior familial subordination and devotion), while Saumitri links him directly to his mother and royal lineage.
  • Kaunteya: Similar because both are prestigious Sanskrit epic matronymics formed with the *vṛddhi* patronymic suffix (son of Kuntī, referring to Arjuna, Yudhiṣṭhira, or Karṇa in the Mahabharata); differs because Kaunteya pertains to the Pāṇḍava brothers of the Lunar Dynasty, whereas Saumitri belongs exclusively to the Solar Dynasty of Ayodhyā.
  • Gāṅgeya: Similar because both are high classical epic matronymics highlighting an illustrious mother; differs because Gāṅgeya denotes Bhīṣma as the "son of the river goddess Gaṅgā" in the Mahabharata, carrying an element of divine elemental descent, whereas Saumitri highlights mortal royal maternity.
  • Vaidehī: Similar because both are epic patronymic/matronymic gentilic designations in the Ramayana; differs because Vaidehī designates Princess Sītā as the "Princess of Videha" (a geographic and patronymic title derived from her adoptive father King Janaka of Videha), whereas Saumitri denotes male martial warrior status.
  • Bharata: Similar because both are royal sons of King Daśaratha and devoted younger brothers of Rāma; differs because Bharata is the son of Queen Kaikeyī and heir-apparent who refuses the throne out of righteousness, whereas Saumitri chooses active, physical, fourteen-year forest exile and battlefield protection alongside Rāma.
  • Śatrughna: Similar because both are biological twin sons born to Queen Sumitrā after the Putrakāmeṣṭi sacrifice; differs because in the singular Saumitri almost universally specifies Lakṣmaṇa (the elder twin who followed Rāma), while Śatrughna accompanied and served Bharata.
  • Ādi-Śeṣa: Similar because in Vaishnava theology both represent the same divine cosmic entity; differs because Ādi-Śeṣa is the primordial thousand-headed serpent upon whom Lord Viṣṇu reclines in the causal ocean, while Saumitri is his terrestrial human incarnation as an epic prince.
  • Urmilāpati: Similar because both identify the same hero; differs because Urmilāpati is a relational marital epithet meaning "Lord of Urmilā" (the daughter of King Janaka's brother Kuśadhvaja), highlighting his role as husband rather than son.
  • Kākuṭstha: Similar because both are noble epic epithets used for princes of Ayodhyā; differs because Kākuṭstha is an ancestral patronymic tracing back to their remote ancestor King Kakutstha ("standing on the hump of an ox"), encompassing Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa, and Bharata alike, whereas Saumitri is exclusive to Sumitrā's offspring.
