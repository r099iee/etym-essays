• red: Similar because scarlet is a variety of red, the general hypernym of the whole family; differs because red covers everything from pink to maroon, while scarlet pinpoints the bright, warm, orange-leaning end of the range with overtones of luxury and ceremony.
  • crimson: Similar because crimson is the other great deep literary red of English, equally old and rich; differs because crimson leans cool, bluish and bloody while scarlet leans warm and orange; crimson whispers, scarlet shouts.
  • vermilion: Similar because vermilion is a brilliant orange-red close to scarlet's hue and equally intense; differs because vermilion names a pigment (mercuric sulphide, cinnabar) and a paint colour that is brighter and more yellow, while scarlet keeps its cloth-and-dye textile history.
  • carmine: Similar because carmine is a red dye colour derived from cochineal, sharing scarlet's dyestuff ancestry; differs because carmine is darker and cooler, closer to crimson, and is used mainly of pigments, inks and cosmetics rather than cloth.
  • cerise: Similar because cerise is another vivid red of French origin, literally "cherry" red; differs because cerise is pinker, fruitier and more modern, a fashion colour, while scarlet carries centuries of ceremonial weight.
  • ruby: Similar because ruby red is another warm, precious deep red; differs because ruby is darker, denser and jewel-toned while scarlet is brilliant, bright and flag-like; ruby names the gem first and the colour second, and gem-red is never as hued with orange.
  • cardinal: Similar because cardinal red is the ecclesiastical scarlet of cardinals' robes and the bird's plumage, overlapping with scarlet in actual garments; differs because cardinal is a narrower proprietary colour name, registered on the bright red side and tied to Rome and the bird.
  • scarlatina: Similar because the medical term derives from the Italian form of scarlet and denotes the same disease as "scarlet fever"; differs because scarlatina names the illness itself, not a colour, and is a clinical register word.
