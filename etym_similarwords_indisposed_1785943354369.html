• Unwell: Similar because both mean mildly ill; differs because unwell is neutral and transparent about health, while indisposed is formal and deliberately vague, and also covers unwillingness.
  • Ill / sick: Similar because both denote illness; differs because ill and sick can denote serious disease, while indisposed is specifically minor, temporary, and polite.
  • Ailing: Similar because both denote poor health; differs because ailing suggests chronic or lingering weakness, while indisposed suggests a passing, unspecified indisposition.
  • Seedy: Similar because both denote being mildly under the weather; differs because seedy is informal, British, and vivid, while indisposed is formal and reserved.
  • Out of sorts: Similar because both denote mild unwellness or low mood; differs because out of sorts is informal and includes bad temper, while indisposed is formal and bodily.
  • Off-colour / under the weather: Similar because both are informal idioms for being slightly ill; differs because they are colloquial, while indisposed belongs to the formal register.
  • Disinclined: Similar because both mean not inclined or unwilling; differs because disinclined states reluctance plainly and is neutral in register, while indisposed pairs the unwillingness with the polite mask of illness.
  • Unwilling: Similar because both express reluctance; differs because unwilling is blunt and direct, while indisposed is the diplomatic way of saying the same thing.
  • Averse: Similar because both express opposition to something; differs because averse is about dislike of the thing itself, while indisposed is about not being in a state to do it.
  • Loath: Similar because both express reluctance; differs because loath is a strong, personal unwillingness to do a specific act, while indisposed is milder and more diffuse.
  • Reluctant: Similar because both denote hesitating to act; differs because reluctant emphasises inner struggle or scruple, while indisposed is an outward status statement rather than an emotion.
  • Incapacitated: Similar because both mean unable to act; differs because incapacitated is severe, legal, and total (disabled or prevented), while indisposed is minor and polite.
  • Laid up: Similar because both mean confined by illness; differs because laid up is informal and implies being bedridden, while indisposed is a mild formality.
  • Unavailable: Similar because both mean not able to attend; differs because unavailable is neutral and can have many causes, while indisposed politely suggests illness or disinclination.
