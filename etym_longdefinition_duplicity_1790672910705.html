duplicity names doubleness as deceit: the state or practice of being double in speech and conduct — professing one thing while intending or doing another. Where duality is neutral doubleness and duplication is copying, duplicity is always moralised and always negative. It is doubleness with intent to mislead: hypocrisy systematised, two-facedness as policy. In the source sentence, rhetoric becomes a dirty word precisely because it is suspected of this: ornament that beautifies on the surface while covering moral duplicity beneath.

The core meaning divides into act and character. As act, duplicity is double-dealing in a particular transaction: an act of duplicity, to practise duplicity, to expose his duplicity. Diplomatic duplicity, electoral duplicity, marital duplicity — each names deliberate misleading through concealment, equivocation, or outright falsehood. As character, it is the habitual disposition to such doubling: a man of notorious duplicity, a regime built on duplicity. The plural duplicities is available but rare, used for repeated instances rather than the trait.

  • Deceitful double-talk: saying one thing to one audience and the opposite to another. Diplomatic duplicity, corporate duplicity. The emphasis is verbal: promises, assurances, and fine phrases that do not match intent.
  • Hypocritical conduct: professing virtue while practising vice. Moral duplicity, religious duplicity. This is the sense on this card: ornament outside, rot inside.
  • Legal / formal sense: in older English law, duplicity also meant doubleness of pleading — joining two distinct grounds in one plea — a technical fault of form, not morals. This sense survives only in legal history and puns upon it.
  • Archaic neutral sense: mere doubleness or twofoldness, as in the duplicity of a leaf. Obsolete since the 17th century; modern readers must not import neutrality into the word.

Register is formal and condemnatory. Duplicity belongs to editorials, histories, sermons, and courtrooms — to condemn duplicity, to denounce duplicity, sheer duplicity — rather than to everyday complaint, where lying, two-facedness, phoniness, or hypocrisy dominate. It implies calculation and persistence rather than a single lie: the duplicitous person maintains two accounts over time, managing the gap between them. Typical collocates are practise duplicity, engage in duplicity, accuse of duplicity, evidence of duplicity, web of duplicity.

  • Duplicity vs hypocrisy: hypocrisy pretends to virtue; duplicity pretends to anything — agreement, loyalty, candour — to gain advantage. All hypocrisy is duplicity, but not all duplicity is hypocrisy.
  • Duplicity vs deceit: deceit is the genus, duplicity the species marked by doubling: two faces, two stories, two loyalties.
  • Duplicity vs ambiguity: ambiguity may be innocent or poetic; duplicity is ambiguity weaponised, maintained deliberately to mislead.

To grasp duplicity fully is to feel its physical metaphor: folded double, like a cloth hiding something in its fold. The word accuses not merely of falsehood but of foldedness — a self creased over to show one surface while hiding another.
