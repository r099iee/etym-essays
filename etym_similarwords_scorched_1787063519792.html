• burned / burnt: Similar because scorched means burned; differs because burned covers everything from a light singe to complete destruction by fire, while scorched is specifically surface damage — browned or blackened outside, often intact inside.
  • singed: Similar because both mean a light surface burn; differs because singed is milder (just the edges or tips, hair and fabric), while scorched is deeper and drier, browning or blackening the surface.
  • charred: Similar because both describe blackened burning; differs because charred means reduced to a black crust or partly carbonised — a stage beyond scorched, which can be merely browned.
  • seared: Similar because both name heat-damaged surfaces; differs because seared usually implies a quick, intense browning by direct contact (especially in cooking or cauterising), while scorched implies longer or more damaging heat, often from fire or sun.
  • roasted: Similar because both involve dry heat; differs because roasted implies slow, thorough cooking of food (or oppressive dry heat in weather), while scorched implies unwanted or damaging heat.
  • parched: Similar because both carry dryness and shrivelling; differs because parched is purely about drying (thirst, drought) with no burning or blackening, while scorched always involves actual heat or fire.
  • withered: Similar because both describe plants or skin damaged by dryness; differs because withering is loss of moisture and life over time, while scorching is a sudden, burn-like damage by heat.
  • blistered: Similar because both describe heat-damaged surfaces; differs because blistered specifies raised, fluid-filled swellings (skin, paint), while scorched is about browning and drying, not blistering as such.
  • incinerated / reduced to ashes: Similar because both are outcomes of fire; differ because incineration means complete burning to ash — the total destruction that scorched specifically is not, since scorched leaves a damaged but recognisable thing.
