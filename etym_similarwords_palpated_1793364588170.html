• Probed: Similar because both involve deep, searching, and investigatory physical exploration; differs because probed implies entering, penetrating, or driving an instrument or finger deeply into a cavity, wound, or unknown space (often associated with discomfort or sharp intrusion), whereas palpated implies a gentler, more surface-sensitive diagnostic touching with fingers or hands.
  • Examined (by touch): Similar because both describe assessing physical condition; differs because examined is a general, umbrella term that can rely purely on visual inspection or laboratory analysis without any physical contact, whereas palpated strictly requires physical touch as its primary diagnostic medium.
  • Felt: Similar because both involve tactile sensation; differs because felt is an everyday, informal Anglo-Saxon verb that can be entirely passive, accidental, or emotional (e.g., 'she felt cold', 'he felt happy'), whereas palpated is a formal, deliberate, and professional physical action.
  • Handled: Similar because both involve manual contact with the hands; differs because handled suggests lifting, grasping, moving, or managing an object (e.g., handling cargo, handling tools), lacking the diagnostic and exploratory sensitivity inherent to palpated.
  • Caressed: Similar in the gentle, stroking quality of physical contact; differs because caressed is motivated purely by emotional affection, tenderness, or erotic intimacy, whereas palpated is an objective, investigative, and analytical procedure.
  • Manipulated: Similar because both involve physical intervention with the fingers; differs because manipulated emphasizes moving, adjusting, repositioning, or operating physical structures (e.g., manipulating dislocated bones, manipulating levers), whereas palpated is primarily focused on receiving sensory data rather than mechanically altering the subject.
  • Tactilely inspected: Similar in formal and analytical register; differs because it is a clumsy periphrastic phrase, whereas palpated is the established, precise classical term of art in medicine and zoology.
  • Groped: Similar because both describe reaching out with hands to touch in the absence of clear sight; differs because groped carries strong negative connotations of clumsy, blind fumbling, disorientation, or unwanted sexual boundary violations.
  • Fumbled: Similar because hands are used; differs because fumbled denotes clumsy, uncoordinated, or failing manual action (such as dropping a ball or misplacing keys), whereas palpated requires refined, exquisitely trained tactile coordination.
