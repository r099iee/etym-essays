- 13th-14th centuries - batter, from Old French 'battre' (to beat), enters with the vocabulary of war and the kitchen; the beaten cooking mixture takes the same name.
- 16th-17th centuries - the battering-ram anchors the verb in sieges: to strike repeatedly and crushingly to breach a wall.
- 18th-19th centuries - figurative use spreads — battered by storms, battered by fate; pancake batter becomes the beaten mix of everyday cooking.
- Contemporary - batter welds insistent repetition to deformation; it is also the word for abuse and for the thin mixture of flour and egg.
