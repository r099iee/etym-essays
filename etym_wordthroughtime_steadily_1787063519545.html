- Old English (pre-1100) - Root noun *stede* ("place, position, standing") and adjective *stæððiġ* ("fixed, firm") emphasized immovable physical location.
- Middle English (1100–1500) - Adjective *stedy* and adverb *stediliche* arose to describe physical firmness of footing, non-shaking hands, and steadfast moral loyalty.
- Early Modern English (1500–1700) - Expanded to navigational courses (steering steady), mechanical rhythms, and constant uninterrupted temporal progress.
- 18th–19th c. (Industrial & Statistical) - Standardized in scientific, industrial, and economic literature to describe uniform rates of acceleration, pressure, temperature, and financial growth.
- Contemporary (Psychological & Colloquial) - Widely used for emotional calmness under stress, consistent behavioral habits, and gradual macroscopic trends.
