[ROOT 1: KIN]

[ROOT 2: FOLK]
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE) *pleh₁- / *pl̥h₁-k- "to fill, multitude, crowd of people" ↓ Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE) *fulką "multitude, crowd, host, army of people" ↓ Old English (c. 700 CE) folc "people, nation, tribe, assembled army" ↓ Middle English (c. 1200 CE) folk / folc "people, common multitude, human community" ↓ FOLK "people, communal group, nation"

[COMPOUND SYNTHESIS]
Middle / Early Modern English (c. 1450 CE) kynnesfolk / kinsfolk "collective people of one's kin; extended family" ↓ Modern English (Present) KINSFOLK "one's extended blood relatives and family network"
