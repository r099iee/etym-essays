• no: Similar because it is the ordinary negative answer; differs because no is neutral and universal, while nay is archaic, dialectal, or procedural.
  • not: Similar because both negate; differs because not negates verbs and clauses, while nay stands alone as sentence-answer or corrector.
  • never: Similar because nay contains ever (ne + ei, not ever); differs because never is temporal denial, while nay is absolute or rhetorical.
  • indeed / in fact: Similar because corrective nay means and indeed; differs because indeed merely confirms, while nay corrects upward to something stronger.
  • or rather / what is more: Similar because they introduce stronger restatements like nay; differs because they are multi-word and neutral, while nay is single and literary.
  • yea / aye: Similar because they are the paired archaic voting opposites; differs because yea affirms, nay denies — they are antonyms in division lobbies.
  • refusal / denial: Similar because a nay is a refusal; differs because they are abstract nouns, while nay is the quoted utterance.
  • naysaying: Similar because it derives from nay; differs because naysaying names habitual pessimism, while nay names one utterance.
  • by no means / on no account: Similar because they give emphatic no; differs because they are phrasal and modern, while nay is single and antique.
  • come now / now now: Similar because prohibitive nay manages discourse like come now; differs because come now cajoles, while nay checks with mock sternness.
