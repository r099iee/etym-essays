• Calm: Similar because both quiet agitation and restore peace; differs because calm is the plain everyday word for seas, nerves, and voices, while allay is formal and literary, reserved for fears, doubts, and pains.
  • Soothe: Similar because both ease pain and distress gently; differs because soothe caresses with softness (soothing balm, soothing voice), while allay lays the trouble itself to rest.
  • Alleviate: Similar because both lessen suffering in formal prose; differs because alleviate lightens burdens and symptoms technically (alleviating poverty), while allay quiets the feeling of fear or suspicion.
  • Assuage: Similar because both are literary verbs for easing grief and thirst; differs because assuage sweetens and satisfies craving (assuaging guilt), while allay stills and settles it.
  • Pacify: Similar because both bring peace to troubled parties; differs because pacify can impose peace by force or concession (pacifying a province), while allay persuades feeling into quiet.
  • Mollify: Similar because both soften anger and offence; differs because mollify works on the angry person (mollifying an insulted ally), while allay works on the fear or doubt itself.
  • Quell: Similar because both put down rising disturbance; differs because quell can crush rebellion with force (quelling a riot), while allay never wounds and only lays to rest.
  • Dispel: Similar because both banish doubts and shadows; differs because dispel scatters like light breaking cloud (dispelling myths), while allay settles like dust sinking to stillness.
  • Quench: Similar because both satisfy thirst and figurative fires; differs because quench extinguishes with finality (quenching flames), while allay eases by degrees and may leave embers.
