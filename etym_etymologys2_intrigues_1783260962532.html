PIE *terkʷ- "to twist, turn, wind" (reconstructed; c. 4500–2500 BCE) → Latin trīcae (pl.) "entanglements, perplexities, tricks, wiles" (attested 1st c. BCE; etymology debated — standardly grouped with the *terkʷ- twist-family as "twisted knots," but some authorities leave it of unknown origin) → Latin intrīcāre "to entangle, perplex" (in- "into" + trīcae; attested 1st c. BCE, e.g. Cicero) → Italian intrigare "to entangle, meddle, plot" (14th–16th c.) → intrigo "plot, tangle, intrigue" (14th–16th c.) → French intriguer "to puzzle, scheme, plot" (16th c.) → intrigue (noun, 16th c.) → English intrigue (verb, 1609, "to trick, perplex"; noun, c. 1611) → INTRIGUES (plural) ◄ TARGET Note on the debate: the immediate Latin base trīcae is itself of uncertain ancestry; the derivation from PIE *terkʷ- is the standard textbook view but is not unanimously accepted. The Latin-to-English path through Italian and French is secure.
