PIE (reconstructed)
*mer- / *mre-k- / *mar-
"to shimmer, sparkle, flash light; particle of dust"
(source of Greek marmairein "to sparkle", English marble)
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (reconstructed)
*mari- / *marīč-
"gleam, particle of light, spark"
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1500–1000 BCE; Rigveda)
marīci (मरीचि)
"ray of light, glittering particle, solar speck"
(also the proper name of the mind-born sage Marīci)
↓
Classical Sanskrit (Pāṇinian secondary vṛddhi derivative)
Mārīca (मारीच)
"descendant of Marīci; pertaining to light-rays; name of the demon"
(strengthening of vowel a → ā with taddhita patronymic suffix -a)
↓
Epic Sanskrit (c. 500 BCE; Valmiki Ramayana)
Mārīca (मारीच)
"the shapeshifting rakshasa who became the golden deer"
↓
Modern Indological Loan / English (19th c.–present)
Mārīca
"the legendary rakshasa and golden-deer sorcerer in the Ramayana"

Note on semantic irony: Sanskrit lexicographers highlight the profound etymological connection between Mārīca (the glittering illusionist), marīci (a ray of light), and marīcikā (a desert mirage). The demon's name literally encodes the shimmering, illusory light that produces a fatal mirage, matching his mythological role as the glittering golden deer that leads the heroes astray.
