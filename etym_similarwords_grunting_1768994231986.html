• Groan: Similar because both are low, guttural vocal sounds of the throat; differs because a groan is long and sustained, expressing pain, sorrow, or protest, while a grunt is short, explosive, and breathless — the sound of effort, rarely an appeal for sympathy.
  • Snort: Similar because both are harsh, unarticulated noises; differs because a snort forces air through the nose and implies contempt or alarm, while a grunt stays in the throat and is unconscious.
  • Growl: Similar because both are low and guttural; differs because a growl is threatening and aimed at another, whereas a grunt is self-contained — the by-product of force, not a warning.
  • Mumble: Similar because both are indistinct vocalisations; differs because mumbling remains intended as speech, mouthing words badly, while grunting has abandoned words entirely.
  • Grumble: Similar because both are low, throaty, discontented noises; differs because a grumble is a muttered complaint directed at circumstances, with the intention of being half-heard, while a grunt carries no complaint at all — it is mere effort or acknowledgment.
  • Murmur: Similar because both are low, soft vocal sounds; differs because a murmur is a continuous, gentle, sometimes musical stream of sound, often deliberately communicative, while a grunt is a single explosive burst with no musicality.
  • Croak: Similar because both are harsh, throaty sounds; differs because a croak is the strained, hoarse sound of a failing or rough voice (as of a frog or a sore throat), while a grunt is a normal effort-sound that makes no claim to voice at all.
  • Squeal: Similar because both are animal noises of the body; differs because a squeal is high and panicked — the opposite end of the vocal range — while a grunt is low, brief, and unemotional.
  • Wheeze: Similar because both are bodily breathing sounds; differs because a wheeze is the whistling sound of laboured breathing through constricted airways, while a grunt is a voiced throat-sound made with effort.
  • Huff: Similar because both are short, expelled sounds of effort or displeasure; differs because a huff is a puff of air blown out in irritation, while a grunt is a voiced sound produced deep in the throat.
