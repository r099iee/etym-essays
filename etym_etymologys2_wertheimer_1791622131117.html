Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*wer- + *ḱeym-
"high ground, river land" + "home, village"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*wariþuz + *haimaz
"river island, elevated dry land" + "settlement"
↓
Old High German (c. 8th c.)
werid + heim
"river peninsula" + "homestead"
↓
Middle High German (c. 12th c.)
Wertheim + -ære
"town on the river island" + "inhabitant of"
↓
German Surname (c. 17th c.)
Wertheimer
"native or person from Wertheim"
↓
Modern Psychology (1912)
Wertheimer
"Max Wertheimer, primary founder of Gestalt psychology"

Note: Toponymic surname derived from the historic town of Wertheim on the Main river; compound of Old High German werid ("island, riverbank, dry land amid water") and heim ("home, dwelling"), with demonymic/agentive suffix -er.
