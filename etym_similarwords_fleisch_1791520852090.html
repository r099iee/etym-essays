• meat: Similar because it is the usual English translation of the culinary sense; differs because English meat never means the living body and historically meant "food" in general.
  • flesh: Similar as the exact English cognate and the closest translation of the anatomical sense; differs because flesh is not the ordinary food word and often sounds biological or literary.
  • beef: Similar as a mass term for edible animal tissue; differs because it is species-specific (cattle) and culinary, from French bœuf, not a general word for meat.
  • pork: Similar as a food term for pig meat; differs in being species-specific and historically French-derived, whereas Fleisch is the general German category.
  • mutton: Similar as sheep meat; differs in specificity and in its archaic, formal or butchery register.
  • venison: Similar as game meat; differs in being restricted to deer and marked as formal or hunting vocabulary.
  • muscle: Similar as soft contractile tissue; differs because it is anatomical and scientific and is not used for meat as food.
  • tissue: Similar as a mass of biological cells; differs because it is a broad scientific term covering organs and plants, not a food word.
  • pulp: Similar as the soft edible part of fruit, matching Fruchtfleisch; differs in being botanical and never used of animal flesh.
  • carrion: Similar as dead animal flesh; differs because it denotes decaying flesh unfit for human food and carries a strongly negative charge.
  • brawn: Similar as muscular flesh, especially of pork; differs in its culinary and slightly archaic register and its association with pressed meat.
