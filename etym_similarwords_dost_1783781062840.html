• do: Similar because it is the same verb — dost is literally "thou do"; differs because do is the modern person-neutral form used for all persons, while dost is the archaic second-person singular with the -st ending and a reverential or archaic register.
  • doest: Similar because it is the same second-person singular present form ("thou doest"); differs because doest preserves the fuller, syllabic ending -est (favoured in verse for metre), while dost is the contracted, monosyllabic form used in prose and in the Authorized Version.
  • didst: Similar because it is the same verb's second-person singular past tense ("thou didst"); differs because didst is past tense while dost is present tense, the pair showing the ablaut/tense contrast of the irregular verb do.
  • doth / doeth: Similar because they are the third-person singular forms of the same verb ("he doth"); differs because doth refers to he/she/it, while dost refers specifically to thou, so the two never interchange in the same clause.
  • performest: Similar because both are archaic second-person singular forms expressing action; differs because performest is a full lexical verb with the specific sense "to carry out a task," while dost is the general, highly bleached verb of action, doing, and auxiliary support.
  • makest: Similar because both come from roots meaning "to put, make" and both can express causing an effect ("thou dost/makest it known"); differs because makest means concretely "to produce or cause," while dost is the general verb of action and auxiliary.
  • art (thou art): Similar because both are second-person singular present forms of a highly irregular verb in early modern English; differs because art is the present of be ("you are"), while dost is the present of do ("you do").
  • thou dost err / thou errest: Similar because both are early modern ways of saying "you err" with the -st ending; differs because dost err uses the emphatic auxiliary do for stress, while errest is the simple inflected form without auxiliary.
