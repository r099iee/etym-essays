• Tired: Similar because both describe a need for rest; differs because tired is the ordinary everyday word for needing sleep or rest, while weary implies a deeper, heavier, more prolonged exhaustion of body, mind, or spirit.
  • Exhausted: Similar because both indicate extreme tiredness; differs because exhausted stresses a total depletion of physical energy, while weary can be mental, emotional, or existential as much as physical.
  • Fatigued: Similar because both refer to weariness from exertion; differs because fatigued is a formal, clinical term, while weary carries poetic and emotional depth.
  • Drained: Similar because both involve loss of vitality; differs because drained emphasizes an emptying of energy or emotion, while weary emphasizes the ongoing burden that produced the tiredness.
  • Jaded: Similar because both describe being worn out by experience; differs because jaded implies cynicism and a loss of interest or appreciation from overexposure, while weary simply reflects exhaustion or impatience.
  • Worn out: Similar because both describe being used up by effort; differs because worn out is colloquial and physical, while weary often adds a spiritual or emotional dimension.
  • Languid: Similar because both suggest low energy; differs because languid describes a graceful, relaxed slowness of manner, while weary describes an unhappy, burdensome exhaustion.
  • Listless: Similar because both involve lacking energy or spirit; differs because listless is a lack of energy or interest as a standing condition, while weary is tiredness caused by strain or repetition.
  • Spent: Similar because both indicate used-up energy; differs because spent emphasizes that resources are entirely consumed, while weary emphasizes the sustained effort that wore one down.
  • World-weary: Similar because both describe exhaustion; differs because world-weary specifically denotes existential boredom and disillusionment with human life, while weary may be a plain state of being tired.
