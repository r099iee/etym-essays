• Cluster: Similar because it is a group of things together; differs because "cluster" emphasises arrangement and grouping without requiring the things to touch, while a clump is dense, irregular, and massed together.
  • Thicket: Similar because it is a dense growth of trees or shrubs; differs because a thicket is tangled, wild undergrowth too rough to shelter under, while a clump is a compact, accidental knot of growth.
  • Copse: Similar because it is a small wood; differs because a copse is a managed, deliberately cut wood, whereas a clump seems to have grown together by accident, like a clot of earth.
  • Grove: Similar because it is a group of trees; differs because a grove is larger, more orderly, and often deliberately planted, while a clump is small, dense, and incidental.
  • Tussock: Similar because it is a small dense cluster; differs because a tussock is specifically a clump of grass, while "clump" applies to trees, plants, and lumps of earth alike.
  • Clod: Similar because it is a compact mass of earth, sharing the same Germanic kl- family; differs because a clod is specifically a lump of soil or dirt, while a clump can be living growth or aggregated material.
  • Mass: Similar because both denote a large body of material without fixed shape; differs because "mass" is the abstract, generic term without the dense, specific aggregation of a clump.
  • Bunch: Similar because both denote a group of things held or growing together; differs because a bunch is typically tied or collected (a bunch of keys, a bunch of grapes), while a clump grows or gathers itself.
  • Huddle: Similar because both describe things pressed closely together; differs because a huddle is a group of people or animals pressing together for warmth or conference, while a clump is inanimate growth or mass.
  • Aggregate: Similar because both denote things combined into a whole; differs because "aggregate" is the colourless, technical word for a collected total, while a clump is dense, irregular, and physical.
