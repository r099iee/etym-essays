CHAIN: FROM PIE TO TOILED (7 stages)

  1. PIE *steud- "to push, strike, beat" (reconstructed; with s-mobile — the base is also given as *(s)teu- "to push, knock". Sanskrit tudáti "he strikes, pushes" reflects the same root without the initial s; the exact shape of the reconstruction is debated).
  2. Latin tundō, tundere "to beat, pound, strike, bruise" (attested from early Latin, 3rd c. BCE; built on the zero grade of the root with a nasal infix, *tu-n-d-).
  3. Latin tūdicula "a small mallet or pounder; an instrument for crushing olives" (attested in classical writers such as Cato and Varro; an instrumental formation from tundere).
  4. Late Latin tūdiculāre "to pound, crush, stir about" (attested in late and medieval Latin; a denominative verb from tūdicula).
  5. Old French toeillier, toillier "to stir about, agitate, mix, rumple" (attested from the 12th c.); Anglo-French toiler "to struggle, contend, be harassed" developed the effortful sense.
  6. Middle English toilen "to pull about, struggle, labour" (attested from the 13th c.).
  7. English toil (verb) "to work hard and continuously" → TOILED (regular weak past tense and past participle, -ed — the exact form of this card).

HOMONYM GUARD

  • The plural noun "toils" meaning "nets, snares" (as in "caught in the toils of the law") is a different word: it descends from Latin tēla "web, loom" via Old French toile, and has nothing to do with the labour verb.
