- Roman Gallia - a proud imperial geography: the conquered Celtic land beyond the Alps, administered, taxed and gradually Latinised
- Late antique and Merovingian usage - "Gaul" still names the whole region even under Frankish kings, so Gregory of Tours writes the history of a land whose rulers are Franks
- Medieval French - Gaule retreats before the rising name France; "Gaul" becomes a bookish, backward-looking term for the ancient past
- Renaissance humanism - the spelling and the idea are revived from Latin; "Gaul" becomes a learned ornament for France and "a Gaul" a mock-heroic name for a Frenchman
- Nineteenth century - Vercingetorix is rebuilt as a national ancestor and "Nos ancêtres les Gaulois" enters schoolroom patriotism; the word turns heroic and populist
- Twentieth and twenty-first centuries - historical scholarship keeps "Gaul" technical, while Asterix turns it comic and affectionate; in ordinary English it is archaic, playful or deliberately learned
