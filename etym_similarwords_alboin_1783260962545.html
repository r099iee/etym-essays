• Odoacer: Similar because both are Germanic rulers who conquered parts of Italy after the fall of the western empire; differs because Odoacer deposed the last Western Roman Emperor in 476 and was himself overthrown by Theodoric, while Alboin led the Lombard invasion of 568, nearly a century later.
  • Theodoric: Similar because both are Germanic kings who ruled Italy; differs because Theodoric was an Ostrogoth ruling from Ravenna (493–526) as a semi-Roman heir, while Alboin was a Lombard establishing Germanic rule in northern Italy (Lombardy).
  • Alaric: Similar because both are famous Germanic kings associated with the fall of Roman power; differs because Alaric was a Visigoth who sacked Rome in 410, while Alboin was a Lombard who invaded Italy in 568 to found a lasting kingdom.
  • Clovis: Similar because both are 6th-century Germanic conquerors who founded dynasties in the continental provinces of the Roman empire, and are bracketed together on this card; differs because Clovis conquered Gaul for the Franks and converted to Catholic Christianity, while Alboin conquered northern Italy for the Lombards and remained a warrior king.
  • Genseric: Similar because both are Germanic kings who conquered Roman territory and sacked a great city; differs because Genseric was the Vandal king who sacked Rome from the sea in 455 and ruled a North African kingdom, while Alboin founded a kingdom in Italy.
  • Charlemagne: Similar because both are rulers connected with the Lombard kingdom; differs because Charlemagne is the Frankish emperor who conquered the Lombards in 774, ending the kingdom Alboin had founded two centuries earlier.
  • Elfwine / Ælfwine: Similar because both are forms of the same Germanic "elf-friend" name; differs because Elfwine is the Old English (West Saxon) form of the name — the structural twin of the Lombard Alboin — not the historical Lombard king himself.
