- 1524 to 1614: Navigational encounters and cartographic origins - Giovanni da Verrazzano recorded an island resembling Mediterranean Rhodes, while Dutch cartographer Adriaen Block mapped Roodt Eylandt (red island) due to its red clay bluffs.
- 1644: Formal colonial adoption - The colonial General Court formally decreed that Aquidneck Island should be officially named Rhode Island, merging with Providence under parliamentary patent.
- 1663: The Royal Charter of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations - King Charles II issued the landmark charter establishing an absolute sanctuary for liberty of conscience, embedding the name in world constitutional history.
- 1776 to 1790: Revolutionary defiance and constitutional sovereignty - Became the first colony to declare independence from Britain (May 4, 1776) and the final state to ratify the US Constitution, fiercely demanding a federal Bill of Rights.
- 19th Century: Industrial pioneering and Gilded Age summer splendor - World-renowned as the cradle of the American Industrial Revolution through Slater Mill, contrasted with Newport's lavish oceanfront high-society mansions.
- 20th to 21st Century: Ocean State identity and constitutional name modernization - Celebrated for ocean sailing, culinary culture, and elite universities; in 2020, voters modernized the state constitution by officially shortening the title to simply the State of Rhode Island.
