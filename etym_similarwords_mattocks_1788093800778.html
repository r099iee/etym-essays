• Pickaxe: Similar because both are heavy two-handed tools with a head set at right angles to a long handle for breaking ground; differs because the pickaxe ends in two sharp points for breaking rock, while the mattock carries at least one broad adze-like cutting blade for grubbing and cutting roots.
  • Adze: Similar because both mount a broad blade at right angles to the handle; differs because the adze's blade is for wood-working (shaping and smoothing timber, swung downward), while the mattock's is for digging and root-cutting in soil.
  • Grub hoe (grubbing hoe): Similar because both are bladed tools for breaking hard ground and severing roots; differs because the grub hoe is lighter, usually single-bladed, and worked from the side of the operator, whereas the mattock is heavier, often double-ended, and swung in a full chopping arc.
  • Hoe: Similar because both dig and weed in gardens; differs because the hoe is a light tool for shallow cultivation and weeding, while the mattock is a heavy two-handed tool for deep, hard, root-bound ground.
  • Spade: Similar because both are standard digging tools; differs because the spade's blade is in line with the shaft and is foot-driven to cut soil cleanly, while the mattock's blade is set across the shaft and works by chopping leverage.
  • Shovel: Similar because both are ground-working tools; differs because the shovel is for lifting and moving loose material, not for breaking hard ground or cutting roots.
  • War pick / pole weapon: Similar because both, in military contexts, trade on a heavy head at the end of a shaft; differs because war picks and pole-arms are purpose-built weapons, whereas the mattock is a labourer's tool pressed into violent service — the similarity is situational, not semantic.
