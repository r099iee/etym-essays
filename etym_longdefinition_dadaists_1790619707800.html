Dadaists denotes members and practitioners of Dada — the international anti-war, anti-art, anti-bourgeois avant-garde that erupted at the Cabaret Voltaire in Zurich in 1916 and spread to Berlin, Cologne, Paris, New York and beyond — here paired with the Italian Futurists as continuers of the war on print who read the alphabet as 26 objects for graphic elaboration.

Where Futurism worshipped the machine and the future, Dada performed negation: nonsense poetry, simultaneous babble, chance collage, readymades, photomontage, aggressive typography with chaotic fonts, tilted lines and shouted manifestos. Its heroes — Tristan Tzara, Hugo Ball, Emmy Hennings, Hans Arp, Hannah Höch, Raoul Hausmann, Kurt Schwitters, Marcel Duchamp, Francis Picabia, Man Ray — made illogic a moral protest against the logic that had produced the trenches of World War I.

  • 1. Strict historical sense: Zurich-Berlin-Paris Dadaists 1916-1924: cabaret performers, manifesto writers, Merz builders, readymade exhibitors; authors of sound poems (Karawane), cut-up poems from newspaper bags,chance collages; politically ranging from anarchist to communist (Berlin Dada fair, 1920).
  • 2. Typographic-material sense activated here: users of the letter as pure shape and sound rather than sense — Ball's phonetic poems in cubist costume, Tzara's newspaper poems, Hausmann's optophonetic posters, Schwitters' Systemschrift; direct heirs of Mallarmé's dice-throw and Marinetti's parole in libertà turned against war.
  • 3. Aesthetic attitude: absurd, nonsensical, deliberately infantile: Dada work courts incomprehension, boredom and laughter; the name itself, French nursery dada for hobby-horse, advertises baby-talk against professorial German Kultur. This explains the book's link to pranks piquing the bourgeoisie.
  • 4. Extended generic sense: any dadaistic provocateur: lowercased or adjectival dadaist now means anyone employing absurd juxtaposition, anti-art gesture or zany protest — punk flyers, meme collage, culture-jamming — without historical membership. Bridge is stylistic resemblance detached from 1916 context.
  • 5. Philosophical sense: in criticism, Dadaist can name principled irrationalism, embrace of chance and refusal of meaning-systems; allies to Surrealism (which Breton founded in 1924 by splitting from Dada) but more nihilistic, less psychoanalytic.

Usage notes: capitalized plural for members (the Berlin Dadaists); singular Dadaist noun/adjective (Dadaist collage); variant Dadaists vs Dadas (French prefers dadaïstes). RP pronunciation /ˈdɑːdɑːɪsts/, with two level long a vowels reflecting French dada /dada/ anglicized.

Cultural afterlife is vast: Dada legitimized collage, montage, performance art, concrete poetry, punk graphics and net-art trolling; its manifestos taught media theory that print conventions — linear order, uniform type, silent reading — are contestable social contracts. The source sentence captures exactly that lesson: Dadaists do not read letters for sense but handle them as things, sounding and showing them to break alphabetic reason.
