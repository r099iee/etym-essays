• Suffocated: Similar because both describe death or distress from lack of air; differs because suffocation is oxygen deprivation generally (an enclosed space, a gas), while choked focuses on a blocked airway.
  • Asphyxiated: Similar because both involve being deprived of breath; differs because asphyxiated is the clinical, technical term for oxygen starvation, while choked is everyday and can be metaphorical.
  • Strangled / throttled: Similar because both mean compressed at the throat to cut off breathing; differs because they stress an external grip (hands, cord) and usually deliberate violence, while choked can be accidental or internal.
  • Gagged: Similar because both can stop up the mouth or throat; differs because gagged means the mouth is stopped (often with cloth) or seizes with revulsion, and it carries a speech-suppression sense.
  • Smothered / stifled: Similar because both mean to suppress or kill breathing; differs because they emphasise covering (a cloth, a mass) or the suppression of a cough or sound rather than the throat blockage of choked.
  • Blocked / clogged / congested: Similar because all describe a passage that is stopped up (pipes, drains, airways); differs because these are mechanical and value-neutral, while choked (of a person) carries breathlessness and distress.
  • Overcome: Similar because "choked with emotion" equals "overcome with emotion"; differs because overcome is far broader — defeat, exhaustion, or any strong feeling — and has no physical air sense.
