- Heraldic description - Blazon entered as the technical heraldry verb: to describe or paint a coat of arms in the formal, coded language of shields and bearings.
- Public proclamation - By the 16th-17th centuries the word broadened from paint to noise: to blazon something abroad was to publish it, trumpet it, and declare it far and wide.
- Vivid adornment - A further extension made blazoning mean decorating anything with bold, conspicuous, formally patterned colour, so that the object announced itself like a banner or a coat of arms.
- Modern literary colour - In everyday speech the verb receded; today blazoned survives as a ceremonial, archaic-flavoured synonym of emblazoned, used when an object is made to proclaim identity and status through colour and symbol.
