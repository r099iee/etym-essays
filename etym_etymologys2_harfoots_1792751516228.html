--- SYNTHESIS & TOLKIEN'S TRANSLATION ---
Westron / Common Speech (fictional Third Age speech)
Kûd-dûkan
"hole-dwellers, hair-footed folk"
↓
Old English / Calqued Compound (reconstructed by Tolkien)
*hǣr-fōt
"hair-foot"
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–15th c.)
herfot
"hair-foot"
↓
Modern English / Literary Coinage (1954–present)
Harfoots
"the ancient hair-footed, burrowing Hobbit breed"

Note on morphology and translation: In Tolkien's linguistic framework (detailed in Appendix F to The Lord of the Rings), Westron names are translated into modern English according to their historical relationships. The genuine Westron name for this breed was Kûd-dûkan (containing the element kûd-, related to kuduk, the native Hobbit word for themselves meaning "hole-dweller"). Tolkien calqued this into English as Harfoot, based on Old English *hǣr-fōt ("hair-foot"), reflecting the phonetic shortening of Old English long vowels in compounds (as in holiday from holy day). Crucially, the plural is Harfoots rather than *Harfeet because it functions as an exocentric proper ethnonym rather than a transparent anatomical noun phrase, exactly parallel to the Hobbit surname Proudfoots.
