Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed)
*ḱes-
"to cut, separate"
(yielding Sanskrit śasati "cuts", Greek keazō "split", Latin castrare, careo)
↓
Proto-Italic (reconstructed)
*kasto-
"cut off, separated from fault, pure"
↓
Latin (3rd–2nd c. BCE)
castus
"morally pure, unpolluted, virtuous, chaste"
↓
Latin (2nd–1st c. BCE)
castīgāre
"to make pure; to correct, reprove, punish"
(compound of castus "pure" + agere "to drive, make, do")
↓
Old French (11th–12th c.)
chastier
"to punish, correct, discipline, teach"
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
chastisen
"to punish corporally, reprove, discipline"
(extended with frequentative suffix -ise from Old French chastisier / chastïement)
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
chastisour / chastiser
"one who punishes, corrects, or disciplines"
(from verb chastisen + Germanic agent suffix -er)
↓
Modern English (16th c.–present)
chastiser
"one who inflicts punishment, discipline, or moral rebuke"
