• against: Similar because against is the direct standard English counterpart of agin, sharing identical core spatial, temporal, and adversarial meanings; differs because against is the universally accepted standard literary and formal prestige form (possessing the historical excrescent genitive -es and inorganic dental stop -t), whereas agin is a regional, dialectal, or eye-dialect representation carrying strong rustic, colloquial, or working-class associations.
  • alongside: Similar because both can describe spatial contiguity and close physical proximity to an object; differs because alongside implies parallel alignment and companionship along the length of something, whereas right agin emphasizes direct, touching, and often abutting physical contact or friction against a boundary.
  • abutting: Similar because both denote direct physical contact with an adjoining boundary or structure; differs because abutting is a formal architectural and legal property term (e.g., an abutting parcel of land), whereas agin is an earthy, informal spoken vernacular expression.
  • opposed to: Similar because both describe ideological resistance, dissent, or disagreement; differs because opposed to belongs to formal political, intellectual, or institutional discourse, whereas dead agin evokes stubborn, instinctive, or grassroots contrariness ("agin the government").
  • contrary to: Similar because both denote being in conflict with rules, expectations, or directions; differs because contrary to is an analytical preposition describing logical incompatibility or violation of doctrine, while agin reflects visceral, personal opposition.
  • confronting: Similar because both imply facing an opposing entity; differs because confronting emphasizes an active, face-to-face challenge or aggressive stare-down, whereas agin can be purely passive spatial adjacency (leaning against a post).
  • versus: Similar because both describe opposing parties in a contest or dispute; differs because versus is Latinate, legalistic, and used in sporting or judicial contexts (plaintiff vs. defendant), whereas agin belongs to informal vernacular speech.
  • adjacent to: Similar because both signify being situated right next to something; differs because adjacent to is clinical, geographical, and detached, lacking the evocative, tangible physical contact and rich dialectal warmth of right agin.
