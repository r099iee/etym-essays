Origin unknown / Pre-Greek substrate: the name Laistrygónes (Λαιστρυγόνες) has no secure Indo-European etymology; it is generally regarded as a Pre-Greek (substrate) name or an unanalyzable folk formation. No PIE root can be responsibly proposed, and the chain therefore begins with the first attested Greek form. Stated explicitly: earlier stages are unknown, not merely unattested. 2. Ancient Greek Laistrygónes (Λαιστρυγόνες), plural, "the Laestrygonians, the giant cannibal tribe of Telepylus" (attested in Homer, Odyssey Book 10, 8th c. BCE). 3. Latin Laestrygones (also Lestrygones) — the Latinized plural of the Greek name, used in Roman literature and antiquarian tradition from the classical period. 4. English Laestrygonian — adjective/noun formed on the Latin stem Laestrygoni- + the English adjectival suffix -ian, attested in classical scholarship and literary prose from the 16th–19th centuries (notably in Edward Gibbon's Decline and Fall, the immediate source of the study sentence). ◄ TARGET Debated/nuanced note: the -ae-/-e- and -y-/-i- spelling variants (Laestrygonian vs. Lestrigonian) reflect the Latin rendering of Greek ai and upsilon; the ultimate etymology of the tribal name is debated and most likely Pre-Greek or otherwise non-Indo-European, so no derivation chain can be given below the Homeric attestation.
