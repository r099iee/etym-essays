Old Norse (c. 900–1200)
baggi
‘pack, bundle, bag’
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
bagge
‘sack of cloth or hide’
↓
Middle English verb (15th c.)
baggen
‘to put into a bag’
↓
Early Modern English (18th c., participle)
bagging
‘filling sacks; hanging loose’
↓
Modern English (current)
bagging
‘packing in sacks; bulging’

Note: the ultimate origin of bagge beyond Old Norse baggi is uncertain and debated; older handbooks proposed Proto-Germanic *balg- joining it to belly, bellows, and bulge, but modern etymologists treat the word as of obscure origin, possibly borrowed through trade cant, and the *balg- link as plausible but unproven.
