• Bundle: Similar because both mean a collection of things wrapped or tied together; differs because bundle is the general word for any tied package, while bindle is specifically the hobo's blanket-wrapped bundle of possessions carried on the shoulder.
  • Pack: Similar because both describe a load of belongings carried on the back; differs because pack usually means a manufactured backpack (or a supply load), while bindle is the improvised cloth-wrapped roll of the tramp.
  • Swag: Similar because both are the travelling worker's cloth-wrapped bedroll; differs because swag is the Australian and New Zealand bushman's term (and later meant stolen goods), while bindle is the American hobo's word.
  • Bedroll: Similar because both are the traveller's rolled-up bedding; differs because bedroll refers specifically to the rolled bedding, while bindle is the full bundle of all one's possessions, bedding included.
  • Knapsack: Similar because both are a carried load of personal belongings; differs because knapsack is a manufactured bag worn on the back with straps, while bindle is an improvised cloth-wrapped parcel.
  • Rucksack / Backpack: Similar because both are portable carriers of possessions; differs because these are modern manufactured bags, while bindle is the archaic, romantic cloth roll of the hobo.
  • Duffel (bag): Similar because both hold a traveller's belongings; differs because a duffel is a stitched cylindrical bag with a drawstring, while a bindle is tied in a loose cloth or blanket, often on a stick.
  • Kit: Similar because both name a set of personal belongings; differs because kit stresses a soldier's or worker's equipment collection, while bindle stresses the tramp's entire movable life.
  • Bindlestiff: Similar because both come from the same hobo slang family; differs because bindlestiff is the person who carries the bindle (the migrant worker), not the bundle itself.
  • Carryall: Similar because both are a container for one's belongings; differs because carryall is a large manufactured bag or case, while bindle is the tied cloth roll.
  • Sack: Similar because both can be a cloth container for belongings; differs because sack is a plain bag without the hobo connotation and without the shoulder-stick image.
  • Packet / Wrap: Similar because both describe a small wrapped parcel (as in the drug-slang bindle); differs because packet and wrap are general terms for small folded parcels, while bindle in that sense is specifically slang for a folded paper of powdered drugs.
