Note (reconstructions flagged): the PIE root, the Vulgar Latin verb *scalāre, and the intermediate *scand-sla are scholarly reconstructions, not attested forms — they are standard in every etymological dictionary but are marked here for honesty. Separate word families (do not confuse): (a) scale "fish scale / to descale / scaled = covered with scales" comes from a different stock — Old English scealu 'shell, husk' and Old French escale 'shell, scale', from Germanic *skalō, ultimately PIE *skel- 'to cut, split'; (b) scale "graduated marks / balance pan" branches off Latin scala again — the 'ladder of marks' on a ruler (English noun, 1550s) and the shell-shaped pan of a balance (c. 1300) — so senses (2) and (3) of 'scaled' answer to wholly different chains.
