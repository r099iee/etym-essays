- Late 16th Century - Physical blinding by intense light; temporary visual impairment.
- Early 17th Century - Metaphorical extension to being overwhelmed or confused by something brilliant, impressive, or astonishing (e.g., talent, beauty, wealth).
- 19th-20th Century - Continued use in both literal and figurative senses, with the metaphorical sense becoming increasingly common in literature and everyday speech.
- Early 20th Century - Specific technical application in "dazzle camouflage" for ships, emphasizing visual confusion and deception.
- Contemporary Usage - Widely used to describe both the physical effect of bright light and the psychological effect of being captivated or impressed by something extraordinary, sometimes with a nuance of superficiality or showiness.
