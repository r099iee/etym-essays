Single linear chain for DISCRETION
Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European root *krei- (variant reconstruction *krey-), "to sieve, separate, discriminate, decide," c. 4500–3000 BCE. The root of sifting grain and of deciding; attested by Greek krī́nō "I separate, judge," Old Irish criathar "a sieve," and Germanic words for a "riddle" (sieve).
Stage 2 — Latin cernō, cernere, crēvī, crētum, "to sift, separate, distinguish, perceive," attested 2nd c. BCE (Plautus, Terence); formed from the PIE root via a nasal-infix present *kri-ne/o-.
Stage 3 — Latin discernō, discernere, discrēvī, discrētum, "to separate apart, distinguish, discern," attested 1st c. BCE (Cicero, Caesar); dis- "apart, asunder" + cernere.
Stage 4 — Latin discrētus, -a, -um, past participle of discernō, "separated, distinct," 1st c. BCE; also used as an adjective "discreet, prudent" (the source of English discreet and discrete).
Stage 5 — Latin discrētiō, discrētiōnis (feminine noun), "separation, distinction; discernment, judgment," Late Latin 4th–5th c. CE; elevated by Saint Benedict's Rule (c. 516–530) into the "mother of all virtues" (mater virtutum).
Stage 6 — Old French discrecion (later discretion), "discernment, prudence, judgment; power to decide," 12th–13th c., reaching English through Anglo-Norman conduits.
Stage 7 — Middle English discrecioun, "discernment; prudence, tact; freedom of decision," 14th c. (Chaucer writes discrecioun).
Stage 8 — English discretion, 15th c., with the modern spelling settling by the 16th c., "tactful prudence; freedom of decision," — FINAL NODE discretion.
Debate note: The connection of Latin cernere to PIE *krei- is traditional and widely accepted (Pokorny, Watkins, Etymonline), but the exact PIE shape is reconstructed variously as *krei- or *krey-, and the nasal-infix present *kri-ne/o- underlying cernō is a specialized formation. Every stage of the recorded Latin–French–English chain (stages 2–8) is attested and undisputed; only the prehistoric shape of stage 1 varies between reconstructions.
