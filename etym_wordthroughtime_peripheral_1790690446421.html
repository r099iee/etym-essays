- Geometrical boundary (Antiquity–Middle Ages) - periphery is Euclid’s circumference; neutral technical line, no value judgment.
- Anatomical and urban edge (1600s–1800s) - peripheral nerves, districts, vision; still spatial-literal, part of an ordered body or city.
- Imperial subordination (late 1800s–mid 1900s) - peripheral colonies and markets; edge now implies governed-from-centre, lesser power.
- Systems abstraction (mid-late 20th c.) - core vs. periphery in economics, persuasion, computing; means structurally secondary or external device.
- Everyday demotion (present) - a peripheral issue or role is dispensable garnish; critics contest the label because centre is itself at stake.
