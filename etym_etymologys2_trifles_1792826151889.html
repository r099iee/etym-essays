Old French (12th–13th c.)
trufle, trifle
‘mockery, raillery, idle tale’
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
trifle
‘false tale, frivolous matter’
↓
Middle English verb (late 14th c.)
triflen
‘to speak idly, act frivolously’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
trifle
‘thing of little value; dessert’
↓
Modern English (current, plural)
trifles
‘small matters, trivialities’

Note: the deeper origin of Old French trufle is disputed; older dictionaries connect it with truffe meaning jest or trick, perhaps on the image of something puffed up with air, but no certain Latin ancestor is established, so the chain properly begins with the attested French noun.
