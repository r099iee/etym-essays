Whizzes is the third-person singular present form of the verb whizz, meaning to move very quickly with a humming, hissing or rushing sound, and by extension to rush or speed in general, and also the plural of the noun whizz meaning a swift rushing movement or, colloquially, an expert. As a verb it evokes both speed and sound together: bees, bullets, cars and thoughts whizz. The tone is vivid, informal and energetic.

The central verbal sense is rapid movement accompanied or implied by a rushing noise. A secondary verbal sense is to pass quickly through time or space without emphasis on sound, as in the holidays whizzed past. A third sense is mental: the brain whizzes, meaning thoughts race. As a noun, a whizz can mean a rushing sound or movement, as in the whizz of traffic, and in a quite separate development a person who is brilliant at something, as in a computer whizz, also spelled whiz.

Polysemy and extensions today include:

  • To rush with hissing sound: arrows whizz overhead. This is the original imitative sense where sound and motion coincide.
  • To speed generally: cars whizz along the motorway. The sound component fades; only speed remains. The source brain whizzes along belongs here, extended metaphorically to thought.
  • Time passing quickly: the afternoon whizzes by. The bridge is subjective speed: enjoyable time feels like swift motion.
  • Noun: rushing sound or trip: the whizz of a drone, a whizz round the shops. Converted from the verb by zero-derivation.
  • Noun: expert, prodigy: a maths whizz, whiz-kid. The bridge is swiftness through difficulties reinterpreted as cleverness, especially in American English.
  • Slang: to urinate or to feel dizzy stimulation: to have a whizz. Marginal slang playing on fizz and buzz, sharing the short-i plus sibilant sound-symbolism of sudden release.

Grammatically, whizzes adds regular -es after a sibilant stem, pronounced as an extra syllable /ɪz/. It contrasts with buzzes and fizzes only in initial consonant symbolism: wh- suggests airy rush, b- suggests low drone, f- suggests effervescence. Collocations include whizz along, whizz past, whizz by, whizz off and whizz through. The word is informal but not vulgar, suitable for lively narration and comic exaggeration, exactly its function in the motoring metaphor of a brain exceeding the speed limit.

For learners, note spelling and pronunciation: double -zz plus -es gives an extra syllable, contrasting with singular whizz. The noun-verb conversion is fully productive, so dictionaries list whizz as both. Aspectually the verb is punctual and iterative, suited to adverbials like along, past, by and off, and it readily forms phrasal combinations that preserve the core image of fast noisy transit applied to vehicles, time and thought alike. Review cue: hear the -izz- as speed made audible. Plural nouns and verb forms coincide here. That extra syllable is the tell.
