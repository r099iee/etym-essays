• Wilderness: Similar because both name vast, trackless, uncultivated country beyond settlement; differs because 'wilderness' is the generic common noun and, since Milton, an emblem of desolation, while 'Wilderland' is a named realm, more geographical and epic than moral.
  • Wasteland: Similar because both denote uninhabited country beyond settled lands; differs because 'wasteland' stresses barrenness and ruin — land that grows nothing — while Wilderland is fertile in danger, full of forest, waters, and beasts.
  • The wilds: Similar because both refer to rough, untamed country; differs because 'the wilds' is colloquial and informal ('the wilds of nowhere'), while 'Wilderland' is ceremonious, archaic, and capitalized.
  • Backcountry: Similar because both denote land beyond the settled centres; differs because 'backcountry' is a modern, practical, administrative word for remote regions, lacking Wilderland's menace and mythic weight.
  • Borderlands: Similar because both suggest land at or beyond the edge of the known; differs because borderlands are a political frontier between settled territories, while Wilderland lies beyond the last outpost of order altogether.
  • Marches: Similar because both name frontier country; differs because 'marches' is a medieval term for a disputed border zone between kingdoms, not an untamed, unmapped region.
  • Hinterland: Similar because both denote country lying behind a coast or frontier; differs because 'hinterland' is a modern economic-geographical term for the region served by a port or city, with no sense of danger or beasts.
  • Outback: Similar because both name vast, sparsely settled interior country; differs because 'outback' is specifically Australian, colonial, and practical, while 'Wilderland' is archaic and mythological.
