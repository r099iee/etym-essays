PIE *plet- / *plat- "to spread out, be broad and flat" (the root of breadth; the source of Greek platýs, English flat, German flach, Sanskrit prath- "to spread") → Proto-Hellenic *platw- "broad" → Ancient Greek platýs (πλατύς) "broad, flat, wide" (with the derivatives plátos "breadth", plateîa "broad street", whence English place, piazza, platz, and plátanos "plane tree") → Ancient Greek Plátōn (Πλάτων) "the Broad One, broad-shouldered", the nickname of the philosopher Aristocles (c. 428–348 BC), said to have been given by his wrestling coach for his broad build → Latin Plātō "Plato" → English Plato. Note on the debate: the standard ancient tradition (Diogenes Laërtius, echoed by later doxographers) says the nickname was given because of his broad shoulders, chest, or forehead; the competing gloss connecting the name with the breadth of his style or with plátos "breadth" of discourse is a secondary rationalisation. What is certain is that Platon is a derivative of platýs "broad," and that the birth name Aristocles was displaced by the nickname. The PIE root is sometimes given as *plet- or *pleth₂-, with the -t-/-th- alternation across branches, but the Greek form platýs is the secure reflex of the "broad, flat, spread out" root.
