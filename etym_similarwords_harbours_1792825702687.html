• Ports: Similar because both are coastal places where ships load, unload, and shelter; differs because a port stresses commerce and administration (customs, cargo, port authorities), while harbours stress shelter itself — every port needs a harbour, but a tiny fishing harbour need never be a port.
  • Havens: Similar because both promise shelter and safety, at sea and in metaphor; differs because a haven is the poetic and general word (safe haven, tax haven), while harbours are working places of quays, buoys, and mooring chains.
  • Anchorages: Similar because both are spots where ships lie at anchor out of the weather; differs because an anchorage is merely suitable holding ground (often open roadstead), while a harbour adds enclosing land or walls plus quays and services.
  • Docks: Similar because both belong to the waterfront world of ships and cargo; differs because docks are the built structures for loading and repair (dry dock, dockside crane), while harbours are the sheltered waters themselves that contain the docks.
  • Marinas: Similar because both shelter small craft in pontoons and berths; differs because a marina is modern, recreational, and purpose-built for yachts, while harbours carry the older working history of fishing fleets and merchantmen.
  • Roadsteads: Similar because both give ships refuge near shore; differs because a roadstead (or roads) is an open-water anchorage off the coast, exposed to some winds, while a harbour is enclosed and safe from all sides.
  • Refuges: Similar because both shelter the vulnerable from danger; differs because a refuge is general and often landlocked (mountain refuge, women's refuge), while harbours are of the sea first and of the soul only by metaphor.
  • Sanctuaries: Similar because both protect what enters them, ships or creatures; differs because a sanctuary carries sacred or legal immunity (bird sanctuary, right of sanctuary), while a harbour offers physical shelter with no holy law behind it.
  • Coves: Similar because many natural harbours began as coves that fishermen found kindly; differs because a cove is the small rounded coastal hollow itself, while a harbour is that hollow put to nautical use, often improved with piers and lights.
