- 18th–19th c. natural-history prose - the two components horny and plated circulate separately among zoologists: horny names the hard, translucent keratin material of a covering, plated names sheets of armour fitted edge to edge; the raw materials of the compound already lie ready in the scientific register.
- Early 20th c. (A Princess of Mars) - Burroughs welds them into the single epithet horny-plated for the vortlups: the compound is born as a double act of wonder and taxonomy, fusing the keratinous hardness of horn with the segmented sheet-armor of a saurian, at once clinical and grotesque.
- 20th c. - the compound circulates in genre fiction and descriptive natural history for real armoured reptiles; it keeps its hybrid image - a body sealed in a living suit of horn - and its faintly fabulous register, half naturalist, half wonder.
- Contemporary - rare and deliberately technical, horny-plated still performs its original double act: it names a segmented, rigid, keratinous body-armour, and it preserves the wonder of a creature assembled, like the vortlup, from the parts of several animals.
