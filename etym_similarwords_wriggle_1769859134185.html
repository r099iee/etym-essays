• Wiggle: Similar because both describe quick twisting side-to-side movement; differs because wiggle is lighter, faster, and more playful (a dancing wiggle), often of a single part, while wriggle is heavier, more effortful, and suggests snake-like whole-body movement or confinement.
  • Squirm: Similar because both describe twisting, uneasy movement; differs because squirm emphasises discomfort, embarrassment, or shame (to squirm under questioning), while wriggle stresses active, persistent effort to move or escape.
  • Writhe: Similar because both describe twisting movements of the body; differs because writhe is slower, more violent, and usually driven by pain or agony, whereas wriggle is quick, small, and energetic rather than anguished.
  • Squiggle: Similar because both describe twisting, wavy motion or shape; differs because squiggle usually refers to a wavy line or scribble on a page, not to bodily movement.
  • Twist: Similar because both describe turning movement; differs because twist is a single rotation of a part about an axis, while wriggle is a repeated, small, side-to-side oscillation of the whole body.
  • Slither: Similar because both describe the movement of limbless creatures; differs because slither is a smooth, gliding slide over a surface (a snake slithering), while wriggle is an active, twisting, small-scale contortion.
  • Worm (one's way): Similar because both describe moving by twisting or insinuation; differs because worm as a verb means to insinuate oneself gradually and slyly into favour or position, while wriggle focuses on the physical or evasive twisting itself.
  • Undulate: Similar because both describe wavy movement; differs because undulate is smooth, regular, wave-like motion of a surface or body, while wriggle is jerky, irregular, and effortful.
  • Jiggle: Similar because both describe small quick movements; differs because jiggle is a rapid shaking up and down or to and fro of a loosely held thing, without the sinuous, snake-like character of wriggling.
  • Evade (figurative sense): Similar because both describe escaping something; differs because evade is a general, neutral word for avoidance, while "wriggle out of" is specifically a twisting, sly, slightly contemptible escape from duty or blame.
  • Dodge (figurative sense): Similar because both describe avoiding something by quick manoeuvre; differs because dodge is a sharp, abrupt lateral move (literally ducking), while wriggle is a sustained, serpentine evasion.
  • Duck (out of) (figurative sense): Similar because both describe avoiding an obligation; differs because duck suggests a quick, evasive lowering of the head, while wriggle suggests a longer, twistier, more artful escape.
