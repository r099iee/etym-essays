- 14th c. - shelf (Old English scylfe) is the household board on which things are laid; shelving names the whole apparatus of shelves, a homely word of the domestic wall.
- 16th c. - the geological turn: the verb to shelve comes to mean 'to slope gradually', of shores and seabeds that descend in ledges; the sense is probably born at sea, among sailors and hydrographers.
- 17th–19th c. - shelving becomes the nautical and geological word for a gradual, stepped incline into the water - a shore a boat can approach, a beach that shoals gently.
- Contemporary - the word lives double: the domestic noun for the household shelf-apparatus, and the rare descriptive participle for land that rises or falls in flat ledges - a slope not smooth but built, ledge by ledge, by the slow withdrawal of water.
