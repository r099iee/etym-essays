- Earliest English sense (14th c., 'tyme', spelled as it sounded, rhyming with 'time') - simply the aromatic herb of the mint family, a plain kitchen and medicinal plant.
- 15th-16th c. - Renaissance Latin scholarship restored the silent 'h', making the written word 'thyme'; the spelling grew classical and learned while the sound stayed /taɪm/.
- Medieval tradition - the bee-plant and the herb of courage: knights embroidered sprigs of it on their scarves, and the bees of Mount Hymettus made from it the most famous honey of antiquity.
- Modern - an everyday seasoning of roasts, stews, and stuffings whose scent has become a byword for dry, sun-baked Mediterranean upland; its fragrance evokes memory and place, not just taste.
