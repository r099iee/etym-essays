• Dosiadas: Similar because he was a fellow Hellenistic author of shaped altar poems; differs because he imitated Simias rather than originating the form.
  • Theocritus: Similar because he wrote patterned pastoral and a famous Syrinx shape poem; differs because his fame rests on Idylls and pastoral invention, not primarily on visual games.
  • Callimachus: Similar because he was a learned Alexandrian innovator prizing brevity and wit; differs because his innovation was elegiac and aetiological rather than visual.
  • Philetas of Cos: Similar because he was an early Hellenistic scholar-poet and possible teacher milieu of Simias; differs because his reputation is glossarial and elegiac, not figured verse.
  • Apollinaire: Similar because he revived shape poetry in modern Calligrammes; differs because he worked two millennia later in French avant-garde print culture.
  • concrete poet / pattern poet: Similar because they make meaning through typographic shape; differs because it is a generic role, while Simias is the historical originator.
  • epigrammatist: Similar because Simias also wrote short inscribed epigrams; differs because epigram stresses brevity and dedication, not necessarily figured layout.
  • grammarian-poet: Similar because it captures his dual scholarly-creative identity; differs because it is a descriptive category, not a personal name.
