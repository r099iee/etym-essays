Proto-Germanic

*fretaną
"to devour"

↓
Old English
fretan
"to devour"
↓
Middle English
(12th–13th c.)
freten
"to devour; to gnaw"
↓
English
(12th c.)
fret
"to be anxious, vexed"
(figurative: "to gnaw at the mind")
↓
English
(late 16th c.)
fretful
"inclined to fret; irritable"
↓
English
(early 17th c.)
fretfully
"in a distressed, irritable manner"
Note: The sense shifted from physical "devour, gnaw" to mental "gnaw at the mind, worry" (the fret/worry sense, from the 12th c.), which yields the modern meaning.
