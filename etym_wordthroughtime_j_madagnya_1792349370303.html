- [Late Vedic Era, c. 1000–600 BCE] - used in Vedic ritual texts and genealogies as a prestigious patronymic designating members of the illustrious Jamadagni-Bhārgava priestly family - [perception: respected as the sacred lineage title of high-ranking priests skilled in Vedic rituals and fire ceremonies]
- [Epic Period, c. 500 BCE–200 CE] - solidified in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata as the terrifying proper epithet for Rāma Paraśurāma, the warrior-Brahmin who annihilated corrupt Kshatriyas - [perception: feared and venerated as an unstoppable divine warrior who restores cosmic balance when kings degenerate into tyranny]
- [Purāṇic Era, c. 300–1000 CE] - canonized across the Purāṇas as the sixth avatar in the classical Daśāvatāra scheme of Lord Viṣṇu and immortalized as an immortal Cirañjīvī residing on Mount Mahendra - [perception: worshipped as a divine protector of dharma and an eternal master of martial and spiritual disciplines]
- [Regional Coastal Folklore, c. 8th–18th c. CE] - celebrated in western and southern India (Konkan, Goa, Kerala) as the divine creator who reclaimed the coastal lands (*Paraśurāma Kṣetra*) from the sea - [perception: revered as a benevolent ancestral founder-deity and civilizing culture-hero of the southwestern seaboard]
- [Modern Era, 19th–21st c.] - analyzed in literary criticism, comparative mythology, and film as a complex, dramatic archetype of divine rage against political oppression - [perception: regarded as a powerful cultural emblem of uncompromising righteousness and the limits of state power]
