• Brawl: Similar because both denote a noisy public fight; differs because a brawl is colloquial and need not carry legal colouring or the sense of frightening the peace.
  • Fracas: Similar because both name a loud disturbance; differs because a fracas leans toward the comic or rowdy and is weaker on bloodshed and legal gravity.
  • Melee: Similar because both describe chaotic fighting; differs because a melee highlights confused multi-person combat rather than the public-peace breach.
  • Riot: Similar because both are public disorder; differs because a riot implies larger-scale, often group or political disorder, not a private fight.
  • Assault: Similar because both involve violence; differs because an assault is typically one-sided unlawful attack in modern legal English, while an affray involves mutual fighting that terrifies the public.
  • Altercation: Similar because both name a quarrel; differs because an altercation may be purely verbal, whereas an affray is physical and public.
  • Duel: Similar because both are fights; differs because a duel is regulated single combat with rules — the opposite of the messy, unregulated affray.
  • Scuffle: Similar because both are fights; differs because a scuffle is minor, brief, and physical, lacking the scale and legal weight of an affray.
  • Disturbance: Similar because both are public disorder; differs because a disturbance may be non-violent (noise, obstruction), while an affray is specifically a fight.
  • Fray: Similar because both name a fight; differs because a fray is a general skirmish or tangle, without the legal sense of breaching the peace.
  • Clash: Similar because both involve confrontation; differs because a clash can be verbal, political, or non-public, while an affray is a public physical fight.
