• Bric-a-brac: Similar because both name accumulations of small miscellaneous objects of little use; differs because bric-a-brac is bought deliberately as ornament, while mathoms arrive unasked as gifts and circulate unwanted.
  • Knick-knacks: Similar because both are small decorative trifles cluttering shelves; differs because knick-knacks are chosen and displayed with pride, while mathoms are kept from thrift or guilt and quietly re-gifted.
  • White elephants: Similar because both are unwanted presents that keep circulating; differs because a white elephant stresses costly uselessness and burden, while a mathom is affectionate and low-value, never quite a burden.
  • Hand-me-downs: Similar because both are used things passed from owner to owner; differs because hand-me-downs are still worn or used (clothes, bicycles), while mathoms have forgotten uses and serve no purpose at all.
  • Keepsakes: Similar because both are objects kept for sentiment rather than function; differs because a keepsake is treasured for memory of a person or event, while a mathom is kept from mere unwillingness to waste.
  • Trinkets: Similar because both are small, cheap, slightly useless things; differs because trinkets are typically new baubles and jewellery, while mathoms are old, second-hand, and storied with previous owners.
  • Clutter: Similar because both describe heaps of unneeded household things; differs because clutter is a disapproving mass noun for mess to clear, while mathoms dignifies each item and excuses its keeper with Hobbit thrift.
  • Regifts: Similar because mathoms famously circulate as birthday presents; differs because regift names the single act of giving again, while mathom names the object itself and its whole career of circulation.
  • Heirlooms: Similar because both are old family objects passed down and never thrown away; differs because an heirloom is prized and valuable by tradition, while a mathom is comic, lowly, and valued only because wasting feels wrong.
