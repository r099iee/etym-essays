Note on hybrid morphological compounding: The adjective stately is a classic Anglo-Romance hybrid formation. The base noun state derives through Old French from Latin status, while the adjectival suffix -ly descends directly from Old English -līċ (Proto-Germanic *līkaz, meaning "having the form or body of"). In Geoffrey Chaucer's General Prologue to the Canterbury Tales (c. 1387), both the French-derived noun estat and the hybrid adjective statelich appear to describe aristocratic dignity and courtly manners.
