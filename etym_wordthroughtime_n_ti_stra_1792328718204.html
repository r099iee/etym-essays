- c. 4th–3rd c. BCE — Formulated in late Vedic and Mauryan state circles (Kauṭilya's Arthaśāstra) as the rigorous administrative science of sovereign survival and expansion.
- c. 1st c. BCE–4th c. CE — Adapted into entertaining pedagogical animal fables (Pañcatantra) to impart strategic cunning and worldly wisdom to young royal scions.
- c. 5th–8th c. CE — Formalized as an independent literary and philosophical genre in classical Sanskrit literature (Kāmandaka's Nītisāra, Bhartṛhari's Nītiśataka) balancing worldly pragmatism with social order.
- c. 9th–12th c. CE — Transmitted globally through translations into Middle Persian, Arabic (Kalīla wa-Dimna), and European vernaculars, profoundly influencing world didactic literature.
- Modern Era — Studied by modern historians, political scientists, and diplomats as an indigenous non-Western classical framework for grand strategy, intelligence doctrine, and diplomatic realism.
