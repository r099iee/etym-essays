• Stolen: Similar because both describe something taken unlawfully; differs because stolen is the neutral, legal, all-purpose participle, while pinched is informal slang for a quick, deft, hand-to-hand theft.
  • Purloined: Similar because both describe taking something dishonestly; differs because purloined is grandly formal and literary (from Old French porloigner "to put away"), while pinched is earthy street slang.
  • Pilfered: Similar because both describe petty, furtive theft; differs because pilfered stresses small repeated thefts of trifles, while pinched stresses the quick single snatch.
  • Lifted: Similar because both are slang for "stolen" by hand; differs because lifted is broader and can suggest shoplifting or casual removal, while pinched pictures the thumb-and-finger grab.
  • Nicked: Similar because both are British slang for stealing (and for arresting); differs because nicked is strongly British and means "arrested" as a near-primary sense, while pinched keeps the squeeze-image more alive.
  • Swiped: Similar because both are informal verbs for theft; differs because swiped suggests a sweeping grab, while pinched suggests a precise, delicate snatch.
  • Filched: Similar because both describe stealthy stealing; differs because filched is slightly archaic and literary, while pinched is demotic slang.
  • Robbed: Similar because both describe taking property unlawfully; differs because robbed implies a larger crime and often a victim present or threatened, while pinched is a light, quick, unviolent theft.
  • Squeezed: Similar because both are the physical sense of compression between two things; differs because squeezed is the plain neutral verb for pressing, while pinched adds the nipping, thumb-and-finger image and all its figurative extensions.
  • Nipped: Similar because both describe a sharp small squeeze or bite; differs because nipped often implies a bite (of cold, frost, an animal) or a small cut, while pinched is specifically the fingers' grip.
  • Drawn: Similar because both describe a face made thin and tight; differs because drawn is the standard adjective for a strained, tired face, while pinched adds the idea of being squeezed hollow by want or cold.
  • Haggard: Similar because both describe a gaunt, strained appearance; differs because haggard stresses exhaustion and wildness, while pinched stresses thinness as if compressed.
  • In straitened circumstances: Similar because both can describe being short of money; differs because the phrase is formal and euphemistic, while pinched for money is homely and direct.
