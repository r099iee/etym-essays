• Reckless: Similar because both describe ignoring danger; differs because reckless means foolish disregard of risk to oneself, while wanton implies deliberate, malicious indifference to harm inflicted on others.
  • Malicious: Similar because both describe causing harm; differs because malicious implies active personal hatred or spite, while wanton emphasizes wild, unprovoked, senseless destruction with no motive at all.
  • Deliberate: Similar because both name intentional action; differs because deliberate is neutral (a deliberate choice can be good), while wanton always carries condemnation and implies pointlessness.
  • Gratuitous: Similar because both describe unprovoked excess (gratuitous violence); differs because gratuitous simply means unnecessary or unwarranted, while wanton adds recklessness and moral malice.
  • Unprovoked: Similar because both describe action without cause; differs because unprovoked is a neutral factual description (an unprovoked attack), while wanton judges the action as cruel and senseless.
  • Licentious: Similar because both describe sexual restraint abandoned; differs because licentious specifically highlights disregard for moral and sexual law, while wanton covers wildness in all forms, not only the sexual.
  • Lewd: Similar because both can mean sexually immodest; differs because lewd is coarsely and explicitly obscene, while wanton is more about unbridled or playful desire than crude display.
  • Lascivious: Similar because both describe lustful behaviour; differs because lascivious is clinical and strongly sexual, while wanton's sexual sense is one strand of a broader meaning.
  • Promiscuous: Similar because both describe unchaste behaviour; differs because promiscuous is the modern, neutral word for casual sexual partners, while wanton is moralistic and literary.
  • Dissolute: Similar because both describe abandonment of restraint; differs because dissolute implies corruption through self-indulgence and vice, while wanton stresses lack of discipline and unruliness.
  • Unrestrained: Similar because both name the absence of limits; differs because unrestrained is neutral and physical (unrestrained growth), while wanton adds blame or wildness.
  • Wayward: Similar because both describe defiance of control; differs because wayward is stubborn, willful deviation (a wayward child), while wanton is wilder and can be playful, lustful, or cruel.
  • Wild: Similar because both describe ungoverned behaviour; differs because wild is the broad, neutral word for untamed energy, while wanton carries specific moral or legal condemnation.
  • Inordinate: Similar because both describe excess beyond proper limits; differs because inordinate is formal and neutral about degree (inordinate pride), while wanton describes the quality of the excess itself.
