• Damp: Similar because both mean slightly wet; differs because "damp" is neutral and can even be pleasant (a damp cloth), while "clammy" is cold, adhesive, and unpleasant.
  • Moist: Similar because both describe slight wetness; differs because "moist" is often desirable (a moist cake) and does not imply cold or adhesion, which are essential to "clammy".
  • Dank: Similar because both are wet, cold, and unpleasant; differs because "dank" suggests mustiness and is used chiefly of places (a dank cellar), while "clammy" is above all of skin and touch.
  • Soggy: Similar because both describe absorbed wetness; differs because "soggy" means soaked through and heavy (of food or ground), with no implication of cold or of clinging adhesion.
  • Sticky: Similar because both describe adhesive contact; differs because "sticky" implies adhesion from sugar, gum, or resin and need not be cold or damp, whereas "clammy" is specifically cold, moist, and clinging.
  • Sweaty: Similar because both describe moist, often cold-feeling skin; differs because "sweaty" explicitly names perspiration, while "clammy" names the sensation (cold, clinging dampness) without saying the cause.
  • Slimy: Similar because both describe wet, unpleasant, clinging textures; differs because "slimy" involves an oozing, slippery film and is more actively disgusting, while "clammy" is cold and adhesive without the slipperiness.
  • Viscous / Glutinous: Similar because both describe thick, adhesive substances; differs because these are technical terms for fluid properties, while "clammy" is a sensory word for unpleasant cold dampness.
  • Gelid: Similar because both imply chilling coldness; differs because "gelid" means intensely cold (like ice) without any dampness, while "clammy" fuses coldness with clinging moisture.
  • Humid / Muggy: Similar because both describe damp air; differs because humid or muggy air is usually warm and atmospheric, whereas "clammy" can describe surfaces and skin as well, with coldness built in.
  • Adhesive: Similar because both involve clinging to the skin; differs because "adhesive" is a neutral, technical word for stickiness, while "clammy" carries revulsion and coldness.
