• Pisa: Similar because Pisa is Lucca's immediate historic Tuscan neighbour and ancient rival on the Serchio/Arno plains; differs because Pisa was a naval maritime empire that fell under Florentine dominion in 1406, whereas Lucca remained an inland mercantile republic that retained its independence until the Napoleonic era.
  • Florence (Firenze): Similar because both were powerful, culturally rich Tuscan city-states; differs because Florence was the dominant imperial powerhouse of Tuscany that repeatedly attempted to conquer Lucca, while Lucca successfully resisted Florentine rule to preserve its unique republican oligarchy.
  • Siena: Similar because both were proud, independent Tuscan republics with deep banking traditions and fierce rivalries with Florence; differs because Siena lost its independence to Cosimo I de' Medici in 1555 and was absorbed into the Grand Duchy of Tuscany, while Lucca survived as an independent republic until 1799.
  • San Marino: Similar because both are historic Italian micro-republics that guarded their ancient liberty (Libertas) through mountainous diplomacy and neutrality; differs because San Marino successfully survived the Napoleonic era and 19th-century unification to remain a sovereign nation today, whereas Lucca was eventually integrated into unified Italy.
  • Parma: Similar because both became northern Italian duchies governed by Bourbon rulers after the Congress of Vienna (1815); differs because Parma was historically an Emilian ducal capital (Farnese/Bourbon) with an aristocratic court, whereas Lucca's heritage was rooted in centuries of merchant republicanism.
  • Bologna: Similar because both are historic northern/central Italian cities famous for medieval architecture and culinary excellence; differs because Bologna was incorporated into the Papal States in the early 16th century, whereas Lucca remained a free secular republic.
  • Genoa: Similar because both were northern Italian republics overthrown by Napoleon in 1797-1805 and lamented together in Tolstoy's War and Peace; differs because Genoa was a coastal maritime and banking empire, whereas Lucca was an inland Tuscan textile, silk, and agrarian commonwealth.
