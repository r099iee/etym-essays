• Buckled: Similar because both fasten with a strap-and-buckle closure; differs because buckled names the clasp clicking shut, while strapped names the whole binding with the band drawn tight.
  • Lashed: Similar because both bind loads firmly against movement; differs because lashed suggests rope or cord whipped round in haste or storm, while strapped suggests proper fitted bands with buckles.
  • Bound: Similar because both tie things fast together; differs because bound is the general word for any tying, while strapped specifies the flat band and the harness image.
  • Fastened: Similar because both make things secure against coming loose; differs because fastened is neutral and covers buttons, zips, and locks, while strapped always keeps its leather-and-webbing flavour.
  • Secured: Similar because both leave a load safe for travel; differs because secured is official and abstract, the quartermaster's word, while strapped is hands-on, the walker's word.
  • Tethered: Similar because both restrain against wandering; differs because tethered ties a living creature to a fixed point by a long line, while strapped binds the load close to its bearer or frame.
  • Cinched: Similar because both tighten a band around packs and saddles; differs because cinched stresses the squeezing girth, often of a horse, while strapped stresses the flat band and buckle of human kit.
  • Harnessed: Similar because both fit working bands onto bodies; differs because harnessed prepares horse or human for pulling work, while strapped prepares packs and persons for carrying or safety.
  • Trussed: Similar because both bind limbs or loads into a tight parcel; differs because trussed belongs to the kitchen and the surgery, fowls and patients, while strapped belongs to the road and the workshop.
