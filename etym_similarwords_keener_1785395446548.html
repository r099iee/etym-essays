• Sharper: Similar because both name heightened acuity of edge, sense, or mind; differs because sharper is most at home with cutting edges and precise intellect and only metaphorical for smell, whereas keener was applied to the senses from the earliest English records and sits more naturally on animal instinct.
  • More acute: Similar in naming finely developed perception; differs because more acute is clinical and anatomical — an acute observer, an acute angle — whereas keener is instinctive and predatory, the sniff of a hunter rather than the verdict of a physician.
  • More perceptive: Similar in describing superior awareness; differs because more perceptive is intellectual, neutral and complimentary, with no appetite in it, while keener carries eager, hungry attentiveness.
  • More sensitive: Similar in describing heightened receptivity; differs because more sensitive implies fragility or refined feeling — the opposite of the aggressive alertness that keener evokes.
  • More eager: Similar in the appetite strand of keenness; differs because more eager covers only desire and willingness, losing the sensory-sharpness meaning that keener retains.
  • More zealous: Similar in enthusiastic intensity; differs because more zealous suggests fervent devotion, often religious or partisan, whereas keener is more sensory and appetitive.
  • More intense: Similar in raised degree of a quality; differs because more intense is a generic intensifier without the specific 'cutting' or 'eager' imagery that keener carries.
  • More piercing: Similar for keener cold, sound, or pain; differs because more piercing is restricted to penetration of the senses and cannot mean eager or enthusiastic.
  • More penetrating: Similar for mental and sensory acuity; differs because more penetrating emphasizes reaching into the depths of a thing, while keener emphasizes the sharpness of the edge itself.
  • More incisive: Similar in sharpness applied to the mind; differs because more incisive is a formal, journalistic word for cutting analysis and is never used of physical senses or of appetite.
