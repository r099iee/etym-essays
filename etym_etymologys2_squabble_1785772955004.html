No Proto-Indo-European root can be reconstructed for squabble. It is a sound-symbolic, expressive formation of the Germanic lexicon, first attested in Early Modern English, whose nearest relatives are North Germanic dialect words. An honest chain therefore begins at the expressive base, not at a PIE root; no PIE etymon should be assumed.

1. (Expressive base, not a regular IE paradigm) North/West Germanic imitative cluster *skwab- / *skvabb- 'splash, flop, wet clatter, chatter' — reconstructed from attested dialect forms by analogy; the initial skw-/skv- plus short vowel plus doubled labial encodes harsh, wet, repeated noise.
2. Scandinavian dialectal attested forms: Swedish dialect skvabba 'to splash, to paddle in water'; Swedish skvabb 'soft wet mass, slush, fat'; Norwegian dialectal skvabb / skvabbel 'chatter, gossip, squabbling'. These are the nearest external relatives on record.
3. English squabble (verb), first recorded c. 1600, 'to quarrel noisily over trifles' — of uncertain but sound-symbolic / North Germanic character; possibly a borrowing from the Scandinavian expressive forms, possibly a partly native English formation on the same imitative base (compare squawk, squall, squelch, squeal). The major dictionaries label the origin 'unknown; probably imitative'.
4. English squabble (noun), c. 1602, 'a noisy petty quarrel'.
5. Modern English squabble — unchanged in form, used as both noun and verb, the exact word on this card.

DEBATE NOTE: older suggestions of a connection to French, or to the English word squab 'a young pigeon' (itself a different word of Germanic origin), are not accepted; the mainstream account treats squabble as an imitative / expressive formation with Scandinavian dialect parallels.
