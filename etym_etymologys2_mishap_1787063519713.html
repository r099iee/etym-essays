Chain 1 — the prefix mis-
PIE (reconstruction)
*mei-
“to change, go past”
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 100 CE)
*missa-
“wrong, astray, wrongly”
↓
Old English (7th–11th c.)
mis-
“bad, wrong, wrongly”
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
mis-
“bad, wrong, ill”

Chain 2 — the base hap
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 100 CE)
*hampaz
“fit, convenient; good luck”
↓
Old Norse (9th–11th c.)
happ
“chance, fortune, luck”
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
hap
“chance, fortune, luck”

The compound
↓
English (c. 1509)
mis- + hap → mishap
“bad luck; an unlucky accident”
↓
MISHAP
Note: The exact shape of the PIE root behind Germanic *missa- is debated (*mei-, *meith-, *mit-), and *hampaz for hap is likewise a reconstruction of uncertain ultimate origin. Both are reconstructions, not attested forms.
