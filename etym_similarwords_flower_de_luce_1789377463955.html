• Iris: Similar because it is the modern botanical, horticultural, and common name for the exact same genus of flowering plants (*Iris*); differs because iris is the scientific and contemporary standard name derived from the Greek goddess of the rainbow, whereas flower-de-luce is an archaic, poetic, and historical English name derived from Old French *fleur de lys*.
  • Fleur-de-lis: Similar because it is the exact French linguistic source and heraldic counterpart of flower-de-luce; differs because fleur-de-lis is primarily used today to denote the stylized heraldic emblem, decorative architectural motif, and royal French insignia, whereas flower-de-luce historically designated the living botanical flower in English gardens and literature.
  • Yellow Flag: Similar because it specifically designates the native European yellow wetland iris (*Iris pseudacorus*), which was the original living flower inspiring the flower-de-luce emblem; differs because yellow flag is an unadorned, modern English common name emphasizing the plant's wetland habitat and banner-like petals, lacking the royal and medieval literary associations of flower-de-luce.
  • Lily: Similar because the literal etymology of French *fleur de lys* means "flower of the lily," and historically medieval herbalists frequently classified irises under the broad umbrella of lilies; differs because true lilies (*Lilium*) belong to an entirely different botanical family (Liliaceae) with radial six-tepaled flowers and leafy upright stems, whereas the flower-de-luce belongs to the Iridaceae family with sword-like basal leaves and distinct three-part standards and falls.
  • Orris: Similar because orris is derived from the fragrant dried rhizome of *Iris florentina* or *Iris germanica* (the cultivated white flower-de-luce); differs because orris specifically denotes the powdered cosmetic, flavoring, or perfumery ingredient derived from the root, whereas flower-de-luce denotes the complete living plant and flower.
  • Gladiolus: Similar because both belong to the iris family (Iridaceae) and possess rigid, vertical, sword-shaped leaves (hence the Latin name *gladiolus*, "little sword"); differs because gladiolus bears a tall, one-sided vertical spike of funnel-shaped blossoms, whereas flower-de-luce features complex, tripartite flowers with upright standards and drooping falls.
