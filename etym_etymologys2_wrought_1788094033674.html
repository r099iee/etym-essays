NOTE: The PIE root *werg- "to do, make" is securely attested in its reflexes: Greek ἔργον (érgon) "work, deed" (whence energy, erg, organ), Avestan varəza- "work, activity", and the Germanic family (English work, wright; Gothic waurkjan "to work"). Old English wyrcan was a class 1 weak verb whose past (worhte) and participle ((ge)worht) were formed with a distinctive -ht- — the direct source of modern wrought; the gh in the modern spelling is the fossil of Old English h (pronounced /x/) in this position, now silent.
