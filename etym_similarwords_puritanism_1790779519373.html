• Asceticism: Similar because both involve rigorous self-discipline, austerity, and the deliberate renunciation of worldly indulgences and physical gratification; differs because asceticism is typically an individual, voluntary spiritual or philosophical practice aimed at personal enlightenment or mystical union, whereas puritanism is an expansive social and moral regime that actively seeks to regulate and enforce behavioral orthodoxy across entire communities.
  • Austerity: Similar because both emphasize severe simplicity, restraint, and the elimination of luxury or superfluous pleasures; differs because austerity often describes material, economic, or aesthetic conditions (such as fiscal austerity or minimalist design) without inherently implying theological zeal, guilt, or moral self-righteousness.
  • Priggishness: Similar because both display an irritatingly strict adherence to moralistic standards and rules; differs because priggishness denotes petty, smug, and pointlessly pedantic punctiliousness over minor social codes, whereas puritanism stems from a grand, systemic, and deeply ideological conviction regarding moral purity and salvation.
  • Moralism: Similar because both place ethical judgment and moral conduct at the very center of human life; differs because moralism can be a secular, philosophical concern with ethical behavior that lacks puritanism's specific historical association with anti-sensual repression, solemnity, and covenantal guilt.
  • Prudery: Similar because both exhibit extreme discomfort with, or active suppression of, sexual, sensual, and physical expression; differs because prudery is predominantly a squeamish, modest, or defensive reaction to bodily functions and sexual matters, whereas puritanism is an all-encompassing ideological system regulating work, worship, recreation, and civic law.
  • Rigorism: Similar because both insist upon absolute, unyielding adherence to strict moral laws without exception or compromise; differs because rigorism is a technical philosophical and casuistical stance regarding the strict interpretation of rules, lacking the cultural, emotional, and aesthetic dimensions intrinsic to puritanism.
  • Sabbatarianism: Similar because both enforce strict religious observance and the prohibition of secular amusements; differs because Sabbatarianism is narrowly focused on the strict religious observance and sanctification of the Sabbath (Sunday), whereas puritanism governs every facet of daily life throughout the entire week.
  • Zealotry: Similar because both manifest uncompromising fanaticism in devotion to an ideological cause; differs because zealotry emphasizes militant, aggressive, and often violent partisan passion, whereas puritanism frequently operates through institutionalized solemnity, structural self-surveillance, and pervasive social conformity.
