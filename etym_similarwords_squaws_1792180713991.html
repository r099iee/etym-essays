• Clan mothers: Similar because both refer to Indigenous North American women within tribal societies; differs because clan mothers is an accurate, highly respectful constitutional title designating senior matriarchs in nations like the Haudenosaunee who hold sovereign political authority, whereas squaws is a degraded colonial term that became a racial slur.
  • Matriarchs: Similar because both describe female elders within kin groups; differs because matriarchs is an honorable, formal anthropological term for senior female leaders across any culture, devoid of the racial and colonial degradation attached to squaws.
  • Indigenous women: Similar because both identify female members of native nations; differs because Indigenous women is the proper, modern, respectful collective designation, whereas squaws is an archaic colonial term recognized today as offensive hate speech.
  • Native wives: Similar because early colonists often used squaws to signify married native women; differs because native wives is a plain descriptive phrase, whereas squaws carries heavy historical baggage of colonial subjugation and drudgery stereotypes.
  • Ikwe / Kwe: Similar because both originate from Algonquian linguistic roots for "woman"; differs because Ikwe (or Kwe) is the living, highly respected Anishinaabemowin (Ojibwe) word celebrating female sacred identity, whereas squaw represents an anglicized, corrupted loanword corrupted by colonial prejudice.
  • Amerindian women: Similar because both categorize native females; differs because Amerindian is an older anthropological classification, while squaw is a derogatory vernacular slur.
  • Consorts: Similar because both denote female domestic partners; differs because consorts conveys royal or elite diplomatic status, contrasting sharply with the subordinate drudgery connotations imposed upon squaws by frontier settlers.
