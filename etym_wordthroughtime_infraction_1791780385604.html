- 1590s–1650s: Physical & Rhetorical Fracturing - Initially entered the English language in both physical contexts (the literal breaking, shattering, or tearing apart of an object) and oratorical theory (the weakening or interruption of a rhythmic cadence in speech), bearing an erudite, formal Latinate weight.
- Late 17th–18th Century: Treaty & Covenant Violations - Narrowed heavily toward formal, diplomatic violations of international treaties, public charters, and solemn covenants, treated by jurists and statesmen with elevated solemnity.
- 19th Century: Institutional & Civil Code Breaches - Expanded into domestic governance, corporate bylaws, moral conduct, and sports regulations, designating any failure to observe formal organizational rules.
- 20th–21st Century: Stratification into Minor / Petty Legal Offenses - In modern statutory law (especially the Model Penal Code and state penal systems), formally codified as the lowest tier of legal transgression—a non-criminal civil offense (such as a speeding ticket or parking violation) devoid of criminal stigma or jail time, while retaining dignified diplomatic force in international treaties.
