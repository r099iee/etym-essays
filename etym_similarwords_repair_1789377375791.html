• Condition: Similar because both refer to the physical state or quality of an object; differs because condition is a neutral descriptor that can range from pristine to destroyed, whereas repair (as a noun of state) specifically evaluates maintenance and soundness relative to being broken or whole.
  • Maintenance: Similar because both involve keeping structures or machines functioning properly; differs because maintenance refers to routine, preventative servicing designed to preserve working order and avoid breakdown, whereas repair is corrective action taken after damage or malfunction has already occurred.
  • Mending: Similar because both denote fixing what is broken, torn, or damaged; differs because mending typically applies to small-scale, domestic, or textile items (such as mending socks or mending a fence), whereas repair spans heavy industrial machinery, civil engineering, and legal contracts.
  • Restoration: Similar because both signify returning something to a previous good state; differs because restoration implies a meticulous, historical return of an antique, work of art, or historic building to its original aesthetic splendor, whereas repair focuses strictly on restoring functional utility and structural soundness.
  • Renovation: Similar because both involve improving the state of a building or object; differs because renovation focuses on updating, modernizing, and remodeling to improve aesthetic appeal or contemporary usefulness, whereas repair addresses specific defects or physical decay.
  • Overhaul: Similar because both describe fixing mechanical or institutional systems; differs because an overhaul implies taking an entire complex mechanism completely apart, inspecting every component, and rebuilding it thoroughly, whereas a repair may target only a single broken part.
