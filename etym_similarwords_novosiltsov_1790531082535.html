• Czartoryski: Similar because Prince Adam Jerzy Czartoryski (1770–1861) was Novosiltsov's closest colleague on Emperor Alexander I's "Secret Committee" and served as Russian Foreign Minister during the 1804–1805 anti-Napoleonic coalition negotiations; differs because Czartoryski was a Polish prince who ultimately broke with the Russian Empire to champion Polish independence during the November Uprising, whereas Novosiltsov remained an imperial Russian statesman.
  • Kochubey: Similar because Count Viktor Kochubey (1768–1834) was another core member of Alexander I's four-man reform circle ("Secret Committee") and later Minister of the Interior; differs because Kochubey focused primarily on domestic administrative reform and diplomatic neutrality, whereas Novosiltsov took the lead on foreign coalition missions and constitutional drafting.
  • Stroganov: Similar because Count Pavel Stroganov (1772–1817) was the ideological instigator of the "Secret Committee" alongside Novosiltsov; differs because Stroganov had strong Jacobin French education and later transitioned into a frontline military general during the Napoleonic Wars.
  • Speransky: Similar because Mikhail Speransky (1772–1839) was another towering constitutional reformer of Alexander I's reign; differs because Speransky was a brilliant jurist of non-noble clerical origin who drafted the 1809 constitutional reform project and civil code, whereas Novosiltsov was an aristocratic diplomat and statesman whose constitutional charter (*Gosudarstvennaya Ustavnaya Gramota*) proposed a federal imperial structure in 1820.
  • Pitt the Younger: Similar because William Pitt the Younger (1759–1806) was the British Prime Minister who directly negotiated the historic 1805 Anglo-Russian treaty with Novosiltsov in London; differs because Pitt represented British maritime, colonial, and financial power, while Novosiltsov represented Russian imperial continental diplomacy.
  • Talleyrand: Similar because Charles-Maurice de Talleyrand-Périgord (1754–1838) was the dominant French diplomatic contemporary during the Napoleonic era; differs because Talleyrand steered French imperial and post-Napoleonic foreign policy through pragmatic realpolitik, whereas Novosiltsov's 1804 mission championed an idealistic collective security framework.
  • Metternich: Similar because Prince Klemens von Metternich (1773–1859) designed the post-Napoleonic European balance of power (the Concert of Europe); differs because Metternich's conservative system sought to preserve dynastic absolutism, whereas Novosiltsov's early 1804 dispatch envisioned an institutional confederation based on the law of nations.
