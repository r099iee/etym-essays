• Experiencing: Similar because it is the most common direct English translation used by Stanislavski scholars (e.g., Jean Benedetti); differs because the English word experiencing is broad, everyday, and emotionally neutral (e.g., experiencing mild turbulence), lacking the profound existential depth, transformative intensity, and "living-through" resonance of perezhivanie.
  • Living-through: Similar because it captures the exact literal morphology (*pere-* + *zhit'*); differs because living-through is an awkward hyphenated English compound used mostly as a theoretical gloss rather than an established cultural term.
  • Empathy: Similar because both involve deep emotional connection; differs because empathy focuses on understanding and feeling another person's feelings from the outside, whereas perezhivanie is the firsthand, immersive internal reality of living that experience yourself on the stage or in life.
  • Catharsis: Similar because both denote powerful emotional experiences in dramatic art; differs because catharsis is an Aristotelian concept describing the audience's purgation of pity and fear at the conclusion of a tragedy, whereas perezhivanie is the actor’s active, ongoing psychological process of living the role during the performance.
  • Internalization: Similar because both involve taking external realities into the psyche; differs because internalization is a cognitive, developmental, or sociological mechanism of absorbing rules and norms, while perezhivanie is an active, vibrant, emotionally conscious state of being.
  • Verisimilitude: Similar because both relate to realism; differs because verisimilitude is the outward appearance of being true or real (an aesthetic quality of a text or set), while perezhivanie is the genuine internal psychological reality of the human subject.
