• Fleck: Similar because both denote a tiny, light spot or small floating/settling particle; differs because 'fleck' often implies a contrasting patch of color or a light flake (e.g. flecks of gold, flecks of foam), whereas 'speck' emphasizes minute size and punctiform dimension.
  • Mote: Similar because both refer to an extremely tiny particle; differs because 'mote' is elevated, archaic, or biblical in register and almost exclusively describes airborne particles caught in beams of sunlight (e.g. a mote of dust in one's eye), whereas 'speck' is common and applies to any surface or background.
  • Dot: Similar because both denote a small, round mark; differs because 'dot' is a deliberate geometric or typographical point (such as over an 'i' or on a map), whereas 'speck' suggests a natural, accidental, or microscopic particle or blemish.
  • Particle: Similar because both refer to an extremely small portion or fragment of matter; differs because 'particle' is a formal, scientific term denoting distinct physical entities (subatomic particles, dust particles), whereas 'speck' is sensory and visual.
  • Spot: Similar because both designate a localized mark or blemish on a surface; differs because 'spot' can be significantly larger and carries broader functional meanings (a parking spot, a bright spot), whereas 'speck' is strictly microscopic or near-microscopic.
  • Grain: Similar because both denote tiny discrete units of particulate matter; differs because 'grain' implies a hard, crystalline, or seed-like texture (a grain of sand, a grain of wheat), whereas 'speck' is defined primarily by its tiny visual appearance.
  • Trace: Similar because both refer to an infinitesimal, barely detectable quantity; differs because 'trace' focuses on chemical, forensic, or historical evidence of past presence, whereas 'speck' is a tangible physical or visual particle.
  • Iota: Similar because both signify an infinitesimal amount; differs because 'iota' is abstract and conceptual (e.g. not an iota of truth), derived from the smallest letter of the Greek alphabet, whereas 'speck' is concrete and physical.
