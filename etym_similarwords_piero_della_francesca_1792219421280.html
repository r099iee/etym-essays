• Paolo Uccello: Similar because both were 15th-century Italian Early Renaissance painters famously obsessed with the mathematics of linear perspective; differs because Uccello's perspective was gothic, decorative, and theatrical (as in The Battle of San Romano), whereas Piero's perspective was rigorously Euclidean, monumental, serene, and mathematically systematic.
  • Masaccio: Similar because both were foundational Florentine/Central Italian painters who revolutionized painting through spatial realism, sculptural human mass, and natural lighting; differs because Masaccio pioneered these qualities early in the 1420s (the Brancacci Chapel) with raw emotional drama, whereas Piero developed them decades later into serene, intellectual mathematical tranquility.
  • Andrea Mantegna: Similar because both were northern/central Italian Quattrocento masters renowned for daring foreshortening, linear precision, and classical antiquity; differs because Mantegna's style was dramatic, sharp, stony, and intensely expressive (e.g. The Lamentation over the Dead Christ), while Piero's figures are placid, calm, and bathed in soft, unified light.
  • Filippo Brunelleschi: Similar because both were pioneers who formulated the mathematical principles of linear perspective in Renaissance art; differs because Brunelleschi was an architect and engineer who demonstrated optical perspective via physical experiments in Florence, while Piero formalized its geometric mathematical proofs and applied them to monumental fresco painting.
  • Leon Battista Alberti: Similar because both were humanist polymaths who wrote foundational theoretical treatises on painting (*De pictura*) and perspective; differs because Alberti was primarily a humanist writer, architect, and aesthetic theorist, whereas Piero was an active master painter and original research mathematician who proved new geometric theorems.
  • Fra Angelico: Similar because both painted devout, luminous Early Renaissance religious works characterized by sacred calm and exquisite color; differs because Fra Angelico's art remained rooted in Late Gothic devotional mysticism, whereas Piero's work was defined by rigorous Euclidean perspective and intellectual humanist inquiry.
  • Leonardo da Vinci: Similar because both were supreme Italian painter-scientists who investigated anatomy, perspective, and optics; differs because Leonardo belonged to the High Renaissance generation that favored organic sfumato, dynamic movement, and psychological tension, whereas Piero was the master of the Quattrocento's crystalline, geometric stillness.
  • Piet Mondrian: Similar because both artists organized pictorial space around a deep, metaphysical reverence for pure geometry and harmonic proportion (as argued by Charles Bouleau); differs because Mondrian was a 20th-century non-objective abstractionist working with primary colors and flat orthogonal grids, while Piero created figurative Renaissance religious narratives.
