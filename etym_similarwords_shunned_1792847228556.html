• Avoided: Similar because both mean kept away from on purpose; differs because avoided is neutral and general, while shunned adds social frost and moral judgement.
  • Ostracised: Similar because both describe exclusion by a group acting together; differs because ostracised stresses formal collective expulsion, while shunned can be the quiet work of individuals one by one.
  • Snubbed: Similar because both involve pointed social rejection; differs because a snub is a single insulting moment, while being shunned is a sustained campaign of distance.
  • Spurned: Similar because both mean rejected with contempt; differs because spurned is hot and active, a lover or offer thrust away, while shunned is cold and passive, a person left untouched.
  • Eschewed: Similar because both mean deliberately kept clear of, often on principle; differs because eschewed is bookish and applies to habits and doctrines, while shunned most often applies to people.
  • Blacklisted: Similar because both mean barred from company or trade; differs because blacklisted implies a written list and practical penalties, while shunned may rest on unspoken consensus alone.
  • Excommunicated: Similar because excommunication is shunning armed with ritual; differs because excommunicated is specifically ecclesiastical expulsion, while shunned belongs to families, villages, and society at large.
  • Ignored: Similar because both leave the person unacknowledged; differs because ignored may be mere inattention, while shunned is inattention sharpened into policy.
  • Boycotted: Similar because both organise refusal of contact or custom; differs because a boycott targets goods, firms, or events for a cause, while shunning targets the person in every sphere at once.
