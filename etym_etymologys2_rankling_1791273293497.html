Old French (c. 12th c.)
rancle / draoncle
"festering sore,"
"ulcer, boil"
↓
Anglo-Norman (c. 13th c.)
rancler
"to fester"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300-1400)
ranklen
"to fester, breed"
"maggots in a wound"
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
rankle (verb)
"to fester; to cause"
"bitter resentment"
↓
Modern English (17th c. - present)
rankling
"persistently"
"irritating the mind"

Note: Old French draoncle / rancle from Latin dracunculus "little serpent," "ulcer, sore", diminutive of draco "serpent"; initial d- lost by dissimilation. Derivation debated in detail but ulcer-to-resentment path is secure.
