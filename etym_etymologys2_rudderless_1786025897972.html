PIE *h₁erh₁-

"to row" — reconstructed root; gives Greek
eretmos "oar" and Latin remus "oar"

↓

Proto-Germanic *rōþrą / *rōþru-

"oar, paddle, steering oar"
(reconstructed; cf. Old Norse rōðr)

↓

Old English rōþor

"oar, paddle for steering, rudder"
(attested before 900 CE)

↓

Middle English rother / rudder

"steering oar; then the hinged stern-post
rudder introduced in the 14th century"

↓

Old English -lēas / Middle English -les

privative suffix "without, devoid of"
(from Old English lēas "loose, devoid of")

↓

English rudderless (compound)

"without a rudder" (literal, 19th c.); figurative
"without direction or leadership" (20th c.)

↓

RUDDERLESS

"lacking a rudder; lacking direction, guidance,
leadership, or clear purpose"

Note: the compound itself is modern (attested from the 19th century); its two elements, however, are both native Germanic words of great antiquity, each with an independent recorded history.
