• Count: Similar because both can mean to number or add up; differs because "count" is purely numerical and is the everyday word, while "reckon" (in this sense) is now formal, archaic, or dialectal.
  • Calculate / Compute: Similar because performing arithmetic; differs because these are precise, technical terms of mathematics, while "reckon" blends calculation with opinion and judgment.
  • Consider: Similar because to weigh in the mind before forming a view; differs because "consider" is a deliberate, neutral act of thought, whereas "reckon" implies reaching a conclusion, often by estimation.
  • Estimate: Similar because forming an approximate judgment; differs because "estimate" is consciously rough and methodical, while "reckon" carries an informal, opinionated flavour.
  • Regard: Similar because to hold an opinion of someone; differs because "regard" is more passive and observational ("regard as"), while "reckon" (in "reckon him a friend") implies a weighing and a settled judgement.
  • Deem: Similar because to form and state a judgement; differs because "deem" is formal, legal, and often solemn, whereas "reckon" is colloquial and can even be humorous.
  • Figure: Similar because American colloquial "figure" = to think/suppose; differs because "figure" is specifically American informal idiom, while "reckon" is used across regions and carries British dialect colour (notably the West Country and Southern American "I reckon").
  • Suppose / Think: Similar because expressing an opinion; differs because these are fully neutral words for belief, while "reckon" still faintly suggests a head that is doing arithmetic or accounting.
  • Account: Similar because counting and judging ("take account of", "account for"); differs because "account" has moved fully into finance and bookkeeping, while "reckon" has kept its mental, personal register.
