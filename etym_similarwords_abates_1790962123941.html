• subsides: Similar because both denote a decline in fury, activity, or intensity; differs because subsides often implies settling downward to a baseline level of stillness or sinking into a quiet state, whereas abates emphasizes the reduction of striking force, sharpness, or volume.
  • wanes: Similar because both describe a progressive decrease; differs because wanes specifically connotes a gradual, cyclical fading of light, influence, or vitality (like the phases of the moon), whereas abates implies the slackening of an aggressive, intense, or violent force.
  • ebbs: Similar because both describe a receding motion or diminishment; differs because ebbs evokes the rhythmic retreat of tidal waters, highlighting a backward flow or gradual draining away of strength.
  • dwindles: Similar because both express reduction; differs because dwindles emphasizes shrinking in size, quantity, physical mass, or remaining supply until almost nothing remains.
  • diminishes: Similar because both refer to becoming smaller or less intense; differs because diminishes is a broad, neutral, analytical term applicable to abstract amounts, importance, or prestige without necessarily implying prior violence or tempestuousness.
  • lessens: Similar because both denote decreasing magnitude; differs because lessens is a simpler, highly general Germanic synonym lacking the specialized legal, meteorological, and medical registers inherent in abates.
  • recedes: Similar because both convey moving away from peak intensity; differs because recedes focuses spatially on physical withdrawal, retreat into the distance, or backward movement.
  • relents: Similar because both convey a softening or decrease in harshness; differs because relents carries strong personal agency or personification, implying an intentional decision to cease being severe, stubborn, or unforgiving.
  • slackens: Similar because both denote a loss of tension or speed; differs because slackens draws upon the mechanical metaphor of a tightening rope loosening or pace slowing down.
  • tapers: Similar because both describe a reduction; differs because tapers (or tapers off) emphasizes a geometric, gradual narrowing to a point or thin finish.
  • mitigates: Similar because both involve making something less severe; differs because mitigates is strictly transitive and implies deliberate human intervention to soften harsh consequences, whereas abates can be intransitive and natural.
  • quells: Similar because both involve suppressing a disturbance; differs because quells is an active, decisive act of forcefully extinguishing or crushing opposition, riot, or fear.
