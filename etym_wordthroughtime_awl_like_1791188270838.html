- Old tool noun (pre-1000): æl = cobbler's piercing spike - humble, essential, craft-positive; perception is workmanlike necessity
- Medieval diversification: brad-awls, saddler's awls, bookbinder's awls - specialised trade pride; awl = skilled handwork
- Scientific adjective formation (18th-19th c.): botanists coin awl-like / awl-shaped / subulate for leaves and spines - neutral-descriptive; perception shifts from workshop to herbarium
- Barbier/Braille era: awl-like stylus for punching dots - philanthropic-inventive; connotation is ingenious adaptation of humble tool for literacy
- Modern general simile: awl-like pain, awl-like crystals - vivid but slightly archaic; contemporary perception is rustic-precise, evoking handcraft in a power-tool age
