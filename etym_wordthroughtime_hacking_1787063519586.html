- 9th–11th c. (Old English *haccian*) - Rough cutting & mincing: Denoted chopping flesh, herbs, or wood into small, uneven fragments with heavy blades.
- 12th–15th c. (Middle English *hakken*) - Martial cleaving & manual labor: Expanded across chivalric romance to describe brutal melee combat and woodsmen clearing thickets.
- 16th–18th c. (Early Modern English) - Dry respiratory coughing & hired horses: Transferred acoustically to describe harsh, broken bronchial coughs, while hack developed into a term for ordinary hired horses.
- 19th c. - Victorian sports violence: Used for legalized shin-kicking in early rugby football before being outlawed in association football.
- Mid-20th c. (c. 1950s–1970s) - MIT computing culture: Adopted by early computer scientists for clever, unorthodox software tinkering and rapid prototyping.
- Late 20th–21st c. - Cybersecurity & global cyberwarfare: Solidified as the definitive international term for illicit digital intrusion and data exploitation.
