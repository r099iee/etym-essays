- classical Roman legend - in Roman tradition Egeria is the water-nymph who met King Numa Pompilius in his sacred grove and dictated the laws and rites of early Rome; her name circulates in English as a learned classical allusion.
- 17th-18th century classical reference - English writers invoke Egeria as the type of the inspiring counsellor behind a ruler, still understood through Plutarch and Livy as a figure of quasi-divine, formative influence.
- 19th century Victorian appellative - the proper name becomes a common noun in political and literary English for a wise female adviser or inspirer of a statesman, thinker, or artist; biographers cast women behind great men (including, questionably, Harriet Taylor Mill) as an "Egeria."
- Contemporary use - rare and allusive, a high-flown literary compliment for a woman who inspires and counsels a public man, carrying the mystique of the oracle behind the throne.
