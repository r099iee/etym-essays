Note on botanical etymology: The botanical term arillus was introduced into modern scientific nomenclature by 18th-century taxonomists (most notably Carl Linnaeus in 1751 and Joseph Gaertner in 1788). The Neo-Latin word was adapted from Medieval Latin arilla, which denoted a dried grape, raisin, or small grape stone (derived as a diminutive from Latin āridus, 'dry'). Enlightenment botanists repurposed the word to classify any extra integument or fleshy wrapper that dries, splits, or detaches from around a mature seed.
