Booty in the source sense means valuable goods seized or won through hunting, raiding or war: spoils, plunder, prey, the prize of the chase.

Early man, the passage says, mingled new plant foods with familiar fruits and flesh but always yearned for the booty of the chase. Here booty is not wages or harvest but the hunter's triumphant takings: meat, hide, horn, the carcass triumphantly brought home. The word carries excitement, risk and prestige absent from farming: booty must be chased, fought for, won. That yearning explains lingering preference for meat and sport even after agriculture made gathering reliable.

In wider modern use, booty retains this core of seized or extra prize, from military plunder to pirate treasure to political spoils. It can be solemn in war-crimes law or playful in treasure-hunt and gaming talk. A wholly separate slang homograph means buttocks, which must not be confused with the spoils sense though puns deliberately merge them.

  • Hunter's spoils / prey (in-context primary): Animals killed and their usable parts; e.g., the booty of the chase as deer, boar, furs. Bridge is effort and triumph: booty rewards daring, unlike crops rewarding patience.
  • Military plunder: Goods seized from enemy, city or battlefield; e.g., Napoleon's booty from Italy, division of booty among troops. Distinguished in prize law from naval prize: booty typically land seizure, prize sea capture adjudicated by courts.
  • Pirate / robbery treasure: Popular-culture chests of gold, jewels, rum; e.g., pirate booty buried on islands. Romanticized, adventurous connotation from 18th-century buccaneering literature.
  • Political / electoral spoils: Offices, contracts or favors distributed to victors; e.g., spoils system, election booty. Cynical, corrupt connotation: politics as warfare by other means.
  • Prize / winnings generally: Informal: any extra gain or treasure; e.g., the booty from a sale, gaming loot called booty. Playful extension where competition replaces combat.
  • Slang homograph — buttocks: Distinct etymology from butt, as in shake your booty, booty call. The semantic bridge to spoils is objectification: body as prize. Learners must keep senses apart, recognizing puns when they meet.

Tone depends on domain: in anthropology and military history booty is technical-neutral; in pirate tales affectionately romantic; in politics condemnatory; in slang cheeky. What unites all spoils senses is unearned-by-labor acquisition through force, luck or victory — goods that come by winning rather than making. That is why the passage opposes mingled gathered foods to yearned-for booty: security versus glory, sustenance versus triumph.

In the passage the contrast is deliberate: patient gathering and mingling versus sudden glorious booty. Agriculture promises security through storage, yet the chase promises intensity, status and savour. Anthropologists still debate that tension between farmer prudence and hunter prestige, and the word booty condenses it: not mere calories but honoured meat, trophies that prove courage and luck.

Modern learners meet the spoils sense in history, fantasy and gaming, where dungeons yield booty and pirates guard booty, and must keep it separate from the buttocks slang except where wordplay is intended. Register awareness matters: war-booty in a museum label is respectful, office booty in politics is accusatory, and pirate booty at Halloween is playful.
