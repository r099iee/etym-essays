- [Late 16th century: technical and legal] - 'Elapse' enters English meaning for time to pass or glide away; connotation is formal, quasi-legal, and used of periods, terms, and intervals in official documents.
- [18th-19th century: literary narrative] - The word becomes a favourite of narrative prose for unhurried, measured time; connotation is calm and methodical - time that slips by smoothly without being marked or endured.
- [Modern usage] - 'Elapse' means simply 'pass' of time in formal contexts; connotation is neutral but bookish - used where 'pass' would be too plain, and it never carries the urgency or ambiguity that 'pass' can.
