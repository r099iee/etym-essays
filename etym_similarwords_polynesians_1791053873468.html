• Pacific Islanders: Similar because they inhabit Pacific islands; differs because Pacific Islander is broader, including Melanesians and Micronesians.
  • Oceanians: Similar because they are Indigenous peoples of Oceania; differs because Oceanian covers Australia, New Zealand and all Pacific, not just Polynesia.
  • Hawaiians / Kanaka Maoli: Similar because they are a Polynesian people; differs because it is one specific nation, not the umbrella.
  • Samoans: Similar because they are a core Polynesian group; differs because Samoan is a distinct language-culture, not all Polynesians.
  • Tongans: Similar because they are Polynesian; differs because Tongan kingdom and Tongic language are unique.
  • Maori: Similar because Maori are Polynesian settlers of New Zealand; differs because Maori identity is nationally specific to Aotearoa.
  • Tahitians / Maohi: Similar because they are central Polynesians; differs because Tahitian is Society-Islands specific.
  • Rapa Nui: Similar because Easter Islanders are Polynesian; differs because Rapa Nui culture and moai are island-specific.
  • Melanesians: Similar because they are neighbouring Pacific Indigenous peoples with Lapita ancestry; differs because Melanesia is western Pacific with distinct languages and darker-skinned stereotype.
  • Micronesians: Similar because they are small-island Pacific navigators; differs because Micronesia is northwestern Pacific with different languages and histories.
  • Austronesians: Similar because Polynesians belong to this family; differs because Austronesian is vastly broader, from Madagascar to Taiwan to Easter Island.
  • Seafarers / Voyagers: Similar because voyaging defines Polynesians; differs because these are occupational, not ethnic terms.
  • Pasifika peoples: Similar because in New Zealand Pasifika largely means Polynesian diaspora; differs because Pasifika is bureaucratic-diasporic, Polynesian is ancestral-geographic.
