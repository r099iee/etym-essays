• Regretfully: Similar because both express sorrow about something; differs because regretfully stresses grief about the matter itself, while apologetically stresses excuse-making and fault-softening toward the other person.
  • Sheepishly: Similar because both describe an embarrassed, self-conscious manner; differs because sheepishly adds visible embarrassment and often a comic blush, while apologetically is the polite excuse tone.
  • Contritely: Similar because both express regret; differs because contrition (from Latin contritus "ground down") is heavier moral repentance, while apologetically is lighter social hedging.
  • Guiltily: Similar because both describe the manner of someone who feels they have done wrong; differs because guiltily names the feeling of guilt, while apologetically names the act of excusing or regretting.
  • Penitently: Similar because both express regret; differs because penitently is a religious or moral register of repentance (from Latin paenitere), while apologetically is everyday social politeness.
  • Ruefully: Similar because both describe a regretful manner; differs because ruefully adds a wry, self-mocking awareness, while apologetically is straightforwardly excuse-making.
  • Meekly: Similar because both describe a submissive, mild manner; differs because meekly (from Old Norse mjúkr "soft") is broader submission without the specific note of apologizing for a fault.
  • Diffidently: Similar because both describe a shy, uncertain manner; differs because diffidence (Latin diffidere "to distrust") is shyness without any fault, while apologetically implies a fault being excused.
  • Deferentially: Similar because both describe respectful, self-effacing speech; differs because deferentially stresses respect for rank or age, while apologetically stresses regret or excuse.
  • Humbly: Similar because both describe a lowly, unassuming manner; differs because humbly (from Latin humilis "low") is a virtue of modesty, while apologetically is specifically the manner of one who excuses an intrusion.
  • Remorsefully: Similar because both express regret; differs because remorsefully involves the heavier, gnawing regret of conscience (from Latin remordere "to bite back"), while apologetically is the lighter social apology.
  • Excusingly: Similar because both describe speech that excuses; differs because excusingly is rarer and emphasizes the giving of reasons, while apologetically emphasizes the expression of regret.
