• Venomousness: Similar because both words describe possessing or projecting lethal biological toxins or spiteful malice; differs because venomousness is concrete and animalistic, evoking serpents and physical bites, whereas virulence is broader, incorporating clinical microbiology, systemic infection, and abstract rhetoric.
  • Toxicity: Similar because both measure the harmful, destructive potency of a substance or behavior; differs because toxicity is a modern, general chemical and sociological umbrella term denoting how poisonous something is, while virulence specifically emphasizes aggressive pathogenicity, rapid onset, and sharp personal malice.
  • Malevolence: Similar because both involve deep-seated ill will toward others; differs because malevolence focuses on internal psychological desire and intention to cause harm (Latin male + volentia, wishing evil), whereas virulence emphasizes the overt, stinging, and corrosive manifestation of that hostility.
  • Acrimony: Similar because both describe bitter, biting hostility in speech or debate; differs because acrimony highlights sharp, sour friction and bad temper between disputants, while virulence denotes a far more lethal, poisonous, and deliberately destructive assault.
  • Spitefulness: Similar because both describe petty malice directed against another; differs because spitefulness is often small-scale, irritating, and trivial, whereas virulence is intense, potent, and catastrophic in its destructive capacity.
  • Malignity: Similar because both denote an evil, destructive nature; differs because malignity emphasizes deep, entrenched, almost metaphysical wickedness and moral corruption, while virulence highlights active infectious spread, acute pain, and sharp venom.
  • Bitterness: Similar because both describe an unpleasant, hostile affective tone; differs because bitterness is an internal emotional posture arising from resentment, disappointment, or wounded pride, whereas virulence is an active, externalized, and aggressive attack.
  • Lethality: Similar because both can describe the fatal outcome of a disease or poison; differs because lethality measures only the end result of death, while virulence encompasses the entire pathogenic process, including invasiveness, tissue destruction, and symptom severity.
  • Infectivity: Similar because both are microbiological descriptors of pathogens; differs because infectivity measures the ease with which a pathogen establishes an initial infection in a host, whereas virulence measures the severity of the damage inflicted once that infection is established.
