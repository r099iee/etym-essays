• Luxurious: Similar because both denote exceptional richness, comfort, and costly surroundings; differs because luxurious emphasizes personal physical comfort, sensual ease, and soft self-indulgence, whereas opulent emphasizes ostentatious, visible expenditure, vast scale, and grandiose architectural or decorative display.
  • Lavish: Similar because both convey an abundance of material wealth; differs because lavish highlights the active, unstinting, or extravagant act of giving, expending, or bestowing resources, whereas opulent describes the permanent, ambient state of grandeur and wealth itself.
  • Sumptuous: Similar because both refer to magnificent, costly richness; differs because sumptuous is especially applied to sensory and aesthetic indulgences like feasts, banquets, tapestries, and apparel that delight the palate and eyes.
  • Affluent: Similar because both indicate the possession of great material wealth; differs because affluent is a neutral, sociological term describing economic liquidity, high income, or prosperous demographics without necessarily implying ornate decoration or ostentatious display.
  • Ostentatious: Similar because both involve the conspicuous exhibition of immense riches; differs because ostentatious is explicitly critical and pejorative, highlighting vulgar pretension, boastfulness, and showy vanity designed to impress onlookers.
  • Resplendent: Similar because both evoke dazzling, impressive visual brilliance; differs because resplendent emphasizes glowing light, shining armor, vibrant color, and radiant beauty rather than financial wealth.
  • Wealthy: Similar as the basic superordinate descriptor for having an abundance of possessions or money; differs because wealthy is a plain, general economic term, whereas opulent implies sensory abundance, decorative excess, and lavish material texture.
