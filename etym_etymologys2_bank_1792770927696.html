Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed)
*bheg- / *bhog- / *bheng-
"to break, bend, curve, form a ridge"
[Source of Germanic terms for elevated ledges and broken mounds]
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*bankiz
"shelf of earth, raised mound, bench"
[Ablaut variant of *binkiz; dual development into bench and bank]
↓
Old Norse (c. 9th–11th c.)
bakki
"ridge, bank of river, hillock, brink"
[Scandinavian assimilation of -nk- to -kk-; reinforced by Old English *banc]
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
banke / banck
"slope of earth, river-bank, raised ground"
[Widespread in Danelaw dialects and Northern/Midland English texts]
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500–1600)
bank
"earthen ridge, river shore, steep slope"
[Differentiated topographically from Italian-derived commercial bank]
↓
Modern English (present)
bank
"sloping earthen ridge; elevated border"
[Specific topographical sense in Tolkien's road embankment]
