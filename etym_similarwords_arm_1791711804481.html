• Equip: Similar because both involve supplying an individual or group with necessary tools or resources; differs because 'arm' specifically connotes lethal weapons, defensive capabilities, or aggressive leverage, whereas 'equip' is general and utilitarian (e.g., equipping an expedition with tents and rations).
  • Fortify: Similar because both signify strengthening defenses against anticipated attacks; differs because 'fortify' focuses on reinforcing physical structures, locations, or internal mental resolve, whereas 'arm' specifically refers to providing weapons or active instruments of combat to persons.
  • Furnish: Similar because both involve outfitting someone with provisions or gear; differs because 'furnish' is a broad, neutral domestic or logistical term carrying zero military or aggressive connotation, while 'arm' emphasizes lethal defense or tactical readiness.
  • Mobilize: Similar because both describe preparing people or forces for active confrontation or crisis; differs because 'mobilize' denotes the operational movement and organization of troops or resources, whereas 'arm' is the concrete act of placing weapons in their hands.
  • Gird: Similar because both carry the historical resonance of preparing oneself for battle or hardship; differs because 'gird' (as in 'gird one's loins') is highly literary and archaic, focusing on fastening belts or armor for physical readiness, whereas 'arm' is common, direct, and specifically denotes weaponization.
  • Munition: Similar because both pertain to military materiel and supplies; differs because 'munition' is an institutional, logistical noun/verb emphasizing ammunition and heavy ordnance stores rather than personal defensive or offensive outfitting.
  • Outrig: Similar because both mean providing complete apparatus for service; differs because 'outrig' is specialized nautical and technical jargon referring to spars, rowlocks, or rigging, lacking the martial combat sense of 'arm'.
