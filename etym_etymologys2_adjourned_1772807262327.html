PIE (reconstructed) *dyew- "sky, day" ↓ Latin (Classical) diēs "day" ↓ Latin (Classical) diurnus "daily" ↓ Late Latin (4th c.) diurnum "a day" ↓ Old French (9th c.) jor "day" ↓ Old French (12th c.) ajorner "to assign a day, summon" ↓ Middle English (14th c.) ajournen "to put off to another day" ↓ English (present) adjourned
"Break off (a meeting, legal case, or
game) with the intention of resuming it
later." Note: ajorner = a- "to" + jorn "day"; "to postpone" derives from "to set a day" for resumption.
