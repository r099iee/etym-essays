[Root 1: PINE (Latin pīnus / PIE *peyt- / *pī- "fat, sap-rich, resinous")]
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*peyt- / *pī-
"fat, sap, pitch, resinous fluid"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*pītsnos
"resinous tree, pine"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
pīnus
"pine tree, pine timber, ship of pine"
↓
Old English (c. 9th–10th c.)
pīn / pīntrēow
"pine tree, evergreen coniferous tree"
Note: Direct early ecclesiastical/commercial borrowing from Latin pīnus into Anglo-Saxon speech, displacing native Proto-Germanic *furhō ('fir, pine', Old English furh).
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
pin / pyne
"pine tree, cone of a pine"
