PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3000 BCE)
*h₂eḱ- / *ak-
"sharp, pointed, stone tool"
↓
Proto-Hellenic (c. 1500 BCE)
*ak-on-
"whetstone, sharpener"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 5th c. BCE)
akónē (ἀκόνη)
"whetstone, hone for sharpening metal"
↓
Koine / Byzantine Greek (c. 6th c. CE)
parakonáō (παρακονάω) / parakónē (παρακόνη)
"to rub against a whetstone; touchstone, sharpener"
[Composed of para- 'beside, against' + akónē]
↓
Old Italian (c. 13th c.)
paragone
"touchstone for testing gold; standard of comparison"
↓
Middle French (c. 15th c.)
parangon
"touchstone, flawless gem, model of excellence"
[Nasalized spelling /n/ in Middle French]
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1540s)
paragon
"peerless pattern of excellence, supreme model"
↓
Modern English (present)
paragon
"model of supreme excellence, perfection, or virtue"
