SPLUTTERED = splutter + past-tense suffix -ed. splutter (1704) is a blend of two roots: splash and sputter. Derivation chains for each component in isolation, then the blend.

CHAIN 1 — SPLASH (echoic; no recoverable PIE etymon):
1. Prehistoric echoic base — the noise of a body striking water, of liquid dashed about; no attested form survives.
2. Middle English plashen / plasschen "to strike or dash water, to dabble" — late 14th–15th century (imitative).
3. plash (v.) "to dash water about, to splatter" — c. 1500 (echoic variant of the same family).
4. splash (v.) "to scatter liquid in drops with a wet, explosive sound" — first recorded 1715, an intensive s-extension of plash.
Note: the expressive s- prefix (splash, smear, squelch) is a productive echoic pattern, not a historical root.

CHAIN 2 — SPUTTER (borrowed, imitative):
1. Prehistoric echoic base — the noise of particles spitting or spattering; no attested form survives.
2. Dutch sputteren (Middle Dutch sputteren) "to sputter, to spit out particles" — 15th–16th century, imitative; borrowed into English.
3. English sputter (v.) "to spit out small particles with explosive sound; to speak rapidly and indistinctly" — 1590s.

BLEND AND FINAL FORM:
4. splash + sputter → splutter (v.) "to speak in a rapid, half-choked, spitting manner; to make sputtering noises" — first recorded 1704.
5. splutter + past-tense suffix -ed.
6. spluttered "spoke with spitting, explosive sounds" — Modern English.
Note: no Proto-Indo-European ancestor can be reconstructed for either component; both splash and sputter are echoic, and the OED marks the 1704 blend as the first record of splutter.
