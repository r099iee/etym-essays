CHAIN 1 — FECK (the base of the word)

  1. PIE *dḡeh₁- "to put, place, set, make" (reconstructed; root of Sanskrit dádhāti "puts", Greek títhēmi "I place", and, with a *-k- extension, Latin facere "to make, do").
  2. Latin faciō, facere "to make, do, bring about" (attested from earliest Latin, 3rd c. BCE; from *dḡeh₁-k-, the k-extension of the PIE root).
  3. Latin efficiō, efficere "to bring about, accomplish, produce" (attested 2nd c. BCE; ex- "out" + facere).
  4. Latin effectus "an accomplishing, effect, result" (substantive of the past participle of efficere; attested 1st c. BCE).
  5. Anglo-Norman / Old French effect, esfect "effect, result" → Middle English effect (attested 14th c.).
  6. Scots feck "effect, value, vigour; the bulk or quantity" (attested 16th c.; aphetic loss of the initial unstressed vowel of "effect").
  7. Scots feckless "ineffective, lacking vigour or value" (attested from the 16th c.; feck + -less "without") → English FECKLESS (adopted into standard English in the 19th century).

CHAIN 2 — -LESS (the suffix)

  1. PIE *lew- "to loosen, divide, separate" (reconstructed; root of Greek lýein "to loosen", Sanskrit lunāti "he cuts").
  2. Proto-Germanic *lausaz "loose, free from, devoid" (reconstructed; cf. Old Norse lauss "loose", Gothic laus "empty, devoid").
  3. Old English lēas "loose, devoid of, destitute of; false" (attested; the "false" sense gives lēasung "lying, falsehood").
  4. Old English suffix -lēas "without, devoid of" (attested in compounds such as cāflēas "without vigour") → Middle English -les → English -less (the suffix of this card).

COMPOUND NOTE

  • The target FECKLESS joins CHAIN 1 (feck) and CHAIN 2 (-less) as a single adjective; the compound is first attested in 16th-century Scots and the whole word is a Scottish formation, not a Latin or French compound.
  • "Reckless" is a false friend: it is reck + -less, from Old English reccan "to care", meaning "without care or heed", and is etymologically unrelated to "feckless".
