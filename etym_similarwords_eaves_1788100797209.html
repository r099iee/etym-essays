• Gutter: Similar because both belong to the roof edge and manage rainwater; differs because the gutter is the trough fitted at or below the eaves to catch and drain the water, while the eaves are the roof’s projecting edge itself; gutters hang from eaves.
  • Eavestrough: Similar because it is built around the eaves; differs because eavestrough (Canadian English) names the gutter channel specifically, never the roof edge; eaves are structure, the trough is plumbing.
  • Fascia: Similar because both are parts of the roof-edge assembly; differs because the fascia is the vertical board or metal band fixed at the ends of the rafters along the eave line, while the eaves are the whole projecting edge, not a single skin.
  • Soffit: Similar because both lie under the roof at its edge; differs because the soffit is the underside surface of the eave (often ventilated), while the eaves are the overhanging edge as a whole, top and underside together.
  • Cornice: Similar because both are projecting features at the top of a wall; differs because a cornice is an ornamental horizontal projection of classical architecture that may have no roofing function at all, while eaves are the functional roof overhang, ornament optional.
  • Overhang: Similar because both name what projects beyond the wall; differs because overhang is a general term (land, balconies, and cliffs overhang too), while eaves is specific to the roof edge of a building.
  • Roofline: Similar because both describe the silhouette edge of a roof; differs because roofline is the visual outline (including gables and parapets), while eaves is the physical lower edge that projects over the wall.
  • Verge: Similar because both are roof-edge terms; differs because the verge is the gable edge of a roof (the sloping end above the gable wall), while the eaves are the horizontal edge over the side walls.
  • Drip edge: Similar because both shed water away from the building; differs because a drip edge is a manufactured metal flashing installed at the eaves’ edge, a detail of construction, while eaves is the architectural feature itself.
  • Brim / Rim: Similar because both name an outer lip or edge; differs because brim and rim are generic edge-words for vessels, hats, and wheels with no roofing sense, while eaves means specifically the roof’s overhanging lip.
