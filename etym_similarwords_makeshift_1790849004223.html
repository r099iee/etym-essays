• Stopgap: Similar because both denote a temporary measure used to meet an urgent need; differs because a stopgap specifically emphasizes "plugging a hole" or preventing a breakdown until a permanent fix arrives, whereas makeshift emphasizes the improvised, cobbled-together, and physical nature of the substitute.
  • Improvised: Similar because both involve making do with whatever is available; differs because improvised is an adjective/participle focusing on spontaneous creation without prior planning (as in jazz or speech), whereas makeshift often highlights the rough, crude, and suboptimal quality of the physical object or setup.
  • Provisional: Similar because both refer to temporary arrangements; differs because provisional is formal, official, and planned (e.g., a provisional government, provisional results), whereas makeshift is informal, rugged, and usually constructed under duress.
  • Expedient: Similar because both are practical solutions chosen under difficult conditions; differs because an expedient is a strategic or moral choice serving self-interest or immediate utility (sometimes carrying connotations of moral compromise), whereas makeshift is purely practical and physical.
  • Jury-rigged: Similar because both describe hastily assembled emergency fixes; differs because jury-rigged is a nautical idiom focusing specifically on mechanical or physical contraptions lashed together with spare parts, whereas makeshift easily describes abstract concepts, psychological mechanisms, and social institutions.
  • Palliative: Similar because both provide temporary relief without solving the underlying cause; differs because palliative is medical or therapeutic, aiming to reduce pain and distress rather than improvise a physical or structural substitute.
