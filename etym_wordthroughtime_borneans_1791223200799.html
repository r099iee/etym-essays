- Pre-colonial self-names - Islanders know themselves as Brunei, Dayak, Dusun, Bajau, Tidung — no single Bornean identity; outsiders (Javanese, Chinese, Arab traders) use port-based exonyms
- Early European naming (1500s–1700s) - Portuguese and Dutch fix Borneo for the whole island after Brunei; Borneans enters travel writing as exotic collective for headhunters, bird-nest traders, and jungle chiefs
- Victorian ethnography (1800s–early 1900s) - Missionaries and administrators catalog Borneans as longhouse communists and living specimens of common property, as in the source; paternalistic-curatorial perception
- Decolonization (1940s–1960s) - Island partitioned among Malaysia, Indonesia, and Brunei; Bornean identity competes with national citizenship and Confrontation-era conflict; political connotation
- Contemporary pride and advocacy - Borneans reclaimed as shared rainforest-heritage and land-rights identity (native customary rights, anti-logging, orangutan conservation); respectful, ecological, solidarity-focused perception
