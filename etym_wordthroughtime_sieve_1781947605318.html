- Old English workaday noun - From Old English sife, sieve has always named a meshed strainer for separating fine from coarse or solid from liquid, a monosyllabic kitchen-garden word unchanged for centuries.
- Proverbial life - By the 18th-19th centuries the word gathers idioms: 'a memory like a sieve' for forgetfulness and the spendthrift's 'water through a sieve', cementing its metaphorical career.
- Modern kitchen and science use - Today sieve remains the plain general term for mesh straining, with sift preferred for fine powders, while 'the sieve of Eratosthenes' keeps the word alive in mathematics.
