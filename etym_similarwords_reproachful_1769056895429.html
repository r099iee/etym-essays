• disapproving: Similar because it signals that someone has failed a standard; differs because disapproving is cooler and more distant — a raised eyebrow or pursed mouth, a verdict passed from outside that may concern taste or fashion and involves no personal hurt.
  • reproving: Similar because it expresses blame; differs because reproving is didactic and parental — it corrects, instructs, and expects amendment, the tone of a teacher or elder rather than a hurt peer.
  • rebuking: Similar because it voices censure; differs because rebuking is sharper and more vocal, spoken out loud and often publicly, where a reproachful glance can be silent and private.
  • condemnatory: Similar because it judges someone at fault; differs because condemnatory escalates to formal, absolute judgement — the language of courts and moralists — with no residue of affection, whereas reproachful carries pain and expectation.
  • accusatory: Similar because it charges another with a wrong; differs because accusatory bluntly levels a specific charge and invites defence, while reproachful blames with disappointment and presupposes a relationship worth appealing to.
  • scolding: Similar because it expresses blame of another's conduct; differs because scolding is openly vocal, chattering, and often school-mistressy, whereas reproachful is quieter, more wounded, and delivered through look or tone rather than a tirade.
  • chiding: Similar because it is a mild, scolding censure; differs because chiding is more verbal and often good-naturedly corrective, while reproachful is hurt-filled and moral, speaking of a failed expectation.
  • admonishing: Similar because it warns and corrects; differs because admonishing is an earnest warning aimed at preventing future error, whereas reproachful looks backward at a fault already committed.
  • censorious: Similar because it is quick to find fault; differs because censorious describes a habitual fault-finding disposition toward people in general, whereas reproachful is a targeted, personal, hurt-laden response to a specific let-down.
  • critical: Similar because it finds fault with something; differs because critical is a general, often reasoned evaluation (of work, behaviour, ideas) with no emotional sting, while reproachful is emotionally wounded censure of a person who has disappointed you.
  • fault-finding: Similar because it hunts out faults; differs because fault-finding is a nagging, pernickety habit of detecting defects, whereas reproachful is a single moral look that names one let-down and begs for recognition.
  • scornful: Similar because it conveys strong disapproval; differs because scornful adds contempt and superiority — the sneer of one who looks down — where the reproachful person stands on the same level and is hurt rather than haughty.
  • upbraiding: Similar because it is a form of open blame; differs because upbraiding is a vigorous, vocal, public reprimand, whereas reproachful is gentler, quieter, and freighted with disappointment rather than anger.
