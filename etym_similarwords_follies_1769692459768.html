• Foolishness: Similar because both denote a lack of good sense; differs because foolishness is a general trait or quality of a person, while a folly is a specific misguided act or choice.
  • Nonsense: Similar because both name irrational or senseless behavior; differs because nonsense points to words or ideas that are meaningless or absurd, while follies are foolish actions committed despite better knowledge.
  • Absurdities: Similar because both name irrational conduct or ideas; differs because absurdities highlight logical incongruity or the ridiculous, whereas follies imply a failure of practical judgment and self-restraint.
  • Imprudence: Similar because both indicate a lack of foresight or caution; differs because imprudence is a more neutral, formal carelessness in decision-making, while folly carries greater moral or comic weight.
  • Indiscretions: Similar because both name unwise actions; differs because an indiscretion is usually a lapse in tact or social judgment, while a folly can be a grand, self-deceiving blunder.
  • Vices: Similar because both describe blameworthy conduct; differs because vices are deep-seated moral corruptions or wicked habits, while follies are lighter, more forgivable weaknesses born of vanity or silliness.
  • Fatuities: Similar because both denote stupidity; differs because fatuity implies an inherent, mindless foolishness, while a folly is an active, energetic but misguided action.
  • Extravagances: Similar because both can describe wasteful, foolish expenditure; differs because extravagance stresses excessive spending or display, while folly stresses the want of sense behind the act.
