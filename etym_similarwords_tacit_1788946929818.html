• Implicit: Similar because both refer to meaning conveyed without explicit verbal expression; differs because implicit often refers to something inherently contained or logically deduced within a statement or situation, whereas tacit emphasizes unspoken assent, passive silence, or mutual non-verbal understanding between people.
  • Implied: Similar because both involve communicating indirectly rather than through direct declaration; differs because implied actively suggests or hints at a conclusion through cues, whereas tacit rests upon quiet acquiescence and the absence of vocal objection.
  • Unspoken: Similar because both denote thoughts, agreements, or feelings left unsaid; differs because unspoken is a plain descriptive term for anything not verbalized, whereas tacit carries an elevated legal, diplomatic, or social weight denoting binding recognition.
  • Inferred: Similar because both relate to unstated communication; differs because inferred denotes the deduction made by an observer from available evidence, whereas tacit describes the nature of the unspoken understanding or pact itself.
  • Understood: Similar because both describe notions grasped without necessity of explanation; differs because understood is common everyday language, whereas tacit is formal, academic, and frequently anchored in legal doctrine or high literary prose.
  • Unexpressed: Similar because both indicate thoughts that are not voiced; differs because unexpressed often suggests suppression or emotional hesitation, whereas tacit indicates that vocalization was omitted because it was completely superfluous.
  • Silent: Similar because both trace back to the absence of audible sound; differs because silent denotes the mere physical absence of noise or utterance, whereas tacit signifies that the silence carries a specific, mutually understood semantic or contractual meaning.
