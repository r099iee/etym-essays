• Modest: Similar because both mean unassuming and free from vanity; differs because "modest" is a broad moral virtue of humility applied to persons, claims, and achievements, while "demure" is specifically a quiet, composed outward demeanor, often of a woman.
  • Coy: Similar because both suggest a shy, reserved manner; differs because "coy" implies calculated, flirtatious, or affected shyness designed to attract, while "demure" implies genuine or studied sobriety and modesty.
  • Reserved: Similar because both describe keeping one's manner under control; differs because "reserved" is neutral and formal, suggesting emotional distance, while "demure" suggests gentle sweetness and modest charm.
  • Reticent: Similar because both describe quietness; differs because "reticent" specifically means reluctant to speak, holding back words, while "demure" covers posture, dress, and general demeanor, not only speech.
  • Diffident: Similar because both describe a withdrawn, quiet manner; differs because "diffident" comes from lack of self-confidence and shy insecurity, while "demure" can be a poised, deliberate choice of restraint.
  • Bashful: Similar because both suggest shrinking modesty; differs because "bashful" implies shy awkwardness, blushes, and social unease, while "demure" is composed and graceful.
  • Prim: Similar because both describe a proper, neat manner; differs because "prim" adds stiffness and priggish neatness (a "prim" person is fussily precise), while "demure" is softer and more modest than stiff.
  • Priggish: Similar because both suggest exaggerated propriety; differs because "priggish" is self-righteous, arrogant, and irritating, while "demure" is gentle and modest.
  • Shy: Similar because both describe a quiet, unassertive person; differs because "shy" is an anxious, involuntary shrinking from attention, while "demure" is a deliberate, dignified self-restraint.
  • Decorous: Similar because both describe proper, seemly behavior; differs because "decorous" emphasizes fitting social propriety and correctness of conduct, while "demure" emphasizes quiet modesty and sweetness.
  • Unassuming: Similar because both mean not putting oneself forward; differs because "unassuming" is a plain, pleasant absence of pretension, with no suggestion of the studied modesty or primness that "demure" can carry.
