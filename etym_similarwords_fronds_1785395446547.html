• Leaves: Similar because both are the photosynthetic organs of plants; differs because a leaf is the ordinary flat blade of a flowering plant, while a frond is a whole compound structure of many leaflets, typical of ferns and palms.
  • Fiddleheads: Similar because both are parts of ferns; differs because fiddleheads are the young, coiled fronds before they unroll — the same organ at an earlier stage, and a culinary word besides.
  • Blades: Similar because both name plant foliage; differs because blades are the flat, narrow, sword-like leaves of grasses and onions, whereas fronds are broad and divided.
  • Sprays: Similar because both suggest foliage spread out from a plant; differs because sprays and branches are woody supports that bear leaves, whereas a frond is both support and leaf in one.
  • Foliage: Similar because both name a mass of leaves; differs because foliage is a general collective word for any plant's leaves, while fronds name specific leaf-like organs of ferns, palms and cycads.
  • Pinnae: Similar because both are parts of a fern; differs because pinnae are the leaflets into which a frond is divided — parts of the frond, not the whole organ.
  • Needles: Similar because both are the leaves of ancient, non-flowering plant groups; differs because needles are the narrow, pointed leaves of conifers, while fronds are broad, divided and feathery.
  • Branches: Similar because both can suggest great spreading arms of a plant; differs because branches are woody, leaf-bearing shoots, whereas a frond is a single leaf-like organ that is both support and leaf in one.
  • Palm: Similar because palms bear fronds; differs because "palm" names the tree, while "fronds" names its foliage — one is the plant, the other the organ.
  • Fern: Similar because ferns bear fronds; differs because "fern" names the plant (the whole spore-reproducing plant), while "fronds" names its leaf organs.
