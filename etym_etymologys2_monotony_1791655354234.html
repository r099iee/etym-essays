↓
Hellenistic Greek (c. 1st c. CE)
μονωτονία (monotonía)

↓
Post-Classical Latin (c. 16th c.)
monotonia

↓
Middle French / French (c. 17th c.)
monotonie
"uniformity of sound or style"
↓
Modern English (c. 1706)
monotony
"unvarying sameness, tedium"

Note: Compounded from Greek mónos ('single, alone') and tónos ('musical pitch, tension', from PIE *ten-, 'to stretch'), entering English via French monotonie.
