Note: Old English tīdung was formed from the verb tīdian with the abstract-noun suffix -ung. The form and currency of the word were reinforced in Middle English by Old Norse tiðindi “events, news” (from tiðr “occurring”), a result of Scandinavian settlement in England. Debate: the PIE base of Germanic *tīdiz is disputed — Watkins and others derive it from *dā- “to divide” (time as a divided portion); some scholars prefer connection with *dey- “to shine” (time reckoned by the sun, cf. Latin dies “day”). The *dā- derivation is presented above; neither is universally accepted.
