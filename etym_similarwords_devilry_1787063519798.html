• Devilment: Similar because both derive from devil and denote mischievous conduct; differs because devilment is almost always playful (pranks), while devilry can be seriously wicked or occult.
  • Wickedness: Similar because both denote moral evil; differs because wickedness is the broad abstract term for evil, with no devilish, magical, or playful nuance.
  • Mischief: Similar because both can describe naughty, trouble-making behaviour; differs because mischief is usually harmless fun while devilry implies a darker or crueller edge.
  • Sorcery / black magic: Similar because devilry historically names diabolical magic; differs because sorcery is the neutral term for magic while devilry is specifically bound to demons and the devil.
  • Villainy: Similar because both denote wicked deeds; differs because villainy stresses criminal or dastardly acts and has no supernatural or playful colouring.
  • Knavery / roguery: Similar because both mean dishonest or mischievous conduct; differs because they imply cunning trickery by a scoundrel, while devilry is wilder and more reckless.
  • Diablerie: Similar because it is a direct French loanword for devilishness and magic; differs because diablerie is literary, rare, and often refers to artistic representations of devils.
  • Tomfoolery: Similar because both can mean foolish, tricky antics; differs because tomfoolery is purely silly and harmless, with no wicked or supernatural connotation.
