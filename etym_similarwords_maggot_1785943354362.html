• Grub: Similar because both are insect larvae; differs because "grub" usually names beetle larvae living in soil or wood (and also means food or a hard worker), while "maggot" specifically names the legless larva of a fly in decaying matter.
  • Larva: Similar because both name the immature post-egg stage of an insect; differs because "larva" is the formal scientific umbrella term for the juvenile stage of all metamorphosing insects, while "maggot" is the everyday name for a fly larva.
  • Worm: Similar because both are soft-bodied, legless, wriggling creatures, and both are used as insults; differs because a worm is a distinct invertebrate (an annelid) or any similar creeping creature, not an insect juvenile stage, and the insult "worm" is weaker and less visceral than "maggot".
  • Caterpillar: Similar because both are immature insects; differs because a caterpillar is the leaf-feeding larva of a butterfly or moth, with legs, head, and often hair — none of the soft, legless, rot-feeding image of a maggot.
  • Gentles: Similar because "gentles" is the angler's name for maggots used as bait; differs because "gentles" is specialised fishing jargon for the same creature, while "maggot" is the general word.
  • Blowfly larva: Similar because blowfly larvae are maggots; differs because the phrase names the larva by its parent species and is used in scientific or medical (debridement) contexts, while "maggot" is the common term.
  • Pupa / chrysalis: Similar because both are stages of insect development; differs because a pupa is the quiescent, cocooned stage into which a larva turns before adulthood, while a maggot is the active feeding larva itself.
  • Mite: Similar because both are tiny creatures associated with infestation; differs because a mite is a minute arachnid (eight-legged), not an insect larva at all, and does not share the fly-lifecycle meaning.
  • Scum / vermin: Similar because both are figurative insults for contemptible people; differs because these are metaphors drawn from filth and pests at large, while "maggot" retains its specific, visceral image of fly larvae in decay.
  • Whim / caprice: Similar because "a maggot in the brain" historically meant a whim; differs because "whim" and "caprice" are ordinary, current words for fancies, whereas the "maggot" senses are obsolete and survive only as figurative-metaphorical echoes of the insect word.
  • Fancy: Similar because the old idiom "maggots in the head" meant strange fancies; differs because "fancy" is the neutral, common word for an inclination or notion, with none of the obsolete wriggling-larva image.
  • Cur / degenerate: Similar because both are insults for a despicable person; differs because "cur" (a contemptible man, from the dog word) and "degenerate" name degraded character without the specific rot-breeding, parasite image of "maggot".
