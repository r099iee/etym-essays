• Swish: Similar because both are soft sounds of movement; differs because a swish is a single sweeping, higher-pitched sound (fabric, whip, tail), while a rustle is a drier, repeated sound of many small contacts.
  • Whisper: Similar because both are faint soft sounds; differs because a whisper is normally human speech or the wind — voiced — while a rustle is the dry crackle of leaves, paper, or silk.
  • Crackle: Similar because both are dry sounds of small snaps; differs because a crackle has sharper, more audible individual pops (fire, frost), while a rustle is softer, smoother, and continuous.
  • Crinkle: Similar because both describe a dry folded sound; differs because crinkle is specifically the sound of crumpled paper or fabric creasing, while rustle covers a wider range of dry movement.
  • Susurrus: Similar because both mean a soft continuous sound; differs because susurrus is a literary, poetic word usually for murmuring wind, water, or voices, without the dry crackling texture of rustle.
  • Murmur: Similar because both are low continuous sounds; differs because a murmur is softer, voiced, and usually of people, water, or bees, not a dry rustling.
  • Steal (cattle sense): Similar because rustling is a form of theft; differs because "steal" is the generic word for taking dishonestly, while "rustle" is specialised to livestock and carries frontier, romantic overtones.
  • Poach: Similar because both mean taking animals unlawfully; differs because poaching is hunting wild game, while rustling is stealing domesticated livestock.
  • Appropriate: Similar because both can mean taking something for oneself; differs because "appropriate" is a euphemistic, often bureaucratic word, while "rustle" is direct and colloquial.
  • Scrounge: Similar because both ("rustle up") mean gathering or getting something quickly; differs because "scrounge" implies cadging or begging, while "rustle up" implies brisk, resourceful improvisation, especially of food.
  • Forage: Similar because both mean searching for and gathering; differs because foraging is usually searching for food in the wild, while rustling (in the sound sense) is simply moving with a dry sound.
