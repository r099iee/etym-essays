Note: The ultimate Indo-European root of Latin malus has been historically connected by Julius Pokorny and traditional philologists to PIE *mel- ("false, bad, wrong", seen in Lithuanian mèlas "lie" and Old Church Slavonic malo "little"). However, modern Indo-Europeanists (notably Michiel de Vaan) point out that cognates outside Italic are phonologically isolated or debated, suggesting malus may represent an ancient Central European substrate term. In legal history, the Anglo-Norman legal phrase malice prepense ("premeditated malice") entered English royal courts in the 14th century, directly generating modern criminal law's malice aforethought.
