TRUMPETS is the plural of trumpet, a brass wind instrument consisting of a long, roughly cylindrical tube flared at the end into a bell, played through a cup-shaped mouthpiece and (in the modern instrument) fitted with valves that alter pitch. The plural commonly denotes (1) more than one such instrument, (2) the loud, brilliant, penetrating sounds the instruments produce ("warning trumpets were sounded"), and (3) by extension any loud public call or announcement, or the eschatological summons of religious tradition.

Primary senses

  • The instruments: soprano brass instruments with the highest and most brilliant voice of the brass family; in an orchestra they sit behind the woodwinds, and in jazz they are frontline solo instruments.
  • The sounds: "the trumpets sounded" — a collective idiom in which the plural stands for the event of trumpeting itself; trumpets are the traditional voice of alarms, fanfares, and military signals because their tone cuts through noise and carries over distance, as in the context sentence where warning trumpets echo along rocky shores.

Secondary and extended senses

  • The elephant's cry: to "trumpet" is the standard verb for an elephant's loud call, named for its resemblance to the instrument's blast; hence "the trumpets of the herd".
  • To proclaim loudly: "to trumpet (forth) the news" — a metaphor from the herald's trumpet, meaning to announce publicly and insistently, often with the nuance of boastfulness (compare "blow one's own trumpet", i.e. praise oneself).
  • Religious register: the trumpet of the Last Judgement ("the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised" — 1 Corinthians 15:52, Authorised Version), and the seven trumpets of Revelation, give "trumpets" permanent apocalyptic weight: the sound that cannot be ignored.
  • Musical kinds: natural trumpet (valveless, Baroque), slide trumpet, keyed trumpet, valved trumpet (after c. 1815), piccolo trumpet, and the closely related cornet and flugelhorn; jazz added the muted trumpet and the stratospheric bebop style.

Semantic bridge

  • Instrument → sound → call: because the trumpet is above all a signalling instrument, its name glides easily from the object to its blast ("a trumpet sounded") to any forceful public announcement.
  • Sound → animal cry: the elephant's call is perceived as trumpet-like, so the verb migrates from human instruments to the natural world.
  • Sound → judgement: the Bible's eschatological trumpet gives the plural a permanent home in religious vocabulary, where it denotes a summons that cannot be refused.

Usage notes

  • In the context sentence ("Then warning trumpets were suddenly sounded"), the plural is idiomatic: trumpets are "sounded" (not "played") when they signal.
  • The related word trump (archaic for "trumpet") survives in "the last trump"; the card-game trump is an unrelated word from French triomphe "triumph" — a classic case of convergent homonyms.
  • The plural dominates signalling contexts ("trumpets sounded", "trumpets blared", "trumpets echoed"), where the instruments are imagined as a chorus or corps announcing something momentous.
