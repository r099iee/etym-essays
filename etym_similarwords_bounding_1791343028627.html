• Leaping: Similar because it involves launching oneself off the ground with muscular force; differs because leaping stresses the single act of clearing height or a gap, whereas bounding implies continuous, rhythmic, elastic springs in forward motion.
  • Springing: Similar because it conveys sudden, elastic propulsion; differs because springing focuses on the initial coiled release of energy from stillness, while bounding emphasizes sustained, repeated strides.
  • Prancing: Similar because it describes lively, high-stepping movement; differs because prancing implies deliberate, proud, or ostentatious display (often associated with horses or self-satisfied people), whereas bounding is characterized by athletic momentum and vigorous distance-covering.
  • Gamboling: Similar because it portrays joyful, exuberant jumping; differs because gamboling specifically implies frolicsome, playful, and carefree skipping (as of lambs or young children), lacking the forceful speed and athletic power of bounding.
  • Cavorting: Similar because it depicts energetic, lively movement; differs because cavorting implies boisterous, often unruly or undignified romping and dancing, whereas bounding remains focused on linear, leaping locomotion.
  • Vaulting: Similar because it entails elevating the body over an obstacle; differs because vaulting usually requires hand support or a pole to clear a barrier, representing a deliberate gymnastic feat rather than natural running leaps.
  • Skipping: Similar because it describes light, rhythmic steps; differs because skipping is a gentle, youthful alternating gait with far less kinetic force, elevation, and speed than bounding.
