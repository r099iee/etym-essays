• Sandbar: Similar because both are ridges of sand; differs because sandbar is essentially nautical, formed by currents and often submerged, while a sand-bank is a raised, habitable slope.
  • Sand dune: Similar because both are masses of sand; differs because a dune is wind-built, mobile, and unstable, far too shifting to house a burrow.
  • Sandpit: Similar because both relate to sand; differs because a sandpit is a hollow where sand is dug out, or a children's play area, neither raised nor habitable.
  • Bank: Similar because both name a raised slope of earth; differs because bank specifies no material, while sand-bank combines the substance (sand) with the raised form.
  • Shoal: Similar because both are shallow sandy areas in water; differs because shoal is a generic term for any shallow, emphasizing navigational hazard rather than a habitable earth slope.
  • Bar: Similar because both are ridges built by water; differs because bar is a general geological term (sandbar, gravel bar) without the domestic or hillside sense of sand-bank.
  • Ridge / embankment: Similar because both are raised linear landforms; differs because they specify no material and are used for roads, railways, and defenses, not for rabbit homes.
  • Beach: Similar because both are sandy; differs because a beach is the intertidal shoreline itself, horizontal rather than ridged, and not a place to burrow.
  • Warren / burrow: Similar because all are rabbit homes; differs because a warren is the network of tunnels and a burrow the hole itself, while the sand-bank is the surface landform the burrow is dug into.
