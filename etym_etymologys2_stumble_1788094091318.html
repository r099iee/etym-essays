Modern English (14th c.–present)
STUMBLE
"to trip; to blunder; to find by chance"

NOTE: No PIE or earlier Germanic stage can be securely reconstructed, so none is invented. The OED records the word as "of uncertain origin", probably from a Scandinavian source (Old Norse stumra; cf. Norwegian stumla, dialectal Swedish stumra; a Middle Low German stommeln "to stumble" is also recorded, pointing to a North Sea Germanic or northern source); the -l- is an unexplained formative, possibly the frequentative suffix. The older suggestion of a PIE root *(s)teu- "to push, strike" (Pokorny) is conjectural and explains neither the vowel nor the -l-; a distant link through the Germanic *stam-/*stum- syllable cluster (cf. stammer, German stümpern "to bungle") is likewise speculative.
