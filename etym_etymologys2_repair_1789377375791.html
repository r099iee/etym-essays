PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*per- / *perh₃-
"to bring forth, procure, produce, prepare"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*parāō
"to prepare, provide, make ready"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
parāre
"to make ready, prepare, furnish, equip"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
reparāre
"to restore, renew, refresh, mend, recover, retrieve"
Note: Compound of the iterative/reversive prefix re- ("again, back") + parāre ("to prepare, make ready").
↓
Old French (c. 12th–13th c.)
reparer (noun: reparacion)
"to restore, mend, make good, renovate"
↓
Middle English (c. 1350–1400)
repairen (noun: repair)
"to mend, restore, renew; condition of physical soundness"
↓
Modern English (present)
REPAIR (Restore / Condition)

--- ROOT WORD 2: REPAIR (To Betake Oneself / Go / Retreat) ---

PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*ph₂tḗr
"father"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
patria
"fatherland, native country"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
repatriāre
"to return to one's fatherland, go back home"
Note: Formed from re- ("back") + patria ("homeland").
↓
Old French (c. 11th–12th c.)
repairier (noun: repaire)
"to return home, resort to a place, seek refuge; haunt, den"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300–1350)
repairen (noun: repaire)
"to go, resort, betake oneself, return"
↓
Modern English (present)
REPAIR (Betake Oneself)
