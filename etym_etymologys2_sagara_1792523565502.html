Compound formation of prefix sa- and noun gara:

[Component 1: Prefix sa- — With / Accompanied by]
Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed)
*sm̥- / *sem-
"together, with, at the same time, one"
(source of Greek ha- / hama, Latin sim- / simul, English same)
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (reconstructed)
*ha- / *sa-
"with, together with, accompanied by"
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1500–1000 BCE)
sa- (स)
"with, having, possessing, accompanied by"

[Component 2: Gara — Poison / Swallowed Venom]
Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed)
*gʷerh₃- / *gʷel-
"to swallow, devour, gulp down"
(source of Greek bibrōskein, Latin vorāre, English devour/gorge)
↓
Proto-Indo-Iranian (reconstructed)
*gāra- / *gara-
"that which is swallowed; fluid potion, venom, poison"
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1500–1000 BCE; Ṛgveda)
gara (गर)
"poison, venom, noxious beverage (that which is swallowed)"

[Unified Bahuvrīhi Compound: Sagara]
↓
Epic Sanskrit Compound (c. 500–200 BCE; Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa)
sa-gara (सगर)
"he who was born with poison; legendary emperor of Ayodhyā"
(sa- [with] + gara [poison]; born alongside neutralized venom)
↓
Vṛddhi Patronymic Derivative (c. 400 BCE–present)
sāgara (सागर)
"the ocean; the abyssal chasm excavated by the sons of Sagara"
↓
Modern Indo-Aryan & Indological English (loanword via epic Sanskrit)
Sagara
"ancient Solar dynasty emperor of Ayodhyā; patronymic originator of the ocean (Sāgara)"

Note on historical debate: Sanskrit grammarians (including Pāṇini and classical commentators on the Rāmāyaṇa such as Govindarāja and Tilaka) unanimously parse Sagara as an exocentric *Bahuvrīhi* compound: saha gareṇa vartate iti sagaraḥ ("he who exists together with poison"), referencing the Purāṇic narrative where the co-wife poisoned the mother. Comparative philologists (such as Manfred Mayrhofer in Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindoarischen) affirm that gara- ("poison") derives from the PIE verbal root *gʷerh₃- ("to swallow"), originally signifying "that which is swallowed or drunk down" (cognate with Sanskrit girati "he swallows" and Russian žrat’ "to devour"). Some modern Western mythologists questioned whether *Sagara* was a back-formation from *sāgara* ("ocean"), but historical-linguistic morphological rules demonstrate that *sāgara* is a regular secondary *vṛddhi* derivation from the primary personal name *Sagara*, confirming the priority of the royal name in ancient Indo-Aryan onomastics.
