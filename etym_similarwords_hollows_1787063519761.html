• valleys: Similar because both are low-lying depressions in the land; differs because a valley is a large, elongated course between ranges with sides and often a river, whereas a hollow is smaller, rounder, and often bowl-shaped.
  • cavities: Similar because both mean empty or hollowed spaces inside something; differs because cavity is the technical, scientific term (especially in dentistry and anatomy), while hollow is the everyday word for any scooped-out place.
  • depressions: Similar because both describe sunken, lower areas; differs because depression is neutral and often technical (a depression in the ground), while hollow suggests a more rounded, sheltering, or hollowed-out shape.
  • recesses: Similar because both name deep, set-back inner spaces; differs because recesses stresses being set back and concealed (the recesses of a cave), while hollows stresses the concave, scooped shape itself.
  • dells: Similar because both are small, sheltered valleys in the landscape; differs because dell is poetic and usually implies trees and pleasant seclusion, while hollow is plainer and can be rocky, bare, or ominous.
  • basins: Similar because both describe bowl-like, sunken areas; differs because basin is usually a technical term in geography (river basin) or a man-made vessel, while hollow is the general word for a natural depression.
  • holes: Similar because both are empty spaces; differs because a hole is smaller, rougher, and often accidental or penetrating, whereas a hollow is a broader, shallower, or more deliberate scoop.
  • pits: Similar because both are sunken, excavated places; differs because a pit implies depth, digging, or a trap and is often dark and forbidding, while a hollow need not be deep and can be gentle.
  • concavities: Similar because both name curved, inward-scooped surfaces; differs because concavity is a formal, geometric term, whereas hollow is the natural, everyday word.
  • grottoes: Similar because both can be sheltered hollows or caves; differs because a grotto is usually decorative, artificial, or picturesque and implies beauty, while a hollow is plain and functional.
