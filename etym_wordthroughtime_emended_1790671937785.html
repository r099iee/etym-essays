- Freed from fault in Roman letters - emendare as elite correction of speech, manuscripts and morals; prestige, censorious care
- Restored by Renaissance humanists - Bentley, Scaliger and printers emend classics by collation and bold conjecture; heroic, quarrelsome scholarship
- Distinguished from amend in English usage - 18th–19th-century grammarians fix emend to texts, amend to things and conduct; precision becomes shibboleth
- Professionalised in critical editions - Lachmann, Housman, Greg-Bowers make emendation methodical, apparatus-bound, humble about intervention
- Thinned by plain-style pressure - modern trade prose prefers correct, revise, fix; emend retreats to academy, law-books and acknowledgements as mark of philological honour
