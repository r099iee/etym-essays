- Vedic & Early Sūtra Era (c. 1000–500 BCE) — Fluid & Aquatic Kinetic Roots: The verbal root *plu* and primary noun *plava* designated swimming, floating, bathing, or undulating watercraft, with kinetic extensions describing sudden surges, plunging dives, or leaping movements across currents.
- Epic Sanskrit Era (c. 500 BCE–200 CE) — Heroic Simian Kenning: Re-crystallized in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata as a ubiquitous poetic kenning (*plavaga*, *plavagarṣabha*, "bull among leapers") celebrating the miraculous leaps, agile acrobatics, and battlefield assaults of the monkey hosts.
- Classical Kāvya & Dramaturgy Era (c. 300–800 CE) — Polysemic Poetic Texture: Employed by master poets like Kālidāsa and Bhāravi with deliberate double-meaning (*śleṣa*), shifting effortlessly between leaping monkeys, hopping frogs in monsoon descriptions, and soaring birds traversing seasonal clouds.
- Medieval Jyotiṣa & Ayurvedic Compendia (c. 600–1500 CE) — Astrological & Clinical Specialization: Adopted into astronomical treatises as an epithet for Śani (Saturn) reflecting his slow, staggered gait across the zodiac constellations, and in veterinary science to describe bounding equine paces.
- Modern Philological & Indological Studies (19th–21st c.) — Lexical & Metric Analysis: Analyzed by Western Indologists and translators as an instructive example of Sanskrit's rich descriptive nominal system, demonstrating how descriptive verb-noun compounds enrich epic narrative without semantic redundancy.
