• Agonized: Similar because both words describe states of severe, unbearable suffering; differs because agonized often emphasizes the active, convulsive physical or vocal thrashing of intense suffering (e.g. an agonized scream or death agony), whereas anguished carries deeper psychological, moral, and emotional resonance, highlighting prolonged internal torment.
  • Tormented: Similar because both denote persistent and excruciating distress; differs because tormented frequently implies the presence of an active persecutor, external antagonist, or recurring obsessive thought that repeatedly inflicts suffering, while anguished highlights the inward, visceral experience of heartbreak and despair.
  • Tortured: Similar because both convey intense physical or emotional pain; differs because tortured carries direct connotations of deliberate, methodical cruelty, coercive interrogation, or twisted, labored overthinking (e.g. tortured logic), whereas anguished denotes pure, unadulterated emotional grief or acute despair.
  • Distressed: Similar because both describe an unpleasant state of mental discomfort; differs because distressed is far milder in register and emotional intensity, referring to general upset, worry, or commercial strain, whereas anguished denotes profound, soul-wrenching agony.
  • Harrowed: Similar because both depict someone devastated by acute pain or shock; differs because harrowed emphasizes the ravaged, traumatized aftermath and gaunt physical toll of having one's feelings torn up (like soil broken by a agricultural harrow), while anguished captures the acute, burning intensity of the suffering itself.
  • Afflicted: Similar because both describe individuals burdened by distress; differs because afflicted is a formal, often passive condition describing someone suffering from a chronic disease, handicap, or misfortune, whereas anguished is an active, intensely felt emotional and somatic state.
