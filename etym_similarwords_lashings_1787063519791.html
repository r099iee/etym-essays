• blows: Similar because lashings in the passage are blows delivered by the tail; differs because blows is a general, neutral term for any striking impact, while lashings specifically evokes whip-strokes and sweeping, whipping motion.
  • strokes: Similar because each lashing is a stroke of a whip; differs because strokes is far broader (swimming, painting, illness) and carries none of the violence or punishment.
  • flogging / beating: Similar because both name repeated punishing blows; differs because flogging and beating name the whole punishment event, while lashings count the individual strokes and can describe any whipping motion, punitive or not.
  • whipping: Similar because lashing and whipping are near-synonyms for whip-strokes; differs because whipping names the action with a whip specifically, while lashings can come from anything whip-like (a rope, a wave, a dragon's tail).
  • thrashing: Similar because both mean severe beating; differs because a thrashing is a complete beating administered to someone, whereas lashings are the blows themselves and can be transferred to non-human forces.
  • smacks / slaps: Similar because both are striking impacts; differ because these are light, open-handed, and human-scale, nowhere near the violent scale of lashings.
  • plenty / lots / heaps / loads (for "lashings of"): Similar because these are the plain modern equivalents of the idiom "lashings of"; differ because lashings of is old-fashioned, British, and food-and-drink oriented, while plenty, lots, heaps, and loads are neutral and universal.
  • oodles / scads / stacks: Similar because these are also colloquial quantity words meaning a large amount; differ because they are more American and playful and have none of the whipping history or the cosy Victorian-tea associations of lashings.
  • bindings / cordage (nautical lashing): Similar because they name the ropework sense of lashing; differ because bindings and cordage are modern, generic trade terms, while lashing is the traditional nautical word for securing loads.
