QUARRELLED (British spelling; American quarreled) is the past tense and past participle of the verb to quarrel: to engage in an angry dispute, a heated disagreement, or a petty squabble with another person or party. The verb names a collision of wills conducted in words — sharp exchanges, fault-finding, accusations, reproaches, hard words, and hot tempers — that by definition stops just short of physical blows. To say that people quarrelled is to say that they fell out verbally, that hostility flared and was voiced, but that the quarrel remained a matter of speech and feeling rather than of fisticuffs or weapons. Literary context — The Hobbit. In the passage, the goblins have captured Thorin and the company and are rifling the ponies' baggage. The narrator's anaphora piles up the same phrase: the goods are "rummaged by goblins, and smelt by goblins, and fingered by goblins, and quarrelled over by goblins." The verb is the climactic, bathetic note in the series. A pack that could not even cooperate in plundering its own prisoners is shown squabbling over the loot — snarling, snatching, accusing one another — and the petty, avaricious tone exposes the goblins' fractious, mercenary natures even as their cruelty is on full display. QUARRELLED here is deliberately anti-epic: it reduces terrifying monsters to bickering children over treasure, and the deflation is precisely the point of Tolkien's irony. Polysemy and related senses. The word today carries a network of related but distinct meanings:
  • Interpersonal altercation (verb, core sense): to argue angrily or peevishly with someone, usually over a specific grievance or trifle. "The couple quarrelled about money." This is the sense used in the card.
  • Quarrel with (idiom): to find fault with or object to a proposal, statement, or arrangement. "I cannot quarrel with that conclusion." Here the object is an idea rather than a person, and the verb shades from emotional dispute toward reasoned objection.
  • Quarrel (noun, sense 1): an angry dispute or altercation; a heated exchange of words. "They had a quarrel over the inheritance."
  • Quarrel (noun, sense 2): a cause or ground of complaint; a grievance (now largely archaic). Middle English inherited from Latin querela the sense "ground for complaint," still faintly present in phrases such as "a quarrel with fate."
  • Quarrel (crossbow bolt): a short, heavy, square-headed bolt discharged from a crossbow or arbalest — an entirely separate word, a homonym descended from Latin quadrus "square," not from the "complain" root. The bolt's name refers to its four-sided head. This is technical, historical, archery jargon, and a lexicographer must always keep the two quarrels apart.
  • Quarrelsome / querulous (adjectives): from the same Latin family, meaning inclined to quarrel (quarrelsome) or habitually complaining (querulous), showing how the root's grievance-sense bred a whole personality type.
  • Quarreling (American) vs. quarrelling (British): the doubled -l- follows the British convention of doubling a final -l- before vowel suffixes; the spelling distinction is purely orthographic and makes no difference in pronunciation.
  • Archaic and legal: quarrel as armed conflict. In Middle English a querele could also mean armed combat or a dispute carried to the point of a duel; the word has since retreated from the battlefield to the drawing room.
Semantic boundaries among near-synonyms. A quarrel is an angry contention driven by grievance or ill feeling, characteristically noisy, personal, and somewhat petty. An argument is a reasoned exchange of opposing views; a dispute is a formal contest over facts, rights, or ownership; bickering is peevish, repeated squabbling over trifles; squabbling is noisy, childish wrangling; wrangling is prolonged, contentious argument; a feud is a long, bitter vendetta between families or parties; and fighting crosses into physical violence, which a quarrel by definition has not. QUARRELLED thus occupies the threshold of violence — hard words, hot tempers, mutual grievance — without crossing it, which is exactly why Tolkien reaches for it to render the goblins' shabby greed.
