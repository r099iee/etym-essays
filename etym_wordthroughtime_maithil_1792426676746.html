- [Vedic & Epic Era (c. 1000–500 BCE)] — originated as a Sanskrit patronymic and relational adjective (derived from Mithilā, capital of Videha founded by King Mithi), functioning as the beloved epic epithet of Sītā in Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa - [perception: sacred emblem of royal dignity, fidelity, and heroic endurance]
- [Classical Sanskrit Drama & Poetry (c. 4th–8th c. CE; Kālidāsa, Bhavabhūti)] — celebrated in masterpieces like *Uttararāmacarita* and *Raghuvaṃśa* as the epitome of tragic feminine virtue and majestic philosophical resilience - [perception: revered as the ultimate literary heroine and embodiment of pure dharma]
- [Medieval Vernacular & Linguistic Emergence (c. 14th–16th c.)] — evolved into the name of the distinct Eastern Indo-Aryan language of northern Bihar and Nepal; immortalized by poet Vidyāpati's devotional and romantic lyrics (*Padāvalī*) - [perception: language of sublime lyric poetry, Vaishnava devotion, and refined scholarship]
- [Early Modern Folk Traditions (c. 17th–19th c.)] — identified with the vibrant ceremonial courtyard and wall painting tradition (*Mithila / Madhubani art*) practiced by village women to invoke blessings during weddings and seasonal rites - [perception: living domestic artistic heritage and maternal cultural transmission]
- [Modern Contemporary & Constitutional Era (20th–21st c.)] — enshrined in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution (2003) and adopted as an official provincial language in Nepal, while remaining universally revered as the epic epithet of Sītā - [perception: vibrant modern linguistic identity coupled with timeless sacred mythological veneration]
