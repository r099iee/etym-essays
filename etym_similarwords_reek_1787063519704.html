• stink: Similar because both name a strong bad smell and a verb "to smell very badly"; differs because "stink" is exclusively olfactory and bluntly colloquial, while "reek" still carries its old "smoke/steam" sense and can be figurative and literary.
  • stench: Similar because both name a foul smell; differs because "stench" is a noun only, heavy and formal (a stench is worse and more ponderous), while "reek" is both noun and verb.
  • smell: Similar because both can mean an odor (usually bad with reek); differs because "smell" is entirely neutral and applies to good, bad, and indifferent odors, while "reek" is always strong and bad.
  • pong: Similar because both mean a bad smell (British slang); differs because "pong" is jokey and mild (a whiff), while "reek" implies a heavy, pervasive stench.
  • fume: Similar because both are smoke-like or pungent emanations; differs because "fumes" are usually plural and denote specific irritating gases (car fumes), while "reek" is a general vapor or a smell.
  • smoke: Similar because both name visible vapor — reek's oldest sense; differs because "smoke" is now the neutral standard word for combustion vapor, while "reek" is the archaic, literary, and Scots word that also means smell.
  • vapor: Similar because both name airborne moisture or fumes; differs because "vapor" is scientific and neutral (water vapor), while "reek" is poetic or pejorative.
  • effluvium: Similar because both name an unpleasant outflow or emanation; differs because "effluvium" is technical, formal, and often a plural or medical word, while "reek" is short, common, and blunt.
  • miasma: Similar because both suggest a heavy, unhealthy atmosphere; differs because "miasma" is a historical and poetic word for a disease-laden vapor and now a metaphor for corruption, while "reek" is a concrete smell.
  • whiff: Similar because both name an odor encountered; differs because a whiff is faint and brief, while reek is strong, persistent, and unpleasant.
  • malodor: Similar because both mean a bad smell; differs because "malodor" is clinical and euphemistic, while "reek" is direct and vivid.
