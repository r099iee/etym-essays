• EFFORT: Similar because both denote the expenditure of energy toward a goal; differs because effort is the bare expenditure of energy, quantifiable and neutral, while endeavour adds sustained purpose, seriousness, and a nuance of dutiful application.
  • ATTEMPT: Similar because both denote trying to do something; differs because attempt stresses merely trying an action, whether casual or serious, while endeavour implies a serious, prolonged, principled striving toward a worthwhile end.
  • ENTERPRISE: Similar because both are undertakings requiring effort; differs because enterprise stresses boldness, initiative, and often commercial or pioneering energy, while endeavour stresses earnest, duty-bound application rather than daring.
  • EXERTION: Similar because both involve putting forth strength; differs because exertion emphasises strain — physical or mental effort under pressure — while endeavour carries the moral tone of obligation and sustained purpose rather than strain.
  • UNDERTAKING: Similar because both name a task or project one takes on; differs because undertaking stresses the assumption of a responsibility or task (something undertaken), while endeavour stresses the earnest striving itself and its quality of seriousness.
  • STRIVING: Similar because both denote earnest, continued effort; differs because striving is the more active, almost muscular word for continuous struggle toward a goal (and is typically gerundial), while endeavour is the more formal, goal-oriented noun naming the effort as a whole.
  • PURSUIT: Similar because both name sustained activity directed at an aim; differs because pursuit stresses the chase of an external goal or interest (the pursuit of happiness), often continuous occupation, while endeavour stresses the deliberate, worthy attempt rather than the chase.
  • VENTURE: Similar because both are undertakings requiring effort; differs because venture adds risk and bold speculation (a business venture, a risky venture), while endeavour carries no such risk and instead implies seriousness and worthiness of purpose.
  • ESSAY (noun): Similar because, in its formal and archaic sense, essay means a tentative attempt or trial (an essay at verse), and indeed the book this context comes from is an "essay" in that older sense; differs because in modern usage essay names a piece of prose composition, leaving attempt sense obsolete, while endeavour is a living, dignified word for a serious attempt.
  • ENDEAVOR (US spelling): Similar because it is the very same word in every respect; differs only in orthography — endeavor is Noah Webster's early-nineteenth-century American normalisation of -our to -or (as colour/color), not a different word or meaning.
