• Slander: Similar because both involve false and damaging statements made against someone; differs because slander is a technical legal and common term restricted to spoken defamation, whereas calumny is a higher-register, literary term emphasizing deep malice and moral heinousness.
  • Libel: Similar because both involve defamatory falsehoods damaging a person's reputation; differs because libel specifically denotes permanent or written/published defamation in legal torts, whereas calumny encompasses the broader moral act and spirit of malicious fabrication.
  • Defamation: Similar because both describe the injury of someone's good name through falsehood; differs because defamation is an overarching, formal legal and generic category, lacking the poetic intensity, venomous connotation, and classical resonance of calumny.
  • Aspersion: Similar because both involve attacks on personal reputation; differs because aspersion (commonly used in the plural aspersions, "to cast aspersions") suggests indirect insinuations, damaging hints, or slurs cast upon one's integrity rather than outright fabricated lies.
  • Vilification: Similar because both involve severe verbal attacks on a person's character; differs because vilification emphasizes intense, abusive, and public denigration aimed at making the person appear vile, regardless of whether the statements are strictly fabricated accusations.
  • Traducement: Similar because both mean the malicious misrepresentation of another's character; differs because traducement is an archaic, literary term focusing on leading someone's reputation astray through betrayal and false reporting.
  • Obloquy: Similar because both involve public disgrace and verbal condemnation; differs because obloquy emphasizes the widespread, collective state of public ill-repute, disgrace, and abusive censure heaped upon someone by society.
  • Detraction: Similar because both unjustly diminish another's reputation; differs because detraction historically denotes uncharitably revealing true faults or minimizing genuine virtues, whereas calumny strictly requires the invention of outright falsehoods.
