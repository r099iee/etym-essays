- Early slang blow: In the late 17th century, 'sock' emerged as rough slang for a violent blow, likely echoing the dull thud of a punch landing. Register: low, colloquial.
- Working-class vernacular: Through the 19th century, 'sock' was a staple of plain-spoken speech among labourers and boxers - 'sock it to him' meant hit hard. Register: masculine, physical.
- Figurative energy: In the 20th century the phrase 'sock it to me/them' broadened into a vigorous exhortation to give something everything you've got, loosened from actual fighting.
- Contemporary: Today 'to sock' still means to punch forcefully, and 'a sock' is a hard blow; the word remains informal and blunt, with a heavy, thudding register.
