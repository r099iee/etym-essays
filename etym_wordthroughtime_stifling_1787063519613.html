- 14th to 15th Century: [Physical Asphyxiation & Smothering] - Entered late Middle English (*stuflen*, *stiflen*) from Old French (*estouffer*), denoting literal physical choking, smothering with smoke or dust, or cutting off respiration.
- 16th to 17th Century: [Atmospheric Heat & Emotional Suppression] - Expanded across Elizabethan and Jacobean drama to describe sweltering, enclosed rooms lacking ventilation, as well as the deliberate suppression of sighs, sobs, rumors, and rebellion.
- 18th to 19th Century: [Victorian Social Critique & Domestic Confinement] - Employed extensively in 19th-century novels (e.g., the Brontës, George Eliot) to characterize suffocating domestic spheres, rigid Victorian moral codes, and industrial factory atmospheres.
- 20th Century to Present: [Institutional, Bureaucratic & Economic Choking] - Universally applied to corporate bureaucracy, state censorship, creative stagnation, and economic over-regulation that choke innovation and personal expression.
