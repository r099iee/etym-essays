• Riding: Similar because both concern the activity of sitting on and controlling a horse; differs because 'riding' is the ordinary, everyday noun and verb for the activity itself, while 'equestrian' is the formal adjective of that activity and its institutions.
  • Horsemanship: Similar because both name skill in riding and managing horses; differs because 'horsemanship' is the abstract skill itself, while 'equestrian' is the adjective of everything pertaining to riders, riding, and horses.
  • Equestrianism: Similar because it is the noun formed directly from equestrian, naming riding as an art or sport; differs because 'equestrianism' is a modern, rather technical noun, while 'equestrian' is the everyday adjective and also a noun for a rider.
  • Mounted: Similar because both describe a person on horseback; differs because 'mounted' is a participle meaning literally 'placed on a horse' (mounted police, mounted troops), while 'equestrian' names the whole relationship to riding and horses, not just being seated on one.
  • Cavalry: Similar because both carry the military sense of horse-riding soldiers; differs because 'cavalry' is the specific noun for the horse-soldier arm of an army, while 'equestrian' is the broad adjective of riders and riding and can describe cavalry culture.
  • Equine: Similar because both derive from the same Latin root equus ('horse') and both relate to horses; differs because 'equine' refers to the animal itself (an equine disease, the equine family), while 'equestrian' refers to the human activity of riding and to riders.
  • Cavalier: Similar because both descend from the horse-riding knightly world and both carry aristocratic colour; differs because 'cavalier' today means offhand, arrogant, or carelessly unconcerned in attitude, while 'equestrian' stays tied to actual riding and horses.
  • Equestrienne: Similar because it is the feminine form of the noun equestrian, meaning a female rider; differs because 'equestrienne' is specifically feminine and now dated, while 'equestrian' is the neutral modern noun and adjective.
  • Hippic: Similar because both relate to horses and riding; differs because 'hippic' (from Greek híppos, 'horse') is a rare, scholarly adjective for horse matters, while 'equestrian' is the common, everyday word.
  • Horsey / horsy: Similar because both describe an enthusiasm for or involvement with horses; differs because 'horsey' is informal and often suggests the club culture of riding (a horsey family, horsey people), while 'equestrian' is formal and institutional.
