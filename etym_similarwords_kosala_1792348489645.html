• Videha: Similar because both were prestigious ancient kingdoms of the eastern Gangetic plain linked by deep matrimonial ties in the epics (Rāma of Kosala marrying Sītā of Videha); differs because Videha lay east across the Sadānīrā (Gandak) River with its capital at Mithilā, celebrated in the Upaniṣads primarily for philosophical debates (under King Janaka and Sage Yājñavalkya) rather than the expansive military hegemony of the Solar Dynasty.
  • Magadha: Similar because both were among the paramount sixteen Mahājanapadas contending for imperial supremacy in northern India during the 6th–5th centuries BCE; differs because Magadha lay south of the Ganges with its fortress capitals at Rājagṛha and Pāṭaliputra, developing a ruthless, highly centralized military bureaucracy that ultimately conquered and annexed Kosala.
  • Kāśī: Similar because both were contiguous Gangetic monarchies possessing immense sacred religious prestige; differs because Kāśī was centered on the ancient holy city of Vārāṇasī along the Ganges and, after early periods of independence, was conquered by Kosala and governed as a wealthy dependent province.
  • Avanti: Similar because both were dominant monarchical powers during the lifetime of the Buddha; differs because Avanti was situated far to the southwest in the Malwa plateau (with twin capitals at Ujjayinī and Māhiṣmatī), controlling the western Arabian Sea trade routes rather than the agrarian Gangetic interior.
  • Aṅga: Similar because both were eastern realms intimately connected in the Rāmāyaṇa narrative (Sage Ṛśyaśṛṅga brings rain to Aṅga before officiating Kosala's sacrifice); differs because Aṅga lay furthest east around modern Bhagalpur and Munger along the lower Ganges, serving primarily as a maritime riverine trading hub.
  • Pāñcāla: Similar because both were venerable Aryan monarchies in northern India celebrated in the Vedic corpus; differs because Pāñcāla occupied the fertile Upper Gangetic Doab to the west between the Ganges and Yamuna, serving as the northern bastion of orthodox Kuru-Pāñcāla Vedic sacrificial ritualism.
  • Matsya: Similar because both were ancient Janapadas mentioned across the epics; differs because Matsya was located further west in the semi-arid Aravalli hills (modern Rajasthan), a smaller regional realm lacking imperial ambitions.
  • Vatsa: Similar because both were prosperous Gangetic kingdoms intimately connected by river trade; differs because Vatsa was centered on the commercial entrepôt of Kauśāmbī at the confluence of the Ganges and Yamuna, governed by King Udayana and renowned for urban trade rather than vast agrarian plains.
