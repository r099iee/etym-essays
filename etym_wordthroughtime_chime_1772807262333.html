- [Bell and its sound] - "Chime" enters English naming the sound or set of bells of a clock or church, and a single bell of a set struck to mark time.
- [Harmonious agreement] - The verb extends figuratively to "be in harmony with" ("chime with") and to joining a conversation ("chime in"), carrying the sense of sounds fitting together.
- [Domestic and warning signals] - Modern usage keeps both the musical instrument (wind chimes, clock chime) and the sharp single warning strike, where a chime sits between music and alarm — clear enough to warn, musical enough to be civilised.
