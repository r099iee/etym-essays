• Socks: Similar because both are knitted coverings of the foot; differs because ‘socks’ are short, covering only the foot and ankle, are everyday and informal, and are the standard word for men, while ‘stockings’ are long and now largely feminine or historical.
  • Hose: Similar because ‘hose’ was the older word for leg coverings of which stockings were the lower part; differs because ‘hose’ survives mainly in compounds (fire-hose, hose-pipe) or in the archaic hosen, and was replaced by ‘stockings’ in the clothing sense.
  • Hosiery: Similar because both belong to the same branch of the clothing trade, the knitted legwear industry; differs because ‘hosiery’ is the collective trade term for socks, stockings, and tights, not the name of a single garment.
  • Tights: Similar because both cover the leg closely from the foot upward; differs because ‘tights’ also cover the trunk and hips (the word marks closeness of fit, from ‘tight’), whereas ‘stockings’ are separate legs reaching only to the thigh.
  • Pantyhose: Similar because both are sheer legwear worn by women; differs because ‘pantyhose’ (panties + hose) is one combined garment covering foot, legs, and hips, while ‘stockings’ are separate legs held up by suspenders — a genuinely different mechanics of wear.
  • Nylons: Similar because both are women’s stockings — ‘nylons’ were simply the synthetic stockings of the 1940s; differs because ‘nylons’ names the material (DuPont’s nylon) and is dated, nostalgic slang, whereas ‘stockings’ is the neutral garment word.
  • Leggings: Similar because both cover the leg closely; differs because ‘leggings’ are opaque, footless, casual leg-coverings worn over or instead of trousers, with no foot portion and with modern sporty connotations.
  • Knee-highs: Similar because both are knitted legwear reaching up the leg; differs because ‘knee-highs’ stop at or under the knee and are worn without suspenders — a practical modern compromise between sock and stocking.
