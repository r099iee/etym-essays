CHAIN 1 — biddan line (the "ask/command" sense of bid, the source of bids): - PIE *gʷʰedʰ- "to request, ask, pray, entreat" (verbal root; zero-grade *gʷʰdʰ- in some formations). - Proto-Germanic *bidjaną "to ask, request, pray, beg" (strong verb, class V; reconstructed, 1st millennium BCE). - Old English biddan "to ask, entreat, beseech, pray, demand" (attested 8th–11th c.; 3sg present bitt / bideþ, past bæd, past participle (ge)beden). - Middle English bidden "to ask, pray, command, invite" (11th–15th c.). - Modern English bid "to command, invite, ask" → third-person singular present bids "commands, asks, invites." CHAIN 2 — bēodan line (the "offer/announce" sense, source of the auction and card-game senses of bid): - PIE *bʰewdʰ- "to be awake, perceive; to announce, offer" (verbal root). - Proto-Germanic *beudaną "to offer, announce, command" (strong verb, class II; reconstructed, 1st millennium BCE). - Old English bēodan "to offer, proclaim, command, announce" (attested 8th–11th c.). - Middle English bēden "to offer, announce" (merged with bidden by the 14th c.). - Modern English bid "to offer a price" (auction) and noun bid; feeds the card-game sense "to declare one's tricks." NOTE ON DEBATE AND NUANCE: Old English distinguished two verbs — biddan "to ask, pray" (class V) and bēodan "to offer, proclaim" (class II) — but they converged in Middle English into a single verb "bid" whose forms and senses blend both lines. The "command" sense of biddan arose through politeness-driven social drift: a superior's "request" functioned as a command. The proposed Greek cognate of *gʷʰedʰ- is ποθέω (pothéō) "I yearn," a link some scholars accept and others dispute; and the root must not be confused with the separate PIE root *gʷʰed- "to seize.
