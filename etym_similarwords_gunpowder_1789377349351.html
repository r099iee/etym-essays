• Black powder: Similar because black powder is the precise technical, historical, and chemical synonym for traditional gunpowder; differs because black powder is the modern term utilized by ballisticians and pyrotechnicians to formally distinguish the traditional charcoal-sulfur-saltpeter formula from modern nitrocellulose propellants, whereas gunpowder remains the broader historical and cultural term.
  • Cordite: Similar because both function as explosive chemical propellants designed to launch military projectiles from gun barrels; differs because cordite is a modern smokeless propellant composed of nitrocellulose, nitroglycerin, and petroleum jelly invented in 1889, generating vastly higher pressures and minimal smoke compared to traditional gunpowder.
  • Propellant: Similar because both generate expanding gases to push a projectile through a barrel or launch an aerial rocket; differs because propellant is a broad functional category encompassing solid rocket fuels, liquid kerosene-oxygen mixes, compressed gases, and smokeless powders, whereas gunpowder specifically denotes the granular charcoal-nitrate compound.
  • Explosive: Similar because both release sudden energy, heat, and high-pressure gases upon ignition; differs because explosive is a comprehensive umbrella category that includes high explosives (such as TNT, dynamite, and RDX, which detonate via supersonic shockwaves), whereas gunpowder is a low explosive that deflagrates through rapid subsonic burning.
  • Flash powder: Similar because both are pyrotechnic mixtures that burn rapidly to produce light, sound, and gas; differs because flash powder utilizes finely powdered metallic fuels (such as magnesium or aluminum) combined with strong oxidizers like potassium perchlorate, creating a blinding flash and concussive boom without the controlled progressive burn rate required for firearm barrels.
  • Nitrocellulose: Similar because both serve as chemical propellant bases in firearms; differs because nitrocellulose (guncotton) is a chemically nitrated plant polymer that leaves virtually no solid soot or corrosive residue, completely supplanting traditional gunpowder in modern combat weaponry.
