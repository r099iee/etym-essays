- Medieval Romance (c. 12th c.) - Old Occitan 'estampida': a lively, rhythmic stamping dance; Spanish 'estampida': a sudden loud crash or detonation.
- 18th Century Mexican Frontier - Vaqueros use 'estampida' for the thunderous, earth-shaking panic rush of semi-wild longhorn cattle herds on the open range.
- 1840s Southwest Borderlands - Anglo-American cattlemen and explorers adopt the term as 'stampede' during the Texas-Mexican contact era and the Santa Fe Trail trade.
- Late 19th Century America - Figurative expansion: applied by urban journalists to political convention delegate bandwagons, financial market panics, and human crowd disasters.
- 20th Century to Present - Western heritage branding (e.g., Calgary Stampede) alongside universal global usage for mass crowd dynamics and collective psychological contagions.
