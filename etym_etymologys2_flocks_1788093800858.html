↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*flukka-
"crowd, troop, band"
↓
Old Norse (9th–13th c.)
flokkr
"crowd, troop, band; batch
of verses, poem"
↓
Old English (c. 1000, one attestation)
flocc
"company, troop, band"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
flok
"herd of sheep; company of
people; flock of birds"
↓
Modern English (present)
flocks
"groups of sheep, goats,
birds, or people"

Note: no secure PIE etymon can be reconstructed for this word — the OED judges the origin obscure, and the Germanic set (Old Norse flokkr, Middle Dutch and Middle Low German vlocke) is the earliest firm evidence. The Old English form flocc survives in only a single isolated attestation, and the Middle English flok was very likely reinforced or re-borrowed from Old Norse flokkr in the Danelaw — the Old English word may have died out and been revived in the Scandinavian sense. The frequently cited link to Latin floccus “tuft, lock of wool” (and thence to PIE *plek- “to plait”, via a proposed bridge “tuft → cluster → gathering”) is speculative and disputed: the phonology is awkward, and the resemblance may be accidental.
