- [1590s: the first stream] - Sputter appears, first for particles bursting out with explosive sound, then at once for rapid, indistinct speech — the mouth spitting syllables.
- [1704: the word is born] - Splutter is coined as a blend of splash and sputter, fusing wet explosion with vocal disorder; a spluttering speaker's words spray out like droplets.
- [18th-19th century: from mouth to machine] - The word extends to failing flames and dying engines, and in public speech it names a losing orator — comic, contemptuous, composure collapsing.
- [Contemporary sense] - Spluttered still carries its wet, explosive, slightly comic charge: rage that spatters from the mouth, an engine that spits and dies — Gollum's voice breaking apart in The Hobbit.
