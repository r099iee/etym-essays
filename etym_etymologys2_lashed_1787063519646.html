NOTE — DEBATED / NUANCED STAGES: (1) The ultimate origin of Latin laqueus beyond the rare verb lacere "to entice, ensnare" is unknown — no secure Indo-European ancestor is agreed; this stage is simply not attested and is stated as such, not guessed. (2) The striking verb lashen (14th c.) has no securely identified ancestor in Latin or Germanic; the OED and Etymonline mark its origin "uncertain", with the standard hypothesis that it is imitative of a whip's crack (a member of the sound-symbolic whack/thwack/slash family). The noun lash "whip-thong" (early 14th c.) appears at the same period, and it is debated whether the noun came from the verb or the verb from the noun; the safest reading is that the whole cluster arose together as sound-symbolic vocabulary for the whip. No stages are invented.
