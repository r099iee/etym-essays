• Gleam: Similar because both name light from a surface; differs because gleam is soft, steady, and often benign, whereas glint is a hard, momentary, reflected spark.
  • Glitter: Similar because both involve light striking a shiny surface; differs because glitter is a scattered series of many flashes, while glint is a single brief point of light.
  • Glimmer: Similar because both describe a small, faint light; differs because a glimmer is unsteady and may be self-emitted or diffuse, while a glint is a sharp, precise reflected flash.
  • Sparkle: Similar because both are small flashes of light; differs because sparkle is lively, festive, and fragmented, while glint is cool, metallic, and momentary.
  • Shimmer: Similar because both name light on a surface; differs because shimmer is a soft trembling sheen, while glint is a single hard point of light.
  • Flash: Similar because both are sudden bursts of light; differs because a flash is larger and more sustained, while a glint is the smallest, hardest kind of flash.
  • Twinkle: Similar because both are small, quick lights; differs because twinkle is rhythmic and usually warm (stars, eyes), while glint is cold and metallic.
  • Glint (in the eye): Similar because it shares the word's flash of light; differs because it is a figurative transfer to the gaze, reading intention, whereas the literal glint is physical light on a surface.
  • Glisten: Similar because both describe light on a surface; differs because glisten suggests a moist or wet surface shining continuously, while glint is a dry, momentary flash.
  • Sheen: Similar because both involve light on a polished surface; differs because sheen is a broad, steady lustre, while glint is a single sharp spark.
