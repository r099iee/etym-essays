• The biographical method: Similar because it denotes his critical approach and is inseparable from his name; differs because it is the abstracted practice, while Sainte-Beuve is the specific historical practitioner whose name warrants it.
  • New Critics: Similar because they are influential critics of literature; differs because they bracket biography and focus on the text, while Sainte-Beuve made life and character central to understanding the work.
  • Proust: Similar because his Contre Sainte-Beuve defines the method's fame and fate; differs because Proust is the antagonist who rejected biographical criticism, not a practitioner of it.
  • Mere reviewer: Similar because both write about books; differs because a reviewer produces ephemeral notices, while Sainte-Beuve aimed at permanent literary-historical portraiture.
  • Moralist-critic: Similar because both sit in judgement on writers; differs because Sainte-Beuve privileged taste, temperament, and the living texture of books over stern moralism.
  • Literary theorist: Similar because both theorize about literature; differs because the theorist builds systems of genres and poetics, while Sainte-Beuve practiced intimate, biographical portraiture without a system.
  • Belletrist / homme de lettres: Similar because both describe a cultivated writer of elegant prose; differs because belletrist is a generic label for elegant essayists, while Sainte-Beuve is a specific critic whose name anchors a method.
  • Impressionistic criticism: Similar because it names the kind of taste-led criticism he practiced; differs because it is the abstract style, while Sainte-Beuve is the named historical model for it in Pater's age.
  • Sainte-Beuvean portrait: Similar because it derives from the name; differs because it names the specific genre of the literary portrait he perfected, not the man or the whole method.
