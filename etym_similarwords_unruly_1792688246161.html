• disobedient: Similar because both describe a refusal to comply with authority; differs because disobedient focuses narrowly on the conscious violation of a direct order or command, whereas unruly suggests a broader, wilder state of continuous turbulence, chaos, and lack of internal or external restraint.
  • refractory: Similar because both denote resistance to control; differs because refractory is a formal, Latinate term often applied in medicine (diseases resistant to treatment) or materials science (substances resistant to heat), implying stubborn, unresponsive obstinacy rather than boisterous wildness.
  • insubordinate: Similar because both describe defiance of superiors; differs because insubordinate belongs strictly to hierarchical, military, or organizational contexts where a subordinate actively defies rank or lawful command, lacking the informal, domestic, or emotional senses of unruly.
  • turbulent: Similar because both evoke disorder and lack of calm; differs because turbulent emphasizes violent, agitated physical or emotional motion (like a stormy sea or boiling fluid), whereas unruly specifically points to the defiance of rules, discipline, or boundaries.
  • recalcitrant: Similar because both involve stubborn defiance; differs because recalcitrant etymologically means "kicking back like a mule" (Latin recalcitrare), emphasizing bitter, sullen resistance to authority or advice, whereas unruly often carries active, noisy, rambunctious energy.
  • ungovernable: Similar because both denote an inability to be kept under control; differs because ungovernable is an absolute, high-stakes political or philosophical descriptor for nations, passions, or mobs that cannot be steered by any sovereign, whereas unruly can scale down gracefully to a child's tangled hair or noisy play.
  • rowdy: Similar because both describe noisy, disorderly behavior; differs because rowdy is a colloquial, informal term specifically evoking rough, boorish, drunken, or tavern-style roughhousing, whereas unruly has an elevated literary and psychological pedigree.
  • fractious: Similar because both denote disruptive irritability; differs because fractious specifically highlights peevishness, petulance, quarrelsome whining, and bad temper (often seen in overtired children or divided political factions), whereas unruly is broader, focusing on uncontained wildness and nonconformity.
