Root word UNBEKNOWN, one linear derivation chain:
1. PIE *ǵneh₃- "to know, perceive, recognize" (prehistoric reconstruction; the great "know" root of the family, source of Latin (g)nōscō, Greek gignṓskō, Sanskrit jānāti).
2. Proto-Germanic *knēaną "to know" (reconstructed).
3. Old English cnāwan "to know, recognize, perceive" (attested from the 8th c.).
4. Old English becnāwan "to recognize, acknowledge" (be- intensifier + cnāwan; attested c. 1000).
5. Middle English beknowen, past participle beknown "known, acknowledged" (attested 14th c.).
6. un- + beknown → English unbeknown "not known" (privative un-; first recorded 1630s).
7. English UNBEKNOWN "without the knowledge of (used with 'to')" (modern form; the variant unbeknownst, with excrescent -st on the model of amongst/against, is recorded c. 1848).

Note on the debate: the -st in unbeknownst is not an etymological suffix but an excrescent addition on the analogy of amongst and against; it is a purely orthographic-phonetic development. The PIE root *ǵneh₃- is reconstructed; its many attested descendants include Latin nōscō (source of English cognition, recognize), Greek gignṓskō (gnosis, diagnosis), and Sanskrit jānāti, source of Hindi जानना (jānnā) "to know."
