• Slander: Similar because it is the spoken damaging of a reputation, the everyday legal term for the kind of injurious speech malign describes; differs because slander is a technical legal term for oral defamation, carrying the courtroom with it, while malign is the more literary and moral verb for spiteful utterance, not a term of art.
  • Libel: Similar because it names the damaging of a reputation by false words; differs because libel is strictly written or published defamation and a precise legal charge, whereas malign covers injurious speech of any medium and remains a general word.
  • Defame: Similar because it means to damage a reputation by false or injurious reports; differs because defame is the general, value-neutral term for harming a reputation, legal in flavour and silent about motive, while malign insists on the deliberate spite and "evil nature" behind the speech.
  • Vilify: Similar because it means to abuse with words; differs because vilify denotes violent, coarse, insulting abuse — louder and more obscene — while malign is quieter, more deliberate, and more poisonous.
  • Disparage: Similar because it means to speak ill of; differs because disparage means to belittle, to speak of as of little worth — it diminishes rather than poisons — while malign actively seeks to injure a reputation.
  • Denigrate: Similar because it means to blacken a reputation by casting aspersions; differs because denigrate (from Latin niger, "black") is more modern and general and often implies aspersion rather than outright falsehood, while malign carries the strong sense of deliberate, even innate, malice.
  • Calumniate: Similar because it means to make false accusations; differs because calumniate is the formal, literary word for slandering by false statements, rarer and more Latinate than malign, which need not be restricted to outright falsehood.
  • Traduce: Similar because it means to misrepresent so as to damage; differs because traduce (Latin traducere, "to lead across") emphasises misrepresentation and betrayal, while malign emphasises the evil intention of the speaker.
  • Asperse: Similar because it means to attack a reputation with damaging remarks; differs because asperse (from Latin spargere, "to scatter") suggests sprinkling a reputation with little defamatory hints, while malign is the open, deliberate attack.
  • Smear: Similar because it means to defame, especially by a campaign of false accusations; differs because smear is colloquial and modern, belonging to politics and media, while malign is literary, moral, and old.
  • Besmirch: Similar because it means to soil a reputation; differs because besmirch is vivid and physical ("to dirty") and is used of honour or reputation, while malign is the act of speaking evil rather than merely staining.
  • Revile: Similar because it means to abuse with angry words; differs because revile stresses abuse hurled directly at someone with anger, whereas malign is a cooler, more calculated defamation aimed at a third party.
