• slanted: Similar because both involve letterforms or structures that lean at an angle from the vertical baseline; differs because slanted is a purely geometric description of angle (or metaphorically biased), whereas italicized specifies a formal typographic style historically derived from cursive calligraphy, featuring distinct calligraphic glyph shapes rather than merely tilted geometry.
  • oblique: Similar because both refer to typography angled to the right; differs because in professional typography, oblique denotes a mechanically tilted or artificially angled version of an existing Roman typeface (common in sans-serif fonts), whereas italicized implies a distinct, authentically designed cursive font with unique serifs and strokes.
  • cursive: Similar because both feature flowing letterforms connected or styled in the manner of rapid handwriting; differs because cursive primarily describes joined-up handwriting or calligraphy executed by hand, whereas italicized refers specifically to movable type or digital typesetting.
  • emphasized: Similar because italic type is widely employed as a visual mechanism for creating prominence and vocal stress; differs because emphasized is a broad functional and cognitive goal that can be achieved via bolding, underlining, vocal intonation, or repetition, whereas italicized is a specific typographic medium.
  • underlined: Similar because both are traditional methods used in manuscripts and typescript to signal emphasis or long-form titles; differs because underlined places a literal horizontal bar beneath the characters (historically used on typewriters that lacked italic slugs), while italicized re-typesets the letterforms into an angled cursive design.
  • underscored: Similar because both serve to heighten visual attention and assert importance; differs because underscored literally means underlined or metaphorically stressed, whereas italicized refers to typeface orientation and humanist aesthetic.
  • emboldened: Similar because both are typographic weight and style treatments used to draw reader attention; differs because emboldened (bolded) increases stroke thickness and optical weight for heavy, striking contrast, whereas italicized achieves contrast through slant and calligraphic grace without darkening the overall page density.
  • highlighted: Similar because both visually isolate and call attention to target passages; differs because highlighted typically involves an overlaid background color (such as yellow fluorescent wash in digital or physical text) or broad conceptual foregrounding, whereas italicized alters the typographic font glyphs themselves.
