• Thou: Similar because both are archaic second-person singular pronouns of the same familiar "T" set; differs because thou is the subject or nominative form ("thou art"), whereas thee is the object or oblique form ("I see thee").
  • Thy: Similar because it belongs to the same archaic second-person-singular paradigm and shares the same ancestry; differs because thy is the possessive adjective ("thy book"), which modifies a noun, never an object of a verb or preposition.
  • Thine: Similar because it is the possessive counterpart of the same set and also survives as an archaism; differs because thine appears before vowel-initial words and as a standalone possessive ("thine eyes," "it is thine"), while thee is purely objective.
  • Ye: Similar because both are archaic second-person pronouns; differs because ye is the plural (or formal) subject form ("ye are the salt of the earth"), whereas thee is the singular object form.
  • You: Similar because both function as the second-person object pronoun; differs because you is the modern, unmarked universal pronoun for singular and plural, subject and object alike, carrying none of the archaic intimacy, solemnity, or dialect colour of thee.
  • Thyself: Similar because it is built on the same objective stem and shares the archaic register; differs because it is the reflexive or emphatic form ("know thyself"), a compound of thee plus self, not a bare object pronoun.
