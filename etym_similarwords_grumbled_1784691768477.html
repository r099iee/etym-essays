• Muttered: Similar because both are low, indistinct speech; differs because muttering need not be complaint, while grumbled is specifically low-voiced discontent with a rumbling quality.
  • Complained: Similar because both voice a grievance; differs because complaining states it openly and to someone in words, while grumbling is a half-suppressed rumble, often addressed to no one.
  • Groused: Similar because both mean grumbling persistently; differs because grouse, echoing the bird's call, is a 19th-century word for complaining in a habitually sour way.
  • Whined: Similar because both voice displeasure; differs because whining is a thin, drawn-out, pleading complaint, whereas a grumble is deeper, rounder and more rumbling.
  • Moaned: Similar because both are low sounds of discontent; differs because moaning is a drawn-out lament of sorrow or pain, while grumbling is a repetitive rumble of peevishness.
  • Growled: Similar because both are low rumbling vocal sounds; differs because a growl is guttural, threatening and often animal, while a grumble is the subdued, half-humorous noise of a person complaining.
  • Rumbled: Similar because both are low rolling sounds; differs because rumbling is the bare noise of thunder, bellies or carts, carrying no complaint, while grumbled always suggests discontented speech or mood.
  • Grunted: Similar because both are short low sounds of dissatisfaction; differs because a grunt is a single brief sound, while a grumble is continuous and rolling.
  • Carped: Similar because both mean finding fault; differs because carping is nagging, petty and specific criticism of particulars, while grumbling is a general, rumbling, low-voiced discontent.
  • Bellyached: Similar because both are informal complaining; differs because bellyaching is vocal, drawn-out and querulous complaint, more theatrical than the quiet rumble of grumbling.
  • Griped: Similar because both express discontent; differs because griping is peevish, persistent complaining (from the intestinal ache), sharper and more specific than the low rumble of a grumble.
  • Grumped: Similar because both express ill humour; differs because grumping is a sulky, silent ill-temper (a grump is a mood), while grumbling keeps the audible rumbling sound of protest.
