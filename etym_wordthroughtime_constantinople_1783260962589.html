- Foundation in 330 - Constantinople, the city of Constantine, was refounded by Constantine the Great on the site of Byzantium and named in his honour, styled officially New Rome.
- The Byzantine millennium - for over a thousand years it was the imperial capital, seat of the Orthodox patriarchate, and wealthiest metropolis of the Mediterranean, and in Gibbon's phrase the rich and polite city where law, learning, and marvels flourished.
- The fall of 1453 - captured by the Ottomans under Mehmed II in 1453, the city passed into Ottoman hands while its name evolved into Istanbul, from the Greek phrase to the City.
- Contemporary sense - Constantinople now evokes the Byzantine millennium (330-1453) in scholarly and historical writing, while Istanbul is the modern Turkish name of the same living city.
