- Old Norse / Middle English onomatopoeic breath word - neutral sound imitation for whistling exhalation
- 14th-16th c. medical and general verb - to breathe hoarsely, horses and men wheeze; slightly low, rustic register
- 18th-19th c. clinical consolidation - wheeze becomes a diagnostic sign of asthma, bronchitis; doctors listen for wheezes
- Late 19th c. slang leap - a wheeze as a clever dodge or joke in British cant; playful, admiring tone
- 20th c. dual-track stability - medical wheeze (pathos, smoking, pollution, inhalers) alongside journalistic wheeze (clever wheeze, publicity wheeze)
- Contemporary - asthma-awareness makes wheeze instantly understood as breathlessness; wheezy also describes old machines, laughter and accordions with affection
