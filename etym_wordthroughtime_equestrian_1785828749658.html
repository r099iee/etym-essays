- Latin equester / equestris — Of a horseman or of the Roman equestrian order; social rank and cavalry identity fused with riding.
- Mid-17th c. English equestrian — Borrowed for horsemanship, riders, and mounted subjects in art and military description; classical prestige coloring.
- 18th c. equestrian — Art discourse solidifies "equestrian statue"; genteel riding culture uses the adjective for accomplishments of the saddle.
- 19th c. equestrian — Circus riders, public spectacles, and organized horse sports expand popular recognition; noun "an equestrian" = skilled rider; equestrienne for women performers.
- 20th c. equestrian — Olympic program and national federations make equestrian a standard sports category; art-historical sense remains textbook vocabulary.
- 21st c. equestrian — Gender-neutral rider noun common; still formal/technical beside casual "horse riding"; strong collocations with statue, center, events, sports.
