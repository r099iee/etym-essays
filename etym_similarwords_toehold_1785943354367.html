• Foothold: Similar because both denote a secure position from which to advance; differs because a foothold takes the whole foot and is firmer and larger, while a toehold takes only the toes and is minimal and precarious.
  • Footing: Similar because both involve a stable base for the feet; differs because footing is continuous, stable contact with the ground, while a toehold is a single small grip point.
  • Handhold: Similar because both are climbing grips; differs because a handhold is for the hands (a ledge or grip grasped by the fingers), while a toehold is for the toes.
  • Grip / hold: Similar because both mean a place to grasp; differs because grip and hold are general and can be for hands or feet, while a toehold is specifically for the toes.
  • Purchase: Similar because both mean a secure hold or leverage; differs because purchase is the friction or grip itself (the quality of holding), while a toehold is a specific position.
  • Leverage: Similar because both describe a small point that yields advantage; differs because leverage is the mechanical or strategic force gained, while a toehold is the physical or market position itself.
  • Beachhead: Similar because both describe an initial entry position; differs because a beachhead is a large, heavily defended landing area secured by invasion forces, not a minimal grip.
  • Bridgehead: Similar because both describe a forward position in hostile territory; differs because a bridgehead is specifically the ground at the end of a bridge (or its tactical equivalent) and is larger and more organised than a toehold.
  • Stepping-stone: Similar because both are means to further progress; differs because a stepping-stone is a temporary stage used to cross over to something beyond, while a toehold is a position held and built upon.
  • Inroad: Similar because both describe a way in; differs because an inroad is an incursion or penetration already made, while a toehold is the initial grip before the inroad.
  • Entrance / entry point: Similar because both describe a way in; differs because an entrance is a neutral doorway into a place or market, while a toehold implies a hard-won, precarious first grip.
  • Presence (business): Similar because both mean an established position in a market; differs because presence is vague and neutral about size, while a toehold emphasises the small, beginning nature of the position.
  • Edge / wedge: Similar because both describe a thin, initial opening; differs because an edge is a marginal advantage and a wedge is an opening forced between parts, while a toehold is a place from which more ground is taken.
  • Lodgement: Similar because both denote a position secured in enemy or new territory; differs because a lodgement is a military foothold large enough to be held, while a toehold is the smallest possible first grip.
