- Channel of water - First a noun: gutter was the channel that carried water off a roof or down a street - a little trench for running liquid.
- A candle's dying - From that channel-image came the verb: a candle gutters when its flame sputters and the melted wax runs down its sides in little channels, burning low and fitfully as it nears its end.
- Figurative burnout - The verb then lent itself to human life: passions, love, and days gutter like candle-ends - dying unevenly, strangled by their own residue, leaving only channels of congealed memory.
- Modern split life - Today the noun leans toward the degraded (the gutter press, life in the gutter), while the verb survives almost only in literary flame-and-life imagery - a word for slow, messy, self-consuming extinction.
