• Vase: Similar because both are hollow, decorative ceramic, glass, or stone vessels; differs because a vase is primarily designed to hold cut flowers or serve as an open ornamental display, whereas an urn typically possesses a narrower neck, a lid, a pedestal foot, and solemn mortuary or beverage-dispensing associations.
  • Amphora: Similar because both are classical ancient vessels with swelling bodies and narrow necks; differs because an amphora specifically features two vertical handles and was historically used for the commercial storage and maritime transport of wine, oil, and grain.
  • Chalice: Similar because both are footed, dignified vessels with ceremonial or sacred connotations; differs because a chalice is an open goblet specifically intended for drinking ceremonial wine or consecrated fluids during religious rituals (such as the Eucharist).
  • Samovar: Similar because both are large heated metal vessels used for boiling water and serving tea; differs because a samovar is an ornate, specifically Eastern European and Russian appliance featuring a central vertical chimney pipe for burning charcoal or embers.
  • Casket: Similar because both serve as final receptacles for human remains; differs because a casket is a rectangular burial box for an uncremated body (or a small chest for jewels), whereas an urn is a rounded vase dedicated specifically to cremated ashes.
  • Reliquary: Similar because both are sacred vessels containing physical remnants of the deceased; differs because a reliquary specifically houses sacred religious relics of saints or holy figures for veneration, whereas an urn holds cremains for memorial preservation.
