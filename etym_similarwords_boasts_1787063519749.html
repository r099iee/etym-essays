• Brag: Similar because a proud statement of one's own achievements; differs because "brag" is almost always pejorative and suggests empty talk repeated for effect, while "boast" can be neutral or even admiring ("a proud boast") and also works as a verb of possession ("the town boasts...").
  • Vaunt: Similar because display of one's merits with showy pride; differs because "vaunt" is formal, archaic-sounding, and mostly literary, while "boast" is the everyday word.
  • Crow: Similar because noisily boasting; differs because "crow" (as a verb) suggests gloating over a rival's failure and is usually confined to spoken idiom.
  • Swagger: Similar because self-assertive display of superiority; differs because "swagger" is primarily a manner of walking or behaving, whereas "boast" is verbal.
  • Bravado: Similar because a show of boldness and self-confidence; differs because "bravado" is a display intended to mask fear, while a boast is the claim itself, which may or may not be genuine.
  • Claim: Similar because a statement asserting something; differs because a "claim" is neutral and can be true, modest, or legal, with no necessary element of pride.
  • Bluster: Similar because loud, aggressive self-assertion; differs because "bluster" stresses the noisy, threatening manner (literally "stormy wind"), while "boast" stresses the content of the proud statement.
  • Pride: Similar because both relate to a high opinion of oneself; differs because "pride" is a feeling or quality, not a spoken statement, and can be positive (dignity, self-respect).
  • Rodomontade: Similar because inflated, vain boasting; differs because it is a learned, literary word for boastful bombast, nearly always contemptuous and rarely used in speech.
