• Bristol: Similar because both are historic colonial seaport towns in Bristol County, Rhode Island, bordering Narragansett Bay; differs because Bristol is larger, served historically as the county seat, and is world-renowned for hosting the oldest continuous Fourth of July celebration in the United States, whereas Warren is celebrated specifically as the founding birthplace of Brown University and possesses a more compact maritime-artisan core.
  • Barrington: Similar because both are adjacent East Bay Rhode Island communities carved out of the same colonial territory; differs because Barrington developed primarily into an affluent, tranquil suburban residential enclave, whereas Warren retained its dense, historic 18th-century commercial working waterfront and manufacturing heritage.
  • Providence: Similar because both are key Rhode Island municipalities deeply tied to the history of Brown University; differs because Providence is the state capital and a bustling metropolitan urban center to which the college moved in 1770, while Warren is a small coastal town where the college was originally established in 1764.
  • Newport: Similar because both are colonial-era Rhode Island seaports boasting rich collections of 18th-century architecture and seafaring traditions; differs because Newport was a colossal colonial trading metropolis and later an internationally famous Gilded Age resort of massive stone mansions, whereas Warren remained a close-knit shipbuilding, whaling, and textile community.
  • Sowams: Similar because Sowams occupied the exact physical landscape where modern Warren now stands; differs because Sowams is the indigenous Wampanoag name for the regional headquarters of Massasoit and the Pokanoket people prior to King Philip's War, whereas Warren is the colonial and modern civic entity.
  • Tiverton: Similar because both are East Bay coastal towns bordering Rhode Island waters with colonial histories; differs because Tiverton lies further south on the Sakonnet River with expansive agricultural lands and coastal bluffs, whereas Warren is situated on the Warren and Palmer river estuaries.
  • Portsmouth: Similar because both were early colonial settlements tied to Rhode Island's founding dissidents; differs because Portsmouth is situated on Aquidneck Island and was founded by Anne Hutchinson in 1638, whereas Warren lies on the mainland East Bay and was incorporated a century later in 1747.
