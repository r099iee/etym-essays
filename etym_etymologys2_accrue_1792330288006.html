Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*ḱer- / *ḱreh₁-
"to grow, nourish, feed, increase"
[Source of Latin creare, crescere, Ceres; English cereal, crescent]
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*krez-ske-
"to begin to grow, come forth, increase"
[Inchoative verbal formation with suffix *-ske-]
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
crescere
"to grow, spring forth, multiply, enlarge"
[Inchoative root verb of biological and quantitative growth]
↓
Classical Latin Compound (c. 1st c. BCE)
accrēscere
"to grow larger, increase by addition, attach to"
[Prefix ad- "to, toward" + crescere "to grow"]
↓
Old / Middle French (c. 12th–14th c.)
accroistre (fem. past participle acreüe / acrue)
"to increase, augment; that which has grown, an increase"
[Noun use of participle in feudal property law for land accretions]
↓
Anglo-Norman / Late Middle English (c. 15th c.)
acrewen / accruen
"to fall to as an accession, accumulate as an advantage"
[Technical legal verb regarding reversionary rights and estates]
↓
Modern English (present)
accrue
"to accumulate, increase, or fall as a periodic right"
