- Classical Latin - desolatio a leaving alone, forsaking - from desolare to abandon; rare in classical prose, common in Christian Latin for the abandoned state of the soul or of ruined places
- 14th c. - Middle English desolacioun devastation, ruin, abandoned condition - via Old French; used in religious writing for the desolation of cities and of the spirit
- 16th-17th c. - desolation becomes a literary word of great emotional weight: the desolation of the land, the desolation of war; also a desolation = a devastated region
- 18th-19th c. - Romantic and Victorian writing adopts desolation for sublime ruined landscapes (ruins, moors, barren wastes); in The Hobbit, the Desolation of the Dragon is the blasted waste left by Smaug
- Modern - desolation keeps both concrete (devastated wasteland) and abstract (profound abandonment and loneliness) senses; it is a high-register literary word, rarely used in casual speech
