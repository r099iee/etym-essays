- [Vedic Samhitā Period (c. 1500–1000 BCE)] — the constituent roots *vāyu* (the personified cosmic blower, who drinks the first cup of Soma in Vedic sacrifice) and *sūnu* (the archaic Indo-European kinship term for a begotten male child) represented primary sacral and social realities — [perception: revered as the glorious sovereign breath of the universe and the sacred generational continuity of familial bloodlines]
- [Epic Itihāsa Age (c. 500–200 BCE; Rāmāyaṇa & Mahābhārata)] — coalesced into the epic patronymic formula *Vāyusūnu* (transliterated as *Vāyusunu*), alternating with *Mārutātmaja* and *Māruti* to celebrate Hanumān's miraculous aerial flights and Bhīma's devastating combat might — [perception: celebrated as an evocative heroic kenning conveying divine speed, righteous fury, unassailable physical strength, and celestial paternity]
- [Classical Sanskrit Kāvya & Purāṇic Era (c. 4th–10th c. CE)] — codified across the major Purāṇas (including the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* and *Vāyu Purāṇa*) and classical dramas as a solemn theological title, symbolizing the manifestation of the cosmic life-breath (*mukhya-prāṇa*) in human and heroic affairs — [perception: venerated as a noble theological title linking cosmic vitality with moral duty (*dharma*) and divine purpose]
- [Medieval Dvaita Vedānta Codification (13th c. CE)] — elevated to central theological prominence by philosopher-theologian Ācārya Madhva, who identified Hanumān, Bhīma, and himself as the three sacred historical avatars of Vāyu (*Vāyu-avatāra*), proclaiming the *Vāyusūnu* to be the supreme mediator between humanity and God — [perception: worshipped by the Dvaita tradition as the paramount soul (*mukhyaprāṇa*), essential for attaining divine grace and spiritual liberation]
- [Modern Liturgical & Devotional Era (19th–21st c.)] — cherished globally in Hindu households, classical music compositions, and vernacular folk traditions as a beloved, affectionate title of Hanumān, embodying boundless courage, selfless devotion, and spiritual refuge — [perception: universally honored as an ancient sacred name representing divine vitality, moral courage, and compassionate divine protection]
