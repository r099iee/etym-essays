• Feast: Similar because both denote a large, elaborate meal eaten in celebration of a festive occasion; differs because feast emphasizes massive abundance of hearty food and communal, joyful merrymaking (often with religious or traditional agrarian associations), whereas banquet specifies a formal, highly structured social event marked by ceremonial decorum, polite protocol, and prepared speeches.
  • Repast: Similar because both refer to an occasion of taking food or a meal; differs because repast is an elevated, literary term for any meal whatsoever—even a humble or solitary one—without any requirement for festive celebration, luxury, or guests, whereas banquet denotes a lavish, public, and collective ceremonial feast.
  • Symposium: Similar because both trace their cultural lineage to Plato and classical Greek banqueting traditions that unite dining with philosophical conversation; differs because symposium has evolved primarily into an academic conference or intellectual colloquium focused on scholarly presentations and panel debates where actual dining is incidental or absent, whereas banquet remains fundamentally centered upon a lavish, formal meal.
  • Gala: Similar because both designate festive, high-status celebratory social gatherings honoring an achievement or milestone; differs because gala focuses on glamorous entertainment, musical performances, fancy attire, and fundraising pageantry where dining is merely one component of a wider stage spectacle, whereas banquet places the multi-course ceremonial dinner and dinner toasts at the very heart of the event.
  • Spread: Similar because both describe an abundant and visually appealing presentation of various foods for multiple diners; differs because spread is an informal, colloquial term evoking a generous, casual buffet or picnic array laid out across a table, whereas banquet represents a prestigious, formal dining event governed by high culinary etiquette.
  • Collation: Similar because both historically trace back to light between-meal refreshments eaten informally; differs because collation denotes a light, modest, often cold meal or informal snack served between regular meal hours (especially in monastic traditions during fasting seasons), whereas modern banquet represents a heavy, opulent, multi-course feast.
