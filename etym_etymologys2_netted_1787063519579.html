Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*ned-
"to tie, bind, knot together"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*natją
"open-meshed woven fabric, snare, net"
↓
Old English (c. 8th c. CE)
nett
"net, snare for fish, birds, animals"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c. CE)
netten
"to trap in a net; weave mesh; clear profit"
↓
Modern English (16th c. CE–present)
netted
"caught in a net; captured; secured profit"

Note: The physical entrapment sense descends from PIE *ned- ("to bind") via Germanic *natją. The financial accounting sense ("net profit") developed by convergence with French net ("clean, pure", from Latin nitidus).
