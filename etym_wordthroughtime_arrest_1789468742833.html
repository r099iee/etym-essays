- Late 14th Century - Mechanical stoppage and legal apprehension; entered Middle English through Anglo-Norman legal codices and chivalric literature to signify capturing an outlaw or bringing an advancing armed force to a standstill.
- 16th–17th Century - Metaphorical expansion to human perception and passion; used in Renaissance drama and metaphysical poetry to describe seizing attention, captivating the gaze, or checking the violent motions of the heart.
- 18th–19th Century - Medical and botanical formalization; adopted in clinical pathology to describe the cessation of vital biological functions (e.g. cardiac arrest, respiratory arrest) or the premature halt of organic plant development.
- 20th–21st Century - Institutional predominance; heavily cemented in popular culture and legal procedural language (Miranda warnings, police procedure), while retaining a high-register aesthetic sense in literary discourse (e.g. an arresting portrait).
