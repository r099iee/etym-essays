• Amiens: Similar because both are world-famous French High Gothic cathedrals recognized for immense scale, structural brilliance, and UNESCO World Heritage status; differs because Amiens is renowned for having the largest interior volume and monumental stone nave, whereas Chartres is celebrated for having the most complete, intact ensemble of original thirteenth-century stained glass.
  • Reims: Similar because both are towering 13th-century masterpieces of French High Gothic architecture with monumental sculpted portals; differs because Reims served historically as the coronation site of the Kings of France, whereas Chartres was primarily a major Marian pilgrimage center and home to the renowned 12th-century philosophical School of Chartres.
  • Bourges: Similar because both are contemporary High Gothic cathedrals built in central France following the late 12th-century fires; differs because Bourges features a unique five-aisled continuous space without a transept, whereas Chartres features a dramatic wide Latin-cross transept and soaring asymmetrical spires.
  • Beauvais: Similar because both represent the audacious heights of medieval French Gothic engineering; differs because Beauvais pushed vaulting to extreme limits resulting in catastrophic structural collapses and remains an unfinished choir, whereas Chartres stands fully preserved and structurally harmonious.
  • Illiers: Similar because both are historic towns in Eure-et-Loir fundamentally woven into the geography of Proust's childhood; differs because Chartres is a majestic regional capital dominated by its world-famous cathedral, whereas Illiers is a small provincial market town that provided the domestic, intimate model for Combray.
