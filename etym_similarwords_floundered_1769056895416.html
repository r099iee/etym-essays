• struggle: Similar because it describes effort against resistance; differs because to struggle may be orderly, purposeful, and even successful, while to flounder is wild, uncoordinated, and often panicked, with no method in it at all.
  • stagger: Similar because it describes losing control of movement; differs because staggering concerns the legs on land — losing one's footing while walking — whereas floundering takes the whole body into a medium such as water or mud and implies splashing, flailing, and little forward progress.
  • stumble: Similar because it describes unsteady, awkward movement; differs because stumbling is a short trip or mis-step on a firm surface, while floundering is a prolonged, whole-body struggle against a resisting medium or against confusion.
  • wade: Similar because it describes moving through water; differs because wading is the deliberate, steady, controlled crossing of shallow water, while floundering is the clumsy, urgent, uncontrolled resistance of the same water.
  • wallow: Similar because it describes slow, heavy movement through mud or water; differs because wallowing is animalistic and sometimes even pleasurable or comfortable, while floundering is urgent, human, and miserable.
  • founder: Similar in sound and in describing failure; differs because to founder is to sink or fail utterly — a ship founders when it goes down, an enterprise when it collapses — while floundering is a helpless thrash that stops short of final destruction.
  • flail: Similar because it describes thrashing limbs; differs because flailing names the wild movement of arms and legs in any context, while floundering adds the sense of being trapped in a resisting medium or in difficulty, unable to get anywhere.
  • thrash: Similar because it describes violent, uncontrolled movement; differs because thrashing is a general violent lashing out, while floundering is the particular helpless struggle of a body (or a venture) mired in something that resists progress.
  • blunder: Similar because it describes clumsy, awkward action; differs because blundering is clumsy error usually through stupidity or carelessness, while floundering is helplessness born of panic or obstruction, without the implication of a stupid mistake.
  • falter: Similar because it describes a loss of composure or momentum; differs because faltering is a wavering of confidence, voice, or step, lighter and more momentary, while floundering is a sustained, disordered struggle to make progress at all.
  • muddle: Similar because it describes confused, disordered effort; differs because muddling is bungling or making a mess of something, often quietly, while floundering pictures a violent, desperate, visible struggle against difficulty.
