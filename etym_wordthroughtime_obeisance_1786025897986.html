- Middle English (14th c.) - [Abstract Feudal Obligation & Religious Duty] - Denoted the sworn legal status, moral duty, or jurisdictional sphere of obeying a lord, monarch, or church authority with solemn gravity.
- Late Medieval to Early Modern (15th–16th c.) - [Transition to Physical Bodily Movement] - Shifted from the abstract legal status to the tangible bodily action (bow, curtsy, prostration) performed to demonstrate that submission.
- Elizabethan to Jacobean Era (late 16th–17th c.) - [Aristocratic Court Protocol & Ceremonial Pomp] - Codified as essential courtly manners, describing elaborate bows, sweeping salutations, and theatrical gestures before royalty.
- 18th–19th Century (Enlightenment to Victorian) - [Literary Tribute & Polite Social Deference] - Broadened into a versatile metaphor for intellectual homage, artistic respect to classical masters, and refined social courtesies.
- 20th–21st Century (Contemporary) - [Archaic Ceremony & Critical Metaphor for Sycophancy] - Preserved in formal ceremonial, historical, or fantasy registers, or deployed critically to expose servile compliance to money and power.
