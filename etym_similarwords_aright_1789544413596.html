• Correctly: Similar because both express exact conformity to factual truth, logic, or established standard; differs because correctly is the standard, neutral modern adverb for all contexts, whereas aright possesses an elevated, archaic, literary, or moral tone.
  • Properly: Similar because both convey adherence to appropriate standards or rules; differs because properly emphasizes social decorum, mechanical procedure, or fitting propriety, while aright focuses on perceptual accuracy and moral correctness.
  • Accurately: Similar because both denote precision without error; differs because accurately relates to exact quantitative measurement, factual data, and scientific precision, whereas aright relates to qualitative understanding and recollection.
  • Rightly: Similar because both share the same Germanic root denoting justice and correctness; differs because rightly is common in modern syntax to justify actions or judgments ("he rightly refused"), whereas aright functions almost exclusively postpositively after verbs like judge, remember, hear, or understand.
  • Truly: Similar because both denote genuine alignment with reality; differs because truly emphasizes sincerity, fidelity, and authentic existence rather than procedural or factual correctness.
  • Faultlessly: Similar because both describe performance without defect; differs because faultlessly denotes immaculate technical execution, while aright denotes sound discernment.
  • Justly: Similar because both reflect moral and legal rectitude; differs because justly specifically emphasizes equity, fairness, and legal justice.
