“Laburnum” entered English from Latin as the botanical name of the golden chain tree, its Latin name carried straight into the garden vocabulary of the 16th century.
“Laburnum” through the centuries stayed the formal, slightly scientific word for the small tree with hanging racemes of bright yellow pea-flowers — the golden chain of late spring.
“Laburnum” in the 19th century was a fashionable garden tree of the villa and the shrubbery, its yellow cascades a standard of English spring gardens, even as its seeds were known to be poisonous.
“Laburnums” today still names the golden-chain trees — and in fantasy the word becomes the shape of hanging firework trails, laburnums of fire that hang in the twilight, the tree’s drooping chains rewritten as sparks.
