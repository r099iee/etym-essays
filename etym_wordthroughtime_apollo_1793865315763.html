- Archaic & Classical Greece (c. 8th–4th c. BCE) - Feared and revered Olympian divinity of plagues, archery, ritual purification, choral song, and oracular truth; master of the Delphic oracle and supreme arbiter of moral boundaries and cosmic order.
- Hellenistic & Roman Antiquity (c. 3rd c. BCE–4th c. CE) - Syncretized with Helios as the radiant solar deity; embraced as the patron god of Augustus Caesar's Golden Age, symbolizing imperial peace, poetry, civil order, and radiant light.
- Renaissance & Neoclassicism (c. 1400–1800) - Celebrated by European humanists as the supreme aesthetic ideal of male physical beauty, mathematical proportion, and poetic genius, epitomized by the celebrated marble sculpture Apollo Belvedere.
- Late 19th Century (Continental Philosophy & Aesthetics) - Conceptualized by Friedrich Nietzsche in 'The Birth of Tragedy' as the 'Apollonian' principle — representing order, clarity, measure, individuation, and rational form in dialectical tension with Dionysian chaos.
- 20th–21st Century (Scientific Eponymy & Colloquial Archetype) - Immortalized in NASA's Apollo lunar space missions as the ultimate emblem of technological triumph and celestial exploration, while remaining a ubiquitous English noun for an extraordinarily handsome youth.
