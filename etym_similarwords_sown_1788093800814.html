• planted: Similar because both place seeds or young growth in soil for development; differs because planted is much broader — it covers seedlings, bulbs, trees and even abstract things set deliberately in place, whereas sown specifically means broadcasting seed and keeps its agricultural core.
  • seeded: Similar because both mean scattering seed; differs because seeded is often technical or neutral (sports brackets, seeded fruit) and can be intransitive (a plant seeds itself), whereas sown always implies deliberate dispersal by an agent.
  • scattered: Similar because both mean thrown or spread about; differs because scattered is generic and directionless, while sown implies purposeful distribution with future growth intended.
  • strewn: Similar because both mean spread over a surface; differs because strewn suggests disorder or randomness with no growth in mind, while sown implies methodical, purpose-built placement.
  • broadcast (verb): Similar because broadcasting seed is a specific method of sowing by scattering; differs because broadcast is modern jargon (agricultural machinery, then radio/television) and lacks the organic "take root and grow" connotation.
  • disseminated: Similar because both mean spread widely, especially of ideas and information; differs because disseminated is formal, abstract and used of data, news or doctrines rather than seed.
  • propagated: Similar because both imply causing spread or growth; differs because propagated is scientific and impersonal (asexual reproduction, viral ideas) and does not presuppose a planting metaphor.
  • sowed: Similar because it is simply the alternative past participle of the same verb; differs because sowed is the newer weak form favoured in speech and American English, while sown is the older strong form, more formal and literary.
