- 17th c., adopted from Dutch 'aanslag' (assault, onset): a violent onset or fierce attack, chiefly of troops or weapons. Connotation: military, deliberate, pressing forward with crushing force.
- 18th–19th c.: the target widens from armies to storms and the elements — an onslaught of wind, rain, or fire. Connotation: still violent, now impersonal and natural rather than human.
- 20th–21st c.: fully metaphorical use — an onslaught of criticism, advertising, email, or paperwork. Connotation: overwhelming weight and inevitability; the aggressor may be anything at all, and the victim is merely swept along.
- Modern register: always heavy and overwhelming; unlike 'attack' it insists on mass and momentum rather than a single blow.
