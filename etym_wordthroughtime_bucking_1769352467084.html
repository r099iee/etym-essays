- Old English: 'buck' named the male deer or goat - a plain, concrete animal term with no dynamic sense
- 19th century: the verb 'to buck' described a horse or bronco springing up with arched back and kicking hind legs - a wild, roguish, western frontier sense of a dangerous animal
- 20th century: extended figuratively to resisting or defying something (bucking the trend, bucking authority) - energetic, rebellious, and defiant
- Contemporary sense: rodeo and equestrian vocabulary for a violent vertical leap, plus a vivid metaphor for stubborn opposition - playful, spirited, and charged with untamed energy
