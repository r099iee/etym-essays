- Vedic & Late Vedic Period (c. 600–200 BCE) — Emerged from archaic prose Dharmasūtras composed by Vedic schools (Āpastamba, Gautama) to guide ritualists and householders in ancestral customary norms.
- Classical Era (c. 200 BCE–400 CE) — Crystallized into monumental metrical verse treatises (the Mānava-Dharmaśāstra or Manusmṛti, followed by Yājñavalkya), transforming localized priestly aphorisms into a universal pan-Indian legal and social constitution.
- Early Medieval to Classical Islamic Era (c. 700–1600 CE) — Flourished through voluminous scholarly commentaries (bhāṣyas) and encyclopedic legal digests (nibandhas) by scholars like Medhātithi, Vijñāneśvara (author of the Mitākṣarā inheritance system), and Jīmūtavāhana (the Dāyabhāga), dynamically adapting ancient texts to regional customary law.
- Colonial Era (late 18th–19th c.) — Translated by British orientalists (Sir William Jones's 1794 translation of Manu) and codified into rigid Anglo-Hindu Law, fundamentally reshaping Indian jurisprudence under colonial courts.
- Modern & Contemporary Era (20th–21st c.) — Subject of radical social critique by modern reformers (such as B.R. Ambedkar), while remaining an indispensable historical archive for global legal history, comparative ethics, and constitutional jurisprudence.
