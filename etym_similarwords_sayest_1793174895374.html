• Say: Similar because sayest is simply the thou-form of say, sharing every shade of utterance; differs because say is the living base verb for all persons, while sayest is frozen archaism usable only with thou.
  • Saith / sayeth: Similar because both are archaic inflections of the same verb, differing only in person; differs because saith pairs with he, she, it, while sayest pairs with thou, the familiar singular.
  • Speakest: Similar because both are thou-forms of verbs of utterance, marching together through Shakespeare; differs because speakest stresses the act of talking, while sayest stresses the content uttered, the thing said.
  • Tellest: Similar because both are second-person archaic forms used in dialogue and scripture; differs because tellest implies informing a listener or narrating, while sayest is the bare act of uttering words.
  • Utterest: Similar because both frame formal pronouncement in antique dress; differs because utterest is loftier and rarer, suggesting solemn declaration, while sayest is the common workhorse of thou-speech.
  • Deemest: Similar because both are thou-verbs of opinion found in old texts; differs because deemest means you judge or consider, an act of mind, while sayest means you utter, an act of mouth.
  • Quoth: Similar because both flavour dialogue with antique authority; differs because quoth is a fossil past tense used with any subject in jest, while sayest is strictly present tense with thou.
  • Statest: Similar because both introduce a person's claim or position; differs because statest is modern formal diction, while sayest is its time-travelling twin from four centuries back.
