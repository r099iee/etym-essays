• Spear: Similar because the shaft is the body of a spear and the words overlap in battle context; differs because spear names the whole weapon, point and all, while shaft isolates only the long wooden staff.
  • Pole: Similar because both are long slender objects; differs because a pole is any long piece, often rough or functional (a flagpole, a tent pole), while a shaft is deliberately shaped and used for a specific craft purpose.
  • Staff: Similar because both are long wooden supports and share the same PIE root; differs because a staff is a walking or ceremonial support, thick and blunt, while a shaft is slender and made for weapons, light, or machinery.
  • Rod: Similar because both are slender rigid cylinders; differs because a rod is usually a machine part, a cane, or a fishing implement, while a shaft carries the weapon, light-column, and passage senses.
  • Beam: Similar because both can be columns of light and long structural pieces; differs because a beam is broader and more massive (a building timber, a wide light), while a shaft is narrow, straight, and bounded.
  • Arrow: Similar because a shaft is the arrow's body; differs because arrow names the whole missile with its head and feathers, while shaft isolates the wooden staff.
  • Stick: Similar because both are pieces of wood; differs because a stick is found, casual, and unshaped, while a shaft is made — scraped and shaved for a purpose.
  • Column: Similar because both are long vertical forms, especially of light or architecture; differs because a column is thick, massive, and load-bearing, while a shaft is slender and bounded.
  • Ray: Similar because both concern light; differs because a ray is a direction or line of light without visible thickness, while a shaft of light has a visible, bounded width.
  • Stave: Similar because both are shaped rods or staffs and share Germanic ancestry; differs because a stave is a fragment, a barrel board, or a musical staff, without the weapon, light, and passage senses.
  • Bar: Similar because both are long rigid pieces; differs because a bar is solid, often rectangular, and used for support or obstruction, while a shaft is slender, shaped, and piercing.
