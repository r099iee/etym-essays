• Moral: Similar because both denote conformity to right and wrong; differs because moral is general and ungendered, without the prestige or the feminine-domestic package of virtuous.
  • Righteous: Similar because both praise moral excellence; differs because righteous implies self-conscious rectitude and public judgment, often sounding severe or self-righteous.
  • Good: Similar because both are everyday terms of ethical approval; differs because good is broad and plain, while virtuous is formal, high-toned, and historically gendered.
  • Upright: Similar because both stress integrity in dealings; differs because upright points to honesty and fairness without the association with female submission.
  • Honorable: Similar because both denote praiseworthy character; differs because honorable stresses reputation, fair dealing, and public respect rather than inner chastity.
  • Decent: Similar because both can mean morally acceptable; differs because decent is modest, informal, and lower in intensity than the full moral glow of virtuous.
  • Chaste: Similar because both can foreground sexual restraint; differs because chaste narrows virtue to bodily purity rather than the broad moral-and-domestic whole.
  • Pure: Similar because both can suggest moral and sexual cleanliness; differs because pure implies spotlessness and innocence, often detached from active moral achievement.
  • Innocent: Similar because both can describe the morally untainted; differs because innocent means free of guilt or worldly knowledge, not necessarily actively virtuous.
  • Principled: Similar because both denote adherence to moral standards; differs because principled emphasizes reasoned moral commitments rather than the older prestige of virtue.
  • Ethical: Similar because both belong to the vocabulary of right conduct; differs because ethical is the modern, neutral, professional term of moral philosophy, lacking the gendered history.
  • Exemplary: Similar because both can mean setting a model of good behavior; differs because exemplary emphasizes being a worthy example rather than possessing inner virtue.
  • Saintly: Similar because both suggest exceptional moral goodness; differs because saintly implies religious holiness of an extreme degree, beyond ordinary virtue.
  • Pious: Similar because both involve devotion to moral or religious duty; differs because pious stresses religious observance and can carry a hint of ostentation.
  • Dutiful: Similar because both can describe obedience to expected moral roles; differs because dutiful emphasizes compliance with duty, the very submission the maxim celebrates.
  • Proper: Similar because both can mean conforming to moral or social standards; differs because proper is chiefly about social correctness rather than deep moral character.
  • Wholesome: Similar because both connote moral healthfulness; differs because wholesome suggests simple, rustic goodness rather than disciplined excellence.
  • High-minded: Similar because both praise elevated moral ideals; differs because high-minded stresses lofty intentions, sometimes at the cost of practicality.
