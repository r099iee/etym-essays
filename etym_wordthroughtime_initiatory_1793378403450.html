- Early 17th c. — ecclesiastical rites and sacramental admission: enters Early Modern English directly from Late Latin, describing holy baptism and sacramental mysteries as the gateway into the Christian community.
- 17th to 18th c. — pedagogical rudiments and elementary philosophy: expands from religious sacraments to education and intellectual methods, describing preliminary lessons, foundational textbooks, and the basic steps of a discipline.
- 19th c. — Victorian criticism and fraternal orders: widely used by art theorists (like Ruskin in Modern Painters) for the rudimentary physical disciplines of drawing, alongside Masonic and esoteric fraternities describing secret induction ceremonies.
- Early 20th c. — anthropological theory and ethnography: formal adoption by anthropologists following Arnold van Gennep to categorize cultural 'rites of passage' that mark transitions across life stages.
- Modern day — specialized institutional and procedural adjective: preserved in academic, legal (initiatory motions), and psychoanalytic contexts, maintaining its double heritage of rigorous rudimentary basics and ritual threshold-crossing.
