• Māruti: Similar because both are patronymics derived from Marut/Māruta denoting the "son of the wind god" and applied to Hanumān; differs because Māruti is formed with the secondary patronymic suffix -i (an apatya suffix), making it a concise, widespread simplex-like name, whereas Mārutātmaja is a full tatpuruṣa compound explicitly containing the theological element ātmaja ("born from the soul/self").
  • Vāyusunu: Similar because both literally mean "son of the wind god" and celebrate Hanumān's atmospheric paternity; differs because Vāyusunu utilizes the ancient Indo-European kinship noun sūnu ("son") and the simplex name of the wind god Vāyu ("the blower"), whereas Mārutātmaja utilizes the storm-god stem Māruta and the metaphysical term ātmaja.
  • Pavanasuta: Similar because both identify Hanumān as the son of the wind; differs because Pavanasuta is formed from Pavana (literally "the purifier", a benign, cleansing aspect of the wind god) and suta ("offspring/son"), making it common in medieval devotional and vernacular poetry (such as Tulsīdās's *Hanumān Chālīsā*), whereas Mārutātmaja is high classical epic Sanskrit.
  • Vātātmaja: Similar because both mean "son of the wind" using the compound element ātmaja; differs because Vātātmaja derives from Vāta (the physical, kinetic, rushing wind, cognate to Latin ventus and English wind), whereas Māruta refers to the personified divine storm deity of the Vedic pantheon.
  • Vāyuputra: Similar because both denote Hanumān as the son of the wind; differs because Vāyuputra uses the standard kinship noun putra ("son", traditionally interpreted as "one who saves parents from the hell named Put"), representing a widespread, everyday patronymic in religious discourse.
  • Añjanīsuta: Similar because both denote Hanumān through his divine parentage; differs because Añjanīsuta (or Añjanānandana) is a matronymic honoring his biological mother, Queen Añjanā (the apsaras incarnate as a vānara), whereas Mārutātmaja is a patronymic honoring his celestial father.
  • Hanumān: Similar because both identify the paramount heroic monkey deity of the Rāmāyaṇa; differs because Hanumān (or Hanumat) is his primary proper name, derived from hanu ("jaw") referring to his jaw broken by Indra's thunderbolt (vajra), whereas Mārutātmaja is an exalted descriptive patronymic kenning.
  • Bhīmasena: Similar because Bhīma is also celebrated in epic literature as a Mārutātmaja (having been begotten upon Queen Kuntī by the wind god Vāyu); differs because Bhīmasena is a human Kuru warrior prince and Pāṇḍava brother endowed with physical combat strength, whereas Hanumān is a divine vānara embodying supreme spiritual mastery, celibacy, and immortality (*cirañjīvī*).
  • Kesarinandana: Similar because both praise Hanumān through his parental lineage; differs because Kesarinandana designates Hanumān as the joy/son of Kesari, his earthly foster father (the vānara king), whereas Mārutātmaja identifies his divine biological father, the wind god.
  • Pavanatanaya: Similar because both denote the wind's son; differs because Pavanatanaya uses tanaya (literally "the son who extends the family line", from root tan-, "to stretch/extend"), carrying distinct metrical cadence in classical Sanskrit verses.
  • Bajraṅgabālī: Similar because both are celebrated epithets of Hanumān; differs because Bajraṅgabālī (derived from Sanskrit Vajrāṅga-balī) is a popular devotional epithet highlighting his physical invulnerability ("one with limbs as hard as a diamond/thunderbolt"), whereas Mārutātmaja focuses on his divine meteorological ancestry.
  • Mahāvīra: Similar because both designate Hanumān in devotional hymns; differs because Mahāvīra is a descriptive title of valor ("great hero") shared across Indian traditions (notably as the historical title of Vardhamāna, the 24th Jain Tīrthaṅkara), whereas Mārutātmaja is uniquely genealogical and patronymic.
