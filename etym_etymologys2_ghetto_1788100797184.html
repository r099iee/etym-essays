Note: the PIE ancestry of Latin iacere is uncertain (de Vaan: no convincing deeper etymology), so the chain begins at Latin. The popular Hebrew folk-etymology from gēṭ (גֵּט) “bill of divorce” is rejected by modern linguistics. The standard account runs through Venetian ghèto “foundry”: the Ghetto Nuovo had been the city's copper-foundry island, and the confinement decree of 29 March 1516 turned the place-name into a common noun.
