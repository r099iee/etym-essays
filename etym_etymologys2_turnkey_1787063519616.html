ROOT 1: TURN
Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*terh₁- / *tor-no-
"to rub, bore, turn on a lathe"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 700 BCE)
τόρνος (tórnos)
"lathe, compass, revolving tool"
↓
Latin (c. 100 BCE)
tornāre (from tornus)
"to turn on a lathe, round off"
↓
Old French (c. 1100 CE)
torner / tourner
"to revolve, rotate, change direction"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200 CE)
turnen / tournen
"to rotate, revolve, pivot"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500 CE)
turn
"to cause to rotate around an axis"

*k̂āy-w- / *keu-
"hook, crooked pin, curved peg"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*kaigaz / *kēgjō
"peg, crooked pin, key"
↓
Old English (c. 850 CE)
cǣġ / cēġe
"instrument for opening locks; solution"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200 CE)
kei / keye
"instrument to operate a lock"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500 CE)
key
"notched metal instrument for moving lock bolts"
↓
Modern English (Compound c. 1650 CE)
