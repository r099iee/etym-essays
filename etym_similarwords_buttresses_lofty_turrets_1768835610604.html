• Support (buttresses): Similar because both hold something up; differs because support is generic and neutral, naming any act of holding up without the masonry precision or structural permanence of a buttress.
  • Prop (buttresses): Similar because both support something against falling; differs because prop suggests something temporary and wooden, a stopgap, not the permanent stone member built into a wall.
  • Pillar (buttresses): Similar because both are structural members that bear weight; differs because a pillar stands vertically and carries load from above, whereas a buttress stands against a wall to resist its outward thrust.
  • Column (buttresses): Similar because both are architectural supports; differs because a column is freestanding, vertical, and load-bearing, while a buttress is engaged with the wall it shoves back against.
  • Pier (buttresses): Similar because both are solid masonry masses; differs because a pier is an independent vertical support, while a buttress is attached to a wall and transmits lateral thrust.
  • Abutment (buttresses): Similar because both resist thrust; differs because an abutment receives the end of an arch or bridge, while a buttress reinforces a wall along its length.
  • Reinforcement (buttresses): Similar because both strengthen a structure; differs because reinforcement is a general term for added strength in any material, while buttress is the specific masonry member.
  • Tall (lofty): Similar because both mean of great height; differs because tall is a plain physical measure, while lofty implies a height that impresses, elevates, or looks down.
  • High (lofty): Similar because both describe vertical elevation; differs because high is neutral and relative, while lofty adds grandeur and often moral or emotional elevation.
  • Towering (lofty): Similar because both describe great height; differs because towering stresses overwhelming scale, while lofty stresses dignified, airy elevation.
  • Elevated (lofty): Similar because both describe raised position; differs because elevated is neutral and literal, while lofty implies the grandeur of height, whether in space or in character.
  • Exalted (lofty): Similar because both describe noble, high character or rank; differs because exalted stresses the act of being raised to greatness, while lofty stresses the height itself.
  • Sublime (lofty): Similar because both describe noble, elevated greatness; differs because sublime is reserved for the highest, overwhelming excellence, while lofty can be merely high and even haughty.
  • Grand (lofty): Similar because both describe impressive scale or style; differs because grand emphasises splendour and magnificence, while lofty emphasises height and elevation.
  • Haughty (lofty): Similar because both describe proud superiority of manner; differs because haughty is purely negative and social, while lofty keeps the physical and noble senses alive alongside.
  • Towers (turrets): Similar because both are tall structures of a building; differs because towers are the large, dominant structures, while turrets are the small, subsidiary little towers.
  • Spires (turrets): Similar because both rise from buildings; differs because a spire is a pointed tapering roof on a tower, while a turret is a complete little tower, often flat-topped or with its own cap.
  • Minarets (turrets): Similar because both are slender towers on buildings; differs because minarets belong to mosques and are tall and slender, while turrets are small defensive or ornamental corner towers.
  • Belfries (turrets): Similar because both are small towers; differs because a belfry specifically houses bells, while a turret serves as lookout, stair, or gun position.
  • Cupolas (turrets): Similar because both crown buildings; differs because a cupola is a dome-shaped roof structure, while a turret is a small tower, typically cylindrical or angular.
  • Gun mounts (turrets): Similar because both describe armoured positions for weapons; differs because a gun mount is a generic term for any weapons fixture, while turret specifies the revolving armoured housing.
