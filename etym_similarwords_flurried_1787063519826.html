• Flustered: Similar because both mean nervously confused or agitated; differs because flustered is the modern everyday word, while flurried is older and more literary, and retains its weather sense of swirling.
  • Agitated: Similar because both mean disturbed or stirred up; differs because agitated is broader and can be emotional or physical (agitated water), while flurried is specifically a brief, nervous, hurried confusion.
  • Rattled: Similar because both mean discomposed by disturbance; differs because rattled is informal and implies shaken nerves or confidence, while flurried is milder and more about being hurried and confused.
  • Perturbed: Similar because both mean disturbed in mind; differs because perturbed is quieter and intellectual (worried, troubled), while flurried is active, busy agitation.
  • Unsettled: Similar because both mean disturbed; differs because unsettled suggests lingering unease or instability, while flurried is a short-lived burst of confusion.
  • Discomposed: Similar because both mean loss of composure; differs because discomposed is formal and implies loss of dignity or calm, while flurried implies hurry and fluster.
  • Ruffled: Similar because both mean disturbed from calm; differs because ruffled suggests offence or irritation rather than busy confusion, while flurried suggests haste and commotion.
  • Bewildered / Confused: Similar because both involve mental disorder; differs because bewildered is deeper disorientation, while flurried is lighter, hurried confusion with activity.
  • Panicked: Similar because both are alarm reactions; differs because panic is intense fear, while flurried is mere discomposure, never terror.
  • Swirling / Gusty (weather sense): Similar because the verb flurried describes gusty, swirling motion; differs because these are plain weather words, while flurried applied to a whirl of bats links weather motion to agitation.
