- [14th-15th century: placing and storing] - 'Bestow' enters English meaning to put, place, or stow something - a thing is bestowed in a chest or a place; connotation is plain and physical, without ceremony.
- [16th century: gracious giving] - The verb rises in register to mean to confer or present as a gift, honour, or favour; connotation becomes formal, hierarchical, and royal - a king bestows titles, patrons bestow favours, God bestows blessings.
- [Modern usage] - 'Bestowed' is now almost exclusively the formal, lofty word for giving something weighty, such as honours, gifts, or qualities; connotation is elevated and slightly archaic, and it is rarely used of ordinary, everyday giving.
