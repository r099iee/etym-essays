- 1800s–1830s: Systemic Hegemony in Berlin - Receptive Prussian intellectual circles hailed Hegel as the supreme philosophical architect who harmonized reason, the modern constitutional state, and historical progress.
- 1840s–1880s: Revolutionary Fracture and Materialist Inversion - Fractured into conservative Right Hegelians and revolutionary Young Hegelians, notably inverted by Marx into dialectical materialism.
- Early-to-Mid 20th Century: Accusations of Totalitarianism vs. French Renaissance - Attacked in Anglo-American circles (Popper, Russell) as a precursor to state authoritarianism, but simultaneously resurrected in France (Kojève, Hyppolite) as the bedrock of existentialism, phenomenology, and psychoanalysis.
- Late 20th to 21st Century: Global Resurgence and Post-Analytic Synthesis - Restored to prominence across political philosophy, recognition theory (Honneth), and post-analytic philosophy (Brandom, McDowell) as the foundational thinker of intersubjectivity and historical self-consciousness.
