- 14th Century (Late Middle English): Anglo-Norman Legal & Feudal Reception - Borrowed from Anglo-Norman French as a formal legal term for feudal agreement, judicial concurrence, and royal sanction.
- 15th–16th Century: Constitutional Codification - Formally enshrined in British parliamentary procedure as "Royal Assent" (the monarch's sovereign approval turning bills into enforceable acts).
- 17th–18th Century: Epistemological & Contractual Refinement - Codified in Enlightenment philosophy (Locke) as the intellectual affirmation of self-evident propositions, and in common law as the essential "mutual assent" of contract formation.
- 19th Century: Newmanian Epistemology of Faith - Deepened by Cardinal Newman's Grammar of Assent, distinguishing rational deduction from unconditional personal conviction (notional vs. real assent).
- Late 20th–21st Century: Bioethical & Pediatric Demarcation - Formally bifurcated from legal "consent" in medical ethics to designate the affirmative agreement of minors in clinical trials.
