- 17th c. (Early Modern English *wabble / wobble*) - Dialectal jiggling & unsteady motion: Borrowed from Low German to describe the quivering of boiling liquids, unsteady gait of animals, or loose mechanical fittings.
- 18th c. - Everyday locomotion & physical objects: Established in common English parlance for unsteadily spinning tops, loose carriage wheels, and the staggering walk of elderly or drunken persons.
- 19th c. (Victorian Era) - Mechanical, geodetic, & astronomical science: Adopted by engineers and astrophysicists to define rotational runout and planetary axis variations (Chandler wobble).
- 20th–21st c. - Political indecision & financial market tremors: Universally employed in journalism to characterize temporary drops in market confidence, sudden policy vacillations, or institutional instability.
