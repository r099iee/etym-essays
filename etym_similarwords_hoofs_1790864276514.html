• Hooves: Similar because hooves is the exact alternating plural of hoof, sharing 100% identical meaning; differs strictly in morphological formation, as hooves preserves the archaic Germanic /f/ to /v/ voicing alternation, while hoofs follows regular modern English -s suffixation.
  • Paws: Similar because both are mammalian foot structures contacting the ground; differs because paws are soft-padded, clawed feet found in carnivores and rodents (such as dogs, cats, and bears), whereas hoofs are solid, hard, keratinized protective casings encasing the entire toe digits of herbivorous ungulate unguligrades.
  • Claws: Similar because both are made of hard keratin; differs because claws are sharp, curved, pointed nails projecting from the tips of digits used for grasping, climbing, tearing, and defense, whereas hoofs are flattened, weight-bearing structural encasements designed for walking and galloping on ground surfaces.
  • Talons: Similar because both are tough keratinous appendages; differs because talons are the formidable, curved, razor-sharp hunting claws belonging specifically to birds of prey (such as eagles and hawks), possessing no locomotion or weight-bearing function like ungulate hoofs.
  • Feet: Similar because hoofs are the functional feet of ungulate animals; differs because feet is the universal anatomical term for the terminal part of any leg supporting an organism's body weight, whereas hoofs specifically denotes the specialized keratinous horn covering of ungulates.
  • Trotters: Similar because trotters are ungulate feet; differs because trotters is an informal or culinary term applied specifically to the feet of pigs or sheep used as food.
