- 16th c. (Onomatopoeic emergence) - Emerged in early Modern English drama and vernacular writing as an echoic verb and noun imitating the sound of a heavy, blunt blow with a fist or cudgel.
- 17th c. (Rhetorical & kinetic extension) - Expanded into pulpit and public speaking descriptions ("lectern-thumping"), as well as mechanical impacts in early trades.
- 18th–19th c. (Cardiovascular & emotional interiority) - Widely adopted in Gothic and Romantic literature to express intense physiological fear, anxiety, and galloping heartbeats in dark, eerie settings.
- 20th c. (Colloquial dominance & pugilism) - Extended in sports journalism and informal registers to mean a decisive, crushing defeat ("thumping victory") or physical thrashing.
- Contemporary (Acoustic & sub-bass slang) - Applied to heavy low-frequency acoustic pulses in electronic dance music, subwoofer output, and visceral rhythmic beats.
