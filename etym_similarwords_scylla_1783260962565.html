• Charybdis: Similar because the whirlpool is Scylla's inseparable opposite in the same strait, the pair forming the idiom "between Scylla and Charybdis." Differs because Charybdis swallows the sea itself (a whirlpool), while Scylla is a rock-dwelling, dog-barking monster who seizes men — two distinct kinds of danger.
  • whirlpool: Similar because Scylla and Charybdis together name marine perils. Differs because a whirlpool is a physical phenomenon, while Scylla is a personified monster; the word also lacks the mythological and idiomatic baggage.
  • monster: Similar because Scylla is the archetypal sea-monster. Differs because monster is a general category term, while Scylla is a specific named being with a fixed myth and a fixed role in a proverb.
  • harpy: Similar because both are female mythological creatures who seize and carry off people. Differs because harpies are winged storm-spirits, while Scylla is a marine cliff-dweller with six dog heads.
  • gorgon: Similar because both are terrifying female monsters of Greek myth. Differs because a gorgon (Medusa) turns men to stone and is land-based, while Scylla rends and devours at sea.
  • dragon: Similar because both are classic predatory monsters devouring victims. Differs because dragons are reptiles associated with treasure and fire in a different myth-system, while Scylla is a distinctly nautical, six-headed canine figure.
  • shark: Similar because both are sea predators that seize and tear swimmers. Differs because a shark is a real animal, while Scylla is mythic; the comparison is metaphorical and loses the dilemma context.
  • rock: Similar because Scylla's lair is a rock in the strait, and the idiom's modern equivalent is "between a rock and a hard place." Differs because a rock is an inert hazard, while Scylla personifies the danger and supplies the proverb's mythic half.
  • dilemma: Similar because "between Scylla and Charybdis" denotes a dilemma. Differs because dilemma is the abstract logical term for a forced choice, while Scylla is the concrete monster whose name metonymically stands for one side of the choice.
  • hazard: Similar because Scylla is a hazard of navigation. Differs because hazard is a generic neutral word for risk, lacking Scylla's personhood, mythology, and proverbial currency.
