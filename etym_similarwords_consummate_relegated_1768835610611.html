• Perfect: Similar because it means reaching the highest standard, lacking all defect; differs because perfect is a purely qualitative ideal — flawless — while consummate is quantitative, a thing whose every part has been summed to the top, nothing left over.
  • Complete: Similar because it means bringing a thing to its end; differs because complete says nothing of quality — a complete job may be merely done — whereas consummate carries excellence carried to the ceiling and the finality of a verdict.
  • Total: Similar because it means whole, with nothing left out; differs because total is neutral arithmetic — all the parts present — while consummate adds the judgement that the whole is also the best possible.
  • Supreme: Similar because it means highest in rank or degree; differs because supreme is positional — above all others — while consummate is internal — every element of the thing itself carried to its ceiling.
  • Masterly: Similar because it means supreme skill, especially of a person; differs because masterly is confined to performance and craft, while consummate can crown works and persons alike with a quiet, quasi-legal severity.
  • Accomplished: Similar because it means highly skilled, finished; differs because accomplished suggests a competence attained, while consummate suggests a perfection that cannot be improved upon — the difference between good and terminal.
  • Demoted: Similar because it means moved to a lower rank or position; differs because demote is the plain word for lowering a rank, while relegate adds the formality of an official dispatch — a sending away, not merely a step down.
  • Consigned: Similar because it means sending something to another place; differs because consign is a neutral handing-over (consigned to the flames, to an address), while relegate is an official, downward dispatch with a shade of demotion.
  • Dispatched: Similar because it means sending someone or something off; differs because dispatched is neutral or military — sent on a mission — while relegate sends away and down, from the centre to the periphery.
  • Banished: Similar because it means sending someone away, often forcibly; differs because banish is the emotional, punitive word of exile, while relegate is bureaucratic and can apply to things as well as persons — banishment with paperwork.
  • Downgraded: Similar because it means reducing status; differs because downgrade is the modern, technical word for lowering a rating, while relegate is the classical word of official removal, still carrying the Roman image of the envoy sent back.
