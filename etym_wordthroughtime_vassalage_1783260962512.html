- [Feudal state] - In its current form since Middle English, 'vassalage' named the honourable condition of a vassal - a free man holding land from a lord in return for homage, fealty and service - a legal, dignified word of feudalism.
- [Collected vassals] - It also served as a collective noun for the whole body of a lord's vassals, the feudal host, in chivalric and historical romance.
- [Political metaphor] - In 17th and 19th century political rhetoric the word became a charged metaphor for national or individual dependence - 'ignominious vassalage' - subordination turned from honour into disgrace.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today it survives mainly in historical writing and in metaphor for servile dependence, its legal dignity gone, its tone of subservience intact.
