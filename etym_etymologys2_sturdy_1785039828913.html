Earliest attested root: PIE *trozdo- "thrush, songbird" (reconstructed) → Latin turdus "thrush" → Vulgar Latin *exturdire "to daze, stun" (ex- "thoroughly" + turdus; literally "to act like a thrush stupefied by fermenting grapes") [traditional hypothesis; OED: "open to grave objection"] → Old French estordir / estourdir "to daze, stun, stupefy" → Old French estourdi "dazed, stunned; then rash, violent, headstrong" (past participle) → Middle English sturdi / stourdi "fierce, cruel, rebellious, obstinate; hard to manage" (c. 1300) → sense shift: "solidly built" of a person (late 14th c.); of objects (c. 1400) → Modern English sturdy "robust, firmly built, sound" → STURDY Debated / nuanced note: The derivation of *exturdire from turdus "thrush" (the bird staggering when drunk on fermenting grapes) is the traditional account, but the Oxford English Dictionary brands it "open to grave objection." The Century Dictionary preferred a link with Latin torpidus "dull, numb" (cf. *extorpesco "to become dull"). Neither proposal is certain; both remain hypotheses. Cognates of the bird-name root: Lithuanian strazdas, Russian drozd, Old English þrysce (modern English thrush).
