• Shaking: Similar because both name involuntary oscillation of the body; differs because shaking is broader — it can be cold, anger, or mechanical vibration — while trembling more readily suggests fine, continuous, emotion-linked motion.
  • Quivering: Similar in fine, rapid vibration; differs because quivering is often lighter and more localised (lips, whiskers, leaves), while trembling covers the whole-body fright response.
  • Quaking: Similar in shaking from fear; differs because quaking sounds heavier and more dramatic or archaic ("quaking with fear"), less suited to a small animal's quick tremor.
  • Shivering: Similar in trembling of the body; differs because shivering points first to cold or fever, whereas Potter's phrase "with fright" makes trembling's emotional cause primary.
  • Shuddering: Similar in a violent shiver; differs because shuddering is a single, more convulsive reaction of revulsion or horror, while trembling is a finer, more continuous motion.
  • Quavering: Similar in unsteady motion, especially of the voice; differs because quavering applies chiefly to sound and implies a wavering pitch rather than a bodily tremor.
  • Wavering: Similar in unsteadiness; differs because wavering is typically about uncertainty of decision or of flame and light, not muscular shaking.
  • Fluttering: Similar in light, rapid agitation; differs because fluttering is small, rapid, and feathery (a heart, an eyelid), not the body-wide tremor of trembling.
  • Vibrating: Similar in rapid oscillation; differs because vibrating is mechanical and clinical, describing physical oscillation without any emotional colour.
  • Tremulous: Similar in shaking quality; differs because tremulous is a literary adjective describing a quality or manner (a tremulous voice), whereas trembling is the active participle with verbal immediacy.
  • Rattling: Similar in shaking; differs because rattling names a loose, noisy, clattering motion of objects, not the fine shaking of living flesh.
  • Wobbling: Similar in unsteady movement; differs because wobbling is loose, rocking movement of things or unsteady balance, not the fine quiver of fear.
  • Palpitating: Similar in rapid, involuntary agitation; differs because palpitating refers to a throbbing heart or pulse, a localized internal beat rather than visible body trembling.
