PIE
*ers-
"to be in motion, wander around"

↓
Latin (2nd c. BCE)
errāre
"to wander, roam, stray;
to go astray, err"

↓
Old French (11th c.)
errer
"to travel, wander;
to go astray, err"

↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
erren
"to wander, go astray;
to make a mistake"

↓
English (16th c.)
erred
"made a mistake;
strayed from the correct path"

Note: the sense shifted from physically "wandering" to morally "going astray / making a mistake". In Old French, errer "to travel" (from Latin iterāre, iter "journey") was long confused with errer "to err".
