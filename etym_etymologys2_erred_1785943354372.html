Single chain for ERRED (past tense of err):
  • Reconstructed Proto-Indo-European *h₁ers- — "to be in motion, to wander" (traditional reconstruction; c. 4500–3000 BCE; shape debated).
  • Latin errāre — "to wander, to go astray, to be mistaken" (first conjugation verb; attested from Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE).
  • Old French errer — "to wander, to stray, to be mistaken" (9th–13th c.); homonymous with errer "to travel, to journey" from Latin iterāre, and the two verbs were frequently confused in medieval French.
  • Middle English erren — "to wander, to go astray, to make mistakes" (borrowed c. 1300 via Old French).
  • Modern English err — "to make a mistake, to be wrong" (the physical sense fading; 14th c. onward).
  • ERRED — the regular weak past tense and past participle in -ed (the exact form of this card; bold target).
Notes on nuance and debate: the PIE root is traditionally set as *ers- or *h₁ers- "to be in motion, to wander," with Sanskrit íyarti "to set in motion" sometimes compared, but the reconstruction is not universally agreed. The medieval collision of the two Old French verbs errer is a real historical detail: knight errant preserves the "travelling" sense from Latin iter "a journey," not the "wandering" sense of errāre, so the two etymological streams ran together in Middle English before the travel sense died out.
