• Vassals: Similar as subjects bound to a lord; differs because vassal emphasizes land tenure and feudal obligation, while bannermen emphasizes military loyalty and the following of a specific flag into war.
  • Retainers: Similar as followers of a great household; differs because retainers can be non-military servants and household officials, while bannermen implies armed supporters who fight under a banner.
  • Liegemen: Similar as sworn, loyal subjects of a lord; differs because liegeman stresses the personal oath of fealty, while bannerman stresses the collective military host that rallies under a banner.
  • Followers: Similar as supporters of a person or cause; differs because followers is a general, modern term for adherents, while bannermen is feudal and military, with the vivid image of troops beneath a flag.
  • Partisans: Similar in fierce loyalty to a side; differs because partisans are often ideological or guerrilla fighters, while bannermen are formalized feudal subordinates of a named lord.
  • Loyalists: Similar as those who remain faithful to a cause; differs because loyalist emphasizes steadfast adherence in times of division, while bannermen emphasizes sworn, military, banner-led service.
  • Standard-bearers: Similar in being associated with a flag; differs because a standard-bearer is the single officer who carries the banner, while bannermen is the whole host that follows it.
  • Men-at-arms: Similar as armed soldiers; differs because men-at-arms are simply professional or levelled soldiers, while bannermen are defined by their feudal allegiance to a specific lord’s banner.
  • Allies: Similar as those who fight alongside; differs because allies are independent equals who cooperate, while bannermen are subordinate followers bound to a superior.
