• Soaked: Similar because both mean saturated with liquid to the point of thorough wetness; differs because soaked implies absorption into a porous material and does not require suddenness or severity, while drenched pictures liquid forced in from outside, often suddenly and violently.
  • Saturated: Similar because both name maximum wetness; differs because saturated is clinical and quantitative, naming the point of full capacity, while drenched is experiential, dramatic, and passive.
  • Sodden: Similar because both describe heavy wetness; differs because sodden describes the lumpen, stupefied, damaged aftermath of wetness rather than the event of being soaked, and lacks drenched's sense of violent onset.
  • Waterlogged: Similar because both mean full of water; differs because waterlogged means so full that buoyancy, movement, or function is lost, and applies chiefly to ground, timber, and craft, while drenched applies to a subject overwhelmed by liquid and keeps its emotional charge.
  • Sopping (sopping wet): Similar because both mean wet through; differs because sopping is colloquial and matter-of-fact, while drenched is literary, dramatic, and suggests involuntary, overwhelming wetting.
  • Doused: Similar because both mean covered with liquid; differs because dousing is a deliberate act of throwing liquid over someone or something, while being drenched is usually passive and accidental, the work of weather or force.
  • Steeped: Similar because both mean saturated with a liquid; differs because steeping is long, gentle immersion for infusion (as of tea or herbs), while drenching is quick, complete, and overwhelming.
  • Immersed: Similar because both mean fully covered by a liquid; differs because immersion is the neutral, physical fact of being under a liquid, while drenched stresses the force, excess, and discomfort of the wetting.
  • Baptized (figurative): Similar because both can mean overwhelmed or immersed by an element; differs because baptism is a ritual immersion with religious meaning, while drenched is purely physical or emotional, with no ceremony or intent.
