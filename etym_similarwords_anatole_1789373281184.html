• Anatolia: Similar because both terms share the identical Ancient Greek etymon anatolē meaning 'sunrise' or 'the East'; differs because Anatolia is a specific geographical and historical toponym referring to the Asia Minor peninsula (modern Turkey), whereas Anatole is a personal masculine forename.
  • Orient: Similar because both words fundamentally denote the direction of the rising sun and the Eastern world; differs because Orient derives from Latin oriens ('rising') and functions as an overarching regional and cultural noun or adjective, whereas Anatole is an onomastic personal name imbued with specific French literary associations.
  • Lucien: Similar because both are traditional French masculine given names of classical origin that symbolize light and dawn (from Latin lux / lucere); differs because Lucien connotes illumination or light itself, while Anatole specifically designates the geographical and celestial phenomenon of the sunrise in the East.
  • Bergotte: Similar because in Proustian literary criticism, both names are inextricably linked to the persona of the master stylist and aesthetic mentor of the Belle Époque; differs because Anatole (France) was the living, historical Nobel laureate, while Bergotte is Marcel Proust’s fictional composite character.
  • Eos: Similar because both share the conceptual mythological core of the daybreak and morning light; differs because Eos is the Greek goddess of the dawn (counterpart of Roman Aurora), carrying mythological and poetic divinity rather than mortal baptismal naming traditions.
