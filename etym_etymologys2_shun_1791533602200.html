PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*(s)kew-
"to cover, conceal, pay attention"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*skunōną
"to shy away from, dread, shrink back"
↓
Old English (c. 10th c.)
onscunian / scunian
"to abhor, dread, flee from, shrink back"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
schonen / schunien
"to shrink from, avoid, decline"
↓
English (modern)
shun
"to persistently avoid or ostracize"

Note: Old English scunian (frequently found in the compound onscunian) has no direct cognates in Old High German or Gothic. Etymologists connect it either to the PIE root *(s)kew- ("to cover, protect, notice", whence also sky, shoe, and hide) via an iterative sense of "taking cover / fleeing to shelter", or tentatively to the root of Old English scyndan ("to hasten, drive forward").
