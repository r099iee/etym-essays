- Early to Mid-19th c.: Coined and utilized primarily in anatomical, physiological, and medical treatises to describe structural abnormalities and non-homologous tissue formations.
- 1930s: Radically re-appropriated by French philosopher Georges Bataille in his essays on the "general economy" to denote the science of the excluded, the sacred, the abject, and the heterogeneous.
- 1960s to 1980s: Integrated into post-structuralism, deconstruction, and literary theory by Jacques Derrida, Michel de Certeau, and Roland Barthes to analyze textual alterity, polyphony, and uncontainable semantic excess.
- Late 20th c. to Present: Broadly applied across cultural studies, post-colonial discourse, and critical theory to examine how systems of power confront and categorize cultural and ontological difference.
