Trusty means deserving of trust: faithful, reliable, and proven over time, the adjective one reaches for when mere competence is not enough and loyalty must be vouched for. A trusty friend keeps confidences, a trusty servant never fails, and a trusty blade never breaks in the hand.

In the source sentence the counsel is to take friends who are trusty and willing: here the word does ethical work at the founding of a fellowship, ranking loyalty above cleverness or strength. Willingness promises action, but trusty promises character, and in a quest where betrayal means death, character is the qualification that matters.

Core meaning in detail: Trusty is trust plus the adjectival -y, literally full of trust in both directions: worthy to be trusted and full of fidelity toward those served. The word implies a record, not a hope: one is called trusty after years of carrying, keeping, and standing by. It attaches naturally to companions, retainers, tools, and weapons, the whole company of things and persons on whom life depends.

All senses, primary and secondary:

  • Reliably faithful and loyal in persons (primary, from c. 1200): a trusty friend, counsellor, or comrade; the dominant sense, proven devotion over time, as in the source sentence.
  • Dependable in things — tools and weapons (transferred): the knight's trusty sword, the woodsman's trusty axe; the bridge is personification, since a blade that never fails is honoured like a retainer who never flees.
  • Trustful, placing trust in others (archaic reversed sense): a trusty child rests in its mother's arms; the bridge is the two-way nature of trust itself, which can mean both the confidence given and the confidence felt.
  • Honest and honourable in office (official register): the Crown's trusty and well-beloved servants of formal address; the bridge is institutional, loyalty sworn and sealed becoming a title of rank.
  • Sturdy and sound in animals (pastoral): a trusty horse, a trusty hound; the bridge is the fellowship of service, beasts admitted to the same moral company as men.
  • Ironic or affectionate for the well-worn (humorous): my trusty old bicycle, his trusty umbrella; the bridge is fondness, as long use turns dependence into devotion and the adjective becomes a medal for survival.
  • True and exact in performance — instruments and records (technical): a trusty compass, a trusty chronicle, a trusty translation; the bridge is fidelity to fact, since an instrument that never misleads earns the same praise as a friend who never betrays.

Register and connotation: Trusty is warm, old-fashioned, and faintly heroic: it belongs to quests, ballads, and formal commendations rather than to contracts or compliance reports. Where reliable is cool engineering and trustworthy is moral assessment, trusty is affection declared, the word of someone who has leaned on the thing praised. Its archaism is its charm, and nobody who calls a sword trusty is merely describing metallurgy.
