• Vermin: Similar because varmint is its dialectal twin, both naming noxious creatures; differs because vermin is standard and urban, covering rats and parasites, while varmint is rural and folksy, covering foxes, raccoons, and rascally boys.
  • Pest: Similar because both name creatures or people that annoy and damage; differs because pest is neutral and official, from insects to nuisances, while varmint is colourful and outdoor, trailing gunsmoke and humour.
  • Rascal: Similar because a young varmint is exactly a rascal; differs because rascal is purely human and often affectionate, while varmint keeps the animal demotion audible underneath.
  • Scamp: Similar because both scold mischievous boys with a grin; differs because scamp is cheerful and town-bred, while varmint is country-bred with fences, dogs, and shotguns behind it.
  • Rogue: Similar because both can dismiss a disreputable adult; differs because rogue suggests knowing charm or even glamour, while varmint suggests low cunning to be trapped or chased.
  • Critter: Similar because both are folksy rural words for small creatures; differs because critter is neutral or fond of any beast, while varmint marks the beast as an enemy of the farm.
  • Predator: Similar because foxes and coyotes are literally predators; differs because predator is cool ecological science, while varmint is the farmer verdict from the henhouse door.
  • Nuisance: Similar because both label persistent annoyances; differs because nuisance is legalistic and general, while varmint is vivid, specific, and faintly comic.
  • Reprobate: Similar because both condemn bad characters; differs because reprobate is moral and bookish, damnation included, while varmint is practical: chase it, shoot it, or laugh at it.
