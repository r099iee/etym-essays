• Madame du Barry: The same person under her formal French style; similar in denoting Jeanne Bécu exactly; differs in register — the French form with madame and the particle is respectful and documentary, while Carlyle’s clipped “Dame Dubarry” is archaic and ironic.
  • Madame de Pompadour: Similar as a maîtresse-en-titre of Louis XV and a familiar name of the reign; differs because Pompadour was a bourgeois patron of the arts with real political influence, and she had been dead a decade when du Barry arrived.
  • maîtresse-en-titre: Similar in naming the office du Barry held; differs in being an institutional label rather than a personal name, and in carrying no moral judgment of its own.
  • royal mistress: Similar as the generic role; differs by being descriptive and modern, without the ceremonial specificity of the French title or the irony of Carlyle’s epithet.
  • courtesan: Similar as a woman whose position depended on a wealthy protector; differs sharply in implication — du Barry was formally installed at court and given an apartment, not a professional operating outside it.
  • favourite: Similar as a person who enjoys a monarch’s intimacy and therefore influence; differs in being courtly, euphemistic and usable for men as well as women.
  • Madame de Maintenon: Similar as a mistress of Louis XIV who came to wield influence; differs in her piety and her secret marriage to the king, which make her the moral opposite of the irreligious du Barry.
  • Diane de Poitiers: Similar as a celebrated royal mistress of the preceding dynasty; differs in era, in her political prominence, and in the absence of any diminutive English epithet.
  • Dame Fortune: Similar only in the honorific pattern Carlyle is echoing; differs because Fortune is an allegory, whereas du Barry is a historical woman named as though she were one.
  • Lady: The closest English equivalent of dame in this use; differs in the British rank system, where Lady attaches to peeresses and knights’ wives and Dame to the female holders of an order of chivalry.
