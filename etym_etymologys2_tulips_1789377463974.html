Classical Persian (c. 10th–14th c.)
dulband (دلبند)
"turban, sash wound around the head"
Note: Compound of dūl ('round, vessel') + band ('binding, tie'); transferred to the tulip due to its visual resemblance to a wrapped turban.
↓
Ottoman Turkish (c. 15th–16th c.)
tülbend (دلبند / تولبند)
"turban, muslin cloth, gauze; tulip flower"
Note: While Ottoman Turks officially called the flower lâle, European visitors heard it compared to a tülbend.
↓
Modern Latin / Renaissance Latin (c. 1555–1570)
tulipa
"tulip plant and flower"
Note: Latinized by Swiss botanist Conrad Gessner and Austrian diplomat Ogier Ghiselin de Busbecq.
↓
Middle French (c. 1560–1580)
tulipan / tulipe
"tulip blossom"
↓
Early Modern English (late 16th c., c. 1578–1600)
tulipa / tulipan → tulip / tulips (plural)
"bulbous spring-flowering plant of the genus Tulipa"
↓
