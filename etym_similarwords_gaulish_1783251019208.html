• Gallic: Similar because it relates to the same ancient Gauls and their land; differs because it is the Latin-derived adjective used in “the Gallic Wars” and, by extension, of modern France in a mock-heroic way, while Gaulish specifically names the language and the ancient people.
  • Celtiberian: Similar because it is a sister Continental Celtic language of the same family and era; differs because it was spoken on the Iberian peninsula by the Celtiberians, not in Gaul itself — the very pairing the source sentence makes.
  • Galatian: Similar because it is a Continental Celtic tongue descended from the same Gaulish-speaking migration; differs because it was spoken by the Celts who settled in central Anatolia in the 3rd century BC, far from Gaul.
  • Celtic: Similar because Gaulish is a member of the Celtic language family; differs because Celtic is the whole family umbrella (Continental and Insular), while Gaulish is one Continental branch within it.
  • Brittonic: Similar because it is a Celtic language and shares features with Gaulish; differs because it belongs to the Insular P-Celtic branch (Welsh, Breton, Cornish), not the Continental branch of Gaul.
  • Goidelic / Gaelic: Similar because they are also Celtic languages; differs because they are the Q-Celtic Insular tongues of Ireland and Scotland, only distantly related to Gaulish.
  • Latin: Similar because it is the language that eventually displaced Gaulish and shaped French; differs because it is Italic, not Celtic, and is the successor rather than the ancestor — the two stand on opposite sides of the source passage's linguistic contrast.
  • Gaul: Similar because it names the land and people that the word Gaulish modifies; differs because Gaul is the place-name, while Gaulish is the adjective and the name of the language.
