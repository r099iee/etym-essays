- [Early Vedic Period, c. 1500–1200 BCE; Ṛgveda] — initially applied as an evocative epithet to great solar, generative, and artisan deities (Savitṛ, Soma, Tvaṣṭṛ), crystallizing in the 10th maṇḍala (RV 10.121) into the distinct personified Creator who emerged from the Golden Embryo (*Hiraṇyagarbha*) — [perception: mysterious, transcendent demiurge invoked with profound metaphysical awe: "To what unknown god shall we offer oblation?"]
- [Middle & Late Vedic Period, c. 1000–600 BCE; Brāhmaṇas] — ascended to supreme dominance as the paramount deity of the sacrificial ritual, the Year (*Saṃvatsara*), and the cosmic victim whose self-dismemberment and ritual reconstruction constitute reality — [perception: central cosmic engine of all ritualism, embodied directly in the sacred Agnicayana hearth]
- [Epic & Purāṇic Period, c. 500 BCE–1000 CE] — absorbed into the classical persona of Brahmā the Creator and simultaneously pluralized into the *Prajāpatis* (the mind-born sons of Brahmā, including Dakṣa, Marīci, and Kaśyapa, who fathered the solar dynasties and living species) — [perception: revered as the primordial ancestral patriarch of kings and the divine father of all terrestrial lineages]
- [Modern Scholarly & Cultural Context, 19th–21st c.] — analyzed in comparative Indo-European mythology and history of religions as the crucial transitional milestone between Vedic ritual polytheism and monistic Vedānta; survives in modern Indo-Aryan languages as a revered proper name and philosophical term — [perception: classic archetypal emblem of cosmic fatherhood, fertile vitality, and royal divine lineage]
