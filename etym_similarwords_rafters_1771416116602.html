• Beams: Similar because both are load-bearing structural timbers of a building frame; differs because a beam is any large horizontal member, while rafters are the specifically sloped members that establish the roof's pitch from ridge to eaves.
  • Joists: Similar because both are parallel timber members forming part of a building's structural skeleton; differs because joists are horizontal and support floors or ceilings, while rafters are inclined and support the roof covering.
  • Trusses: Similar because both belong to the roof's load-bearing framework; differs because a truss is an engineered assembly of triangulated members, whereas rafters are the individual sloping timbers themselves.
  • Purlins: Similar because both are members of a raftered roof; differs because a purlin is a horizontal member that supports rafters at mid-span, not an inclined roof member itself.
  • Spars: Similar because both are long poles of timber; differs because spars belong to a ship's masts and rigging, while rafters support the roof of a building.
  • Girders: Similar because both are principal load-bearing members; differs because a girder is a very large horizontal beam (often of steel) spanning wide openings, not an inclined roof member.
  • Studs: Similar because both are regularly spaced framing members in a building; differs because studs are vertical wall members, while rafters are inclined roof members.
  • Collar beams / tie beams: Similar because both are roof timbers; differs because collar and tie beams run horizontally to tie pairs of rafters together against spreading, rather than forming the slope themselves.
  • Ridge board: Similar because both are parts of the roof frame; differs because the ridge is the horizontal apex member at which the rafters meet, not one of the sloping members.
