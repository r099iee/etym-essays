• uppercuts: Similar because both are rising blows or strokes from below in fighting; differs because uppercut is specifically a boxing punch rising with a clenched fist, while undercut in Tolkien's sense is a slicing blade-cut that passes up beneath the guard.
  • stabs: Similar because both are sword-attack types made with the point; differs because a stab is a straight thrust with the point, while an undercut is a slicing cut that rises from beneath.
  • jabs: Similar because both are quick, short blows or thrusts in combat; differs because a jab is a fast straight punch or poke with no direction-from-below implication, while an undercut specifically rises.
  • thrusts: Similar because both describe offensive blade movements; differs because a thrust is a forward push with the point, while an undercut is a slash delivered upward from below.
  • cuts: Similar because both are slashing blows with a blade; differs because cut is generic and directionless, while undercut names the specific rising, from-below trajectory.
  • undermines: Similar because both mean to weaken or damage from beneath; differs because undermine is purely figurative or structural (sapping foundations, eroding trust), while undercut retains the concrete cutting image and also has the commercial price sense.
  • undersells: Similar because both mean to beat competitors by charging less; differs because undersell is exclusively about selling more cheaply, while undercut also carries the blade sense and the general "weaken" sense.
  • subverts: Similar because both imply damaging something from within or beneath; differs because subvert means overturning an established system, government, or value, while undercut is lighter and applies to arguments, authority, prices, and structures alike.
  • weakens: Similar because both describe making something less strong; differs because weaken is a general, neutral verb, while undercut specifies the direction (from below) and often implies slyness or unfair competition.
  • erodes: Similar because both describe eating away at a foundation or base; differs because erode suggests gradual natural wearing (water, time, friction), while undercut suggests a deliberate cut or blow from beneath.
