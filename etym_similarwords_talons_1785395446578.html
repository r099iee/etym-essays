• Claw: Similar because both are sharp, curved, pointed growths at the ends of digits used for seizing or tearing; differs because claw is the everyday, emotionally neutral word for the nails of cats, bears, crabs, and machinery, while talon is almost exclusively raptorial and literary — the hooked grasping claw of a bird of prey.
  • Spur: Similar because both are sharp points on a bird's leg; differs because a spur is a defensive spike used for fighting, not a grasping weapon — the eagle's rear fighting claw, whereas a talon is the hooked, gripping claw of the front of the foot.
  • Nail: Similar because both are hard growths at the end of a digit; differs because a nail is the flat plate on human fingers and toes, whereas a talon is the deeply hooked, scimitar-curved grasping claw of a raptor.
  • Hoof: Similar because both are hard terminal growths on an animal's limb; differs because a hoof is the horny covering of the ungulates' foot, built for support and running, not for grasping or killing, and carries none of talon's predatory menace.
  • Grip: Similar because both involve the seizing, holding force of a talon; differs because grip names the action or the hold, not the organ — a pounce is the strike, a grip is the hold — while talon is the hooked claw itself.
  • Pounce: Similar because both belong to the attack of a bird of prey; differs because a pounce is the sudden downward strike or swoop, an action in time, whereas talons are the physical instruments of that strike.
  • Hook: Similar because both are curved, pointed implements that catch and hold; differs because a hook is a manufactured or mechanical device, morally neutral, while a talon is the natural weapon of a predator and carries menace.
  • Grasp / grip of the hand: Similar because both express seizing control; differs because these are human, deliberate, and can be gentle, whereas to be in someone's talons is to be caught and held by an implacable force — the figurative sense that made 'talons of the law' a stock phrase.
