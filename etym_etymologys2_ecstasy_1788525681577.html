PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500 BCE)
*h₁eǵʰs ("out, outside") + *steh₂- ("to stand")
"standing out, placed outside"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 5th c. BCE)
ἐξίστημι (exístēmi)
"to displace, put out of position, drive out of one's senses"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 4th–3rd c. BCE)
ἔκστασις (ékstasis)
"displacement, distraction, amazement, trance, madness"
↓
Late Latin / Ecclesiastical Latin (c. 4th c. CE; Vulgate)
ecstasis
"trance, mystical rapture, terror, transport of mind"
↓
Old French (c. 13th–14th c.)
estaise / extase
"trance, rapture, mystical transport"
↓
Middle English (c. late 14th c.; Wycliffe, Chaucer)
extasie / ecstasi
"trance, swoon, state of being beside oneself"
↓
Modern English (Present)
ecstasy
"overwhelming rapture, supreme joy, transcendent delight"

Note: Formed as an action noun with suffix -σις (-sis) from ἐξίστημι (ex- "out" + hístēmi "to set, stand"). The semantic inversion from Greek "frenzy, distress, dislocation" to modern "supreme delight" took place via the mediating concept of religious mystical transport.
