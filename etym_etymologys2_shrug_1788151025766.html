Origin uncertain(no secure PIE chain can be reconstructed)
*skrug- / *skrukk- (suggested Germanic base)
"to bend, crouch, wrinkle, shrink"
(compare Danish skrugge "to stoop, crouch," Norwegian skrukka/skrugga "to wrinkle, crease, curl up," Low German schrucken "to jerk, shock"; these are cognate guesses, not certainties — the OED marks the etymology of 'shrug' as obscure)
↓
Middle English(attested 14th c.)
schruggen / shruggen
"to shudder, shiver, tremble; to draw up the shoulders"
↓
Early Modern English(15th–16th c.)
shrug (verb)
"to raise the shoulders as a gesture of doubt, indifference, or refusal"
↓
Modern English(noun from the 17th c.; 'shrug off' from the 19th c.; garment sense from the 19th c.)
shrug
"the shoulder gesture; to dismiss lightly"
