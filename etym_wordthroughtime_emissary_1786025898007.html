- Early 17th Century (c. 1600–1650) - Borrowed into English from Latin and French during the rise of centralized European nation-states, denoting a confidential agent or covert scout sent abroad.
- Late 17th to 18th Century - Acquired a sinister, suspicious political connotation, often associated with espionage, Jesuit intrigues, and clandestine royal plots.
- 18th to 19th Century - Neutralized and institutionalized in diplomatic and historical literature as an official diplomatic representative or special envoy.
- 19th Century - Adopted into specialized medical anatomy (emissary veins) to describe blood vessels transmitting fluid outward through cranial apertures.
- 20th to 21st Century - Widely used in international relations, journalism, and high-stakes corporate diplomacy for confidential troubleshooters and presidential envoys.
