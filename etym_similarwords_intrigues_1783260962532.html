• Plots: Similar because both name secret plans to achieve an end; differs because a plot is a concrete, worked-out plan aimed at a specific (often criminal) outcome, while intrigues are looser, social, long-term webs of covert influence.
  • Conspiracies: Similar because both denote secret unlawful collaboration; differs because conspiracy carries heavy legal weight and implies a group united for a felonious purpose, whereas intrigues may be mere games of influence without illegality.
  • Schemes: Similar because both name planned operations; differs because scheme is the broadest rival, spanning everything from harmless planning to fraud, and lacks the courtly, secretive, social colouring of intrigue.
  • Machinations: Similar because both describe crafty contrivances; differs because machinations stresses malicious, hostile, laborious design, whereas intrigues focuses on covert social webs and factional manoeuvring.
  • Cabals: Similar because both belong to courtly secret plotting; differs because a cabal is the small secret faction itself (the group of plotters), whereas intrigues are the scheming activities they weave.
  • Collusion: Similar because both involve secret cooperation for a shady end; differs because collusion is a legal and business term for covert agreement, often between supposed opponents, without intrigue's social flamboyance.
  • Connivance: Similar because both involve secret complicity; differs because connivance is passive looking-away or tacit assent, whereas intrigues are active, engineered schemes.
  • Stratagems: Similar because both are cunning devices; differs because a stratagem is a single tactical ruse in war or contest, while intrigues are sustained social or political campaigns.
  • Wiles: Similar because both are crafty means of persuasion; differs because wiles are personal, often seductive arts of enticement, whereas intrigues are institutional and structural webs.
  • Affair (amorous sense): Similar because both can mean a secret liaison; differs because an affair is simply the clandestine relationship itself, whereas an "amorous intrigue" adds the flavour of plot and contrivance.
  • Fascination (modern noun sense): Similar because both name intense interest in the modern uncountable sense; differs because fascination is a passive state of being drawn, while intrigue retains an undertone of mystery and the half-hidden.
