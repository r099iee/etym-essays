• Shell: Similar because both refer to a hard, protective outer covering of an animal; differs because 'shell' is a broad, common term encompassing bivalve mollusks (clams), snails, eggs, and nuts, whereas 'carapace' is a precise anatomical term for the dorsal armor of chelonians, crustaceans, and arachnids.
  • Exoskeleton: Similar because both denote hard external support structures in invertebrates; differs because an 'exoskeleton' refers to the entire jointed external skeletal system of an arthropod, whereas the 'carapace' is specifically the dorsal shield covering the cephalothorax or body.
  • Plastron: Similar because both are structural shell components of turtles; differs because the 'plastron' is the nearly flat ventral (bottom/belly) shell, whereas the 'carapace' is the arched dorsal (upper/back) shell.
  • Cuirass: Similar because both refer to rigid armor protecting the torso; differs because a 'cuirass' is a piece of human military body armor (consisting of breastplate and backplate) made of metal or leather, whereas a 'carapace' is natural biological armor or an organic metaphor.
  • Armor: Similar because both represent protective shielding against physical or emotional attacks; differs because 'armor' is an overarching term covering all natural, artificial, plate, chain, or metaphorical defenses.
  • Shield: Similar because both serve to block harm; differs because a 'shield' is an active, often handheld defensive implement or energetic field, whereas a 'carapace' is an integrated, continuous structural encasement.
  • Husk: Similar because both represent dry outer protective casings; differs because 'husk' is predominantly botanical and fibrous, lacking the calcified, rigid bony or chitinous hardness of a carapace.
