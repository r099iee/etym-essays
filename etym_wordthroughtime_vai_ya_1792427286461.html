- [Early Vedic Era (c. 1500–1000 BCE)] — originated from *viś* as the common tribal citizenry, pastoral householders, and clansfolk distinct from the warrior nobility and priesthood — [perception: the free settled folk and agricultural backbone of the Indo-Aryan community]
- [Late Vedic & Brāhmaṇa Period (c. 1000–600 BCE)] — codified in the *Puruṣasūkta* (emerging from the thighs of Puruṣa) as the third dvija (twice-born) varṇa dedicated to cattle-rearing, agriculture, and sacrificial patronage — [perception: essential economic producers and pious twice-born householders]
- [Age of Second Urbanization & Heterodoxy (c. 6th–3rd c. BCE)] — expanded rapidly into organized merchant corporations (*śreṇis*) and wealthy urban bankers (*seṭṭhis*), becoming chief patrons of Buddhism and Jainism — [perception: affluent, influential urban financiers and leaders of maritime and caravan trade]
- [Classical Dharmaśāstra & Epic Age (c. 2nd c. BCE – 6th c. CE)] — duties formally codified in the *Manusmṛti* and *Bhagavad Gītā* (*kṛṣi-gaurakṣya-vāṇijya*), extolled in epic phalaśruti as acquiring mercantile profit and wealth — [perception: righteous custodians of trade, banking, agriculture, and charity]
- [Modern Socio-Economic Era (19th–21st c.)] — evolved into influential commercial, industrial, and philanthropic communities (Marwaris, Banias, Chettiars), driving India's modern economy and national institutions — [perception: dynamic economic innovators, philanthropists, and pillars of enterprise]
