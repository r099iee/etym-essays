- “Turn; a turn of work” (Old English cierran) — the noun charre = a piece of work, a chore; utterly everyday and neutral
- “Charcole, charcoal” (14th c.) — wood “turned” into coal by fire; practical, humble, technical
- “To char, to scorch” (c. 1560s) — back-formation from charcoal; a neutral-technical verb of burning
- “Charred” as ruin (17th–19th c.) — burnt and blackened; fire, disaster, death; ominous and mournful register
- 20th c. forensic imagery — “charred remains”, “charred rubble” in war and fire reporting
- Late 20th–21st c. gourmet reversal — “charred vegetables”, “charred meat” as desirable, smoky, chef-approved; positive culinary register
