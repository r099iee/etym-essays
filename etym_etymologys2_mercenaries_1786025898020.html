Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*merk-
"to trade, reward, wage, commerce"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
merces
"wages, pay, reward, hire"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. CE)
mercenarius
"hireling, working for wages only"
↓
Old French (c. 13th c. CE)
mercenarie
"hireling, hired soldier"
↓
Middle English (late 14th c. CE)
mercenary / mercenarie
"hired soldier; person working for money"
↓
Modern English (16th c. CE–present)
mercenaries
"soldiers hired for pay; opportunistic hirelings"

Note: Mercenaries traces to Latin merces ("wages"), from PIE *merk- (whence also market, merchandise, and merchant). The word has retained its dual character as a descriptive term for hired combatants and an ethical indictment of transaction-only loyalty.
