• Vain: Similar because both denote an utter failure to accomplish an intended objective or produce a desired outcome; differs because vain often emphasizes the proud, conceited, or self-deluded hopes of the person making the attempt (as in "a vain boast" or "dying in vain"), whereas futile stresses the inherent, objective impossibility and pointlessness of the action itself.
  • Pointless: Similar because both indicate that an action serves no constructive purpose; differs because pointless focuses on the total absence of meaning, rational motive, or justifiable aim from the outset (suggesting arbitrary or nonsensical behavior), whereas futile often describes a deeply purposeful, desperate, or heroic struggle that nevertheless cannot overcome insurmountable realities.
  • Fruitless: Similar because both characterize actions that yield no profitable or positive return; differs because fruitless uses an organic, agricultural metaphor denoting the lack of harvest, offspring, or tangible results after patient cultivation, whereas futile carries a colder, more absolute finality of inefficacy.
  • Ineffectual: Similar because both describe that which fails to produce an intended effect; differs because ineffectual frequently points to personal weakness, incompetence, lack of force, or inadequate agency in the person or mechanism executing the action (an ineffectual leader, an ineffectual law), whereas futile refers directly to the undertaking itself as doomed to failure regardless of who attempts it.
  • Bootless: Similar because both express that an effort brings no advantage or profit; differs because bootless is an elevated, archaic, and poetic term (rooted in Old English *bōt* meaning "remedy, profit, atonement") emphasizing the tragic unprofitability of prayers or pleas, whereas futile is standard across modern technical, medical, and philosophical prose.
  • Useless: Similar because both denote a total lack of practical utility; differs because useless is the broadest, most colloquial Anglo-Saxon term applying readily to broken objects, blunt tools, or discarded items, whereas futile is an elevated Latinate descriptor applied primarily to dynamic actions, efforts, processes, and existential struggles.
  • Abortive: Similar because both describe an enterprise that fails to reach fruition; differs because abortive specifies an endeavor that was cut short, abandoned, or terminated in its nascent stages before full development (such as an abortive coup or abortive mission), whereas futile describes efforts that may continue exhaustively to the bitter end without accomplishing anything.
  • Idle: Similar because both characterize efforts or words that produce no tangible effect; differs because idle suggests laziness, lack of serious intent, triviality, or groundless speculation (such as idle gossip or idle threats), whereas futile involves active, often agonizing exertion that simply cannot succeed.
