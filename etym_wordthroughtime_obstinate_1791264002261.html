- Resolute and steadfast in purpose, holding firm to a vow or resolve - Admired Roman firmness, neutral to positive connotation of standing one's ground
- Fixed and unyielding in mind, refusing to be moved by persuasion - Growing moral judgement, stubbornness seen as fault rather than virtue
- Perverse and unreasonable in persistence, clinging to error despite evidence - Fully pejorative turn, obstinacy as vice in Christian and early modern moral writing
- Stubborn in opinion and behaviour generally, from people to fevers and stains - Extended to things and symptoms, but still critical and exasperated in tone
- Modern psychological and colloquial use for both principled tenacity and foolish rigidity - Contemporary usage keeps the negative core but allows grudging admiration in contexts like negotiation or survival
