“Sorcerous” has always meant “of, like, or characteristic of a sorcerer” — magic tied to hidden arts, lots, and forbidden knowledge rather than to showy stage trickery.
“Sorcerous” in older literary English carried a genuinely dark, uncanny weight — the sorcerer as one who deals in fate and spells, nearer to the occult than to the merely magical.
“Sorcerous” by the modern era found the plain word “magical” had broadened to cover everything marvellous, leaving it more literary and slightly archaic, with a faint glamour of the sinister.
“Sorcerous” is deliberately revived in fantasy for exactly this register — smoke, dimness, and power beyond the ordinary — as when Tolkien makes Gandalf look “strange and sorcerous” in the firelight.
