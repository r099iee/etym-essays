• Talisman: Similar because both are objects believed to carry supernatural protection or power; differs because an amulet passively wards off evil and is worn on the body, while a talisman actively brings fortune or power and need not be worn.
  • Charm: Similar because both are small magical objects; differs because charm covers spells, words, and trinkets alike, while amulet is specifically a worn object of protection.
  • Mascot: Similar because both bring good luck; differs because a mascot brings luck to a group or team and is often figurative, while an amulet is a personal protective object.
  • Fetish: Similar because both are objects with believed power; differs because a fetish is believed to contain a spirit or to be itself endowed with power, while an amulet merely wards off harm.
  • Phylactery: Similar because both are objects worn for protection; differs because a phylactery is a religious leather box of scripture (or, in fantasy, a soul-receptacle), while an amulet is a general protective charm.
  • Totem: Similar because both are spiritually significant objects; differs because a totem is a tribal emblem or spirit-ancestor symbol, while an amulet is an individual protective charm.
  • Relic: Similar because both are venerated objects; differs because a relic is a holy remnant, typically of a saint, while an amulet is a functional protective charm.
  • Periapt: Similar because both are objects worn to ward off evil; differs because periapt is an archaic and rare learned synonym, while amulet is the common modern term.
  • Trinket: Similar because both are small objects; differs because a trinket is a small ornament of little value, while an amulet is valued for its protective function.
  • Mojo: Similar because both are personal magical items; differs because mojo is a hoodoo charm-bag for luck and power with strong regional slang flavour, while amulet is neutral and learned.
  • Pendant: Similar because both are worn objects; differs because a pendant is a hanging ornament with no magic attached, while an amulet is defined by its protective function.
  • Ward: Similar because both protect against evil; differs because a ward is often a protective symbol, sigil, or incantation rather than a worn object.
  • Juju: Similar because both are objects with spiritual power; differs because juju belongs to a West African magical-charm tradition, while amulet is the neutral general term.
