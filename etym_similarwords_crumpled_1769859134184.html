• Crushed: Similar because both describe a material pressed and deformed by force; differs because crushed emphasizes total compression and breakage of shape (crushed ice, a crushed can), while crumpled keeps the idea of flexible creasing that still has folds and wrinkles rather than shattering.
  • Wrinkled: Similar because both describe a non-smooth, furrowed surface; differs because wrinkled applies especially to skin, fabric, or paper drawn into lines or ridges, often by age or drying, whereas crumpled specifically implies crushing or crushing force and a more chaotic, folded result.
  • Creased: Similar because both describe surfaces with lines or folds; differs because a creased object has sharp, deliberate fold-lines (as in a pressed trouser or a folded letter), while a crumpled object has random, messy, overlapping folds.
  • Crinkled: Similar because both describe a surface bent into small irregular folds; differs because crinkled suggests many small wavy or crackling lines (crinkled tinfoil), lighter and more even than the heavy, irregular disorder of crumpled.
  • Rumpled: Similar because both describe disarranged, creased fabric or clothing; differs because rumpled is typically the untidiness of worn or slept-in clothes, without the violent crushing implied by crumpled.
  • Scrunched: Similar because both describe something squeezed into a tight, messy ball; differs because scrunched is more informal and stresses the crushing into a compact wad (scrunched-up paper), while crumpled is broader and can describe loose creasing as well.
  • Bunched: Similar because both describe material gathered together irregularly; differs because bunched emphasizes gathering in a heap or cluster rather than the individual creases and folds that define crumpled.
  • Folded: Similar because both describe material arranged with bends; differs because folded implies neat, deliberate, even folds, the exact opposite of the random disorder of crumpled.
  • Corrugated: Similar because both describe ridged, grooved surfaces; differs because corrugated means even, parallel, engineered ridges (corrugated iron), while crumpled is irregular and accidental.
  • Puckered: Similar because both describe a surface drawn into small folds; differs because puckered suggests tight gathers at a seam or the pursing of lips, orderly and localized, unlike the sweeping disorder of crumpling.
  • Squashed: Similar because both describe something flattened or compressed by pressure; differs because squashed stresses flatness and loss of volume (a squashed insect), while crumpled stresses the folded, wrinkled surface rather than mere flatness.
  • Collapsed (figurative sense): Similar because both describe a person or structure giving way under pressure; differs because collapsed is total failure and fall, while crumpled in the figurative sense keeps the image of folding in on oneself, sometimes slowly and lamentably.
  • Buckled: Similar because both describe bending and giving way under pressure; differs because buckled is used of rigid structures (a buckled wheel, buckled knees) that warp in one place, while crumpled suggests a whole surface covered with folds.
