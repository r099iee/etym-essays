• Tycoon: Similar because both name a person of great wealth and power, especially in business; differs because tycoon emphasizes modern industrial or media empire-building, is more journalistic and glamorous, and lacks magnate's historic aristocratic dignity.
  • Mogul: Similar because both denote a powerful and wealthy figure; differs because mogul suggests modern entertainment or media dominance and a faintly exotic, informal flavour, whereas magnate is formal and Latinate.
  • Oligarch: Similar because both denote concentrated wealth and influence; differs because oligarch implies rule by a small wealthy clique and carries political critique, while magnate need not imply political domination.
  • Baron: Similar because both can name a powerful magnate (robber baron, oil baron); differs because baron keeps the mock-aristocratic metaphor of old nobility more explicitly and can sound more colloquial.
  • Aristocrat: Similar because both suggest great status and prominence; differs because aristocrat denotes hereditary rank and titles, which a magnate may lack while dominating through money and clan.
  • Noble: Similar because both refer to high-standing great figures; differs because noble is tied to title and birth, while magnate centres power and property rather than pedigree.
  • Bigwig: Similar because both denote an important person; differs because bigwig is colloquial and informal, whereas magnate is formal and weighty.
  • VIP: Similar because both mark someone of consequence; differs because VIP stresses celebrity or priority access and is light and bureaucratic, not the scale of wealth magnate implies.
  • Captain of industry: Similar because both name a powerful business leader; differs because 'captain of industry' is a fixed figurative phrase with positive, nation-building overtones, while magnate is a plain noun without that rhetoric.
  • Strongman: Similar because both denote dominating local power; differs because strongman implies coercive personal or political force, while magnate stresses dynastic wealth and standing.
