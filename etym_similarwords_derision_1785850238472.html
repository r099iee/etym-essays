• Mockery: Similar because both denote contemptuous ridicule; differs because "mockery" is the broader action of imitating, jeering at, or belittling a target, while "derision" names the contemptuous laughter-and-scorn attitude and is more elevated in register.
  • Ridicule: Similar because both are near-synonyms for contemptuous mockery; differs because "ridicule" stresses making something look absurd and laughable, and can be playful, while "derision" stresses the scornful laughing at it and is never playful.
  • Scorn: Similar because both express contempt for a person or thing; differs because "scorn" is the inner feeling of disdain, not necessarily expressed aloud, while "derision" is that contempt made audible in laughter and jeers.
  • Contempt: Similar because both express a judgment of worthlessness; differs because "contempt" is a cold attitude or feeling that can be silent, while "derision" is its active, noisy expression in mockery.
  • Disdain: Similar because both convey a sense of superiority over a target; differs because "disdain" is aloof, quiet, and aristocratic, while "derision" is loud and participatory.
  • Sneering: Similar because both mock contemptuously; differs because "sneering" is a specific contemptuous facial expression or remark — a narrow gesture — while "derision" is the whole attitude and its collective outburst.
  • Scoffing: Similar because both mean ridiculing with contempt; differs because "scoffing" emphasizes mocking words of disbelief, often aimed at a claim, while "derision" can be wordless laughter at a person.
  • Jeering: Similar because both can be loud public mockery, as in the card's sentence about shouts and cries; differs because "jeering" names the taunting shouts themselves, while "derision" names the contemptuous attitude those shouts express.
  • Taunting: Similar because both target a person with contempt; differs because "taunting" deliberately provokes the victim to anger with insults, while "derision" aims to dismiss and humiliate, not to goad.
  • Satire: Similar because satire uses derision for moral correction; differs because satire is a crafted literary or artistic form with a moral purpose, while derision is the raw contemptuous mockery itself.
  • Humiliation: Similar because derision humiliates its target; differs because "humiliation" is the resulting feeling or state of shame, while "derision" is the act of mockery that produces it.
  • Laughing-stock: Similar because a laughing-stock is the person or thing at which derision is aimed; differs because "laughing-stock" names the target of the mockery, not the mockery itself.
