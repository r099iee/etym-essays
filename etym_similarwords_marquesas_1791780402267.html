• Archipelago: Similar because the Marquesas constitute a prime geographical example of an archipelago (a scattered cluster or chain of oceanic islands); differs because archipelago is an abstract, generic geographical term, whereas the Marquesas designates a specific, historically and culturally unique Polynesian island chain.
  • French Polynesia: Similar because the Marquesas form one of the five administrative subdivisions of French Polynesia; differs because French Polynesia is the overarching overseas collectivity encompassing 121 islands across five distinct archipelagos (the Society Islands, Tuamotus, Gambier, Austral, and Marquesas), spanning an oceanic expanse equivalent to Western Europe.
  • Society Islands: Similar because both are volcanic high-island archipelagos within French Polynesia settled by ancient Polynesian navigators; differs because the Society Islands (including Tahiti, Moorea, and Bora Bora) are surrounded by expansive barrier reefs and tranquil lagoons, and hold contemporary political and economic dominance, whereas the Marquesas are far more isolated, lack protective barrier reefs, and possess distinct linguistic and cultural traditions.
  • Atoll: Similar because both are Pacific oceanic landforms inhabited by Polynesian peoples; differs because an atoll is a low-lying ring of coral surrounding a central lagoon with negligible terrestrial elevation, whereas the Marquesas are jagged volcanic spires rising sheer out of the ocean thousands of meters into the clouds without protective outer reefs.
  • Tuamotus: Similar because the Tuamotu Archipelago is the nearest neighboring island group to the Marquesas within French Polynesia; differs because the Tuamotus comprise almost eighty flat, low-lying coral atolls renowned for pearl farming and treacherous reefs, contrasting starkly with the towering basalt crags and deep river valleys of the Marquesas.
  • Polynesian Triangle: Similar because the Marquesas occupy a vital geographic position within this oceanic expanse; differs because the Polynesian Triangle is a massive macro-region bounded by Hawaii, New Zealand (Aotearoa), and Easter Island (Rapa Nui), within which the Marquesas functioned as a key early dispersal and navigational hub.
