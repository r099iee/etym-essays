• struck: Similar because both are past forms meaning 'hit with force'; differs because struck is ordinary and neutral, while smote is archaic, solemn, and weighty.
  • hit: Similar because both mean to deliver a blow; differs because hit is the everyday verb for any impact, while smote implies a single crushing, often legendary stroke.
  • beat: Similar because both involve blows; differs because beat suggests repeated strikes and is neutral, while smote is one decisive blow.
  • knocked: Similar because both mean to strike something; differs because knocked is casual and often lighter, while smote is epic and destructive.
  • thumped / thudded: Similar because both name heavy, dull impacts; differ because they are onomatopoeic and informal, while smote is dignified and archaic.
  • battered: Similar because both suggest severe striking; differs because battered is repeated, prolonged abuse, while smote is a single overwhelming blow.
  • lashed: Similar because both mean to strike forcefully; differs because lashed suggests a whipping, linear stroke, while smote is a blunt, crushing blow.
  • afflicted / devastated: Similar because both extend to emotional or divine injury; differ because they are modern and abstract, whereas smote keeps its concrete, biblical violence.
  • socked / walloped / slugged: Similar because all mean to hit hard; differ because they are slangy, comic, and casual, the exact opposite of smote's solemnity.
