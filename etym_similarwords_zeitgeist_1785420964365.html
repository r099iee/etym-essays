• Spirit of the age / spirit of the times: Similar because both name the defining intellectual and cultural temper of a period — the nearest English equivalent; differs because the English phrase is periphrastic and lacks the single-word, quasi-technical authority of the German loanword.
  • Ethos: Similar because both name the characteristic spirit of a group or age; differs because an ethos can persist across centuries and belong to an institution, profession, or community, while a zeitgeist is anchored to one historical period.
  • Milieu: Similar because both describe a surrounding cultural setting; differs because a milieu is spatial, social, and concrete — an environment or setting — while the zeitgeist is temporal and abstract, the spirit of a time rather than a place.
  • Climate (of opinion): Similar because both name the prevailing intellectual atmosphere; differs because "climate of opinion" is a looser, more metaphorical phrase emphasizing prevailing assumptions, while zeitgeist implies a coherent epochal spirit with historical and philosophical weight.
  • Temper (of the times): Similar because both name the prevailing disposition of an age; differs because temper stresses the emotional and attitudinal cast, while zeitgeist embraces the whole intellectual outlook.
  • Trend: Similar because both catch what is currently fashionable in taste; differs because a trend or fad is short-lived, superficial, and limited in scope, while the zeitgeist is deeper, broader, and era-defining.
  • Mood: Similar because both can describe the felt atmosphere of a moment; differs because mood is vaguer, more emotional, and shorter-lived, lacking the deliberate, philosophical sense of a coherent intellectual outlook.
  • Culture: Similar because both can name the shared outlook of a period or group; differs because culture is far broader — all the customs, arts, and institutions of a society — whereas zeitgeist isolates the spirit of a specific historical moment.
  • Ambience / vibe: Similar because both name the felt quality of a setting or moment; differs because these are colloquial, sensory, and place-based, while zeitgeist is intellectual, temporal, and era-based.
  • World-view (Weltanschauung): Similar because both are German-derived words for a shared way of seeing; differs because a Weltanschauung belongs to an individual or group and is a systematic outlook, while the zeitgeist is the collective spirit of a whole age.
