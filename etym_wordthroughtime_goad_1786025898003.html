- Old English (c. 800s) - [Concrete Pastoral Implement] - Attested as *gād*, denoting the physical iron-pointed staff used by Anglo-Saxon farmers to steer and urge oxen teams.
- Middle English (c. 1300s) - [Spiritual & Moral Stimulus] - Broadened in religious and allegorical writing (Chaucer, Langland) to represent the sharp pricking of conscience, sin, or divine grace urging sinners to repent.
- Early Modern English (1500s–1600s) - [Verbal Baiting & Provocation] - Solidified as an active transitive verb meaning to deliberately irritate, torment, or bait someone into emotional outbursts or reckless combat.
- 1800s–Present - [Psychological Catalyst & Rhetoric] - Retained in modern English as a sophisticated verb for psychological instigation, political baiting, and driving individuals past their breaking points.
