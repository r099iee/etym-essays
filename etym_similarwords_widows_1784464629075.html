• Widowers: Similar because they are people whose spouses have died; differs because a widower is the male form, while a widow is a woman, in traditional English usage.
  • Orphans: Similar because they have lost close family members; differs because an orphan has lost a parent or parents and may be of either sex at any age of childhood, while a widow has lost a husband.
  • Spinsters: Similar because they are unmarried women; differs because a spinster has never married (or left marriage without a husband's death), while a widow's status comes specifically from her husband's death.
  • Bereaved: Similar because they have suffered a death in the family; differs because bereaved is the general adjective for anyone who has lost a loved one, while a widow has specifically lost her husband.
  • Divorcées: Similar because they are women whose marriage has ended; differs because a divorcée's marriage ended by legal decree, while a widow's ended by the husband's death.
  • Grass widows: Similar because they are women separated from their husbands; differs because a grass widow's husband is away (or the marriage ended informally), while a widow's husband is dead.
  • Surviving spouses: Similar because they are the living partner after a spouse's death; differs because surviving spouse is the modern legal / gender-neutral term for both sexes, while widow is the traditional feminine word.
