• Elevate: Similar because both mean to raise up; differs because elevate is the neutral member — any raising, physical or figurative, with no implication of glory, worship, or intensity — while exalt implies elevation to a very great height attended by reverence and praise.
  • Raise: Similar because both mean to lift or lift up; differs because raise is the plainest, most physical verb — a hand, a roof, a flag is raised, emotionally flat — while exalt is intense, honorific, and near-worshipful, reserved for great height and glory.
  • Glorify: Similar because both mean to praise or honour highly; differs because glorify centres on making something radiant, divine, or splendid — the glorification of God or a person’s reputation — while exalt insists on raising in rank and degree as well as praising, carrying the sense of elevation with it.
  • Magnify: Similar because both mean to praise extravagantly; differs because magnify emphasises enlarging the subject’s reputation through words (‘magnify the Lord’), while exalt emphasises raising the subject to a higher position of glory rather than merely speaking of it more grandly.
  • Uplift: Similar because both mean to raise to a higher state; differs because uplift is chiefly used of the spirit, mood, or morale — uplifting music or words — and is modern and informal, while exalt is formal, honorific, and can be physical or political as well as emotional.
  • Ennoble: Similar because both mean to raise to a higher condition; differs because ennoble specifically means to confer nobility, dignity, or moral refinement — to raise in moral or social rank — while exalt is broader, covering praise, glory, and physical height as well as rank.
  • Promote: Similar because both mean to advance in rank; differs because promote is the ordinary, institutional word for advancement in employment or status, wholly secular and neutral, while exalt implies a great height of glory and reverence, not a step on a career ladder.
  • Aggrandize: Similar because both mean to increase power or standing; differs because aggrandize often carries disapproval — self-aggrandizement, the inflation of one’s own importance — while exalt is approving and ceremonial, the elevation of another in genuine honour.
  • Extol: Similar because both mean to praise highly; differs because extol is specifically verbal praise — to extol is to praise in words, to laud — while exalt raises the subject in rank and glory and can be an act of elevation, not merely speech.
  • Honour / Honor: Similar because both mean to treat with respect and esteem; differs because honour is the broad, everyday term for respect, while exalt is the grand, ceremonial verb of raising to the height of glory, with a liturgical and royal weight honour lacks.
  • Elate: Similar because both mean to raise high in feeling; differs because elate is restricted to the emotional sense — to make joyful or proud — and is modern and psychological, while exalt covers rank, glory, and physical height as well as elation of spirit.
