PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*h₂welh₁-
“to be strong, have power”
↓
Latin (3rd–2nd c. BCE)
valēre
“to be strong, be well, prevail, be worth”
↓
Late Latin (4th c. CE)
valor, valōrem
“worth, value, strength”
↓
Old French (12th c.)
valor, valour
“worth, value; courage”
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
valour
“worth, value; bravery in battle”
↓
English (modern; US spelling valor)
VALOUR
“great courage in danger or battle”

Note: the PIE root is reconstructed as *h₂welh₁- (de Vaan) or *ual- (Pokorny); the Germanic cognates (Old English wealdan “to rule”, Gothic waldan) show the same root with a dental extension whose exact ablaut is debated. The shift from “worth” to “courage” took place in Old French under chivalric ideology and is securely attested.
