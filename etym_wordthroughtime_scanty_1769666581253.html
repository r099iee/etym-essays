- 1650s: 'scanty' emerges as an adjectival twin of 'scant' (from Old Norse for 'short') meaning deficient in quantity, barely enough; neutral and descriptive.
- 18th–19th c.: widens from provisions to clothing and income; in Victorian prose 'scanty' dress carries moralised disapproval (immodesty) and 'scanty means' signals respectable poverty.
- 20th–21st c.: still means barely sufficient; strongly associated with revealing attire (scanty bikini — mildly disapproving), meagre rations and thin evidence; retains a slightly formal, literary air.
