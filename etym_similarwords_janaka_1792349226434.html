• Rājarṣi: Similar because both denote an enlightened king who combines royal sovereignty with deep spiritual wisdom; differs because rājarṣi is a broad generic Sanskrit class noun meaning "royal sage" or "monarch-seer", whereas Janaka is the specific historical and epic personage who serves as the quintessential living embodiment of that archetype.
  • Daśaratha: Similar because both are venerable, heroic elder monarchs in the Rāmāyaṇa who act as devoted fathers to the central divine couple (Daśaratha to Rāma, Janaka to Sītā); differs because Daśaratha is ruled by intense emotional attachment and tragic royal vows that lead to his grief-stricken demise, whereas Janaka is defined by unshakeable equanimity, philosophical detachment, and spiritual mastery.
  • Yājñavalkya: Similar because both are central, towering figures of supreme wisdom in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad collaborating in the pursuit of Brahman; differs because Yājñavalkya is a professional Brahmin sage and wandering renunciant who receives gifts, whereas Janaka is the royal Kshatriya sovereign and patron who tests and rewards the sages.
  • Hariścandra: Similar because both are storied, righteous kings of ancient Indian legend renowned for uncompromising adherence to *dharma*; differs because Hariścandra is tested through agonizing personal degradation, loss of kingdom, and extreme ascetic sacrifice, whereas Janaka achieves supreme spiritual realization while peacefully maintaining his throne through wisdom and karma-yoga.
  • Yudhiṣṭhira: Similar because both are royal sovereigns celebrated for moral rectitude and philosophical contemplation; differs because Yudhiṣṭhira in the Mahābhārata is constantly tortured by moral doubt, the trauma of war, and existential guilt, whereas Janaka personifies serene, joyful non-dual realization and effortless inner liberation.
  • Māndhātā: Similar because both are ancient Vedic kings of legendary piety and imperial power; differs because Māndhātā is celebrated primarily for world-conquering martial might, territorial expansion, and massive Vedic ritual sacrifices, whereas Janaka is renowned for metaphysical inquiry and the eradication of egoic desire.
  • Manu: Similar because both are foundational ancestral monarchs and progenitors associated with cosmic order (*dharma*); differs because Manu is the primal cosmic lawgiver and progenitor of the entire human race (*Mānavas*), whereas Janaka is an imperial ruler of a historical kingdom and father of Sītā.
  • Aśvapati Kaikeya: Similar because both are celebrated Upanishadic Kshatriya kings who possessed esoteric knowledge of Brahman and instructed learned Brahmins; differs because Aśvapati appears in the Chāndogya Upaniṣad instructing sages on the Vaiśvānara Self, whereas Janaka is the supreme patron of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad and a main character across epic narrative literature.
