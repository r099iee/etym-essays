- 1650s — a new scold-word: dawdle appears for trifling and loitering, of obscure origin, already sounding like what it means.
- 18th c. — schoolroom and parade ground: masters and sergeants adopt it for laggards, and dawdling becomes punishable slowness.
- 19th c. — novelists love it: Dickensian clerks and schoolboys dawdle through chapters, and the word gathers comic affection.
- early 20th c. — psychology of delay: dawdling shades into reluctance, the feet slow because the heart dreads arriving.
- mid 20th c. — the brisk age scolds it: efficiency culture makes the dawdler a figure of fun and reproach in cartoons and sitcoms.
- Today — scolding but fond: parents still cry out against dawdling on school mornings, while poets quietly defend the ramble it names.
