• Domesticated: Similar because both mean "brought under human control" of animals; differs because "domesticated" refers to species bred over generations to live with humans, while "tame" can describe a single wild animal merely made used to people.
  • Docile: Similar because both mean "gentle, easily led"; differs because "docile" describes a temperament of ready obedience (of humans or animals) and is never used for dullness of events, whereas "tame" spans animals, people, and things.
  • Gentle: Similar because both mean "not fierce"; differs because "gentle" is purely about mildness and tenderness and carries no connotation of dullness or spiritlessness.
  • Mild: Similar because both mean "not extreme"; differs because "mild" is neutral about flavour, weather, and temperament, with a softer judgement than the slightly pejorative "tame".
  • Meek: Similar because both mean "submissive, not assertive"; differs because "meek" is a strong biblical virtue word (the meek shall inherit the earth) and refers only to people, not animals or events.
  • Submissive / subdued: Similar because both mean "brought under control, yielding"; differs because these are formal, psychological words for compliance and emphasise yielding to authority, while "tame" also covers dull entertainment.
  • Broken (in): Similar because both describe an animal whose wildness has been overcome by training; differs because "broken (in)" is specifically about horses and training to obey, whereas "tame" is broader.
  • House-trained / housebroken: Similar because both describe an animal made fit for living indoors; differs because these are narrowly about toilet and indoor manners, not about removing wildness generally.
  • Cultivated: Similar because both can mean "brought under human tending"; differs because "cultivated" applies to plants/crops and, of people, to refined taste — a very different axis from animal taming.
  • Bland / insipid: Similar because both mean "boring, lacking strong character" of events or works; differs because these are sharper, more critical terms for tastelessness, while "tame" merely notes the absence of daring.
  • Dull / boring / unexciting: Similar because, like "tame", they describe events and things that fail to engage; differs because these are the general words for boredom, while "tame" implies the thing was made safe/genteel, not merely monotonous.
  • Flat / uninspired: Similar because both describe performances or writing lacking energy; differs because these focus on lack of inspiration, while "tame" focuses on excessive caution and lack of daring.
  • Safe / harmless: Similar because both mean "not dangerous or risky"; differs because these are neutral or approving (safe is good), whereas "tame" in the aesthetic sense is mildly disapproving of excessive safety.
  • Tractable / manageable: Similar because both mean "easy to control"; differs because these are neutral technical words for controllability, with none of "tame"'s animal or aesthetic colour.
