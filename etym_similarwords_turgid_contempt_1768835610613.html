• Tumid: Similar because it means swollen and distended; differs because tumid belongs to the separate Latin tumere family (tumor, tumescent, thumb, from PIE *teuh₂-), while turgid comes from turgēre — near-twins in sense, distinct in blood.
  • Swollen: Similar because it means enlarged beyond the natural measure; differs because swollen is the plain, physical word, neutral and concrete, while turgid adds the idea of tautness — stretched tight from within, at the point of strain.
  • Distended: Similar because it means stretched or swollen by internal pressure; differs because distended is the clinical term for a body part stretched from within, while turgid has migrated to style and atmosphere as well as flesh.
  • Bloated: Similar because it means swollen and puffed up; differs because bloated suggests decay and excess (a bloated corpse, a bloated bureaucracy), while turgid suggests taut, pressurised fullness — firm, not rotten.
  • Inflated: Similar because it means blown up beyond natural measure; differs because inflated is the economic and rhetorical word for artificial excess (inflated prices, inflated claims), while turgid is the aesthetic word for style swollen taut from within.
  • Bombastic: Similar because it means puffed-up, inflated language; differs because bombastic stresses the noise and vanity of the style, while turgid stresses the pressure — words swollen taut from within, on the verge of bursting.
  • Scorn: Similar because it means regarding something as worthless; differs because scorn is openly expressive, theatrical disgust, while contempt is the quiet, settled verdict — a judgement of worthlessness passed and recorded.
  • Disdain: Similar because it means a superior disregard; differs because disdain is haughty, aristocratic aloofness, while contempt is colder and more judicial, cutting the other down to nothing.
  • Derision: Similar because it means treating something as ridiculous; differs because derision is mocking laughter — public, noisy — while contempt is silent, final, and can be entirely inward.
  • Despite: Similar because it names the older word for spite and contempt; differs because despite survives only in the fossil phrase 'in despite of,' while contempt is fully alive and carries the legal weight of 'contempt of court.'
  • Contemptuous: Similar because it is the adjective of the same feeling; differs because contemptuous is the attributive form (a contemptuous glance) describing the attitude, while contempt is the noun naming the judgement itself.
  • Contemptible: Similar because it derives from the same root; differs because contemptible is passive — deserving contempt — while contempt is the active feeling that judges worthlessness; the two face in opposite directions.
