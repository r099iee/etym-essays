• Stunned: Similar because both describe temporary mental paralysis from a shock or blow; differs because stunned stresses the sudden impact itself and can mean mere surprise, while dazed implies ongoing confusion.
  • Stupefied: Similar because both mean rendered slow-witted and unresponsive; differs because stupefied is stronger (nearly senseless) and often suggests drugs, drink, or astonishment.
  • Bewildered: Similar because both involve loss of clarity; differs because bewildered emphasises being led astray or lost in complexity, while dazed suggests a dulled, foggy state.
  • Confused: Similar because both describe muddled thinking; differs because confused is the general term for lack of clarity and need not involve a shock or blow.
  • Befuddled: Similar because both mean mentally fogged; differs because befuddled hints at alcohol or mere muddle and is slightly informal and humorous.
  • Groggy: Similar because both describe weak, unsteady, half-awake states; differs because groggy stresses physical weakness and swaying (often on waking), while dazed stresses mental blankness.
  • Numb: Similar because both describe reduced sensitivity, physical or emotional; differs because numb is primarily about feeling, not thought, and applies to cold, anaesthesia, or emotional shutdown.
  • Shell-shocked: Similar because both cover trauma-induced stupor; differs because shell-shocked is specific to combat trauma and implies severe, lasting psychological injury.
