- 1906-1907 joking coinage: Brander Matthews uses it, then Gelett Burgess puts Miss Belinda Blurb on a mock jacket for Are You a Bromide? at a booksellers' dinner - perceived as insider humour mocking fulsome jacket praise.
- 1910s-1920s trade slang: publishers' "blurb" for flamboyant advertisement - mildly derisive, used with a wink among booksellers.
- Mid-20th c. standard industry term: dust-jacket blurbs, front-flap blurbs - perception neutralises into professional vocabulary; every book needs one.
- Late 20th c. verb and generalisation: to blurb a book; blurbs for films, records, festivals - the commerce of praise spreads beyond books, still faintly cynical.
- Digital age: Amazon blurbs, Netflix blurbs, back-cover culture - ubiquitous, expected, and read with practised scepticism; "blurb" now names a whole literacy of hype.
