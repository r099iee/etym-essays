• Tribulations: Similar because both refer to severe trials, suffering, and distress; differs because tribulations specifically highlights oppressive, harrowing trials and prolonged struggles that test endurance, often paired with persecution or spiritual discipline.
  • Calamities: Similar because both denote devastating misfortunes; differs because calamities emphasizes sudden, disastrous events causing widespread ruin or catastrophic loss, whereas afflictions focuses more on the enduring state of pain or personal suffering.
  • Adversities: Similar because both describe unfavorable conditions and hardships; differs because adversities refers broadly to opposing forces, obstacles, and general ill-fortune, carrying a less visceral sense of deep bodily or spiritual agony than afflictions.
  • Woes: Similar because both describe deep distress and sorrow; differs because woes has a poetic, lamenting tone centered on profound emotional grief and lamentation rather than physical ailment or structural misfortune.
  • Torments: Similar because both involve acute distress; differs because torments suggests severe, agonizing, and often excruciating physical or mental torture, frequently inflicted by an active agent or acute agitation.
  • Hardships: Similar because both involve difficult life circumstances; differs because hardships emphasizes practical deprivation, austere conditions, and toil, lacking the solemn, spiritual, or existential gravity of afflictions.
  • Crosses: Similar because both describe burdens of suffering that must be borne; differs because crosses is an explicitly Christian metaphor emphasizing patient, self-sacrificing endurance of a particular lifelong burden or trial.
