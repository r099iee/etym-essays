• summary: Similar because both present the essentials of a subject to a reader; differs because a summary compresses what is already known into its barest outline, whereas an exposition unfolds and explains underlying principles in full detail.
  • interpretation: Similar because both unpack a text or idea for an audience; differs because interpretation stresses subjective meaning-making and the interpreter’s standpoint, whereas exposition aims at objective, orderly clarification of what is there to be seen.
  • explanation: Similar because both make a subject understandable; differs because explanation is the plain, general word for rendering something clear, whereas exposition implies a fuller, more systematic and structured laying-out, often with parts arranged for inspection.
  • account: Similar because both give a connected statement of a subject; differs because an account is a report or narrative of events or facts, often neutral and chronological, while an exposition is an analytical setting-forth of ideas or theory.
  • argument: Similar because both develop a subject in connected prose; differs because an argument seeks to persuade the reader toward a conclusion, whereas an exposition seeks to make a subject intelligible without necessarily taking a side.
  • disquisition: Similar because both are formal, lengthy treatments of a subject; differs because a disquisition is a formal discourse or treatise, more learned and self-contained in form, while an exposition can be brief and is defined by the act of setting-forth rather than by length.
  • elucidation: Similar because both make something obscure clear; differs because elucidation stresses removing obscurity from a difficult point, whereas exposition stresses the systematic arrangement of a whole subject.
  • explication: Similar because both analyse a text or idea in detail; differs because explication (from French, itself from Latin explicare, ‘to unfold’) is the close, line-by-line analysis of a text, whereas exposition is the broader setting-forth of a theme or theory.
  • exhibition / display: Similar because both are public showings of things; differs because an exhibition or display is primarily the physical showing of objects, while an exposition is, in this public sense, a formal large-scale fair or show — and the same word does the intellectual work of setting an argument out.
  • presentation: Similar because both lay a subject out for an audience; differs because presentation is a modern, neutral word for any act of showing or delivering material, whereas exposition carries an older, more formal and analytic weight, and its narrative and musical senses are specialised.
