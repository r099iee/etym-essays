PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*terh₂- / *ter-men-
"to cross over, pass through, reach an end; boundary stone, marker"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*termen-
"boundary marker, limit"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
terminus → termināre
"boundary, limit; to delimit, bound, bring to a close"
↓
Late Latin / Christian Latin (c. 2nd–5th c. CE)
interminābilis
"boundless, limitless, having no boundary; eternal"
Note: Compound formed from negative prefix in- ('without, not') + termināre ('to limit') + passive adjective suffix -ābilis ('-able'). First used in theological treatises to describe the infinite majesty of God.
