- c. 1300–1500 - Middle English introduction via Old Norse and Dutch trade; denoted a broad, flat-bottomed drinking bowl or metal cup used in communal banquets.
- c. 1500–1750 - Early Modern English; established standard term for handleless secular drinking cups made of pewter, silver, or horn, common in taverns and domestic dining.
- c. 1750–1850 - Transition into laboratory science and Romantic literature; poeticized as an opulent wine vessel (Keats) while simultaneously being adopted by early chemists for flat-bottomed mixing glassware.
- c. 1850–1900 - Industrial glass manufacture; scientific definition became dominant worldwide (standard Griffin/Berzelius laboratory beakers), while archaeology introduced the "Bell-Beaker culture."
- c. 1900–present - Modern dual usage; universally recognized as essential laboratory glassware in science and education, while retaining colloquial Commonwealth usage for children's plastic cups and informal literary drinkware.
