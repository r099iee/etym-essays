• cannibalistic: Similar because both denote man-eating and savage predation, the core trait of the Laestrygonians. Differs because cannibalistic is a general, modern, even clinical word, while Laestrygonian is a learned mythonym tied to a specific Homeric tribe.
  • anthropophagous: Similar because both describe man-eating peoples. Differs because anthropophagous is the formal scientific/ethnographic term, while Laestrygonian is literary and archaic, carrying epic rather than scientific weight.
  • savage: Similar because both connote wildness and brutality beyond civilization. Differs because savage is common, broad, and applicable to any violent force, while Laestrygonian is rare, bookish, and specifically giant-cannibal.
  • barbarous: Similar because both mark a people as outside Greek or civilized norms. Differs because barbarous usually means "non-Greek-speaking/uncultured" without implying cannibalism, while Laestrygonian specifically implies man-eating monsters.
  • monstrous: Similar because both describe what is frighteningly inhuman. Differs because monstrous is a general moral-aesthetic term for anything horrible, while Laestrygonian names a concrete mythic tribe of giants.
  • gigantic: Similar because the Laestrygonians are giants. Differs because gigantic is an ordinary adjective for enormous size, while Laestrygonian adds the cannibal attribute and the epic pedigree.
  • ogre-like: Similar because both evoke man-eating monsters of folklore. Differs because an ogre is a fairytale figure, while the Laestrygonians belong to classical epic; ogre-like is colloquial where Laestrygonian is learned.
  • Cyclopean: Similar because the Cyclopes are also giant, lawless, man-eating Homeric beings. Differs because Cyclopean also has a technical meaning (huge stone masonry) and the Cyclopes are pastoral one-eyed giants, while Laestrygonians are a spear-throwing cannibal tribe.
  • savage (reiterated distinction above).
  • fierce: Similar because both imply terrifying aggression. Differs because fierce is generic and positive-neutral (fierce loyalty), while Laestrygonian is exclusively mythic-cannibalistic.
