PIE *peh₂- “to protect, to guard, to feed, to shepherd” (c. 4500–2500 BCE) — a root of tending, covering both the guarding of the flock and the feeding of the family → (with the *-sḱ- suffix) Latin pāscere “to feed, pasture, graze” (attested from the 3rd c. BCE) → Latin pāstor “shepherd, herdsman — the feeder” (from the *peh₂-t- stem + agent suffix -tor; attested 2nd–1st c. BCE) → Latin pāstorālis “of a shepherd, pastoral” (adjectival formation; attested in Late Latin, c. 4th–5th c. CE) → Old French pastoral “of shepherds” (c. 12th c.) → Middle English pastoral “of shepherds, rural” (attested 15th c.) → English pastoral (adjective and noun; modern). Note the same PIE root's sister careers: Greek poimḗn “shepherd” (hence the pastoral office in Greek as in Latin), Vedic pāti “he protects,” Sanskrit pāla “guardian” (as in gopāla “cowherd”), and the Germanic feeding strand that gave English feed, food, fodder, and fat.
