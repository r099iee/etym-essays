• Bells: Similar because both are metal percussion instruments producing loud, resonant sound; differs because bells are cup-shaped with an internal clapper and are rung, while gongs are flat or bossed discs beaten externally with a mallet.
  • Cymbals: Similar because both are struck metal percussion; differs because cymbals are two thin plates clashed together for a bright, short metallic crash, while a gong gives a deep, sustained, throbbing hum that decays slowly.
  • Tam-tam: Similar because both are large, flat, struck metal discs; differs because the tam-tam is a gong of indefinite pitch used in Western orchestras for an engulfing roar, whereas a tuned gong produces a definite pitched tone.
  • Drums: Similar because both are percussion instruments struck to produce rhythm; differs because drums sound from a stretched membrane, while gongs sound from the resonance of a solid metal disc.
  • Chimes: Similar because both are struck metal producing ringing tone; differs because chimes are tuned tubes or rods struck for melody, while a gong is a single disc giving one massive, complex tone.
  • Knocker: Similar because both are percussion objects used to make loud noise; differs because a knocker is a striking device that produces a sharp, crisp report, while a gong hums, swells, and slowly decays.
  • Medal / decoration: Similar in British military slang where "gongs" means chest medals; differs because a medal is an award, while the gong-slang metaphor trades on the shiny bronze disc, not on sound.
