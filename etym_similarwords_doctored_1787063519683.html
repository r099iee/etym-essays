• treated: Similar because both mean “given medical care”; differs because treated is the neutral, clinical word, while doctored sounds homely and old-fashioned.
  • medicated: Similar because both describe medical intervention; differs because medicated refers specifically to administering drugs, not general care.
  • cured: Similar because both describe the outcome of medical care; differs because cured asserts recovery, while doctored only records the treatment.
  • healed: Similar because both imply a return to health; differs because healing is the natural or divine process, while doctoring is the professional intervention.
  • patched up: Similar because both mean “repaired roughly”; differs because patching is physical mending only, with no medical or dishonest senses.
  • fixed: Similar because both mean “repaired”; differs because fixed is broad and neutral, while doctored implies a hand that may have made things worse.
  • altered: Similar because both mean “changed from the original”; differs because alteration is neutral, while doctoring implies deception or interference.
  • falsified: Similar because both describe making something untrue; differs because falsifying is explicitly lying or forging, while doctoring can also be a benign fix.
  • tampered with: Similar because both mean “interfered with dishonestly”; differs because tampering suggests meddling by a stranger, while doctoring is a skilled alteration.
  • adulterated: Similar because both describe spoiled food or drink; differs because adulteration is about mixing in inferior substances, while doctoring is a wider kind of faking.
  • spiked: Similar because both describe secretly adding something to a drink; differs because spiking is usually adding drugs or alcohol, a narrower and more sinister act.
  • manipulated: Similar because both describe controlled alteration of evidence or media; differs because manipulation is broader and can be psychological, while doctoring is specifically the act of altering the object.
  • forged: Similar because both describe making something falsely; differs because forging creates a fake document or signature, while doctoring alters a real one.
  • neutered: Similar because both describe the veterinary sense of sterilising an animal; differs because neutering is the clinical term, while doctoring is the polite euphemism.
