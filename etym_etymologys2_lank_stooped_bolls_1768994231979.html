lank: PIE *kleng- “to bend, turn, flex” (c. 4500–2500 BCE) → Proto-Germanic *hlankaz / *hlanka- “slender, flexible, bent” (c. 500 BCE–200 CE) → Old English hlanc “loose, slender, empty, gaunt” (attested c. 1000) → Middle English lank “slender, thin, meager” (c. 1300) → English lank “thin and slack, limp” (modern). Note: the “bend” sense of the same root survives in the English flank (via Frankish *hlanka “side, hip” → Old French flanc) and probably in link, the chain-ring that bends and joins (grouped by the OED under the same *kleng- family). No secure Italic, Hellenic, Indo-Iranian, Balto-Slavic, or Celtic reflex is attested in the standard handbooks; the root is essentially Germanic-centered, and that limitation is stated here rather than hidden. stooped: PIE *stewp- / *steup- “to be bent, bow, crouch, stoop” (c. 4500–2500 BCE) → Proto-Germanic *stūpōną “to be bent, to stoop” (c. 500 BCE–200 CE) → Old English stūpian “to bow down, bend, stoop” (attested c. 1000) → Middle English stoupen “to bend forward, stoop, submit” (c. 1300) → English stoop “to bend the body forward and downward; to condescend” (modern) → the past participle stooped “bent forward, habitually folded” (adjectival use from the 16th c. onward). Note: the same root gives the falconer's stoop (a bird's swift downward swoop) and the sense of moral lowering (“to stoop to deceit”); the Germanic and Italic distribution is secure, with Baltic cognates debated. bolls: PIE *bʰel- “to swell, blow up, inflate” (c. 4500–2500 BCE) → Proto-Germanic *bullōn- / *bul- “rounded object, ball, swelling” (c. 500 BCE–200 CE) → Old English bolla “bowl, cup, round vessel” (attested c. 900) → Middle English bolle / boll “round vessel; by extension the round seed-pod or capsule of a plant” (the pod sense attested c. 1300) → English boll “the rounded seed-capsule of cotton or flax” (modern). The semantic bridge is pure roundness: the same swell-root gave ball, bowl, boll, and the Middle English shift from “round vessel” to “round seed-capsule” applies the shared image of a swollen globe. The root *bʰel- is securely attested and abundant across Indo-European (ball, bellow, bloom, blow, follicle), so this chain is well-supported.
