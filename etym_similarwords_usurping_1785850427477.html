• Seizing: Similar because both involve taking by force; differs because seizing is a general action (seizing an opportunity, seizing weapons), while usurping specifically means taking a legitimate title, power, or authority unlawfully.
  • Arrogating: Similar because both involve claiming without right; differs because arrogating means claiming a right or privilege for oneself haughtily, while usurping involves actively ousting the rightful holder.
  • Appropriating: Similar because both mean taking for oneself; differs because appropriating can be done legally or quietly (appropriating funds), while usurping is a high-profile violation of sovereign or rightful authority.
  • Supplanting: Similar because both involve replacing another; differs because supplanting can occur naturally or through superior competition, whereas usurping requires wrongful or illegal force.
  • Wresting: Similar because both describe forcible seizure; differs because wresting highlights physical twisting effort (wresting a weapon away), while usurping focuses on taking over formal positions or power.
  • Encroaching: Similar because both involve intruding on rights; differs because encroaching is gradual step-by-step infringement, while usurping is a total takeover of authority.
  • Commandeering: Similar because both involve taking over what is not one's own; differs because commandeering is usually authorized for wartime use (commandeering a vehicle), while usurping is always wrongful.
  • Hijacking: Similar because both describe forcible takeover; differs because hijacking is sudden, violent seizure of a vehicle or system, while usurping is often gradual and political.
  • Expropriating: Similar because both involve taking property; differs because expropriating can be done lawfully by the state with compensation, while usurping is inherently illegal.
  • Taking over: Similar because both mean assuming control; differs because taking over is neutral and informal (a new CEO taking over), while usurping carries the accusation of illegitimacy.
  • Annexing: Similar because both describe absorbing territory or power; differs because annexing is usually a state act of territorial seizure, while usurping concerns office, right, or authority.
  • Presuming: Similar because both involve assuming what one is not entitled to; differs because presuming emphasizes audacity in claiming (presuming to judge), while usurping emphasizes forcible displacement of the rightful holder.
