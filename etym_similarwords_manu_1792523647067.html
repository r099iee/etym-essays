• Noah: Similar because both are the celebrated righteous patriarchs in world flood mythologies who build a colossal vessel, preserve pairs of living creatures and seeds of life from a universal cataclysmic deluge, and repopulate the earth; differs because Noah belongs to Abrahamic biblical monotheism and covenants with Yahweh, whereas Manu is guided by Lord Viṣṇu's horned fish avatar (*Matsya*), rules as an imperial king, and founds the great Solar and Lunar royal dynasties.
  • Adam: Similar because both are revered as the first man and primal biological progenitor of the human race; differs because Adam was fashioned from the dust of the ground in the Garden of Eden and fell from grace through original sin, whereas Manu is a majestic sovereign, flood survivor, ascetic priest, and cosmic lawgiver who instituted *dharma*.
  • Prajāpati: Similar because both are ancestral fathers associated with cosmic generation; differs because Prajāpati is a transcendent, divine demiurge and Vedic creator god who emanates creatures from ascetic heat (*tapas*), whereas Manu is the human archetype, earthly monarch, and survivor-founder of civilized society.
  • Ikṣvāku: Similar because both are foundational ancestral sovereigns of Ayodhyā in the Solar dynasty; differs because Ikṣvāku was the eldest son of Manu who served as the first formal monarch to reign directly over Ayodhyā and give his name to the royal clan (*Ikṣvāku-vaṃśa*), whereas Manu is his divine father and the original builder of the metropolis.
  • Utnapishtim: Similar because in ancient Mesopotamian mythology (the Epic of Gilgamesh) Utnapishtim is the righteous hero who built a great boat to preserve life from an all-destroying flood; differs because Utnapishtim was granted immortal godhood by the gods and withdrew forever to the mouth of the rivers, whereas Manu remained the active legal, political, and cultural founder of human civilization.
  • Deucalion: Similar because in Greek mythology Deucalion (son of Prometheus) survived Zeus's cosmic deluge in a chest and repopulated humanity by casting stones behind him; differs because Deucalion's narrative lacks the grand cyclical Manvantara cosmogony and the comprehensive legal treatises associated with Manu.
  • Mānavendra: Similar because it is an epic royal epithet applied directly to Manu in the Rāmāyaṇa; differs because Mānavendra is a descriptive compound title meaning "Lord / Indra of Men (Mānavas)", whereas Manu is the foundational proper name.
  • Purūravas: Similar because both are legendary ancestral monarchs of great antiquity; differs because Purūravas is the grandson of Manu (born of his daughter Ilā) who founded the Lunar dynasty and is famous for his passionate, tragic romance with the celestial nymph Urvaśī, whereas Manu is the universal lawgiver and city-builder.
  • Mānava: Similar because both derive directly from the same linguistic base; differs because Mānava is a patronymic common noun and adjective meaning "a human being, descendant of Manu, mortal", whereas Manu is the primal ancestor himself.
  • Minos: Similar because in Greek mythology King Minos of Crete is the legendary ancestral king and supreme lawgiver who received statutory laws directly from Zeus and became an eternal judge of souls, paralleling Manu's legal reception from Brahmā; differs because Minos is intimately tied to the Aegean myth of the Minotaur and labyrinth.
  • Yama: Similar because in early Vedic mythology Yama and Manu are twin brothers (both sons of Vivasvān); differs because Yama was the first mortal to die and pioneer the path to the afterlife, becoming the sovereign Lord of Death and Justice in the netherworld, whereas Manu remained the living father of terrestrial humanity.
  • Solon: Similar because both are venerated in their respective civilizations as foundational constitutional lawgivers; differs because Solon was a historical 6th-century BCE Athenian statesman and poet who reformed Athenian democracy, whereas Manu is an ancient mythological progenitor and cosmic lawgiver.
