• Murals: Similar because both refer to large-scale artworks executed directly upon permanent architectural wall surfaces; differs because mural is an all-encompassing generic umbrella term for any painting on a wall (regardless of medium, including oil, tempera, ceramic tile, or spray paint), whereas frescoes specifically denote paintings chemically bound to damp lime plaster.
  • Wall Paintings: Similar because both denote physical imagery executed onto interior or exterior walls; differs because wall painting is an everyday descriptive phrase that lacks the precise technical chemistry and art-historical prestige associated with lime-carbonation frescoes.
  • Fresco-secco: Similar because both involve the decoration of lime-plastered walls within ecclesiastical and civic buildings; differs because fresco-secco is painted onto dry, cured plaster using organic binders (such as egg, glue, or oil), making it far more prone to peeling and flaking than true crystalline buon fresco.
  • Sinopie: Similar because both are integral physical components of monumental Italian wall-painting projects; differs because a sinopia is the preparatory monochromatic underdrawing sketched in reddish-brown earth pigment upon the rough arriccio plaster coat, which is subsequently hidden beneath the final painted fresco layer.
  • Mosaics: Similar because both are permanent, monumental decorative media applied directly to interior architectural walls, vaults, and domes; differs because mosaics are constructed by setting thousands of individual colored glass, stone, or ceramic tesserae into mortar, whereas frescoes are painted with liquid pigment on plaster.
  • Tapestries: Similar because both were historically deployed in grand churches, palaces, and halls to cover vast masonry expanses with narrative or biblical iconography; differs because tapestries are heavy, woven portable textile hangings that provide thermal insulation, whereas frescoes are immovable, structural masonry arts.
  • Panoramas: Similar because both present grand, continuous, sweeping visual narratives across large spatial dimensions; differs because panoramas are typically 360-degree cylindrical canvasses or wide horizontal landscapes designed for popular public exhibition, whereas frescoes are integrated architectural adornments.
  • Encaustic Paintings: Similar because both are ancient, highly durable methods of wall and panel decoration; differs because encaustic involves applying pigments mixed with molten beeswax and burning them in with heat, a method favored in Greco-Roman Egypt rather than lime plaster carbonation.
  • Distemper Murals: Similar because both are water-based wall decorations; differs because distemper relies on animal glue (collagen) or chalk binders on plaster or canvas, which dissolve easily in humid environments, unlike the waterproof calcium-carbonate matrix of true frescoes.
