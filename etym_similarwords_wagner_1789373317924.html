• Ruskinian: Similar because both John Ruskin and Richard Wagner were foundational nineteenth-century artistic influences upon Marcel Proust and European modernism; differs because Ruskinian emphasizes moral aesthetics, the spiritual sanctity of manual craftsmanship, Gothic architectural fidelity, and reverent observation of natural details, whereas Wagner represents intoxicating musical ecstasy, mythic drama, and the total sensory immersion of the *Gesamtkunstwerk*.
  • Bergsonian: Similar because both French philosopher Henri Bergson and Richard Wagner revolutionized modern conceptions of subjective time, memory, and emotional consciousness; differs because Bergsonian denotes formal philosophical concepts of intuition, vital impulse (*élan vital*), and indivisible continuous duration (*la durée*), whereas Wagner embodies the artistic and compositional realization of those internal psychological states through sound and leitmotif.
  • Debussyan: Similar because both Claude Debussy and Richard Wagner revolutionized modern orchestral harmony and deeply inspired Symbolist and modernist writers; differs because Debussyan music represents a deliberate aesthetic reaction against Wagnerian bombast, colossal myth, and heavy orchestration, choosing instead delicate impressionistic nuance, subtle modal coloring, light, and understated atmospheric mystery.
  • Schopenhauerian: Similar because philosopher Arthur Schopenhauer and Richard Wagner shared an intimate intellectual bond, with Schopenhauer's metaphysics of the Will serving as the primary philosophical foundation for Wagner's *Tristan und Isolde*; differs because Schopenhauerian denotes the pessimistic philosophical system advocating ascetic detachment and renunciation of worldly desire, whereas Wagner translated that metaphysical longing into intoxicating sensory and musical indulgence.
  • Mallarméan: Similar because both French poet Stéphane Mallarmé and Richard Wagner sought a supreme, transcendent aesthetic synthesis to replace conventional religious faith; differs because Mallarméan operates within the rarefied, hermetic medium of French Symbolist verse and typographical blank space on the printed page, whereas Wagner commands monumental physical stages, giant orchestras, and epic mythic sagas.
  • Operatic: Similar because both relate to grand musical drama staged with singers and orchestral scoring; differs because operatic is a broad, generic classification encompassing everything from comic Italian bel canto to light operetta, often used colloquially to imply artificial melodrama, whereas Wagner specifically denotes through-composed, philosophical music drama built upon continuous symphonic leitmotifs.
