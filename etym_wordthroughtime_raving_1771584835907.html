- [14th century: to wander in mind] - 'Rave' enters English meaning to be deranged, to roam in delirium; 'raving' describes the wild, senseless speech of madness; connotation is plain and alarming - the outward sign of a mind unhinged.
- [16th-18th century: intensifier of extremes] - 'Raving' hardens into an intensifier: 'raving mad', 'raving lunatic' - utterly, spectacularly mad; the word also slips into admiration, a 'raving beauty' being one so dazzling it borders on the unbelievable; connotation swings between fearful excess and extravagant praise.
- [Contemporary sense] - A participle-adjective for loud, incoherent, apparently senseless speech, and an intensifier of extremes ('raving lunatic', 'raving beauty'); connotation is of emotional overdrive - a mind or a passion running too fast for the world to follow.
