- In Middle English the verb 'find' gains the sense 'to provide, supply, furnish,' so that a master 'finds' his servants in meat and lodging; out of its past participle 'found' grows a noun for what is provided.
- In the 19th century this noun steps onto the labour market: to hire on 'for wages and found' is the standard frontier and farm bargain, where a worker's keep — meals and a bed — is thrown in as part of the pay.
- Through the late 19th and 20th centuries the usage hardens into the fossil phrases 'board and found,' 'room and found' and 'wages and found,' even as industrial wage-work makes the old hiring bargain obsolete.
- In the present the noun is archaic and historical, a mass word from farm diaries and frontier novels, and it keeps a quiet pun: your provisions are what your employer has 'found' (provided) you in.
