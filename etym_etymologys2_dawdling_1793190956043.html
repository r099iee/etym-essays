Early Modern English (c. 1650s)
dawdle
‘to trifle, loiter, waste time’
↓
Modern English (18th c. onward)
dawdle + -ing
‘loitering’ + continuous suffix
‘ongoing idle slowness’
↓
Modern English (current)
dawdling
‘moving slowly; wasting time’

Note: the origin of dawdle itself is unknown; older guesses linking it with daddle, to toddle, or with Low German forms are unproven, so the chain honestly begins at the first attested English form with no invented PIE root.
