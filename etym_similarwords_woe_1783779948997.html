• Sorrow: Similar because both denote deep sadness; differs because sorrow is an internal emotional state, while woe encompasses external afflictions, grief, and the cry of lamentation.
  • Misery: Similar in great distress; differs because misery stresses wretched physical or mental discomfort, whereas woe carries tragic, solemn, or poetic weight.
  • Anguish: Similar in acute suffering; differs because anguish emphasizes intense physical or mental torment at a moment, while woe is an enduring condition of grief and misfortune.
  • Grief: Similar because both name profound sorrow, often at a loss; differs because grief is the modern everyday word for mourning, while woe is older, more diffuse (misfortune plus sorrow), and more archaic or literary.
  • Distress: Similar in trouble and suffering; differs because distress is more neutral and clinical, describing pressure or hardship, without woe's lamenting, solemn tone.
  • Calamity: Similar in disaster and misfortune; differs because calamity names a sudden catastrophic event, whereas woe names the grief and affliction themselves, not just the event.
  • Affliction: Similar in suffering and trouble; differs because affliction emphasizes a burden imposed on someone, while woe can be either the inner feeling or the outward misfortune.
  • Lament: Similar in the cry of grief; differs because lament is the act or expression of mourning (noun and verb), while woe is the grief and misfortune themselves.
  • Alas: Similar as the interjectional cry of sorrow; differs because alas is purely an interjection, while woe is chiefly a noun, with the interjection as an archaic offshoot.
