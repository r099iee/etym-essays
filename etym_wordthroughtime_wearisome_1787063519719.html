- Late 1300s: “causing weariness, exhausting” - a plain compound of “weary” + “-some”, used of hard labour and long journeys
- 1400s–1500s: extended from the body to the mind - wearisome talk, wearisome disputes - the word begins to mean tedious as well as tiring
- 1500s–1600s: could also be applied to the person (“I am wearisome” = I am weary) - a looser, now archaic use
- 1600s–1800s: settles on the cause, not the sufferer - wearisome is a quality of things, people, and speech that drain us
- 1800s–present: predominantly mental - “wearisome” now usually means tedious, dreary, and patience-draining, with bodily exhaustion as the older undertone
- In modern narrative prose it is a workhorse of dreary atmosphere - “lonesome and wearisome”, the slow drain of waiting
