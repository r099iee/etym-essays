• Scrutinized: Similar because both denote close, searching, and careful examination; differs because scrutinize carries an inherent skepticism or investigative intensity, looking specifically for defects, fraud, or hidden discrepancies, whereas traditional peruse focuses on comprehensive, linear reading from beginning to end.
  • Examined: Similar because both involve inspecting material thoroughly; differs because examine is a broad, neutral workhorse term applicable to physical bodies, machinery, evidence, and abstract concepts, whereas peruse is primarily restricted to written texts, documents, or visual surfaces.
  • Studied: Similar because both denote sustained mental engagement with a text; differs because study emphasizes intellectual comprehension, memorization, and mastery of concepts for future recall, whereas peruse describes the physical and critical act of reading through the text itself.
  • Inspected: Similar because both convey deliberate visual checking; differs because inspect carries an official, institutional, or regulatory authority (as in inspecting military troops or building works), whereas peruse conveys scholarly, bibliographical, or private readerly attention.
  • Pored over: Similar because both imply intense, absorbed reading; differs because pore over evokes physical proximity, squinting concentration, and prolonged labor over difficult, minute, or ancient details, whereas peruse implies an orderly, structured review.
  • Browsed: Similar because in modern colloquial usage both words can refer to looking over reading material at leisure; differs because historically and formally they are polar opposites—browse literally means to feed lightly on vegetation and implies selective, wandering, non-sequential tasting, whereas traditional perusal requires unbroken, complete scrutiny.
  • Skimmed: Similar because colloquial modern speakers sometimes use peruse loosely to mean glancing through; differs because skim specifically means reading rapidly to capture the gist while skipping substantial detail, representing the precise antithesis of traditional thorough perusal.
  • Scanned: Similar because both describe reading a text; differs because scan often involves searching specifically for a targeted keyword, date, or metric (or metrically analyzing poetry), whereas peruse involves taking in the entirety of the text.
  • Vetted: Similar because both entail evaluating documents or information before making a decision; differs because vet specifically denotes checking background, pedigree, security clearance, or factual accuracy for approval, whereas peruse is the reading process itself.
  • Read through: Similar because both signify following a text from start to finish; differs because read through is neutral, informal, and plain in register, whereas peruse carries formal, dignified, and pedantic literary weight.
