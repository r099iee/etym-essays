• stained: Similar because both mean marked or discoloured; differs because stained comes from many agents (wine, blood, dye) and is neutral-to-descriptive, while blotted is ink-centred and implies smudging.
  • smeared: Similar because both describe spreading a mark; differs because smeared emphasises rubbing and spreading (paint, grease), while blotted emphasises an absorbed or dropped stain — and blotting also means dabbing dry.
  • spotted: Similar because both describe marks on a surface; differs because spotted is about discrete small marks, often patterned, while blotted is about irregular absorbed stains.
  • splotched: Similar because both mean marked with irregular blotches; differs because splotched is colloquial and purely visual, while blotted carries the ink-and-erasure history.
  • erased: Similar because both mean removed; differs because erasing is rubbing out (an eraser), while blotted out means covering or obliterating, often wholesale destruction.
  • obliterated: Similar because both mean destroyed utterly; differs because obliterated (from Latin oblitterare) is total and violent — nothing remains — while blotted out may be a mere concealment.
  • effaced: Similar because both mean wiped away; differs because effaced is formal and implies deliberate self-removal or wearing away, while blotted is physical and ink-related.
  • defaced: Similar because both mean damaged in appearance; differs because defaced implies vandalism of surfaces (walls, coins), while blotted implies ink-based or accidental marking.
  • sullied: Similar because both can mean morally tarnished; differs because sullied is purely figurative (reputation), while blotted can be literal or figurative.
  • tarnished: Similar because both describe loss of brightness or purity; differs because tarnished is used of metals and reputations, while blotted is used of paper and records.
  • obscured: Similar because both mean made hard to see; differs because obscured is neutral (fog, shadow), while blotted out implies deliberate or complete concealment.
