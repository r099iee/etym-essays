- Early port and frontier (9th–16th c.) - Arab, Bengali, and Arakanese traders know it as a great eastern harbor and forest frontier; perception is cosmopolitan gateway plus wild hinterland
- Mughal–Portuguese rivalry (16th–17th c.) - Called Islamabad by Mughals and Porto Grande by Portuguese; European perception is fabulously rich, pirate-haunted trading prize
- British district and hill dependency (1760–1947) - Anglicized to Chittagong; lowland port plus administered Hill Tracts; imperial perception splits between commercial asset and ethnographic curiosity about communal hill tribes as in the source
- Partition and East Pakistan era (1947–1971) - Eastern border port of Pakistan, industrialized; perception is strategic lifeline and site of 1930 Armoury Raid nationalist memory
- Bangladesh present (1971–today) - Second city and booming container port, officially Chattogram since 2018; contemporary perception is economic engine, ship-breaking coast, and sensitive hill-politics zone; older tribal-collective label now seen as dated
