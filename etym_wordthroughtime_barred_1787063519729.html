- 13th century: "barred" = fastened or bolted with bars - purely physical securing of doors and windows.
- 14th-15th centuries: = blocked, obstructed (roads, gates, passes) - a barrier placed across the way.
- 16th century: = excluded, forbidden (persons barred from places, rights, office) - the physical bar becomes an institutional prohibition.
- 17th century onward: = marked with bars or stripes - heraldry, textiles, sky and plumage description; the visual sense crystallises beside the physical and legal ones.
- 19th-20th centuries: biology (barred owl, barred plumage) and later astronomy (barred spiral galaxy) adopt the striped sense; law grows "time-barred," "called to the bar," "barred from practice."
- 21st century: the striped sense thrives in nature writing and design; the prohibition sense is standard in law and journalism - "barred" is three words in one.
