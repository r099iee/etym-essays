- Early 1900s hobo slang (US) - Purely literal: a cloth-wrapped bundle of a drifter's possessions, slung over the shoulder; utilitarian and matter-of-fact.

- Great Depression (1930s) - Becomes an emblem of itinerant migrant labor; the bindle-stiff carrying all his worldly goods is read with equal parts pity, grit, and romanticized wanderlust.

- Mid-20th century onward - Hobo culture fades, so "bindle" survives mainly as a literary/historical marker of Depression-era fiction (Steinbeck) and as nostalgic hobo slang; in computing it was borrowed as the name of a small, portable data container.
