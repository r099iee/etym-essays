• Rare: Similar because both denote uncommon occurrence; differs because rare emphasizes exceptional, precious unusualness, while scarce stresses a shortage of supply relative to demand.
  • Uncommon: Similar because both mean not frequently found; differs because uncommon is a neutral statistical descriptor with no implication of insufficiency, whereas scarce implies too few to satisfy need.
  • Meager: Similar because both indicate small amount; differs because meager describes the thin, sparse provision of what exists, while scarce focuses on overall short supply.
  • Scant: Similar because both mean barely enough; differs because scant is a near-synonym emphasizing clipped, limited measure, often attributive ("scant regard"), while scarce more strongly implies shortage in the wider market.
  • Sparse: Similar because both mean few in number; differs because sparse describes thin distribution across space, not necessarily insufficiency of supply.
  • Insufficient: Similar because both mean not enough; differs because insufficient is a formal term measuring shortfall against a standard, while scarce emphasizes difficulty of finding.
  • Deficient: Similar because both mean lacking; differs because deficient implies falling below a required standard or quality, whereas scarce implies a quantity problem.
  • Inadequate: Similar because both mean insufficient for purpose; differs because inadequate is about capacity or quality, while scarce is about limited supply.
  • Limited: Similar because both mean restricted in amount; differs because limited is neutral (a limited edition is deliberate), while scarce carries an implied shortfall or desperation.
  • Wanting: Similar because both mean lacking; differs because wanting describes deficiency in quality or behavior, while scarce describes physical scarcity of goods.
  • In short supply: Similar because both mean scarce in quantity; differs because this phrase is a modern economic idiom, more informal and concrete than scarce.
  • Exiguous: Similar because both mean extremely small in amount; differs because exiguous is a rare, learned word for diminutive quantity, without market implications.
  • Depleted: Similar because both imply reduced availability; differs because depleted stresses that supply was exhausted or drawn down, whereas scarce describes the resulting state.
  • Stingy (older sense): Similar because Old French escars also meant parsimonious; differs because stingy now names a person’s refusal to give, a sense scarce has lost.
  • Few: Similar because both mean small in number; differs because few is a neutral quantifier without the need-versus-supply tension of scarce.
