• Ruling: Similar because both denote the exercise of supreme power; differs because ruling stresses active government over subjects and can apply to any authority, while reigning stresses occupancy of the throne.
  • Governing: Similar because both are concerned with authority over a state; differs because governing is the administrative conduct of affairs, applying to presidents and ministers as much as kings, without the royal register.
  • Incumbent: Similar because both mean currently holding an office; differs because incumbent is the modern bureaucratic word for any post-holder, far too colourless for a dynasty and throne.
  • On the throne: Similar because both denote a monarch presently in office; differs because the idiom is periphrastic, more casual and narrative, while reigning is the formal attributive adjective.
  • In power: Similar because both denote current authority; differs because in power is general and applies to any government or party, while reigning is reserved for the crown.
  • Prevailing: Similar because both denote what currently holds sway; differs because prevailing is used of opinions, winds, or fashions, while reigning retains its royal, title-holding colour.
  • Dominant: Similar because both denote what is foremost at a given time; differs because dominant stresses actual superiority or control, while reigning stresses the formal title of the present occupant.
  • Current: Similar because both mean present and operative now; differs because current is entirely neutral and non-royal, while reigning carries the crown and the throne.
  • Regnant: Similar because both denote a monarch in office (as in "queen regnant"); differs because regnant is the technical, chiefly historical adjective for a reigning sovereign, while reigning is the ordinary, widely used word.
  • Chief: Similar because both denote the person at the head; differs because chief is generic and applies to any leader or head, while reigning specifically denotes the present occupant of the throne.
