• Newmarket: Similar because both are inextricably linked to elite British horse racing; differs because Newmarket is the specific market town and racecourse headquarters (the "Home of Horseracing"), whereas Cambridgeshire is the entire surrounding county and the specific name of the historic autumn handicap race.
  • Cesarewitch: Similar because both are prestigious historic autumn handicap races run at Newmarket; differs because the Cesarewitch is a long-distance staying race (over 2 miles and 2 furlongs), whereas the Cambridgeshire is a middle-distance sprint-staying test (1 mile and 1 furlong), together forming the famed "Autumn Double."
  • East Anglia: Similar because Cambridgeshire is located within this geographic region; differs because East Anglia is a broad cultural and historical realm encompassing Norfolk, Suffolk, and parts of Essex, whereas Cambridgeshire is a specific legal and ceremonial county.
  • The Fens: Similar because fenland terrain covers a major portion of northern Cambridgeshire; differs because the Fens refers strictly to the geographic and ecological wetland basin spanning four counties, while Cambridgeshire includes upland chalk downs and urban academic centers.
  • Derby: Similar because both are iconic British horse races named after English counties or noble titles; differs because the Derby at Epsom is a Group 1 classic for three-year-olds at set weights, whereas the Cambridgeshire is a massive handicap race open to horses of multiple ages.
