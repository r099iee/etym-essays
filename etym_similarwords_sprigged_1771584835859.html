• Flowered: Similar because both describe cloth or surfaces ornamented with floral motifs; differs because flowered is the general, untechnical word for any flower-patterned cloth, while sprigged specifically names small, scattered sprig or twig sprays, delicate and light.
  • Embroidered: Similar because both describe decoratively ornamented fabric; differs because embroidered means needlework stitched onto the finished cloth, while a sprigged design is usually woven or printed into the textile itself, and embroidery is only one possible technique.
  • Printed: Similar because both describe a pattern applied to fabric by a mechanical transfer; differs because printed is the broad modern term for any stamped or transferred design, while sprigged carries the precise, old-fashioned image of small floral sprigs, which may be woven or printed.
  • Patterned: Similar because both describe a surface bearing a repeated design; differs because patterned is the neutral, technical umbrella term that specifies arrangement without naming any particular motif, while sprigged narrows the field to small, scattered, sprig-like flora.
  • Chintzed: Similar because both describe glazed cotton furnishing fabrics with floral designs; differs because chintz is specifically the glazed, usually colourful, printed cotton of Indian origin, while sprigged describes the motif (small sprigs) and can apply to muslin, linen, or silk without the glazed finish.
  • Dotted: Similar because both describe a surface covered with small repeated marks; differs because dotted refers to round points or spots, while sprigged refers to small twig- or flower-shaped motifs, organic rather than geometric.
  • Scattered: Similar because both convey the idea of things spread loosely over a surface; differs because scattered is a general verb participle describing any loose distribution, while sprigged is a specific textile term for a floral ornament of small sprigs.
  • Budded: Similar because both evoke small vegetable forms used as ornament; differs because budded suggests unopened flower buds, while sprigged suggests whole little sprays of leaves and flowers — a shoot, not a bud.
