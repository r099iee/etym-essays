- 18th century: Pushed roughly or compelled to move along with urgent speed and force - Connoted roughhandling, crowd turbulence, and forceful eviction.
- Early to Mid 19th century: Pickpocketed or swindled through deliberate crowd-jostling and street trickery - Acquired a distinct London underworld criminal connotation.
- Late 19th to Early 20th century: Worked vigorously, pursued commerce aggressively, or applied relentless energetic drive - Underwent Americanization, transforming into a celebrated badge of commercial diligence and competitive sportsmanship.
- Mid to Late 20th century: Engaged in informal, illicit street economies, pool-hall sharking, or sex work - Retained a countercultural, gritty urban edge of survivalism.
- 21st century: Pursued multiple side jobs, entrepreneurship, and hyper-productivity ('hustle culture') - Became ubiquitous in digital business parlance, representing energetic self-reliance while occasionally drawing critique for fostering workaholic burnout.
