- Mid-17th century (c. 1650s) - Borrowed from French meaning 'given freely, without charge,' an entirely positive word for gifts offered out of pure goodwill in legal, financial, and theological contexts
- 18th-19th century - Shifts from 'given without payment' to 'done without cause, justification, or necessity,' acquiring moral and aesthetic censure
- 19th-20th century - The pejorative sense becomes dominant: gratuitous violence, gratuitous nudity, gratuitous insult - additions that are unprovoked and 'add nothing but noise'
- Modern - Two-tier register: the neutral 'free of charge' sense survives in technical and legal documents, while in ordinary and academic speech the word is overwhelmingly condemnatory
