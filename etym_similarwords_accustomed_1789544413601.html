• Habituated: Similar because both denote becoming adapted or accustomed to a particular condition or environment; differs because habituated often carries a clinical, psychological, or physiological connotation (such as habituation to a drug or sensory stimulus), whereas accustomed is a broader, natural social and personal descriptor.
  • Used (to): Similar because both express familiarity gained through repeated experience; differs because used to is an informal, colloquial construction, whereas accustomed represents the formal, literary, and elevated equivalent.
  • Acclimated / Acclimatized: Similar because both describe adjusting to new circumstances; differs because acclimated specifically refers to physiological adaptation to new climates, altitudes, temperatures, or environmental conditions.
  • Inured: Similar because both describe becoming hardened or accustomed through long exposure; differs because inured specifically implies becoming desensitized to painful, harsh, unpleasant, or grueling conditions (e.g. inured to suffering or cold).
  • Customary: Similar because both derive from the noun custom and describe established practices; differs because customary is applied primarily to actions, traditions, ceremonies, or rules ("it is customary to bow"), whereas accustomed can describe both the habitual state of a person ("he was accustomed") and the familiar object ("his accustomed place").
  • Wonted: Similar because both describe habitual, customary practices or states; differs because wonted is an archaic and purely literary adjective ("his wonted good humor") lacking an active participial verbal form.
  • Familiarized: Similar because both involve gaining familiarity with a subject or setting; differs because familiarized implies a deliberate process of acquiring knowledge or acquaintance rather than organic habit formation.
