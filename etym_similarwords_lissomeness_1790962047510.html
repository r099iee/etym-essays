• Litheness: Similar because both represent the exact same core quality of slender, supple physical grace; differs because 'litheness' is the direct older noun derived from Old English līðe, whereas 'lissomeness' is an extended poetic variant formed from the contracted adjective lissome (lithesome), carrying a slightly more playful, buoyant, or literary aesthetic.
  • Suppleness: Similar because both denote flexibility and lack of stiffness; differs because 'suppleness' (from Latin supplex, 'bending under') can apply to materials, leather, wood, and skin as well as bodies, whereas 'lissomeness' specifically describes living human or animal agility and graceful movement.
  • Limberness: Similar because both refer to the ability to bend the body easily without injury; differs because 'limberness' emphasizes athletic warm-ups, muscle elasticity, and physical readiness, whereas 'lissomeness' emphasizes aesthetic beauty, elegance, and lightness of form.
  • Agility: Similar because both involve ease of physical movement; differs because 'agility' emphasizes quickness of reflexes, rapid change of direction, and mental/physical speed, whereas 'lissomeness' focuses primarily on fluid flexibility, graceful posture, and slender suppleness.
  • Flexibility: Similar because both describe the capacity to bend; differs because 'flexibility' is a general, neutral, and scientific term applicable to joints, polymers, schedules, and rules, whereas 'lissomeness' is evocative, sensory, and poetic.
  • Gracefulness: Similar because both convey aesthetic beauty in movement; differs because 'gracefulness' is a broad aesthetic quality that can exist without extreme physical flexibility, whereas 'lissomeness' specifically requires lithe bodily suppleness.
  • Nimbleness: Similar because both denote light, effortless motion; differs because 'nimbleness' emphasizes dexterity of fingers, precision of footwork, and clever lightness, whereas 'lissomeness' emphasizes flowing full-body bendability.
  • Pliantness: Similar because both refer to the capacity to bend easily; differs because 'pliantness' often carries connotations of yielding to external pressure or being easily manipulated/molded, whereas 'lissomeness' denotes active, self-directed athletic grace.
