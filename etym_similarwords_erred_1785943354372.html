• Blundered: Similar because both mean making a mistake; differs because blundered implies a clumsy, stupid, or high-profile mistake, while erred is dignified, formal, and morally or intellectually neutral.
  • Miscalculated: Similar because both involve error in judgement; differs because miscalculated is specific to numbers, estimates, or planning, while erred covers any kind of straying from truth or right.
  • Strayed: Similar because both name wandering off a correct path; differs because strayed keeps the physical and literal sense of leaving a route or group, while erred emphasizes the mistake in judgement itself.
  • Sinned: Similar because both describe moral wrongdoing; differs because sinned specifically means violating divine or religious law, whereas erred covers logic, mathematics, and secular rules as well.
  • Transgressed: Similar because both name breaking a moral or legal boundary; differs because transgressed is deliberate overstepping of a rule, while erred may be an innocent or intellectual mistake.
  • Misjudged: Similar because both name a wrong assessment; differs because misjudged is limited to evaluating people, situations, or distances, while erred is the general term for any mistake.
  • Slipped up: Similar because both mean making a mistake; differs because slipped up is informal and suggests a small, careless lapse, whereas erred is formal and weighty.
  • Lapsed: Similar because both describe falling away from a standard; differs because lapsed suggests a temporary falling away (a lapse of attention, a lapsed Catholic), while erred is a single wrong action or judgement.
  • Faltered: Similar because both involve a misstep; differs because faltered implies losing strength or resolve and stumbling during an action, while erred means having taken a wrong turn.
  • Fumbled: Similar because both describe clumsy failure; differs because fumbled is physical and awkward handling, while erred is mental or moral and carries no clumsiness.
  • Went astray: Similar because both mean deviating from the right path; differs because went astray is broader and often physical or pastoral in tone, while erred is the formal verdict of having made a mistake.
  • Veered: Similar because both denote departing from a course; differs because veered is a neutral, physical change of direction with no error or blame attached, while erred implies a judgement of wrongness.
