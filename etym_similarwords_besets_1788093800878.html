• besiege: Similar because both mean surrounding a place or person from all sides with hostile intent; differs because besiege is primarily military (investing a fortress) and suggests deliberate, prolonged siege operations, while beset is broader and equally at home with abstract troubles.
  • assail: Similar because both mean attacking or attacking persistently; differs because assail focuses on the violent onset of the attack itself (words, blows), while beset stresses encirclement and continued pressure rather than a single assault.
  • beleaguer: Similar because both mean to lay siege to and press hard on all sides; differs because beleaguer is fully figurative in modern use and slightly bookish, often of people “beleaguered by problems”, whereas beset retains a trace of physical surrounding.
  • surround: Similar because both mean to enclose on all sides; differs because surround is a neutral, value-free verb for any encirclement, while beset implies a hostile, restrictive or oppressive encircler.
  • hem in (hem): Similar because both mean to confine by drawing close around; differs because hem in emphasises restriction of movement and escape, while beset adds the idea of persistent attack or harassment.
  • encircle / encompass: Similar because both mean to form a ring round something; differs because encircle and encompass are neutral geometric descriptions, whereas beset imports menace, pressure or affliction.
  • envelop: Similar because both mean to close completely around something; differs because envelop is soft and non-hostile (fog, silence envelops), while beset is aggressive and adversarial.
  • bedevil: Similar because both mean to trouble persistently; differs because bedevil suggests mischievous, capricious or demonic harassment and is informal, while beset suggests overwhelming, inescapable pressure and is formal.
  • plague: Similar because both mean to afflict or harass repeatedly; differs because plague implies recurrent, disease-like vexation and can be used lightly, while beset implies encirclement by circumstances beyond control.
  • harry: Similar because both mean to press upon and disturb persistently; differs because harry implies raiding, chasing and worrying (often of troops or animals), while beset implies being trapped at the centre of a ring.
  • harass: Similar because both mean to annoy or attack repeatedly; differs because harass indicates petty, deliberate and often personal annoyance, while beset has grander, graver weight — whole conditions, not single persons, beset one.
  • oppress: Similar because both mean to weigh down and distress; differs because oppress stresses crushing weight and injustice laid on the victim, while beset stresses surrounding pressure from every side.
  • torment: Similar because both mean to inflict suffering; differs because torment is intensely personal and mental, while beset describes the situation, not the inner pain, of being surrounded by ills.
  • badger: Similar because both mean to pester relentlessly; differs because badger is informal and implies questioning or nagging by people, while beset is formal and operates at the level of circumstance.
