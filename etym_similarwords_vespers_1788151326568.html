• evensong: similar because both are the evening office of prayer; differs because evensong is the Anglican service fusing vespers with compline (Cranmer, 1549), vespers the Roman Catholic/Lutheran office with its own psalmody and Magnificat.
  • evening prayer: similar because both denote dusk worship; differs because "evening prayer" is the generic modern name, "vespers" the technical office with fixed psalms, hymn, and canticle.
  • compline: similar because both close the liturgical day; differs because compline is the final office after vespers — the night prayer, shorter, quieter, and more penitential.
  • lucernarium: similar because both are the lamp-lighting prayer of evening; differs because the lucernarium is the ancient pre-office form from which vespers historically grew.
  • dusk service: similar because both occur at twilight; differs because "dusk service" is descriptive and occasional and lacks the canonical psalmody of vespers.
  • Magnificat: similar because both are central to evening worship; differs because the Magnificat is the canticle (Mary's song) sung within vespers, not the service itself.
  • mass / eucharist: similar because both are church services; differs because vespers is a daily office of praise without sacrifice, the mass a eucharistic celebration with communion.
