• Cut down: Similar because both denote severing the trunk or base of a plant to make it drop to the earth; differs because cut down is a generic, everyday phrasal verb applicable to small bushes, flowers, or budgets, whereas felled is a heavy, specialized verb carrying weight, technical forestry authority, or dramatic impact.
  • Hewn: Similar because both describe chopping timber or shaping wood with sharp blades; differs because hewn emphasizes the repetitive striking, cutting, and shaping of wood or stone into building materials (such as rough-hewn beams), whereas felled focuses strictly on the act of bringing the standing tree crashing to the ground.
  • Toppled: Similar because both describe an object falling from an upright position to the horizontal ground; differs because toppled emphasizes unstable balance, tipping over at the top, or being unseated by wind or structural failure, whereas felled strictly implies deliberate, forceful external action causing the fall.
  • Prostrated: Similar because both describe casting a body flat on the earth; differs because prostrated is a formal, psychological, or devotional term describing lying face-down in submission, reverence, or debilitating exhaustion, lacking the violent mechanical severing of felled.
  • Decapitated: Similar because both involve severing with a sharp instrument; differs because decapitated refers strictly to removing the head from the neck of a living being, whereas felled refers to dropping the entire structural body of a tree or fighter.
  • Demolished: Similar because both denote bringing down a large structure; differs because demolished applies to human-built architectural edifices, walls, or theories through tearing down or explosive destruction, whereas felled applies to organic trees or standing human combatants.
  • Floor / Floored: Similar because both describe knocking someone flat onto the ground; differs because floored is a colloquial boxing and conversational term describing being knocked down or utterly astonished/flabbergasted, whereas felled has higher literary and epic gravity.
  • Slain: Similar because both can describe killing or striking down an adversary; differs because slain denotes lethal combat resulting in death in heroic or formal registers, whereas felled focuses on the physical trajectory of dropping to the ground, whether fatal or non-fatal.
