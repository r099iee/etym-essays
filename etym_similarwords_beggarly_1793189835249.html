• Beggar-like: Similar because it is the plain paraphrase of beggarly, resembling a beggar; differs because beggar-like is a neutral descriptive compound, while beggarly adds centuries of moral sneer and pity.
  • Ragged: Similar because both picture shabby, torn, poverty-worn clothing and looks; differs because ragged describes the cloth itself (torn, frayed), while beggarly judges the whole condition — clothes, means, and spirit.
  • Shabby: Similar because both mean poor-looking, worn, and run-down in dress or surroundings; differs because shabby is gentler and broader (shabby-genteel respectability), while beggarly is harsher — street-level destitution.
  • Squalid: Similar because both evoke filthy, degraded poverty of lodgings and life; differs because squalid stresses dirt and degradation, while beggarly stresses meanness and insufficiency.
  • Paltry: Similar because both sneer at contemptibly small amounts — paltry sums, beggarly portions; differs because paltry is purely quantitative scorn, while beggarly drags in the whole beggar's dish of associations.
  • Meagre: Similar because both describe portions, wages, and fare too thin to satisfy; differs because meagre is lean and clinical (a meagre diet), while beggarly is moral and mocking.
  • Miserly: Similar because both describe a poverty of giving — the beggarly host and the miserly uncle are brothers; differs because miserly names the hoarding character, while beggarly names the shabby result.
  • Sordid: Similar because both condemn meanness that soils — sordid motives, beggarly excuses; differs because sordid stresses moral dirt (grubby gain), while beggarly stresses inadequacy and low station.
  • Ignoble: Similar because both deny dignity — the beggarly spirit and the ignoble act alike lack honour; differs because ignoble is abstract and high-register, while beggarly keeps one foot in the gutter.
