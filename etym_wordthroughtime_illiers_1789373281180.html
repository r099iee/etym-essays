- Gallo-Roman Era (4th–6th c.) - Known as *Hilariacum*, a rural estate dedicated to or founded by someone named Hilarius; perceived as a minor Gallo-Roman agricultural villa.
- Medieval Era (11th–15th c.) - Evolved into a fortified market town in the county of Chartres; centered on the parish church of Saint-Jacques along the pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela.
- 19th Century - Characterized as a sleepy, conservative provincial commune in the fertile Beauce; perceived by urban Parisians like the Prousts as a bastion of timeless French rural ritual.
- Early 20th Century - Recognized worldwide following the publication of *À la recherche du temps perdu* as the sacred biographical wellspring of Proust's fictional universe.
- 1971–Present - Formally renamed *Illiers-Combray*; celebrated globally as a literary sanctuary and premier site of modernist cultural pilgrimage.
