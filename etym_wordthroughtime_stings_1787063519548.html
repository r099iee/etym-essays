- Old English (pre-1100) - Strong verb *stingan* ("to prick, pierce, thrust with a weapon") and noun *stincg* covered both animal venom organs and piercing spear/dagger thrusts.
- Middle English (1100–1500) - Spelled *stingen* / *stynge*, retaining both the literal entomological venom puncture and the piercing thrust of military blades.
- Early Modern English (1500–1700) - Elevated in religious and dramatic literature (Shakespeare, King James Bible) to symbolize moral torment, remorse, and the venom of slander.
- 19th c. (Botanical & Naturalist classification) - Standardized in biology for urticating plant hairs (nettles) and cnidarian nematocysts.
- 20th c. (Criminal deception & police undercover ops) - Slang evolved from confidence scams to undercover deceptive police operations ("sting operation"), alongside general metaphorical usages.
