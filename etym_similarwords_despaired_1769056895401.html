• hopelessness: Similar in naming the loss of hope; differs because hopelessness is the clinical state — having no hope — while despair is the felt, agonised experience of that state.
  • despondency: Similar in being a sinking loss of spirit; differs because despondency can still flicker toward recovery, whereas despair is a settled conviction that the desired outcome will never come.
  • dejection: Similar in describing low, saddened spirits; differs because dejection is flat and passive, a mere drooping of mood, without despair's desperate, engulfing edge.
  • desperation: Similar in springing from the same loss of hope; differs because desperation is paradoxically despair plus energy — the reckless, feverish action that despair provokes, whereas despair itself is passive and still.
  • gloom: Similar in describing darkness of mood; differs because gloom is a diffuse, sometimes temperamental darkness, while despair is a focused, total absence of hope.
  • desolation: Similar in describing an utterly emptied state; differs because desolation is a condition of being laid waste or alone, while despair is specifically the loss of hope.
  • misery: Similar in describing intense unhappiness; differs because misery is suffering and wretchedness generally, which need not involve any loss of hope.
  • discouragement: Similar in being a loss of confidence; differs because discouragement is mild, temporary, and usually recoverable, whereas despair is complete and settled.
  • defeatism: Similar in accepting failure in advance; differs because defeatism is an attitude or doctrine — the expectation of defeat — while despair is the felt emotional collapse that may accompany it.
  • pessimism: Similar in expecting the worst; differs because pessimism is a worldview or habit of thought, often serene, while despair is an acute emotional state.
  • resignation: Similar in ceasing to struggle against fate; differs because resignation is calm acceptance, even peace, whereas despair is anguished hopelessness.
  • dismay: Similar in being a sudden loss of confidence; differs because dismay is a startled, momentary alarm, while despair is a deep, abiding loss of hope.
  • anguish: Similar in describing intense mental suffering; differs because anguish is the extreme pain itself, which may coexist with hope, while despair is the extinction of hope.
  • give up: Similar in abandoning effort or hope; differs because to give up is an act of surrender, possibly pragmatic, while to despair is an emotional state of hopelessness that may or may not end in surrender.
  • lose heart: Similar in losing the courage to continue; differs because losing heart is a wavering of courage that can return, whereas despair is the settled absence of hope.
  • abandon hope: Similar in relinquishing expectation; differs because abandoning hope can be a deliberate decision, while despairing is being overtaken by hopelessness.
