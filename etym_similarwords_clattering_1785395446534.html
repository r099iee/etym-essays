• Rattle: Similar because it names a rapid series of short, sharp, percussive sounds, exactly the sound-field of clatter; differs because a rattle is thinner, more mechanical, and more evenly spaced, and the noun also names a toy and a snake, which clatter never does.
  • Clank: Similar because both belong to the family of striking-metal sounds; differs because a clank is a single heavier, duller, lower note of metal on metal, whereas clatter is a light, hollow, multiple percussion.
  • Clang: Similar because both name metallic percussion; differs because a clang is one loud, resonant, prolonged ring rather than a rapid busy series of knocks.
  • Clash: Similar because both describe hard objects striking together; differs because a clash implies a sharper, more violent collision, often with conflict or opposition behind it, while clatter is mere noisy contact without force or hostility.
  • Clack: Similar because of the shared sound-symbolic onset and the idea of a hard, sharp knock; differs because a clack is a single dry sound or the loose chattering of a valve, not a sustained bombardment.
  • Bang: Similar because both are noises of hard impact; differs because a bang is one loud explosive report, while clatter is a rapid, continuous, lighter succession.
  • Crash: Similar because both can describe falling objects making noise; differs because a crash is a louder, more shattering single impact, the breaking of something large, where clatter is the repeated light percussion of many small things.
  • Chatter: Similar because both are rapid, repeated, imitative sounds; differs because chatter is applied to teeth, birds, and speech, implying smallness and agitation, while clatter implies hard, hollow objects striking.
  • Racket: Similar because both denote a loud, confused din; differs because racket is a general word for any unpleasant noise, while clatter names a specific hollow, percussive sound of hard things meeting.
