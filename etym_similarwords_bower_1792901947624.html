• Arbour: Similar because both are leafy garden shelters of trained branches; differs because arbour is the gardener's structural word, often of latticework, while bower is the poet's word, wilder and more romantic.
  • Gazebo: Similar because both are garden retreats for sitting in shade; differs because a gazebo is a built pavilion with a roof and view, architectural and open, while a bower is embowered, enclosed in living green.
  • Alcove: Similar because both are small recessed retreats set apart; differs because an alcove is indoors, a niche in a wall, while a bower belongs to woods and gardens, roofed with leaves.
  • Chamber: Similar because the lady's bower is exactly a private chamber; differs because chamber is neutral and general, any room, while bower adds intimacy, femininity, and medieval romance.
  • Boudoir: Similar because both name a lady's private withdrawing room; differs because boudoir is French, fashionable, and faintly risqué, while bower is native, innocent, and ballad-scented.
  • Retreat: Similar because both are places of withdrawal from the world; differs because retreat stresses the act of going apart, even military or spiritual, while bower stresses the leafy embrace itself.
  • Nook: Similar because both are cosy sheltered corners; differs because a nook is small-scale and domestic, chimney nooks and breakfast nooks, while a bower can roof a whole company beneath trees.
  • Sanctuary: Similar because both shelter and protect those within; differs because sanctuary carries sacred and legal weight, a holy refuge, while a bower shelters through seclusion and shade alone.
