• Sycophants: Similar because both name people who seek favour from the powerful; differs because sycophants emphasizes self-serving flattery anywhere, while courtiers carries formal palace/institutional status.
  • Retainers: Similar because both are attached to a noble household; differs because retainers are loyal servants or dependents, whereas courtiers seek political clout and favour.
  • Flatterers: Similar because both use praise for personal gain; differs because flatterers names a verbal habit, while courtiers describes a social role in a power structure.
  • Attendants: Similar because both serve a superior; differs because attendants is a neutral functional term, while courtiers carries connotations of intrigue and ambition.
  • Toadies: Similar because both denote people who ingratiate themselves with the powerful; differs because toady is wholly contemptuous and colloquial, while courtier retains historical dignity.
  • Lackeys: Similar because both imply service to a superior; differs because lackey suggests menial, servile obedience, whereas courtier implies rank and political access.
  • Yes-men: Similar because both describe compliant supporters around a leader; differs because yes-men names a modern behavioural type (unquestioning agreement), while courtiers is historical and institutional.
  • Cronies: Similar because both denote close associates of a powerful person; differs because cronies implies old friendship or patronage, while courtiers implies formal courtly attendance.
  • Favourites: Similar because both denote those specially admitted to a ruler's confidence; differs because favourite is the narrower status of the specially preferred one, while courtier is the broader role.
  • Hangers-on: Similar because both denote people who attach themselves to the great; differs because hangers-on emphasizes parasitic idleness, while courtiers could hold real office and influence.
  • Acolytes: Similar because both denote devoted followers around a central figure; differs because acolyte is religious/ministerial in origin and implies devotion, while courtier implies ambition.
  • Parasites: Similar because both can describe people who live off the powerful; differs because parasite is wholly condemnatory and biological in metaphor, while courtier is neutral to ambivalent.
  • Opportunists: Similar because both seek advantage through access to power; differs because opportunist is a modern general term for advantage-seeking, while courtier specifies the courtly setting.
  • Aides / Advisers: Similar because both can be people around a ruler giving counsel; differs because aide and adviser are modern professional/functional terms, while courtier implies ceremonial, social proximity.
  • Minions: Similar because both denote subordinates of a powerful person; differs because minion emphasizes subservience and being a mere instrument, while courtier implies rank and manners.
