• Coaxing: Similar because both are gentle persuasion; differs because coaxing is the closest synonym but is less automatically charged with flattery and is often simpler, while cajoling implies artful sweet-talk.
  • Wheedling: Similar because both use soft, flattering speech to persuade; differs because wheedling stresses sly, self-interested flattery and can sound more manipulative or childish.
  • Flattering: Similar because both involve praise; differs because flattery is praise alone and need not aim at getting someone to act, while cajoling always has a persuasive goal.
  • Inveigling: Similar because both entangle by persuasion; differs because inveigling suggests enticing into something unwise by deception, darker than the protective coaxing of cajoling.
  • Urging: Similar because both try to move someone to action; differs because urging can be blunt and earnest, lacking the sweet, artful charm of cajoling.
  • Pressuring: Similar because both exert influence to gain compliance; differs because pressuring is forceful or moral and lacks the gentle, flattering method of cajoling.
  • Sweet-talking: Similar because both are flattering persuasion; differs because sweet-talking is colloquial and blunter, while cajoling implies more sustained and artful effort.
  • Buttering up: Similar because both use flattery; differs because buttering up is purely praise (often cynical) without the sustained persuasion toward an action.
  • Persuading: Similar because both aim to change someone's mind; differs because persuading is the neutral general term that can be rational and direct, while cajoling is specifically coaxing and flattering.
  • Beguiling: Similar because both charm someone into compliance; differs because beguiling stresses enchantment or deception and can be wholly non-verbal.
  • Enticing: Similar because both draw someone toward a course; differs because enticing appeals to desire or temptation rather than applying verbal coaxing and flattery.
