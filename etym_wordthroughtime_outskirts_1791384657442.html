- Late 16th c. "outer borders of a territory or city" - coined in Elizabethan English (Shakespeare, Hakluyt); skirt (border of a garment) compounded with out to signify the outermost margins of forests, fields, or cities.
- 17th–18th c. "transitional rural-urban borderland" - used in travelers' accounts and military dispatches to denote encampments, coaching inns, and commons lying outside city gates.
- 19th c. "industrial sprawl & working-class fringes" - Industrial Revolution expands cities rapidly; the outskirts become sites of factories, brickfields, railway yards, and semi-rural slums.
- Early 20th c. "metaphorical margin" - widely adopted for social, political, or psychological fringes ("on the outskirts of society"), while remaining the standard term for town boundaries.
- Present "urban-rural fringe & logistics hubs" - associated today with ring roads, distribution centers, big-box stores, and suburban expansion across global cities.
