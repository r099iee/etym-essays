• Pondered: Similar because both describe long, concentrated thought; differs because pondering is calm and intellectual, weighing alternatives, whereas brooding is troubled, inward, and often resentful.
  • Ruminated: Similar because both mean turning something over in the mind repeatedly; differs because ruminate is more formal and detached (and literally means chewing cud), while brooding carries gloom and emotional weight.
  • Moped: Similar because both describe a person sunk in low spirits; differs because moping is passive, feeble self-pity, while brooding implies active, concentrated, and often simmering thought.
  • Fretted: Similar because both involve anxious mental dwelling; differs because fretting is agitated, restless worry, whereas brooding is silent and still — the stillness of the sitting hen.
  • Dwelt (on): Similar because both mean returning repeatedly to a subject; differs because to dwell on is neutral or even positive, while brooding always tints the return with gloom.
  • Sulked: Similar because both can describe a person withdrawn and silent; differs because sulking is a childish show of displeasure aimed at an audience, while brooding is private and self-contained.
  • Gloomed: Similar because both evoke heaviness and darkness of mood; differs because gloomed is rarer and less precise about the act of thinking, whereas brooding names a specific behaviour.
  • Obsessed: Similar because both describe a mind fixed on one thing; differs because obsession is a clinical, involuntary fixation, while brooding is a chosen (if unhealthy) habit of thought.
  • Hung over / lowered: Similar because both describe a threatening presence in a landscape; differs because to lower is purely about clouds or weather, while brooding can attach to danger, silence, or fate and keeps the hen-image of still watchfulness.
  • Meditated: Similar because both describe sustained inward focus; differs because meditation is calm, disciplined, and spiritual, the exact opposite of brooding’s anxious gloom.
