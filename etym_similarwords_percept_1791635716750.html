• Sensation: Similar because both refer to mental phenomena arising from sensory input; differs because a sensation is the raw, uninterpreted neural signal from sensory receptors (light waves hitting the eye, cold on the skin), whereas a percept is the meaningful, organized mental product after cognitive processing.
  • Concept: Similar because both are fundamental units of cognitive mental representation; differs decisively because a concept is an abstract, universal idea or category (e.g., "justice", "canine"), whereas a percept is an immediate, concrete, sensory-based mental experience of a particular object.
  • Image (Mental Image): Similar because both involve internal visual or sensory representations in the mind; differs because a mental image can be generated internally via imagination, dreaming, or memory without external stimuli, whereas a percept is directly triggered by active sensory perception of the environment.
  • Impression: Similar because both denote effects produced on the mind through the senses; differs because impression is a broader, more colloquial or literary term that often implies a vague, preliminary feeling, emotional reaction, or lasting memory, rather than a structured perceptual entity.
  • Qualia: Similar because both relate to the subjective phenomenal character of sensory experience; differs because qualia refers to the raw, introspective, qualitative "what-it-is-likeness" of an experience (such as the specific redness of red), while a percept is the structured, recognized whole object of perception.
  • Apperception: Similar because both involve the conscious awareness of sensory input; differs because apperception is the higher-level cognitive act of assimilating a new percept into an already existing body of knowledge and self-consciousness (Leibniz, Kant), rather than the perceptual object itself.
  • Sensum / Sense-datum: Similar because both represent immediate objects of awareness in perception; differs because in analytical epistemology (Russell, Moore), a sense-datum is the hypothetical mind-dependent raw patch of color or sound directly given before any interpretation, whereas a percept acknowledges the brain's active cognitive organization.
  • Representation: Similar because a percept is an internal representation of the external world; differs because representation is a vast overarching term in semiotics, computer science, and cognitive philosophy encompassing symbols, diagrams, sentences, and neural activation vectors.
  • Phenomenon: Similar because both denote that which appears to conscious experience; differs because in Kantian philosophy, a phenomenon is an object of possible human experience (contrasted with the unknowable noumenon or thing-in-itself), while percept specifically designates the psychological sensory construct.
  • Construct: Similar because a percept is actively built or constructed by the brain's visual and neural algorithms; differs because construct is commonly used for complex abstract theoretical frameworks (e.g., social constructs, personality constructs) rather than immediate sensory images.
