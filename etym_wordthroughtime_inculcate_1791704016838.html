- 16th c. — Humanist Latinate adoption: imported from Latin inculcāre during the Tudor revival of classical rhetoric and pedagogy, used both for pressing arguments forcefully into a debate and stamping moral doctrine into pupils.
- 17th c. — Theological & catechismal focus: dominated Protestant and Catholic religious literature, describing the vital pastoral duty to inculcate Christian dogma and biblical commandments into children through daily domestic catechism.
- 18th c. — Enlightenment civic philosophy: expanded into secular educational treatises (such as John Locke's reflections on education) to denote molding the impressionable minds of future citizens with civic virtues, rationality, and moral restraint.
- 19th c. — Victorian institutional discipline: central to public school ethos, military academies, and imperial administrative manuals, emphasizing the inculcation of duty, stoicism, patriotic loyalty, and Christian gentlemanliness.
- 20th to 21st c. — Pedagogical skepticism & sociological critique: while still used positively in formal education to denote instilling ethics or critical taste, the term also gained critical scrutiny in sociology and cultural theory regarding how institutions unconsciously inculcate capitalist norms, social conformity, and ideological biases.
