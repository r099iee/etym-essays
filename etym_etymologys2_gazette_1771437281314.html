Stage 1 — (Ultimate base uncertain; NOT Proto-Indo-European). The word's documented life begins in the marketplace of 16th-century Venice. Two rival theories: (1) the coin theory — Italian gazzetta "a small Venetian copper coin," a diminutive possibly from Greek gaza (γάζα) "royal treasury," itself a loanword from an Old Iranian word for "treasure" (cf. Avestan gaza-), giving a Mediterranean–Iranian chain; (2) the magpie theory — Venetian gazza "magpie," the chattering bird, giving gazzetta "idle chatter, small news." The base cannot be securely established. Stage 2 — Italian gazzetta "a small Venetian copper coin" (16th century; diminutive form). Stage 3 — Venetian gazeta / gazzetta "a small news-sheet costing one such coin" (c. 1563; the first printed news-letters of Venice — the price named the product). Stage 4 — Italian gazzetta "a newspaper, a news-sheet" (16th century; the coin's name generalized to the medium). Stage 5 — French gazette "a news-sheet, a small newspaper" (16th century; borrowed from Italian). Stage 6 — English gazette "a small newspaper" (c. 1600; borrowed via French). Stage 7 — English gazette "the official journal of record of the state" — fixed by the founding of The London Gazette (1665); the verb to gazette "to publish or appoint officially" follows in the 1660s. Stage 8 — English GAZETTE (this card's word, in the examination-results sense Yu-cun hunts out). NOTE ON DEBATE: the coin theory and the magpie theory are both old and both still cited; standard dictionaries are divided. What is not in doubt is the semantic sequence coin → news-sheet → newspaper → official record, which is attested independently of the word's ultimate origin.
