• Spar: Similar because both are stout poles in a ship’s rigging; differs because ‘spar’ is the generic class (boom, yard, gaff, bowsprit are all spars), while the mast is the specific vertical sail-carrying pole.
  • Yard: Similar because both belong to the rigging and carry sails; differs because a yard is a crosswise spar hung from the mast to spread a square sail — horizontal, movable, subordinate to the mast.
  • Boom: Similar because both are rigging spars; differs because the boom pivots at the foot of the mast along a fore-and-aft sail, low and horizontal, whereas the mast is the tall vertical member.
  • Pole: Similar because both are long round timbers or metal tubes standing upright; differs because ‘pole’ is generic and carries none of the nautical or rigging meaning.
  • Flagpole / flagstaff: Similar because both are tall upright poles flying a flag; differs because they are land-based, without sails or rigging, and ‘staff’ stresses the ceremonial standing rather than seamanship.
  • Radio / television tower: Similar because both are tall land structures raising aerials; differs because towers are self-supporting or guyed engineering structures, while a ‘mast’ in this trade is specifically the guyed lattice or tubular aerial-support, and neither carries sails.
  • Pylon: Similar because both are tall metal lattice structures; differs because ‘pylon’ is the engineer’s word for electricity-supply towers, with no sail or sea association.
  • Derrick: Similar because both are spar-like load-bearing poles, on ships and docks alike; differs because a derrick is rigged with tackle for lifting cargo, not for carrying sails.
