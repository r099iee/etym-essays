• Nap: Similar because a short sleep; differs because a "nap" is usually intentional and can be deep, while a "doze" is lighter, often unintended, and hovers between waking and sleeping.
  • Snooze: Similar because a light, short sleep; differs because "snooze" is jocular and informal, and it is now dominated by the alarm-clock sense ("snooze button"), whereas "doze" retains a gentler, half-conscious quality.
  • Drowse: Similar because sleepy or half-asleep state; differs because "drowse" is mostly literary or dialectal and suggests dullness rather than actual light sleep.
  • Slumber: Similar because sleep; differs because "slumber" is poetic for peaceful, full sleep, with no implication of lightness or brevity.
  • Catnap: Similar because a brief sleep; differs because a catnap is a deliberately snatched, very short sleep (as cats take), usually deeper than a doze.
  • Siesta: Similar because a short daytime sleep; differs because a siesta is a cultural institution (a midday rest, typically in Spain and Latin America), fixed in time and custom.
  • Dose: Similar because near-homophone and historically entangled; differs because "dose" is a quantity of medicine, and its old sense "to drug someone into drowsiness" confused speakers with "doze"; they are now quite distinct in meaning.
  • Doze off (phrasal): Similar because the same verb; differs because "doze off" adds the sudden, involuntary transition from wakefulness into light sleep, the archetypal modern use.
