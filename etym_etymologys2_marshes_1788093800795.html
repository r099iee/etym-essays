PIE (prehistoric)
*móri
"sea, lake, body of standing water"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 1st millennium BCE)
*mari
"sea, lake"
↓
Proto-Germanic (pre-dialectal)
*mariskaz
"marsh; land partly covered by water"
(formed from *mari + adjective suffix
* -iskaz "belonging to, like" = "sea-ish land")
↓
Proto-West Germanic (c. 5th c. CE)
*marisk
"swamp, waterlogged ground"
↓
Old English (c. 8th–10th c.)
mersc / merisc
"marsh, swamp, fen"
↓
Middle English (13th–15th c.)
merssh / mersch / mersh
"marsh, wet low ground"
↓
English (spelling "marsh" from 16th c.)
MARSHES
"waterlogged, soft, reedy wetland tracts"

Note: MARSHES is the regular plural of marsh (marsh + -es), used since early modern English for wetland districts. The development *marisk- > Old English mersc shows loss of the unstressed *-isk- syllable and West Germanic fronting (*a > e); parallel forms appear across Germanic (Old Saxon mersk, Old Norse merskr, Dutch meers, German Marsch).
