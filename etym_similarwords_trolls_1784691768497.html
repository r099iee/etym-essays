• Giants: Similar because both are huge supernatural beings of northern mythology who menace humans; differs because giants is the generic term for colossal humanoid beings in many traditions, while trolls are the specifically Scandinavian cave/mountain beings with magic, shape-shifting, and the sunlight-to-stone lore.
  • Ogres: Similar because both are man-eating monstrous beings; differs because ogres belong to French Romance fairy-tale tradition and are usually un-magical brutes, while trolls have Norse magic and the stone-at-dawn law — and the card itself notes "not-ogres".
  • Goblins: Similar because both are hostile fairy-tale beings that bedevil humans; differs because goblins are smaller, malicious, mischievous cave or household sprites, while trolls are large, physically imposing, and often stupid.
  • Monsters: Similar because both are frightening, abnormal creatures; differs because monster is the broad generic term for any frightful beast, while trolls are specifically humanoid folklore beings of Norse origin.
  • Jötnar (Jotuns): Similar because they are the giant beings of Norse mythology, sometimes loosely translated "trolls"; differs because jötnar are the cosmically ancient giants who oppose the gods in Norse myth, while trolls are the humbler, more folkloric cave-beings of later tradition.
  • Provocateur: Similar because both denote one who stirs up conflict; differs because a provocateur deliberately provokes, often for political ends, while an internet troll provokes anonymously and mischievously for amusement.
  • Instigator: Similar because both are people who start trouble; differs because an instigator may provoke for any reason, while a troll's provocation is its whole purpose, typically online and anonymous.
  • Flamebaiter: Similar because both post inflammatory messages to provoke reactions; differs because flamebaiter is specific to online flame wars, while troll has the added folklore baggage and the lurking-under-the-bridge image.
  • Griefer: Similar because both deliberately disrupt and annoy others online; differs because a griefer acts within online games to spoil others' play, while a troll works in discussion spaces to derail argument.
  • Boor: Similar because both can describe a clumsy, uncouth person; differs because boor is a plain social insult for an ill-mannered person, while troll in this transferred sense keeps a hint of the hulking folkloric brute.
  • Oaf: Similar because both can name a clumsy, stupid person; differs because oaf is purely a human insult (from the old elf-child changeling lore), while troll names the mythical being first and the stupid person only by transfer.
