• Scrubbed: Similar because both mean vigorous cleaning by friction with brushes, pads, or abrasive agents; differs because scrubbed foregrounds the rubbing motion and everyday hygiene or deck-work, while scoured more readily suggests removing deep-set grime, polishing metal, or (figuratively) sweeping an entire area clean of what is sought.
  • Searched: Similar because both can describe examining a place or dataset for something lost or hidden; differs because searched may be partial, casual, or merely attempted, while scoured implies exhaustive, abrasive thoroughness — every corner treated as if scrubbed.
  • Combed: Similar because both describe systematic coverage of terrain or material ('combed the woods'); differs in tone because combed feels methodical and linear, like the teeth of a comb, while scoured feels harsher, more forceful, and closer to a cleansing or military sweep.
  • Ransacked: Similar because both involve thorough movement through a space; differs sharply in intent and aftermath because ransacking implies chaotic plundering and disorder, while scouring (search sense) is disciplined pursuit or cleaning without necessary theft or wreckage.
  • Swept: Similar because both describe passing over an area to clear or examine it; differs because swept suggests broad, rapid movement, while scoured implies fine-tooth, effortful coverage.
  • Rubbed / Polished: Similar because both describe friction applied to a surface; differs because they are gentler or surface-specific, while scoured adds abrasive force and thoroughness.
  • Eroded (technical): Similar because both describe the removal of material from a surface by an abrasive force; differs because eroded is domain-locked to geomorphology and hydraulic engineering (water, wind), while scoured spans household, search, and figurative use.
