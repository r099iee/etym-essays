PIE (reconstructed, c. 3500 BCE)
*ḱeh₂i- / *ḱoi-
‘bright, shining, gray, pale’
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*hairaz
‘gray, old, venerable, noble’
↓
Old English (c. 8th c.)
hār
‘gray, hoary, old, venerable’
↓
Old English (c. 10th c.)
hārnes
‘grayness of hair, hoariness’
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
hor / hore / horinesse
‘grayness, venerable age, frostiness’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
hoary / hoariness
‘whiteness of hair, ancientness’
↓
Modern English (current)
hoariness
‘state of being hoary, venerable antiquity’

Note: Proto-Germanic *hairaz is also the direct ancestor of Old Norse hárr (‘gray-haired, old’) and Old High German hēr (‘venerable, noble, exalted, holy’), which in Modern German evolved into the honorific noun Herr (‘Lord, master, gentleman’). In English, the native adjective hoar developed an extended form hoary through the addition of the adjectival suffix -y (Old English -ig), from which the abstract noun hoariness was refashioned with native suffix -ness.
