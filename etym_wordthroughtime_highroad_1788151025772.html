- [17th c. compound, wholly literal] - [the highroad is the chief public road, the king’s highway; an administrative and everyday word, free of metaphor]
- [18th–19th c. stagecoach age] - [the highroad becomes the emblem of national communication and improvement; prose of the turnpike era treats it with patriotic warmth]
- [19th c. figurative turn "the sure course"] - [as railways take over the literal traffic, "highroad to X" (success, ruin, fortune) becomes a dignified rhetorical phrase]
- [20th c. American split] - [American English lets highway absorb both literal and metaphorical roles; highroad recedes into British and literary use]
- [20th–21st c. moral idiom] - ["take the high road" = act in the principled, magnanimous way; the road-like imagery fades and the word becomes a pure metaphor for virtue]
