- Early scientific sense (physics & mechanics) - Entered 19th-century natural philosophy to denote a state in which balanced forces have been upset; precise, technical register.
- Economic & social sense - Adopted by economists and social theorists (market disequilibrium) to describe a system displaced from equilibrium; abstract, academic register.
- Psychological & clinical sense - Extended in the 20th century to mental and physiological balance (psychological disequilibrium); diagnostic, weighty tone.
- Contemporary sense - Now used broadly across ecology, finance, and everyday writing for any loss of balance or stability; relaxed from technical jargon into a general metaphor for instability.
