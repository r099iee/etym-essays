NOTE: Latin pāstūra is formed on pāstus, the past participle of pāscere, with the noun suffix -ūra; its first attestation is debated (classical Latin preferred pascuum for grazing land, and pāstūra is thoroughly established only in late Latin and in Romance). pāscere continues the PIE root *peh₂- in its s-extension (*peh₂-s-), the same root as English food, feed, fodder (via Germanic *fōd-) and Latin pābulum “fodder,” pāstor “shepherd.” The dual idea — feeding and protecting — is built into the root itself.
