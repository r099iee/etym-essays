• pliant: Similar because it means easily bent or influenced; differs because it stresses ready compliance and can imply weak yielding, while supple adds grace and resilience.
  • flexible: Similar because it means capable of bending; differs because it is neutral and technical, while supple implies smooth elegance and living softness.
  • lithe: Similar because it describes agile bodily grace; differs because it focuses on slim youthful movement, while supple focuses on bendability of material or mind.
  • limber: Similar because it means easily bent at joints; differs because it is used mainly for bodies and is colloquial sporty, while supple covers leather, prose, diplomacy too.
  • lissome: Similar because it means gracefully slender and flexible; differs because it is poetic and stresses slim charm, while supple is more everyday and craft-based.
  • elastic: Similar because it means returning to shape after stretching; differs because it implies rubbery snap-back, while supple implies soft yielding without necessarily springing.
  • resilient: Similar because it means recovering readily; differs because it stresses toughness after pressure, while supple stresses ease during bending.
  • adaptable: Similar because it means adjusting to new conditions; differs because it is abstract and organizational, while supple retains a bodily metaphor of bending.
  • versatile: Similar because it means turning easily to new tasks; differs because it stresses many-sided skill, while supple stresses fluid transition.
  • yielding: Similar because it means giving way under pressure; differs because it can imply surrender, while supple implies strength in yielding.
  • soft: Similar because supple things feel soft; differs because soft is about surface and lacks the idea of bending without breaking.
  • agile: Similar because it implies quick graceful movement; differs because agile is about speed and dexterity, supple about range of bending.
