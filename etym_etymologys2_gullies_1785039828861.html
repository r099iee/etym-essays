PIE base *gul- / *glu- "to swallow, gulp" (onomatopoeic base, per de Vaan; gathered by Watkins under PIE *gwel- "to swallow, gulp") → Latin gula "throat, gullet; appetite" (1st c. BCE) → Vulgar Latin → Old French gole "throat, neck" (9th–14th c.) → Old French golet / goulet (diminutive of gole) "little throat; neck of a bottle; gutter; narrow channel or bay" (12th–14th c.) → Middle English golet "gullet, throat; water channel" (c. 1300) → English gully "channel in earth made by running water" (1650s, shortened from the French diminutive; the topographical sense extends the 'narrow throat/channel' image) → plural gullies. Note on debate: the PIE stage is reconstructed from the onomatopoeic root *gul-/*glu- 'to swallow' — the sound of swallowing made into words — and the family is attested in Latin gula, Old English ceole 'throat', Old Irish gelim 'I devour', Old Church Slavonic glutu 'gullet', and Russian glot 'gulp'; de Vaan regards the base as onomatopoeic rather than a fully recoverable PIE root, so the very earliest stage should be read as probable sound-symbolism rather than a certain formal root.
