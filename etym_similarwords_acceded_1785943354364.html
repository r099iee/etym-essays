• Conceded: Similar because both mean yielding to another's position; differs because "conceded" implies reluctantly admitting defeat or truth after an argument (concede a point, concede defeat), while "acceded" implies agreeing to a request or joining a pact.
  • Assented: Similar because both mean expressing agreement; differs because "assented" focuses on mental concurrence or official approval (royal assent to a bill), while "acceded" includes assuming office or joining treaties as well as agreeing.
  • Acquiesced: Similar because both mean giving in; differs because "acquiesced" implies passive, quiet submission without enthusiasm, whereas "acceded" is a formal, often public act of agreement or entry.
  • Consented: Similar because both mean granting permission or agreement; differs because "consented" is the general word for permission (to consent to an operation), while "acceded" carries formal legal and diplomatic weight.
  • Agreed: Similar because both mean reaching a common position; differs because "agreed" is the neutral, everyday verb for any coming together of views, while "acceded" is formal and implies yielding to a pre-existing demand or joining a pre-existing instrument.
  • Yielded: Similar because both mean giving way to pressure; differs because "yielded" emphasises giving ground or surrendering (yield to temptation, yield the road), while "acceded" emphasises formally consenting or entering upon something.
  • Submitted: Similar because both can mean giving in to authority; differs because "submitted" implies deferring to a superior power or authority, a humbler act, while "acceded" can be the act of an equal formally joining or agreeing.
  • Subscribed: Similar because both can mean formally agreeing to a document; differs because "subscribe" literally means to sign one's name (and, by extension, to pay for a publication), while "accede" means to join or agree without signing.
  • Complied: Similar because both mean doing what was asked; differs because "complied" means obeying a rule, request, or order in action, while "acceded" emphasises consenting to the request itself.
  • Accepted: Similar because both mean receiving or agreeing to something offered; differs because "accepted" is broad and neutral (accept an offer, accept a fact), while "acceded" is formal and specifically about requests, treaties, and offices.
  • Succeeded: Similar because both describe taking over an office; differs because "succeeded" emphasises following after a predecessor (he succeeded his father), while "acceded" emphasises entering the office itself (he acceded to the throne).
  • Approved: Similar because both mean giving consent; differs because "approved" means formally sanctioning or rating as good (approve a plan), while "acceded" means agreeing to or entering into something already proposed.
  • Went along with: Similar because both mean accepting a proposal; differs because "went along with" is informal and often reluctant or easy-going, while "acceded" is formal and ceremonial.
