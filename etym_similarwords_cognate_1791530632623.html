• Related: The broadest synonym, meaning connected in some way; it differs by being vague about the nature of the connection, whereas cognate specifically asserts common descent.
  • Akin: Similar in the sense of family resemblance or shared character; it is warmer and more literary, but it does not require a documented common ancestor.
  • Kindred: Closely similar, and etymologically “born of the same kin”; it is archaic and emotional, whereas cognate is technical and comparative.
  • Consanguineous: Literally “of the same blood,” used in law and genetics for blood relatives; it is narrower and more clinical than cognate and rarely applied to words.
  • Agnate: A Roman-law relative traced through males only; it is the precise opposite term within the same legal system, so it cannot replace cognate.
  • Allied: Connected by treaty, interest or resemblance; it implies alliance rather than birth, and is common in political and military writing.
  • Homologous: In biology, derived from the same ancestral structure; it is the closest scientific parallel, but it belongs to anatomy and genetics rather than language.
  • Analogous: Similar in function but not in origin; it is the direct antithesis of cognate, since analogy is resemblance without descent.
  • Corresponding: Matching in position, function or form; it is neutral and structural, with no claim about ancestry.
  • Parallel: Developing along similar lines; it suggests comparable trajectories rather than a shared source.
  • Equivalent: Equal in value or meaning; it is a translation concept, whereas cognate is a descent concept.
  • Derivative: A word formed from another within the same language (as fatherly from father); it is a parent–child relation, not a sibling relation.
  • Doublet: One of two forms of the same word entering a language by different routes; it is a special case of cognate status within a single language.
  • Etymon: The ancestral form itself, not the descendant; cognates share an etymon, but an etymon is not a cognate.
  • Paronym: A word related in form or derived from the same stem; it is looser and often used for near-homographs rather than true descent.
  • False friend: A lookalike word with a different meaning; it is the deceptive counterpart of a cognate and must be kept distinct.
