- Geographic coinage (1756-1770s) - French Polynésie for all South Seas; inhabitants are undifferentiated noble savages in European imagination.
- Racialized tripartition (1830s-19th c.) - Dumont d'Urville narrows Polynesia to eastern triangle vs Melanesia/Micronesia; Polynesians ranked as light-skinned, hierarchical, handsome — admired yet colonized.
- Ethnological admiration (late 19th-early 20th c.) - Classic chiefdoms studied as pristine states; large nets and temples prove capacity for organization; paternalistic praise.
- Navigational vindication (mid-late 20th c.) - From passive drifters to intentional star-navigators after Hokulea voyages; perception becomes respect for science and agency.
- Present pan-ethnic pride - Specific island nations assert names while embracing Polynesian as shared voyaging heritage and diaspora solidarity; tourist stereotype contested.
