• Mural: Similar because it is a large wall painting; differs because mural is generic (any medium including acrylic), while fresco is specifically on wet lime plaster.
  • Wall painting: Similar because it is the plain English hypernym; differs because wall painting includes secco and modern acrylic, while fresco claims chemical binding with wet plaster.
  • Buon fresco (true fresco): Similar because it is the strict technique; differs because buon fresco is the full technical term for wet-plaster work, while fresco alone can loosely include secco retouching.
  • Fresco secco (dry fresco): Similar because it is lime painting on dry wall; differs because secco uses binder on dry plaster and flakes easily, while buon fresco locks pigment into the wall.
  • Tempera: Similar because it is another early Italian matte medium; differs because tempera binds pigment with egg on panel, while fresco binds with lime in wall.
  • Distemper: Similar because it is matte water-based wall paint; differs because distemper uses glue size and sits on the surface, while fresco becomes the surface.
  • Secco: Similar as shorthand for dry-wall overpainting; differs because secco is the touch-up stage, while fresco is the whole wet-wall system.
  • Giornata: Similar because it is the day's patch of wet plaster; differs because giornata names the unit of work, while fresco names the finished picture.
  • Al fresco: Similar because it shares the Italian root for fresh and cool; differs because al fresco means outdoors in the cool air, while fresco alone means the painting.
  • Affresco: Similar because it is the full Italian phrase (pittura a fresco); differs because affresco is the Italian dictionary headword, while fresco is the English clipping.
