• Delegation: Similar because both denote a designated group of individuals commissioned to represent a larger body; differs because delegation is the modern, universal, and standard term for any representative group (such as a delegation to an international summit), whereas deputation carries a more formal, classical, or specifically petition-bearing connotation (appearing before an authority to deliver a request or address).
  • Embassy: Similar because both represent diplomatic missions acting on behalf of a sovereign authority; differs because an embassy is a permanent diplomatic outpost headed by an ambassador with permanent residential premises and full diplomatic immunity in a foreign capital, whereas a deputation is a temporary, task-specific group that disbands after its audience.
  • Legation: Similar because both involve official diplomatic representation abroad; differs because a legation historically denoted a permanent diplomatic mission of slightly lower rank than an embassy (led by a minister rather than an ambassador), whereas a deputation is convened for a single transient mission or petition.
  • Contingent: Similar because both describe a representative group drawn from a larger collective; differs because a contingent emphasizes a group sent to participate alongside others in a larger event, military operation, or convention (e.g., "the Australian Olympic contingent"), without necessarily bearing a formal petition to an authority.
  • Committee: Similar because both are small groups appointed by an assembly to perform defined duties; differs because a committee is typically an internal deliberative body tasked with ongoing study, policymaking, or administration, whereas a deputation is an external mission sent outward to communicate with another body.
  • Envoy: Similar because both represent an authority on an official diplomatic errand; differs because an envoy is typically an individual diplomat or special emissary, whereas a deputation is explicitly a collective delegation of multiple persons.
  • Commission: Similar because both are formally appointed bodies granted public authority; differs because a commission is a formal administrative, regulatory, or investigative panel endowed with executive or judicial powers to enforce laws or examine scandals, whereas a deputation is a representative body sent to petition or negotiate.
