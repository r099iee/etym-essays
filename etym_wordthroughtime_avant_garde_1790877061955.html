- 12th–15th c. - Old French military strategy: The advance guard (*avant-garde*) marching at the forefront of the army.
- 15th–17th c. - Early Modern English borrowing: Used directly as a military loanword alongside the native syncopated form *vanguard*.
- 1820s–1840s - Saint-Simonian socio-political adoption: Reimagined in France by utopian socialists as artists leading the spiritual and moral progress of humanity.
- 1880s–1930s - High Modernist aesthetic rupture: Adopted globally to define revolutionary art movements (Futurism, Dadaism, Constructivism, Surrealism) defying bourgeois culture.
- Post-WWII–Present - Institutionalization & contemporary critique: Applied to radical experimental theatre, film, and fashion; simultaneously scrutinized for its eventual absorption into elite art markets.
