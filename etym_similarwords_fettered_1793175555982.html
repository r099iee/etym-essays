• Chained: Similar because both mean bound with iron links; differs because chained is the general word for any chain anywhere on the body, while fettered specifies the ankles and the theft of stride.
  • Shackled: Similar because shackles are also iron bonds for prisoners; differs because shackled today suggests wrists or hands as often as feet, while fettered keeps its ancient foot-sense and a more literary ring.
  • Manacled: Similar because both describe iron bonds on a captive; differs because manacled strictly means bound at the hands (from Latin manus), the mirror image of the foot-bound fettered.
  • Handcuffed: Similar because both name metal restraints locking two limbs together; differs because handcuffed is modern police hardware for wrists, while fettered is dungeon iron for ankles.
  • Hobbled: Similar because both shorten the stride by binding the legs; differs because hobbled suggests a strap or rope that merely tangles movement (horses at graze), while fettered suggests riveted iron meant to forbid it.
  • Bound: Similar because both mean tied or fastened against freedom; differs because bound is the broad plain word (ropes, cords, promises), while fettered is specifically iron, specifically feet, specifically captivity.
  • Enchained: Similar because both evoke captivity in links of metal; differs because enchained is poetic and absolute (souls, nations), while fettered is concrete enough to clank, with the hardware always in view.
  • Hampered: Similar because both describe freedom of movement taken away; differs because hampered (from a basket or hamper) suggests encumbrance and awkwardness, while fettered suggests locked iron and forbidding.
  • Constrained: Similar because both mean held back from free action; differs because constrained is cool and abstract (budgets, grammar, politeness), while fettered is hot with the dungeon's menace.
