compound
↓ Greek (classical)
νεκρομαντεία
"divination by
the dead"

↓ Late Latin (Late Antiquity)
necromantia
"divination by
the dead"

↓ Old French (12th c.)
nigromance
"divination by
the dead"

↓ English (c. 1300)
necromancy
"divination by the dead;
black magic"

↓ English (c. 1400)
necromancer
"one who conjures the
spirits of the dead"

Note: Old French nigromance was
reshaped under influence of Latin
niger 'black' ('black magic'); the
modern spelling restores necro-
from necromantia. Greek νέκυς
'corpse' also occurs. The root
*men- 'to think' is distinct from
the homophonous *men- 'to remain'.
