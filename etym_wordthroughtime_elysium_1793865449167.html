- Archaic Greece & Homeric Epic (c. 8th c. BCE) - An earthly paradise at the western ends of the earth (Elysian Plain) ruled by Rhadamanthus, where mortal demigods favored by Zeus escaped physical death and enjoyed eternal bodily ease.
- Classical & Hellenistic Greece (c. 5th–2nd c. BCE) - Relocated to the underworld or Islands of the Blessed; moralized through mystery cults (Eleusinian, Orphic, Dionysian) as a reward for virtuous souls, philosophers, and the initiated rather than mere aristocratic pedigree.
- Roman Imperial Antiquity (c. 1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE) - Masterfully codified in Virgil's Aeneid as the luminous subterranean realm of righteous heroes, lawmakers, poets, and loving kin; synonymous with posthumous peace and sublime light.
- Renaissance & Enlightenment (c. 1500–1800) - Secularized into European poetry, neoclassical drama, and landscape architecture as the supreme aesthetic ideal of tranquil rural retirement, classical harmony, and idyllic natural beauty (inspiring Paris's Champs-Élysées).
- 19th–21st Century (Colloquial & Sci-Fi Archetype) - A figurative noun for any sanctuary of supreme delight, idyllic retirement, or futuristic techno-utopian enclave insulated from global distress.
