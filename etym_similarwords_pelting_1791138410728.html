• battering: Similar because it describes repeated violent striking; differs because it suggests heavier, crushing blows rather than many small stinging impacts.
  • pounding: Similar because it conveys heavy relentless rain or blows; differs because it stresses dull thudding force rather than sharp stinging hits.
  • lashing: Similar because lashing rain also beats fiercely; differs because it adds a whipping, wind-driven image rather than thrown missiles.
  • driving: Similar because driving rain is wind-propelled and forceful; differs because it stresses direction and propulsion rather than impact.
  • pouring: Similar because it denotes very heavy rain; differs because it measures volume and flow, not sting or assault.
  • teeming: Similar because it means raining copiously; differs because it is more literary and stresses abundance, not violence.
  • thrashing: Similar because it suggests violent beating motion; differs because it implies flailing or whipping rather than falling missiles.
  • bombarding: Similar because it means assailing with a shower of projectiles; differs because it is more formal and military, often figurative.
  • peppering: Similar because it means hitting with scattered shots or blows; differs because it stresses scattered distribution rather than sheer drenching force.
  • assailing: Similar because it means attacking repeatedly; differs because it is formal and general, lacking the specific rain and missile image.
  • pattering: Similar because it describes rapid repeated taps of rain; differs because it is light and gentle, the opposite in intensity.
  • pelting (rushing sense: tearing, hurtling): Similar in form because to pelt along means to rush; differs because it describes human speed, not falling weather.
