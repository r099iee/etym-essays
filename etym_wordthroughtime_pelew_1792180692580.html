- Late 18th c. (1780s–1790s) — Coined in British maritime English following Captain Henry Wilson's 1783 shipwreck; skyrocketed to fame via George Keate's 1788 bestseller An Account of the Pelew Islands, embodying the Enlightenment ideal of the virtuous, uncorrupted Pacific paradise.
- 19th c. (1800s–1880s) — Established as the standard English cartographic and geographic toponym across British Admiralty charts, Victorian geography books, and missionary accounts.
- Late 19th to Early 20th c. (1890s–1940s) — Gradually displaced in official cartography by the German and Japanese administrative spellings Palau after Spain sold the islands to Germany in 1899 and Japan seized them during WWI.
- Mid-to-Late 20th c. (1950s–1980s) — Relegated to historical and anthropological literature (as in Durant's The Story of Civilization), cited specifically for its classic matrilineal councils.
- 21st c. — Survives strictly as an archival and historiographical term, with the nation officially celebrated globally as the Republic of Palau (Belau).
