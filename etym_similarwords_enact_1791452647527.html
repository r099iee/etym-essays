• Legislate: similar because it means to make laws; differs because it focuses on the legislative process and the body that makes law, while enact focuses on giving a particular measure legal force.
  • Pass: similar because a legislature passes a bill before it becomes law; differs because passing is one stage in the procedure, while enacting is the formal establishment of the law.
  • Decree: similar because it means to order or establish something authoritatively; differs because decree often implies a single ruler or executive act rather than a deliberative legislature.
  • Ordain: similar because it can mean to establish or command by authority; differs because it is more formal and often religious or ecclesiastical.
  • Ratify: similar because it gives formal approval to a measure; differs because ratification confirms something already agreed, while enactment brings a new law into effect.
  • Implement: similar because it means to put a policy or plan into practice; differs because it stresses practical machinery and administration rather than formal or performative realisation.
  • Execute: similar because it means to carry out a plan or order; differs because it can also mean to put someone to death and is more general about completing an action.
  • Perform: similar because it means to act a role or present a scene; differs because perform is the general theatrical verb and does not necessarily imply making something legally or socially real.
  • Portray: similar because it means to represent a character on stage or in film; differs because it stresses depiction rather than the full bringing-into-action of a role.
  • Represent: similar because it can mean to depict or stand for something; differs because it is broader and does not necessarily involve performance or implementation.
  • Stage: similar because it means to put on a play or spectacle; differs because it refers to the production event rather than the act of embodying a role.
  • Realise: similar because it means to make something actual; differs because it can also mean to understand, and it is less formal than enact in legal contexts.
  • Effect: similar because it means to bring something about; differs because it is more abstract and often used in formal or legal prose.
  • Accomplish: similar because it means to complete or carry out something; differs because it stresses successful completion rather than formal establishment.
  • Personate: similar because it means to act or impersonate a character; differs because it is rare, archaic, and often implies impersonation or fraud.
  • Re-enact: similar because it means to perform or establish again; differs because it specifically signals repetition, as in historical re-enactment or renewed legislation.
