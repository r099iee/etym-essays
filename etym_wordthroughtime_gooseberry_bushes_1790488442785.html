- 1530s gooseberry (fruit and bush) - thorny northern fruit; kitchen-garden crop; homely and useful
- 16th–18th c. cultivated bush - hundreds of Lancashire show varieties; pruning, picking, pies and wine; thrifty and proud
- Early 19th c. to play gooseberry - third wheel with lovers; chaperone picking fruit while others court; comic and awkward
- 1790s–Victorian Old Gooseberry / fool - devil nickname and cream pudding; slang, sweet, and superstitious
- Ruskin 1830s–1880s garden full - Perth aunt's bushes sloping to Tay; childhood abundance, scratches, and jam; nostalgic
- Modern garden and idiom - mildew-resistant Invicta, jam, and I don't want to play gooseberry; practical and joking
