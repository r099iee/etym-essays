• Stimulus: Similar because both are things that rouse or evoke a response; differs because a stimulus is any morally neutral external condition that triggers a reaction, while an incitement is a spur that seizes and moves the will — in Mill's usage, an ennobling force.
  • Incentive: Similar because both prompt or encourage action; differs because an incentive is a calculated reward or inducement offered in advance, whereas an incitement is a rousing impulse that comes from outside and carries the person along.
  • Motive: Similar because both explain why a person acts; differs because a motive resides within the agent as an internal reason, while an incitement comes from outside as a force that moves him.
  • Provocation: Similar because both spur someone on; differs because provocation in modern use points toward angering or egging someone into wrongdoing, while elevated 19th-century incitement could be a noble spurring of the spirit.
  • Instigation: Similar because both denote the stirring up of an action; differs because instigation is closer to the scheming, behind-the-scenes egging on of a deed, whereas incitement stresses the public or persuasive rousing of the will.
  • Spur / Goad: Similar because both are sharp things that drive a person forward; differs because spur and goad are vivid, physical metaphors for a narrow prod, while incitement is a broader, more intellectual and often verbal rousing.
  • Encouragement: Similar because both urge someone on; differs because encouragement is supportive, kindly, and aimed at confidence, while incitement is a stronger, more passionate urging that may also be directed to ill.
  • Inducement: Similar because both draw a person toward action; differs because an inducement typically works by persuasion or reward rather than by rousing passion, whereas an incitement fires the will directly.
