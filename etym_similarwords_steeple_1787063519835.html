• Spire: Similar because both are tall, pointed church features and are often confused; differs in that the spire is specifically the tapering pointed top, while the steeple is the whole tower-and-point assembly.
  • Tower: Similar because a steeple is a type of tower; differs because a tower need not be pointed and may serve any function (defensive, clock, observation), while a steeple is specifically the tapering church tower.
  • Bell tower: Similar because both hold bells and rise high; differs because a bell tower (or campanile) is defined by its bells and may be flat-topped, whereas a steeple is defined by its tapering point.
  • Pinnacle: Similar because both are tall, pointed architectural features; differs because a pinnacle is a small ornamental spike on a roof or tower, not a free-standing tower itself.
  • Minaret: Similar because both are tall, slender towers beside places of worship; differs because a minaret belongs to mosques, has balconies for the call to prayer, and is often round, not pointed like a Christian steeple.
  • Turret: Similar because both are small towers; differs because a turret is usually a small projecting tower on a building's corner or wall, not the dominant pointed church tower.
