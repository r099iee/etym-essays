• Desire: Similar in wanting intensely; differs because desire is the neutral, general verb without moral colouring, while covet adds the judgement — wanting what one has no right to want.
  • Crave: Similar in wanting with urgency; differs because craving is bodily and appetitive (one craves food or drink), whereas coveting is a moralized longing, more of the mind and the law than of the stomach.
  • Envy: Similar in longing for what another has; differs because envy keeps its eye on the neighbour and resents his possession, while coveting keeps its eye on the thing itself.
  • Yearn: Similar in longing deeply; differs because yearning is an emotional ache, often for something distant or lost, without the moral weight or the judging edge that coveting carries.
  • Hanker: Similar in wanting restlessly; differs because hanker is colloquial and mild, a passing restless appetite, while covet is intense, judged, and simmering.
  • Long: Similar in desiring deeply; differs because long is the plain, open verb of wishing, carrying neither covet's moral charge nor its historical weight of commandment and sin.
  • Parapet: Similar in being a low wall along a defensive wall-top; differs because a parapet is solid and unbroken, while battlements are notched with merlons and crenel gaps for firing through.
  • Rampart: Similar in being part of a fortification; differs because a rampart is the broad earthen or stone bank around a fortress, while battlements are the notched wall-top built above such defences for fighting.
  • Crenellation: Similar in describing the notched castle parapet; differs because crenellation is the abstract pattern of merlons and crenels, while battlements are the physical wall-top that carries that pattern.
  • Merlon: Similar in belonging to the same defensive structure; differs because a merlon is the individual solid section between the crenel gaps, while battlements are the whole crenellated parapet composed of merlons and crenels.
