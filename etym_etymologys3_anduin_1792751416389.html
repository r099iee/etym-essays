1. Root Word 1 Evolution: Primitive Quendian *ÁNAD- to Sindarin and ("Long"). The first morphological element of the hydronym Anduin derives from Tolkien's internally constructed Primitive Quendian linguistic base *ÁNAD- (with its syncopated variant *ÁND-), signifying "long, extended in space or time, far-reaching." In the historical phonology of the Elvish tongues devised by Tolkien, this primitive root yielded distinct reflexes across the two primary branches of the Eldarin family. In Quenya (High-elven), which preserved vocalic endings and resisted final consonant clustering, the root developed into the adjective anda ("long"), famously attested in compound names such as Andúril ("Flame of the West") and the poetic descriptor Andamunda ("Long-snout", the Elvish term for an elephant/mûmak). In Sindarin (Grey-elven), which underwent extensive apocope (loss of final vowels) and consonant mutation modeled on Welsh historical phonology, the final vowel was dropped, producing the monosyllabic base adjective and ("long"). When functioning as a prefix in compound formation, the final dental stop [d] frequently assimilated or combined with subsequent consonants, as observed in Anfalas ("Langstrand" / "Long Shore", from and + falas) and Andrath ("Long Climb" / "High Defile").

2. Root Word 2 Evolution: Primitive Quendian *DUI- to Sindarin duin ("Great River"). The second morphological element originates from the Primitive Quendian verbal root *DUI-, conveying the kinetic physical action "to flow rapidly, hasten, run forward, or stream forth." In Common Eldarin, this verbal root was nominalized with the formative suffix *-ne to yield *duīne, which specifically designated a large, deep river flowing along an established course toward the ocean. In Quenya, this root remained relatively rare, overshadowed by the root *SIR- (yielding sírë, "river, stream"). However, in Sindarin, *duīne underwent characteristic vocalic narrowing and diphthongal preservation, yielding duin (pronounced [du.in] or [dujn]). In Tolkien's meticulous linguistic notes on Middle-earth hydronymy (published in The Rivers and Beacon-hills of Gondor and The Silmarillion appendix), he explicitly recorded that in Grey-elven nomenclature, duin was applied exclusively to the greatest watercourses of continental scale, distinguishing them from smaller streams (celon), torrents (rant), or general flowing rivers (sîr). Other celebrated Sindarin rivers bearing this morpheme include the Baranduin (the "Brandywine", from baran "golden-brown" + duin), the Celduin (the "River Running", from cel- "to run/flow" + duin), and the Esgalduin ("River of the Veiled Shadow" in ancient Doriath).

3. Synthesis, Internal Compounding, and Elvish Phonological Fusion. When the two Elvish elements were combined to name the longest river in north-western Middle-earth, the compound was formed as Ando-duine, which through historical Sindarin contraction and haplology coalesced into Anduin. The name translates transparently into the Common Speech (Westron) as "the Long River," and by customary honorific as "the Great River" (Sindarin: Anduin Vhor or Anduin the Great). Internally within Arda's historical chronology, the river was mapped and named by the Grey-elves (Sindar) and Nandor during the Great Journey in the Years of the Trees, long before the Sun and Moon were created. Because the Nandorin Elves (the Green-elves or Silvan folk) halted their westward march when confronted by the colossal wall of the Misty Mountains, the broad vales of Anduin became their first major continental realm, forever sanctifying the river in Elvish song.

4. External Philological Architecture: Welsh Aesthetics and Indo-European Hydronymy. From an external philological perspective, Tolkien's creation of Anduin exemplifies his legendary methodology of linguistic invention. As the Rawlinson and Bosworth Professor of Anglo-Saxon at Pembroke College, Oxford, and later Merton Professor of English Language and Literature, Tolkien possessed an exhaustive knowledge of comparative Indo-European philology. In constructing Sindarin, he deliberately chose Brythonic Celtic—specifically Old and Middle Welsh—as his primary aesthetic and phonetic template. The vocalic music of Anduin (the fluid pairing of [an] and [du.in]) deliberately echoes Welsh hydronymic and geographic aesthetics, such as Welsh river names containing dŵr / dwfr ("water", from Proto-Celtic *dubros).

Furthermore, Tolkien's conceptualization of the Anduin parallels the great river systems of real-world European antiquity. In comparative Indo-European linguistics, the fundamental river-root is *deh₂nu- ("river, flowing water", Sanskrit dānu), which birthed the continental hydronyms of Central and Eastern Europe: the Danube (Latin Danuvius), the Don, the Donets, the Dnieper, and the Dniester. Just as the Danube formed the grand imperial frontier separating the civilization of the Roman Empire from the vast barbarian forest of Germania and Scythia, the Anduin in Tolkien's legendarium forms the monumental geopolitical boundary between the civilized Dúnedain realm of Gondor and the untamed, shadow-haunted wastes of the East. Through this profound philological alchemy, Anduin resonates with authentic mythic and geographical reality, standing as one of the most enduring toponymic creations in modern world literature.
