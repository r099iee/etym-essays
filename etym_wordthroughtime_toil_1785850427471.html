- 12th–13th c. (French source senses): agitation, dragging about, struggle, disorder — perceived as physical turmoil more than virtuous work
- ME entry: toil as battle, strife, confusion, and severe exertion — military and conflictual overtones strong
- 14th–15th c.: growing link to hard bodily labor and hardship of peasantry and travelers
- Early Modern: moralized labor — "honest toil," vain toil; biblical and proverbial resonance; verb of painful progress
- 18th–19th c.: industrial and reform discourse — toil as the burden of the working classes; sentimental and political novel staple
- 20th c.–present: still marks exhausting continuous labor (physical or mental); slightly elevated/literary vs plain "work"; retains climb-struggle uses ("toil up the ridge"); less common in casual speech than in narrative and rhetoric
