• crimson: Similar because both are deep reds; differs because crimson is a cool, deep red with a hint of blue, from the kermes insect dye, while claret is warmer, more translucent, and wine-derived — a red with light in it.
  • scarlet: Similar because both are rich reds; differs because scarlet is a bright, fiery red tending to orange (traditionally wool dyed with kermes), while claret is darker, deeper, and carries the wine's luminosity.
  • ruby: Similar because both are deep jewel-reds; differs because ruby is the brilliant, hard red of the gemstone, while claret is the liquid, luminous red of the wine.
  • vermilion: Similar because both are vivid reds; differs because vermilion is an opaque, slightly orange red (the mercury pigment), while claret is translucent and deeper.
  • maroon: Similar because both are dark reds; differs because maroon is a brownish, flatter dark red, while claret keeps the clarity and brightness of wine.
  • burgundy: Similar because both are the reds of famous French wine regions; differs because burgundy is cooler and sometimes paler (the red of Burgundy), while claret is the deeper red of Bordeaux.
  • wine: Similar because both are the colour of red wine; differs because wine is the generic term for the wine-red colour, while claret specifies the particular deep translucent red of Bordeaux.
  • garnet: Similar because both are deep reds named for gemstones; differs because garnet is a dark, slightly brownish red, while claret is brighter and more transparent.
  • oxblood: Similar because both are deep, dark reds; differs because oxblood is a very dark, brownish red, while claret is more luminous and wine-clear.
  • cardinal: Similar because both are rich reds; differs because cardinal is the vivid red of church vestments, brighter and less liquid than claret.
  • cerise: Similar because both are reds; differs because cerise is a bright cherry-red, lighter and cooler, while claret is deep and warm.
  • amber: Similar because both are warm golden colours named for a natural material; differs because amber is the yellow-brown of fossil resin, softer and browner, while topaz is clearer, more brilliant, and warmed toward orange.
  • gold: Similar because both are golden yellows; differs because gold is the metallic sheen of the metal, while topaz is the gem's clear, crystalline golden tone.
  • golden: Similar because both mean golden-yellow; differs because golden is the general, everyday word, while topaz is the specific, jewel-like colour of the gem.
  • honey: Similar because both are warm golden yellows; differs because honey has a soft brown undertone and a suggestion of sweetness, while topaz is brighter, clearer, and mineral.
  • saffron: Similar because both are golden yellows; differs because saffron is a strong, orange-leaning yellow from the crocus stigma, more intense, while topaz is gentler and more translucent.
  • tawny: Similar because both are warm brown-orange-golds; differs because tawny is darker and duller, a brownish orange, while topaz is clear and brilliant.
  • ochre: Similar because both are yellows; differs because ochre is an earthy, opaque, dull yellow, while topaz is a luminous crystalline yellow.
  • citrine: Similar because both are yellow gemstone colours; differs because citrine is the paler lemon-yellow quartz, while topaz is the deeper golden-yellow.
  • xanthous: Similar because both mean yellow; differs because xanthous is a rare scientific term for yellow, while topaz is the living gem-colour word.
  • goldenrod: Similar because both are golden yellows; differs because goldenrod is a flat, solid yellow, while topaz is translucent and jewel-like.
  • brass: Similar because both are yellowish-gold colours; differs because brass is the dull metallic yellow of the alloy, while topaz is clear and gem-bright.
