West Germanic base *adal- "liquid filth, mire, dirty liquid" (cognates: Middle Low German adele "filth, dirt"; the deeper ancestry beyond West Germanic is uncertain — see note) (prehistoric West Germanic) → Old English adela "liquid filth, mire, dung-water, foul puddle" (recorded in Old English glosses and prose) → Middle English addle / adel "foul, putrid, rotten; empty, vain" — chiefly in addle egg / adel eye "a rotten or barren egg that fails to hatch" (13th–15th centuries) → Early Modern English addled "rotten (of eggs); empty, vain; (of the mind) confused, muddled" — via compounds addle-headed, addle-pated, addle-brained and the verb addle "to confuse, spoil (the wits)" (16th–17th centuries) → English addled (adjective) "confused, unable to think clearly; (of an egg) rotten, infertile" → ADDLED Note on debate: the jump from "rotten egg" to "muddled mind" is a native English metaphor (skull as shell, brain as contents that spoil). Beyond West Germanic, some handbooks discuss possible links to other Germanic filth/mire words, but the cognate set is small and no secure Indo-European root has been demonstrated; the deeper ancestry should be treated as unknown rather than invented.
