- [18th-century coinage] - 'Plumpish' appears as a soft-hewn variant of plump - somewhat rounded - applied to fruit, birds and people with an affectionate eye.
- [19th-century etiquette use] - In novels of manners, plumpish is the polite, fond word for a pleasingly rounded girl or child, without censure; a marker of health and good feeding.
- [20th-century body-conscious turn] - As thinness becomes the beauty ideal, plumpish turns from simple description toward euphemism - a careful, kind way to say 'slightly overweight'.
- [Contemporary register] - Gently affectionate or tactful; deliberately imprecise, softening the body to a pleasant middle ground between slender and plump.
