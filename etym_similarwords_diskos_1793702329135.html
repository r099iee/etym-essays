• Disc: Similar because it directly derives from Greek "diskos" and refers to a flat, circular object; differs because it often carries a more technical or scientific connotation (e.g., anatomical disc, optical disc, compact disc) and entered English via French, retaining the /sk/ sound.
  • Dish: Similar because it also derives from Greek "diskos" (via Latin and Old English) and originally referred to a flat plate; differs because its meaning has specialized to primarily denote a vessel for serving food, the food itself, or a parabolic antenna, and it underwent a phonetic shift to /ʃ/.
  • Plate: Similar because it refers to a flat, usually circular, object used for serving food or as a decorative item; differs because its etymology is from Old French "plat" (flat), not directly from "diskos," and it can be made of various materials and used in broader contexts (e.g., license plate, tectonic plate).
  • Platter: Similar because it denotes a large, flat dish or plate, typically oval or round, used for serving food; differs because it emphasizes size and serving function, and its etymology is from Old French "platel," a diminutive of "plat."
  • Quoit: Similar because it refers to a flat, circular ring thrown in a game, directly reflecting one of the ancient uses of "diskos"; differs because it is specific to a particular game and its etymology is uncertain, possibly from Old French "coite" (cushion).
  • Saucer: Similar because it is a small, shallow, circular dish, typically placed under a cup; differs because it is specifically designed as an accompaniment to a cup and its etymology is from Old French "sauciere" (sauce-dish).
