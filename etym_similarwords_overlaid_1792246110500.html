• Superimposed: Similar because both describe placing one entity directly on top of another; differs because superimposed emphasizes the conceptual, deliberate, or geometric addition of one image or layer onto another, whereas overlaid carries stronger physical, artisanal, and neurobiological substrate connotations.
  • Layered: Similar because both describe structures composed of multiple tiers; differs because layered describes the overall multi-tiered condition of a composite object, whereas overlaid specifically highlights the action or state of the upper layer being spread upon the bottom base.
  • Stratified: Similar because both involve structural superposition; differs because stratified is primarily a geological, sociological, or biological classification describing formal, distinct vertical beds or social ranks formed over time, whereas overlaid denotes one specific layer covering an existing surface.
  • Veneered: Similar because both involve applying a thin decorative layer to a surface; differs because veneered specifically implies cosmetic thin sheets of fine wood or deceptive facades designed to mask an inferior interior, whereas overlaid can be structural, protective, functional, or neural.
  • Cladded: Similar because both refer to bonding an outer protective layer to an underlying base; differs because cladded is restricted almost entirely to heavy industrial metallurgy, engineering, and architecture (e.g. fire-resistant wall cladding), whereas overlaid applies broadly across cognitive science, art, and computer networking.
  • Apposed: Similar because both describe putting surfaces in close physical proximity; differs because apposed is a formal anatomical and scientific term denoting placing structures side-by-side or in surface juxtaposition without requiring one to cover or dominate the other from above.
  • Projected: Similar because both can display visual imagery across a surface; differs because projected implies the optical transmission of light rays through space from a distance (as with a digital projector), whereas overlaid implies an integrated composite layer resting directly on the target plane.
  • Embedded: Similar because both integrate components into a larger structural system; differs because embedded means set deeply, firmly, and completely within a surrounding matrix, whereas overlaid remains resting on top of the foundation as an upper layer.
