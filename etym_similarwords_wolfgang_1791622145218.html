• Köhler: Similar because it denotes the exact same historical pioneer of Gestalt psychology (Wolfgang Köhler) when referenced in scientific literature; differs because 'Köhler' is the German occupational family surname (originally meaning 'charcoal-burner'), whereas 'Wolfgang' is his personal baptismal given name.
  • Wertheimer: Similar because Max Wertheimer was Köhler's closest intellectual collaborator and the founding father of the Gestalt movement who conducted the seminal 1910–1912 apparent motion experiments; differs because Wertheimer concentrated primarily on productive thinking and the foundational phi phenomenon, whereas Wolfgang Köhler extended Gestalt principles to animal cognition, electrophysiology, and field physics.
  • Koffka: Similar because Kurt Koffka was the third founding triumvir of the Berlin Gestalt school alongside Wertheimer and Köhler; differs because Koffka acted primarily as the systematic epistemologist and developmental theorist who introduced Gestalt theory to the English-speaking academic world, whereas Wolfgang Köhler was the empirical experimentalist and theoretical physicist of the group.
  • Lewin: Similar because Kurt Lewin belonged to the core Berlin Gestalt circle and applied field-theoretical models to human behavior; differs because Lewin focused on social psychology, dynamic group interactions, organizational psychology, and topological life-spaces, whereas Wolfgang focused on perceptual organization, physical field equilibria, and animal problem-solving.
  • Wolfram: Similar because it is an ancient Germanic dithematic masculine name sharing the identical prototheme *wolf* ('wolf'); differs because its second element derives from Proto-Germanic *\*hrabnaz* ('raven'), signifying 'wolf-raven' (a totemic pairing honoring the carrion beasts of Odin), whereas Wolfgang incorporates *gang* ('stride, gait, path').
  • Bernhard: Similar because both are classic High German dithematic names derived from fierce apex animal totems of early Germanic warrior culture; differs because Bernhard combines *bero* ('bear') and *hard* ('brave, hardy'), meaning 'bold as a bear', whereas Wolfgang signifies the stride or advancing path of the wolf.
  • Rudolf: Similar because both are traditional Germanic personal names rooted in the veneration of the wolf; differs because Rudolf derives from Old High German *Hruodolf*, combining *hruod* ('fame, glory') with *wolf* ('fame-wolf' or 'noble wolf'), focusing on renowned heraldic status rather than dynamic kinetic movement.
  • Sigmund: Similar because it is an illustrious Germanic masculine name intimately connected to twentieth-century psychological revolutions (namely Sigmund Freud's psychoanalysis); differs because Sigmund etymologically signifies 'protection through victory' (*sigu* + *mund*), and historically represents depth psychoanalysis rather than Köhler's Gestalt perceptual psychophysics.
