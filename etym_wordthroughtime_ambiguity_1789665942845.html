- Late medieval scholastic sense - a fault of logic and exegesis to be distinguished and removed; perception: technical vice, dangerous in scripture and law.
- Early modern rhetorical sense - equivocation and double-speak in politics and theatre; perception: morally suspect, associated with Jesuitical evasion and courtly flattery.
- 19th-century philological sense - comparative grammar and dictionary-making catalogue ambiguous forms; perception: problem to be solved by science of language.
- Modernist literary reversal - Empson 1930 and New Criticism revalue ambiguity as poetic virtue and density; perception: richness, universality, mark of serious literature.
- Contemporary expansive sense - tolerance of ambiguity as psychological health, constructive ambiguity in diplomacy, queer ambiguity as freedom; perception: often positive, a capacity for plural worlds rather than a failure of clarity.
