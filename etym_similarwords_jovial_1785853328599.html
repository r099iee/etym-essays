• Jolly: Similar because both mean merry and full of good spirits; differs because "jolly" is simple, festive, and boisterous (a jolly Santa), while "jovial" implies expansive, warm-hearted, refined conviviality.
  • Merry: Similar because both express cheerfulness; differs because "merry" is lighthearted, playful, and festive, while "jovial" has a fuller, grander, more hearty warmth.
  • Convivial: Similar because both describe warm friendliness; differs because "convivial" specifically emphasizes enjoyment of food, drink, and company together, while "jovial" is a personal temperament of cheer.
  • Genial: Similar because both describe warm, approachable friendliness; differs because "genial" is mild, pleasant, and even-tempered, without the loud, hearty laughter of "jovial".
  • Cheerful: Similar because both mean full of good spirits; differs because "cheerful" is a general, often lighter positivity, while "jovial" is specifically sociable, hearty, and expansive.
  • Good-humored: Similar because both describe an agreeable, un-irritable disposition; differs because "good-humored" emphasizes patience and tolerance of annoyances, while "jovial" emphasizes outgoing high spirits.
  • Hearty: Similar because both describe warm, robust friendliness; differs because "hearty" stresses vigor and sincerity of warmth (a hearty welcome), while "jovial" stresses mirth and good humor.
  • Buoyant: Similar because both suggest high spirits; differs because "buoyant" emphasizes resilience and bouncing back from difficulty, while "jovial" emphasizes sociable cheer regardless of circumstance.
  • Joyous: Similar because both relate to joy; differs because "joyous" describes an inner state of deep happiness, while "jovial" is an outgoing social demeanor of laughter and warmth.
  • Rollicking: Similar because both describe high-spirited, hearty enjoyment; differs because "rollicking" is carefree, rowdy, physical fun, while "jovial" is good-humored but composed and sociable.
