- c. 1300 (Middle English) - Borrowed from Old French *enoint* (past participle of *enoindre*), functioning strictly in biblical translations and royal coronation rites for holy consecration.
- 15th–16th Century (Tudor Era) - Expanded from divine kingship and sacraments to encompass medicinal applications of herbal salves and surgical unguents to wounds.
- 17th–19th Century (Stuart to Victorian Era) - Broadened to daily domestic grooming and cosmetology, describing the application of hair oils, pomades, and brilliantines (as in Conan Doyle's *The Blue Carbuncle*).
- 20th–21st Century (Modern Era) - Retained in religious liturgy while developing a ubiquitous figurative sense in political journalism for elite selection and succession of candidates.
