• Group: Similar because it denotes a number of people or things together; differs because a group is flat and neutral — no colour or motion — while a bevy implies a bright, animated, pleasing cluster.
  • Crowd: Similar because it describes many people together; differs because a crowd is dense, massed, and impersonal, whereas a bevy is light, elegant, and easily countable.
  • Throng: Similar because it names a multitude; differs because a throng presses and jostles — congestion — while a bevy suggests graceful abundance, not pressure.
  • Troop: Similar because it denotes an organised body of people; differs because a troop is disciplined, purposeful, and quasi-military, while a bevy is charming and unregimented.
  • Band: Similar because it names a company of people acting together; differs because a band is informal and cohesive and nowadays evokes musicians or criminals, not a feathery, ornamental cluster.
  • Flock: Similar because it is an animal-origin collective used of birds or people; differs because a flock applied to people suggests sheep-like following, whereas a bevy suggests lively, self-possessed attendance.
  • Gaggle: Similar because it is a collective noun for a bird group (geese) transferred to people; differs because a gaggle of people is noisy, disorderly, and faintly derisive, the opposite of a bevy's brightness.
  • Covey: Similar because it is a hunting collective for partridges, a near-relative of bevy among the terms of venery; differs because covey stays anchored to game birds and their broods, while bevy has broadened to people and things.
  • Swarm: Similar because it names a large moving group; differs because a swarm is dense, restless, and insect-like — a buzzing mass — while a bevy is light, countable, and charming.
  • Host: Similar because it denotes a large number of people; differs because a host is vast and indefinite, nearer to an army or a multitude, while a bevy is small-to-medium and gracefully delineated.
  • Clutch: Similar because it is a collective for eggs or chicks, sharing bevy's bird-origin; differs because a clutch stays in the brood/litter sense and never gained bevy's feminine or journalistic elegance.
  • Collection: Similar because it denotes an assemblage of things; differs because a collection is static, gathered by design (art, stamps), while a bevy is a living, moving, spontaneous gathering.
