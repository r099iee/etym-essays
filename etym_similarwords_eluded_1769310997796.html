• Evaded: Similar because both mean avoiding capture or pursuit; differs because evaded stresses clever manoeuvring and often a rule or obligation, while eluded stresses a slippery, almost playful escape that leaves the pursuer baffled.
  • Escaped: Similar because both mean getting free of confinement or danger; differs because escaped is broader and may be accidental or desperate, while eluded implies the pursuer's failure and the escapee's skill.
  • Dodged: Similar because both describe quick avoidance; differs because dodged is typically a swift physical duck or sidestep, while eluded extends to intellectual and prolonged evasion.
  • Avoided: Similar because both mean keeping away from something; differs because avoided is often a deliberate staying away beforehand, whereas eluded is escaping something already present or directed at one.
  • Fled: Similar because both describe departure from pursuit; differs because fled suggests urgent running away in fear, while eluded suggests composed, skilful evasion.
  • Slipped away: Similar because both describe unobtrusive departure; differs because slipped away is colloquial and emphasises secrecy, while eluded is formal and emphasises the pursuer's defeat.
  • Baffled: Similar because both connect to defeating the mind ("the answer eluded/baffled him"); differs because baffled describes the confusion caused in the mind, while eluded describes the thing's escape from that mind.
  • Circumvented: Similar because both mean getting around an obstacle or rule; differs because circumvented implies outwitting a system, regulation, or person through strategy, while eluded is less strategic and more nimble.
  • Parried: Similar because both mean deflecting an attack; differs because parried is specifically a fencing-style defence against a single thrust, while eluded covers broader and longer evasion.
  • Outwitted: Similar because both involve winning by cleverness; differs because outwitted means beating another's thinking in a contest of wits, while eluded focuses on remaining beyond reach.
  • Deceived: Similar because both involve tricking a pursuer; differs because deceived means causing someone to believe a falsehood, while eluded means avoiding capture without necessarily lying.
