- Middle English pile "a pointed stake" - a builder's term borrowed from Old French, naming the timber driven into riverbeds and soft ground to carry bridges and houses.
- Lake-dwellings and medieval bridges - the word anchors the image of pile-built settlements and pile bridges in chronicles, travel writing, and antiquarian accounts.
- 18th–19th c. - the Industrial Age: 'pile-driving' machinery, then iron and later concrete piles; the word enters the professional vocabulary of civil engineering.
- Modern usage - "piles" (plural) is the everyday plural for these driven supports; meanwhile the identical spelling has absorbed the older "heap" word, so "piles" today can mean driven stakes, heaps or stacks, and (in British informality) haemorrhoids.
