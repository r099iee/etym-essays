- Welsh origin - Mordred begins as Medraut, a shadowy figure in Welsh tradition who is Arthur's nephew, with the villain's role barely developed.
- Geoffrey of Monmouth and later romance - Geoffrey's History of the Kings of Britain (12th century) transformed him into the full usurper-traitor who seizes queen and throne while Arthur is abroad, and later versions made him Arthur's incestuous son.
- Malory and the tragic close - in Malory and the great romances he meets Arthur in the mutually fatal Battle of Camlann, so his name became the emblem of the disaster that ends the Round Table and the golden age of Logres.
- Contemporary sense - Mordred remains the archetype of treacherous kin and doomed usurpation in Arthurian legend, the political traitor whose betrayal destroys the very fellowship it betrays.
