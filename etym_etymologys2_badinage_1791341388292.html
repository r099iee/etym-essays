Imitative Root (Pre-Romance)
*bat-
"to gape; echoic of opening the lips"
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 4th–6th c.)
*batare
"to gape, yawn, stand open-mouthed"
↓
Old Occitan (c. 12th c.)
badar
"to gape, stare foolishly"
↓
Old Occitan (c. 13th c.)
badin
"a simpleton, gaping fool"
↓
Middle French (c. 14th c.)
badin
"foolish, playful, jesting clown"
↓
Middle French (c. 15th c.)
badiner
"to play the fool, joke, banter"
↓
French (c. 16th c.)
badinage
"playful talk, jesting, banter"
↓
English (c. 1673)
badinage
"light, playful conversation or banter"

Note: The Vulgar Latin reconstructed base *batare is universally accepted by Romance philologists as an onomatopoeic creation mimicking the wide opening of the mouth in yawning or gaping, surviving also in French bayer aux corneilles and English abash.
