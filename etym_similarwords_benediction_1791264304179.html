• Blessing: Similar because it means invoking good or divine favour; differs because it is the general everyday word, while benediction is formal, liturgical, and solemn.
  • Invocation: Similar because it calls upon the divine in worship; differs because it opens prayer by summoning presence, while benediction closes it by bestowing favour.
  • Consecration: Similar because it makes something holy through rite; differs because it dedicates persons or things permanently, while benediction briefly blesses people departing.
  • Absolution: Similar because it is a priestly utterance conferring spiritual benefit; differs because it specifically remits sin, while benediction merely wishes good.
  • Beatitude: Similar because it concerns blessedness and divine favour; differs because it denotes a state of bliss or a Gospel saying, not a spoken rite.
  • Grace: Similar because it can mean a short blessing prayer; differs because it usually refers to thanks before meals or to unmerited divine favour generally.
  • Orison: Similar because it is a formal prayer; differs because it is literary and general, while benediction is specifically a blessing formula.
  • Commendation: Similar because it expresses approval and goodwill; differs because it is secular praise, lacking benediction's sacred invocation.
  • Sanction: Similar because in the extended sense it means authoritative approval, as in giving one's benediction to a plan; differs because it is legalistic and can also mean penalty.
  • Approbation: Similar because it means formal approval or endorsement; differs because it is secular and administrative, while benediction carries spiritual warmth.
  • Anointing: Similar because it confers holiness or favour; differs because it involves oil and appointment to office, more material than spoken benediction.
  • Doxology: Similar because it is a liturgical formula of praise; differs because it praises God upward, while benediction speaks blessing downward to the people.
