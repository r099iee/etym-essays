- Early 17th Century (1613): Emerged as a prominent Russian boyar patronymic dynasty when sixteen-year-old Mikhail Romanov was elected Tsar by the Zemsky Sobor, ending the catastrophic "Time of Troubles" and establishing the House of Romanov.
- 18th Century (Peter the Great & Catherine the Great): Transformed from a traditional Muscovite Tsardom into a colossal, Westernized European Empire, with the dynasty adopting the official title of Emperor (Imperator) of All the Russias and projecting immense military power.
- 19th Century: Solidified the doctrine of "Orthodoxy, Autocracy, and Nationality," ruling as the absolute autocrats of one-sixth of the globe, while facing growing peasant unrest, intellectual dissent, and revolutionary terrorism.
- Early 20th Century (1900–1917): Entered its final "twilight" phase under Emperor Nicholas II, marked by intense industrialization, dazzling cultural flourishing (the Silver Age), disastrous wars, political ossification, and eventual collapse in the February Revolution of 1917.
- Post-1918 to Contemporary Era: Following the execution of Nicholas II and his family in 1918, the name became a historical and cultural metonym for imperial opulence, tragic dynastic downfall, Faberge craftsmanship, and the lost grandeur of pre-Soviet Russia.
