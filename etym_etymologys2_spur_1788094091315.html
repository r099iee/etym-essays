Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed)
*spere-
"ankle"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, 1st millennium BCE)
*spuron
"spur (the instrument worn on the ankle)"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c. CE)
spora, spura
"spur for urging a horse"
↓
Middle English (13th–14th c.)
spure, spore
"spur; anything that urges on"
↓
Modern English (spelling fixed by 16th c.)
spur

Note: Two derivations are current. The mainstream one, given above, derives the Germanic word from PIE *spere- 'ankle' — the spur is the instrument worn on the heel — with Greek sphuron 'ankle' as the key cognate (compare Latin calcar 'spur', from calx 'heel', which names the instrument by the same body part). An alternative connects it with the Germanic base of spurn ('to kick'), making the spur 'the kicking instrument'. Both are defensible; neither is certain beyond the Germanic stage.
