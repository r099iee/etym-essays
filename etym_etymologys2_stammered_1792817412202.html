Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE)
*stamra-
“stammering, faltering”
“in speech”

stamr

stamerian

↓
Middle English (14th c.)
stameren

↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
stammer

↓
Modern English (current)
stammered
“past of stammer”

Note: the deeper PIE source is debated: handbooks often connect the Germanic set to a root meaning to knock against or stumble (compare the family of stem and stumble), with speech stumbling like feet — but an imitative origin for the syllable-repeating sound itself is also proposed, so no single PIE ancestor is asserted with certainty.
