PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000–3500 BCE)
*bhel-
"to blow, swell, grow"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–200 CE)
*belkanan
"to belch, burst out with sound"
↓
Old English (c. 1000)
bealcan
"to belch, eructate"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
belchen
"to belch; to discharge violently"
↓
Modern English (from c. 1500)
belch
"to expel gas from the stomach; to hurl out fire, smoke"
↓
Past-tense inflection (regular -ed)
belched

Note on debate: the link to PIE *bhel- "to blow, swell" is the standard reconstruction, but some scholars argue the word is primarily imitative/onomatopoeic (the sound of a burp) and that its Germanic relatives (OE bealgor "anger, fury", OE belg "bag, bellows" from *belg-) merely cluster around the same sound-symbolic core. The Old English bealcan is securely attested; the reconstructed stages above it are scholarly inference, not direct evidence.
