CHAIN 1 — THE CELTIC ELEMENT (Celt-): Stage 1 — PIE *ḱel- "to hide, conceal, cover" (proposed root of the Celtic ethnonym; DEBATED — a rival school derives the name from PIE *ḱel- "to strike, drive", making the Celts "the hidden ones" or "the warriors"; the deeper root is genuinely uncertain). Stage 2 — Proto-Celtic *kelto- "the Celts" (reconstructed self-name of the Celtic peoples; not directly attested, but the basis of every later form). Stage 3 — Greek Κελτοί Keltoí "the Celts" (attested from the 6th century BC, e.g. in Hecataeus of Miletus; used by Herodotus, 5th century BC). Stage 4 — Latin Celtae "the Celts" (1st century BC: Caesar's Bellum Gallicum, Pliny, the geographers). Stage 5 — Latin Celtiberī "the Celtiberians" — compound of Celtae + Hiberī, literally "the Celts among the Iberi" (attested in the sources for the Celtiberian wars, 2nd century BC: Polybius, Appian, Livy). Stage 6 — English Celtiberian "of or relating to the Celtiberians or their language" (17th century, from Latin Celtiberī + the adjective suffix -ian < Latin -iānus). FINAL NODE: CELTIBERIAN. DEBATE NOTE: the identification of the root of Keltoí is contested (Pokorny's "to hide" vs the rival "to strike" theory); the attested Greco-Latin chain from Keltoí onward is secure. CHAIN 2 — THE IBERIAN ELEMENT (Iber-): Stage 1 — Pre-Indo-European (possibly Vasconic / Basque-related) substrate element *Ibēr "the Ebro (river)" — the ancient river-name, by extension the regional name of the peninsula. NO PIE root exists: this is non-Indo-European material. The comparison with Basque ibai "river" and ibar "valley" is probable but UNPROVEN and must be flagged as a hypothesis. Stage 2 — Greek Ἴβηρ Ibēr "the Ebro; the country of the Iberi" (Greek geographers applied the name first to the river and its valley). Stage 3 — Greek Ἰβηρία Ibēría "Iberia, the peninsula" (attested from the 5th–4th century BC) and Greek Ἴβηρες Ibēres "the Iberians" (the native, non-Celtic coastal peoples). Stage 4 — Latin Hiberia / Iberia "Iberia" and Latin Hiberī / Iberī "the Iberians" (the peoples of the eastern and southern coasts of Spain). Stage 5 — Latin Celtiberī "the Celts among the Iberians" — the fusion-ethnonym formed by the Roman historians of the 2nd century BC. Stage 6 — English Celtiberian (17th century). FINAL NODE: CELTIBERIAN. NOTE: the two chains converge at the Latin compound Celtiberī and produce one English word; the Iber- element has no Indo-European pedigree, and its Basque connection is probable-but-unproven, not a fact.
