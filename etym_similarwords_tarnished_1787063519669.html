• Discoloured: Similar because both describe a loss of original colour; differs because tarnished specifically denotes the dulling of a polished metal surface through oxidation, while discoloured is broader and applies to any colour change from any cause.
  • Dull: Similar because both describe a loss of brightness or lustre; differs because dull is a general adjective, whereas tarnished implies a surface once shiny and now dimmed by chemical reaction, and also carries a moral or metaphorical charge.
  • Tainted: Similar because both can describe a spoiled reputation or purity; differs because tainted suggests contamination or infection of something originally pure, while tarnished suggests the dimming of a formerly bright surface.
  • Stained: Similar because both indicate disfigurement of a surface; differs because a stain is a coloured mark deposited on the surface from outside, whereas tarnish is a film of corrosion forming on the surface itself.
  • Oxidised: Similar because both describe a surface reaction of metals with air; differs because oxidised is a precise scientific term naming a specific chemical process, while tarnished is the everyday, value-laden word for the visible result.
  • Rusted: Similar because both describe metal corrosion; differs because rusting specifically refers to iron and steel forming reddish, flaking iron oxide that destroys the metal, whereas tarnish is a superficial film on silver, copper, or brass that can be polished away.
  • Dimmed: Similar because both describe loss of brightness; differs because dimmed mainly applies to light, lamps, or vision, while tarnished applies to surfaces and reputations.
  • Besmirched: Similar because both describe a soiled or sullied reputation; differs because besmirched is more literary and suggests active smearing, while tarnished is the commoner, milder metaphor.
  • Discredited / Defamed: Similar because both describe damage to reputation; differs because these are active verbs describing someone’s hostile actions against another, whereas tarnished is often a passive, gradual state.
  • Soiled: Similar because both describe a loss of cleanliness or purity; differs because soiled implies dirt added from outside, while tarnished implies a chemical change in the surface itself.
