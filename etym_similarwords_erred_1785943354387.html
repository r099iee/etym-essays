• BLUNDERED: Similar because both mean to have made a mistake; differs because blundered implies a clumsy, careless, often foolish mistake of some consequence, with a slightly comic or condemnatory tone, while erred is formal, dignified, and morally or intellectually neutral — a court errs, a wise man can err, but one blunders clumsily.
  • MISTAKEN: Similar because both express being wrong; differs because mistaken is the everyday adjective of being in error about facts or beliefs (I was mistaken about the date), while erred is the formal verb of having made an error, emphasizing the act rather than the state.
  • STRAYED: Similar because both carry the root sense of wandering from the right path, and both are used morally; differs because strayed keeps the physical sense of wandering (a strayed sheep, a strayed bullet) and is the milder moral verb of drifting off course, while erred is the dignified word for a formal mistake or misjudgment.
  • WANDERED: Similar because both descend from the same Latin root errāre "to wander" and share the underlying image of leaving the road; differs because wandered is entirely literal and spatial today, with no sense of mistake or wrongdoing, while erred has moved almost wholly into the intellectual and moral meaning of being wrong.
  • MISCALCULATED: Similar because both mean to have made an error; differs because miscalculated is specific to arithmetic or planning, implying a wrong computation or estimate, while erred is general, covering every kind of mistake from factual to moral.
  • MISJUDGED: Similar because both can mean to have come to a wrong conclusion; differs because misjudged names a specific error of assessment (of a person, situation, or distance), while erred is the broader verb of being wrong in any respect.
  • SLIPPED: Similar because both can describe a minor error; differs because slipped suggests a small, accidental lapse (a slip of the tongue) and is informal, while erred is formal and can describe grave errors of judgment or law.
  • DEVIATED: Similar because both mean to depart from a correct course; differs because deviated is the neutral, technical verb of departing from a path, standard, or norm without the moral gravity of erred, which retains the sense of a genuine mistake or straying from truth.
  • SINNED: Similar because both carry the moral sense of straying from right conduct; differs because sinned is exclusively religious and moral, meaning to violate divine or moral law, while erred is primarily about being mistaken and only secondarily and lightly about moral failure.
  • TRANSGRESSED: Similar because both can mean to go beyond the bounds of right conduct; differs because transgression is a deliberate crossing of a law or limit, a serious offence, while erring may be entirely unintentional — one errs by being wrong, one transgresses by breaking a rule.
  • MISSED THE MARK: Similar because both mean to have failed to hit what was right or correct; differs because this idiom keeps the archery image and is informal and vivid, while erred is the formal, abstract verb of being mistaken.
  • GOOFED / SCREWED UP / MESSED UP: Similar because all mean to have made a mistake; differs because these are slang and informal, implying carelessness and often something mundane, while erred is the dignified, formal verb of genuine or solemn error.
  • FUMBLED / FLUBBED: Similar because both mean to have performed badly or made a clumsy error; differs because these describe clumsy execution of a task, with a comic tone, while erred describes a mistake of judgment or fact without any implication of clumsiness.
  • BOTCHED: Similar because both can describe a failed or mistaken piece of work; differs because botched means ruined through incompetence or clumsiness (a botched job, a botched surgery), while erred is a mistake in judgment that may be made by the most competent of people.
