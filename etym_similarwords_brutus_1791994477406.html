• Judas Iscariot: Similar because both names serve as Western civilization's primary archetypes of catastrophic, intimate betrayal of a supreme benefactor; differs because Judas acted for personal pecuniary gain (thirty pieces of silver) within a sacred theological narrative, whereas Brutus acted out of constitutional republican ideology, philosophical principle, and ancestral duty within a secular political sphere.
  • Cassius: Similar because Gaius Cassius Longinus was Brutus's closest ally, brother-in-law, and co-leader of the conspiracy to assassinate Julius Caesar, dying by his own hand at Philippi; differs because Cassius was characterized (historically by Plutarch and dramatically by Shakespeare) as driven by pragmatic envy, personal resentment, and military cynicism ("a lean and hungry look"), whereas Brutus was renowned for solemn Stoic virtue and moral hesitation.
  • Benedict Arnold: Similar because both historical figures became synonymous with treason and treachery against their own political community; differs because Arnold was a military commander who committed high treason by defecting to enemy British forces during wartime in exchange for money and rank, whereas Brutus was an urban magistrate executing tyrannicide against an internal domestic autocrat to preserve native republican institutions.
  • Guy Fawkes: Similar because both orchestrated covert political conspiracies to assassinate heads of state in the name of an overarching political-religious ideology; differs because Fawkes was an insurgent operative involved in a mass-casualty terrorist demolition plot (the Gunpowder Plot of 1605) against Parliament and the King, whereas Brutus was a high-ranking sitting magistrate performing targeted tyrannicide with daggers inside the Senate house.
  • Quisling: Similar because both proper names transformed into nominalized epithets for someone who betrays their homeland or leaders; differs because Vidkun Quisling collaborated directly with foreign Nazi invaders to run a puppet government in occupied Norway, whereas Brutus fought against foreign influence and imperial autocracy to defend the traditional Roman senatorial constitution.
  • Ephialtes of Trachis: Similar because both are immortalized in classical antiquity for treacherous acts that sealed the doom of their comrades; differs because Ephialtes betrayed the Spartan defenders at Thermopylae to the Persians purely for financial gain, lacking any philosophical, ethical, or patriotic pretense.
