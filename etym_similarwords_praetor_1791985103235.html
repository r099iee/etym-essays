• consul: Similar because it was the next and higher office on the same career ladder, held by the same kind of senator with the same imperium; differs because the consul was the senior magistrate who gave his name to the year and commanded the main armies, while the praetor was subordinate in rank and specialised in jurisdiction.
  • quaestor: Similar because it was the junior magistracy on the same cursus and carried financial and administrative duties; differs because the quaestor had no imperium and no judicial power of his own, and the praetorship was a far more senior and elective honour.
  • aedile: Similar because it was another step on the same ladder and often held shortly before the praetorship; differs because the aedile's sphere was the city — markets, streets, games and public order — rather than law and command.
  • censor: Similar because it was a senior Roman magistracy of great prestige; differs because the censorship was irregular, held usually by ex-consuls, and concerned the census and morals rather than jurisdiction, so it sat outside the normal sequence.
  • tribune of the plebs: Similar because a tribune could also propose legislation and intervene in legal proceedings; differs because the tribunate was plebeian, sacrosanct and veto-based, a counter-power rather than a command, and it conferred no imperium.
  • propraetor: Similar because it is the same man with the same imperium after his year of office; differs because a propraetor governed a province abroad and acted in a pro-magisterial capacity, whereas the praetor proper exercised his power in Rome.
  • proconsul: Similar because it is the corresponding extension of the consulship and also denotes a provincial governor; differs because a proconsul outranked a propraetor territorially and socially, and the title marked a higher original office.
  • praefectus: Similar because prefects also governed, judged and commanded in the Roman system; differs because a prefect was an appointed delegate, usually of equestrian rank, holding power on behalf of someone else, whereas a praetor was an elected magistrate holding his own imperium.
  • iudex: Similar because both words belong to the administration of justice; differs because the iudex was a private juror or arbitrator who heard a single case under a formula, while the praetor was the magistrate who framed the formula and controlled access to law.
