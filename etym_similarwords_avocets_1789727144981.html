• stilts: Similar because they are the sister Recurvirostridae waders of shallow lagoons with extremely long legs; differs because stilts have straight needle bills and pink-red legs and more piercing calls, while avocets have upcurved bills and blue-grey legs.
  • godwits: Similar because they are large long-billed waders of estuaries probing for worms; differs because godwits have long straight or slightly uptilted bills used for deep probing, not side-sweeping, and browner mottled plumage.
  • curlews: Similar because they are large waders with long curved bills feeding on tidal flats; differs because curlew bills curve downward, not upward, and birds are brown-streaked and famous for bubbling calls rather than pied and colonial.
  • sandpipers: Similar because they are small to medium probing waders of shores and pools; differs because sandpipers are generally smaller, shorter-legged, with straight bills and rapid pecking rather than tactile sweeping.
  • plovers: Similar because they are compact waders nesting in scrapes on shingle and sharing avocet breeding beaches; differs because plovers have short pigeon-like bills and run-stop-peck feeding, not long scything bills.
  • oystercatchers: Similar because they are bold pied waders of estuaries with loud piping calls; differs because oystercatchers have heavy straight orange-red bills for hammering shellfish and stockier build.
  • ibises: Similar because they are long-legged waders with long decurved bills sweeping for invertebrates; differs because ibises are larger, often dark or scarlet, with downcurved bills and very different family and voice.
  • spoonbills: Similar because they are elegant pale waders sweeping shallow water with specialised bills; differs because spoonbill bills are flattened spatulate, not fine and recurved, and birds are much larger heron-like.
  • phalaropes: Similar because they are delicate waders that swim and spin to stir prey; differs because phalaropes are tiny, lobed-toed swimmers with straight bills and reversed sex roles, not colonial lagoon sweepers.
