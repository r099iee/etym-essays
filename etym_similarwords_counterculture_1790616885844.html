• subculture: Similar because it denotes a distinct group with its own norms; differs because a subculture merely differs from the mainstream, while a counterculture actively opposes and seeks to replace it.
  • underground: Similar because it suggests hidden, non-mainstream art and politics; differs because it stresses secrecy and marginal distribution rather than explicit ideological opposition.
  • alternative culture / alternative movement: Similar because it offers a different lifestyle; differs because it is softer and more neutral, implying choice rather than confrontation.
  • bohemia: Similar because it evokes artistic nonconformity and unconventional life; differs because it centres on artists and poverty-chic rather than broad political revolt.
  • avant-garde: Similar because it breaks aesthetic and social rules; differs because it is primarily artistic and forward-looking, often elite, rather than mass-movement opposition.
  • dissent / dissidence: Similar because it expresses principled opposition; differs because it is political and often individual, lacking the full lived lifestyle implied by counterculture.
  • protest movement: Similar because it mobilises against authority; differs because it is event- and issue-focused, while counterculture implies an entire way of life.
  • nonconformity: Similar because it rejects conformity; differs because it can be purely individual and apolitical, whereas counterculture is collective and programmatic.
  • radicalism: Similar because it seeks root change; differs because it stresses doctrine and politics over culture, music, dress and daily life.
