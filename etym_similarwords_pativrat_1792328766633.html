• Sādhvī: Similar because both denote a virtuous, chaste, and spiritually elevated Hindu woman; differs because sādhvī emphasizes general moral purity, saintliness, or even an ascetic/monastic lifestyle, whereas pativratā specifically and exclusively requires marital devotion and fidelity to a living or deceased husband.
  • Sahadharmiṇī: Similar because both describe a dedicated Hindu wife in relation to her husband; differs because sahadharmiṇī emphasizes equal co-partnership in the performance of sacred religious rituals and moral duties (literally "she who practices dharma together"), whereas pativratā focuses on the singular, sacrificial vow of devotion to the husband.
  • Ardhāṅginī: Similar because both describe the sacred role of a wife in Hindu marital cosmology; differs because ardhāṅginī is an ontological and metaphysical title meaning "she who forms half of the husband's body" (rooted in the androgynous Ardhanārīśvara ideal), rather than an active ascetic vow of devotion.
  • Suvratā: Similar because both refer to a woman who maintains strict, virtuous vows; differs because suvratā can refer to any woman practicing commendable vows or religious fasts in general, lacking the explicit, lifelong marital focus of pativratā.
  • Chaste: Similar because both describe unwavering sexual fidelity and moral purity; differs because chaste is an English adjective denoting restraint, abstinence, or fidelity in a broad moral sense, whereas pativratā is an active spiritual archetype endowed with cosmic power derived from conjugal devotion.
  • Devotee: Similar because both involve deep, unshakeable loyalty and reverence toward an individual or deity; differs because a devotee typically implies religious veneration of a god or guru (bhakta), whereas a pativratā directs that divine intensity directly toward her wedded spouse as an earthly divinity.
  • Helpmate: Similar because both refer to a supportive wife aiding her partner through life's trials; differs because helpmate is a pragmatic, domestic English descriptor lacking the transcendent, supernatural, and ascetic connotations inherent in pativratā.
  • Consort: Similar because both denote a wife, especially one paired with a ruler or divine figure; differs because consort is a formal, institutional title of royal or celestial status, without implying the personal moral asceticism and sacred vows defining a pativratā.
