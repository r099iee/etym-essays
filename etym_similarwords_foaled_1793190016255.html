• Foal (noun): Similar because it is the same root naming the newborn horse itself; differs because foal the noun is the young animal while foaled the verb form is the completed act of its birth.
  • Colt: Similar because a colt is a young male horse freshly foaled; differs because colt specifies sex and youth while foaled names the birth event regardless of sex.
  • Filly: Similar because a filly is a young female horse freshly foaled; differs because filly specifies the female young while foaled covers any birth of horse-kind.
  • Yearling: Similar because every foaled foal becomes a yearling at twelve months; differs because yearling is the next age stage while foaled fixes the birth moment itself.
  • Calved: Similar because it is the exact bovine twin — a cow calves as a mare foals; differs because calved belongs to cattle and also to icebergs while foaled belongs strictly to horses and their kin.
  • Lambed: Similar because a ewe lambs in the same species-specific birth pattern; differs because lambed is sheepfold vocabulary while foaled is stable vocabulary.
  • Whelped: Similar because a bitch whelps as a mare foals, each verb guarding its species; differs because whelped serves dogs and wolves while foaled serves horses, zebras, and asses.
  • Farrowed: Similar because a sow farrows with the same birth-verb logic; differs because farrowed means a litter of piglets while foaled almost always means a single precious foal.
  • Born: Similar because was foaled and was born state the same fact of coming into the world; differs because born is universal and neutral while foaled is equine, technical, and pedigree-flavoured.
