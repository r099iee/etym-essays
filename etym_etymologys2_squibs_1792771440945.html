Middle English (c. 14th c.)
squyrt / squirt
"sudden jet or spray of liquid"
(imitative echoic sound-stem)
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1520s)
squib
"small paper pipe filled with gunpowder"
(imitative of hissing, spitting sparks)
↓
Elizabethan English (c. 1580s)
squib
"short, sharp satirical essay or lampoon"
(metaphoric shift: explosive paper missile)
↓
Victorian English (19th c.)
squib (damp squib)
"an anticlimactic failure; a fizzle"
↓
Modern English (plural form)
squibs
"small explosive fireworks; lampoons"
