- 1520s–1600s “the fire-people of the straits” - Spanish fueguinos as travellers’ exotics glimpsed from ships; frightening fires on unknown shores.
- 1830s–1870s “Darwin’s Fuegians” - Voyage of the Beagle makes them world-famous as the supposed ‘lowest’ savages; pity, disgust, and scientific excitement mix.
- 1880s–1930s “textbook primitives” - histories of civilisation (the source passage’s era) use Fuegians as Exhibit A of omnivorous, raw-eating ‘nature peoples’; connotation condescending but earnestly ethnographic.
- Mid-20th c. “victims and informants” - as the Selk’nam genocide becomes known and Bridges’/Gusinde’s work circulates, perception shifts to tragedy and belated respect.
- Present “heritage ethnonym” - blanket “Fuegian” recedes before Yaghan, Kawésqar, Selk’nam; surviving communities revive language and canoe heritage; the old word now signals historical literature.
