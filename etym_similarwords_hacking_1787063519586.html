• Slashing: Similar because both involve striking with an edged weapon; differs because slashing implies a wide, sweeping, sweeping lateral stroke that produces long, shallow cuts with the tip or edge of a blade, whereas hacking implies a downward, perpendicular, heavy chopping blow that bites deep into wood or bone.
  • Chopping: Similar because both involve heavy downward blows; differs because chopping is a neutral, purposeful, and structured mechanical term (e.g., chopping firewood, chopping vegetables), whereas hacking conveys roughness, unevenness, wildness, or frantic combat.
  • Cleaving: Similar because both mean splitting with a heavy blade; differs because cleaving is a high-literary, monumental term denoting splitting something completely in two with immense, clean, heroic force (e.g., cleaving a helmet in twain).
  • Severing: Similar because both involve cutting; differs because severing is an objective, clinical result denoting the complete physical separation or cutting off of a part from the whole, without specifying the rough manner of striking.
  • Mutilating: Similar because both can describe damaging flesh; differs because mutilating centers entirely on the horrific, permanent disfigurement or destruction of bodily integrity.
  • Cracking: Similar in tech domains (cracking security); differs because cracking specifically denotes breaking through cryptographic ciphers, password hashes, or software copy-protection locks through algorithmic calculation.
  • Infiltrating: Similar in cyber warfare; differs because infiltrating emphasizes stealthy, undetected entry behind enemy lines over a long duration rather than aggressive technical exploitation.
