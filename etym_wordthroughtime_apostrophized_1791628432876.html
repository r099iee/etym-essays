- Early 17th c. – Rhetorical diversion & dramatic oratory - Denoted an orator or stage actor breaking away from the dialogue to deliver an impassioned speech to an absent figure, deity, or inanimate object.
- Mid-17th to 18th c. – Orthographic elision & typographical punctuation - Developed its secondary technical printing sense for inserting an apostrophe mark to signify omitted letters or dialectal contractions.
- Late 18th to 19th c. – Romantic lyricism & sublime nature poetry - Reached its artistic zenith in Romantic poetry, where poets apostrophized the ocean, the seasons, urns, and nightingales in ecstatic communion.
- Early 20th c. – Anthropological animism & cultural history - Employed by historians and anthropologists (such as Will Durant) to describe traditional peoples directly addressing their frozen soil, crops, or animal spirits.
- Contemporary – Literary structural analysis & technical typography - Retained in literary criticism to analyze dramatic rhetoric and lyric figures, while functioning in grammar to denote inserting apostrophe marks.
