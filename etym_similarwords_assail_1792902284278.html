• Attack: Similar because both mean to fall upon with hostile force; differs because attack is the plain general word for all offensives, while assail is literary and stresses sudden leaping violence.
  • Assault: Similar because both name a vigorous hostile onset, the noun assault being assail's closest kin; differs because assault is often legal and technical, a charged crime, while assail ranges freely over words, doubts, and odours too.
  • Beset: Similar because both describe being set upon from all sides; differs because beset stresses encirclement and persistent trouble, while assail stresses the launching of the attack itself.
  • Storm: Similar because both picture rushing violently upon a defence; differs because storm names the massed final rush on a fortress, while assail covers any attack from a fist to a headline.
  • Besiege: Similar because both are hostile pressure against resistance; differs because besiege is slow encirclement over time, while assail is the sudden leap and blow.
  • Pounce on: Similar because both capture the leaping surprise at the word's root; differs because pounce on is informal and often gleeful, a cat or a critic seizing a slip, while assail stays grave and martial.
  • Lambaste: Similar because both can mean a fierce verbal attack; differs because lambaste is colloquial and noisy, a beating with words, while assail is dignified enough for doubts and fortresses alike.
  • Harass: Similar because both wear a target down through hostile pressure; differs because harass is repeated nibbling attrition, while assail is the launched onset, one fierce spring.
