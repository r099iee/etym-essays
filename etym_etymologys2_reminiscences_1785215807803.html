PIE *men- "to think, remember, have in mind, be mindful of" (reconstructed root, c. 4th–3rd millennium BCE; attested across Anatolian, Indo-Iranian, Greek, Italic, Celtic, Germanic, and Balto-Slavic: Hittite mema- "to speak, recite", Sanskrit mánas- "mind", Greek mnēmē "memory", Latin meminī "I remember", Old English munan "to remember", Gothic muns "thought") → Latin meminī "I remember, I have in mind" (perfect-stative verb, originally *memnos-; attested from the 2nd c. BCE) → Latin reminīscī "to call back to mind, to recollect" (classical Latin; prefix re- "again" + inchoative stem minisci, from the root of meminī) → Latin reminiscentia "recollection, the active retrieval of stored memories" (Late Latin; in the medieval schools distinguished from passive memoria, the storage of impressions) → English reminiscence "the act of recalling past experience" (attested c. 1658; learned borrowing from Latin) → Modern English plural REMINISCENCES "published recollections; the literary genre of retrospective memoir" (the plural genre-sense established in the 18th–19th c.)
