English (first attested 1701) fuss "a flurry of anxious bustle; needless commotion" ↓ English (18th c.) fuss "to be overbusy, to fidget" (verb); "fussy" (adjective) ↓ English (19th c.) fuss "a fuss over / of — excessive doting attention" ↓ English (Modern) FUSS "excessive excitement, concern, or attention;" "to make a fuss" Note on origin: fuss first appears in English writing in 1701 with no known earlier ancestor. Most authorities treat it as echoic/imitative — a word mimicking the fidgety, bustling sound of needless activity — and no PIE or Latin chain can be honestly documented, so the chain above is deliberately short.
