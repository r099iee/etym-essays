- Late 16th Century (1590s): Medical and anatomical Latinate borrowing - Introduced in anatomical treatises and obstetrical translations as a clinical, dignified term for pregnancy and carrying offspring.
- 17th to 18th Century: Natural history and agricultural expansion - Extended across zoology, botany, and husbandry to describe animals carrying eggs, fertile soil, and trees weighed down with fruit.
- 19th Century: Victorian biological taxonomy and scientific standardization - Firmly established in evolutionary biology, entomology, and herpetology to denote egg-bearing or gestating females, avoiding the anthropocentric term "pregnant".
- 20th to 21st Century: Elevated literary metaphor and atmospheric tension - Flourished in modernist and contemporary literary fiction for pregnant pauses, fraught atmospheres, and moments heavy with impending consequence.
