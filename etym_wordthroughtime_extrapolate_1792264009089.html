- Late 19th c. (1870s) — Coined in Victorian mathematics, trigonometry, and physical sciences by deliberate morphological analogy with interpolate (prefixing Latin extra- to represent calculating values outside plotted boundaries).
- Early 20th c. (1900s–1930s) — Firmly established across formal statistics, cartography, and laboratory physics to designate numerical curve-fitting and the projection of empirical charts beyond experimental limits.
- Mid-20th c. (1940s–1960s) — Generalized into macroeconomic modeling, demography, and military operations research; adopted by early cybernetics and science fiction critics (as the definitive term for speculative narrative worldbuilding).
- Late 20th c. (1970s–1990s) — Pervasive adoption in computer science and software pedagogy (notably popularized in Kernighan and Ritchie's C manual in 1978) to signify the cognitive transfer of programming patterns from toy code to production systems.
- 21st c. — Ubiquitous in popular discourse, corporate strategy, AI machine learning theory (evaluating whether deep neural networks can genuinely extrapolate out-of-distribution or merely interpolate within training data manifolds), and epistemological debates.
