- [16th-century entry] - Borrowed in two registers at once: the general command or admonition, and the court order; also 'royal injunctions' - directives to the church from crown and bishop.
- [17th-18th century moral weight] - In sermons and conduct books, an injunction is a solemn, binding charge laid on conscience - a command backed by authority and duty.
- [19th-20th century legal consolidation] - The legal sense takes over public use: a court injunction forbidding or requiring an act, from labour disputes and nuisances to modern cyber-injunctions.
- [Contemporary register] - Dominantly legal and procedural, though the older sense of an authoritative warning from an elder or authority survives in literary and religious writing.
