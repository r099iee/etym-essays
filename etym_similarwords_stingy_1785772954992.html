• Miserly — Similar because both condemn an extreme unwillingness to give or spend. Differs because miserly pictures a pathological hoarder who denies himself to protect accumulated wealth (the miser), whereas stingy can describe ordinary, everyday ungenerosity — a bad tip, a small portion, withheld praise — without implying obsessive avarice.
  • Parsimonious — Similar because both mean very reluctant to spend or give. Differs because parsimonious is formal and can be neutral or technical (a parsimonious explanation is one using few assumptions), while stingy is colloquial and always carries moral disapproval.
  • Niggardly — Similar because both mean grudgingly sparing and scant in giving. Differs because niggardly stresses meager, stinting quantity and is now commonly avoided in speech due to phonetic resemblance to an unrelated slur, whereas stingy is the neutral-safe everyday synonym with the same meaning.
  • Mean (chiefly BrE) — Similar because in British English "mean with money" equals "stingy." Differs because mean also means "cruel, spiteful, unkind" (its dominant American sense), while stingy is specific to ungenerosity and carries no cruelty overtones.
  • Tight-fisted (tightfisted) — Similar because both describe a person who refuses to spend or share. Differs because tightfisted is a vivid metaphor of the closed fist, more idiomatic and slightly more informal, while stingy is the plainer, more general term that also extends to praise and affection.
  • Penurious — Similar because both can mean unwilling to spend. Differs because penurious primarily means "extremely poor, poverty-stricken" and only secondarily "parsimonious," while stingy implies meanness of spirit regardless of the person's wealth.
  • Frugal — Contrast word; similar only in that both involve restrained spending. Differs decisively because frugal is positive or neutral (prudent, economical, avoiding waste), whereas stingy is pejorative and implies withholding what one could afford to give.
  • Grasping / avaricious — Similar because both involve a selfish relation to wealth. Differs because grasping and avaricious emphasize the desire to acquire more, while stingy emphasizes the refusal to give or share what one already has.
