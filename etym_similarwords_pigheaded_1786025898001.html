• Stubborn: Similar because both denote an unwillingness to yield or change one's mind; differs because stubborn is the generic, neutral-to-negative term, whereas pigheaded carries much stronger contempt and implies irrational stupidity.
  • Obstinate: Similar because both convey an unyielding resistance to persuasion; differs because obstinate belongs to a higher, more formal register and can describe inanimate objects or diseases, whereas pigheaded is informal, colloquial, and specifically human-focused.
  • Bullheaded: Similar because both use animal metaphors to depict thick-headed obstinacy; differs because bullheaded emphasizes aggressive, headlong charging into obstacles with brute force, whereas pigheaded emphasizes immovable, digging-in resistance and stubborn inertia.
  • Mulish: Similar because both derive from animal obstinacy; differs because mulish emphasizes passive, sullen, balking immobility (like a mule refusing to walk), while pigheaded highlights foolish intellectual arrogance and opinionated rigidity.
  • Intransigent: Similar because both denote refusal to compromise or change a position; differs because intransigent is a formal political and diplomatic term indicating an ideological refusal to negotiate, lacking the rustic, earthy insult inherent in pigheaded.
  • Recalcitrant: Similar because both involve resisting authority and control; differs because recalcitrant implies rebellious non-compliance and kicking against authority, whereas pigheaded is focused on general mental inflexibility.
  • Refractory: Similar because both convey unmanageable resistance; differs because refractory is a clinical, technical, or formal term applied to stubborn patients, unyielding materials, or rebellious groups.
  • Obdurate: Similar because both involve hardened refusal to yield; differs because obdurate implies moral hardness, lack of compassion, and an unfeeling heart rather than intellectual foolishness.
  • Dogmatic: Similar because both involve holding firm to opinions without listening to others; differs because dogmatic stems from adherence to an ideological, theological, or theoretical system, whereas pigheaded is an innate personal trait of obstinacy.
  • Headstrong: Similar because both convey willful pursuit of one's desires; differs because headstrong highlights impulsive, reckless determination to rush forward regardless of consequences, rather than the stubborn resistance of pigheaded.
  • Willful: Similar because both indicate insistence on having one's own way; differs because willful emphasizes deliberate, intentional defiance and self-will, often used for disobedient children.
  • Intractable: Similar because both describe situations or persons that cannot be easily managed or guided; differs because intractable is formal and often used for problems, diseases, or complex systems.
