Chain for root word 'shoeing' (from the earliest Germanic stage; no secure PIE root): - Proto-Germanic *skōhaz 'a covering for the foot' (reconstructed; the word for 'shoe'; attested in all the Germanic languages — Gothic skohs, Old Norse skór, Old English scōh, Old Frisian skōch, Old Saxon skōh, Old High German scuoh — and confined to Germanic, with no securely attested cognates outside it); 1st millennium BCE. - Old English scōh 'a shoe' (attested; also applied to the horseshoe — the 'shoe' of a horse — already in Old English); 8th-11th c. CE. - Middle English sho / shoo / s(c)hone 'shoe' (attested; the plural shone and the verb shon, 'to shoe,' are regular in Middle English); 12th-15th c. CE. - Modern English shoe 'outer covering for the foot; also a horseshoe' (spelling with -oe- fixed in the early modern period); 16th c. CE onward. - Verb formation: the noun was converted to a verb already in Old English — scōgian / gescōgan 'to fit with shoes, to shoe a horse' — and the verb survives continuously in Middle English (shon) and Modern English (to shoe, shoeing, shod, unshod); 8th c. CE onward. - Modern English shoeing: the verbal noun and present participle formed from the verb to shoe with the suffix -ing, naming the act or process of fitting shoes (attested from the Middle English period onward, e.g. 'shoeing a horse'). - Final node, bold: shoeing. Debate note: the PIE ancestry of Proto-Germanic *skōhaz is unknown. Some scholars have tentatively connected it with PIE *skeu- / *skēu- 'to cover, conceal, hide' (a root of covering-words) or with *skek-, but the word is securely attested only in Germanic, and standard authorities (OED; Kluge, Etymologisches Wörterbuch) mark its deeper origin as uncertain. Do not assume a PIE root; the chain is securely documented only from Proto-Germanic onward.
