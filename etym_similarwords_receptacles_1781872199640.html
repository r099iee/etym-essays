• Container: Similar because both name something that holds things; differs because container is neutral and modern-industrial, lacking the Latinate dignity and the hint of "receiving" that receptacle carries.
  • Vessel: Similar because both mean a holder; differs because vessel is more poetic and can suggest blood, ships, or liturgy, rather than the capacity to receive and transmit aesthetic power.
  • Repository: Similar because both name a place where things are stored; differs because repository stresses static warehousing and archive, while Pater's receptacles transmit their powers to the observer.
  • Reservoir: Similar because both mean a store; differs because reservoir implies a reserve supply (of water or resources), whereas a receptacle implies designed fitness to receive and present.
  • Holder: Similar because both name something that holds; differs because holder is everyday and general, without the receiving-fitness or the technical registers of receptacle.
  • Receiver: Similar because both are built on "receiving"; differs because receiver usually means the person or device that takes signals or transmissions, while a receptacle is the passive container that receives and holds.
  • Socket: Similar because both can name the electrical outlet that receives a plug; differs because socket is the everyday word for the specific electrical fixture, while receptacle is the broader and more formal term covering containers generally.
  • Box: Similar because both denote a physical holder; differs because box is plain and material, lacking the etymological sense of receiving and the elevated philosophical use.
  • Receptacle (singular) vs Receptacles (plural): Similar because they are the same word in number; differs because the plural on this card is a collective abstraction naming a whole class of objects—works of art—each a vessel of powers, rather than one physical container.
