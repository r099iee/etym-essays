- Throat / bosom - Neutral anatomical and clothing sense in Old French and early Middle English; no moral judgement, simply the neck and upper chest.
- Hawk's cropful / throatful - Specialised falconry sense; connotation of aristocratic sport, technical and neutral.
- To cram, to stuff oneself - Verb emerges c.1500; gains pejorative colour of greed, excess, animal appetite; moral disapproval in religious writing on gluttony.
- Savage / natural appetite - 18th-19th c. travel and anthropology use gorge to describe supposedly primitive binge-fast cycles; condescending, othering tone implying lack of foresight, as in source context.
- Gorge as sublime landscape - Late 18th-19th c. Romantic picturesque; gorge becomes awe-inspiring, thrilling, positive in geology and tourism.
- Modern binge / disorder - 20th-21st c. clinical and everyday use; gorge is both casual overindulgence (gorged on cake) and pathological (gorge-purge); retains excess but often self-deprecating humorous rather than moralistic.
