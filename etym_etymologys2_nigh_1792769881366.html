↓

nigh
"near, close at hand; almost, nearly"

Note on morphological evolution: Old English nēah represents one of the most famous grammatical shifts in Germanic philology. It was the original positive grade ("near"). Its comparative was nēarra ("nearer"), which over centuries was reinterpreted as a positive root and gave English modern near. Its superlative was nīehst / nēahst ("closest"), which developed phonetically into modern next. Thus, nigh, near, and next are the positive, comparative, and superlative forms of the very same ancient Germanic root.
