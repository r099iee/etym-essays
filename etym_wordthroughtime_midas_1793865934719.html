- 8th century BCE - [Historical Anatolian Monarchy] - Originated as the historical royal name of King Mita of the Mushki/Phrygians in central Anatolia, an immensely wealthy monarch recorded in Neo-Assyrian cuneiform annals of Sargon II. - 5th to 1st century BCE - [Classical Greek Mythological Parable] - Cemented in Greek oral and dramatic tradition as a legendary king of Phrygia whose hospitality to Silenus earned him Dionysus's fatal gift of the golden touch, and whose musical foolishness earned him donkey ears from Apollo; perceived as the archetype of tragic cupidity and aesthetic blindness. - 1st century CE - [Ovidian Moralization and Roman Satire] - Codified by Ovid in Book XI of Metamorphoses, shifting perception into a sophisticated literary psychological study of avarice, starvation amidst luxury, and the inescapable exposure of secrets. - 14th to 17th century - [Renaissance Allegory of Folly] - Moralized in European literature (Chaucer's Wife of Bath's Tale, Shakespeare's Merchant of Venice) as an emblem of spiritual blindness, bad artistic judgment, and the absurdity of valuing inert gold over life and love. - 19th century to Present - [Idiomatic Proverbial Midas Touch] - Shifting into commercial, financial, and popular English idiom ('the Midas touch'), largely shedding its tragic and foolish connotations in popular jargon to denote an enviable talent for effortless moneymaking, while retaining in high literature its original warning against materialist ruin.
