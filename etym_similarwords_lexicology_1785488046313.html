• Lexicography: Similar because both concern the words of a language as recorded and organised; differs because lexicography is the applied craft of writing and editing dictionaries, while lexicology is the theoretical, descriptive science of the word-stock and how it is organised.
  • Morphology: Similar because both are linguistic disciplines that analyse words; differs because morphology studies only the internal grammatical structure of words — roots, prefixes, suffixes — while lexicology takes the whole vocabulary as a system.
  • Semantics: Similar because both are branches of linguistics concerned with meaning; differs because semantics studies meaning in general and is not specifically about words as dictionary units, whereas lexicology is about the lexicon itself.
  • Etymology: Similar because both trace the historical lives of words; differs because etymology is the historical study of individual word origins — a method lexicology draws upon but is not reducible to, since lexicology studies the whole system.
  • Philology: Similar because both are historical-language disciplines; differs because philology studies language through literary and historical texts as a whole, while lexicology concentrates on the vocabulary as a system.
  • Onomastics: Similar because both study words as a structured set of names; differs because onomastics is limited to proper names — place-names and personal names — while lexicology covers the entire word-stock.
  • Vocabulary studies: Similar because both mean the study of the word-stock; differs because vocabulary studies is a looser, non-technical phrase, while lexicology names a defined branch of linguistics.
