Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*per- + *kʷel- / *kel-
"to pass through, lead across" + "to filter, strain, glide"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
porta + cōlāre
"city gate, entrance" + "to filter, strain, glide down"
(From Latin porta "gate" & cōlum "sieve/strainer")
↓
Late Latin / Vulgar Latin (c. 5th c. CE)
porta colātīcia
"sliding gate, gate that glides down"
↓
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 1160)
porte coleice
"sliding door, falling lattice gate of a fortress"
(porte "door" + coleis "sliding/gliding", fem. coleice)
↓
Middle English (c. 1330)
portcolis / portcules
"portcullis; fortified sliding iron/timber grille"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550)
portcullis
"vertically sliding castle gate; royal heraldic emblem"
