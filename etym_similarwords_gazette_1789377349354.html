• Newspaper: Similar because both refer to regular printed or digital publications delivering news, commentary, and public information; differs because newspaper is the broad, everyday term for commercial, mass-market dailies focused on general public interest, whereas gazette often carries an official, legal, or governmental status as an authoritative record of state.
  • Periodical: Similar because both denote publications issued at recurring, predictable intervals; differs because periodical is a broad academic or bibliographical category encompassing magazines, journals, and reviews published weekly, monthly, or quarterly, whereas gazette specifically connotes news-sheets, official decrees, or legal notices.
  • Journal: Similar because both can describe formal or scholarly periodicals and official accounts; differs because journal typically emphasizes scholarly research, academic peer review, or reflective personal accounts, whereas gazette focuses on statutory announcements, public events, and public notices.
  • Bulletin: Similar because both communicate concise public news or official updates; differs because a bulletin is typically a brief, immediate statement or emergency release focusing on a single critical event, whereas a gazette is a comprehensive, organized compendium of diverse public records and notices.
  • Broadside: Similar because both historically refer to printed sheets distributed to inform the public of news or proclamations; differs because a broadside is a single, large sheet printed on one side only and frequently used for ephemeral ballads or sensational political propaganda, whereas a gazette is a bound or folded multi-page publication of record.
  • Chronicle: Similar because both present continuous, sequential accounts of historical and public occurrences; differs because a chronicle is a narrative register or grand historical record of events over time, whereas a gazette is an administrative instrument designed for immediate statutory notice and public transparency.
