spasmodic is an adjective with two linked senses: literally, pertaining to or characterized by spasm, sudden involuntary muscular contraction; figuratively, occurring in brief, irregular bursts with pauses in between, lacking steadiness or continuity. The figurative sense dominates general prose, while the literal sense survives in medicine.

At its core spasmodic describes a temporal pattern: jerk, pause, jerk, pause. Whether of coughs, gunfire, charity, or economic growth, the activity is fitful, jerky, and unsustained. It implies energy that cannot maintain itself and control that cannot regularize itself.

  • Medical literal sense - convulsive: Affected with or resembling spasm: spasmodic cough, spasmodic stricture, spasmodic dysphonia, spasmodic torticollis. Here it means sudden, involuntary, often painful contraction of muscle, or the abnormal tone that produces it. Semantic basis: direct continuation of Greek spasmodes, of the nature of spasm. Collocates with attack, contraction, movement, pain, affliction.
  • Temporal figurative sense - fitful and intermittent: The dominant modern sense. Spasmodic efforts, spasmodic attempts, spasmodic fighting, spasmodic growth mean irregular, occasional, starting and stopping, never settling into routine. The source sentence uses exactly this: pre-agricultural labour was spasmodic activity, hunting and foraging by fits, as opposed to regular work secured by tillage routine and labour organization. Bridge: metaphor from muscle that contracts and releases to effort that surges and lapses.
  • Energetic but uncontrolled manner - jerky and abrupt: A related nuance stressing quality rather than timing: spasmodic gestures, spasmodic prose, spasmodic violence suggest suddenness, violence, lack of smoothness. The action is not merely occasional but abrupt and uncoordinated when it does occur. Bridge: the jerkiness of a convulsing limb transferred to style and behaviour.
  • Psychological temperament - high-strung and excitable: Colloquially, a spasmodic person is nervy, jumpy, over-reactive, prone to bursts of emotion. Less common now than fitful or erratic for people, but found in 19th-century novels. Bridge: from body that twitches to mind that cannot remain calm.
  • Domain-specific extensions: In economics, spasmodic development means booms separated by slumps without steady accumulation. In military history, spasmodic shelling or spasmodic resistance means irregular fire and uncoordinated defence. In literary history, Spasmodic with a capital S names a mid-Victorian school of poetry attacked for strained, convulsed expression. In each case the term carries mild criticism: what is spasmodic lacks discipline, system, or maturity.
  • Grammar, collocation, and register: Attributive and predicative: spasmodic efforts, efforts were spasmodic. Adverb spasmodically, noun spasmodicity rarely used. Often collocates with effort, attempt, burst, outbreak, firing, growth, interest, reform. Register is formal and analytical; in conversation people more often say on and off, in fits and starts, stop-start. It is not synonymous with sporadic in every context: sporadic stresses rarity and scattered distribution, spasmodic stresses jerkiness and failed regularity with implied energy wasted between bursts.
  • Distinction from spastic and spasmodical: Spastic in medicine means affected by spasticity, chronic stiffness; as a noun it became an offensive slur and should be avoided for people. Spasmodic remains neutral and describes pattern, not a person as such. Spasmodical is an obsolete variant with identical meaning.

To master spasmodic is to feel the muscle inside the metaphor: a healthy routine is smoothenthe sustained tone of a well-trained body, while spasmodic activity is cramp, exciting while it lasts but incapable of building, tilling, or lasting.
