• Trials: Similar because both denote tests of endurance; differs because a trial can be a single test of character or a legal proceeding and is lighter unless intensified, while tribulations are repeated and oppressive.
  • Hardships: Similar because both denote difficult conditions; differs because hardships stress material difficulty, whereas tribulations carry a spiritual, grinding, almost biblical testing flavour.
  • Afflictions: Similar because both mean suffering; differs because afflictions are solemn and often physical or providential, while tribulations emphasize a prolonged series rather than individual blows.
  • Ordeals: Similar because both denote severe suffering; differs because an ordeal usually suggests one extreme test, whereas tribulations are extended and plural by nature.
  • Troubles: Similar because both name difficulties; differs because troubles is everyday and milder, covering minor worries, whereas tribulations are grave, prolonged, and elevated.
  • Sufferings: Similar because both denote pain and hardship; differs because sufferings is general and emotional and does not carry the threshing / testing metaphor of tribulations.
  • Adversities: Similar because both denote unfavourable, trying conditions; differs because adversity is a neutral, often abstract term for misfortune, while tribulations imply active crushing pressure.
  • Misfortunes: Similar because both denote bad experiences; differs because misfortune is often random bad luck or an accident, whereas tribulation is prolonged, oppressive suffering.
  • Distress: Similar because both involve severe mental or physical strain; differs because distress names the state of strain itself, while tribulations name the events or trials that produce it.
  • Woes: Similar because both denote great troubles; differs because woes is often rhetorical and complaint-laden (lamenting 'woe is me'), while tribulations is more dignified and endurance-oriented.
  • Calvary / cross (figurative): Similar because both name a great burden to bear; differs because these are intensely religious figures of sacrificial suffering, while tribulation is the more general word for crushing trials.
  • Persecution: Similar because both involve sustained oppression; differs because persecution is targeted, inflicted by enemies on a group for belief or identity, whereas tribulation is the general suffering, which may or may not be inflicted by persecutors.
