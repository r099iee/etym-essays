• wriggled: Similar because both are twisting, side-to-side body motions; differs because wriggling stresses rapid, small, local motion — a worm on a hook — without squirmed's sustained forward progress through a constricted space.
  • writhed: Similar because both are whole-body contortions; differs because writhing implies involuntary agony or pain, usually in place rather than along a path, while squirming suggests deliberate, striving progress through a gap.
  • slithered: Similar because both describe moving through a narrow space; differs because slithering is a smooth, sleek belly-glide, as of a snake, without the strained, twisting effort of squirming.
  • wormed: Similar because both describe worm-like motion into a tight spot; differs because worming emphasizes stealth or ingratiation, a way of getting somewhere one is not wanted, more than the physical twist itself.
  • crawled: Similar because both are low, animal progress through a space; differs because crawling is limb-based and deliberate, on hands and knees, without the whole-body serpentine twist of squirming.
  • twisted: Similar because both involve torsion of the body; differs because twisting names a single torsion, while squirming is a continuous succession of turning, writhing motions.
  • twitched: Similar because both are involuntary body movements; differs because twitching is a small, brief, localized spasm, while squirming is a sustained whole-body wriggling.
  • contorted: Similar because both involve distorting the body; differs because contorting describes a strained posture held in place, while squirming is a continuous motion of wriggling along a path.
