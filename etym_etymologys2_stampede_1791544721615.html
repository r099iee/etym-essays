Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*stemb- / *stemp-
"to trample, stamp, tread heavily"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*stampōną
"to crush, pound, tread with foot"
↓
Late Latin / Vulgar Latin (c. 6th c. CE)
*stampare
"to stamp, imprint, strike down"
(borrowed from Germanic into Romance)
↓
Old Occitan / Catalan (c. 12th c.)
estampida
"noisy stamping dance, musical tune"
↓
Spanish (c. 15th c.)
estampida
"loud report, sudden crash, burst"
↓
Mexican Spanish (c. 18th c.)
estampida
"sudden panicked rush of cattle"
↓
American English (c. 1843)
stampede
"sudden panicked flight of a herd
or crowd; collective rush"

Note: While Spanish 'estampida' derives from Germanic '*stampōną' via Vulgar Latin, English also inherited native 'stamp' directly from the same Germanic root. Thus, 'stampede' represents a re-borrowed Romance loan whose deep Germanic cognate was already native in English.
