• Expelled: Similar because both mean forcibly removed from a place or institution; differs because expelled belongs to schools, clubs, and countries (expelled from school, from parliament), while evicted belongs to dwellings and property.
  • Ejected: Similar because both mean thrown out, often summarily; differs because ejected stresses sudden physical removal (ejected from the bar, from the game), while evicted stresses a formal process ending occupation.
  • Ousted: Similar because both mean removed from a held position against one's will; differs because ousted targets offices, thrones, and titles (ousted from power), while evicted targets rooms, houses, and land.
  • Dispossessed: Similar because both describe legal loss of property or land; differs because dispossessed is the lawyer's solemn term for being stripped of ownership or ancestral land, while evicted is the everyday word for tenants turned out of rented rooms.
  • Banished: Similar because both mean sent away by authority and forbidden to return; differs because banished exiles from a realm or community (often for years, even for life), while evicted merely ends a tenancy.
  • Deported: Similar because both are formal removals executed by officers of the state; differs because deported removes across a national border under immigration law, while evicted removes across a property line under landlord-and-tenant law.
  • Removed: Similar because both describe being taken away from a place or post; differs because removed is neutral and general (a stain removed, an official removed), while evicted always carries the sting of lost shelter and legal compulsion.
  • Turned out: Similar because both picture occupants put out of doors with their belongings; differs because turned out is colloquial and can be voluntary or comic (turned out for dinner), while evicted is formal and never funny except, as here, by deliberate overstatement.
