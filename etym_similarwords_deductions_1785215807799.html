• Inferences: Similar because every deduction is an inference, a conclusion drawn from evidence; differs because inference is the broader category covering inductive and abductive reasoning as well, while a deduction is the specific rigorous method that moves from general premises.
  • Conclusions: Similar because a deduction is a final judgment reached by reasoning; differs because a conclusion may be reached by any means whatever, whereas deduction names the method as well as its result.
  • Inductions: Similar because an induction is also a reasoned product of the mind and the traditional counterpart of deduction in logic; differs because induction moves upward from particular cases to general principles, the exact reverse direction.
  • Assumptions: Similar because both belong to the machinery of argument; differs because assumptions are unproved starting points, while deductions are derived results that occupy the opposite role.
  • Corollaries: Similar because a corollary is a deduction from an already-established proposition; differs because it denotes the specific consequence that follows immediately from a preceding result, a narrower subclass of deductions.
  • Subtractions: Similar in the financial register, where a deduction is an amount taken from a total; differs because subtraction is a neutral arithmetic operation, while deduction in finance implies a rule, an entitlement, or a levy.
  • Abatements: Similar in being amounts allowed off a charge; differs because an abatement is a reduction or discount granted on a bill or tax, a legal-administrative term without the logical sense.
