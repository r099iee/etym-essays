• Petit Trianon: Similar because it names the very same building and is the source of the English phrase; differs because it is the standard modern French and English designation, whereas “Little Trianon” is a nineteenth-century loan-translation with an archaic, literary flavour.
  • Trianon: Similar because it is the root of the compound and can denote the estate in which the small château stands; differs because “Trianon” alone is broader and ambiguous, covering the Grand Trianon, the park and the hamlet, while “Little Trianon” pins down one building.
  • Grand Trianon: Similar because it is the other royal palace on the same estate, built for the same purpose of private escape; differs because it is far larger, earlier (1687), of marble, and used for official entertaining, whereas the Petit Trianon is small, domestic and associated with Marie-Antoinette.
  • Versailles: Similar because the Petit Trianon lies within the park of Versailles; differs because Versailles names the whole palace-town and the political system centred on it, while “Little Trianon” names a dependent private retreat at its edge.
  • Hameau de la Reine: Similar because it is another rustic retreat in the same gardens and shares the queen’s association; differs because it is a model village of cottages built in 1783, not a château, and its charm is deliberately theatrical rather than architectural.
  • hermitage: Similar because both denote a small, secluded residence used for withdrawal from court life; differs because a hermitage originally belongs to a religious solitary and implies austerity, while the Petit Trianon is a luxurious royal pavilion.
  • pavilion: Similar because the Petit Trianon is a compact garden building of pavilion-like proportions; differs because a pavilion is usually a subordinate ornamental structure, whereas the Petit Trianon is a fully equipped residence with kitchens, bedrooms and a theatre nearby.
  • folly: Similar because both can suggest a costly ornamental building set in landscaped grounds; differs because a folly is functionless or whimsical, while the Petit Trianon was a working royal household and a centre of court life in miniature.
  • château: Similar because the Petit Trianon is a château in the French sense; differs because the common noun is generic and can be applied to thousands of buildings, while “Little Trianon” is a unique proper name.
