• Cappadocia: Similar because both were prominent ancient Anatolian regions and Roman provinces in Asia Minor; differs because Cappadocia was an inland, landlocked high plateau situated north of the Taurus Mountains with an arid continental climate, whereas Cilicia was a coastal Mediterranean maritime region with subtropical lowlands and naval ports.
  • Pamphylia: Similar because both were adjoining southern Anatolian coastal regions along the Mediterranean; differs because Pamphylia lay immediately to the west of Cilicia Aspera and was colonized heavily by Greeks from the archaic period, whereas Cilicia retained deeper Luwian and Hurrian indigenous traditions before Hellenization.
  • Syria: Similar because both bordered the northeastern corner of the Mediterranean Sea and interacted closely under Seleucid and Roman administration; differs because Syria belonged geographically and linguistically to the Semitic/Levantine world, whereas Cilicia was geographically Anatolian, separated from Syria by the Amanus mountain barrier.
  • Galatia: Similar because both were Roman provinces in Asia Minor; differs because Galatia occupied the central Anatolian plateau and was settled by Celtic Gallic tribes in the third century BCE, whereas Cilicia had ancient Bronze Age Anatolian roots and a Mediterranean coastline.
  • Anatolia / Asia Minor: Similar because Cilicia is a constituent geographical sub-region within them; differs because Anatolia represents the entire peninsular landmass comprising modern Turkey, whereas Cilicia refers specifically to the southeastern littoral plain and adjoining Taurus mountains.
  • Armenia Minor (Lesser Armenia): Similar because the medieval Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia was often referred to by European chroniclers as Lesser Armenia; differs because Armenia Minor originally denoted the historic territory west of the Euphrates, whereas Cilicia was the maritime coastal kingdom settled by Armenian emigrants during the Crusades.
