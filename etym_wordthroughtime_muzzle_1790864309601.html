- Late Middle English (c. 14th c.) - mosel / musel borrowed from Old French to describe the snout of a beast or a cage fitted over an animal's mouth.
- 15th to 16th Century - Acquired ballistic usage in early artillery and gunnery for the open discharge mouth of a cannon or musket barrel.
- 16th to 17th Century - Extended into figurative verbal territory (to muzzle) meaning to suppress, silence, or censor speech and dissent.
- 18th to 19th Century - Ballistics firmly codified muzzle velocity and muzzle-loading; literature deployed muzzle for delicate animal descriptions and political satire.
- 20th to 21st Century - Continues as a universal term in veterinary science, firearms engineering, canine legislation (muzzle laws), and constitutional debates over free press.
