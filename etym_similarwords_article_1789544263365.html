• Item: Similar because both refer to an individual, distinct physical unit, object, or entry in a list; differs because item is a concise commercial and catalog term (from Latin for "likewise/also"), whereas article carries broader polysemic depth in journalism, constitutional law, grammar, and formal speech (e.g., "an article of clothing").
  • Object: Similar because both denote a tangible, physical entity; differs because object is a broad philosophical and material term for any material thing perceived by the senses, whereas article emphasizes an individual piece of manufactured property, merchandise, or wardrobe.
  • Clause: Similar because both refer to distinct numbered provisions or sections in a legal contract or treaty; differs because a clause is a specific legal paragraph or grammatical sentence constituent containing a subject and predicate, whereas an article is often a major, overarching numbered chapter or division containing multiple clauses.
  • Essay: Similar because both denote non-fictional prose compositions; differs because an essay is a personal, reflective, or philosophical exploratory composition, whereas an article is typically an informative, objective journalistic or academic report published in a newspaper, magazine, or encyclopedia.
  • Commodity: Similar because both refer to manufactured goods of commerce; differs because a commodity refers to standardized raw materials or bulk goods traded on exchanges (like oil, grain, or copper), whereas an article is a specific finished item or artifact.
  • Artifact: Similar because both describe physical objects of human manufacture; differs because an artifact carries archaeological, anthropological, or historical significance as a relic of human culture, whereas an article is an everyday item of dress or trade.
