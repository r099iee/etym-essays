• Killed: Similar because it means died by violence; differs because killed is factual and neutral, recording the event without meaning, whereas martyred supplies the sacrificial witness — death for one's beliefs that vindicates the victim.
  • Executed: Similar because it means put to death by authority; differs because executed is legal and judicial, framing the death as criminal justice, which strips it of honour and witness, whereas martyrdom is a holy, defiant death.
  • Sacrificed: Similar because it means died for a higher cause; differs because sacrifice emphasises the offering and its utility — something given up for a purpose — while martyrdom stresses bearing witness to the truth by dying.
  • Slaughtered: Similar because it means killed brutally; differs because slaughtered evokes animals or a battlefield — brutal and dehumanising — while martyrdom is a dignified, vindicated death for belief.
  • Slain: Similar because it means killed, usually in a formal or literary register; differs because slain is a poetic or archaic word for a violent death without any of the testimony and vindication that martyrdom carries.
  • Persecuted: Similar because it means made to suffer for one's beliefs; differs because persecution is the suffering that may end in martyrdom — one can be persecuted and survive — whereas to be martyred is to be killed, the final, fatal stage.
  • Put to death: Similar because it means caused to die; differs because put to death is a colourless, official periphrasis that records the fact without the religious or moral vindication inherent in martyred.
