- [Oldest liturgical sense] - In its current form since Middle English, 'office' first meant a duty, service or prescribed ceremonial function - the Office for the Dead, the Divine Office - a solemn, liturgical word.
- [Official position] - From medieval usage it broadened to any formal charge or post within an institution - the office of priest, consul or king - a legally loaded, dignified sense.
- [Room of work] - By the 19th century, with the growth of administration, 'office' came to mean chiefly the room where business is transacted, losing its ceremonial weight.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today the word is overwhelmingly spatial and bureaucratic - going to the office, office politics - while the old sense of a solemn duty survives only in liturgical and formal phrases.
