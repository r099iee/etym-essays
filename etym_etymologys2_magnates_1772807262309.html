SINGLE ROOT WORD (magnate). Linear chain from earliest reconstructible stage to the card form: PIE *méǥh₂ 'great, large' (c. 4000–3000 BCE, reconstructed) → Latin magnus 'great, large, important' (attested from the 3rd c. BCE) → Late Latin magnas, plural magnātēs 'great man / great men, noble' (attested 4th c. CE) → Medieval Latin magnates 'persons of high rank, the great men of a realm' (medieval charters and chronicles) → English magnate 'a person of rank or influence' (early 15th c., via Old French magnat or as a learned borrowing) → MAGNATES (English plural of magnate, 'wealthy and powerful people', used as the card's headword). Debate note: The Late Latin noun magnas is transparently formed from magnus ('great'), but some scholars suggest the oblique stem magnātis was reinforced by, or modelled on, Greek megistānes 'the great men' (from mégas 'great'), an aristocratic title-word of the eastern empire; the exact mechanism by which the plural magnātēs stabilized is thus slightly debated. The PIE stage *méǥh₂ is secure and yields Sanskrit mahā́, Greek mégas, and Germanic micel/much, but the Latin-to-English line is a straightforward noble/business borrowing, not a native Germanic development.
