↓
Modern English (current)
presumptuous

Note: Derived from the Latin compound verb praesūmere, formed from prae- (‘before’) and sūmere (‘to take, assume’, a contraction of *sub-imere from sub- ‘under, up from under’ + emere ‘to take, buy’, from PIE *em-). From its supine stem praesumpt-, Late Latin coined praesūmptuōsus to describe one who habitually anticipates privileges or acts upon unwarranted expectations. The intrusive consonant -p- arose via euphonic epenthesis in Latin, as the speech organs naturally close the lips for the bilabial nasal /m/ before releasing into the voiceless dental plosive /t/.
