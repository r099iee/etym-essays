Non-PIE / nursery (Lallwort) root *am- “mother, nurse, affectionate baby-talk” (prehistoric; cf. Latin amma “wet-nurse”, Greek ammá “mother”, Sanskrit ambā “mother”; no secure Proto-Indo-European reconstruction) → Latin amāre “to love” (3rd c. BCE; Plautus; of uncertain origin, likely the nursery root extended into adult affection, per de Vaan and Ernout–Meillet) → Latin amābilis “worthy of love, lovable” (1st c. BCE; amāre + suffix -bilis), with the parallel branch Latin amīcus “friend” (3rd c. BCE) → amīcābilis “friendly” (Late Latin) → Late Latin amicābilis “friendly” (in Old French drawn toward amābilis “lovable”) → Old French amiable “friendly, kind, lovable” (12th c.; a blend of amicābilis and amābilis) → Middle English amiable “friendly, lovable” (c. 1300; borrowed via Anglo-French) → amiable ◄ TARGET.

Note on the debated stage: the origin of Latin amāre is genuinely uncertain — no convincing Indo-European etymology exists, and the connection to the nursery word amma is the leading but inferential hypothesis.
