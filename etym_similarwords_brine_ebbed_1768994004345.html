• seawater: Similar in being the salty water of the sea; differs because it is merely descriptive and neutral, while brine names the saturated, killing solution in which life fails.
  • salt: Similar in denoting the mineral substance of brine; differs because it names the ingredient rather than the liquid — brine is water so salted it preserves flesh only by making it uninhabitable.
  • saltwater: Similar in being water containing salt; differs because it is the plain generic term for any saline water, without brine's implication of concentration and sterility.
  • pickle: Similar in being a preserving salt solution; differs because pickle names the process and the product (and the vinegar bath) rather than the saline liquor itself.
  • lye: Similar in being a strong chemical solution used in processing; differs because lye is caustic alkali (from wood ash), not salt — opposite in composition, related only in strength.
  • brine pool: Similar in being a dense salty body of water; differs because it is the specific geological term for the salt-saturated basins of dying seas or ocean floors, a technical narrowing of brine.
  • receded: Similar in water flowing back from its former level; differs because it is formal and neutral, while ebbed preserves the tidal rhythm — slow, inevitable, elegiac withdrawal.
  • retreated: Similar in moving back from a position; differs because it suggests deliberate withdrawal, as of an army, whereas ebbing is the water's own helpless going out.
  • subsided: Similar in falling from a high level; differs because it implies sinking down or settling, losing the rhythm of the tide that ebb carries.
  • drained: Similar in the loss of water from fullness; differs because it implies an external agency drawing the water off, whereas ebbed is the water's own inexorable going out.
  • withdrew: Similar in retiring from fullness or presence; differs because it is the general word of retreat, lacking the tidal image and the despair of ebb.
  • abated: Similar in lessening in force or amount; differs because it stresses the reduction of intensity, not the emptying of volume that ebb describes.
  • waned: Similar in decreasing from fullness toward nothing; differs because it belongs to the moon and to brightness (from the same root as "wan"), while ebb belongs to water and the tide.
