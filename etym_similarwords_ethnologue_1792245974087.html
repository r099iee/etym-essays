• Glottolog: Similar because both are comprehensive master databases cataloging the world's languages and language families; differs because Glottolog (maintained by the Max Planck Institute) adheres to strict scientific bibliographic citations and conservative genealogical classifications, often rejecting Ethnologue's missionary-derived speaker estimates.
  • Linguasphere Observatory: Similar because both provide comprehensive global language classification frameworks; differs because Linguasphere utilizes an innovative decimal geolinguistic coding system (the Linguasphere Register) structured on concentric scales of communication rather than ISO 639-3 codes.
  • World Atlas of Language Structures (WALS): Similar because both catalog global linguistic diversity; differs because WALS is a structural database mapping specific phonological, grammatical, and lexical features (such as word order or vowel inventories) across languages, rather than providing sociolinguistic demographic profiles.
  • UNESCO Atlas of the World's Languages in Danger: Similar because both evaluate the endangerment and vitality of minority languages; differs because the UNESCO Atlas focuses exclusively on threatened and dying tongues using a five-tier endangerment rubric, whereas Ethnologue catalogs all languages, healthy and extinct alike.
  • Omniglot: Similar because both are prominent online reference repositories of global linguistic diversity; differs because Omniglot specializes exclusively in writing systems, alphabets, scripts, and orthographies, rather than genealogical trees or speaker populations.
  • Catalogue of Endangered Languages (ELCat): Similar because both track language endangerment data; differs because ELCat (managed by the Endangered Languages Project) is dedicated specifically to the 3,000+ most severely endangered tongues, offering open-access crowdsourced updates.
  • Compendium of the World's Languages: Similar because both profile world languages; differs because George L. Campbell's Compendium is a curated multi-volume textbook presenting structural grammatical sketches of major languages, rather than an exhaustive census of all 7,000+ living tongues.
