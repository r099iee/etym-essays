- Antiquity — stakes in a ring: Romans ditch and stake nightly marching camps; Germanic tribes ring villages with pointed posts.
- 1500s — the Spanish word hardens: estacada, a stake barrier, names colonial palisades in the New World.
- 1600s — English adopts it: stockade enters for frontier palisades and trading-post walls, anglicised toward stock (timber store).
- 1700s–1800s — the frontier age: American forts, presidios, and laagers raise stockades from Kentucky to the Cape.
- 1860s — the prison shadow: Andersonville and other Civil War pens make stockade synonymous with military prison.
- Today — adventure and punishment: Treasure Island romance and the brig’s stockade keep the posts standing in story and slang.
