- Dark-water description - Old/Middle English: any peaty or muddy stream called black water; purely visual, everyday.
- Pant below, Blackwater above? - medieval: upper water keeps British Panta, lower tidal water called Blackwater; dual naming reflects fresh vs. salt perception.
- Working estuary - 18th–19th c.: Blackwater means barges, mills, saltworks, and oyster dredges; industrious, commercial connotation.
- Battle and literary water - 20th c.: Panta/Blackwater gains elegiac register via the Maldon poem and then Baker’s peregrine marshes; wild, wintry, sublime.
- Protected system - contemporary: Blackwater means SPA/Ramsar, water-body status, sailing and salt brands; conservation plus leisure, with sanitary/ecological homonyms requiring capitalisation to disambiguate.
