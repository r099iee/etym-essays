• landed: Similar because both mean coming to rest after moving through the air or water; differs because "landed" is neutral, everyday and much broader (ships, aircraft, goods, jobs), with no ceremonial charge.
  • descended: Similar because both involve downward motion from a height; differs because "descended" describes movement along a path rather than settling at a point, and covers stairs, slopes and bloodlines.
  • settled: Similar because both end in a resting position; differs because "settled" is slower, implies remaining in place, and is not tied to flight or height.
  • perched: Similar because both describe a bird coming to rest; differs because "perched" specifies a narrow support (branch, ledge) and implies gripping, often briefly.
  • dismounted: Similar because both mean getting down from a horse or vehicle; differs because "dismounted" applies only to riders and is the technical, deliberate term.
  • touched down: Similar because both denote an aircraft meeting the ground; differs because "touched down" is modern aviation jargon for the single moment wheels meet runway, not the whole settling.
  • got off / stepped off: Similar because both express leaving a vehicle; differs because they are colloquial modern idioms with none of the dignity or flight associations.
  • lighted (light on/upon): Similar because it is the same verb family without the prefix, with the same "settle on" sense; differs because "light upon" is even more archaic and means chiefly "chance to find".
  • alit: Similar because it is the same verb; differs because it is merely the alternative (less common) past form, patterned on "lit".
  • dropped: Similar because both involve falling to a lower place; differs because "dropped" implies loss of support or gravity, not controlled descent.
  • swooped: Similar because both describe fast aerial downward movement; differs because "swooped" is rapid and predatory, and does not end in settling.
  • came down: Similar because both mean moving to a lower position; differs because "came down" is broad and colloquial, used of prices, rain and buildings as well as bodies.
