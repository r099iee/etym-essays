• Brainwashing: Similar because both refer to the manipulative altering of a person's beliefs and thoughts; differs because brainwashing is a coercive, high-intensity psychological process involving physical isolation, sleep deprivation, and intense trauma to break down an individual's personality, whereas indoctrination is usually a broader, institutional, and educational process of dogmatic conditioning.
  • Inculcation: Similar because both involve the repeated, persistent drilling of ideas or values into a student's mind; differs because inculcation is a neutral or positive educational technique (e.g., inculcating civic virtue or honesty) that does not necessarily suppress critical thinking, whereas indoctrination implies dogmatic, uncritical acceptance.
  • Conditioning: Similar because both describe the shaping of psychological responses and mental attitudes; differs because conditioning is primarily a behavioral and psychological concept involving stimulus-response reinforcement (e.g., Pavlovian or Skinnerian conditioning), whereas indoctrination involves complex ideological and doctrinal narratives.
  • Proselytization: Similar because both seek to bring others around to a specific ideological or religious viewpoint; differs because proselytization is the outward act of attempting to convert an outsider to one's faith through persuasion or preaching, while indoctrination is the internal, systematic molding of adherents already inside the institution.
  • Dogmatization: Similar because both impose rigid, unquestionable principles; differs because dogmatization focuses on the formal philosophical act of declaring specific doctrines to be sacred and inviolable, whereas indoctrination focuses on the pedagogical transmission and drilling of those doctrines into minds.
  • Socialization: Similar because both describe how individuals absorb cultural norms and institutional values; differs because socialization is a natural, organic, universal sociological process by which human beings learn to function within their community, while indoctrination is a deliberate, top-down, and manipulative imposition of orthodoxy.
  • Catechesis: Similar because both involve formal instruction in religious doctrines; differs because catechesis is an open, traditional Christian religious practice of religious instruction before baptism or confirmation, whereas indoctrination carries modern sinister overtones of cognitive closure.
