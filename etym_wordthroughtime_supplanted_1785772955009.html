- Medieval English: Strongly associated with tripping up, treachery, and dispossession—perception is moralized and interpersonal (courts, inheritances, biblical parallels).
- Early Modern period: Court, church, and political prose use "supplant" for ousting rivals; still personal and competitive, less purely physical.
- 18th–19th centuries: Broadens to methods, customs, and technologies replacing older ones; tone becomes more historical and analytical.
- 20th century: Routine in science and economics for displacement without individual villain; "supplanted by" is a standard collocation for obsolescence and succession.
- Present: Dual feel remains—can still imply scheming when humans are agents, or cool systemic replacement when systems and tools are agents; formal, not slangy.
