• copacetic: Similar because both denote that everything is in excellent order and running smoothly; differs because copacetic carries a slightly more understated, vintage cool tone without the rhythmic, nursery-rhyme reduplication of hotsy-totsy.
  • hunky-dory: Similar because both express that a situation is completely fine and free of trouble; differs because hunky-dory is an older 19th-century American idiom (derived from hunky, safe/good) that does not carry the secondary sense of pretentiousness.
  • shipshape: Similar because both describe an orderly state; differs because shipshape stems from nautical terminology and emphasizes rigorous physical organization, tidiness, and mechanical readiness.
  • tickety-boo: Similar because both are cheerful colloquialisms indicating everything is alright; differs because tickety-boo is of British military / Commonwealth origin (often linked to Hindi ṭhīk hai) and lacks American Jazz Age connotations.
  • peachy: Similar because both convey agreeable satisfaction; differs because peachy draws upon the sensory metaphor of sweet fruit and is often used with heavy modern sarcasm ("just peachy").
  • dandy: Similar because both indicate that things are fine; differs because dandy originally referred to an exquisitely dressed gentleman before evolving into a general adjective for high quality.
  • hoity-toity: Similar because hoity-toity matches the secondary sense of hotsy-totsy (snobbish, putting on airs); differs because hoity-toity never means "in fine order" and exclusively denotes haughty arrogance.
  • swanky: Similar because both can describe glamorous, upscale settings; differs because swanky focuses specifically on stylish elegance and ostentatious wealth rather than peaceful resolution.
  • A-OK: Similar because both confirm complete operational success; differs because A-OK originates in 1960s NASA aerospace communications, projecting modern technical precision rather than jazz-age exuberance.
  • snazzy: Similar because both are early 20th-century American slang; differs because snazzy specifically denotes flashy, eye-catching visual style or clothing rather than a satisfactory situation.
