Root 1 — INCANTATIONS:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE; echoic)
*kan-
"to sing"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
canere
"to sing"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
cantāre
"to sing, chant (frequentative)"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
incantāre
"to chant over, enchant"
(in- 'in, on' + cantāre 'to sing')
↓
Latin (Late Latin, 4th c.)
incantātiō
"a chanting, incantation"
↓
Old French (12th c.)
incantacion
"an incantation, charm"
↓
Middle English (14th c.)
incantacioun
"an incantation, spell"
↓
Modern English (15th c.)
incantation
"a spell recited in a chant"
↓
incantations
"chanted spells"

Debate: the PIE root *kan- "to sing" is echoic; the chain into Latin canere → cantāre → incantāre is standard.

Root 2 — CANTRAPS:

Origin unknown (no certain etymon)
"possibly from Gaelic canntaireachd 'a chant'
or from a Scots word"
↓
Scottish (16th c.)
cantrip
"a magic spell, trick"
↓
Scottish (16th–17th c.)
cantrap
"a magic spell (variant of cantrip)"
↓
cantraps
"minor spells"

Debate: the origin of cantrip/cantrap is unknown and much debated; links to Gaelic canntaireachd 'a chant' or to a Scots formation have been suggested, but none is secure.

Root 3 — RUNES:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE; uncertain)
*rewH-
"to roar, murmur"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–500 CE)
*rūnō
"a secret, mystery, rune"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
rūn
"a secret, mystery, counsel"
↓
Modern English (17th c.)
rune
"a runic letter; a magic symbol"
↓
runes
"secret symbols"

Debate: modern English rune was re-introduced in the 17th c. from Old Norse rún, while OE rūn 'secret, mystery' lived on as 'roun' 'a whisper, secret'; the link to PIE *rewH- "to roar, murmur" is uncertain.

Root 4 — THAUMATURGIES:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*wérǵom
"work"
↓
Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE)
ἔργον (érgon)
"work, deed"
↓
Ancient Greek (5th c. BCE; of uncertain origin)
θαῦμα (thaûma)
"a wonder, marvel"
(compound: thaûma + érgon)
↓
Ancient Greek (4th c. BCE)
θαυματουργός (thaumatourgós)
"a wonder-worker"
↓
Ancient Greek (4th c. BCE)
θαυματουργία (thaumatourgía)
"wonder-working"
↓
Modern English (16th c.)
thaumaturgy
"the working of wonders; magic"
↓
thaumaturgies
"miraculous magical acts"

Debate: the element θαῦμα 'wonder, marvel' is of uncertain origin, with no secure PIE etymon; ἔργον 'work' is from PIE *wérǵom.
