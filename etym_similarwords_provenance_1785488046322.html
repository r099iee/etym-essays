• Origin: Similar because both name the starting point of something; differs because origin is the general word for a beginning, while provenance is the documented biography of an object or text.
  • Source: Similar because both name the place or person from which something issues; differs because a source is impersonal and open, while provenance implies an authenticated chain of ownership and custody.
  • Derivation: Similar because both trace how something develops from an earlier form; differs because derivation (especially of words) traces the route of development, while provenance traces custody and ownership.
  • Pedigree: Similar because both denote a traced line of descent; differs because pedigree applies to people and animals, while provenance belongs to objects — paintings, manuscripts, coins.
  • Genealogy: Similar because both are systematic records of descent; differs because genealogy is the scholarly study of human family lines, while provenance documents the history of a thing.
  • Lineage: Similar because both name a chain of succession; differs because lineage is used of families and traditions, while provenance is the ownership-and-origin chain of an object.
  • History: Similar because both describe a thing's past; differs because history is the general narrative record, while provenance is the specific, documented chain that authenticates an object.
  • Ancestry: Similar because both concern what came before; differs because ancestry is biological and human, while provenance is documentary and applies to artefacts.
