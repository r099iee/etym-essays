- Old English - 'Mealt' is the ancient word for grain steeped, germinated, and dried so it will brew; the term and the practice are as old as English ale itself, the everyday speech of farm and brewhouse.
- Middle English to 18th century - With brewing institutionalized, malt becomes a precise technical word: the maltster's craft, the legal and commercial foundation of ale and beer, regulated in law and trade.
- Contemporary sense - Malt keeps its brewing core but has spread: malted milk, maltose the sugar, and malt whisky all carry the word, which now names both the processed grain and the sweet, toasty, biscuit character it gives drink and food.
