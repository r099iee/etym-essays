- 19th century (opium-war era) - Standard English romanization of the Southern Capital; enters global consciousness through the Treaty of Nanking (1842), evoking imperial power and foreign negotiation.
- Early 20th century - Anchors China's cultural heartland and the seat of the Republic of China (1927-1937); carries dynastic prestige and the literary soul of the Han south.
- 1937 era - Becomes indelibly charged with tragedy in historical discourse through the wartime atrocities; the name loses its purely stately register.
- Contemporary - Superseded in official usage by the pinyin "Nanjing"; survives as a dated, nostalgic historical form evoking old maps, imperial prose, and dynastic memory.
