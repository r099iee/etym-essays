• Arundhatī: Similar because both are venerated celestial wives in Hindu lore and personified constellations/stars representing supreme wifely devotion; differs because Arundhatī (Alcor in Ursa Major) is the wife of the sage Vasiṣṭha and symbolizes ascetic chastity, austere moral fortitude, and conjugal constancy, whereas Rohiṇī (Aldebaran) is the erotic, passionate favorite consort of the moon god Candra, embodying romantic beauty and lunar alignment.
  • Sītā: Similar because both embody the absolute ideal of the faithful Hindu wife who accompanies her husband through every trial; differs because Sītā is the incarnate human princess of Mithilā and goddess of the earth who endures earthly tragedy, exile, and trial by fire, whereas Rohiṇī is a primordial stellar goddess and personified lunar asterism.
  • Anurādhā: Similar because both are prominent nakṣatras (lunar mansions) personified as daughters of Dakṣa and celestial brides of Candra; differs because Anurādhā (corresponding to Beta, Delta, and Pi Scorpii) is ruled by Mitra and represents friendship, harmony, and disciplined devotion, lacking the exceptional romantic preeminence and radiant bovine fertility associated with Rohiṇī.
  • Kṛttikā: Similar because both are adjacent zodiacal asterisms in the constellation Taurus; differs because Kṛttikā (the Pleiades) represents cutting, fiery energy ruled by Agni, famous for nursing the infant war god Kārttikeya, whereas Rohiṇī represents gentle moisture, vegetative growth, and romantic beauty.
  • Revatī: Similar because both are auspicious lunar mansions personified as daughters of Dakṣa and cosmic consorts; differs because Revatī is the twenty-seventh (final) nakṣatra representing cosmic journey, dissolution, and shepherd-like protection under Pūṣan, whereas Rohiṇī governs the fertile beginning and growth of the lunar cycle.
  • Devakī: Similar because both are noble wives of King Vasudeva who play pivotal maternal roles in the preservation of the divine avatāras; differs because Devakī is the biological mother of Lord Kṛṣṇa who endured horrific imprisonment and the murder of six sons by Kaṃsa, while Rohiṇī was sent to Gokula to foster and give birth to Balarāma in pastoral safety.
  • Yaśodā: Similar because both are pastoral maternal figures in Gokula who nurtured the young divine brothers (Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma); differs because Yaśodā is the foster mother of Kṛṣṇa married to the cowherd chieftain Nanda, celebrated for vātsalya-rasa (maternal affection), whereas Rohiṇī is a high-born Yadava queen and biological mother of Balarāma.
  • Nakṣatra: Similar because Rohiṇī is the quintessential member of the nakṣatra system; differs because nakṣatra is the general generic category denoting any of the 27 lunar asterisms, whereas Rohiṇī is the individual, preeminent fourth mansion.
  • Aldebaran: Similar because both denote the exact same primary astronomical celestial body (Alpha Tauri); differs because Aldebaran is an Arabic astronomical designation (al-Dabarān, "the follower") denoting its position following behind the Pleiades across the night sky, whereas Rohiṇī is the Sanskrit cultural and mytho-religious personification rooted in bovine red luminosity.
  • Candra: Similar because Rohiṇī and Candra form an indissoluble celestial pair in classical poetic imagery; differs because Candra is the masculine lunar deity himself, while Rohiṇī is the stellar consort whom he passionately pursues.
