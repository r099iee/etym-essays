- Old English (c. 800–1100) - Broad Meaning of Striving & Labor: Attested as *tillian* and *teolian*, meaning to strive for, aim at, labor, procure, or obtain through persistent diligence.
- Middle English (c. 1150–1400) - Agrarian Specialization: Gradually narrowed from generalized laboring to the specific, foundational act of working, plowing, and preparing soil for agricultural crops.
- Early Modern English (c. 1500–1700) - Scriptural & Classical Agrarian Canon: Cemented in the King James Bible (1611) and Elizabethan agricultural treatises as the definitive literary verb for agricultural labor and civilization.
- 18th to 19th Century - Agrarian Revolution & Literary Symbol: Celebrated in romantic and realist literature as the noble, honest toil of the yeoman farmer transforming nature into civilized bounty.
- 20th to 21st Century - Technical Agronomic Classification: Acquired precise environmental and scientific distinctions in modern soil conservation (e.g., conventional till, minimum-till, no-till agriculture).
