- Anglo-Saxon Era (c. 8th–10th c.) - Named Cweorndūn: meaning 'the hill where quern-stones (millstones) are quarried'.
- 1086 - Recorded in the Domesday Book as Querendon, establishing its feudal agrarian presence.
- 1696 - Hugo Meynell founds the Quorn Hunt, revolutionizing English foxhunting into high-speed equestrian sport.
- 18th–19th c. - Zenith of the Quorn Hunt: revered as the ultimate social and sporting playground of British aristocracy and royalty.
- 1889 - The village of Quorndon officially abbreviates its name to Quorn to avoid postal confusion.
- 1985–Present - Marlow Foods launches the global Quorn brand of mycoprotein meat substitutes, named after the Leicestershire village.
