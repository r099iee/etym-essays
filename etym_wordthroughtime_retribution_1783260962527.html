- [15th-century loan] - Entering English in the 1400s (from Latin retributio, 'a giving back'), 'retribution' first meant requital or return - a giving back in kind, whether reward or punishment.
- [Divine and providential sense] - In the Bible and religious prose it became the word for God's just repayment - divine retribution - an impersonal moral law punishing wrong.
- [Judicial and historical use] - In the 18th and 19th centuries it settled into the language of justice and history: just retribution following wrong, the working-out of a moral balance-sheet, grave and providential.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'retribution' means deserved punishment restoring moral balance, distinct from revenge; it still carries the impersonal, judicial ring of consequence fitting crime.
