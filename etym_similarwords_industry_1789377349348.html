• Diligence: Similar because both denote persistent, painstaking effort and conscientious application to a task; differs because diligence emphasizes attentive care, thoroughness, and scrupulous caution in performing a specific duty (from Latin diligere "to esteem/love"), whereas industry emphasizes sustained, energetic, and productive hard work over time and has evolved into an economic sector of mechanized manufacturing.
  • Assiduity: Similar because both refer to unflagging, steady application to work or study; differs because assiduity is a formal, elevated literary term denoting constant, unremitting personal attentiveness and devoted presence (literally "sitting constantly at a task"), whereas industry encompasses broad economic systems, factories, and commercial sectors.
  • Enterprise: Similar because both denote bold, purposeful economic and commercial activity; differs because enterprise focuses on daring initiative, willingness to take risks, adventurous ventures, and entrepreneurial innovation, whereas industry emphasizes steady, routine, disciplined labor and large-scale manufacturing infrastructure.
  • Manufacturing: Similar because both describe the economic production of goods; differs because manufacturing is a strictly technical and operational term denoting the physical process of converting raw materials into finished products through machinery and labor (literally "making by hand"), lacking the moral, psychological, and personal virtue dimensions inherent in industry.
  • Commerce: Similar because both are fundamental components of national and global economic systems; differs because commerce refers specifically to the buying, selling, exchange, and distribution of goods and services between markets (trade and mercantilism), whereas industry focuses on the actual physical production, extraction, and manufacture of those goods.
  • Toil: Similar because both refer to hard human labor; differs because toil emphasizes exhausting, grueling, painful, and often monotonous physical exertion that wears down the body, whereas industry carries a positive, purposeful, and rewarding connotation of productive achievement and skilled mastery.
