- [Antiquity – Classical Greece & Rome] - Hyakinthos / Hyacinthus represents the tragic mythological youth and the dark purple spring flower sprung from his blood, its curling petals comparing to beautiful hair - [connotation: sacred myth, divine sorrow, youthful male beauty, and floral resurrection]
- [14th–16th c.] - Middle and Early Modern English borrow hyacinth / jacinth, denoting both the precious gemstone and the fragrant spring garden flower - [connotation: precious rarity, botanical luxury, and biblical gemstone imagery]
- [17th–18th c.] - Dutch horticultural breeding turns the hyacinth into a worldwide gardening sensation, while Milton uses "hyacinthine locks" in epic poetry - [connotation: elite horticultural status, intense sweet perfume, and classical poetic elegance]
- [19th c. – Victorian Aestheticism] - Romantic and Decadent writers (Poe, Wilde, Swinburne) revive the compound hyacinth-blossom as a supreme aesthetic symbol of dark, fragrant curls and melancholy passion - [connotation: sensuous Aestheticism, romantic yearning, ivory-and-purple visual harmonies]
- [20th–21st c.] - Retains its high poetic and mythological prestige while serving as a global staple of spring floristry, garden exhibitions, and Persian Nowruz celebrations - [connotation: quintessential spring renewal, intoxicating fragrance, and classical literary romance]
