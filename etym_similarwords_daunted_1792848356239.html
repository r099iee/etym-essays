• Intimidated: Similar because both name courage shrinking before something formidable; differs because intimidated stresses fear of a threatening agent, while daunted stresses discouragement by difficulty or magnitude itself.
  • Discouraged: Similar because both describe resolve ebbing; differs because discouraged is broader and flatter (any loss of heart), while daunted keeps the antique flavour of fear before the formidable.
  • Disheartened: Similar because both mean losing heart; differs because disheartened follows failure or bad news, while daunted strikes beforehand, at the prospect itself.
  • Cowed: Similar because both describe subdued spirit; differs because cowed implies submission to a bully or threat, often with shame, while daunted admits no master — only the weight of the task.
  • Overawed: Similar because both involve shrinking before greatness; differs because overawed mixes reverence with fear (overawed by the cathedral), while daunted is fear of the difficulty, not awe at the majesty.
  • Dismayed: Similar because both are dignified words for sinking heart; differs because dismayed reacts to unwelcome news or sights, while daunted faces an undertaking not yet begun.
  • Abashed: Similar because both are Latinate words for diminished confidence; differs because abashed is embarrassment before others, while daunted is apprehension before an obstacle.
  • Unfazed: Similar as the idiomatic opposite's neighbour (nothing daunted ≈ unfazed); differs because unfazed is casual modern cool, while undaunted is formal heroic steadiness.
