• INCARNATION: Similar because both give concrete form to an abstraction — an idea "taking flesh"; differs because incarnation carries a specifically religious overtone (the flesh-taking of a divine being) and is therefore too weighted for purely aesthetic or ordinary use.
  • PERSONIFICATION: Similar because both make an abstraction concrete; differs because personification confines the abstraction to a single human person and is primarily a figure of rhetoric or a literary device, whereas an embodiment need not be a person at all (a poem, a building, a sound can be an embodiment).
  • REALISATION: Similar because both make something actual or fulfilled; differs because realisation stresses the fulfilment of a potential or plan with no insistence on physical form, while embodiment insists on a tangible, sensory body.
  • MANIFESTATION: Similar because both make something appear; differs because manifestation stresses the becoming-apparent of what was hidden — its visibility or perceptibility — rather than its physical embodiment.
  • MATERIALISATION: Similar because both bring something immaterial into the world of things; differs because materialisation emphasises the appearance of what was previously immaterial, often with a faintly supernatural or fantastical air (a ghost materialising), and is rarer in ordinary praise.
  • INSTANTIATION: Similar because both name a particular case of a general pattern; differs because instantiation is the cold, technical term of logic and computer science for one instance of an abstract type, with no suggestion of physicality or of the value-laden "perfect example."
  • EPITOME: Similar because both denote a perfect typical example of a quality or class; differs because epitome emphasises the concentrated summary of a type (the epitome of elegance), while embodiment emphasises the giving of a body to something, and can be used of acts, sounds, and institutions as well as persons.
  • CONCRETISATION: Similar because both mean "making abstract things concrete"; differs because concretisation is a dry, learned, almost bureaucratic synonym (common in philosophy and translation) that lacks embodiment's living image of a body and its emotional warmth.
