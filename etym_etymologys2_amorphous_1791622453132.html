Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*n̥- + *mergʷ- / *morgʷ-
"un-, not, without" + "shape, boundary, form"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE)
ἀ- (a-) + μορφή (morphē)
"without" + "form, outward shape, beauty"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 5th c. BCE)
ἄμορφος (ámorphos)
"misshapen, shapeless, unsightly, deformed"
↓
Late Latin / Scientific Latin (18th c.)
amorphus
"lacking regular crystalline or mineral form"
↓
English (c. 1730s–1740s)
amorphous
"lacking definite shape or crystalline structure"

Note: The ultimate origin of Greek μορφή (morphē) is debated; some scholars trace it to PIE *mergʷ- ("boundary, border"), while others consider it a Pre-Greek substrate loanword.
