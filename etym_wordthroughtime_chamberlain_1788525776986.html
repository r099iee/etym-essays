- [Antiquity to Early Middle Ages] - Greek kamára and Latin camera designate a vaulted private chamber; adopted into Frankish courts as *kamerling for chamber custodians - [connotation: trusted personal domestic servant to Frankish kings]
- [11th–13th c. Old French to ME] - French chamberlenc enters Middle English as chaumberlein following the Norman Conquest - [connotation: powerful feudal magnate controlling royal palace access and private royal treasure]
- [14th–16th c. Late ME to Early Modern] - Established as Great Officers of State (Lord Chamberlain) and municipal treasurers across England and Scotland - [connotation: pinnacle of courtly authority, master of royal ceremonies, and guardian of civic wealth]
- [17th–19th c.] - Retains immense social glamour, while Lord Chamberlain gains statutory power over theatrical censorship (1737–1968) - [connotation: symbol of high aristocratic pedigree, court protocol, and institutional authority]
- [20th–21st c.] - Endures as a prestigious ceremonial, municipal, and papal office, and as a famous surname in British political history - [connotation: dignified historic title of ceremonial and municipal stewardship]
