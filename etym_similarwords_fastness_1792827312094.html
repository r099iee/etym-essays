• Stronghold: Similar because both name a fortified place of safety and resistance; differs because stronghold is the plain modern word for any defended base, while fastness is archaic and wilder, suggesting mountains and remoteness.
  • Fortress: Similar because both mean great defensive works of stone; differs because a fortress stresses massive built walls and garrisons, while a fastness stresses landscape-aided security, half nature and half masonry.
  • Citadel: Similar because both are last-resort refuges above or within a city; differs because a citadel is the fortified core of a town, while a fastness is usually remote and lonely, far from streets.
  • Refuge: Similar because both promise safety to the pursued; differs because refuge is general and gentle (refuges for the homeless, for wildlife), while fastness is martial and forbidding.
  • Hideout: Similar because both shelter fugitives and outlaws; differs because a hideout is small, secret, and often shabby, while a fastness is grand, ancient, and built to withstand siege.
  • Firmness: Similar because both name the quality of holding fast and not giving way; differs because firmness is the everyday abstract noun (a firm handshake, firm resolve), while fastness in this sense is rare and literary.
  • Swiftness: Similar because both can name great speed; differs because swiftness is the ordinary elegant word for rapid motion, while fastness for speed is blunt and technical, commonest in testing and sport.
  • Colour-fastness: Similar because it is literally a specialised use of fastness itself; differs because colour-fastness narrows the meaning to dyes resisting wash and sun, a laboratory compound of the general noun.
  • Steadfastness: Similar because both praise unyielding loyalty; differs because steadfastness is the moral virtue in people, while fastness reaches loyalty only by metaphor from walls and knots.
