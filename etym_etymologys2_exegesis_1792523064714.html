Note on Indo-European cognates and semantic development: Comparative philologists (including Pierre Chantraine, Hjalmar Frisk, and Robert Beekes) trace Greek hēgéomai to the PIE root *sāg- ("to track, seek, perceive keenly"). This root is cognate with Latin sāgīre ("to perceive keenly with the senses"), from which Latin derived sagāx (source of English sagacious and sage) and praesāgīre (source of English presage). In the Germanic branch, the root gave Proto-Germanic *sōkjaną, which evolved into Old English sēcan and modern English seek. Thus, at its deepest etymological core, to perform an exegesis is to "track down and seek out" the trail of truth embedded in human language.
