- [13th century the cursed and evil] - [Shrewd derives from the noun 'shrew', a small fierce creature regarded as evil; the adjective first means evil, cursed, malicious — connotation dark and threatening]
- [14th-16th century the wily turn] - [Softens from wicked to cunning: a shrewd disposition is keen, knowing, and sharp; connotation shifts from the devilish to the canny]
- [17th-18th century the modern sense] - [Acquires its settled meaning — sharp, penetrating, unsentimental practical judgment, not easily deceived; connotation admiring yet hard-edged, a compliment with a cutting quality]
- [19th-21st century the hard compliment] - [A standard compliment for astute, worldly intelligence — the 'shrewd observer' whose endorsement carries weight precisely because it is hard-won; a faint undertone of the word's dark past still lingers]
