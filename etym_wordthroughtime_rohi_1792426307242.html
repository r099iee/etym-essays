- [Vedic Era (c. 1500–800 BCE)] — originated in the Ṛgveda and Taittirīya Saṃhitā as an astronomical and pastoral designation (*rohiṇī*) meaning "the red cow" or "the ruddy dawn/star," marking the vital spring equinox asterism ruled by Prajāpati — [perception: revered as a sacred celestial cow and divine source of generative rain, milk, and seasonal fertility]
- [Epic Period (c. 500 BCE–200 CE)] — anthropomorphized in the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa as the preeminent daughter of Dakṣa and the impassioned favorite bride of Candra, serving as the quintessential literary simile for Sītā's unshakeable spousal devotion — [perception: celebrated as the universal poetic paragon of conjugal loyalty and romantic preference]
- [Classical Purāṇic & Harivaṃśa Period (c. 300–1000 CE)] — expanded into Vaishnava narrative genealogy as the senior queen of Vasudeva and the loving mother of Balarāma and Subhadrā in Gokula, while in Jyotiṣa it became immortalized as the sacred birth star (Janma-nakṣatra) of Lord Kṛṣṇa — [perception: sanctified as an exalted matriarch of the Yādavas and a cosmic portal of divine avatāra descent]
- [Medieval & Pre-Modern Era (c. 1100–1800 CE)] — integrated into Āyurvedic medical lexicons as the name of medicinal healing plants (*Picrorhiza kurroa*) and pharyngeal diseases, while remaining a cherished name in devotional poetry and temple festivals celebrating Janmāṣṭamī — [perception: practical technical utility in traditional pharmacology paired with pervasive liturgical veneration]
- [Modern Era (19th–21st c.)] — widely used across the Indian subcontinent as an auspicious feminine given name and honored in modern astronomy as the cultural Indian identification for the red supergiant Aldebaran; also chosen by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) for its pioneering series of "Rohini" sounding rockets and satellites — [perception: enduring symbol of timeless cultural heritage, feminine grace, and modern scientific aspiration]
