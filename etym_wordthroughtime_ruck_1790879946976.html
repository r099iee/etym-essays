- 13th–14th Century - Borrowed from Old Norse into Middle English meaning a heap, pile, or stack of hay, grain, or turf.
- 15th–16th Century - Developed the sense of a fold, crease, or wrinkle in cloth or leather.
- 18th–19th Century - Applied in horse racing to the main, undistinguished pack of horses trailing behind the leaders; rapidly generalized to the mass of ordinary, mediocre people.
- Late 19th–20th Century - Formalized as a technical term in rugby football for a loose pack contesting the ball on the ground.
- Late 20th–21st Century - Influenced by military rucksack terminology, "ruck" emerged as a verb and noun for loaded pack marching and endurance fitness.
