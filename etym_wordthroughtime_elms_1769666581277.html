- [Old English–medieval era] - "Elms" simply named the tall, shade-giving deciduous trees of the genus Ulmus — a neutral, everyday word for a familiar native tree, valued for its strong timber and generous summer shade.

- [18th–19th c.] - As landowners planted grand elm avenues and village greens, "elms" became culturally tied to stately, dignified English landscape, communal shade, and rustic peace; the word took on a measured, pastoral dignity.

- [20th c. onward] - The arrival of Dutch elm disease transformed the emotional register: "elms" now often carries elegiac nostalgia, symbolizing a celebrated yet vanishing landscape and the memory of a pastoral age lost to disease and change.
