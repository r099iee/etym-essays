• Provisionally: Similar because both indicate that an arrangement is temporary and subject to future alteration or cancellation; differs because provisionally emphasizes that an agreement is a temporary stopgap adopted until a permanent solution is determined, whereas revocably specifically highlights the legal power or right to formally cancel it.
  • Conditionally: Similar because both qualify an action by making its permanence dependent on certain factors; differs because conditionally emphasizes that the validity of the act depends upon the occurrence or satisfaction of specific external prerequisites (stipulations), whereas revocably focuses on the unilateral power of the grantor to pull it back.
  • Defeasibly: Similar because both describe legal rights, estates, or claims that are capable of being terminated or annulled; differs because defeasibly is a specialized Anglo-American property term meaning subject to being defeated upon the occurrence of a specific subsequent event, whereas revocably emphasizes an intentional act of revocation by the grantor.
  • Temporarily: Similar because both denote an absence of eternal or permanent duration; differs because temporarily simply denotes a limited span of time, whereas revocably denotes a specific legal or formal vulnerability to being rescinded regardless of how long it lasts.
  • Tentatively: Similar because both convey hesitation, caution, and an absence of finality; differs because tentatively emphasizes an exploratory, experimental, or uncommitted mindset, whereas revocably can apply to an entirely formal, solemn legal decree that simply contains a power of revocation.
  • Precariously: Similar because both involve arrangements that could collapse or be terminated at any moment; differs because precariously carries a strong negative connotation of perilous insecurity, instability, and danger, while revocably is a neutral, orderly procedural descriptor.
  • Rescindably: Similar because both denote the capacity of an agreement or law to be cancelled or voided; differs because rescindably focuses specifically on the unwinding, voiding, or abrogation of a bilateral transaction from the beginning, whereas revocably focuses broadly on the power of withdrawal or recall.
