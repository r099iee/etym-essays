- Mid-16th-century entry - zealous, from Latin zelus via Greek zelos, entered English in religious and biblical contexts, denoting fervent, wholehearted devotion, especially newly kindled religious commitment, and could still shade toward the negative sense of jealous over-ardor.
- 17th-19th-century expansion - the word broadened from religion to any cause, pursuit, or partisanship, keeping its approving force while overzealous marked the point where fervor tips into excess.
- Contemporary sense - zealous denotes eager energy combined with unswerving loyalty to a cause; unlike fanatical it can still be used approvingly, though it has retreated to a fairly formal register in ordinary speech.
