- Late 19th c. - "shimmer" establishes itself for a faint, quivering light — heat-haze, water, wet streets — and "shimmery" is its informal adjective for that low, wavering glow
- 20th c. - the word attaches to fabric: shimmering silks, satins, and sequins turn "shimmery" into a glitzy, show-business adjective of caught light
- Contemporary - shimmery is the word for a soft, tremulous, continuously alive light that does not flash or dazzle; set beside "shadowy" it paints a hushed, magical night — moonlight trembling over leaf and stone — the visual signature of enchantment
