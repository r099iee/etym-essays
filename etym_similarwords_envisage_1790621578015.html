• envision: Similar because it means to picture future possibilities; differs because it is the American preferred form, more visual and visionary and corporate, while envisage is British, more deliberative and administrative.
  • imagine: Similar because it means to form mental images; differs because it is broader, more everyday and creative, allowing fantasy, while envisage implies serious contemplation of real possibilities.
  • visualize: Similar because it means to make a mental image; differs because it stresses quasi-perceptual seeing and is used for techniques and graphics, while envisage stresses intellectual anticipation.
  • conceive: Similar because it means to form an idea in the mind; differs because it stresses origination of an idea, while envisage stresses contemplating its future shape.
  • contemplate: Similar because it means to consider thoughtfully; differs because it is slower and more meditative, while envisage points toward future outcome.
  • anticipate: Similar because it means to expect beforehand; differs because it implies preparation and forestalling, while envisage implies picturing.
  • foresee: Similar because it means to see beforehand; differs because it claims predictive knowledge, while envisage admits hypothetical imagination.
  • forecast: Similar in planning contexts; differs because it is quantitative and data-driven, while envisage is qualitative and imaginative.
  • picture: Similar as verb (picture a world without oil); differs because it is informal and vividly visual, while envisage is formal.
  • fancy: Similar in British use (fancy living there); differs because it is colloquial and whimsical, while envisage is formal and responsible.
  • project: Similar in planning (project growth); differs because it is technical-extrapolative, while envisage is mental-contemplative.
  • entertain: Similar in phrase entertain an idea; differs because it means to hold open as possible, while envisage means to work out its shape.
