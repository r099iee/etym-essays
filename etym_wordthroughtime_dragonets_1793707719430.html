- 17th Century (Late) - First attested use in English to describe a small marine fish, likely due to their perceived 'dragon-like' appearance (large eyes, elaborate fins, sometimes spiny) - Primarily a scientific/natural history term, with a descriptive, somewhat fantastical connotation.
- 18th-19th Century - Continued use in ichthyological texts and natural history observations. The term remains largely confined to scientific and specialized contexts, maintaining its descriptive accuracy for the fish family. Connotation is factual and classificatory.
- 20th Century (Mid-Late) - Increased awareness, particularly with the rise of marine aquaria, as some species (like the Mandarinfish) become popular. The term gains a slightly broader, though still specialized, recognition. The 'dragon-like' aspect is appreciated for its aesthetic and exotic appeal.
- 21st Century (Contemporary) - Widely recognized in marine biology, aquaculture, and the aquarium hobby. The term carries connotations of vibrant beauty, unique morphology, and ecological interest. Its metaphorical use as a 'small dragon' remains rare but evokes a sense of miniature fantasy or ancient, gnarled forms, as seen in the provided literary context.
