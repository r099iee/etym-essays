• fishermen: Similar because it denotes people who catch fish by any method; differs because it is fully generic while angler strictly implies hook and line.
  • fisherfolk: Similar because it covers fishing people collectively; differs because it is communal and literary, while anglers stresses individual rod users.
  • rod fishermen: Similar because it specifies hook-and-line technique; differs because it is a transparent compound, while angler is the lexicalised single word.
  • fly-fishers: Similar because they are a subtype of anglers using artificial flies; differs because fly-fishing is a specialised elite method, not all angling.
  • coarse fishers: Similar because they angle for non-game freshwater species; differs because coarse is a British sport category, narrower than angler.
  • trollers: Similar because they fish by trailing baited lines; differs because trolling specifies boat movement, while angling includes still bank fishing.
  • spinners: Similar because they angle with spinning lures; differs because it names tackle type, not the general actor.
  • poachers: Similar because they also take fish illicitly; differs because poaching implies illegality, while angling implies licensed sport.
  • netters: Similar because they are commercial fish catchers; differs because nets encircle shoals, opposite to single-hook angling.
  • trawlermen: Similar because they earn a living from fish capture; differs because trawling is industrial net-dragging, not rod angling.
  • spearfishers: Similar because they take individual fish by aim; differs because the spear pierces visibly, while the hook is taken voluntarily.
  • hunters: Similar because both pursue wild animals with skill and patience; differs because hunting targets land game, angling targets fish.
