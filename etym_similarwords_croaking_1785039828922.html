• Growling: Similar because both are low, harsh throat-noises; differs because a growl is a continuous menacing rumble from the chest, more angry than pained, while croaking is hoarse and grating, the sound of a rough throat scraping.
  • Howling: Similar because both are loud animal cries; differs because howling is long, sustained, and mournful like a wolf, whereas croaking is short, rattling, and froglike.
  • Shrieking: Similar because both are intense vocal cries; differs because shrieking is high and piercing, the opposite register of croaking's deep, guttural rattle.
  • Yammering: Similar because both are noisy utterances of distress; differs because yammering is loud, monotonous, complaining talk — articulate and human — while croaking is pure, sub-human animal noise.
  • Gurgling: Similar because both are throat-sounds; differs because gurgling is the liquid, bubbling noise of air through fluid, while croaking is dry, harsh, and grating.
  • Rasping: Similar because both denote a rough, grating sound in the throat; differs because rasping is the general word for any harsh scraping noise, while croaking specifically evokes the frog or raven and their folklore.
  • Wheezing: Similar because both are strained breath-noises; differs because wheezing is the whistling sound of laboured breathing, while croaking is the vocal, guttural rasp of the throat itself.
  • Cawing: Similar because both are harsh bird-cries; differs because cawing is the specific, flat cry of crows and rooks, while croaking is deeper, more guttural, and extended to frogs, hoarse voices, and dying.
  • Croaking (death slang): Similar because both name the last rough breath of a dying creature; differs because the slang sense of croak ("to die", 1812) is a specialised extension, while the plain participle retains the animal-call meaning.
