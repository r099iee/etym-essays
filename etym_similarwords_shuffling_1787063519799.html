• Scuffling: Similar because both describe feet dragging or scraping; differs because scuffling suggests a struggle or the noise of shoes scraping, while shuffling is the quieter, slower drag.
  • Shambling: Similar because both describe a clumsy, unsteady walk; differs because shambling is a loose, awkward gait (often of a large body), while shuffling is a short dragging step.
  • Trudging: Similar because both describe heavy, laborious walking; differs because trudging stresses fatigue and weight (a long march), while shuffling stresses the dragging motion itself.
  • Hobbling: Similar because both describe impaired walking; differs because hobbling is an uneven, limping walk from injury, while shuffling is a flat dragging step, often from age or caution.
  • Dragging: Similar because both involve pulling the feet along; differs because dragging is the general verb for pulling anything and lacks the card-mixing and evasiveness senses.
  • Evasive / shifty: Similar because "shuffling" can mean dodging questions and acting dishonestly; differs because evasive and shifty are character adjectives with no physical walking or card sense.
  • Mixed / jumbled: Similar because shuffled cards are thoroughly mixed; differs because mixed and jumbled are neutral ordering words with no gait, dance, or evasiveness sense.
