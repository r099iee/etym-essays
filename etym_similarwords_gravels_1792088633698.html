• Pebbles: Similar because both refer to small, water-rounded rock fragments; differs because pebbles is an inherently countable noun designating individual stones (typically 4 to 64 mm), whereas gravels primarily represents categorized strata, mass aggregate varieties, or the third-person verb.
  • Shingle: Similar because both denote water-worn coastal or fluvial stone deposits; differs because shingle refers specifically to coarse, flat, loose beach stones accumulated along marine shorelines, lacking the broader engineering classification and verbal polysemy of gravels.
  • Scree: Similar because both consist of loose rock debris; differs because scree (or talus) comprises sharp, fractured, angular rock fragments accumulated at the base of cliffs through freeze-thaw weathering, whereas gravels are predominantly water-rounded, river-borne, or commercially processed aggregates.
  • Ballast: Similar because both denote coarse aggregate used in heavy civil engineering; differs because ballast is functionally defined as heavy crushed stone or slag laid to stabilize railroad ties or provide naval ship stability, regardless of geological origin.
  • Perplexes: Similar because both denote causing severe intellectual confusion or mental impasse; differs because perplexes is a formal Latinate verb describing emotional uncertainty and bewilderment, whereas gravels carries a vivid, colloquial, physical metaphor of being run aground and completely stalled like a ship on a pebble bar.
  • Calculi: Similar because both historically refer to mineralized concretions in the human urinary tract; differs because calculi is the formal medical plural for solid mineral stones of any size, whereas gravels specifically designated fine, sand-like crystalline sediment discharged from the kidneys.
