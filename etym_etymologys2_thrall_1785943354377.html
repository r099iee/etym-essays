Middle English (13th c.)
thral

↓
Modern English
thrall
"bondage, servitude; a slave"

Note: The Old English word was borrowed from Old Norse during the Viking Age. The modern sense "state of bondage" developed from the older noun meaning "a slave". The pre-Norse history is debated: it may continue a Proto-Germanic *þrahilaz "runner" (cf. Gothic þragjan "to run", PIE *tregh- "to run"), i.e. a servant who runs errands, but the link is not certain.
