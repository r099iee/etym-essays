Drinking sense — from genever:
Latin (1st c. BCE)
juniperus
‘juniper tree, juniper’
↓
Old French (13th c.)
genievre
‘juniper; juniper drink’
↓
Middle Dutch (15th c.)
genever
‘juniper spirit’
↓
English (early 18th c.)
geneva
‘Dutch juniper spirit’
↓
Modern English (18th c., clipped)
gin
‘juniper spirit’

Trap sense — from engine:
PIE (reconstructed, c. 4000 BCE)
*ǵenh₁-
‘to beget, produce’
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
ingenium
‘innate talent, device’
↓
Old French (12th c.)
engin
‘skill; machine, trap’
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
gin
‘snare, trap, device’
↓
Modern English (current)
gin
‘trap; cotton machine’

Note: Latin juniperus itself is of obscure origin — de Vaan lists no secure etymology, though it is often analysed as a compound of uncertain members — so the chain properly begins at the Latin tree-name; the two modern gins are true homonyms, one clipped from Dutch, one worn down from French, meeting by accident in three letters.
