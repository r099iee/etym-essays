Semitic Root (c. 1000 BCE)
n-w-b (ن-و-b)
"to represent, stand in place of, substitute, alternate"
↓
Classical Arabic active participle (c. 7th c. CE)
nā'ib (نَائِب)
"deputy, vicegerent, lieutenant, proxy, representative"
↓
Classical Arabic honorific / broken plural (c. 10th–12th c. CE)
nuwwāb / nawwāb (نُوَّاب)
"deputies, governors, viceroys acting for the sovereign"
↓
Persian and Mughal Court Urdu (c. 16th c.)
nawwāb (نواب)
"provincial governor, viceroy, or prince under the Mughal Emperor"
↓
Portuguese colonial maritime borrowing (c. 16th c.)
nababo
"Mughal governor or Indian provincial ruler"
↓
Early Modern English borrowing (c. 1610s)
nabob
"a native deputy ruler or viceroy in Mughal India"
↓
Georgian British social satire semantic shift (c. 1760s–1780s)
nabob
"an East India Company official returned to Britain with immense, ostentatious wealth"
↓
Modern English generalized noun (19th–21st c.)
nabob
"a conspicuously wealthy, influential, and luxurious personage; a tycoon"
