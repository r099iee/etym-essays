- Old English (dwellan): "to mislead, deceive" — a word of clouded paths, of travellers led astray and made to wander in error
- Old English into Middle English: "to tarry, linger, be delayed" — the strayed wanderer who is held back in a place comes to be seen as one who simply remains, the seed of residence
- Middle English (dwellen, 14th c.): "to abide, stay, reside" — lingering hardens into permanent settlement; dweller and dwelling (the house itself) are coined from it
- Modern English: "to live or reside" — the plain, weighty verb for making one's permanent home, still carrying an archaic solemnity that fits a being hidden forever in an unreachable realm
- Present-day idiom (dwell on / dwell upon): "to linger over a thought" — the ancient sense of tarrying survives, shifted from the body to the mind
