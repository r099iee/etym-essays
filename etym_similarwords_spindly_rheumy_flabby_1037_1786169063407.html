• Lanky: Similar in being tall and thin; differs because lanky is loose-limbed and tall, while spindly emphasizes mechanical fragility — limbs like matchsticks.
  • Gaunt: Similar in marked thinness; differs because gaunt is thin from hunger or suffering, whereas spindly is thin as if stretched out weak.
  • Reed-like: Similar in slender fragility; differs because reed-like suggests delicate grace, whereas spindly highlights ungainly weakness.
  • Scrawny: Similar in thinness and weakness; differs because scrawny implies bony leanness from poor feeding, while spindly implies disproportionate elongation.
  • Gangly: Similar in awkward, thin elongation; differs because gangly suggests adolescent clumsiness, while spindly is a general structural fragility.
  • Skinny: Similar in thinness; differs because skinny is neutral thinness, while spindly adds weakness and disproportionate slenderness.
  • Spindle-shanks (archaic): Similar in the same spindle image; differs because this is specific to legs, while spindly generalizes to any structure.
  • Bleary: Similar in describing dimmed eyes; differs because bleary is hazy from tiredness or drink without discharge, while rheumy names watery eyes leaking moisture.
  • Watery: Similar in eye moisture; differs because watery is neutral and temporary, whereas rheumy implies chronic, age-related fluid discharge.
  • Weeping: Similar in eyes leaking fluid; differs because weeping implies tears (emotion or irritation), while rheumy names the thin mucous discharge of age or illness.
  • Damp: Similar in moisture; differs because damp describes surfaces or air, while rheumy is specific to bodily discharges.
  • Catarrhal: Similar in mucous discharge; differs because catarrhal is clinical and nasal, whereas rheumy is literary and primarily ocular.
  • Limp: Similar in lacking firmness; differs because limp describes lacking stiffness in inert bodies, while flabby names soft flesh lacking muscle tone.
  • Flaccid: Similar in soft flesh; differs because flaccid is a formal or clinical term for lacking turgor, while flabby carries a vivid tactile sense of loose flesh flopping.
  • Loose: Similar in lacking firmness; differs because loose is general slackness of fit, while flabby specifically names soft, yielding flesh or slack substance.
  • Sagging: Similar in drooping softness; differs because sagging emphasizes gravity's pull on loose material, while flabby emphasizes the loss of tone and resilience.
  • Pudgy: Similar in soft, plump flesh; differs because pudgy is a plump softness that may be healthy or endearing, while flabby is softness without tone or vigour.
  • Blubbery: Similar in loose, heavy softness; differs because blubbery suggests thick layers of fat, while flabby suggests slack, toneless flesh.
  • Weak-willed (figurative): Similar in the figurative sense of flabby character; differs because weak-willed names the defect of will directly, while flabby transfers a physical image of softness.
  • Slack: Similar in lacking tension; differs because slack applies to ropes, effort, or discipline, while flabby is chiefly flesh and substance.
