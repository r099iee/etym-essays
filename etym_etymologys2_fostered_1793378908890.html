Note: The Proto-Indo-European root *pā- (‘to protect, feed’) underwent regular sound change in Proto-Germanic via Grimm's Law, shifting the voiceless labial stop *p to the voiceless labial fricative *f. With an instrumental suffix *-tro-, it produced Proto-Germanic *fōstrą (‘means of feeding, nourishment’), the direct ancestor of Old English fōstor. Cognates from the same PIE root include Latin pascere (‘to graze, pasture’), panis (‘bread’), and native English food, feed, and fodder.
