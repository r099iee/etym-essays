- Old English (swapan) - "to sweep, to rush along"; the verb and its kin (sweep, swipe) described a driving, forceful sweeping motion.
- Middle English - "to swoop" meant to sweep, swing, or move in a curve; still purely physical, without any hunting sense.
- 16th c. - narrowed to the hawk's verb: a bird of prey suddenly descending from above in a curve to seize its quarry.
- 17th-19th c. - extended to any swift, decisive downward plunge; Shakespeare's "in one fell swoop" fixed the sense of sudden, devastating totality.
- Modern register - vivid, dynamic, morally weighted: a swoop is graceful yet merciless, an attack that also reads as an intervention.
