brooches
- c. 1300 — Middle English broche names one thing that is both the cook's roasting spit and the lady's ornamental pin; what unites them is piercing pointedness.
- 16th–17th centuries — the spelling splits: "brooch" is reserved for the jewellery, "broach" for the spit and for piercing (and, figuratively, for broaching a topic); the ornament sense becomes firmly the lady's cloak-and-gown fastener.
- 19th century — the Victorian age mass-produces the brooch as a sentimental keepsake — cameos, lockets, jet for mourning — so the word comes to name the small personal ornament of every rank, from queen to governess.
- Contemporary — slightly old-fashioned and descriptive, at home in antique-shop windows, costume catalogues, and historical fantasy; still the standard word for pinned ornamental jewellery, scaled to the wearer rather than the throne.
