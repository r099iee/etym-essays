• Floated: Similar because it describes being supported by water; differs because 'floated' implies passive suspension without active propulsion, whereas 'swam' implies active movement.
  • Paddled: Similar because it involves moving through water using limbs; differs because 'paddled' often suggests a less efficient, more rudimentary, or slower form of swimming, often with hands or feet.
  • Glided: Similar because it describes smooth movement, potentially through water; differs because 'glided' emphasizes effortless, continuous motion, which can be a part of swimming but isn't the whole act of propulsion.
  • Reeled: Similar (in the context of 'head swam') because it describes a sensation of spinning or losing balance; differs because 'reeled' specifically implies a staggering or swaying motion, often from dizziness or shock, without the aquatic connotation.
  • Spun: Similar (in the context of 'head swam') because it describes a sensation of rotation or dizziness; differs because 'spun' is a more general term for rotation and doesn't inherently carry the feeling of disorientation or the 'fluid' sense implied by 'swam' in this context.
  • Navigated (water): Similar because it involves moving through water; differs because 'navigated' implies a more deliberate, planned, and often skilled movement, focusing on direction and course, rather than just the act of propulsion.
