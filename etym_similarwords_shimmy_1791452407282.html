• Wiggle: shares the idea of small twisting or lateral movements; differs because it is gentler, more controlled and often playful, without the rapid vibration of a shimmy.
  • Wriggle: similar in suggesting sinuous movement, especially of the body; differs because it implies twisting to escape or to fit through something, not a rhythmic shake.
  • Shake: the broadest term for the motion; differs because it is neutral and unspecified, whereas shimmy names a particular rapid, repeating, lateral quiver.
  • Quiver: shares the rapid trembling quality; differs because it usually suggests fine, involuntary tremor, often of fear or cold, rather than deliberate bodily display.
  • Shiver: similar in being a rapid involuntary tremor; differs because it is caused by cold, fever or fear and involves the whole body, not a controlled dance movement.
  • Tremble: close in meaning to quiver; differs in being slower, more visible and more strongly associated with emotion or weakness.
  • Wobble: shares the mechanical and bodily instability; differs because it implies a slower, larger, side-to-side sway, often of an object losing balance.
  • Vibrate: the technical term for rapid oscillation; differs in register, being neutral and scientific, and in lacking the bodily, dance-hall connotations.
  • Jiggle: similar in suggesting small quick shakes; differs because it is lighter, often humorous, and usually applied to small objects or loose flesh.
  • Jitter: shares the idea of rapid small movements; differs because it is associated with nervousness, machines and electronic signals rather than sensuality or dance.
  • Shudder: similar in being a convulsive shaking; differs because it is a single violent tremor of horror or revulsion, not a repeated rhythmic shake.
  • Sashay: a dance-related verb of confident movement; differs because it means to glide or strut, with no shaking at all.
  • Sway: shares lateral motion; differs in being slow, smooth and rhythmic rather than rapid and quivering.
  • Shinny: the climbing verb that has influenced shimmy up; differs because it refers specifically to gripping and hauling with the limbs, not to shaking.
  • Oscillate: the technical term for the wheel movement; differs because it is neutral and mathematical, while shimmy is vivid and informal.
  • Chemise: the garment from which the word derives; differs because it names the clothing, not the motion, and belongs to the register of dress history.
