• Dangerous: Similar because both mean likely to cause harm; differs because dangerous is the plain, factual, all-purpose word carrying no elevation, whereas perilous is literary and archaic, implying imminent, dire, possibly fatal threat.
  • Hazardous: Similar because both mean involving risk; differs because hazardous is the more technical, clinical word (hazardous materials, hazardous conditions) involving chance and probability, while perilous implies a present, deadly danger.
  • Risky: Similar because both mean exposing to possible harm or loss; differs because risky is impersonal and outcome-oriented — a bet on the future — whereas perilous is grave and personal, a danger to life or to what one values.
  • Unsafe: Similar because both mean not free from danger; differs because unsafe is practical and everyday, applied to structures, practices, and places, while perilous is elevated and literary.
  • Fraught: Similar because both mean charged with danger; differs because fraught (with danger) is diffuse and suggests a situation pregnant with risk, whereas perilous points to an actual, concrete hazard.
  • Precarious: Similar because both mean dangerously exposed; differs because precarious means insecure and dependent on chance — a position that may collapse — while perilous stresses the imminent threat itself.
  • Treacherous: Similar because both mean dangerous; differs because treacherous implies hidden, deceptive danger — the road or surface that looks safe and is not — whereas perilous is an open, acknowledged hazard.
  • Menacing: Similar because both mean threatening harm; differs because menacing describes the appearance of threat (a menacing look), while perilous describes the objective condition of danger.
  • Parlous: Similar because both descend from the same Latin stem and mean dangerous; differs because parlous is the archaic 14th–16th c. corruption of perilous, now jocular or dialectal, while perilous is the standard elevated form.
  • Dicey: Similar because both mean involving risk; differs because dicey is informal and casual — a risky, uncertain situation — while perilous is grave, formal, and old-fashioned.
