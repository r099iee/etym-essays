• Zest: Similar because both name keen, vigorous enjoyment or appetite; differs because 'zest' emphasizes energetic enthusiasm and also means the flavourful outer peel of citrus fruit, while 'relish' emphasizes savouring pleasure and also names a condiment.
  • Gusto: Similar because both name hearty, eager enjoyment; differs because 'gusto' (from Italian, meaning taste) is all energy and verve, while 'relish' is the more savouring, appreciative word and can also be a concrete condiment.
  • Enthusiasm: Similar because both describe strong positive interest; differs because 'enthusiasm' is eager general interest in an activity or cause, while 'relish' is the actual pleasure taken in doing or savouring something.
  • Savour (savor): Similar because both involve enjoying something to the full and both can mean taste; differs because 'savour' is primarily a verb of lingeringly tasting and appreciating, while 'relish' is more often a noun and can also be a food condiment.
  • Appreciation: Similar because both name a valuing and enjoyment of something; differs because 'appreciation' is the calm, intellectual recognition of worth, while 'relish' is the eager, pleasurable appetite for something.
  • Delight: Similar because both name keen pleasure; differs because 'delight' is the state of being highly pleased, while 'relish' is the appetite and enjoyment brought to the activity of pursuing or savouring.
  • Pleasure: Similar because both name enjoyment; differs because 'pleasure' is the broad, neutral word for any enjoyment, while 'relish' is the sharp, eager, savouring form of it.
  • Tang: Similar because both can name a sharp, distinctive flavour that gives food interest; differs because 'tang' is specifically the strong taste itself (a tang of lemon), while 'relish' is the enjoyment of it or the condiment that supplies it.
  • Piquancy: Similar because both relate to a pleasantly sharp, appetizing quality; differs because 'piquancy' is the quality of being piquant — the sharpness itself — while 'relish' is the pleasure taken or the condiment, not the quality.
  • Condiment: Similar because both can name something added to food for flavour; differs because 'condiment' is the generic class of seasonings (salt, mustard, ketchup), while 'relish' is a specific piquant chopped pickle and, unlike 'condiment', is also a psychological noun of enjoyment.
  • Fondness: Similar because both can name a warm liking for something; differs because 'fondness' is an affectionate, enduring preference, while 'relish' is the eager, active enjoyment of a particular pleasure.
  • Keenness: Similar because both name eager appetite or intensity of interest; differs because 'keenness' stresses sharp, eager desire (a keenness to begin), while 'relish' stresses the savouring pleasure actually taken.
