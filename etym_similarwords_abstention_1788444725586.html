• abstinence: Similar because both denote the act of refraining from an action or pleasure; differs because abstinence refers almost exclusively to sustained personal self-restraint regarding physical indulgences (such as alcohol, fasting, or sexual activity), whereas abstention centers on formal political non-voting or specific legal acts.
  • non-voting: Similar because both describe the failure to cast a ballot; differs because non-voting is a broad, informal descriptive term encompassing lazy apathy and disenfranchisement, whereas abstention is a deliberate, recorded procedural decision.
  • recusal: Similar because both involve voluntarily stepping aside from a decision; differs because recusal is specifically a legal act where a judge, prosecutor, or board member steps down entirely due to a disqualifying conflict of interest or bias.
  • forbearance: Similar because both describe patient self-restraint; differs because forbearance highlights leniency, mercy, and tolerance in refraining from enforcing a legal right, punishing an offender, or demanding payment.
  • refraining: Similar as the general verbal noun for holding back; differs because refraining is an everyday, informal term lacking institutional or parliamentary status.
  • temperance: Similar because both involve moderation and self-control; differs because temperance is a classical moral virtue emphasizing balanced moderation rather than an absolute procedural refusal to vote.
  • avoidance: Similar because both involve staying away from an action; differs because avoidance implies evading, circumventing, or escaping an unpleasant situation rather than making a principled stand.
  • boycott: Similar because both can serve as political protests against a vote or institution; differs because a boycott is an organized, collective refusal to participate, buy, or cooperate, designed to inflict economic or political damage.
  • withholding: Similar because both describe holding back something due; differs because withholding often refers to physical items, taxes, information, or consent rather than formal ballots.
  • sobriety: Similar in the context of refraining from intoxicants; differs because sobriety describes the continuous psychological and physiological state of being clear-headed and free from alcohol.
  • non-participation: Similar as a generic umbrella term; differs because non-participation lacks the specific electoral and parliamentary mechanics inherent to an abstention.
