- 1550s - Scholastic & philosophical adoption: Used strictly to designate the human capacity to laugh (man as the only risible animal).
- 1620s–1750s - Classical literary expansion: Broadened to describe that which causes or excites laughter, often in theatrical contexts.
- 1800s - Victorian critique & satiric prose: Acquired a predominantly derogatory connotation, signifying preposterous pretensions, bad poetry, or political blunders.
- Present Day - Stylistic & critical usage: Retained in high-register prose, journalism, and artistic criticism to label ideas, policies, or performances that fail through absurd overreach.
