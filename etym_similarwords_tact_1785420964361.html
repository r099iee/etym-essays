• Diplomacy: Similar because both describe adroit handling of people and sensitive situations; differs because diplomacy is the strategic, often institutional art of negotiation between parties, whereas tact is a private, intuitive social grace grounded in feeling.
  • Discretion: Similar because both describe careful judgment about what to reveal; differs because discretion stresses secrecy, confidentiality, and prudent concealment of what should not be said, whereas tact concerns how to say things, not whether to hide them.
  • Finesse: Similar because both describe refined, subtle handling of a situation; differs because finesse implies skillfully executed subtlety or adroitness in performance and negotiation, whereas tact is a felt sensitivity to the other person's sensibilities.
  • Savoir-faire: Similar because both describe polished, adroit social behaviour; differs because savoir-faire is the general grace of knowing what to do in any social situation, whereas tact specifically concerns avoiding offence and managing delicate truths.
  • Discernment: Similar because both involve keen perception in dealing with people; differs because discernment is the intellectual faculty of judging well and seeing distinctions, whereas tact is the practical, feeling-based art of handling others without hurting them.
  • Judgment: Similar because both involve knowing the right thing to do; differs because judgment is the broad capacity for sound decisions, whereas tact is specifically the social grace of saying and doing the fitting thing in delicate matters.
  • Delicacy: Similar because both describe a refined, careful touch in handling matters; differs because delicacy can also mean fragility or the quality of being dainty, whereas tact is always the active skill of managing people and situations.
  • Sensitivity: Similar because both involve awareness of others' feelings; differs because sensitivity is a general receptiveness, often emotional, whereas tact is the applied, outward skill of acting on that awareness to avoid offence.
  • Adroitness: Similar because both describe skill in handling situations; differs because adroitness is general cleverness and dexterity in any task, whereas tact is specifically the social and interpersonal form of that skill.
  • Consideration: Similar because both involve thoughtfulness toward others; differs because consideration is a general attitude of respect and thoughtfulness, whereas tact is the specific performance of that attitude in delicate or difficult exchanges.
