- Late 16th c. — superior power and martial mastery: prevalence enters English denoting ascendancy, overwhelming strength, or triumph in battle and physical contest.
- 17th c. — moral and rhetorical efficacy: used in religious sermons and philosophical treatises for the prevailing power of prayer, reason, or virtue over temptation.
- 18th c. — extensive currency and commonness: shifts toward widespread occurrence, describing customs, fashion, opinions, or meteorological conditions dominating an entire region.
- 19th c. — epidemiological and statistical foundation: adoption by sanitary reformers (such as William Farr) to quantify the total number of existing disease cases in a community.
- 20th to 21st c. — clinical, sociological, and algorithmic standard: precise metric distinguishing total existing burden from new incidence, used ubiquitously in public health, sociology, and digital analytics.
