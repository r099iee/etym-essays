• Landslide: Similar because both are rapid gravitational downslope mass movements; differs because landslide is dominated by rock and soil while an avalanche is classically snow, though avalanche can cover rock and ice too.
  • Snowslide: Similar because it is a direct synonym for a sliding mass of snow; differs because it is plainer and lacks the drama, technical weight, and figurative reach of avalanche.
  • Rockslide / rockfall: Similar because both describe rock descending a slope quickly; differs because rockfall is a discrete free fall of fragments and rockslide a sliding mass, whereas avalanche implies snow with possible debris.
  • Mudslide / mudflow: Similar because both are sudden downhill flows of material; differs because the medium is saturated earth rather than snow, and they typically follow rain or thaw rather than snowpack failure.
  • Flood: Similar because both denote an overwhelming onrush, literal and figurative; differs because flood is primarily water spreading over land, while avalanche is a descending mass and lacks the cascade-collapse nuance.
  • Torrent / deluge: Similar because both describe a swift, overwhelming quantity; differs because they apply to water or abstract onslaughts and do not imply a collapsing slope.
  • Cascade: Similar because both suggest a growing, flowing descent; differs because cascade implies a staged, stepped flow, often smaller and gentler, with no catastrophic collapse.
  • Onslaught: Similar because both describe an overpowering, mounting attack or influx; differs because onslaught is exclusively figurative and aggressive, with no physical snow-slide sense.
  • Barrage: Similar because both convey a relentless outpouring ("a barrage of questions"); differs because barrage is a military bombardment or a dam, lacking any natural-slope meaning.
  • Slough: Similar because it can mean a mass that falls or a collapse of earth; differs because slough also means a muddy marsh or a moulted skin, an entirely different semantic range.
