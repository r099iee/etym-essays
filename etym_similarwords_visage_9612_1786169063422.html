• Face: Similar because both name the front of the human head; differs because 'face' is the neutral everyday word, while 'visage' is formal, literary, and implies the face as seen and read.
  • Countenance: Similar because both name the face as expressive appearance; differs because 'countenance' stresses the look of composure and approval ('to give countenance'), and leans on bearing rather than features.
  • Physiognomy: Similar because both describe the face's permanent character; differs because 'physiognomy' is the technical/pseudo-scientific reading of character from facial features, while 'visage' is the visible appearance itself.
  • Features: Similar because both concern the visible face; differs because 'features' names the individual parts (eyes, nose, mouth) and their arrangement, while 'visage' is the whole assembled look.
  • Expression: Similar because both can mean the look on the face at a moment; differs because 'expression' is the passing emotional show, while 'visage' is the whole face including its fixed structure.
  • Mug: Similar because both (informally) name the face; differs because 'mug' is coarse slang ('mugshot') with no dignity, while 'visage' is elevated to the point of archaism.
  • Kisser / Dial / Phiz / Phizog: Similar as slang synonyms for the face; differs because they are jocular and vulgar, maximally distant in register from the grave 'visage'.
  • Pan / Puss: Similar as informal words for the face; differs because 'pan' ('deadpan') and 'puss' are American slang for the whole face with comic flatness.
  • Look: Similar because both can name the appearance of the face; differs because 'look' is diffuse (a style, an expression, an appearance of anything), while 'visage' is specific to the human face.
  • Mien: Similar because both describe the outward bearing of a person; differs because 'mien' is the whole demeanour and air (not just the face), while 'visage' is the facial surface.
  • Front: Similar because both can name the outward-facing appearance; differs because 'front' is metaphorical and often deceptive ('putting up a front'), while 'visage' is physical and legible.
  • Portrait: Similar because both connect the face to representation and reading; differs because 'portrait' is the painted image, while 'visage' is the living face that a portrait copies.
  • Appearance: Similar because both concern how something looks; differs because 'appearance' is general and often superficial (appearances deceive), while 'visage' is the concrete human face.
  • Persona: Similar in the 'public face' sense (the visage a person presents); differs because 'persona' is the social mask worn, while 'visage' is the actual face observed.
  • Counterance of a place / Aspect: Similar because both can name the look of a landscape or thing; differs because 'aspect' is the neutral, general word for how anything faces, while 'visage' keeps the human face alive in the metaphor.
