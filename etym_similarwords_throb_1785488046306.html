• Pulse: Similar because both name a regular beating rhythm; differs because pulse is the neutral, quasi-technical term for any regular beat, especially of the arteries, while throb carries emotional intensity and the felt insistence of a living body.
  • Beat: Similar because both describe a rhythmic stroke; differs because beat is fully generic, covering music, clocks, drums and hearts alike with no implication of living tissue, whereas throb is the heartbeat of something alive.
  • Pulsation: Similar because both name a throbbing or beating movement; differs because pulsation is the clinical noun, cold and descriptive, while throb keeps a physical, emotional warmth.
  • Palpitation: Similar because both describe a beating sensation, especially of the heart; differs because palpitation implies a rapid, irregular, anxious hammering - the flutter of fear or heart-disease - while a throb is steady and reassuring.
  • Thump: Similar because both describe a heavy, dull repeated impact; differs because thump is heavier and more percussive, a solid blow, while throb is the regular beat of a living pulse.
  • Thud: Similar because both describe a dull impact sound; differs because thud names a single deadened fall or blow with no rhythm, while throb is a continuing, regular pulsation.
  • Thrum: Similar because both describe a pulsing vibration; differs because thrum suggests a soft, continuous droning rather than a discrete beat, whereas throb is a strong, regular, insistent pulsation.
  • Vibration: Similar because both describe a quivering or shaking movement; differs because vibration is mechanical and lifeless, the hum of a machine, while throb implies a living body and a felt rhythm.
  • Pounding: Similar because both describe a violent, repeated beating; differs because pounding is heavier, more violent and more percussive, while throb is steady, deep and reassuring.
  • Drumming: Similar because both describe a rhythmic beating; differs because drumming is specifically percussive, suggesting an instrument being struck, while throb suggests the beat of living tissue or of vitality itself.
  • Pang: Similar because both describe a sudden, felt bodily sensation; differs because pang is a sharp, momentary stab (of pain or longing), while a throb is a dull, continuing, rhythmic pulse.
  • Ache: Similar because both can describe a continuing bodily pain; differs because ache is a general, steady discomfort without rhythm, while a throb is specifically rhythmic, beating in time with the pulse.
