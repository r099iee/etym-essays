• Staff: Similar because both mean to supply with personnel; differs because 'staff' is neutral about gender and modern and managerial, while 'man' is traditional and implies operating or defending a specific post.
  • Crew: Similar because both mean to supply with a crew; differs because 'crew' is nautical or aviation-specific, while 'man' covers any post or position.
  • Garrison: Similar because both mean to station troops to defend; differs because 'garrison' is strictly military and implies permanent occupation of a fort.
  • Occupy: Similar because both mean to take position in; differs because 'occupy' lacks the 'supply with people' sense and is much broader.
  • Operate: Similar because both mean to work or run a machine or position; differs because 'operate' focuses on the function, while 'man' focuses on the personnel who perform it.
  • Person (noun): Similar because both denote a human being; differs because 'person' is gender-neutral and formal, while 'man' historically covered it but is now male-marked.
  • Human (noun): Similar because both denote a member of Homo sapiens; differs because 'human' emphasises species, while 'man' is the everyday male term or the generic-elevated term.
  • Male (noun): Similar because both denote an adult male; differs because 'male' is clinical and biological, while 'man' is social and everyday.
  • Guy / Fellow / Bloke: Similar because all are informal words for an adult male; differs because they are colloquial and lack the formal, generic, and verb uses of 'man'.
  • Husband: Similar because 'man' has an archaic sense of husband; differs because 'husband' is specific and legal.
  • Humankind / Humanity: Similar because both cover the generic collective sense of 'man'; differs because these are the modern gender-neutral replacements preferred in careful usage.
