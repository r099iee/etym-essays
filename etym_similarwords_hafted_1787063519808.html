• Handled: Similar because both mean "having a handle"; differs because handled is the ordinary generic word for any handle (bags, cups, tools) and also means "dealt with" in figurative use, while hafted is specific to bladed and pointed implements and is more technical or literary.
  • Helved: Similar because both mean "fitted with a handle"; differs because helved (from the noun helve) is used specifically of axe-handles and is even rarer and more archaic, whereas hafted covers knives, axes, spears, and daggers alike.
  • Hilted: Similar because both mean "fitted with a gripping part"; differs because hilted applies to swords and daggers and describes the guard-and-grip assembly, while hafted generally refers to the plain shaft or handle of a tool or weapon.
  • Shafted: Similar because both can describe a weapon fitted with a long wooden handle (spears, arrows, pikes); differs because shafted names the long shaft rather than the hand-grip and is also a coarse British slang insult, whereas hafted is neutral and technical.
  • Mounted: Similar because both describe a blade fixed to a support; differs because mounted is far broader (walls, horses, exhibitions) and does not specifically mean "given a handle", while hafted names the handle joint exactly.
  • Fitted / equipped: Similar because both mean "provided with a needed part"; differs because they are generic terms usable of any device or person, whereas hafted is the precise, narrow technical term for fitting a handle to a bladed implement.
  • Gripped / grasped: Similar because both are connected with the hand-holding part of a tool; differs because these describe the action of holding rather than the mounted handle, and neither can mean "an implement provided with a haft".
  • Armed: Similar because both can describe a weapon prepared for use; differs because armed means "given weapons" (or, of a device, "made operational") and says nothing about a handle, whereas hafted is specifically about the handle of one blade.
