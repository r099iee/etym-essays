• Hurries: Similar because both mean to move or act quickly; differs because hurry is the ordinary, neutral word, while hastens is formal and literary and also carries the transitive sense “to cause sooner”.
  • Rushes: Similar because both imply speed; differs because rushing implies reckless, headlong speed and often lateness, while hastening is purposeful urgency.
  • Speeds (up): Similar because both mean to increase rate; differs because speed up is mechanical and neutral about purpose, while hasten implies urgency and often a desired early completion.
  • Scurries: Similar because both are quick movement; differs because scurrying suggests small, scampering, slightly fussy movement (mice, clerks), while hastening is purposeful and dignified.
  • Quickens: Similar because both mean to make faster; differs because quicken often means to accelerate an existing process or a pulse, while hasten can mean to go fast oneself or to bring something about sooner.
  • Dashes / Bolts: Similar because both are fast movement; differs because dash and bolt are sudden, violent, single bursts, while hasten is sustained and composed urgency.
  • Accelerates: Similar because both mean to increase speed; differs because accelerate is technical, neutral, and usually said of processes and vehicles, while hasten is personal and said of people and events.
  • Expedites: Similar because both mean to make happen sooner; differs because expedite is bureaucratic or formal language for processing things faster, while hasten is general-purpose urgency.
  • Precipitates: Similar because both mean to cause to happen sooner; differs because precipitate is technical (chemists precipitate reactions, events precipitate crises) and often implies causing abruptly, while hasten is milder and can be of one’s own motion.
  • Makes haste: Similar because both are idiomatic expressions of urgency; differs because “making haste” is a fixed phrase meaning simply hurrying, while hasten covers both hurrying oneself and causing something sooner.
