• eccentricities: Similar because both refer to unconventional habits, unusual personality traits, or deviations from mainstream social norms; differs because eccentricities is almost exclusively applied to human behavior, psychological temperaments, and lifestyle choices of individuals (often with a connotation of harmless, aristocratic, or intellectual nonconformity), whereas oddities applies with equal ease to concrete physical objects, rare biological specimens, and bizarre external events.
  • idiosyncrasies: Similar because both describe distinctive quirks or peculiarities; differs because idiosyncrasies emphasizes the deeply ingrained, structural uniqueness of an individual's psychological or physiological constitution (how their specific brain or body uniquely reacts), whereas oddities highlights how strange, laughable, or out-of-place the trait appears to external observers.
  • peculiarities: Similar because both denote features that distinguish something from the general norm; differs because peculiarities can refer neutrally to defining, distinctive characteristics of an entire dialect, species, or legal system without implying strangeness, whereas oddities always denotes an overt aura of the bizarre, incongruous, or curious.
  • anomalies: Similar because both describe things that do not conform to expected patterns; differs because anomalies is a more formal, clinical, or scientific term denoting statistical outliers, empirical irregularities, or deviations from physical laws, whereas oddities carries a lighter, folksy, or curio-cabinet connotation of entertainment and wonder.
  • quirks: Similar because both describe minor, surprising behavioral peculiarities; differs because quirks suggests endearing, sudden, or trivial personal twists in habit, speech, or mechanical operation, whereas oddities can describe profound, eerie, or grotesque phenomena.
  • curiosities: Similar because both describe rare, fascinating, or unusual items worthy of inspection; differs because curiosities emphasizes the intellectual interest, aesthetic fascination, and investigative impulse aroused in the observer, whereas oddities highlights the inherent weirdness or mismatch of the object itself.
  • rarities: Similar because both describe things seldom encountered in everyday life; differs because rarities emphasizes scarcity, preciousness, and collector value, whereas an oddity may be common yet bizarre, possessing no inherent financial value.
  • singularities: Similar because both denote instances of extraordinary uniqueness; differs because singularities in philosophical, physical, or elevated literary registers implies an unprecedented, incomparable pinnacle or mathematical point of infinite density, whereas oddities is accessible, plural, and comfortably grounded in human eccentricity and odd bric-a-brac.
