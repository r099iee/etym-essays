Arles (proper noun) — A city in Provence, southern France, on the left bank of the Rhône where the river begins to fan into its delta, about thirty kilometres from the Mediterranean. In antiquity it was Arelate, one of the leading cities of the Roman province of Gallia Narbonensis and — chosen by the Emperor Constantine as an occasional imperial residence — sometimes styled "the little Rome of Gaul"; in the Middle Ages it gave its name to the Kingdom of Arles (Burgundy); and in modern times it is world-famous as the Provençal city where Vincent van Gogh painted some of his most celebrated pictures.

In the source context. Gibbon lists Arles among the seats of "German princes" — Paris, Toledo, Arles, and Ravenna — who reigned in Romanized, Christianized lands and listened reverently to bishops. Arles had indeed been a great see: its Council of 314 was one of the earliest church councils in the West, attended at Constantine's summons by bishops including three from Britain, and the city's bishopric became a metropolitan see of southern Gaul. Gibbon's Arles stands for the fusion of Germanic kingship, Roman law, and Nicene Christianity that the heathen kingdoms of Britain had not yet achieved.

History. The site was settled in antiquity at the point where the Via Domitia crossed the Rhône. Under Julius Caesar and Augustus it was colonized with Roman veterans (c. 46 BC, veterans of the Sixth Legion), became a major port and the head of a canal to the sea, and under Constantine (c. 306–337) served as an imperial residence. In the 5th century it was the seat of the Praetorian Prefecture of the Gauls and the capital of the Visigothic kings before passing to the Franks. From 933 to the 14th century it was the symbolic capital of the Kingdom of Arles (the Kingdom of Burgundy) within the Holy Roman Empire. Its Roman remains — the amphitheatre (Les Arènes), the theatre, the Alyscamps necropolis, the baths of Constantine — survive as a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 1981).

Polysemy and later senses.
  • The city itself: primary sense — a living Provençal city and UNESCO site, famed for its Roman monuments, its bull-running festivals (the Camargue tradition), and the Fêtes d'Arles.
  • The Kingdom of Arles: in medieval history, the name of the Holy Roman Kingdom of Burgundy-Provence (10th–14th centuries), so the toponym is metonymic for a medieval polity.
  • Van Gogh's Arles: in art history, "Arles" names the 1888–89 period and place of Van Gogh's Sunflowers, The Night Café, and Starry Night Over the Rhône — a metonym for late Impressionist genius and for the artist's most luminous years.
  • The Council of Arles: in church history, the councils held at Arles in 314 and 353, and by extension the city's long role as a metropolitan see.
  • Arlésienne / Arlesian: the derived adjective and noun for the city and its people, as in Bizet's L'Arlésienne, and in local usage the inhabitants (Arlésiens).

Semantic bridge. The name descends from the Gaulish compound *Arelate, "place before the marsh" (are- "near, in front of" + late- "marsh"), referring to the marshy land of the Rhône delta where the city rose; two thousand years of Roman, Frankish, and Provençal history then made the mere toponym synonymous with Roman Gaul and, much later, with Van Gogh.
