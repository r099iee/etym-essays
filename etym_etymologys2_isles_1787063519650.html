Note: The origin of Latin insula is debated. The traditional etymology reads it as in salo, "that which is in the sea" (from salum "the sea"); a rival view connects it with solum "ground, soil", i.e. "land standing in the sea". The PIE root *seh₂l- "salt" is secure for sal, but the bridge to insula rests on the debated in salo analysis. Note also that the silent -s- of modern isle/isles was restored in English spelling in the 15th-16th centuries under Latin influence (Old French had dropped it); island is a separate native compound (Old English īegland) later respelled to mimic isle.
