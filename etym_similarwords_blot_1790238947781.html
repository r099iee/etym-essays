• Blemish: Similar because both denote a mark or flaw that spoils perfection; differs because blemish often refers to minor physical skin marks or small aesthetic imperfections, whereas blot conveys a darker, heavier stain or severe moral disgrace.
  • Stain: Similar because both describe discoloration or moral dishonor; differs because stain is a broader, general physical process of discoloration deeply absorbed into fibers, whereas blot specifically suggests an ink-drop, discrete spot, or conspicuous eyesore.
  • Smirch: Similar because both describe dirtying a surface or reputation; differs because smirch emphasizes smoky soot, grime, or subtle tarnishing rather than a distinct liquid mark.
  • Taint: Similar because both involve corrupting purity; differs because taint suggests insidious internal contamination, decay, or infection spreading through something, whereas blot is a visible external mark or blemish.
  • Eyesore: Similar because both describe something visually offensive on a scene or landscape; differs because eyesore is strictly visual and architectural/environmental, lacking the scribal, absorbent, and moral dimensions of blot.
  • Slur: Similar because both relate to damage to one's reputation; differs because slur is an active spoken or written insult/insinuation aimed at disparaging someone, whereas a blot is an existing stain or fact of dishonor.
  • Stigma: Similar because both represent a mark of social disgrace; differs because stigma denotes a societal brand or prejudice attached to a condition, identity, or group by community disapproval.
  • Defect: Similar because both imply imperfection; differs because defect refers to an internal mechanical, structural, or functional deficiency rather than an external stain or eyesore.
