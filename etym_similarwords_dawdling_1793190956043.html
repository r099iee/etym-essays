• Loitering: Similar because both hang about slowly instead of proceeding; differs because loitering stresses suspicious hanging around with no destination, while dawdling stresses slowness toward a real destination.
  • Lingering: Similar because both delay and remain; differs because lingering is often tender or reluctant parting, while dawdling is idle drift scolded by the punctual.
  • Idling: Similar because both waste time doing nothing useful; differs because idling is stationary engine-running emptiness, while dawdling moves, only too slowly.
  • Dallying: Similar because both trifle and delay; differs because dallying adds flirtation or playful toying, while dawdling is plain slowness without the romance.
  • Loafing: Similar because both avoid work; differs because loafing is deliberate shirking with hands in pockets, while dawdling may be mere distraction and drift.
  • Trifling: Similar because both fuss over unimportant things; differs because trifling stresses the worthlessness of the object, while dawdling stresses the slowness of the pace.
  • Pottering: Similar because both amble among small tasks; differs because pottering is contented purposeful puttering praised in retirees, while dawdling is the same behavior scolded in the late.
  • Ambling: Similar because both move at an easy unhurried gait; differs because ambling is the pleasant gait itself, while dawdling judges that gait against a clock.
  • Procrastinating: Similar because both put off what should be done; differs because procrastinating is the formal psychology of avoidance, while dawdling is its small visible symptom on the road.
