• Lietuva: The Lithuanian endonym for the country; identical referent, but native and untranslated, and used in official and cultural contexts.
  • Lithuanian language: The explicit phrase for the language; same as the primary sense, but disambiguates from the nationality.
  • Latvian: The other living East Baltic language; similar structure and history, but differs in accent, vocabulary and national tradition.
  • Old Prussian: The extinct West Baltic language; similar as a Baltic member, but attested only in fragments and dead since c. 1700.
  • Samogitian: The western Lithuanian dialect; similar and often mutually intelligible, but distinct enough to be treated as a separate variety.
  • Litvak: A Lithuanian Jew; similar territorial reference, but an ethnoreligious term rather than a national one.
  • Belarusian: The neighbour whose territory was largely part of the Grand Duchy; similar historical overlap, but a different Slavic language and identity.
  • Baltic: The wider family and regional adjective; similar as a classifier, but broader than Lithuanian alone.
  • Polish-Lithuanian: The hyphenated adjective for the Commonwealth; similar historical reference, but denotes the union rather than the nation.
  • Lettish: The older English name for Latvian, sometimes confused with Lithuanian in older sources; similar Baltic label, but refers to the sister nation.
