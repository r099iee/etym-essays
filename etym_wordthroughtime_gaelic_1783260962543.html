- 16th-17th-century entry - Gaelic, formed from the ethnic name Gael with -ic, entered English as the name for the Celtic speech of the Irish and of the Scottish Highlands, and for two centuries served loosely as a synonym for the Celtic tongue of Britain as a whole.
- 18th-19th-century romanticization - the word became attached to the Highland revival and the Ossianic vogue, acquiring a wistful, folkloric aura of ancientness, and was pressed into service as an umbrella term standing for all surviving Celtic speech, precise or not.
- Contemporary sense - the precise linguistic label for the Goidelic branch (Irish, Scottish Gaelic, Manx), set against Brythonic, and now a term freighted with cultural revival and endangered-language advocacy, while still being used loosely for any Celtic tongue in popular writing.
