Note: Formed as a compound of Latin prefix prae- ("before, in front", from PIE *per-) and capere ("to take, seize, grasp", from PIE *kap-), with regular Latin vowel weakening a → i in compound verbs (praecipere). The neuter past participle noun praeceptum literally denoted that which was "taken or grasped beforehand"—an instruction or rule taken into the mind in advance of action, hence a guiding maxim, moral injunction, or legal mandate.
