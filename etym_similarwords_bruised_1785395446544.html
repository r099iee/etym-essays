• Cut: Similar because both describe bodily harm from a blow; differs because a cut is an open wound that breaks the skin and bleeds, whereas a bruise leaves the surface whole and only leaks blood beneath it.
  • Wounded: Similar because both name physical or emotional hurt; differs because wounded suggests a more serious, usually open injury and, figuratively, deep lasting trauma, while bruised is lighter — hurt pride and shaken spirits, not a broken spirit.
  • Battered: Similar because both describe flesh knocked about; differs because battered implies repeated heavy blows over time and general rough handling, rather than the single discrete mark of a bruise.
  • Contused: Similar because contused is the clinical word for the same injury; differs because it is reserved for medical reports, while bruised remains the everyday household term.
  • Injured: Similar because both describe harm to a person; differs because injured is far broader — every kind of harm — with no specific image, while bruised names a particular, familiar injury to the flesh.
  • Damaged: Similar because both describe harm done; differs because damaged applies to anything at all, from a car to a reputation, carrying no image of the body, while bruised is bodily and specific.
  • Sore: Similar because both describe the aftermath of a knock; differs because sore names the feeling of pain, not the mark, and can be caused by overuse or stiffness with no bruise at all.
  • Black and blue: Similar because it is the vivid colloquial phrase for a bruise; differs because it is an idiom and adjective phrase, more informal, and describes the discolouration rather than the injury as a whole.
  • Crushed: Similar because both derive from the old sense of pounding and can be figurative; differs because crushed is far stronger — it can mean completely broken (crushed hopes, crushed bones), while bruised is partial and recoverable.
  • Knocked / banged: Similar because both describe the impact that causes a bruise; differs because they name the blow, not the injury, and are informal and often mild.
