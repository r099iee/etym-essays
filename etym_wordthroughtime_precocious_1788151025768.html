- "Botany (1630s) - 'early-ripening': precocious corn and fruit, a neutral term of husbandry"
- "Transfer to humans (1650s-1700) - from fruit to minds and bodies: 'precocious' parts, talents, and children enter the record; admiration meets anxiety"
- "Age of education (18th c.) - the child prodigy becomes a public wonder; Rousseau and the educators warn that forcing ripeness corrupts the natural order"
- "The century of child-study (19th-early 20th c.) - psychology and paediatrics make precocity a measurable, sometimes pathological, category; the gifted-child movement argues over it"
- "Today - the word splits into two registers: admiring ('precocious talent') and medical ('precocious puberty'); zoology quietly owns the cognate 'precocial' for animals born ready"
