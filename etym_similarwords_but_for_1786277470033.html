• Were it not for: Similar because both establish a counterfactual condition whose absence allows a different outcome; differs because were it not for utilizes explicit subjunctive clause syntax, giving it a more formal and elevated rhetorical tone.
  • Except for: Similar because both identify an exclusion or saving condition; differs because except for primarily marks a static subtraction or exemption from a general statement, whereas but for typically introduces a dynamic counterfactual cause preventing a catastrophe.
  • Save for: Similar because both signal an exception; differs because save for belongs to an archaic or literary register focusing on reservation or exclusion rather than formal legal causation.
  • Without: Similar because both indicate the absence or lack of a specific factor; differs because without is a basic single-word preposition with broad prepositional usages (e.g. accompaniment, state), lacking the dedicated counterfactual conditional structure of but for.
  • Barring: Similar because both introduce a condition that prevents or alters an event; differs because barring looks forward to potential future obstacles ("barring unforeseen delays"), whereas but for typically reflects retroactively upon a realized counterfactual condition.
  • In the absence of: Similar because both specify the non-existence of a necessary factor; differs because in the absence of is an administrative, technical phrasal preposition that lacks the immediate dramatic or counterfactual force of but for.
