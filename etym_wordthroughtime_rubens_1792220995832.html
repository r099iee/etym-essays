- Biblical Antiquity–16th c. — Originates as a patronymic surname in the Low Countries (Rubens / Roobens), meaning "son of Ruben" (from the Hebrew patriarchal name *Re’uven*).
- 1608–1640 — Peter Paul Rubens rises to international fame as the court painter to Archduke Albert and Isabella in Antwerp; knighted by the kings of England and Spain, his name becomes universally recognized across European courts as the pinnacle of painterly genius.
- Late 17th–18th c. — Institutionalized in French art theory through the *Rubénistes*, who championed his chromatic vitality against academic classicism; English collectors amass his oil sketches and masterworks.
- 19th c. — Revered by the Romantic generation (Delacroix) and the Impressionists (Renoir) as the undisputed god of color and physical movement; critics begin using his name as a synonym for generous, vital feminine beauty.
- 20th–21st c. — The adjective Rubenesque enters general English dictionaries to designate full-figured, voluptuous beauty; Rubens is universally enshrined as the defining colossus of the European Baroque.
