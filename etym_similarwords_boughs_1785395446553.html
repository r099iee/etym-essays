• Branches: Similar in naming a limb of a tree; differs because branch is the neutral, inclusive everyday word for any limb of any size, whereas bough insists on a large, principal limb growing directly from the trunk.
  • Limbs: Similar in naming a large tree branch; differs because limb is chiefly American and colloquial, the word of the climbing child rather than of the poet, and lacks bough's archaic elevation.
  • Twigs: Similar in being part of a tree's woody growth; differs because twigs are small terminal shoots — the opposite end of the scale from the great bough that bears them.
  • Sprays: Similar in being growth from a tree; differs because sprays are light flowering or leafy shoots, not the thick principal branches that bough denotes.
  • Stems: Similar in being structural woody parts; differs because stem is the technical botanical term for the main axis of a plant, while bough is the literary word for a tree's great lateral limb.
  • Shoots: Similar in being new growth of a tree; differs because shoots are young, slender, newly sprouted growth — the very opposite of the massive, old bough.
  • Canopy: Similar in evoking the leafy upper layer of a forest; differs because canopy is the collective mass of foliage and crowns, not individual branches, and is a technical term of ecology.
