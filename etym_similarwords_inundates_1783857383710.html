• Floods: Similar because both mean to cover with water or to overwhelm with volume; differs because flood is the plain, native, everyday verb (and noun), while inundate is formal, Latinate, and always transitive — one floods a field but is inundated with work.
  • Swamps: Similar because both mean to overwhelm with quantity; differs because swamp implies bogging down, sinking into heavy mud or water (the victim is stuck and struggling), while inundate implies a rising tide that covers everything — the image is submersion rather than sinking.
  • Deluges: Similar because both mean to overwhelm with water; differs because deluge stresses a sudden, catastrophic downpour or biblical-scale flood, while inundate can describe a steady, rising overflow as well as a sudden one.
  • Overwhelms: Similar because both mean to defeat or crush by superior force or volume; differs because overwhelm is broader and can be emotional or physical ("overwhelmed with grief"), while inundate keeps the water metaphor explicit and is restricted to quantity, volume, or literal flooding.
  • Submerges: Similar because both mean to put or come under water; differs because submerge is the neutral, technical verb for going beneath the surface (a submarine submerges), while inundate is the flood-event word for water covering land.
  • Engulfs: Similar because both mean to completely cover or swallow up; differs because engulf stresses being swallowed and consumed (flames engulf a building, the sea engulfs a ship), while inundate stresses overflow and quantity rather than consumption.
  • Drowns: Similar because both mean to be overwhelmed by water; differs because drown is used of living things dying under water, or figuratively of being overwhelmed ("drowned in paperwork"), with a fatal, violent edge, while inundate describes the act of flooding and is less visceral.
  • Saturates: Similar because both mean to fill or soak to capacity; differs because saturate means to make completely wet or to fill up to the point of no further absorption, while inundate implies water in such excess that it covers rather than merely soaks.
  • Submerges / subsumes: Similar because both involve being covered or absorbed by a larger mass; differs because subsume is abstract and logical (a category absorbed into a larger one), while inundate remains concrete and watery.
  • Bombards: Similar because both mean to assault with a large quantity; differs because bombard implies repeated, forceful, deliberate attacks ("bombarded with questions"), while inundate implies a passive, overwhelming overflow that needs no attacker.
  • Saturates / floods the market: Similar because both describe an excess of supply; differs because "floods the market" is idiomatic economics for oversupply of goods, while inundate is general and applies to any overload.
