- Late 14th century - a play in a cycle of mystery plays, or the wheeled stage on which it was performed; the word belongs to the craft guilds and religious drama.
- 15th century - a scene in a royal welcome or Roman triumph; also a story, a tale, or an ornamental hanging. The sense of constructed display widens.
- 16th–17th centuries - royal entries, Lord Mayor’s Shows, masques, and civic tableaux keep the word alive after the medieval cycles decline; pageantry appears in the 1650s.
- Early 19th century - the generalized sense of a showy parade or spectacular procession is recorded (1805); historical pageants become popular civic entertainment.
- 20th century - beauty pageants, Christmas pageants, and school pageants make the word a familiar part of popular culture; the pejorative “mere pageant” also flourishes.
- 21st century - the word covers community history plays, religious dramatisations, beauty competitions, and ironic descriptions of political or media spectacle.
