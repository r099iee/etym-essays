• Roosted: Similar because both describe a bird settling on a resting place; differs because roosted means settled for the night on a habitual resting-place, while perched is any settling on a high, narrow support, day or night.
  • Alighted: Similar because both describe a bird landing on a support; differs because alighted names the moment of landing, while perched names the settled state after the landing - one is arrival, the other repose.
  • Settled: Similar because both describe a creature coming to rest; differs because settled is general and can mean any resting anywhere, while perched insists on a raised, narrow support.
  • Sat: Similar because both describe a resting posture; differs because sat is the neutral, all-purpose word for resting on the haunches and carries no elevation, while perched adds height and a slightly precarious narrowness.
  • Poised: Similar because both describe a body held in a balanced position; differs because poised suggests readiness to move or act, a suspended tension, whereas perched suggests settled repose with no impending action.
  • Balanced: Similar because both imply a position of equilibrium on a narrow support; differs because balanced emphasises the physics of staying up, while perched treats the resting as natural and unremarkable.
  • Squatted: Similar because both describe a compact resting position; differs because squatting means crouching close to the ground, while perching is high up and often on a narrow support.
  • Hovered: Similar because both are bird-words describing a position in the air; differs because hovering is staying in one place by flight, with no contact, while perched means settled with the feet on a support.
  • Lodged: Similar because both describe a thing fixed or resting somewhere; differs because lodged implies wedged or stuck, often caught awkwardly, while perched implies deliberate, graceful placement at a height.
  • Positioned: Similar because both describe deliberate placement; differs because positioned is colourless and managerial, while perched carries elevation, picturesqueness, and a hint of balance.
  • Situated: Similar because both locate a thing in a place; differs because situated is dry and geographical, while perched adds height and visual interest - a town is situated in a valley, but perched on a cliff.
  • Stood: Similar because both describe a thing occupying a raised position; differs because stood is general vertical presence, while perched specifies a narrow, high support with an air of balance.
