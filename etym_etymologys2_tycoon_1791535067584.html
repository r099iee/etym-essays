Middle Chinese (c. 6th–7th c.)
大君 (dà-jūn)
"great ruler; high sovereign prince"
↓
Early Modern Japanese (17th c.)
大君 (taikun)
"diplomatic title of the Tokugawa Shogun"
↓
American English (1857)
tycoon
"title applied to the Shogun of Japan"
↓
American English (1861–1865)
tycoon
"nickname for President Abraham Lincoln"
↓
Modern English (late 19th c.–pres.)
tycoon
"wealthy, powerful business magnate"

Note: Derived from Sino-Japanese 大君 (taikun, compounding 大 "great" + 君 "lord/ruler"), an ancient Chinese classical title revived in Edo-period Japan as a diplomatic designation for the Shogun to communicate with foreign states without infringing upon the Kyoto Emperor. Transferred into American English after Commodore Perry's expedition, popularized as a nickname for Lincoln, and eventually applied to Gilded Age industrial barons.
