The name Structuralists is built like its doctrine: modest Latin bricks stacked into a Parisian tower. At the base lies struere, to heap up, build, arrange, the verb of masons and generals, piling stones and ordering ranks. Its noun structura means a fitting-together, whether of walls or arguments. French borrows the family for architecture and anatomy; English adds the school-suffix -ist on the model of realist and socialist; the plural capitalises into a generation. No sound-law drama attends it: the interest is entirely in who claimed the label and why.

From building-site to theory-brand:

  • Latin struere in work and war: walls heaped, fires laid, plots constructed; the past participle structus lives on in construct and destroy, building and unbuilding with the same hands.
  • Early modern structure: 15th-century English uses structure for buildings, then for anatomy, then for any ordered whole; by the 19th century geologists, biologists, and engineers all describe structures, and structural becomes the routine adjective of arrangement.
  • 1900s — -ist as flag: realist, formalist, modernist, and finally structuralist turn adjectives into memberships; to add -ist is to claim comrades, journals, and enemies.
  • 1928 — Propp unknowing charter: Morphology of the Folktale reduces a hundred tales to thirty-one functions, structuralism in practice a generation before the Paris name.
  • 1955–1966 — the Paris years: Lévi-Strauss Tristes Tropiques, Barthes Mythologies, Lacan seminars, Althusser readings of Marx, Foucault Les Mots et les choses; journalists shorten the coalition to les structuralistes, and English imports the plural wholesale with capital S.
  • 1971 — the Maranda moment cited in the source: Elli and Pierre Maranda publish structural analysis of myth at the height of confidence, exactly the achievement orality theory both admires and reproaches for ignoring the written contrast.

Sound and spelling journey: Latin u in struere passes through French into English u pronounced /ʌ/ under English stress habits; the consonant cluster str- survives intact across three thousand years, one of the stablest onsets in the family; -al weakens to /ərəl/ and -ist adds /ɪst/ with plural -s, giving /ˈstrʌktʃərəlɪsts/ with the characteristically English affrication of t before u. Capitalisation carries meaning: lower-case structuralists can be any system-minded analysts, capital Structuralists the historical movement, and the prompt preserves the capital.

Afterlife and the source-sentence judgement: post-structuralists kept the nouns while denying the nouns referent, so textbooks now file Structuralists as a chapter rather than a creed. Folkloristics remembers them gratefully: motif indexes gained grammars, variants gained transformations, tellers gained logic. Orality theory, voiced in the source sentence, adds the friendly amendment: splendid on relations within tradition, quiet on relations between media. That amendment is itself structural, one more opposition, mouth versus page, and the school it gently chides would have appreciated the symmetry.
