- Late Middle English (1300s-1400s) - personage borrowed via Anglo-Norman French; used concurrently for an ecclesiastical benefice (parsonage), bodily stature/mien, and a person of noble rank or consequence; high legal and clerical register.
- Early Modern English (1500s-1600s) - Dramatic and literary expansion; Shakespeare, Spenser, and Jonson use personages for characters inhabiting plays/masques and for aristocratic grandees; bodily appearance sense flourishes.
- Eighteenth Century (1700s) - Enlightenment travelogues, historiography, and satire; Sterne and Gibbon employ personages for historical giants and pompous provincial dignitaries encountered on the Grand Tour; ironic register emerges.
- Victorian & Nineteenth Century (1800s) - High Victorian formal prose; personages becomes standard for royal families, diplomatic figures, and leading statesmen in official gazettes and Victorian novels.
- Contemporary & Modern English (1900s-present) - Specialized literary, historical, and ironic usage; retains an aura of classical grandeur or dry theatrical irony when contrasted with modern media celebrities.
