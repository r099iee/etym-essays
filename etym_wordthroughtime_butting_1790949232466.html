- 12th to 13th Century - Middle English borrows butten from Anglo-Norman / Old French boter, meaning to thrust, strike with the head or horns, or push forward.
- 14th to 16th Century - Expands into technical trades (carpentry, architecture) to describe placing timbers end-to-end (butt joints) and abutting land boundaries.
- Late 19th Century - American colloquial slang coins the phrasal verb butt in, transferring the physical headbutt into social and conversational intrusion.
- 20th Century - The idiom butting heads becomes a universal metaphor in business, politics, and psychology for stubborn intellectual deadlock.
- 21st Century - Ubiquitous in conversational English worldwide across physical, metaphorical, and technical registers.
