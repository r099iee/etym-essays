- 1820s–1880s: Utilitarian and maritime origins - loose-fitting trousers worn by sailors, dockworkers, and laborers to allow free movement, viewed socially as coarse working-class or nautical attire.
- 1890s–1920s: Sporting and country leisure - adapted by gentlemen for tennis, cricket, and golf; gradually acquired associations with affluent country-house relaxation and summer sporting ease.
- 1930s–1940s: Fashion revolution and female emancipation - popularized by glamorous Hollywood screen icons and adopted by millions of women performing wartime factory work, sparking widespread public debate over women wearing trousers.
- 1950s–1970s: Suburban middle-class standard - fully normalized across Western society as comfortable everyday leisurewear for both men and women, becoming the archetype of mid-century suburban casual living.
- 1980s–present: Business casual staple - solidly entrenched in modern corporate and smart-casual dress codes as an elegant alternative to denim jeans and a relaxed alternative to formal suit trousers.
