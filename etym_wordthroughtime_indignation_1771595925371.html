- [14th-16th century: righteous anger at unworthiness] - 'Indignation' enters English naming anger at what is beneath the standard - the divine and moral anger at unworthiness; connotation is biblical and solemn, the wrath of God and of justice.
- [17th-19th century: the principled protest] - The word secularises into the moral anger of reformers and controversialists at injustice, fraud, and falsehood; connotation is righteous and elevated - the controlled fury of the public-spirited defender of truth.
- [Contemporary sense] - Anger provoked by perceived unfairness or unworthiness; connotation remains moral but is now double-edged - genuinely principled outrage in protest, and faintly comic in the stock phrase 'righteous indignation' when the outrage outruns the cause.
