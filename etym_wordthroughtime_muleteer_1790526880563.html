- Late 1500s Elizabethan entry - borrowed from French 'muletier' into early English; descriptive of continental European and Mediterranean pack-animal drivers
- 1600s-1700s Golden Age of Picaresque literature - Cervantes' 'Don Quixote' and Laurence Sterne's 'Tristram Shandy'; iconic stock character of the open road, rustic inns, and stubborn linear momentum
- 1800s Peninsular War and American frontier expansion - vital military logistics and western pack-train commerce; rough, hardy pioneer transport specialist
- 1914-1918 World War I - Zion Mule Corps and mountain artillery pack units in Gallipoli and Salonika; formalized military transport role
- Mid-1900s to present - largely superseded by motorized trucking and aviation; preserved as a colorful historical, literary, and metaphorical term
