- Mid-16th century sore and stain — blotch as boil, pimple, and ink mark; perceived as bodily and scribal fault, low but everyday.
- 17th century horticultural and medical fixing — blotched leaves and blotched skins enter herbals and physick; perception is diagnostic, the patch as symptom.
- 18th–19th century aesthetic disapproval — blotched complexions, blotched prints, blotched reputations; the word carries moral-cosmetic censure of unevenness in an age of polish.
- Early 20th century natural-history neutrality — blotched plumage, blotched eggs, blotched skin become precise camouflage and post-mortem descriptors; repulsion softens into observation.
- Contemporary clinical-descriptive balance — dermatology keeps blotchy erythema while prose keeps pink-and-grey pathos; blotched remains plain, tactile, faintly unpleasant, prized for refusing to prettify decay.
