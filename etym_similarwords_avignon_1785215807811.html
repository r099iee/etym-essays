• Arles: Similar because it is a historic Roman-founded city of southern Gaul on the Rhône, grouped with Avignon in histories of Provence; differs because Arles (Latin Arelate) takes its name from a Celtic word meaning "by the marsh" or "by the fortified place," and is remembered for its Roman amphitheatre, while Avignon is fixed in the English mind by its papal court, its bridge-song, and its Mill association.
  • Nîmes: Similar because it is an ancient southern French city of Roman foundation; differs because Nîmes (Nemausus) is named for a Celtic spring-god or healing deity and is known for its Roman temple and arena, with no papal or biographical fame.
  • Aix-en-Provence: Similar because it is a historic Provençal city near the Rhône; differs because Aix (Aquae Sextiae, "the waters of Sextius") is explicitly Latin in origin, named for a thermal spring, while Avignon's claim on memory is the papal court, the bridge, and Mill's grief.
  • Marseille: Similar because it is the great port city of southern France on the Mediterranean; differs because Marseille (Greek Massalia) was founded by Greek colonists from Phocaea and has a maritime, commercial identity, while Avignon is an inland Rhône city defined by popes and pilgrimage.
  • Vaucluse (region): Similar because Avignon is its principal city and part of the same Provençal landscape; differs because Vaucluse is the departmental name (from the valley of the Sorgue), while Avignon is a specific city with its own papal history.
  • Rome: Similar because Avignon was, during the papal exile, the rival and substitute seat of the papacy, the "second Rome of the West"; differs because Rome remained the eternal home of the papacy and the centre of Catholic Christendom, while Avignon was a temporary (and, to many, illegitimate) residence.
  • Pont du Gard / Saint-Rémy (Provençal monuments): Similar because they belong to the same Rhône-Provençal itinerary of Roman and medieval monuments; differs because they are single monuments rather than a living city with an unbroken papal, commercial, and literary biography.
