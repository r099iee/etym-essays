PIE (reconstructed)
*gʷelH- / *gʷel-
"to pierce, reach by throwing; pain, death"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*kwaljaną
"to torment, inflict pain, kill"
↓
Proto-Germanic (intransitive grade)
*kwelaną
"to suffer, perish, die"
↓
Middle Dutch (c. 13th c.)
quelen
"to suffer, languish, waste away"
↓
Middle English (c. 1400)
quaylen
"to die, perish; waste away, fade"
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
quail
"to lose courage, faint, shrink back"
↓
Modern English (current form)
quail
"to shrink in fear, cower"
Note: The verb quail is completely unrelated etymologically to the noun quail (the game bird, Old French quaille, from imitative Medieval Latin quaccula). Middle English quaylen was also influenced by or tangled with an obsolete verb quail ('to curdle, coagulate', from Old French coailler, Latin coagulare), but the primary lineage descends from Proto-Germanic *kwelaną ('to suffer, die', source of Old English cwelan 'to die' and modern English quell).
