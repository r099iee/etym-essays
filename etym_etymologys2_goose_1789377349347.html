PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*ǵʰh₂éns
"goose, gander, waterfowl"
Note: One of the oldest reconstructible animal names in Indo-European, cognate with Latin anser, Greek khēn, Sanskrit haṃsá.
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE)
*gans (plural *gansiz)
"goose"
Note: Grimm's Law shifted PIE aspirated stop *ǵʰ to Germanic voiced velar stop *g.
↓
Proto-West Germanic / Ingvaeonic (c. 3rd–5th c. CE)
*gą̄s (plural *gą̄si)
"goose"
Note: Ingvaeonic nasal spirant law dropped nasal /n/ before fricative /s/ with compensatory lengthening of preceding vowel (*ans > *ą̄s).
↓
Old English (c. 700–1000 CE)
gōs (plural gēs)
"goose"
Note: The plural gēs evolved via i-mutation (umlaut) from prehistoric *gōsi > *gø̄s > gēs, preserving the irregular modern plural geese.
↓
Middle English (c. 1150–1450)
gos / goos (plural gees)
"goose; foolish person; simpleton"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
goose (plural geese)
"goose; silly person; tailor's smoothing iron"
↓
