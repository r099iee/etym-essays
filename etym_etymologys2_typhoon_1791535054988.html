Note: A renowned example of etymological convergence (cross-linguistic conflation). Arabic ṭūfān ("hurricane, deluge", from Semitic root *ṭ-w-f "to circle, overflow", partly influenced by Greek Typhōn, the mythic titan of storm winds) was carried by maritime traders to India and the South China Sea, where it merged with southern Chinese taaí-fūng (大風, "great wind") and tai-hong (颱風, "typhoon"). Portuguese navigators adopted tufão, passing it into English as tuffon. In the 18th and 19th centuries, English scholars altered the spelling to typhoon through deliberate association with Greek Typhōn.
