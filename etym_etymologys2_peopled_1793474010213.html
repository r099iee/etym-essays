Note: The ultimate root is the Proto-Indo-European verbal root *pleh₁- ('to fill, full', cognate with Greek plēthos 'multitude' and Latin plēnus 'full'). In Proto-Italic, a reduplicated nominal formation *po-pl-o- arose, originally designating the entire body of citizens assembled under arms for military muster. In Classical Rome, this evolved into populus ('the sovereign citizen body of Rome', as in Senatus Populusque Romanus). Borrowed into Middle English via Old French, the noun gave rise to the verb to people, which directly yielded the participial form peopled.
