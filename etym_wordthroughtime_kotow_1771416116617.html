- [Early 19th-century encounter] - Borrowed into English by Western visitors as the name of the Chinese court's full prostration - kneeling and knocking the forehead on the ground before the Emperor; recorded as an alien, exotic ritual.
- [19th-century diplomatic flashpoint] - The kowtow becomes a notorious symbol of diplomatic hierarchy as European envoys clash with Qing protocol; charged with political meaning.
- [20th-century metaphorical turn] - English speakers apply the word figuratively to any grovelling surrender of pride - 'to kowtow to authority' - moving from court ritual to a general verb of servility.
- [Contemporary register] - Nearly always figurative and mildly dismissive; describes abject, unprincipled deference rather than a literal gesture.
