beefsteak in its core modern sense is a thick, flat slice of beef, typically cut across the muscle fibre from the loin, rump, rib or chuck, intended to be cooked quickly by grilling, frying, broiling or barbecuing and served as a single portion. In British and American restaurant usage it is essentially synonymous with steak when the meat is beef, but beefsteak stresses the species and the substantial, juicy, red-meated character: a rump beefsteak, a grilled beefsteak with onions, a rare beefsteak. The word carries a sensory charge of redness, blood-juice, sizzle and nourishing heaviness that explains its simile use in your context — He looked like a raw beefsteak means a face that is swollen, florid, purplish-red and meaty, as after drinking, exertion, anger or a blow.

Beyond the plate, beefsteak has developed a cluster of secondary senses, all bridged by the idea of thick, red, fleshy beef-like substance. In horticulture the beefsteak tomato is a very large, ribbed, juicy cultivar with dense red flesh and few seeds, named because a horizontal slice looks like a slab of raw beef. In mycology the beefsteak fungus (Fistulina hepatica) is a bracket fungus that exudes a blood-red juice and has a marbled, meat-like interior; it is even called poor-man's beefsteak and was once cooked as a meat substitute. In houseplants the beefsteak begonia and beefsteak plant (Perilla frutescens) are named for large, fleshy, blood-red or purplish leaves.

  • Culinary core: a cut of beef for quick cooking — fillet, sirloin, rump, rib-eye, T-bone, porterhouse. Served rare, medium-rare, medium, well-done; graded by marbling, ageing, provenance (Aberdeen Angus beefsteak, Kobe beefsteak).
  • Raw-meat simile: like a raw beefsteak for a bruised eye, a drinker's nose, wind-chapped cheeks — red, swollen, glistening. Semantic bridge is direct visual resemblance plus connotation of exposed flesh.
  • Beefsteak tomato / fungus / plant: transferred epithet for non-meat things that are thick, red, juicy and layered like beef. Bridge is colour + texture + sliceability.
  • Social-institutional sense: a beefsteak club, beefsteak dinner — historically an all-male convivial dining society (18th-century Sublime Society of Beef Steaks in London), later an American political fundraiser featuring grilled beef. Bridge is hearty, masculine, indulgent festivity.
  • Figurative masculine body: in showbusiness slang, beefsteak can denote a stocky, muscular male physique or a male pin-up, parallel to cheesecake for women. Bridge is meat-as-body objectification.

In domain usage, butchers distinguish beefsteak from veal steak, pork steak, tuna steak and cauliflower steak, where steak alone has broadened to any thick slice for grilling. Nutrition labels, menus and food science use beefsteak for composition (protein, myoglobin redness, marbling fat), while literary prose uses it for visceral colour. Collocations include beefsteak pudding (obsolete for steak pudding), beefsteak pie, beefsteak mushrooms, beat someone to a beefsteak (pulp to red ruin). Register is concrete and slightly old-fashioned in British English — modern menus prefer simply steak or the cut name — but beefsteak survives strongly in compounds and similes because no other word packs redness, thickness and animal flesh so compactly.
