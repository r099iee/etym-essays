• Dwarfs (dwarves): Similar because both words denote very small human beings, and the Pygmies were long classed among the dwarfs of the encyclopaedias; differs because “dwarf” is the general term for short-statured persons and for mythic earth-craftsmen, while “pygmies” refers to the specific legendary race and to real forest peoples — and “dwarf” of people is itself now usually replaced by “little person” in careful usage.
  • Midgets: Similar because “midget” is the older English word for a proportionally small person, overlapping “pygmy” in the insult zone; differs because “midget” is now overwhelmingly considered offensive and refers to individual stature, while “pygmy” retains serious ethnonymic and taxonomic uses — two size-words showing how such vocabulary curdles into slurs.
  • Lilliputians: Similar because the Lilliputians are a famous fabled people of tiny stature (Swift, Gulliver’s Travels, 1726), like Homer’s pygmies; differs because Lilliput is a one-book political satire about miniature English factions — clearly fiction — whereas pygmies were believed in as real geography for over two millennia and later met real peoples; register: invention versus half-believed lore.
  • Homunculi: Similar because homunculi are tiny humans of alchemy and literature, another “small people” cluster; differs because a homunculus is an individual manufactured creature in a flask, never a race or a people; “pygmy” is a people-word, “homunculus” a laboratory curiosity.
  • Runts: Similar because a runt is the smallest and weakest of a litter — the “pygmy” of the litter — and both words can belittle; differs because “runt” is breeders’ and play-ground slang for an undersized animal, with pity and weakness built in, while “pygmies” (of peoples or species) is formal and often scientific.
  • Pipsqueaks: Similar because a pipsqueak is a contemptuous name for a small or insignificant person, matching the insulting sense of “pygmy”; differs because “pipsqueak” is jokey, informal and purely abusive of individuals, with no ethnography or taxonomy attached — while “pygmies” can still be used respectfully of the Baka or Mbuti.
  • Shrimps: Similar because “shrimp” is another informal size-insult for a small person, and pygmies were themselves once classed with such tiny marvels; differs because “shrimp” is seafood-derived slang used almost always of individuals, and carries none of the mythic, geographic or scientific weight of “pygmies”.
  • Small fry: Similar because “small fry” denotes unimportant people or children — the insignificance sense of “pygmy”; differs because “small fry” is an idiom of rank and age (underlings, kids) rather than of stature or race, and has no singular, no capital letter and no millennial history.
