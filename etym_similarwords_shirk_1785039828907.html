• Evade: Similar because both mean getting away from a duty or pursuit; differs because evade is the broad, neutral term, with no connotation of cowardice, dishonesty, or laziness that shirk always carries.
  • Dodge: Similar because both mean to avoid something; differs because dodge implies quick evasive movement or sidestepping a blow or question, not the sly abandonment of an obligation.
  • Avoid: Similar because both mean to keep away from; differs because avoid is general and harmless, with no implication of laziness, fear, or breached duty.
  • Duck: Similar because both mean to sidestep an obligation; differs because duck is colloquial and quick, often of dodging a question or chore, without the sneer of shirk.
  • Slack / slack off: Similar because both involve reduced effort; differs because slacking means working with less energy, not abandoning or evading the task entirely.
  • Malinger: Similar because both mean escaping work; differs because malingering specifically feigns illness or incapacity to avoid duty, while shirking is open evasion through laziness or fear.
  • Skulk: Similar because both imply furtive, sneaking behavior; differs because skulking is lurking and hiding in the shadows, with no necessary object of duty, while shirking targets a specific obligation.
  • Shun: Similar because both mean to keep away from; differs because shun is morally neutral avoidance of a person or thing out of distaste, not the evasion of one's own duty.
  • Elude: Similar because both mean to escape something; differs because elude stresses outwitting a pursuer or grasp, physical or mental, not the neglect of a responsibility.
  • Sponge / freeload: Similar because both have parasitic overtones; differs because sponging means living off others' generosity, whereas shirking means dodging one's own assigned labor.
  • Sidestep: Similar because both mean to avoid something skillfully; differs because sidestepping can be legitimate and artful (dodging a question), while shirking is always a breach of duty.
