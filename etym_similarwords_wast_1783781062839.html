• were: Similar because it is the same verb in the same tense — "thou wast" equals "you were"; differs because were is the modern person-neutral past form (also the subjunctive), while wast is the archaic second-person singular indicative with the -st ending and a reverential, scriptural register.
  • was: Similar because it shares the same past stem and meaning of past existence or state; differs because was is the first/third person singular form in both old and modern usage, never second person, whereas wast is specifically "thou was."
  • wert: Similar because it is the other archaic second-person singular past of be ("thou wert"); differs because wert is the subjunctive (used for conditions and wishes, as in "wert thou here..."), while wast is the simple indicative (plain fact, "thou wast present").
  • wertest / wert (variant): Similar because both are archaic thou-forms of the past of be; differs because the contracted wert and the older fuller forms belong to the subjunctive and conditional uses, not the indicative assertion carried by wast.
  • didst be: Similar because it is a periphrastic archaic past of "be" in the second person singular; differs because it is a rare, emphatic construction using the auxiliary do, whereas wast is the simple inflected form and is the standard scriptural word.
  • remained / dwelt: Similar because the PIE root *wes- behind was originally meant "to remain, stay, dwell," so the deep semantics overlap; differs because modern remained/dwelt are full lexical verbs naming continued presence, while wast is the copular past of be asserting existence, identity, or state.
