• Stoat: Similar because it is a small, slender, long-bodied mustelid of the same family and is often loosely called a weasel; differs because the stoat (Mustela erminea) is larger, has a black-tipped tail, and turns white (as ermine) in snowy regions.
  • Ferret: Similar because it is a small domesticated hunting mustelid that also hunts in burrows; differs because the ferret is the domesticated form of the polecat, not the wild least weasel.
  • Polecat: Similar because it is a closely related, musk-scented mustelid of the same genus lineage; differs because it is heavier, darker-furred, and even more notorious for its stench than the weasel.
  • Marten: Similar because it is a member of the same mustelid family; differs because the marten is a tree-dwelling forest mustelid, larger than the weasel, with a bushier tail and a taste for squirrels and birds.
  • Mink: Similar because it is a slender mustelid of the same family; differs because the mink is semi-aquatic, larger, and famously farmed for its fur, while the weasel is a small terrestrial hunter.
  • Otter: Similar because it is an agile mustelid carnivore; differs because the otter is a fully aquatic animal of rivers and coasts, far larger than the weasel.
  • Badger: Similar because it is a burrowing mustelid carnivore; differs because the badger is large, stocky, flat-bodied, and mostly nocturnal, with none of the weasel’s slipperiness.
  • Sneak: Similar because both words can name a sly, underhand person; differs because “sneak” is purely figurative and human-centred, while “weasel” names the actual animal first and the sly person only by extension.
  • Snake: Similar because both are contemptuous terms for a treacherous, evasive person; differs because “snake” stresses cold treachery and poison, while “weasel” stresses smallness, slipperiness, and getting out of tight spots.
  • Fox: Similar because both animals are proverbial for cunning; differs because “fox” (also “foxy”) stresses cleverness and attractiveness, while “weasel” stresses petty slyness and evasiveness.
  • Rat: Similar because both are small, vermin-adjacent animals used as terms of contempt; differs because “rat” names an informer or deserter, while “weasel” names an evasive, slippery schemer.
