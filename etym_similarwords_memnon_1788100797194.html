• Achilles: Similar because the two are the paired duellists of the Eastern war — each the greatest warrior of his side; differs because Achilles is the Greek champion and the poem's centre, while Memnon is the noble foreigner whose death foreshadows Achilles'.
  • Hector: Similar because the other great doomed defender of Troy; differs because Hector is Trojan-born, family-bound, and dies in the Iliad proper, whereas Memnon arrives after the Iliad's story ends.
  • Antilochus: Similar because bound to Memnon's story as his famous victim; differs because Antilochus is a Greek hero, son of Nestor, mourned in the Odyssey.
  • Eos / Aurora: Similar because his mother, the Dawn; differs because she is a sky goddess, while Memnon is mortal-born and falls in battle.
  • Tithonus: Similar because his father, the Trojan prince granted eternal life without eternal youth; differs because Tithonus belongs to the dawn-myth of the cicada, far from the battlefield.
  • Amenhotep III: Similar because the historical pharaoh whose colossi the Greeks called Memnon; differs because he is a real 14th-century-BCE king and temple-builder, not a Homeric hero.
  • The Memnonides: Similar because the birds sprung from his pyre, his living memorial; differs because they are transformed beings, not a personality.
