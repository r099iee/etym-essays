[Compound Formation: rasā + tala → Rasātala]
Epic & Classical Sanskrit (c. 5th c. BCE–present)
Rasātala
"the underworld; the floor of the subterranean waters"

Note on compound morphology and cognates: The noun Rasātala is a tatpuruṣa or karmadhāraya compound composed of the feminine substantive rasā (the subterranean river of myth, moisture, or the moist earth) and the neuter/masculine substantive tala (surface, bottom, foundation). The root of rasā is cognate with Latin rōs ("dew"), Lithuanian rasà ("dew"), and Old Church Slavonic rosa ("dew"). The second component, tala, derives from PIE *telh₂- ("flat ground"), cognate with Latin tellūs ("earth, globe"), Old Irish talam ("earth"), and Old High German dilli ("plank, flooring"). Thus, Rasātala etymologically signifies "the ground/floor of the subterranean moisture," which naturally evolved to denote the deepest subterranean realm beneath the continental earth.
