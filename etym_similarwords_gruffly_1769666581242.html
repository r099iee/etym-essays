• Roughly: Similar because both describe coarse, unpolished behavior or sound; differs because roughly is far broader (also meaning approximately or violently), while gruffly specifically concerns voice and a blunt, low-pitched manner.
  • Hoarsely: Similar because both describe a throaty, low, grating voice; differs because hoarsely implies vocal strain or sickness, while gruffly is a natural deep roughness or deliberate sternness.
  • Huskily: Similar because both describe a low, slightly rough voice; differs because huskily is often attractive or emotional (a husky whisper), while gruffly carries an edge of surliness or bluntness.
  • Throatily: Similar because both describe speech produced deep in the throat; differs because throatily is primarily acoustic and neutral, whereas gruffly adds the sense of a rough, stern temperament.
  • Gutturally: Similar because both describe harsh, throat-formed speech; differs because gutturally emphasizes the physical place of articulation (back of the throat), while gruffly focuses on low pitch and a rough, blunt manner.
  • Brusquely: Similar because both describe a short, blunt, unfriendly manner; differs because brusquely stresses curt speed and abruptness without any throaty vocal quality, while gruffly can also describe the voice itself.
  • Curtly: Similar because both describe brief, dismissive speech; differs because curtly is purely about brevity and rudeness, while gruffly is about a rough, low, gravelly tone and gruff temper.
  • Abruptly: Similar because both describe sudden, clipped speech; differs because abruptly is neutral and structural (suddenness), whereas gruffly is tonal and temperamental.
  • Bluntly: Similar because both describe plain, unvarnished speech; differs because bluntly emphasizes directness and honesty, while gruffly emphasizes a rough, harsh, low-pitched delivery.
  • Tersely: Similar because both describe economical, clipped language; differs because tersely is a neutral, stylistic term for brevity, while gruffly implies roughness and surliness.
  • Surly (used adverbially as surly): Similar because both denote a bad-tempered, churlish manner; differs because surly is more openly hostile and grumpy, while gruffly can be merely rough and low-voiced without full hostility.
  • Crustily: Similar because both describe a testy, short-tempered, rough manner; differs because crustily is rarer and implies a snappish, grumbling irritability, while gruffly often keeps a dignified, deep-voiced quality.
  • Testily: Similar because both describe impatient, irritable replies; differs because testily stresses irritation and annoyance, while gruffly stresses a rough, low-pitched, stern delivery.
