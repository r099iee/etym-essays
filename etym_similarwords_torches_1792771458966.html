• Flambeaux: Similar because both are large hand-carried festive flames; differs because flambeaux is the grand ceremonial French cousin (weddings, Mardi Gras), while torches is the everyday word from caves to campsites.
  • Brands: Similar because a firebrand is literally a burning stick of wood; differs because brand stresses the burning timber itself (often snatched from a fire), while torch stresses a purpose-made carrier of flame.
  • Lanterns: Similar because both are portable lights carried in the hand; differs because a lantern encloses its flame or bulb in glass or casing, while a torch burns open to the air.
  • Candles: Similar because both are small portable flames for light and ceremony; differs because a candle is wax with a wick for still indoor air, while a torch is a robust outdoor flame built to survive wind and motion.
  • Flares: Similar because both are hand-held burning signals giving bright light; differs because a flare burns briefly and intensely for distress or celebration, while a torch burns steadily for prolonged illumination.
  • Flashlights: Similar because both are hand-held portable lights (British electric torches); differs because a flashlight uses batteries and bulbs with no flame at all, while a true torch is living fire.
  • Sparklers: Similar because in the source sentence both are hand-held festive lights waved at celebrations; differs because sparklers shower sparks and die in a minute, while torches give a sustained flame for light.
  • Beacons: Similar because both are signal-fires carrying meaning across distance; differs because a beacon is a fixed fire on a hill or tower, while torches are carried wherever the bearer walks.
  • Tapers: Similar because both are slender carriers of flame used in ceremony; differs because a taper is a thin wax stick for lighting other candles, while a torch is a stout staff of flame in its own right.
