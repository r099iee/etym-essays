• cup: The direct Modern English descendant, naming the same vessel and its derived senses; differs only in the loss of the final vowel and the modern shortening of the vowel.
  • mug: A heavy cylindrical cup with a handle, used for hot drinks; similar in being a personal drinking vessel, but sturdier, larger and more informal than a cup.
  • glass: A drinking vessel made of glass, and the material itself; similar as a table vessel, but it names the material and typically has no handle.
  • tumbler: A flat-bottomed drinking glass without a stem or handle; similar in being a simple vessel, but specifically glass and associated with spirits or water.
  • goblet: A stemmed drinking vessel with a bowl; similar as a cup, but formal, usually of metal or glass, and associated with wine and ceremony.
  • chalice: The sacramental cup of the Eucharist; similar in being a cup, but exclusively religious, usually precious metal, and far more solemn in register.
  • beaker: A tall, wide-mouthed vessel, often conical, used in laboratories or for ale; similar in shape, but plainer, larger and associated with science or heavy drinking.
  • tankard: A large one-handled drinking vessel, often lidded; similar as a drinking vessel, but bigger, historically associated with alehouses and pewter.
  • bowl: A rounded open vessel for food or liquid; similar in shape, but wider and shallower, and used for eating or mixing rather than drinking.
  • vessel: A general term for any container for liquid; similar in being a container, but far more abstract and formal, and not specifically for drinking.
  • draught: The quantity of liquid drunk at one time; similar in denoting a served portion, but it names the drink rather than the container.
  • cupful: The measure contained by a cup; similar in deriving from the vessel, but explicitly a quantity rather than the object.
  • grail: The cup of the Last Supper in Arthurian legend; similar in being a sacred cup, but legendary, unique and charged with quest symbolism.
