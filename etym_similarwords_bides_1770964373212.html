• waits: Similar because both mean remaining in expectation of an event; differs because waits is the neutral, everyday verb for remaining until something happens, while bides carries archaic dignity, poetic weight, and the connotation of confident, purposeful patience.
  • awaits: Similar because both mean expecting something to come; differs because awaits is transitive, directing attention to the external object of expectation ("awaits the buyer"), while bides emphasizes the internal, self-contained state of patient endurance even when it takes an object.
  • tarries: Similar because both mean staying or remaining in a place; differs because tarries implies delay, dawdling, or reluctance, whereas bides describes a purposeful, faith-driven pause grounded in confidence.
  • lingers: Similar because both mean continuing to stay; differs because lingers suggests staying behind reluctantly or clinging to a fading moment, whereas bides looks forward with serenity toward a destined future.
  • abides: Similar because both descend from the same Old English verb family and both mean continuing or enduring; differs because abides in modern English chiefly means "tolerates, puts up with" ("cannot abide") or "keeps (a promise)", while bides centers on waiting and remaining with confidence.
  • endures: Similar because both imply surviving hardship or time; differs because endures stresses suffering or resisting pressure, while bides stresses calm, patient waiting without an emphasis on suffering.
  • remains: Similar because both mean continuing to be or stay in place; differs because remains is a colorless, general verb of continuation (also of what is left over), while bides adds the emotional register of patient expectation.
  • stays: Similar because both mean remaining in a place; differs because stays is the plain, everyday word for remaining (temporarily or permanently), lacking the archaic, literary, and purposeful connotations of bides.
  • hovers: Similar because both can describe remaining near an expected moment; differs because hovers implies being suspended, uncertain, or indecisive in the air, whereas bides implies grounded, confident stillness.
