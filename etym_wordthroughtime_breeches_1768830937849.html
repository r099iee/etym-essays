- The split garment (Old English onward) - breeches is the garment covering the place where the body splits into two legs; the double plural fossilizes the two legs taken together
- The gentleman's garment (16th-early 19th c.) - knee-length breeches are the everyday dress of men and the mark of the master of the house, so 'to wear the breeches' means to rule the household
- The word divides (19th-20th c.) - the reanalysed singular breech goes technical (breech-birth, breech-loading) while everyday clothing speech shifts to trousers
- Contemporary - a niche, historical, and riding word, alive in idioms ('too big for his breeches') and in the riding costume of those who sit astride
