• encomiums: Similar because it denotes formal expressions of warm, glowing praise; differs because encomium focuses on praising specific virtues or achievements in speech or writing, whereas panegyric historically emphasizes grand public delivery before a solemn assembly and tends toward greater rhetorical elaboration.
  • eulogies: Similar because it refers to formal speeches or writings praising someone; differs because eulogies are almost universally delivered in modern English upon the occasion of someone's death (funeral orations), whereas panegyrics are delivered in honor of living rulers, abstract virtues, institutions, or public festivals.
  • tributes: Similar because it signifies an acknowledgment of gratitude, respect, or admiration; differs because tribute is a broad, everyday word that can be non-verbal (e.g., a floral tribute, a tribute concert), while panegyric is specifically an elaborate oratorical or literary composition.
  • paeans: Similar because it represents an expression of triumphant praise and rejoicing; differs because paean originated as a choral hymn or battle song of thanksgiving (originally to Apollo), retaining an ecstatic, musical, and triumphant character, whereas panegyric is a structured rhetorical oration.
  • laudations: Similar because it denotes high praise and commendation; differs because laudation is more abstract and formalistic, lacking the historical association with festival oratory and literary genre that defines panegyric.
  • plaudits: Similar because it involves expressions of public approval and applause; differs because plaudits are typically spontaneous, collective bursts of approval or critical acclaim, whereas a panegyric is a sustained, solitary composition.
  • flattery: Similar because both involve high praise; differs crucially because flattery is inherently insincere and manipulative, whereas panegyric is a legitimate classical literary and oratorical form, though it may occasionally descend into flattery.
  • homages: Similar because it expresses honor and public reverence; differs because homage historically derives from feudal allegiance and can take the form of artistic imitation or symbolic gesture, while panegyric is purely textual and rhetorical.
