- Late 17th Century - Physical sense: A short, thick, blunt block of firewood or timber; a rustic, heavy chunk.
- 18th to Early 19th Century - Culinary & anatomical extension: The thick, blunt rump end of a loin of meat ('chump end' / 'chump chop'); colloquially applied to human anatomy (the head or buttock).
- Mid-19th Century - Slang idioms: Emergence of 'off one's chump' meaning crazy, insane, or out of one's mind (playing on 'chump' as the head).
- Late 19th to Early 20th Century - Lexical shift to foolish person: Applied derisively to a thick-headed, clumsy, or easily duped person; popularized heavily in P. G. Wodehouse, British public school slang, and American sporting/prizefighting cant for an easy opponent or 'palooka'.
- Contemporary Usage - Retains mild, affectionate, or exasperated colloquial status for someone gullible, clumsy, or outmaneuvered, without carrying severe profanity or malice.
