• Infusion: Similar in the pouring of one substance into another; differs because infusion pours something into a thing (tea into water), whereas suffusion pours up from beneath and through the surface itself.
  • Diffusion: Similar in spreading through a medium; differs because diffusion spreads a thing apart through space, while suffusion rises internally and fully permeates from within.
  • Effusion: Similar in the pouring of a liquid; differs because effusion pours out (an outpouring of blood or emotion), while suffusion rises up from beneath the surface.
  • Perfusion: Similar in the spreading of a fluid; differs because perfusion is the clinical term for forcing fluid through an organ or vessel, while suffusion is a natural rising color.
  • Blush: Similar in the spreading of red color; differs because a blush is the sudden reddening of the face by blood and belongs strictly to the skin, while suffusion can color any substance from the inside out.
  • Flush: Similar in the spreading of red color; differs because a flush is a sudden reddening (of the face, or a flush of light) that is quick and temporary, while suffusion is a deeper, more diffuse saturation.
  • Staining: Similar in the imparting of color; differs because staining applies color to the outside of a surface, while suffusion colors from within.
  • Crimson: Similar in being a vivid red; differs because crimson (from the kermes dye) is a deeper, bluer, bloodier red, while scarlet is brighter with an orange or vermilion cast.
  • Vermilion: Similar in being an orange-red; differs because vermilion is a mineral pigment (mercury sulfide), hard and opaque, while scarlet is a dye-red, fiery and translucent.
  • Maroon: Similar in being a red; differs because maroon is a dark brownish-red at the deep end of the scale, while scarlet is the light, blazing end — the very progression the passage uses.
  • Cardinal: Similar in naming a red; differs because cardinal (as a color) is the red of cardinals' robes, close to scarlet but a formal, slightly deeper hue, while scarlet is the general blazing red.
  • Cerise: Similar in being a bright red; differs because cerise is pinker, closer to cherry, while scarlet is more orange and more blazing.
