• noise: Similar because both name loud acoustic disturbance; differs because noise is a general, often mechanical or ambient sound, while a clamour specifically requires urgent, collective human shouting with intent.
  • din: Similar because both are loud, sustained, confused sounds; differs because din may come from machines or crowds without emotion, while a clamour carries the connotation of vehement, demanding human voices.
  • uproar: Similar because both are public commotions of voices; differs because an uproar suggests a sudden explosion of anger or outrage from a distinct incident, while a clamour is a prolonged, overlapping, petitioning outcry.
  • roar: Similar because both are loud, powerful sounds; differs because a roar is deep, continuous, and unified (a crowd, a lion, the sea), while a clamour is fragmented, dissonant, and made of many competing voices.
  • racket: Similar because both name a loud noise; differs because racket is informal, annoying, and often mechanical or playful, while clamour is formal, purposeful, and human.
  • outcry: Similar because both name a public protest or shouting; differs because outcry stresses a spontaneous burst of public indignation, while clamour stresses the persistent, many-voiced noise of demand.
  • hubbub: Similar because both name the confused noise of a crowd; differs because hubbub is the cheerful or neutral bustle of a crowd without urgent demand, while clamour is insistent and demanding.
  • bedlam: Similar because both name chaotic, deafening disorder; differs because bedlam names a state of insane confusion (from the hospital Bethlem), while clamour is specifically vocal outcry.
  • shout: Similar because both involve loud calling; differs because a shout is a single loud cry, while a clamour is the combined noise of many shouts.
  • demand: Similar because both involve insisting on something; differs because demand is the assertion itself, while clamour is the noisy, collective way it is voiced.
  • cacophony: Similar because both are harsh, discordant sound; differs because cacophony is a technical, aesthetic word for dissonance (from Greek "bad sound"), while clamour is human outcry with purpose.
