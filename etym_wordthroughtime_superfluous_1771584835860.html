- [15th-16th century literal overflow] - Superfluous keeps its hydraulic root alive: things that run over the brim - excess of humours, food or goods - 'more than enough' in a bodily, almost physical sense.
- [17th-18th century rhetorical turn] - The word moves to style and conduct: superfluous words, superfluous gestures - criticism of what is added without need.
- [19th-20th century plain 'unnecessary'] - Settles into its modern sense: unnecessary because more than required; slightly more formal and elegant than plain 'unnecessary'.
- [Contemporary register] - Formal, with a hint of the overflowing; used when the extra thing is not just unwanted but exceeds what is needed.
