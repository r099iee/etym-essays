• Aristocracy: Similar because both name rule or society by the hereditary well-born; differs because aristocracy stresses government by the best (and by extension the whole upper class), while nobility stresses titled rank itself and its moral aura.
  • Peerage: Similar because in Britain the peerage is the legal core of the nobility; differs because peerage is the precise technical list of title-holders, while nobility is the broader estate including families, ethos, and virtue.
  • Gentry: Similar because both are landed upper orders appearing beside nobility in the source sentence; differs because the gentry are untitled landowners below the peers, while the nobility hold titles and historically greater privilege.
  • Elite: Similar because both denote a small leading stratum set above the mass; differs because elite is modern, meritocratic, and morally neutral (any field), while nobility carries blood, history, and honour.
  • Patricians: Similar because both name a hereditary governing class conscious of its dignity; differs because patricians belongs to ancient Rome (and by metaphor to any old oligarchy), while nobility belongs to feudal and modern Europe.
  • Magnanimity: Similar because both praise greatness of soul and generosity to others; differs because magnanimity is purely a virtue (forgiving enemies, giving largely), while nobility spans rank, style, and character.
  • Honour: Similar because honour is the code the nobility claimed to live by; differs because honour names the principle and reputation itself (often prickly and personal), while nobility names the rank or the elevated character.
  • Distinction: Similar because both mark eminence above the ordinary; differs because distinction is individual and earned (a career of distinction), while nobility may be inherited or may describe grandeur of manner.
  • Grandeur: Similar because both convey imposing dignity, as of buildings or bearing; differs because grandeur stresses impressiveness of scale and effect, while nobility stresses elevation of rank or soul.
