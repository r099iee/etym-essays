• waved: Similar because both describe broad motion of the hand through the air; differs because waving is deliberate, social, and directed at someone, while flapped is loose, blind, and purposeless.
  • flailed: Similar because both are wild, sweeping, uncoordinated motions of the limbs; differs because flailing is more violent and thrashes side to side, whereas flapped stays flat and loose — a single broad plane sweeping the air.
  • fluttered: Similar because both are light, repeated, wing-like motions; differs because fluttering is small, tremulous, and delicate, lacking the broad flat sweep and helpless weight of flapping.
  • groped: Similar because both describe blind motion in the dark; differs because groping is purposeful tactile searching for contact, while flapping beats the air without seeking anything.
  • fumbled: Similar because both imply clumsy, uncontrolled movement; differs because fumbling involves handling objects awkwardly, while flapping moves a broad flat thing through air.
  • beat: Similar because both are physical verbs of repeated motion; differs because beating implies force and rhythm against something, while flapping is loose and strikes nothing.
  • thrashed: Similar because both are wild, uncoordinated movements; differs because thrashing is violent, convulsive, and often born of distress, far more forceful than the loose flat sweep of flapping.
  • wobbled: Similar because both describe loose, unsteady motion; differs because wobbling is a rocking from side to side around a base, not the up-and-down beating of a broad plane.
  • shook: Similar because both are quick, repeated movements; differs because shaking is a small, rapid oscillation usually in place, without the wide up-and-down sweep of flapping.
