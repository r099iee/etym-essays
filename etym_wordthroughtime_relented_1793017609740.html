- Latin roots — pliant and slow: lentus means supple, lingering, slow to move, the willow bending rather than breaking.
- Late Latin — softening verbs: *lentare and kin give French the family of lent, slow, and ralentir, to slow down.
- Late 1300s — melting in English: Middle English relenten first means to dissolve or thaw, snow and salt softening into solution.
- 1500s — hearts melt: the verb turns inward, and hard men and proud princes relent, severity dissolving into pity.
- 1600s — heaven and weather: translators have God relent from judgement, and poets have storms and fevers relent, the will behind the pressure now divine or elemental.
- Today — pressure with a will: creditors, pursuers, winters, and haunted Forests relent, always after hardness, always as an event the sufferer feels.
