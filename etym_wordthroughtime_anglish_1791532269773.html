- 5th–9th century: the speech of the Angles in Britain - the name is Englisc, a plain ethnic and linguistic label with no stylistic charge.
- 10th–12th century: the language of a newly unified English church and kingdom - Englisc becomes a literary and administrative word, and the Angles' name covers all the Germanic peoples of England.
- 13th–15th century: competing spellings - Engelish, Englisch, Anglish and Inglish coexist; Anglish is a minority form that keeps the tribal Ang- visible.
- 16th–18th century: standardisation - English wins, and Anglish retreats into antiquarian and dialect writing as an archaism.
- 19th–mid 20th century: antiquarian and literary revival - Anglish is used for medieval flavour and, increasingly, for a Germanic-purist style of prose.
- Late 20th–21st century: the modern movement - Anglish becomes the name of an online community and a stylistic programme that replaces borrowed words with native ones.
