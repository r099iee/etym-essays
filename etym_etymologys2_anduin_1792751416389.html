Note on philological construction: J.R.R. Tolkien constructed Sindarin phonology and morphology primarily on the model of Brythonic Celtic, specifically Old and Middle Welsh. The element and- ("long") parallels Welsh an- and ancient Celtic intensifiers, while the hydronymic formative -duin shares phonetic affinities with Celtic water terms such as Welsh dwfr / dŵr ("water") and the pan-European river names derived from Proto-Indo-European *deh₂nu- ("river, flowing water", source of Danube, Don, Dnieper, Dniester). In Tolkien's internal linguistic history, duin is strictly distinguished from sîr (a generic flowing river, as in Sirion): a duin is a river of great volume and length that flows directly into the sea.
