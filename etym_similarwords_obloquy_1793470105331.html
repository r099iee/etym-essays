• Opprobrium: Similar because both denote severe public dishonor and reproach; differs because opprobrium (from Latin opprobrare, ‘to taunt or reproach’) focuses heavily on the lasting, indelible shame, stigma, and contempt attached to shameful conduct, whereas obloquy emphasizes the active storm of verbal abuse and defamatory denunciation.
  • Calumny: Similar because both involve harmful public speech against someone; differs because calumny consists strictly of false, malicious, and fabricated accusations designed to deceive the public, while obloquy can be a reaction to real actions and denotes the sheer volume and fury of public condemnation.
  • Ignominy: Similar because both encompass public dishonor; differs because ignominy (literally ‘loss of one’s name’) describes the passive state of profound personal humiliation and disgrace (e.g., suffering the ignominy of defeat), whereas obloquy involves the active verbal barrage causing that state.
  • Infamy: Similar because both refer to widespread bad reputation; differs because infamy denotes a permanent historical notoriety for monstrous deeds (e.g., ‘a day that will live in infamy’), whereas obloquy is the verbal storm of outrage, condemnation, and reproach.
  • Vilification: Similar because both denote harsh, degrading speech; differs because vilification is an active, modern process of defaming or degrading someone’s character, whereas obloquy carries classical rhetorical weight and denotes both the verbal barrage and the resulting state of disgrace.
  • Reproach: Similar because both express disapproval and blame; differs because reproach can be gentle, personal, and quiet (e.g., ‘a mother’s tender reproach’), while obloquy is always public, overwhelming, and ferocious.
  • Censure: Similar because both involve moral condemnation; differs because censure is typically formal, institutional, and dispassionate (e.g., ‘a vote of censure in parliament’), whereas obloquy is impassioned, widespread, and emotionally abusive.
  • Defamation: Similar because both attack reputation; differs because defamation is a specific legal category concerning false communications that harm standing, whereas obloquy is a broader literary, historical, and moral concept.
  • Scurrility: Similar because both involve abusive language; differs because scurrility emphasizes coarse, vulgar, buffoonish, and indecent language, while obloquy can be composed of solemn, high-minded moral denunciation.
