- Late 16th to 17th Century: Entered Early Modern English from Anglo-Norman French, initially describing the physical disturbance of water or beer, specifically stirring up dregs, mud, and sediment to make a clear liquid turbid and cloudy.
- 18th to 19th Century: Extended to maritime and weather descriptions of oceans, rivers, and boiling clouds churning with violent, foaming currents; simultaneously developed the dialectal American variant *rile* (meaning to anger or irritate).
- Late 19th to Mid-20th Century: Adopted into psychological, psychoanalytic, and literary prose to describe subconscious emotional turmoil, repressed instincts, and simmering discontent beneath polite social veneers.
- Late 20th to 21st Century: Widely utilized in financial journalism, international relations, and political analysis to describe turbulent, unstable markets, shifting geopolitical alliances, and volatile public controversies.
