• Affectation: Similar because both involve unnatural, artificial behavior or speech adopted to impress others; differs because affectation is a broad psychological term encompassing any phony posture, accent, or pose, whereas preciosity specifically denotes an over-delicate, hyper-refined, and fastidious aesthetic elegance in language or high culture.
  • Purpleness (Purple Prose): Similar because both describe ornate, overwrought, and self-conscious literary styles; differs because purpleness emphasizes melodramatic emotional intensity, extravagant adjectives, and lush sensory overload, whereas preciosity emphasizes delicate, fragile fastidiousness, intellectual pedantry, and neurotic avoidance of plain words.
  • Mannerism: Similar because both designate distinctive stylistic habits that deviate from classical naturalness; differs because mannerism refers to idiosyncratic, repeated artistic techniques or gestures (whether bold, eccentric, or delicate), whereas preciosity is always characterized by fragile over-refinement, softness, and hyper-delicacy.
  • Pedantry: Similar because both involve an irritating, showy display of learning; differs because pedantry focuses on rigid, humorless obsession with trivial rules, factual accuracy, and technical definitions, whereas preciosity focuses on aesthetic elegance, decorative metaphors, and polite linguistic squeamishness.
  • Euphuism: Similar because both designate historical literary movements characterized by extreme stylistic artificiality; differs because euphuism (stemming from John Lyly's 1578 work *Euphues*) relies on rigid syntactic symmetry, antithesis, and alliteration drawn from mythological natural history, whereas preciosity is modeled on French salon circumlocution, delicate courtship codes, and polite euphemism.
  • Pretentiousness: Similar because both involve claiming a higher artistic or intellectual status than is justified; differs because pretentiousness can be crude, loud, and boastful, whereas preciosity is invariably delicate, whispery, fastidious, and excessively polished.
