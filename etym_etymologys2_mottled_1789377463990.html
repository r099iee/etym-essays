Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 12th–13th c.)
mottelé / matelé
"spotted, curdled, clotted, variegated like curds"
Note: From Old French mater ('to curdle, clot'), ultimately from Latin mactāre ('to strike down') or Germanic *matt- ('dull, curdled').
↓
Middle English (c. 1350–1400)
motley / motlee
"parti-colored woollen cloth, the multicolored garb of a jester"
↓
Early Modern English (Back-Formation) (c. 1580–1600)
mottle (verb & noun)
"to mark with different colors; a blotch or spot"
Note: Formed as a back-formation from motley, treating the -ley ending as a diminutive or adjectival suffix.
↓
Early Modern English (Adjective Formation) (c. 1610–1630)
mottled (participial adjective)
"variegated with spots or blotches of diverse colors"
↓
Modern English (present)
MOTTLED
