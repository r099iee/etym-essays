Stage 1 — PIE *op- "to work, be busy, produce in abundance; hence to have power and plenty" (Pokorny *op-; Sanskrit ápas "work, sacred act", ápnas "possessions, property"). Stage 2 — Latin ops, opis (f.) "power, might, plenty, wealth, resources" (also the goddess Ops, wife of Saturn, honoured at the Opalia; classical). Stage 3 — Latin opulentus "rich, wealthy, powerful, abundant" (op- + -ulentus "full of", the suffix of violentus, turbulentus, succulentus; classical: Cicero, Livy). Stage 4 — Latin opulentia "wealth, abundance, magnificence" (abstract noun; classical: Livy, Pliny). Stage 5 — Old French opulence "wealth" (transmitted to English via the learned French channel; 12th–14th centuries). Stage 6 — English opulence "wealth, affluence" (16th century, with the adjective opulent; Shakespeare uses opulent in the early 17th century). FINAL NODE: OPULENCE. SIBLINGS from the same root: Latin opus "work" → English operate, opera, opus; Latin copia "abundance" → English copious, copy, cornucopia; Latin officium (via opifex "work-maker") → English office — the same root as this batch's OFFICE card.
