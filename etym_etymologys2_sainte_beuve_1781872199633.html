The surname SAINTE-BEUVE is a French compound of two independent elements; each has its own chain. CHAIN 1 (SAINTE-): PIE *seh₂k- (often written *sak-) 'to make sacred, to sanctify; to make a treaty' (reconstructed) → Latin sacer 'sacred, holy; also accursed in ritual sense' (attested in early Latin inscriptions, c. 6th–3rd c. BCE) → Latin sancīre 'to make sacred, to ratify, to ordain' (attested from the 2nd c. BCE) → Latin sanctus 'holy, consecrated' (past participle; attested classical Latin) → Late Latin / Ecclesiastical Latin Sanctus 'holy one, saint' (title of holy persons in Christian usage, c. 2nd–4th c. CE) → Old French saint / seinte 'holy; saint' (feminine sainte; attested from c. 9th c.) → French Sainte- 'Saint-' (feminine attributive prefix in compounds and hagiotoponyms) → SAINTE in SAINTE-BEUVE. CHAIN 2 (BEUVE): West Germanic given name *Būbo / *Bōvo 'boy, youth' (byname from a stem meaning 'boy, young man'; cf. Old High German buobo 'boy'; reconstructed within early Germanic name-stock) → Old High German Bovo / Buobo / Bobbo 'Bovo' (attested given names, c. 8th–10th c.) → Medieval Latin Bovo / Boba 'Bova' (Latinized saint's and noble name; cult of Sainte Beuve / Saint Bova in Frankish lands, attested medieval) → Old French Beuve 'Bovo' (given and saint name; e.g., the romance Beuve de Hantone / Bevis of Hampton, c. 12th–13th c.) → French surname element Beuve (fossilized in the compound) → compound hereditary surname SAINTE-BEUVE → Charles Augustin Sainte-Beuve (1804–1869), the critic → SAINTE-BEUVE. Anti-merge note: The two halves belong to two independent lines—PIE *seh₂k- (Sainte) and West Germanic Bovo (Beuve)—joined only at the level of French compound naming; they must not be nested under a single fake master root. Folk etymology deriving Beuve from a word for 'brook/stream' is not the mainstream onomastic account for this name.
