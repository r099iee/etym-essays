• Duty: Similar because both denote an obligation of service; differs because duty is a moral or legal obligation felt inwardly, without the institutional pomp or ceremonial charge of office.
  • Task: Similar because both denote a piece of imposed work; differs because a task has no ceremonial colour — it is simply something to do, not a solemn charge.
  • Job: Similar because both can denote employment; differs because a job is paid employment, flatly secular, lacking the formal and ceremonial senses of office.
  • Function: Similar because both name a thing's prescribed activity; differs because function is mechanical and neutral, whereas office implies a formal, often hereditary or appointed charge.
  • Role: Similar because both are parts one performs; differs because a role is exchangeable and theatrical, while office carries the weight of a real institutional appointment.
  • Post / Position: Similar because both name a slot within an organisation; differs because post and position are the empty slot, while office includes the duty, dignity and ceremony attached to it.
  • Charge: Similar because both denote a responsibility given to someone; differs because a charge is more personal and can be informal, while office is institutional and formal.
  • Station: Similar because both denote a place or rank one occupies; differs because station stresses social rank or physical place, not the active duty performed.
  • Ministry: Similar because both denote service, especially religious; differs because ministry is the special term for Christian religious service, narrower and more sacred than office.
  • Service: Similar because both can mean an act done for another; differs because service is general and can be commercial or menial, while office implies a prescribed, dignified charge.
  • Ceremony / Rite: Similar because both are the public forms performed; differs because they are the forms themselves, while office is the prescribed duty of performing them — the office is what the officiant does, the rite is what is done.
  • Bureau: Similar because both can name a workplace or department; differs because bureau is the French-loan word for a writing desk, a government department, or an office staff, without the liturgical or duty senses.
