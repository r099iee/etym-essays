• New France (Nouvelle-France): Similar because both refer to the vast North American colonial empire claimed by the Kingdom of France between the sixteenth and eighteenth centuries; differs because New France was the overarching administrative realm that encompassed Canada (Quebec), Acadia, Hudson Bay, and Louisiana, whereas Louisiana specifically designated the southern and central Mississippi River watershed.
  • Lower Mississippi Valley: Similar because both describe the geographic, alluvial basin encompassing southern Louisiana, Mississippi, eastern Arkansas, and western Tennessee; differs because Lower Mississippi Valley is a strictly physiographic and geological term describing the river floodplains, while Louisiana is a legal, political, and cultural entity with defined boundaries.
  • Acadiana: Similar because both denote the heartland of French-descended culture in the American South; differs because Acadiana (also known as Cajun Country) is a distinct twenty-two-parish cultural and geographical subregion of southwestern Louisiana officially designated by the state legislature, whereas Louisiana encompasses the entire sovereign state.
  • The Pelican State: Similar because both refer to the identical political entity of the State of Louisiana; differs because The Pelican State is the state’s official nickname and rhetorical moniker, inspired by the brown pelican depicted on the state flag and seal as a historic Christian and heraldic symbol of maternal self-sacrifice.
  • Deep South: Similar because both describe the subtropical, culturally conservative region of the southern United States bordering the Gulf Coast; differs because the Deep South is an expansive sociocultural region encompassing Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi, South Carolina, and Louisiana, whereas Louisiana is unique within the region for its French civil law traditions and Catholic heritage.
  • Gulf Coast: Similar because Louisiana forms a central component of this southern maritime zone; differs because Gulf Coast is a broad geographic and economic designation covering the entire coastal perimeter of the Gulf of Mexico (including Texas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, and Florida).
  • The Bayou State: Similar because both identify the identical state territory; differs because The Bayou State is an informal, popular sobriquet celebrating the state’s thousands of miles of slow-moving waterways and wetland bayou ecosystems.
