• Enrage: Similar because both mean to make extremely angry; differs because enrage is slightly more literary/formal and describes being roused to rage without the specific “fury” intensity, while infuriate links directly to fury and often implies white-hot, explosive anger.
  • Madden: Similar because both mean to drive someone to an extreme state of anger; differs because madden emphasizes pushing someone toward madness and loss of reason, whereas infuriate emphasizes filling with fury while staying within rage rather than insanity.
  • Anger: Similar because both mean to provoke anger; differs because anger (the verb) is the neutral general term for making someone angry, while infuriate denotes the highest degree of that anger.
  • Incense: Similar because both mean to rouse to strong anger; differs because incense is more formal and suggests a slow, smouldering build-up, while infuriate suggests sudden explosive fury.
  • Outrage: Similar because both mean to provoke strong anger; differs because outrage emphasizes anger at a moral wrong or insult and can also be a noun meaning a shocking act, while infuriate emphasizes the emotional state produced in a person.
  • Provoke: Similar because both involve causing someone to react angrily; differs because provoke is broader — it can mean to stimulate any reaction (thought, laughter, an animal to attack) and often implies deliberate baiting, while infuriate names the specific extreme anger produced.
  • Gall: Similar because both mean to irritate deeply; differs because gall means to chafe or irritate persistently (like a sore spot) and is milder, while infuriate is far stronger and more explosive.
  • Exasperate: Similar because both mean to annoy intensely; differs because exasperate means to make someone irritable beyond patience (exasperation), a frustrated impatience, whereas infuriate produces violent rage.
  • Rile: Similar because both mean to make angry; differs because rile is informal and means to irritate or stir up moderately (also “to rile up” a crowd), while infuriate is standard and describes extreme anger.
  • Vex: Similar because both mean to trouble with anger; differs because vex is much milder and often means to distress or annoy with persistent small troubles, while infuriate is at the opposite, maximum end of the anger scale.
  • Antagonize: Similar because both mean to provoke hostility; differs because antagonize means to make someone an enemy or opponent (creating opposition), while infuriate means to make them furiously angry regardless of enmity.
