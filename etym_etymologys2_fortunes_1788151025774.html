Note on nuance: Latin fors derives from the root *bher- "to carry, to bring": fortune is literally "that which is brought to one." The nominative singular fors was already rare in classical prose (surviving chiefly in forsitan, "perhaps," literally "it may be by chance"); the living paradigm ran through the oblique cases. The noun fortūna is securely derived from fors, and its connection to the cult of the goddess Fortuna (Praeneste, Rome) is not seriously disputed; the plural "fortunes" in the sense "one’s lot" is attested from Latin fortūnae onward.
