Pre-Indo-Aryan Substrate / Dravidian (c. 2000 BCE)
*kaṇṭ- / *kaṇṭu
"joint, knot, bulb, stem of reed or cane"
[Substrate origin per Mayrhofer (EWAia); cf. Proto-Dravidian *kaṇ- / *kaṇṭu "joint, node of bamboo"]
↓
Vedic Sanskrit (c. 1200 BCE)
kāṇḍá (काण्ड)
"single joint of a plant, stalk, reed internode, branch"
[Attested in Atharvaveda and Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa; neuter/masculine noun]
↓
Late Vedic & Ritual Prose (c. 800 BCE)
kāṇḍa (काण्ड)
"section, portion, ritual codex, division of text"
[Applied to structural parts of the Taittirīya Saṃhitā]
↓
Epic Sanskrit (c. 4th c. BCE)
kāṇḍa (काण्ड)
"major book, principal canto, narrative epoch"
[Canonical divisions of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa into 7 major books]
↓
19th c. English Borrowing (c. 1800 CE)
kanda / kāṇḍa
"book or primary division of a Sanskrit epic"
[Adopted into English literary and Indological scholarship]
↓
Modern English (present)
kāṇḍas
"major canonical books of Sanskrit epics"
[Anglicized plural with suffix -s, retaining formal IAST orthography]
