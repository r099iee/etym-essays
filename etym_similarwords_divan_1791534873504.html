• Sofa: Similar because both refer to upholstered seating furniture designed for accommodating multiple persons; differs because a sofa typically features a structured backrest and raised arms, whereas a traditional divan is low, backless, armless, and designed to rest directly against a wall or serve as a daybed.
  • Couch: Similar because both denote comfortable furniture intended for reclining, lounging, or sitting; differs because couch is a broad, informal generic term that usually implies an upholstered frame with back and arms, while a divan carries specific historical, orientalist, and minimalist architectural connotations.
  • Daybed: Similar because both function interchangeably as seating by day and a sleeping surface by night without an upright sofa back; differs because a daybed often has headboards or side rails resembling a twin bed, while a divan originated as a continuous cushioned bench platform along palace walls.
  • Ottoman: Similar because both are upholstered furniture pieces of Turkish and Middle Eastern origin introduced to Western living rooms during the orientalist craze; differs because an ottoman is primarily a padded footstool or low seat without a back or sides used as an accessory, whereas a divan is a primary lounging couch or bed base.
  • Chaise longue: Similar because both are designed for reclining in relaxed leisure; differs because a chaise longue is an asymmetrical elongated chair supporting outstretched legs with an elevated headrest/back, whereas a divan provides a flat, symmetrical horizontal mattress-like surface.
  • Settee: Similar because both are compact indoor seating options for more than one person; differs because a settee is traditionally an upright, formal wooden-framed bench with a slender upholstered back and arms, offering far less reclining comfort than a plush divan.
  • Canapé: Similar because both designate elegant upholstered couches in aristocratic interiors; differs because a canapé is an ornate, French rococo or neoclassical carved-wood sofa with distinctive exposed legs and padded backrests, whereas a divan emphasizes plush Middle Eastern drapery and minimalist boxy framing.
