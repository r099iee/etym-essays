- c. 1300 - Entered Middle English (*poken*) from Middle Dutch/Low German, meaning to thrust, prod with a pointed stick, or jab a fire.
- 16th c. - Expanded into investigative senses, describing probing around or *poking one's nose* into another's affairs.
- 17th–18th c. - Developed intransitive senses of physical protrusion (*poking out*) and dawdling movement (*poking along*).
- 19th c. - Popularized in Victorian colloquialisms like *poking fun* at someone's eccentricities.
- 20th–21st c. - Adopted in digital communications (the Facebook *poke*) as a virtual ping, while retaining its universal physical meaning.
