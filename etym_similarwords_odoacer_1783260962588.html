• Theodoric: Similar because both are Germanic rulers of Italy in the same generation and both stand at the end of the Western Empire; differs because Theodoric was the Ostrogoth who invaded in 489, besieged, and finally assassinated Odoacer in 493, becoming the second barbarian king of Italy.
  • Alaric: Similar because both are Germanic chieftains whose acts decisively wounded the Western Roman Empire; differs because Alaric sacked Rome in 410 and died soon after without founding a regime, whereas Odoacer deposed the emperor in 476 and ruled Italy for seventeen years.
  • Ricimer: Similar because both were Germanic generals who dominated Italy in the late fifth century as kingmakers; differs because Ricimer ruled behind puppet emperors from 456 to 472 without ever taking the throne, whereas Odoacer abolished the Western imperial line and ruled openly as king.
  • Attila: Similar because both are feared barbarian leaders whose names close the book on Roman antiquity; differs because Attila was the Hunnic conqueror who raided but never ruled a successor-state, while Odoacer established the first Germanic kingdom of Italy.
  • Gaiseric: Similar because both are fifth-century Germanic kings who dismantled Roman power in the West; differs because Gaiseric was the Vandal king of North Africa who sacked Rome in 455 and built an enduring maritime kingdom, whereas Odoacer ruled Italy and ended the Western line.
  • Euric: Similar because both are fifth-century Germanic kings who ruled over former Roman provinces; differs because Euric was the Visigothic king of Aquitaine who issued a famous law code, while Odoacer kept Roman administration in place rather than legislating a Gothic one.
  • Totila: Similar because both are barbarian kings of Italy with names fixed in the historical record; differs because Totila was the sixth-century Ostrogothic king who fought Belisarius in the Gothic War, a century after Odoacer, and is paired with him in the source as a documented historical figure.
  • Romulus Augustulus: Similar because the two names are inseparable in the story of 476; differs because Romulus Augustulus was the last Western Roman Emperor, a boy deposed and pensioned off, whereas Odoacer was the man who deposed him and took power.
  • Usurper: Similar because Odoacer seized effective power without legal Roman title; differs because usurper implies an illegal claimant within a system, whereas Odoacer abolished the Western emperorship itself and was recognized as king.
  • Kingmaker: Similar because Odoacer's army made and unmade the last Western rulers; differs because kingmaker normally stays behind the throne, while Odoacer took the throne for himself.
  • Federate chieftain: Similar because Odoacer began as a commander of Roman federate troops; differs because federate chieftain names the subordinate office of a client ally, while Odoacer is the man who turned that office into kingship of Italy.
