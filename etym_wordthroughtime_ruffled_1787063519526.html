- [c. 1300–1400: Middle English 'ruffelen'] - Originally meant to disorder, tangle, bungle, or disturb the smooth surface of cloth or hair.
- [c. 1500–1600: Elizabethan Era] - Evolved to describe decorative gathered fabric or collars ('ruffs'); simultaneously acquired the sense of stirring leaves, plumage, or water.
- [c. 1600–1750: Early Modern English] - Developed the idiomatic psychological sense 'to ruffle someone's feathers', meaning to irritate, offend, or upset someone's pride.
- [c. 1800–Present: Modern English] - Extensively used across both tactile/sensory domains (rustling dry leaves, disturbing water) and emotional domains (ruffled composure, unruffled calm).
