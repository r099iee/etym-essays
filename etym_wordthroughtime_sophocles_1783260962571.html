- Classical Athens - Sophocles (c. 497-406 BC), the most successful of the three great tragedians with some twenty-four festival victories, perfected tragedy by adding the third actor and turning it toward the individual hero and his catastrophe.
- Ancient critical tradition - Aristotle made his Oedipus Rex the model tragedy in the Poetics, and through antiquity his name stood for the very summit of Greek dramatic art.
- Modern canon - Sophocles remains central to the Western dramatic canon, and Freud's naming of the Oedipus complex after his hero kept his name in general currency in the twentieth century.
- Contemporary sense - Sophocles denotes the highest reach of Greek genius in Macaulay's triad of masterpieces, the tragedian of fate and the heroic self whose plays still survive and are staged.
