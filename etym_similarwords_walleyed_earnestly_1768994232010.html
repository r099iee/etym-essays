• squint-eyed: Similar in naming a misaligned or crookedly directed eye; differs because squint-eyed suggests the eyes narrowed or drawn together with suspicion or effort, while walleyed is the slack, outward-turned (or pale) gaze.
  • boss-eyed: Similar in being an old, blunt term for a skew or cross-glancing eye; differs because boss-eyed carries a comic, contemptuous edge and usually an inward cast, whereas walleyed names outward divergence with detached, almost clinical neutrality.
  • cross-eyed: Similar in naming a misdirected gaze; differs because the cross-eye turns inward toward the nose (esotropia), the opposite direction from the walleye's outward turn.
  • goggle-eyed: Similar in describing striking, wide eyes; differs because goggle-eyed means protruding, bulging, staring-wide eyes, the energetic opposite of the walleye's slack, wandering divergence.
  • bug-eyed: Similar in being a coarse, descriptive term for a striking eye; differs because bug-eyed pictures bulging, popping eyes of alarm or absurdity, not the pale or outward-turned walleye.
  • exotropic: Similar in denoting the outward-turned eye; differs because exotropic is the modern clinical adjective, coldly diagnostic, where walleyed is the old pictorial folk word.
  • glassy-eyed: Similar in describing a blank, unfocused gaze; differs because glassy-eyed emphasises the fixed, empty, deadened stare, while walleyed keeps the anatomical sense of divergence or pallor.
  • far-sighted: Similar in being a word about how the eye looks or works; differs because far-sighted (hyperopic) is a purely optical condition of focusing, not a skew or pale eye.
  • seriously: Similar to "earnestly" in expressing serious intent; differs because seriously is the plain everyday adverb, while earnestly adds gravity, urgency, and heartfelt conviction.
  • sincerely: Similar in expressing genuine, true feeling; differs because sincerely stresses truthfulness of intention, whereas earnestly stresses the intensity and solemnity with which the intention is held.
  • wholeheartedly: Similar in denoting full, committed intent; differs because wholeheartedly implies warm, unreserved emotional investment, while earnestly can be grave, controlled, and duty-bound.
  • zealously: Similar in describing intense, committed action; differs because zealously implies fervour, often religious or partisan, that can overheat, whereas earnestly is steady, sober conviction.
  • gravely: Similar in describing a serious manner; differs because gravely points to the sombre, weighty tone of the manner, while earnestly points to the sincerity and intensity of the intent.
  • devoutly: Similar in describing sincere, intense feeling; differs because devoutly is bound to religious or reverential feeling, while earnestly is secular and general.
  • fervently: Similar in denoting ardent feeling; differs because fervently runs hot with emotion, while earnestly stays cooler, rooted in seriousness rather than heat.
  • solemnly: Similar in describing a grave, weighty manner; differs because solemnly emphasises ritual dignity and formality, while earnestly emphasises personal conviction and seriousness.
  • intently: Similar in describing concentrated attention; differs because intently focuses on the closeness of the watching or listening, while earnestly focuses on the sincerity and seriousness of the purpose.
