Root 1: MAY
↓
PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*magh-
"to be able, have power"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*maganą
"to be able, avail"
↓
Old English (8th c.)
magan
"to be able, can"
↓
Modern English
may
"be able; be possible"

Root 2: HAP
↓
PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*kob-
"to fit, suit; good fortune"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*hampą
"chance, good luck"
↓
Old Norse (9th–13th c.)
happ
"good luck, chance"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
hap
"chance, fortune, luck"

Compound: MAY + HAP
↓
Modern English (16th c.)
mayhap
"perhaps" (from "may hap" = it may happen)
↓
Modern English
mayhaps
"perhaps, maybe"

Note: mayhaps is a fanciful variant of mayhap formed with an adverbial -s (after the pattern of "always", "needs"); it is characteristic of modern fantasy dialogue and was popularized by the A Song of Ice and Fire novels.
