• Hamburg: Similar because both are prominent northern German port city-states and foundational members of the Hanseatic League; differs because Hamburg is situated on the Elbe River, possesses a far larger global container port, and operates as a single unified city-state rather than a two-city enclave state.
  • Lübeck: Similar because both were influential Hanseatic trade centers celebrated for medieval brick Gothic architecture and merchant autonomy; differs because Lübeck was the historical 'Queen of the Hanseatic League' located on the Baltic Sea rather than the North Sea maritime trade network.
  • Rostock: Similar because both are northern German maritime trading ports with deep Hanseatic roots; differs because Rostock is situated in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern along the Baltic coast and historically held a distinct regional identity.
  • Antwerp: Similar because both are major historical European riverine trading ports with extensive global shipping routes; differs because Antwerp is located in Flanders (Belgium) on the Scheldt River and was a Roman Catholic/Spanish Netherlands economic metropolis.
  • Hull: Similar because both are strategic North Sea merchant ports with centuries of reciprocal maritime trade; differs because Hull (Kingston upon Hull) is located in East Yorkshire, England, and functioned as the English counterpart receiving continental goods.
