• Red-eyed: Similar because both describe eyes strained or irritated by lack of sleep, tears, or fatigue; differs because red-eyed focuses specifically on visible vascular bloodshot sclera (and is famously used in "red-eye flight"), whereas bleary-eyed captures the watery, rheumy, unfocused blurriness and general cognitive fog.
  • Groggy: Similar because both convey a dazed, sluggish state following sleep or intoxication; differs because groggy describes whole-body physical unsteadiness and mental disorientation (historically derived from drinking navy grog), whereas bleary-eyed specifically highlights the ocular and visual expression of exhaustion.
  • Sleepy-eyed: Similar because both denote the appearance of tiredness; differs because sleepy-eyed is gentle, mild, or even endearing, suggesting heavy, relaxed, drooping lids, whereas bleary-eyed conveys active irritation, crustiness, dishevelment, or harsh post-exertion fatigue.
  • Haggard: Similar because both reflect the toll of severe fatigue on facial appearance; differs because haggard emphasizes gaunt, hollow, emaciated features and prolonged trauma or illness (derived from wild falconry), whereas bleary-eyed is localized to ocular puffiness, blurriness, and acute morning grogginess.
  • Glassy-eyed: Similar because both describe an abnormal, unfocused eye appearance; differs because glassy-eyed conveys a fixed, unblinking, vacant, or lifeless stare (often resulting from shock, trauma, severe illness, or narcotics), while bleary-eyed implies watery, squinting, fluttering, or crusty eyelids.
  • Eyestrained: Similar because both describe ocular discomfort from overuse; differs because eyestrained is a clinical, technical term for optical muscle fatigue caused by prolonged reading or screens, whereas bleary-eyed encompasses weeping, hangovers, sleep deprivation, and newly awoken disorientation.
  • Heavy-lidded: Similar because both describe drooping eyelids associated with sleepiness; differs because heavy-lidded often carries a sensual, languid, or aristocratic aesthetic in literature, whereas bleary-eyed is unglamorous, realistic, and messy.
