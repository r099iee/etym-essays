• Van Gogh (Vincent van Gogh): Similar because both were monumental Post-Impressionist titans who moved beyond Impressionism to forge deeply personal, expressive visual languages in the south of France; differs because Van Gogh's art was propelled by raw emotional intensity, spiritual urgency, and swirling, impasto brushwork, whereas Cézanne's art was characterized by disciplined structural analysis, chromatic modulation, and geometric restraint.
  • Gauguin (Paul Gauguin): Similar because both broke away from Parisian salon orthodoxies to pioneer Post-Impressionist synthetics and bold structural compositions; differs because Gauguin pursued exotic symbolism, flat decorative cloisonnism, and mythic escapism in Brittany and Tahiti, whereas Cézanne remained anchored in the empirical observation of nature, still lifes, and the landscape of Aix-en-Provence.
  • Seurat (Georges Seurat): Similar because both painters sought to rescue Impressionism from fleeting formlessness by introducing scientific, methodical, and architectural structure to modern painting; differs because Seurat developed Pointillism and Divisionism based on rigid optical color theory and dot application, whereas Cézanne relied on intuitive structural planes and modulated directional brushstrokes.
  • Monet (Claude Monet): Similar because Cézanne initially exhibited alongside Monet and learned from his plein-air technique and pure chromatic light; differs because Monet dedicated himself to capturing transient atmospheric light and dissolving surfaces, famously prompting Cézanne's remark: "Monet is only an eye, but good Lord, what an eye!" while Cézanne sought timeless, enduring solidity.
  • Picasso (Pablo Picasso): Similar because both revolutionized the trajectory of 20th-century Western art through spatial deconstruction and radical perspectives; differs because Picasso was the mercurial inventor who built upon Cézanne's geometric breakthroughs to launch Cubism, collage, and surrealist experimentation across seven decades.
  • Post-Impressionist: Similar because Cézanne is universally classified as one of the defining masters of the Post-Impressionist era; differs because Post-Impressionist is a broad stylistic umbrella coined by Roger Fry to encompass diverse late-19th-century movements, whereas Cézanne identifies the specific historical master and his unique method of planar realization.
