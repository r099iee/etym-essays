Pre-Greek substrate toponym *kar- "point, peak" (suggested, debated) + characteristic Pre-Greek suffix *-nthos — the Aegean substrate element also seen in Zakynthos, Tiryns, Labýrinthos ↓ Ancient Greek Κόρινθος (Kórinthos) — Corinth, the wealthy isthmian city-state (from the 8th c. BCE) whose port shipped small sweet seedless dried grapes ↓ Latin Corinthus — Corinth (the city, under Roman rule from 146 BCE) ↓ Old French / Anglo-French "raisins de Corinthe" — "raisins of Corinth," the medieval trade designation for the dried grapes ↓ Anglo-French reisin de Corauntz / Middle English raysyns of Coraunte, corans — "raisins of Corinth" (late 14th c.; the French form progressively shortened) ↓ Shortened commodity name corantes / currants — "the Corinths," dropping "raisins of" (c. 1500); the final -s of the French place-form was reanalyzed as an English plural, so a singular currant was back-formed ↓ English currant — the small dried seedless Corinth/Zante grape used in cookery; from the 1570s also transferred to the fresh tart berries of genus Ribes (blackcurrant, redcurrant, whitecurrant) DEBATED / NUANCED: The city-name Korinthos is widely held to be Pre-Greek (non-Indo-European substrate), so no PIE root for the fruit exists; the *kar- "point, peak" element is a suggestion, not a certainty. The homophone current (flowing) is etymologically unrelated — do not merge the fruit-name with any PIE "run" root.
