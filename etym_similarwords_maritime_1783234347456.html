• Naval: Similar because both relate to the sea and sea-power; differs because naval narrows to fleets, warships, and sea-fighting, while a maritime power may dominate through trade without a great battle-fleet.
  • Marine: Similar because it is the Latin cousin meaning "of the sea"; differs because it now attaches to ships' equipment, the merchant fleet, or the Marines (and names a soldier), lacking the geopolitical and legal breadth of maritime.
  • Nautical: Similar because it relates to the sea; differs because it is technical and craft-specific (seamanship, charts, the nautical mile), never the grandeur of nations or law.
  • Seafaring: Similar because it describes activity at sea; differs because it names the act of voyaging, not the structural fact that a whole state is organised around the sea.
  • Oceanic: Similar because it means "of the sea"; differs because it refers to the deep open ocean and its natural features, not human shipping, trade, or law.
  • Pelagic: Similar because it relates to the open sea; differs because it is a technical word for ocean waters and their life (fish, birds), with no institutional or commercial meaning.
  • Coastal: Similar because it relates to the sea and land; differs because it denotes the land strip bordering the sea, with none of the shipping, naval, or legal content of maritime.
  • Aquatic: Similar because it means "of water"; differs because it covers all water (fresh and salt, plant and animal life), not specifically the sea and its human activities.
  • Amphibious: Similar because it relates to sea and land; differs because it names operations or creatures that work on both land and water, not a state's sea-orientation.
