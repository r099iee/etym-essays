• Clearing: Similar because both are open spaces in a wood; differs because a clearing is the plain, functional word for any open patch, natural or man-made, carrying no suggestion of light, while a glade is specifically a bright, often grassy, sunlit opening.
  • Dell: Similar because both are small, secluded, pleasant places in or near woodland; differs because a dell is a small tree-lined valley or hollow, a depression in the ground, whereas a glade is a level open space where light falls.
  • Grove: Similar because both belong to the vocabulary of wooded places; differs because a grove is a small stand or cluster of trees — the opposite of the empty, open glade.
  • Meadow: Similar because both are open, grassy, often lovely places; differs because a meadow is open grassland, usually outside the wood and used for pasture or hay, while a glade is an opening enclosed by trees within the forest.
  • Lawn: Similar because both are level open grassy ground; differs because a lawn is a cultivated, mown space around a building, while a glade is a wild, natural opening in woodland.
  • Glen: Similar because both are pleasant open places in the landscape; differs because a glen is a narrow, often steep-sided valley (Scottish), with streams, while a glade is a level clearing in a wood.
  • Opening: Similar because both name a gap or clear space among trees; differs because an opening is the neutral, general word, while glade adds the qualities of brightness, grass and poetic appeal.
  • Oasis: Similar because both are welcome, refreshing open places surrounded by harshness; differs because an oasis is a fertile spot in a desert, while a glade is a bright opening in a forest.
  • Haven: Similar because both are places of safety and rest; differs because a haven is a harbour or refuge of any kind, while a glade is specifically the bright forest opening that offers such refuge.
  • Sward: Similar because both denote stretches of grass; differs because a sward is simply an expanse of grassy turf, without glade's woodland setting and luminous quality.
  • Copse / Spinney: Similar because both are small woodland features in the landscape; differ because they name small woods or thickets (the trees), while a glade is the open space among them.
