- 17th-century usage: described a sharp, rough, or sour manner of speech and behaviour - read as outright rudeness and carried strong public disapproval
- 19th–20th century: shifted toward marking blunt, abrupt delivery - censured less as malice than as impatience, coldness, and a lack of social polish
- Contemporary sense: blunt, offhand, and sharply unceremonious speech - still mildly negative, but now evoking prickly efficiency and snappy impatience rather than genuine hostility
