• Biting: Similar because both describe sharp, cutting pain, cold, or criticism; differs because biting suggests teeth or an edge that seizes and cuts, while stinging suggests a needle-point that burns and throbs.
  • Burning: Similar because both describe the hot, smarting quality of a sting; differs because burning implies sustained heat and wide pain, while stinging is localised, prickling, and brief.
  • Sharp: Similar because both name pointed, well-defined pain or language; differs because sharp is the generic quality adjective, lacking the animal-injury image and the specific burning prickle.
  • Smarting: Similar because both describe the painful throbbing of a fresh wound; differs because smarting is passive (the pain you feel), while stinging is active and causative (the thing that wounds).
  • Prickling / pricking: Similar because both describe the sensation of many tiny points like needles or thorns; differs because pricking is lighter and more superficial, while stinging implies venom or burning depth.
  • Piercing: Similar because both describe penetration by a sharp point; differs because piercing implies going through something and can be neutral or even elegant (a piercing cry), while stinging is painful and venomous by default.
  • Cutting: Similar because both describe painful cold, wind, or criticism; differs because cutting implies a blade's clean edge and is also positive in tailoring, while stinging is always the wound itself.
  • Searing / scorching / scalding: Similar because all describe burning, punishing heat or words; differ because they imply dry or wet heat of high intensity over a larger area, while stinging is the sharp needle-point of heat rather than the mass of it.
  • Caustic: Similar because caustic wit corrodes as a stinging chemical burns; differs because caustic is intellectual and scientific (a strong alkali), applied chiefly to speech, while stinging is bodily first.
  • Acrid / acerbic: Similar because both describe a sharp, irritating quality assaulting the senses (smoke, taste) or the temper (acerbic remarks); differ because they are about irritant sharpness of smell or manner, not pain.
  • Scathing: Similar because both describe devastating criticism; differs because scathing implies cruelty laid on thickly (it scorches everything), while stinging suggests a single, surgically painful hit.
  • Withering: Similar because withering criticism destroys a target's confidence like a blight; differs because withering is dry, annihilating, and prolonged, while stinging is quick, sharp, and local.
  • Barbed: Similar because a barbed remark has a hook that snags the target, as a sting does; differs because barbed stresses the retained hook (the words keep hurting), while stinging stresses the sudden strike.
  • Venomous: Similar because both derive from poison-injected wounds; differs because venomous is literal about animals and metaphorical about malice (venomous = intending harm), while stinging can be honest or incidental.
  • Pungent: Similar because both describe a sharp assault on the senses; differs because pungent is chiefly smell and taste (a pungent odour), while stinging is physical pain and speech, not flavour.
