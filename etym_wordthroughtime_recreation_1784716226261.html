- 14th-15th century - 'recreation' enters English meaning the refreshment and restoration of body or spirit after weariness — a renewing, literally a re-creating of strength; more a state of being restored than a named activity.
- 16th-17th century - the word shifts toward activity: recreation becomes the pleasant pursuit itself that restores — pastimes, games, sport; moralists endorse it so long as it refreshes rather than wastes, keeping its medicinal logic.
- 18th-19th century - with the growth of leisure and polite society, recreation is institutionalized as wholesome amusement, distinct from mere idleness; the 'rational recreation' movement ties it to self-improvement and temperance.
- 20th century - mass leisure turns recreation into an industry and a bureaucracy: recreation centers, recreational sports; it becomes the standard administrative word for play and relaxation, and the old sense of 'rebuilding strength' survives only as etymology.
