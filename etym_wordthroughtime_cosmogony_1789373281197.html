- [Ancient Greece (5th c. BCE–1st c. CE)] - Formed as kosmogonía among pre-Socratic thinkers and mythographers to categorize philosophical and mythological explanations of the world's birth out of Chaos - [connotation: sacred metaphysics, mythic physics, foundational natural philosophy]
- [17th c.–18th c.] - Borrowed into English during the Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment, oscillating between theological creation treatises and early mechanical theories of planetary formation (Newton, Kant) - [connotation: scholarly debate, bridging scripture with mechanical physics]
- [19th c.–Mid-20th c.] - Established in modern astrophysics, geology, and physical chemistry as the scientific study of stellar, planetary, and galactic origins (nebular hypothesis, Big Bang theory) - [connotation: rigorous scientific astrophysics, evolutionary astronomy]
- [Late 20th c.–Present] - Widely employed in literary criticism, anthropology, and world-building to describe any sprawling, self-contained fictional or cultural mythos that generates an entire symbolic reality - [connotation: totalizing aesthetic architecture, profound world-building, cultural epic]
