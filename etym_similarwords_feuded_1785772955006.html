• Quarreled: Similar because it is also a past verb of interpersonal conflict with a person or group; differs because quarreling may be brief, spontaneous, and reconcilable, whereas feuding implies deep, prolonged, often identity-defining hostility that resists reconciliation.
  • Clashed: Similar because it names sharp mutual conflict between parties; differs because a clash highlights a discrete collision (ideas, armies, personalities) rather than the multi-episode, serial, long-term structure of a feud.
  • Bickered: Similar because it describes ongoing low-level friction between people; differs because bickering is petty, trivial, and low-stakes, whereas feuding carries malice, honor, or power and feels historically or publicly serious.
  • Rivaled: Similar because it expresses sustained opposition between peers over time; differs because rivalry can be respectful, even friendly, competition without hatred, whereas feuded adds bitter affect and often deliberate harm to reputation or interest.
  • Warred: Similar because both suggest extreme, long-lasting, mutual conflict; differs because warred often implies formal or large-scale armed struggle between polities, whereas feuded classically names private or factional vengeance systems (even when the modern "weapons" are media campaigns).
  • Fought: Similar because it is a general verb of struggling or combat against an opponent; differs because fought can describe a single battle, a boxing match, or a debate, while feuded necessarily implies repeated, long-running hostility rather than one contest.
  • Contended: Similar because it means to compete or struggle against opposition; differs because contending is frequently a legitimate or impersonal competition (contend for a prize), while feuding is personal, bitter, and carries lasting enmity.
  • Squabbled: Similar because it names noisy, petty disagreement between parties; differs because squabbling is over trifles and is easily settled, whereas feuding involves deep stakes and a refusal to let the matter drop.
  • Spat: Similar because it is a short, angry exchange with another person; differs because a spat is momentary and minor (a lovers' spat), the polar opposite of the years-long severity that feuded conveys.
  • Tussled: Similar because it denotes a physical or figurative struggle with an opponent; differs because a tussle is a brief, close-quarters scramble, lacking the institutional memory and hereditary vengeance of a feud.
  • Wrangled: Similar because it describes an extended dispute or argument; differs because wrangling centers on loud verbal argument and often has a winner, whereas feuding is a state of ongoing enmity that may never be resolved by argument alone.
  • Vendetta (noun): Similar because it denotes a long, bitter feud of revenge between families or groups; differs because vendetta foregrounds revenge-taking and is a noun, while feuded is a verb reporting that two parties actively carried on the hostility.
  • Enmity / Hostility (nouns): Similar because they name the state of active dislike and opposition that a feud embodies; differs because they are static nouns for an attitude, whereas feuded is a dynamic verb asserting repeated mutual action over time.
