profusely is an adverb meaning "to a great degree, in large amounts, lavishly, or abundantly", and it almost always describes an outflow — of words, money, emotion, blood, sweat, or thanks — that is generous to the point of overflowing. The word is built on the metaphor of liquid being poured forth without restraint: its Latin ancestor profusus is the past participle of profundere "to pour out, pour forth," a compound of pro- "forward, forth" and fundere "to pour." To do something profusely is therefore, at the root, to do it as though pouring it out in an unstoppable stream.

In the supplied context. "Yu-cun made an elaborate bow to his patron and thanked him profusely." The adverb captures the overflowing, unstoppable character of the gratitude: the elaborate bow and the copious expressions of thanks make the appreciation visible and almost liquid in its abundance. Profusely here conveys not mere politeness but a gushing, almost excessive, and reverent outpouring of thanks to a benefactor — precisely the kind of social display expected in a culture where rank and obligation are deeply felt.

Range of uses (polysemy and collocation).

  • With verbs of giving or producing: spend profusely, give profusely, sweat profusely, bleed profusely — emphasis on quantity and the inability to stop.
  • With verbs of emotion and speech: apologize profusely, thank profusely, praise profusely — emphasis on excess, abundance, or extravagance of expression.
  • With verbs of growth: plants grow profusely — meaning lushly, densely, abundantly.
  • As intensifier of adjectives: profusely grateful, profusely decorated — meaning extremely, lavishly.
  • Related adjective: profuse "pouring forth freely; lavish, extravagant" (e.g., profuse apologies, profuse perspiration).

Register and nuance. Profusely is a formal-to-neutral adverb, slightly literary, and it nearly always implies an overflow that goes beyond what is strictly necessary — it can be flattering ("thanked him profusely," suggesting sincerity and warmth) or mildly negative ("he sweated profusely," "apologized profusely," suggesting excess or desperation). Unlike "abundantly," which is a neutral statement of plentiful supply, profusely keeps the dynamic image of pouring forth; and unlike "lavishly," which suggests the deliberate spending of money, profusely can apply equally to blood, sweat, tears, words, and thanks.

Usage notes. The adverb almost never modifies static states — one is not "profusely happy" or "profusely wealthy" in normal usage — because the word requires an action, substance, or expression that can be conceived of as pouring out. It collocates with verbs of emission, giving, and speaking, and with adjectives of manner. The image of an overwhelmed source — a fountain, a wound, a person of gushing emotion — is what unites all its uses.

Metaphoric shift and semantic bridge. The bridge from the physical to the figurative is the oldest in the word's history: because liquids pour and overflow, Latin profusus could already describe both actual liquids (profusus sanguis, "poured-out blood") and abstract abundance (profusa largitas, "lavish generosity"). English inherited this double capacity whole. A person apologizes profusely exactly as a wound bleeds profusely: the emotion is conceived as a substance that cannot be held in, spilling past all social or physical barriers. This is why the adverb works so naturally with expressions of gratitude and regret — both are feelings that "pour out" of a person — and it explains the particular dignity of the reading context, where Yu-cun's elaborate bow and overflowing thanks are presented as a single, unstoppable act of reverence toward his patron.

Distinctions worth remembering. Profusely is never used of a single, controlled act (one does not "profusely say hello"), and it never carries the connotation of craftsmanship or choice that "lavishly" does. It is the adverb of sheer, gushing quantity, and whenever it appears it asks the reader to imagine a flow without a tap — an outpouring that only stops when the source is empty.
