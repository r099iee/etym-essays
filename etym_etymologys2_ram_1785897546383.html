↓ Proto-Germanic (c. 1st c. BCE–5th c. CE)
*rammaz
"male sheep"
↓ Proto-West Germanic (c. 1st–8th c. CE)
*ramm
"male sheep"
↓ Old English (c. 8th–9th c.)
ramm
"male sheep; battering ram"
↓ Middle English (12th–15th c.)
ram
"male sheep; battering device"
↓ English (present)
RAM
"adult male sheep; heavy battering device"
Debate: no secure PIE etymon is established for *rammaz;
a link to a Germanic base meaning "strong, fierce" (cf. Old Norse
rammr) has been proposed. The verb to ram "to strike
violently" (14th c.) was formed from the noun, and the battering-ram
sense is already Old English, rendering Latin aries.
