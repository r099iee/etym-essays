Note: The formation of peruse is an Anglo-French or hybrid English development combining the Latin intensive prefix per- (‘thoroughly, completely’) with the verb use (from Old French user, Latin ūti). A long-standing historical debate among nineteenth-century philologists (such as Skeat) pondered whether the word might be connected to Anglo-Norman *perreuser or Latin pervidēre (‘to survey’), but the Oxford English Dictionary and modern etymological consensus firmly trace its earliest attested meaning (c. 1475) to the literal sense of ‘using up completely’ or ‘going through’ items in sequence before shifting metaphorically to examining texts.
