• Hippety-hop / hippity-hop: Similar because it is the closest reduplicative motion-mimic for rabbit-like hopping; differs because it is more openly playful and bouncy, whereas Potter's lippity is cautious, soft, and deliberately "not very fast."
  • Tiptoe: Similar because both suggest quiet, careful movement; differs because tiptoe implies human bipedal walking on the toes, not the springy four-legged hop of a rabbit.
  • Patter: Similar because it names light, rapid footfalls; differs because patter suggests many quick taps (rain, footsteps, applause) with no vertical bounce, unlike the repeated hops of lippity.
  • Pit-a-pat / pitter-patter: Similar because both are reduplicative sound-mimics of light rhythmic beats; differs because they imitate a heartbeat or quick footsteps rather than a hopping gait.
  • Scamper: Similar because it describes quick, light movement; differs because scamper is faster, more panicked, and less rhythmic, while lippity is slow, careful, and steady.
  • Scurry: Similar because it suggests hurried, light movement; differs because scurrying is flustered and rapid, with no musical bounce and no suggestion of hopping.
  • Hop: Similar because both denote leaping movement of a small animal; differs because hop is a plain, general verb, while lippity carries the added onomatopoeic and emotional coloring of gentle, rhythmic sound.
  • Skip: Similar because both imply light, springy, repeated movement; differs because skipping is a cheerful human gait, while lippity is the wary, exploratory hop of a frightened rabbit.
  • Bound / leap: Similar because both are jumping movements; differs because they are vigorous and single, covering distance, while lippity is small, repeated, and unhurried.
