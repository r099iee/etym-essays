NOTE — DEBATED / NUANCED STAGES: (1) The splash sense (14th c., "water lapping a shore") is genuinely debated: the standard view (OED, Etymonline) treats it as a transfer from the drinking sense — water makes the sound of an animal drinking — but an alternative view treats it as independently imitative (lap, slap, slop, splash, slurp form a sound-symbolic cluster). Both views are stated; no stage is guessed. (2) The unrelated homonym lap "fold, overlap" (Old English læppa, from Proto-Germanic *lappan- "a rag, flap") is the source of the racing lap and "lapped in luxury"; it is a different word that shares the form, and is noted here to prevent confusion.
