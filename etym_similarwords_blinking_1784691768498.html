• Bloody: Similar because both are British intensives placed before nouns to express irritation; differs because bloody is a genuine oath, much stronger and historically taboo, while blinking is a deliberate euphemism, mild and printable.
  • Blooming: Similar because both are alliterative B-word euphemisms for bloody; differs because blooming is gentler and often almost affectionate, while blinking is more exasperated and comic.
  • Blithering: Similar because both are B-word intensives used of a person or thing; differs because blithering adds the sense of foolishness ("a blithering idiot"), while blinking merely intensifies without insulting the object's intelligence.
  • Blessed: Similar because both are mild substitutes for a stronger oath; differs because blessed is a religious euphemism ("blessed nuisance"), while blinking is a physiological one.
  • Damned: Similar because both intensify a following noun ("a damned nuisance"); differs because damned is a real profanity with religious force, while blinking is comic and harmless.
  • Confounded: Similar because both are mild, old-fashioned intensives of annoyance; differs because confounded is more formal and upper-class in flavour, while blinking is earthy and working-class.
  • Ruddy: Similar because both are British euphemistic intensives standing in for bloody; differs because ruddy alliterates more directly with bloody and leans rustic, while blinking leans comic and urban.
  • Winking: Similar because both describe rapid eye movement; differs because winking is a deliberate, often conspiratorial closure of one eye, while blinking is the reflex closure of both.
  • Flickering: Similar because both describe light that goes on and off intermittently; differs because flickering is the standard neutral word for unsteady light, while blinking (of light) pictures a rhythm more like the eye's.
  • Twinkling: Similar because both describe small intermittent flashes of light (a star can twinkle or blink); differs because twinkling is pretty and gentle, while blinking is more mechanical and regular.
  • Flashing: Similar because both describe light pulsing on and off; differs because flashing is faster, brighter, and more alarming, while blinking is gentler and more rhythmical.
  • Nictating: Similar because both describe the act of blinking the eye; differs because nictating is the clinical/technical term (nictitating membrane), while blinking is the plain everyday word.
