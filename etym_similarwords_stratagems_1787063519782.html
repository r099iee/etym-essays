• strategy: Similar because both derive from the same Greek word for generalship and both belong to military planning; differs because strategy is the overall long-term plan of a campaign, while a stratagem is one specific cunning trick or feint within it.
  • tactics: Similar because both concern the concrete manoeuvres of war; differs because tactics are the orderly arrangements and movements of forces in battle, while stratagems are deceptive schemes designed to trick the enemy.
  • scheme: Similar because both mean a planned course of action, often secret or self-serving; differs because scheme is broader and can be innocent or neutral (a pension scheme), while stratagem always implies cunning and deception.
  • ploy: Similar because both denote a cunning manoeuvre to gain an advantage; differs because ploy is informal, everyday, and often small-scale (a parent's ploy), while stratagem is formal and grand, rooted in warfare.
  • ruse: Similar because both mean a deceptive trick; differs because ruse emphasises the false appearance or deception itself, while stratagem emphasises the plan and generalship behind the deception.
  • trick: Similar because both are clever deceptions; differs because trick is general and can be playful or childish (card tricks, dog tricks), while stratagem is grave, strategic, and military in flavour.
  • artifice: Similar because both mean a skilful, crafty device; differs because artifice stresses skill and contrivance and can be neutral (artful construction), while stratagem stresses the deceptive purpose in a contest.
  • subterfuge: Similar because both mean deception to escape or gain advantage; differs because subterfuge is sneaky concealment, often to evade blame, while stratagem is a bold, strategic scheme, often secretly admired.
  • manoeuvre: Similar because both are planned, skilful moves; differs because a manoeuvre need not involve deceit (a tactical movement of troops) and is used of politics and driving, while a stratagem is inherently a cunning trick.
  • gambit: Similar because both are opening moves designed to gain advantage; differs because a gambit originally means sacrificing a chess piece and is used of calculated opening moves, while a stratagem covers the whole art of deceptive planning.
  • feint: Similar because both are deceptive moves in combat; differs because a feint is a sham attack to draw the guard — a single physical fake — while a stratagem can be any elaborate trick of war.
  • wile: Similar because both are cunning devices; differs because wile (often plural wiles) suggests charming, seductive cunning, while stratagem suggests cool military planning.
