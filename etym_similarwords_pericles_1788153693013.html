• Augustus: Similar because both names label a golden age of art, peace, and empire (the Periclean and Augustan ages are the two classical 'good emperors' of culture); differs because Augustus founded an imperial monarchy while Pericles championed radical democracy, and Augustus' age followed civil war while Pericles' preceded it.
  • Lorenzo de' Medici ('the Magnificent'): Similar because Pater himself pairs them — both presided over the most brilliant cultural generation of their cities (Florence's Quattrocento); differs because Lorenzo ruled a court and a banking family as patron-prince, Pericles governed a democratic assembly and built with public treasure.
  • Marcus Aurelius: Similar because both are the philosophical torches of their ages, remembered more for mind than for conquest; differs because Aurelius was a Stoic emperor writing meditations for himself, Pericles a speaking statesman whose power was the public word.
  • Themistocles (Athenian rival-generation): Similar because both were the great Athenian strategoi of the fifth century, and Themistocles shares the -kles 'glory' name-pattern; differs because Themistocles won the empire's foundation at Salamis and died in exile, while Pericles consolidated and then overreached.
  • Cleon: Similar as Pericles' Athenian successor in democratic leadership and the only comparable orator of the Peloponnesian War; differs because Cleon is the demagogue's type, ridiculed by Aristophanes and denounced by Thucydides — the dark mirror of Periclean eloquence.
  • Heracles: Similar because Heracles is the most famous bearer of the -kles glory-element (Herakles, 'glory of Hera'); differs in kind — a hero of myth and muscle, not a historical statesman.
  • Solon: Similar because both are canonical Athenian lawgiver-statesmen whose names label constitutional change; differs because Solon founded the institutions (the 'shaking off of burdens') two centuries earlier, while Pericles lived them to their imperial extreme.
