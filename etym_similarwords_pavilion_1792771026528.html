• marquee: Similar because both refer to very large, temporary canvas or fabric structures erected outdoors for festive gatherings, banquets, and summer receptions; differs because a marquee is primarily a modern, functional British event-hire term for a large utility tent, whereas pavilion carries an exalted chivalric, architectural, and historical elegance, and can also denote a permanent ornamental building or sports clubhouse.
  • tent: Similar because both are portable shelters made of flexible fabric stretched over a framework of poles and held by guy ropes; differs because a tent is a generic, utilitarian term covering everything from a minimalist one-person mountaineering bivouac to military shelters, whereas a pavilion denotes an expansive, ornate, celebratory, or royal fabric palace.
  • canopy: Similar because both provide overhead cloth shelter and shade for dignitaries or public gatherings; differs because a canopy is often a suspended overhead fabric roof without enclosed side walls (such as a bed canopy or throne baldachin), whereas a pavilion is an enclosed or semi-enclosed three-dimensional pavilion structure with substantial structural footprint.
  • gazebo: Similar because both represent light, ornamental garden structures designed to provide outdoor shelter and commanding landscape views; differs because a gazebo is almost invariably a small, permanent, open-sided hexagonal or octagonal wooden turret built in a domestic garden, whereas a pavilion is vastly larger, capable of housing banquets, assemblies, or sports teams.
  • kiosk: Similar because both trace historically to light, open architectural garden follies borrowed from Middle Eastern and European palace grounds; differs because a modern kiosk has shrunk into a small commercial booth or electronic terminal selling newspapers, tickets, or food, while pavilion retains its grandeur.
  • pergola: Similar because both are garden landscape features enhancing outdoor leisure; differs because a pergola is an open framework of wooden beams and cross-rafters designed to support climbing vines without providing complete weatherproof shelter, whereas a pavilion has a solid weatherproof roof or canvas ceiling.
  • rotunda: Similar because both can function as standalone decorative park or palace buildings; differs because a rotunda is strictly a circular building crowned with a classical dome and supported by stone columns, whereas a pavilion can take any shape and often features pitched roofs, verandas, or fabric canopies.
  • awning: Similar because both utilize heavy-duty fabric or canvas to provide shade from sun and shelter from rain; differs because an awning is a modest secondary roof projection attached directly to the exterior wall of a building above a shopfront window or terrace, lacking independent structural enclosure.
