• Perish: Similar because both mean to cease living, especially on a large or pitiless scale; differs because perish is sterner and more utter — perishing of cold, perishable goods — while die is the plain universal word for the event itself.
  • Expire: Similar because both name the ending of life, breath, or validity; differs because expire is formal and clinical — expiring in hospital, an expired lease — while die is direct and at home in every register.
  • Pass away: Similar because both describe the death of a person; differs because pass away is the gentle euphemism chosen for mourners, while die states the bare fact without cushioning.
  • Decease: Similar because both denote death, chiefly in records and notices; differs because decease is legal and official — the deceased, decease duties — while die belongs to speech, story, and pulpit.
  • Succumb: Similar because both end in death after struggle or illness; differs because succumb stresses yielding — succumbing to wounds, to temptation — while die names only the outcome, whatever the resistance.
  • Fade: Similar because both describe a gradual dying — fading light, dying applause; differs because fade is slow diminishment that may stop short of extinction, while die completes the passage out of being.
  • Dice: Similar because a die is literally one of the dice, the same cube in singular dress; differs because dice is the everyday plural and collective — a pair of dice — while die the singular survives in proverbs and workshops.
  • Cube: Similar because a gaming die is a small marked cube of bone or plastic; differs because cube is pure geometry — sugar cubes, ice cubes — while die adds the pips, the throw, and the stake.
  • Stamp: Similar because a coining die is an engraved stamp that impresses its image; differs because stamp is the general tool and act — postage stamps, rubber stamps — while die is the hardened steel instrument of the mint and the machine-shop.
  • Mould: Similar because both shape material — the die strikes, the mould casts; differs because a mould receives molten matter poured in, while a die strikes or cuts cold metal with force.
