- 15th c. entry - Middle English propicious, from Old French propicieux and Latin propitius: "favorably disposed," said properly of a deity or power above one.
- 16th-17th c. religious and augural use - The word thrives in prayer and theology: propitiation, the sacrifice that makes an offended God favorable again; fortune read in the omens of birds.
- 18th-19th c. secularisation - Broadens to mere circumstances and timing - a propitious moment or season - losing the divine agent but keeping the promise of success.
- Contemporary register - Formal, dignified, faintly dated in speech; still carries its Roman augural scent of a power actively flying forward to meet one's hopes.
