- Late 16th to Early 17th Century - Enters Early Modern English as *inaugurate* / *inaugurating*, strictly referring to the formal religious and ceremonial investiture of kings, bishops, and high civic magistrates with auspicious rites.
- Mid-17th to 18th Century - Broadens in neoclassical literature and civic governance to include the formal opening or dedication of important public buildings, monuments, and academic universities.
- 19th Century - Undergoes extensive metaphorical secularization in the Victorian era, widely used by historians, critics, and scientists to characterize events, inventions, or discoveries that launch major historical, industrial, or scientific eras (e.g., inaugurating the steam railway network).
- Early to Mid-20th Century - Flourishes in modernist literary criticism, aesthetics, and political philosophy to describe revolutionary artistic breakthroughs (e.g., Proust inaugurating new novelistic sensibilities) and geopolitical orders (e.g., inaugurating the United Nations).
- Late 20th Century to Present - Maintains high ceremonial dignity in constitutional politics (presidential inaugurations) while functioning in everyday media, business, and technology to denote the ambitious unveiling of digital platforms, space missions, and architectural landmarks.
