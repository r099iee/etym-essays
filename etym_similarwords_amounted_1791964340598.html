• totalled: Similar because it reports a final sum; differs because it is strictly arithmetical and transitive, with no evaluative sense of “was equivalent to”.
  • came to: Similar because it is the closest everyday equivalent for a bill or a sum; differs because it is colloquial and ambiguous — “he came to” also means he regained consciousness.
  • added up to: Similar because it describes parts accumulating into a total, and idiomatically into a significance; differs because it stresses the counting of individual items and belongs to informal speech.
  • was tantamount to: Similar because it is the exact evaluative equivalent, and it descends from the very same French verb (tant amunter, “to amount to as much”); differs because it is stronger and more formal, typically implying a serious or reprehensible equivalence.
  • constituted: Similar because it asserts what something was in effect; differs because it is legal and philosophical in register and names a formal status rather than a quantity.
  • represented: Similar because it can mean “stood for” or “equalled in significance”; differs because it suggests a symbol, proportion or sample rather than a total.
  • resulted in: Similar because it can describe how something turned out; differs because it foregrounds cause and consequence, not equivalence.
  • boiled down to: Similar because it reduces a matter to its effective core; differs because it is colloquial and faintly dismissive.
  • signified: Similar because it can mean “meant” or “indicated”; differs because it is semantic rather than quantitative and carries no idea of a sum.
