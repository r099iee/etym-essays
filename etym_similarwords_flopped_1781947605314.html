• Collapse: Similar because it also means to fall down suddenly, often from exhaustion; differs because it is more clinical and dramatic, suggesting a structure or a person giving way under strain, while a flop is a loose, cushioned, even comic surrender.
  • Slump: Similar because it means to fall or sink heavily; differs because it implies a gradual sagging into a sitting posture with bowed head, while flopped is a sudden, total, often horizontal drop.
  • Plop: Similar because it is an echoic verb of a soft object falling; differs because it stresses a small, light sound (often into water), while flop is flatter, heavier, and limper.
  • Sprawl: Similar because it describes collapsing with limbs spread; differs because it emphasises limbs flung at angles, whereas a flop is a more compact all-at-once giving-way.
  • Drop: Similar because it is the general verb of letting fall or falling; differs because it is neutral and colourless, with none of the limp, heavy, comic weight of flop.
  • Plump: Similar because it means to sit or fall down heavily and abruptly; differs because it is more archaic and dialectal, and lacks the sense of a completely inert, unresisting body.
  • Tumble: Similar because it is a sudden fall; differs because it implies rolling or loss of balance with possible rotation, whereas a flop falls flat without rolling.
  • Fail: Similar in the metaphoric theatre sense, where a show "flops"; differs because fail is the broad neutral term for non-success, while flop keeps the image of a physical collapse.
  • Keel over: Similar because it is a sudden heavy fall; differs because it suggests fainting or death and carries alarm, while flop is safe, cushioned, and comic.
  • Wilt: Similar because it conveys loss of vigour; differs because it describes a slow drooping (of plants or energy), not a sudden heavy collapse.
