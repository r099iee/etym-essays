• Astute: Similar because both mean quick, penetrating judgment that turns perception to advantage; differs because astute stresses intellectual quickness and the spotting of opportunity, while shrewd adds practical experience and a hint of cool, calculating self-interest.
  • Canny: Similar because both mean cleverly wary and knowing; differs because canny (chiefly British and Scottish) is shrewdness tempered by caution and economy — a thrifty wariness of being cheated — while shrewd is broader and more assertive.
  • Sagacious: Similar because both mean wise and discerning; differs because sagacious suggests profound, weighty, often philosophical wisdom, while shrewd is faster, more street-level, and more worldly.
  • Acute: Similar because both mean sharpness of mind; differs because acute can be momentary or even physical (acute hearing) and lacks the seasoned, practical knowingness of shrewd.
  • Sharp: Similar because both mean quick-witted and penetrating; differs because sharp is purely intellectual quickness and may be cutting, while shrewd implies practical, worldly judgment.
  • Perceptive: Similar because both mean quick to notice things; differs because perceptive names the simple act of accurate noticing and omits the practical know-how and hard edge of shrewdness.
  • Insightful: Similar because both mean seeing into the truth of things; differs because insightful is warm and sympathetic, while shrewd is cool, worldly, and slightly calculating.
  • Clever: Similar because both mean mentally agile; differs because clever is intellectual agility without judgment, while shrewd adds the weighing of men and situations.
  • Crafty: Similar because both mean knowing and adroit; differs because crafty stresses trickery and deception, while shrewd is honourable in its best uses and only slides toward craftiness at its edge.
  • Sly: Similar because both mean secretively knowing; differs because sly connotes deceit and underhandedness, whereas shrewd is open, sharp-eyed judgment.
