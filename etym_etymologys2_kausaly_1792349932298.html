Sanskrit (grammatical vṛddhi derivation + patronymic suffix -ya)
कौसल्य (Kausalya)
"belonging to, or hailing from, the land of Kosala"
↓
Epic Sanskrit (c. 5th c. BCE; Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa; feminine ending -ā)
Kausalyā
"the Princess of Kosala; senior queen of Daśaratha and mother of Rāma"

Note on grammatical derivation: In Pāṇinian Sanskrit grammar (Aṣṭādhyāyī), the name Kausalyā (कौसल्या) is formed by regular vṛddhi lengthening of the initial vowel of the toponym Kosala (from short/guṇa o to the diphthong au), followed by the taddhita patronymic/gentilic suffix -ya (-yañ), and the feminine nominalizing marker -ṭāp (-ā). Morphologically, it is an exact parallel to other epic royal gentilics such as Mādrī ("Princess of Madra"), Gāndhārī ("Princess of Gāndhāra"), and Vaidehī ("Princess of Videha"). In ancient Indian courtly practice, princesses were frequently addressed by their regional gentilic rather than their personal birth name to honor the political alliance between royal houses.
