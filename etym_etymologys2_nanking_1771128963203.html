Two etymological chains are required, one for each root morpheme of the compound 南京 (Nánjīng): 南 (nán, "south") and 京 (jīng, "capital"). The final English form "Nanking" combines both chains.

Chain for 南 (nán), "south":

  • Proto-Sino-Tibetan: no securely reconstructed ancestor for "south" is agreed upon; the deeper pre-Chinese history of 南 is unknown and debated, and some scholars have proposed links to broader Tibeto-Burman directional vocabulary without consensus. The first fully attested stage is Old Chinese.
  • Old Chinese 南, reconstructed *nˤəm (Baxter–Sagart) or *nəm (Schuessler), "south; southern": attested in Shang dynasty oracle-bone inscriptions (c. 1200–1046 BCE) among the four cardinal directions.
  • Middle Chinese 南, *nom (Qieyun, 601 CE), "south": the standard reading recorded in the Sui-era rhyme dictionary.
  • Modern Mandarin 南, nán, "south": the form used in contemporary standard Chinese.
  • Final node: NAN- (south) in NANKING.

Chain for 京 (jīng), "capital":

  • Old Chinese 京, reconstructed *kraŋ (Baxter–Sagart) or *k(r)jang (older scholarship), "capital city; artificial mound; high place": attested in Western Zhou bronze inscriptions (c. 1046–771 BCE) as the seat of a ruler.
  • Middle Chinese 京, *kjæŋ (Qieyun, 601 CE), "capital city": the standard reading codified in the rhyme dictionary.
  • Modern Mandarin 京, jīng, "capital": as in Běijīng "Northern Capital" and Jīngdū "capital city".
  • Final node: -KING (capital) in NANKING, the English -k reflecting older and regional pronunciations of the syllable that modern pinyin writes jing.

Compound and English borrowing:

  • Chinese compound 南京 Nánjīng "Southern Capital": formed from the two roots above; applied to the city as the southern counterpart of 北京 Běijīng "Northern Capital".
  • English "Nanking": adapted from the compound through older European romanization systems (16th–19th century), with the final -k of the older/dialectal pronunciation of 京 preserved in spelling; stabilized in English usage through documents such as the Treaty of Nanking (1842).
  • Final node: NANKING.

Debate note: The exact Old Chinese reconstruction of both characters is debated among historical linguists (Baxter–Sagart versus Schuessler and others), and the pre-Old-Chinese etymology of 南 and 京 within Sino-Tibetan is not securely established; what is attested with confidence is the Old Chinese, Middle Chinese, and Mandarin sequence given above.
