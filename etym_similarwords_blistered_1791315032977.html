• Scalded: Similar because both describe an organic or inorganic surface painfully damaged, inflamed, or altered by extreme heat; differs because scalded specifically denotes injury inflicted by moist heat, steam, or boiling liquid, whereas blistered emphasizes the physical formation of raised, fluid-filled bubbles or hollow surface pockets.
  • Vesicated: Similar because both refer medically to the eruption of fluid-filled cutaneous bullae upon the epidermis; differs because vesicated is a formal, clinical pathological term used in medical diagnoses or chemical warfare contexts (e.g., vesicant agents), while blistered is a versatile common word spanning domestic, technical, culinary, and figurative registers.
  • Charred: Similar because both characterize surfaces subjected to intense thermal exposure and radiant heat; differs because charred denotes the burning of organic material to black carbon or ash through partial combustion, whereas blistered denotes bubbling, swelling, and epidermal peeling without complete carbonization.
  • Battered: Similar because both describe an object or individual visibly worn down, damaged, and bearing the physical scars of harsh treatment; differs because battered implies repetitive physical blunt force impacts, blows, or weathering, while blistered conveys thermal, frictional, or acute emotional burning and raw sensitivity.
  • Wretched: Similar because when applied to personal appearance in humorous or dramatic contexts, both convey profound misery, dejection, and pathetic affliction; differs because wretched describes an internal state of deep sorrow, degradation, or unworthiness, whereas looking blistered presents a comical visual metaphor of someone physically scorched and inflamed by misfortune.
  • Peeling: Similar because both often occur together on sunburned skin, heated paint, or weathered coatings as layers detach; differs because peeling denotes the subsequent shedding, flaking, or sloughing off of dried surface sheets, whereas blistered denotes the initial swelling, dome-shaped bubbling, and fluid retention beneath the skin.
  • Haggard: Similar because both convey an appearance of being thoroughly worn out, wasted, and distressed by severe suffering; differs because haggard implies a gaunt, hollow-eyed, emaciated countenance resulting from prolonged sleeplessness or fasting, while blistered emphasizes raw, burning, acute disfigurement.
