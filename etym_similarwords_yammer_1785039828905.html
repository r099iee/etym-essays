• Wail: Similar because both are loud, sustained cries of distress; differs because wail is mournful and tuneful, a cry with some melody, while yammer is shapeless, grating, and complaining.
  • Shriek: Similar because both are loud outcries; differs because a shriek is a single, piercing burst, while yammering is a continuing, repetitive clamour.
  • Screech: Similar in harsh, high-pitched loudness; differs because a screech is a sudden, sharp, strident cry, while yammering is drawn-out and persistent.
  • Howl: Similar because both suggest prolonged animal or wind-like sound; differs because a howl is full-throated and mournful, while yammering is thinner, more grating, and more complaining.
  • Yelp: Similar in sharp animal cries; differs because a yelp is short and sharp, while yammering is persistent and drawn-out.
  • Whine: Similar because both are complaining sounds; differs because a whine is thin, nasal, querulous, and low in volume, while yammering is loud and clamorous.
  • Grumble: Similar in expressing complaint; differs because grumble is low and muttered, while yammering is loud and high-pitched.
  • Jabber: Similar because both are rapid, shapeless noise; differs because jabber is rapid chatter that is not necessarily loud, while yammering is emphatically loud and persistent.
  • Chatter: Similar in incessant talk; differs because chatter is light, rapid, often cheerful talk, without yammer's loud, complaining, grating tone.
  • Bleat: Similar in the goblin-chant pairing as a contemptuous word for helpless noise; differs because bleat is the thin, trembling cry of a sheep, while yammer is a loud, shapeless clamour of complaint.
