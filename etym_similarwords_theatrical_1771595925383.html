• Dramatic: Similar because it is of or like performance; differs because dramatic is essentially neutral, even admiring (a dramatic rescue, a dramatic change), whereas theatrical applied to behaviour is almost always censorious.
  • Histrionic: Similar because it means theatrical over-performance; differs because histrionic is the more savage word — hysterical over-acting and emotional excess — while theatrical may stop at mere staging and display, a calculated effect without inner substance.
  • Melodramatic: Similar because it means inflated showiness; differs because melodramatic means overwrought feeling and exaggerated emotion, whereas theatrical need not be emotional at all — it is about the pose and the sense of performing for spectators.
  • Affected: Similar because it is insincere or put-on; differs because affected describes the mannered artificiality itself, while theatrical adds the essential motive — performing to be seen, before an imagined audience.
  • Artificial: Similar because it is false rather than natural; differs because artificial is the broadest term for anything made rather than natural, whereas theatrical names the specific falseness of display and performance.
  • Performative: Similar because it is done for effect; differs because performative is modern and often analytic — a speech-act or gesture that constitutes what it claims — while theatrical is the older, blunter word for showy display.
  • Stagey (stagy): Similar because it means exaggeratedly theatrical; differs because stagey is the colloquial word for behaviour that overdoes the acting, carrying more comic condescension than Ruskin's severe usage.
  • Showy: Similar because it is calculated to attract notice; differs because showy points at the ostentatious display itself, whereas theatrical implies the whole frame of performance — an audience, a role, an applause-seeking motive.
  • Overdone: Similar because it is excessive and artificial; differs because overdone describes the mere excess without the theatre-frame, whereas theatrical makes a systematic criticism of art's relation to truth.
