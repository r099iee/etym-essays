- Old English - 'wearg' (and Old Norse 'vargr'): an outlaw, a wolf, a devourer, literally the 'accursed one' cast out of society; a grim legal and legendary word, never a tame natural-history term.
- 1937 - revived and repurposed by Tolkien in 'The Hobbit' as a capitalized proper noun naming a race of giant, malevolent, quasi-sentient wolves allied with the goblins; the word becomes a species, a nation, an army rather than an animal.
- Mid-20th century - enters the wider fantasy genre through 'The Lord of the Rings'; 'warg' becomes the standard term for the intelligent evil war-beast, distinct from the natural wolf.
- Late 20th-21st century - spreads through role-playing games, wargaming and epic fantasy (and as a verb 'to warg', meaning to enter an animal's mind, in 'A Song of Ice and Fire'); the Old English 'outlaw' sense lives on in scholarship, so the word now carries both an ancient legal register and a modern genre register.
