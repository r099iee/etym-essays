• Mixture: Similar because both name combinations of different elements; differs because a mixture is often blended or stirred together, while an assortment keeps its kinds distinct side by side.
  • Variety: Similar because both promise many different kinds; differs because variety stresses difference itself as a quality, while assortment is the concrete gathered collection.
  • Collection: Similar because both are gatherings of items; differs because a collection is often curated over time with care, while an assortment can be casual, commercial, or accidental.
  • Medley: Similar because both are pleasing jumbles of different things; differs because medley is artistic and lively (songs, dishes), while assortment is the shopkeeper’s orderly spread.
  • Miscellany: Similar because both are mixtures of various kinds; differs because miscellany is literary and often textual (essays, articles), while assortment is commercial and tangible.
  • Jumble: Similar because the source pile is genuinely a jumble of parcels and furniture; differs because jumble stresses disorder, while assortment suggests variety presented for choice.
  • Array: Similar because both present ordered selections for viewing; differs because an array is formally displayed (troops, data), while an assortment is casually gathered goods.
  • Selection: Similar because shops offer selections exactly as they offer assortments; differs because selection implies chosen pickings, while assortment implies the full spread from which to pick.
  • Assemblage: Similar because both are gatherings of disparate parts; differs because assemblage is formal or artistic (sculpture, people), while assortment stays close to goods and sweets.
