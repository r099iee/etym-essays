- 16th c. — noxious creatures: vermin names worms, lice, and harmful beasts gnawing crops and bodies alike.
- 17th–18th c. — country vowels shift it: rural speakers round vermin toward varmint, keeping the broad outdoor sense of raiding beasts.
- 19th c. — boys join the beasts: American and English rustics call trespassing lads young varmints, half threat and half pet name.
- Early 20th c. — the Western fixes it: cowboy fiction and film make varmints of rustlers, coyotes, and villains, drawl included.
- Late 20th c. — sporting goods adopt it: varmint rifles, varmint calls, and varmint hunters turn the insult into a magazine category.
- Today — pest, rascal, and punchline: farmers still curse fox varmints, neighbours tease young varmints, and the word stays folksy, funny, and faintly fierce.
