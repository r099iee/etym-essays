• Springlike: similar (of spring); differs in being a neutral descriptive compound, while vernal is formal and slightly elevated.
  • Springy: a trap — similar in spelling only; differs entirely in meaning (elastic, bouncy); never a synonym.
  • Fresh: similar in the figurative sense (new, vigorous); differs in being generic, while vernal ties freshness to the season and to youth.
  • Youthful: similar (the springtime of life); differs in being about age, while vernal keeps the season metaphor.
  • Budding: similar (in early growth); differs in being concrete botany (or careers), while vernal is the broader season-adjective.
  • Blooming: similar (in flower, flourishing); differs in describing present flowering, while vernal names the season of flowering.
  • Nascent: similar (just beginning); differs in being abstract and technical, while vernal is seasonal and poetic.
  • Primaveral: a rare synonym from Italian (prima vera); differs in being extremely rare and Romance-flavoured.
  • Vernant (archaic): similar (flourishing, verdant); differs in being obsolete or poetic and stressing greenness rather than spring.
  • Equinoctial: related term (pertaining to the equinox); differs in being technical astronomy and covering both equinoxes, not spring alone.
