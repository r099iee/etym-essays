Note on grammatical formation and etymological morphology: Morphologically, Amarāvatī is an exemplary Pāṇinian Sanskrit compound toponym. The base consists of the negative compound amara (अमर), constructed from the privative negative prefix a- (cognate with Greek a- and English un-, from PIE *n̥-) and the verbal root mṛ (मृ, "to die", from PIE *mer-). To this nominal stem is affixed the secondary possessive morpheme -vat (मतुप/वतुप), signifying "possessing" or "abounding in." Because ancient Sanskrit words for cities and capitals (such as purī, nagarī, or rājadhānī) are grammatically feminine, the feminine gender suffix -ī (ṅīp) is appended, creating the feminine proper noun Amarāvatī, which literally translates as "She [the City] that possesses the immortals."
