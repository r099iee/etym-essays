• Murder: Similar because both name the unlawful killing of a human being; differs because murder requires malice aforethought (or extreme recklessness) and carries far greater stigma and penalty, while manslaughter is the mitigated grade without that intent package.
  • Homicide: Similar because both denote a human killing another human; differs because homicide is the neutral umbrella term covering lawful and unlawful killing, not a mitigated criminal charge like manslaughter.
  • Killing: Similar because both name the taking of a life; differs because killing is general and moral-descriptive rather than a technical criminal offense with grades.
  • Slaying: Similar because both denote violent taking of life; differs because slaying is archaic or literary and lacks the precise statutory meaning of manslaughter.
  • Assassination: Similar because both involve wrongful death; differs because assassination implies targeted political or hired killing, not an unpremeditated or negligent slaying.
  • Culpable homicide: Similar because both are graded homicide offenses below murder; differs because culpable homicide is the umbrella statutory term in Scots and some other systems, while manslaughter is the specific offense name in common-law jurisdictions.
  • Butchery / slaughter: Similar because both preserve the violence inside 'manslaughter'; differs because butchery and slaughter describe mass or animal killing without legal grading.
  • Negligent homicide: Similar because both name killings without intent to kill; differs because negligent homicide is the narrower modern statutory label in some US codes, less grave-sounding than the compound manslaughter.
