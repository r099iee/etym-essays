• loaded: Similar because both mean carrying or bearing a load; differs because "loaded" is the everyday word and can be casual or neutral, even figurative in slang ("loaded" = drunk, or wealthy), while "laden" is literary, grave, and passive.
  • weighed down: Similar because both stress the sagging effect of weight; differs because "weighed down" is a phrase emphasizing the downward pull, while "laden" is the simple state of being loaded.
  • burdened: Similar because both mean weighed with a burden; differs because "burdened" is often figurative or emotional (burdened with cares) and implies discomfort, while "laden" can be purely physical.
  • encumbered: Similar because both mean hindered by weight; differs because "encumbered" stresses obstruction of movement or function, not mere heaviness.
  • freighted: Similar because both mean loaded, especially of ships; differs because "freighted" belongs to commercial shipping and, figuratively, to meanings and implications.
  • weighted: Similar because both mean given weight; differs because "weighted" implies a deliberate addition of weight for balance or purpose.
  • charged: Similar because both can mean filled to capacity with something; differs because "charged" is used of electricity, emotion, or official accusation — different load-bearing fields.
  • full: Similar because both mean containing much; differs because "full" is blunt and lacks the dignity and the sense of pressing weight carried by "laden."
  • stuffed / packed: Similar because both mean filled with contents; differs because these are blunter and more informal, without the grave register of "laden."
  • heaped / piled: Similar because both denote goods piled up; differs because "heaped" is about stacking above a surface, "laden" about bearing the pile on a carrier.
  • oppressed: Similar in the figurative sense of being weighed down (by cares or tyranny); differs because "oppressed" is about unjust domination, not carrying goods.
  • loaded down / overloaded: Similar because both suggest a heavy load; differs because "overloaded" implies too much, while "laden" is neutral about whether the load is excessive.
  • ballasted: Similar because both mean weighted for stability; differs because "ballasted" is nautical and technical, about stabilizing a vessel, not bearing cargo.
