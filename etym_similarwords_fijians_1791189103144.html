• iTaukei: Similar because it is the official Indigenous Fijian self-designation; differs because it legally excludes Indo-Fijians and other nationals whom civic Fijians includes.
  • Indo-Fijians: Similar because they are Fiji nationals of Indian descent sharing citizenship and island life; differs because ancestry, language (Fiji Hindi), religion, and land status differ markedly.
  • Melanesians: Similar because Indigenous Fijians are classified as Melanesian peoples; differs because Melanesia is a vast region (PNG, Solomons, Vanuatu, Fiji), not the Fiji-specific identity.
  • Polynesians: Similar because Fiji sits at the Melanesia-Polynesia boundary and shares voyaging heritage; differs because Polynesian languages/cultures (Tonga, Samoa) are distinct, as your passage itself contrasts.
  • Rotumans: Similar because Rotumans are Fiji citizens from Rotuma island with Polynesian affinities; differs because they have separate language, provincial status, and smaller numbers.
  • Tongans: Similar because Tonga is Fiji's closest Polynesian neighbour with deep chiefly intermarriage and the linguistic source of Fiji's English name; differs because Tongans are a separate sovereign kingdom/people.
  • Samoans: Similar because they are fellow Pacific Islanders with comparable chiefly, kava, and rugby cultures; differs because nationality, language, and colonial history are entirely separate.
  • Pacific Islanders: Similar because it is the inclusive regional umbrella covering all Fijians; differs because it is generic and erases Fiji-specific vanua, indenture, and coup history.
  • Fiji nationals / Fiji Islanders: Similar because post-2013 law makes these synonyms for civic Fijians; differs because they are bureaucratic paraphrases avoiding the ethnic ambiguity of bare Fijians.
  • Kanaka (historical): Similar because 19th-century traders called many Melanesian labourers including Fijians Kanakas; differs because Kanaka is now archaic/offensive and centred plantation labour, not citizenship.
