• Conversationalist: Similar because both refer to a person engaged in verbal communication; differs because conversationalist emphasizes someone's skill, charm, or enjoyment of casual social talk (e.g., 'a brilliant conversationalist'), whereas interlocutor is a formal, neutral designation for any active participant in a dialogue or negotiation.
  • Dialogue Partner: Similar because both specify a counterpart in a two-way discussion; differs because dialogue partner is a modern compound phrase, while interlocutor is the formal Latinate single-word noun carrying greater literary, legal, and diplomatic weight.
  • Spokesperson: Similar because both speak on behalf of others; differs because a spokesperson delivers one-way official statements to the press or public, whereas an interlocutor engages in dynamic, two-way interactive exchanges and negotiations.
  • Intermediary: Similar because both can facilitate communication between conflicting parties; differs because an intermediary acts strictly as a neutral middleman or go-between connecting two sides who cannot speak directly, whereas an interlocutor is the actual party doing the speaking and conversing.
  • Respondent: Similar because both participate in question-and-answer interactions; differs because a respondent merely answers questions posed by another (as in a survey, trial, or interview), lacking the fully reciprocal, collaborative conversational role implied by interlocutor.
