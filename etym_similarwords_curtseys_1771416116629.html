• Bows: Similar because both are bodily gestures of greeting and respect performed before a superior; differs because a bow inclines the upper body from the waist on two feet and is used by men and women alike, whereas a curtsey is specifically a woman's bending of the knees with the skirts gathered.
  • Genuflections: Similar because both are deep bodily acts of deference; differs because genuflection drops the performer to one knee and carries liturgical or quasi-sacred weight before an altar, while a curtsey stays social, keeps the body upright, and uses both legs.
  • Obeisances: Similar because both are formal acts of homage and submission; differs because obeisance is the broad umbrella term covering any bow, curtsey, or prostration and is unmarked for gender, while a curtsey is the specific, gender-coded knee-bend.
  • Salutes: Similar because both are conventional gestures of respect; differs because a salute is usually a military or formal hand-and-head gesture, quick and gender-neutral, whereas a curtsey is a feminine full-body dip performed in social or courtly contexts.
  • Kowtows: Similar because both are rituals of deference to rank or authority; differs because a kowtow involves kneeling and touching the forehead to the ground, marking far deeper submission, whereas a curtsey keeps the head up and the body upright, a proud and dignified dip.
  • Bobs: Similar because a bob is a small, quick type of curtsey; differs because a bob is the rapid, almost perfunctory servant's version, while a full curtsey is slow, deep, and reserved for formal greeting.
  • Courtesies: Similar because both name polite behaviour owed to others; differs because courtesies covers all courteous acts, words, and gestures, while a curtsey is the single, specific bodily gesture — the word from which "courtesy" itself was specialized in the 16th century.
  • Curtsies (variant spelling): Similar because both are the very same word in different spellings; differs because curtsy is the modernized spelling and curtsey the older, so the two differ only in orthography, not in sense.
