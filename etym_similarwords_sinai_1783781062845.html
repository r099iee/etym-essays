• Horeb: Similar because Horeb is the alternative biblical name for the same mountain of God, used interchangeably in Exodus and in Deuteronomy; differs because Horeb (preferred by the Elohist source and used for the burning bush in Exodus 3) stresses the desert side of the mount, while Sinai (preferred by the Yahwist and Priestly sources, and used for the lawgiving in Exodus 19–20) stresses the theophany of the Law.
  • Nebo: Similar because Mount Nebo is the other great mountain of Moses, where he viewed the Promised Land and died (Deuteronomy 34); differs because Nebo is the mountain of the view and the death, while Sinai is the mountain of the Law and the covenant.
  • Zion / Sion: Similar because Zion is the other great holy mountain of Scripture, the hill of the sanctuary and the presence of God; differs because Zion is the hill of Jerusalem, the city-mountain of the Temple, while Sinai is the wilderness mountain of the Law far to the south.
  • Carmel: Similar because Mount Carmel is another biblical mountain of theophany and of God's answer (Elijah's contest with the prophets of Baal, 1 Kings 18); differs because Carmel is a headland of northern Israel and the scene of the fire from heaven, not of the Lawgiving.
  • Tabor: Similar because Mount Tabor is another holy mountain of Israel, associated with the transfiguration of Christ and with Deborah's battle; differs because Tabor is in Galilee and carries the New Testament transfiguration, while Sinai carries the Old Covenant lawgiving.
  • Sin (wilderness of Sin): Similar because the wilderness of Sin (Exodus 16:1) lies near Sinai and shares the sacred geography and the manna narrative; differs because Sin is the name of a wilderness region (and possibly of the moon-god), while Sinai is the name of the mountain itself.
  • Paran: Similar because the wilderness of Paran is a neighboring Sinai-region wilderness in the same desert journey; differs because Paran is a stage of the wanderings rather than the mountain of the Law.
