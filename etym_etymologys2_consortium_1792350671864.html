Note on philological evolution: Latin consortium literally signifies "having a common lot or casting of lots together" (com- "together" + sors "lot"). In archaic Roman law, when a paterfamilias died, his heirs remained in a collective partnership of undivided inheritance known as consortium ercto non cito ("partnership with unshared ownership"), where each co-heir had full rights over the undivided estate. From this ancient familial sharing of ancestral fortune, the concept expanded to matrimonial companionship and eventually to modern business syndicates.
