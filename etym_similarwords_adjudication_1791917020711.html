• judgment: Similar because it is the decision produced by a court or a reasoning mind; differs because judgment may be purely mental or advisory, whereas adjudication names the authoritative procedure and its binding outcome.
  • ruling: Similar because both are pronouncements by a judge; differs because a ruling usually decides a single point or motion and may be interlocutory, while adjudication can denote the entire process ending in a final determination.
  • verdict: Similar because both conclude a contested case; differs because a verdict is strictly the jury's (or judge's) finding on the facts, one stage inside an adjudication, not the whole legal act.
  • determination: Similar because both are official decisions; differs because determination is far broader and includes purely administrative or unilateral conclusions with no adversarial hearing.
  • arbitration: Similar because both resolve disputes through a third party who imposes a decision; differs because arbitration is private and consensual, whereas adjudication typically implies a public court or statutory authority.
  • award: Similar because both can allocate money, property, or a prize; differs because an award is the thing granted, while adjudication is the act of deciding that grants it.
  • mediation: Similar because both are third-party dispute resolution; differs because a mediator helps the parties agree and decides nothing, while an adjudicator decides for them.
  • settlement: Similar because both end a dispute; differs because a settlement is agreed by the parties, whereas adjudication is imposed by an authority and may leave one party dissatisfied.
  • sentence: Similar because both are formal judicial acts; differs because a sentence is the penalty imposed after guilt is established, a later stage that presupposes adjudication.
