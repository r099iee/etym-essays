Derivation chain for SLUMPED (single root word): Stage 1 — (No PIE root: the word is of uncertain, probably imitative or sound-symbolic origin, belonging to the family of sl- words such as slip, slop, slush, slosh, and to the -ump words such as bump, thump, lump.) English dialectal slump "to fall or sink heavily, to drop into mud or slush, to stumble" (first attested 17th century, c. 1670s, chiefly in northern English and Scottish dialects). Stage 2 — Standard English slump "to fall or sink suddenly and heavily, losing muscular tension or support" (18th–19th century), generalizing from the dialectal sense to bodies, objects, and structures. Stage 3 — English slump (noun) "a sudden heavy fall; a marked decline," first applied to prices and markets in the 19th century, and to a major economic depression ("the Slump," the Depression) in the 20th century (1930s); also a batting slump in sport.
