- [7th c. CE / Prophetic & Rashidun Era] - adopted in Medina (632 CE) following Muhammad's death as *Khalīfat Rasūl Allāh* ("Successor to the Messenger of God") - [perception: revered as a humble, pious, and consultative steward bound by austerity and moral devotion]
- [8th–10th c. / Umayyad & Early Abbasid Dynasties] - transformed into an imperial dynastic title (*Khalīfat Allāh*, "Deputy of God"), ruling a vast transcontinental empire from Damascus and Baghdad - [perception: viewed as the dazzling, absolute sovereign of the Islamic world, patron of sciences, arts, and military glory]
- [11th–13th c. / Late Abbasid Fragmentation] - stripped of direct temporal and military rule by Buyid and Seljuk sultans, retreating into a sacred pontifical figurehead in Baghdad - [perception: respected as the indispensable source of legal and spiritual legitimacy for regional sultans, without governing power]
- [12th–14th c. / Crusades & Middle English transmission] - borrowed into Old French and Middle English as *caliphe* / *calif* through Crusader chronicles and travelers' accounts - [perception: regarded by medieval Christendom with fearful fascination as the supreme 'pope' or emperor of the Saracens]
- [16th–19th c. / Ottoman & Orientalist Era] - claimed by the Ottoman Sultans in Istanbul; romanticized simultaneously in European literature through *The Arabian Nights* - [perception: viewed by Westerners as an exotic, absolute Oriental potentate; looked to by Muslims under European colonial rule as a protector of global Islamic unity]
- [1924 / Abolition by Atatürk] - formally abolished by the Turkish Republic on March 3, 1924, sparking ideological shock across the Muslim world - [perception: mourned by traditionalists as the tragic end of a 1,300-year-old constitutional institution; celebrated by modern secularists as a leap toward national sovereignty]
- [Late 20th–21st c. / Modern Discourse] - debated intensely in international geopolitics and Islamic political theology - [perception: analyzed in academic scholarship as a historical imperial institution; misappropriated by violent extremist fringe groups, while remembered by mainstream historians as an epochal civilizational legacy]
