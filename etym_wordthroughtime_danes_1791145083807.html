- Migration Age (5th–8th c.) - Dani as distant northern tribe in Gothic and Frankish reports; perception vague-exotic: edge-of-world pagans.
- Viking Age (9th–11th c.) - Dene as terrifying invaders and then rulers (Danelaw, Cnut); connotation fearful-hostile in England, proud-heroic in Denmark.
- High Middle Ages (12th–15th c.) - Danes as historical villains and ancestors (chronicles, Danegeld legends); perception memorial: past scourge turned Christian neighbour kingdom.
- Early modern (16th–18th c.) - Danes as minor Baltic power and traders; connotation neutral-mercantile, Viking past largely forgotten.
- 19th c. Viking revival - Danes romanticised as sea-kings and explorers; perception admiring-nordicist, feeding nationalism and tourism.
- Durant 1930s - Eleventh-century Danes frozen as late savages/cannibals to prove universal primitivism; perception primitivist, now rejected.
- Contemporary - Danes as modern EU citizens (design, bacon, wind power) with Viking heritage as branding; connotation friendly-prosperous, funerals very much known.
