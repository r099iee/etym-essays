• anteroom: Similar because both name the small room standing before a main chamber; differs only in building material — antechamber uses the French-derived chamber and carries a more formal, palatial, period flavor, while anteroom is plainer and more modern.
  • vestibule: Similar because both are transitional spaces preceding a larger area; differs because a vestibule is the entrance lobby just inside an outer door, passage-like and architectural-technical, whereas an antechamber stands before a principal chamber and carries ceremony.
  • lobby: Similar because both are places where people wait before proceeding; differs because a lobby is the modern, public, democratic waiting area of hotels, theaters, and offices, while an antechamber is small, exclusive, and hierarchical, a filter before a great room.
  • foyer: Similar because both are entrance or assembly spaces preceding the main area; differs because a foyer is large, welcoming, and theater-hotel in character, while an antechamber is a small, ceremonious waiting room before a specific chamber.
  • waiting room: Similar because both are rooms where one awaits admission; differs because waiting room is the plain modern term for doctor's, station, or office waiting, losing the architectural precision and courtly hierarchy of antechamber.
  • hall / hallway: Similar because both are spaces connecting and preceding rooms; differs because a hall is a passage for movement or a grand assembly room, while an antechamber is a furnished room for pausing and waiting before a principal chamber.
  • reception room: Similar because both relate to receiving visitors; differs because a reception room is itself a main room for formal entertaining, whereas an antechamber is subordinate and prior, serving only as the threshold.
  • parlor / withdrawing room: Similar because both are formal interior rooms associated with social ritual; differs because these are principal social rooms of a house, not buffer rooms before another chamber.
  • narthex: Similar because both are waiting/entry spaces before a sacred main area; differs because a narthex is specifically the porch of a church before the nave — an ecclesiastical technical term — while an antechamber is secular and palatial.
  • portico: Similar because both are architectural elements of approach; differs because a portico is an exterior colonnaded porch, open to the air, while an antechamber is an enclosed interior room.
