- 2nd millennium BCE — Attested in Hittite cuneiform and Hurrian records as the kingdom of Kizzuwatna, and in early Iron Age Neo-Assyrian inscriptions as Hilakku (the western mountains) and Que (the fertile eastern plain).
- 5th–4th c. BCE — Standardized in Classical Greek historical literature (Herodotus, Xenophon) as Kilikía (Κιλικία), designating the Persian satrapy and the vital strategic pass of the Cilician Gates.
- 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE — Latinized by the Romans as Cilicia, famed in Roman public imagination first as a dangerous nest of Mediterranean pirates and subsequently as Cicero's proconsular province.
- 1st–4th c. CE — Reified across the Greco-Roman world and the early Christian Church as the birthplace and early mission field of the Apostle Paul.
- 11th–14th c. — Known across Medieval Europe as "Little Armenia" or "Cilician Armenia," a vital maritime trading partner, Crusader bastion, and royal kingdom.
- 16th–21st c. — Retains prominent historical, biblical, and geographical usage in English to designate this pivotal ancient crossroad of Mediterranean civilization.
