• Verdigris: Similar in being the green film on aged copper and bronze; differs because verdigris is the actual chemical compound, often treated by conservators as corrosion to be removed, whereas patina is the admired, stable aged surface of which verdigris is only a part.
  • Tarnish: Similar in being a dull discolouring film on metal; differs because tarnish is the thin, dull film on silver or copper that is routinely polished away, while patina is a valued, preservable aged surface.
  • Rust: Similar in being an oxidation film on metal; differs because rust is destructive red-brown corrosion of iron that weakens the metal, whereas patina on bronze or copper is protective and prized.
  • Film: Similar in being a thin layer on a surface; differs because film is the completely neutral, general word for any thin covering, with none of patina's aesthetic and historical value.
  • Coating: Similar in being a surface layer; differs because a coating is applied deliberately by human hands, whereas a patina forms of its own accord through age and oxidization.
  • Bloom: Similar in naming a fine, soft surface deposit; differs because bloom is more general (the bloom of fruit, of a surface) and carries no technical sense of metal ageing.
  • Glaze: Similar in being a glossy surface layer; differs because glaze is a fired or applied vitreous finish, while patina is an acquired, unmanufactured skin of age.
  • Veneer: Similar in being a thin outer layer; differs because veneer is a thin layer added for appearance and carries a hint of falseness, whereas patina is authentic, acquired, and prized.
  • Crust: Similar in being a hardened outer layer; differs because crust suggests hardness and roughness (a crust of dirt), while patina is thin, smooth, and admired.
  • Sheen: Similar in being a soft reflected glow on a surface; differs because sheen is purely visual lustre and may be new or artificial, while patina is a film accumulated over time.
