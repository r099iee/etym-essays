Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*grā-st- / *tḱeh₁-
"to eat, belly" / "skilled, wise"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE)
γαστήρ (gastḗr) + σοφία (sophíā)
"stomach, belly" + "wisdom, philosophy"
↓
Hellenistic Greek (c. 3rd c. BCE)
γαστροσοφία (gastrosophía)
"wisdom of the belly / culinary lore"
↓
French (c. 1820s)
gastrosophie (coined by Charles Fourier)
"philosophy and science of table pleasures"
↓
German (1851)
Gastrosophie (popularized by Eugen von Vaerst)
"doctrine of refined culinary wisdom"
↓
English (c. 1870s)
gastrosophy
"philosophical study of culinary pleasures"
