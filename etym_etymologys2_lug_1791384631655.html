Note: The ultimate Indo-European antiquity of lug is contested among historical linguists. Most authorities connect it to Scandinavian dialectal roots (such as Swedish dialectal lugga, meaning "to pull someone's hair in a fight", and Norwegian lugga), which are cognate with or derived from Proto-Germanic *lūkaną ("to pull, weed out, tear up"). An alternative hypothesis posits an expressive, onomatopoeic creation echoing the grunt and jerking motion of heavy physical tugging. The noun sense "ear" arose in northern Middle English as a figurative application of the part seized when someone was "lugged" in a brawl, which subsequently transferred to vessel handles and engineering flanges.
