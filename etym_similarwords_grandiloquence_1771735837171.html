• Bombast: Similar because both name showy grand language; differs because bombast is inflated, empty, showy speech — sound without substance — and is always a reproach, whereas grandiloquence is grand in scale and dignified in manner, risking but not necessarily committing the sin of pretension.
  • Pomposity: Similar because both involve self-important display; differs because pomposity points to the manner of a person who overestimates his own gravity — the solemnity of the speaker — while grandiloquence concerns the grand style of the language itself.
  • Eloquence: Similar because both name impressive fluent speech; differs because eloquence is unreservedly positive — persuasive and moving, with no hint of excess — whereas grandiloquence carries the risk of overstatement.
  • Magniloquence: Similar because both name elevated speech; differs because magniloquence is the nearest twin and nearly interchangeable, though it can carry a slightly more neutral, purely stylistic sense of speaking grandly.
  • Loquacity: Similar because both derive from the root meaning “to speak” and concern speech; differs because loquacity is sheer talkativeness — quantity of talk, often trivial — with no grandeur at all, while grandiloquence is grand in manner.
  • Fustian: Similar because both name inflated, pretentious language; differs because fustian is coarse and laboriously pompous, heavily condemnatory, while grandiloquence can be light, admired, or ironic depending on context.
  • Rhetoric: Similar because both concern elevated persuasive speech; differs because rhetoric is the neutral art or study of effective speaking, while grandiloquence is a specific grand manner of speech.
  • Grandiosity: Similar because both concern grandness of scale and manner; differs because grandiosity is a quality of persons, plans, or delusions — an inflated sense of importance — while grandiloquence is specifically a manner of speech or writing.
