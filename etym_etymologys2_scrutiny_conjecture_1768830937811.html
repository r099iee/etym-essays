Root 1 — SCRUTINY:

Latin (of uncertain origin)
scrūta
"rubbish, rags, old clothes"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
scrūtārī
"to search, examine"
(lit. 'to search through rags')
↓
Latin (late Latin)
scrūtinium
"an examination, a search"
↓
Medieval Latin (12th c.)
scrutinium
"a close examination"
↓
Modern English (15th c.)
scrutiny
"close examination, critical observation"

Debate: scrūta "rubbish, rags" is of uncertain origin; scrūtārī literally meant "to search through rags".

Root 2 — CONJECTURE:

PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*yeh₁-
"to throw"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
iacere
"to throw, hurl"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
conicere
"to throw together; to guess"
(com- 'together' + iacere 'to throw')
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
coniectūra
"a guess, inference, conjecture"
↓
Old French (13th c.)
conjecture
"conjecture, surmise"
↓
conjecture
"a conclusion based on incomplete information"

Debate: the PIE root *yeh₁- "to throw" for Latin iacere is uncertain and debated.
