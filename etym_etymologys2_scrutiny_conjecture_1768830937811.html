Derivation chain for SCRUTINY (root word one of two): Stage 1 — PIE *(s)ker- "to cut, separate, divide" (reconstructed; pre-4th millennium BCE) — the probable source of the Latin root, via the image of cut-off scraps; the link is plausible but not universally accepted. Stage 2 — Latin scrūta (plural) "rags, trash, refuse, broken odds and ends" (attested Classical Latin) — literally "cut-off scraps," the refuse of the household. Stage 3 — Latin scrūtārī "to rummage through, to search, to sift trash" (attested Classical Latin) — the scavenger's verb, later elevated metaphorically; then Late Latin scrūtinium "a search, a formal examination." Stage 4 — Old French scrutine "a scrutiny, examination" (12th–13th centuries) — borrowed from Late Latin. Stage 5 — Middle English scrutiny / scrutinie "a formal search or examination, especially in canon law" (entered 14th century). Stage 6 — Modern English scrutiny "close, critical, item-by-item examination" ◄ TARGET. Note on the debate: The derivation of Latin scrūta from PIE *(s)ker- "to cut" is probable but not proven; the semantic bridge runs through "cut-off pieces, scraps, refuse," and some scholars treat scrūta as a pre-Latin substratum item. The Latin chain (scrūta → scrūtārī → scrūtinium) and the Old French-to-English chain are certain.
Derivation chain for CONJECTURE (root word two of two): Stage 1 — PIE *yeh₁- "to throw, hurl" (reconstructed; pre-4th millennium BCE) — the root of throwing, casting, and aiming. Stage 2 — Latin iacere "to throw, hurl, cast" (attested Classical Latin) — the plain verb of throwing. Stage 3 — Latin conicere "to throw together, to put together in the mind" (con- "together" + iacere) (attested Classical Latin); noun coniectūra "a casting together, a guess, an inference; also a divination by signs." Stage 4 — Old French conjecture "a guess, an inference" (12th–13th centuries) — borrowed from Latin coniectūra. Stage 5 — Middle English conjecture "a guess, an inference from signs" (entered 14th century). Stage 6 — Modern English conjecture "an opinion or conclusion formed on incomplete evidence" ◄ TARGET. Note: The chain *yeh₁- → iacere → conicere → coniectūra → conjecture is fully secure. No genuine Sanskrit or Germanic cognate of iacere is agreed upon, so those branches are omitted; the Latin stem iac- also underlies English jet, eject, inject, project, and subject, all collateral derivatives of the same root.
