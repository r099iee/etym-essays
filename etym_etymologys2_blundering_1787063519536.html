Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*bʰlendʰ-
"to become turbid, blind, confused; wander"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*blandaną / *blundaz
"to mix, blind, confuse, shut eyes"
↓
Old Norse (c. 900 CE)
blunda
"to shut the eyes, slumber, wander blindly"
↓
Middle English (c. 1325 CE)
blundren / blondren
"to stumble blindly, act foolishly, mix up"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1590 CE)
blunder (verb) + -ing
"moving clumsily; making gross errors"
↓
Modern English (Attested Participle)
blundering
"moving blindly and clumsily; acting ineptly"
