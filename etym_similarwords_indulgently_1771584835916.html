• Leniently: Similar because both mean with relaxed strictness, ready to overlook faults; differs because leniently is formal and administrative — one is lenient with rules, relaxing a punishment because fairness or mercy demands it — and carries no warmth, whereas indulgently is affectionate, personal, and pleasurable, favouring the person rather than the rule.
  • Tolerantly: Similar because both describe putting up with something one might object to; differs because tolerantly implies a stoical forbearance of something disliked, a suffered endurance rather than pleasure, while indulgently means actively taking pleasure in granting and pampering.
  • Permissively: Similar because both describe allowing rather than forbidding; differs because permissively carries a hint of criticism — the laxness of those who cannot say no, often judged in parenting — while indulgently is warm and doting, not reproachful.
  • Forbearingly: Similar because both describe restraint from reproach or punishment; differs because forbearingly stresses self-restraint and patient endurance under provocation, whereas indulgently stresses the affectionate, gratifying readiness to yield to a beloved person.
  • Kindly: Similar because both describe a warm, benevolent manner; differs because kindly is general goodwill without the specific ideas of overlooking faults, granting wishes, or spoiling, and it lacks the faintly patronising pleasure of indulgence.
  • Gently: Similar because both describe a soft, unforceful manner; differs because gently concerns manner of touch, tone, or action itself, without any implication of leniency, concession, or gratifying another's wishes.
  • Spoilingly (informal): Similar because both describe treating someone with excessive, affectionate leniency; differs because spoilingly is colloquial and implies pampering to the point of harm, while indulgently is neutral-to-warm and does not necessarily imply a bad outcome.
  • Magnanimously: Similar because both describe generous forgiveness of faults; differs because magnanimously stresses greatness of spirit, nobleness, and high-mindedness in forgiving, a grander and more impersonal quality, whereas indulgently is intimate, yielding, and full of personal affection.
