- Latin (c. 1st c. BCE – 4th c. CE): ordinare meant to arrange into ranks, put in order, or appoint officials.
- Early Christian Era: Adopted by the Church to denote admitting clergy into the sacred ranks (ordo) and divine predestination of cosmic history.
- Middle English (c. 13th–14th c.): Borrowed from Anglo-Norman as ordeinen, applied both to conferring holy orders and royal legal enactments.
- 16th – 18th Century: Central to Reformation theological debates on priesthood; adopted in constitutional law to signify sovereign establishment of statutes.
- Modern Era: Widely used for formally accredited clergy and figuratively for anyone or anything seemingly appointed by fate or destined by history.
