• Freight: Similar because both name transported goods; differs because "freight" stresses the charge for carriage and is overwhelmingly uncountable and collective ("freight trains"), while "cargoes" are countable shiploads.
  • Goods: Similar because both mean merchandise; differs because "goods" is a general term for commodities that need not be in transit, whereas "cargoes" specifically means loads being transported.
  • Merchandise: Similar because both concern trade commodities; differs because "merchandise" views goods as articles for sale in a shop, not necessarily loaded on a vehicle.
  • Shipments: Similar because both denote goods being transported; differs because a "shipment" focuses on the instance or act of shipping, while "cargoes" focuses on the loads themselves.
  • Loads: Similar because both mean things carried; differs because "loads" is informal and general (a person's burden, a washing-machine load), lacking the commercial and maritime specificity of "cargoes."
  • Consignments: Similar because both are batches of goods entrusted to a carrier; differs because "consignment" is a formal commercial term for a single batch sent to an agent or buyer.
  • Wares: Similar because both are articles of trade; differs because "wares" is archaic or market-flavoured and refers to goods offered for sale rather than loads in transit.
  • Freightage / tonnage: Similar because both quantify goods carried; differs because these are abstract measures or charges (by weight/volume), not the goods themselves.
