- 17th c.–18th c. - Emerged in English pastoral poetry, early veterinary treatises, and natural history chronicles to describe wild stallions, lions, and pastoral herds in romantic landscape depictions.
- 19th c. (Victorian era) - Widely popularized in Victorian nature writing, equestrian prose, and adventure fiction (e.g., descriptions of Russian steppe horses, American mustangs, and African predators), evoking untamed animal vigor and resistance to artificial domestication.
- 20th c.–Present - Retained in literary, historical, and zoological contexts as an evocative sensory epithet conveying unbridled rustic texture, wilderness, and weathered natural beauty.
