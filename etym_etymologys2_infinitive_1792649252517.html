Note on Greek calque and grammatical philosophy: The Latin grammatical term modus infīnītīvus is a direct loan-translation (calque) of the Hellenistic Greek grammatical term énklisis aparémphatos (ἔγκλισις ἀπαρέμφατος), coined by Alexandrian grammarians like Dionysius Thrax (2nd c. BCE). The Greek adjective aparémphatos literally meant "not showing alongside" or "not indicating in addition"—meaning that while finite verbs indicate person, number, and mood alongside the verbal action, the infinitive expresses the action purely in the abstract, without person or number limitations. Roman grammarians translated this concept of "non-limitation" by using the prefix in- ("not") with fīnītus ("bounded, limited"), creating infīnītīvus.
