- 508 BCE (traditional) — The cognomen is attached to Gaius Mucius after he burns his right hand before Porsena; Scaevola means “left-handed” and becomes hereditary in his line.
- 2nd–1st c. BCE — Mucius Scaevola becomes the name of a legal dynasty; two cousins, the Augur and the Pontifex, make it a byword for juristic authority.
- 1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE — In Cicero and later juristic writing the name stands for the old Senatorial order and for technical legal mastery; the legendary hand-burner is cited separately as an example of courage.
- Renaissance–18th c. — European historians of Roman law keep the jurists' names alive; the legend is retold in emblem books and in Dante's Paradiso.
- 19th–21st c. — Used almost exclusively in classical scholarship and in histories of law, where the bare name now requires a disambiguating title (the Augur, the Pontifex) to be intelligible.
