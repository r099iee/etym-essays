- Old English — the hedgerow provider: hæsel names the coppiced shrub of nuts, rods, and wattles, a working tree of every village.
- Medieval — rods of power: hazel wands and divining rods enter folklore; the pliable pole becomes the water-finder’s instrument.
- 16th c. — a colour is born: the nutshell’s mottled brown lends its name to eyes, and hazel eyes enters poetry as praise.
- 17th–18th c. — the improver’s tree: husbandry books celebrate hazel coppice rotations; plantations of filberts and cobs supply London markets.
- Celtic revival — wisdom restored: antiquaries recover the Irish hazels of wisdom, and the tree gains a mythic halo it never quite loses.
- Today — nuts, eyes, and hedges: the shrub feeds wildlife and walkers, the colour describes beloved eyes, and coppice crafts keep the poles in use.
