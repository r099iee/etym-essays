• Clip-clop: Similar because it names the identical hoofbeat sound; differs because clip-clop is the doubled, often child-directed form stressing the two-beat rhythm, while clop is the plainer single-beat unit of the novelist.
  • Clatter: Similar because both are echoic words for hard striking sounds; differs because clatter is rapid, chaotic, and multiple (pans, hoofs at gallop), while clop is slow, round, and measured, one beat per step.
  • Clap: Similar because it shares the cl- contact cluster and the stopped -p; differs because a clap is sharp and flat (hands, thunder), while a clop is hollow and round, with the hoof's resonance inside it.
  • Thud: Similar because both are dull percussive impacts of weight on ground; differs because a thud is low, soft, and dead (a sack dropping), while a clop rings — the horseshoe gives it metal and hollowness.
  • Trot: Similar because trotting hoofs make the sound, and both belong to the stable; differs because trot names the gait itself (two-beat diagonal), while clop names only the noise the gait produces.
  • Clip: Similar because clip-clop is built from it and both are short sharp sounds; differs because clip alone is thin and quick (shears, a clip round the ear), while clop is the full hollow hoof-note.
  • Knock: Similar because both are hollow wooden-sounding impacts; differs because a knock comes from a door or an engine and expects an answer, while a clop passes by on the road and belongs to travellers.
  • Plod: Similar because both evoke slow heavy forward movement; differs because plod names the labouring manner of walking itself, while clop names the sound that may — or may not — accompany it.
