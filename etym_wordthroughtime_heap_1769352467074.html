- Piled mound (earliest English sense) - a group of things thrown or gathered together in an untidy mass; concrete and observationally neutral.
- Quantity extension (16th–18th c.) - “a heap of people/trouble”; a large, abundant quantity in informal speech; the register turns colloquial and emphatic.
- Idiomatic shading (19th–20th c.) - “in a heap” (a person collapsed), “bottom of the heap” (lowest in a social order); gains social and emotional connotations of disorder or low status.
- Technical sense (20th c.) - computing: an unstructured memory area for dynamic allocation; a precise, value-neutral jargon term.
