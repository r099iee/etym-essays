• Serrated: Similar because both describe a patterned edge; differs because serrated names a saw-tooth edge with sharp, pointed teeth and a cutting function, while scalloped edges are rounded, blunt semicircles, purely ornamental and with no cutting purpose.
  • Crenellated: Similar because both describe a notched outline; differs because crenellated means the square, battlement-like notches of castle walls — straight-topped rectangular merlons and crenels — the geometric opposite of the scallop's continuous curve.
  • Notched: Similar because both mean having cuts in an edge; differs because notched is the generic term for any cut or nick and carries no decorative regularity — a notched edge may be accidental — while a scalloped edge is deliberately and regularly patterned.
  • Wavy: Similar because both describe an undulating line; differs because wavy undulates in a single smooth continuous line, while a scalloped edge is a row of repeated, self-contained semicircular arches.
  • Fluted: Similar because both carry rounded curved decoration; differs because fluting consists of parallel rounded grooves or channels cut into a surface (a column, a moulding), whereas scalloping is a series of rounded curves cut into an edge.
  • Indented: Similar because both mean a margin that is not straight; differs because indented means set in or marked with inward notches or hollows, without the regular outward semicircular bulges of scalloping.
  • Pinked: Similar because both are decorative ways of finishing an edge; differs because pinking cuts a zigzag of tiny V-shaped notches (as with pinking shears) to prevent fraying, while scalloping cuts larger rounded arcs.
  • Festooned: Similar because both are decorative; differs because festooned means hung or draped with garlands, chains, or flowers — decoration laid upon the surface — while scalloped means the edge itself is cut into curves.
  • Crenulate: Similar because both describe a small-scale notched margin; differs because crenulate is the technical botanical word (as of a leaf margin) for small rounded scallops or fine serrations, while scalloped usually implies larger, more distinct semicircles.
