• Panted: Similar because both describe heavy, audible respiration resulting from intense physical exertion; differs because panted emphasizes rapid, shallow, open-mouthed breathing driven by oxygen debt, whereas puffed highlights the forceful, rhythmic expulsion of air through rounded or pursed lips.
  • Gasped: Similar because both involve sudden, audible respiratory sounds; differs because gasped indicates an abrupt, involuntary inhalation provoked by panic, shock, pain, or acute breathlessness, whereas puffed describes repeated, gusting outward exhalations or swelling.
  • Bloated: Similar because both describe abnormal enlargement or distension; differs because bloated carries a visceral, diseased, or uncomfortable connotation of excess water, gas, decay, or overindulgence, whereas puffed suggests soft, light, airy, or localized rounded swelling.
  • Swollen: Similar because both designate enlarged tissue volume; differs because swollen is the formal clinical and physiological term for enlargement caused by inflammation, injury, or edema, whereas puffed is a milder, descriptive term often applied to soft tissues like eyelids, cheeks, or sleeves.
  • Inflated: Similar because both describe being filled and expanded with air or gas; differs because inflated is a technical or mechanical descriptor (and an economic concept regarding currency), whereas puffed carries organic, tactile, and onomatopoeic connotations of soft contours.
  • Touted: Similar because both can describe promoting something with excessive praise; differs because touted implies persistent, aggressive marketing or solicitation by a promoter, whereas puffed (as in puffery) specifically denotes hyperbolic, dishonest, or flattering literary reviews and advertisements.
  • Blown: Similar because both describe the movement or effect of air; differs because blown is the broad past participle of blow denoting continuous displacement by wind, whereas puffed implies intermittent, gusty, or gentle bursts.
  • Wheezed: Similar because both refer to labored breathing; differs because wheezed specifies a high-pitched whistling or raspy sound generated by narrowed bronchial passages, whereas puffed highlights the blowing of breath from the mouth.
