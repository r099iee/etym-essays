• Cheerful: in good spirits; similar in mood, but cheerful names the feeling, while jaunty adds style, movement, and self-confidence.
  • Sprightly: lively and brisk; very close, but sprightly emphasises energy and quickness more than confident style.
  • Breezy: casual and relaxed in manner; similar ease, but breezy suggests offhand informality rather than smart self-assurance.
  • Chipper: cheerfully lively, often after recovery; similar brightness, but more informal and less concerned with appearance.
  • Perky: briskly cheerful and self-assured; close, but perky can sound more girlish or more insistently upbeat, and can be mildly irritating.
  • Lively: full of energy; broader and more neutral, without the implication of stylish confidence.
  • Airy: light and graceful in manner; similar lightness, but airy can suggest insubstantiality or unconcern.
  • Debonair: confident, stylish, and charming; close in elegance, but more sophisticated and usually applied to men and manners.
  • Dapper: neat and stylish in dress; similar in appearance, but concerned specifically with trim clothing rather than manner.
  • Spruce: neat and smart in appearance; similar, but spruce is about tidiness, not high spirits.
  • Rakish: stylish with a hint of daring or impropriety; similar dash, but darker and more dangerous in tone.
  • Cocky: arrogantly self-confident; the negative extreme of jaunty’s assurance, lacking its charm.
  • Insouciant: casually unconcerned; similar ease, but more French, more studied, and less brisk.
  • Buoyant: cheerful and optimistic, able to rise after setbacks; similar resilience, but more about mood than manner.
