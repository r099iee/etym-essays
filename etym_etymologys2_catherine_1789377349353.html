Ancient Greek (c. 3rd–4th c. CE)
Αἰκατερίνη (Aikaterínē) / Ἑκατερίνη (Hekaterínē)
"personal name of St. Catherine of Alexandria"
Note: Ultimate etymology debated; traditionally associated by early Christian folk etymology with Greek katharós ("pure"), prompting the Latin -th- spelling; alternatively linked to the goddess Hecate or of Coptic/Egyptian origin.

Ecclesiastical Latin (c. 6th–11th c.)
Catharina / Katerina
"Christian virgin martyr and saint"
↓
Old French / Anglo-Norman (c. 11th–13th c.)
Caterine / Katherine
"saint's name; popular baptismal given name"
↓
Middle English (c. 13th–15th c.)
Katherine / Kateryne / Catherine
"widely venerated saint; female given name"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th–18th c.)
Catherine (in Catherine wheel)
"spinning pyrotechnic firework; wheel window"
Note: Eponymously transferred to spinning fireworks and architectural wheel windows after the spiked breaking wheel upon which St. Catherine was miraculously preserved before her martyrdom.
