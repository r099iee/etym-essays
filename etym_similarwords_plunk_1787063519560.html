• Thud: Similar because both describe a dull, heavy impact sound; differs because 'thud' denotes a muffled, non-resonant collision of soft or dense bodies, whereas 'plunk' often carries a sharper, more hollow, or twanging acoustic resonance.
  • Plop: Similar because both describe dropping something with sound; differs because 'plop' conveys a soft, wet, or fluid drop into liquid or soft mud without the mechanical or percussive weight of 'plunk'.
  • Plump: Similar because both can function adverbially and verbally for dropping heavily or directly; differs because 'plump' emphasizes rotund bulk, fleshy weight, or sudden full-bodied descent.
  • Drop: Similar because both denote allowing an object to fall downward; differs because 'drop' is a completely neutral, standard physical verb devoid of onomatopoeic color, acoustic description, or informal register.
  • Pluck: Similar because both apply to stringed musical instruments; differs because 'pluck' is the standard technical musical term for releasing a string with the fingers or plectrum, whereas 'plunk' emphasizes a casual, percussive, or rustic twang.
  • Bang: Similar because both indicate a sudden noisy impact; differs because 'bang' conveys a loud, explosive, or violent crash rather than a hollow, localized thud.
  • Flop: Similar because both describe sudden dropping; differs because 'flop' implies limpness, looseness, total lack of physical structure, or clumsy collapse.
