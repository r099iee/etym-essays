• Knotty: Similar because it refers to something full of knots, especially in wood; differs because 'knotty' can also mean difficult or intricate in a more general sense, while 'gnarled' specifically implies a twisted, rugged appearance often due to age or hardship.
  • Twisted: Similar because it describes something bent or contorted; differs because 'twisted' is a broader term that doesn't necessarily imply the ruggedness, age, or protuberances inherent in 'gnarled'.
  • Rugged: Similar because it suggests a rough, uneven, and tough surface; differs because 'rugged' can apply to landscapes or people in a general sense of hardiness, whereas 'gnarled' specifically points to the presence of knots and contortions.
  • Contorted: Similar because it means twisted or bent out of its normal shape; differs because 'contorted' can imply a more extreme or unnatural distortion, while 'gnarled' often suggests a natural, albeit irregular, development over time.
  • Weather-beaten: Similar because it implies a worn appearance due to exposure to the elements; differs because 'weather-beaten' focuses on the surface wear, while 'gnarled' emphasizes the underlying structural knots and twists.
  • Bumpy: Similar because it describes a surface with protuberances; differs because 'bumpy' is a more general and less evocative term, lacking the specific connotation of age, toughness, and organic twisting found in 'gnarled'.
  • Craggy: Similar when describing rough, uneven surfaces, often associated with rocks or cliffs; differs because 'craggy' specifically refers to a steep, rugged, and broken appearance, while 'gnarled' is more about the twisted, knotty texture of organic material like wood or flesh.
