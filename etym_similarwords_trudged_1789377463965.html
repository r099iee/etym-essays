• Plodded: Similar because both describe slow, heavy, and laborious walking; differs because plodded emphasizes dull, monotonous, rhythmic mechanical movement with low mental energy (like a plow-ox in a furrow), whereas trudged conveys greater physical exertion against an external burden, distance, or adverse weather.
  • Slogged: Similar because both denote grueling, exhausting movement through harsh conditions; differs because slogged emphasizes violent struggle through thick, sticky mire, deep mud, or punishing physical battle ("slogging through the swamp"), whereas trudged emphasizes steady, weary, foot-by-foot persistence.
  • Tramped: Similar because both imply walking long distances over rough roads; differs because tramped suggests heavy, firm-footed marching or vagabond walking that can be energetic, purposeful, or carefree, whereas trudged always implies severe exhaustion, heavy loads, or weary reluctance.
  • Shuffled: Similar because both convey slow, weary locomotion; differs because shuffled describes dragging the feet along the floor without lifting them, characteristic of frail elderly people or extreme apathy, whereas trudged involves lifting the feet to overcome external ground resistance or burden.
  • Lumbered: Similar because both denote heavy, cumbersome movement; differs because lumbered specifically conveys awkward, clumsy, elephantine bulk or unwieldy physical mass, whereas trudged describes the fatigue and effort of the walker regardless of their body size.
  • Toiled: Similar because both denote hard, exhausting labor; differs because toiled is a general verb of strenuous physical or mental work that does not specify locomotion, whereas trudged strictly denotes the physical act of walking heavily under duress.
  • Marched: Similar because both involve walking long distances in uniform progression; differs because marched implies disciplined, rhythmic, upright, and organized military cadence, whereas trudged signifies broken, stooped, weary, and uncoordinated foot-slogging.
