• Serological: Similar because both terms describe diagnostic and physiological phenomena involving substances present in blood serum; differs because serological refers specifically to the in vitro diagnostic analysis, laboratory measurement, and immunological testing of serum (such as titer assays and antigen-antibody detection kits), whereas humoral describes in vivo physiological functions, natural fluid-borne immune defenses, and historical theories of bodily fluids.
  • Fluidic: Similar because both words describe states, components, or mechanisms involving liquids; differs because fluidic is predominantly a mechanical, physical, and engineering descriptor referring to fluid dynamics, microfluidic circuits, and hydrodynamic flow, whereas humoral is strictly biomedical, physiological, and etymologically grounded in biological fluids.
  • Sanguineous: Similar because blood is the primary vital fluid of classical physiology and the principal circulatory conduit of modern humoral immunity; differs because sanguineous relates strictly and exclusively to blood itself (its physical presence, bloody color, or sanguine temperament), whereas humoral encompasses all bodily fluids (including lymph, bile, phlegm, and interstitial fluid) and systemic adaptive immunity.
  • Endocrine: Similar because both designate non-neural, systemic communication mediated by chemical substances circulating through extracellular fluids; differs because endocrine specifically denotes internal secretion by ductless glands releasing specialized biochemical messengers (hormones) into the bloodstream, whereas humoral is a broader physiological and immunological term encompassing antibodies, electrolytes, metabolic substrates, and classical humours.
  • Lymphatic: Similar because lymph is a critical physiological fluid carrying dissolved immune proteins and circulating lymphocytes; differs because lymphatic refers specifically to the anatomical network of vessels, nodes, and lymphoid organs that drain interstitial fluid, whereas humoral characterizes the functional soluble molecules dissolved within any extracellular fluid.
  • Hematogenous: Similar because both describe biological entities that travel through the cardiovascular circulation; differs because hematogenous specifically indicates spread, dissemination, or origin via the blood vascular network (e.g., hematogenous metastasis of neoplastic cells or bacterial seeding), whereas humoral denotes soluble physiological regulation and antibody-mediated defense across both blood and lymph.
  • Circulating: Similar because both identify biological factors that move continuously throughout the cardiovascular and lymphatic systems; differs because circulating is a general kinetic participle describing the dynamic movement of any entity (cells, gas bubbles, drugs, or nutrients), while humoral specifically denotes soluble biochemical or immunologic agents dissolved in bodily liquids.
  • Temperamental: Similar because both terms historically trace back to the Galenic belief that psychological disposition is governed by constitutional fluid mixtures; differs because temperamental denotes outward psychological behavior, emotional volatility, and mood swings, whereas humoral originally designated the underlying somatic fluid imbalances that caused those behavioral traits.
