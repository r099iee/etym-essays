1. PIE *kʷo- 'this/that; interrogative-relative pronominal stem' (the who/what stem; Pokorny *kʷo-/*kʷi-; reflexes across the family include Latin quis, Greek τίς, Sanskrit kás, Gothic hwas). 2. PIE directional derivation *kʷo- + allative suffix *-dʰr-/-tr- (motion toward) → the interrogative goal-adverb 'to where'. 3. Proto-Germanic *hwadrē 'whither, to what place' (also *hwæder; the -dr- suffix matches the deictic pair *hi- 'this here' → hider 'hither' and *þa- 'that there' → þider 'thither'). 4. Old English hwider, hwæder 'to what place, whither' (recorded in Anglo-Saxon prose and verse; also written hwider). 5. Middle English whider, wheder, whidder 'to what place' (12th–15th c.). 6. English whither (the -th- spelling restored in the 15th century by analogy with thither and hither, which had kept their -th-; the word thereafter survives as an archaic, literary, and poetic adverb, as in Wordsworth's 'whither shall I turn', The Prelude, 1805). Debated nuance: the exact vocalism of the Proto-Germanic form (whether *hwadrē or *hwæder) is uncertain, but the suffixal structure — interrogative stem + directional -dr/-tr — is firmly established by the parallel set hider/thither/whither. The same PIE stem with different suffixes produced Latin quō 'whither, to what place', Greek ποῖ 'whither', and Sanskrit kútra 'where', all independent reflexes of the same pronominal base.
