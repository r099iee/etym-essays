• Vines: Similar because both refer to climbing or trailing plants with elongated, flexible, non-woody or woody stems; differs because vine is a broad botanical category (including grapes, melons, and passionflowers) that often climb toward light, whereas creepers specifically emphasizes plants that trail low over surfaces or climb flat walls via adhesive pads or rootlets.
  • Climbers: Similar because both denote plants that ascend structures, trellises, or trees; differs because climbers is a general functional term encompassing twiners, tendril-bearers, and rambling roses, whereas creepers emphasizes surface-clinging, procumbent, or ground-covering growth habits.
  • Ivies: Similar because ivies are the archetypal evergreen creepers that cling tenaciously to stone walls and tree trunks; differs because ivy specifically identifies plants of the araliaceous genus Hedera, whereas creepers is a broad vernacular term encompassing diverse botanical families including Parthenocissus, Campsis, and Lonicera.
  • Tendrils: Similar because tendrils are intimately associated with climbing and creeping plants; differs because a tendril is not the plant itself, but a specialized threadlike morphological organ (modified leaf or stem) used to curl around supports, whereas creepers denotes the entire living plant organism.
  • Lianas: Similar because both are climbing plants found clinging to forest structures; differs because lianas are massive, thick, woody climbing vines characteristic of tropical rainforest canopies that hang freely from tree limbs, whereas creepers are generally temperate, surface-hugging garden and wall plants.
  • Runners: Similar because both describe horizontal vegetative stems that spread across the ground; differs because a runner (or stolon, like that of a strawberry plant) specifically refers to a specialized stem that roots at nodes to produce daughter plants, whereas creepers refers broadly to the trailing or wall-climbing habit of the whole plant.
