- Old English (c. 9th–10th c.) — Attested as oferleċġan, meaning literally to lay something over, cover, oppress with weight, or smother.
- 14th–16th c. — Developed into Middle and Early Modern English overlayen / overlaid, specializing in fine artisan crafts: gilding sacred objects with gold leaf, lining fabrics, and veneering wood.
- 17th–18th c. — Expanded into cartography, architectural drawing, and natural philosophy, describing physical tracing sheets placed over base maps and blueprints.
- 19th c. — Adopted into geological stratification treatises and psychological descriptions of memory, where fresh experiences are described as overlaid upon subconscious impressions.
- Late 20th c. — Emerged in telecommunications (overlay networks) and cognitive neuroscience (neuronal recycling of reading overlaid on spoken language).
- 21st c. — Ubiquitous in augmented reality (AR), computer graphics, heads-up displays (HUD), and software-defined network virtualization.
