• lurk: Similar because both describe furtive, threatening concealment in the service of an attack; differs because lurking is staying still in ambush, hidden in one place, whereas prowling is steady, restless motion carried out in search of prey.
  • skulk: Similar because both mean moving or hiding stealthily to avoid notice; differs because skulking stresses guilt and cowering withdrawal, often after something shameful, whereas prowling is a predator's patient, hungry quartering of an area.
  • slink: Similar because both describe furtive, quiet movement; differs because slinking emphasizes a sinuous, ashamed, or abject gait — a body trying to pass unseen — whereas prowling is purposeful, deliberate hunting motion.
  • stalk: Similar because both are stealthy hunting movements; differs because stalking fixes on one identified quarry and closes in deliberately and directionally, whereas prowling roves about opportunistically, casting for anything rather than tracking a particular target.
  • rove: Similar because both mean moving about without a fixed route; differs because roving is open and purposeless wandering, without stealth or a hunting aim, whereas prowling always carries stealth and a search for prey or plunder.
  • roam: Similar because both describe wandering over an area; differs because roaming is unhurried, often idle or exploratory movement with no predatory intent, whereas prowling is silent, purposeful searching.
  • wander: Similar because both mean moving about aimlessly; differs because wandering is casual and destitute of purpose or stealth, whereas prowling is vigilant, secretive hunting motion.
  • patrol: Similar because both involve moving over an area repeatedly; differs because patrolling is systematic, open, duty-bound surveillance of a beat by an authorized guard, whereas prowling is covert, private, and predatory.
  • range: Similar because both describe animals moving over territory; differs because ranging is the wide, natural movement of an animal over its home ground, whereas prowling is specifically silent, patient hunting within a circuit.
  • sneak: Similar because both are furtive movements meant to avoid detection; differs because sneaking is a general stealthy motion, often to hide or approach unseen, whereas prowling adds the restless, hunting search for prey or plunder.
  • creep: Similar because both are quiet, low movements; differs because creeping is slow, cautious, low-to-the-ground motion, whereas prowling is a sustained roving circuit of search, not a single cautious approach.
