• gadget: Similar because both refer to small mechanical devices or clever implements; differs because a gadget is typically compact, commercially manufactured, and intended for convenient everyday utility (e.g., a kitchen gadget), whereas a contraption implies an awkward, rambling, or visibly intricate physical structure.
  • contrivance: Similar because both stem from the Latinate root of inventing or scheming; differs because contrivance is higher in register and applies equally to intellectual stratagems, literary plots, and abstract schemes, whereas contraption is predominantly physical, kinetic, and slightly humorous.
  • apparatus: Similar because both describe a complex arrangement of components designed for a specific function; differs because apparatus is a formal, scientific, or institutional term carrying technical gravity (e.g., laboratory apparatus or bureaucratic apparatus), lacking the whimsical informality of contraption.
  • mechanism: Similar because both consist of interconnected moving parts operating together; differs because mechanism is an objective, value-neutral engineering and scientific term focusing on internal mechanical operations and cause-and-effect laws.
  • gizmo: Similar because both serve as colloquial designations for devices; differs because gizmo is modern slang frequently used as a placeholder name for small, unfamiliar electronic or handheld objects whose exact name is forgotten, without the elaborate multi-part construction of a contraption.
  • doohickey: Similar because both refer casually to mechanical items; differs because doohickey is purely an informal placeholder noun (like thingamajig or whatchamacallit) for an unspecified small part, whereas a contraption is a functioning, complete machine.
  • machine: Similar because both denote physical systems that apply mechanical power to perform work; differs because machine is the standard, neutral category noun, whereas contraption highlights eccentricity, complexity, or makeshift construction.
  • Rube Goldberg machine: Similar because both celebrate convoluted, multi-stage mechanical complexity; differs because a Rube Goldberg machine is an exaggerated, specific idiomatic trope where a simple task is deliberately performed via an absurd chain reaction of domino-like events.
  • rig: Similar because both describe improvised or customized equipment; differs because rig emphasizes functional adaptation for heavy work (e.g., an oil rig or fishing rig) rather than bizarre or amusing over-engineering.
  • appliance: Similar because both are physical devices built to accomplish a domestic or technical task; differs because an appliance is a standardized, enclosed consumer product (like a toaster or refrigerator), whereas a contraption is characterized by visible, idiosyncratic, or non-standard construction.
