- Latin providentia foresight, divine care - positive virtue, theological and Stoic
- 16th-17th c. provident / improvident adjectives - moral bookkeeping: provident householder versus improvident spendthrift
- 18th-19th c. political economy - improvidence becomes a technical vice of the poor; provident societies, savings banks as cure; Malthusian overtone
- Late 19th-early 20th c. anthropology - primitive improvidence as evolutionary stage before storage, herding, agriculture; chapter-heading usage
- Mid-20th c. critique - ethnographers show sharing and mobility are rational, not improvident; term seen as ethnocentric
- Contemporary - rare moral archaism; survives in economic history and insurance, everyday replaced by short-sighted, hand-to-mouth
