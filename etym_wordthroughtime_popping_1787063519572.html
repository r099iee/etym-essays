- Late Middle English (c. 14th c.): poppen was an echoic verb meaning to strike lightly, knock, or make a small puffing sound.
- 16th – 17th Century: Evolved to denote sudden physical motions, unexpected appearances ('popping in'), and shooting light firearms ('pop-guns').
- 18th Century: Applied to describe bulging, protruding eyeballs under extreme terror, shock, or physical asphyxiation.
- 19th Century: Expanded into American food terminology ('popcorn') and carbonated beverages ('soda pop').
- Modern Era: Codified across eye-popping visual descriptions, street dance styles ('popping'), audio technology, and colloquial slang for exciting events.
