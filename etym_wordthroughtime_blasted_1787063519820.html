- "struck by a violent gust of wind" - weather sense, from the noun blast "a blowing" (Old English blǣst)
- "withered, blighted (of plants)" - the malevolent dry wind of the "blasting" heat; poetic sense (16th c.; Milton's "blasted heath")
- "blown up by gunpowder; shattered by explosion" - the gunpowder age revolutionises the word (15th-16th c.)
- "cursed, damned" - mild oath: "blast it!", "that blasted door" (17th c. onward)
- "ruined, devastated" - of places and hopes alike ("blasted hopes"); epic-devastation sense (18th-19th c.)
- modern - explosion and curse senses dominate; "have a blast" (enjoy) and "blasted" (drunk, slang) flip the register; Tolkien uses the searing, dragon-fire sense of devastation
