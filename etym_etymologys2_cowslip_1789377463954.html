*gʷōws
"cow, ox, cattle"

*kūz

↓

cū
"cow, bovine animal"
↓

cou / cow

[Root Word 2: SLIP (Old English slyppe / slypa)]
PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*slew- / *slewb-
"to slip, slide, glide; slimy, viscous substance"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*slup- / *slub-
"slimy mass, viscous liquid, slip"
↓
Old English (c. 8th–10th c.)
slyppe / slypa
"slime, paste, viscous dung, cow droppings"
Note: Historically cow-dung / cow-pat; folk etymology later re-interpreted the second element as 'lip' (cow's lip).
↓
Old English Compound (c. 10th c.)
cū-slyppe
"cow-dung flower, meadow primula growing in dung-fertilized pasture"
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
cowslyppe / cowslope / cowslip
"yellow spring wildflower of pastures (Primula veris)"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
cowslip / cow's-lip
"Primula veris; golden spring flower gathered for wine and salads"
↓
Modern English (present)
COWSLIP
