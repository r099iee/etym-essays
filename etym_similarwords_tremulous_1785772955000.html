• Quivering: Similar because both describe rapid small motion. Differs because quivering is more purely kinetic and can describe objects without emotion (quivering aspic, quivering leaves), whereas tremulous almost always colours the shake with vulnerability, fear, age, or intense feeling.
  • Trembling: Similar because both denote shaking. Differs in grammar and register: trembling is common and verbal (a participial description of the act), while tremulous is adjectival, Latinate, and literary, naming a quality of tone or appearance rather than the act alone.
  • Wavering: Similar because both can describe an unsteady voice or flame. Differs because wavering also means indecision (wavering loyalty, wavering resolve), while tremulous means micro-shaking or emotionally unsteady sound and appearance, not irresolute choice.
  • Shaky: Similar because both mean unsteady. Differs because shaky is informal and broad (shaky hands, shaky argument), while tremulous is formal and typically physical-emotional; "a shaky argument" is idiom, while "a tremulous argument" is a category stretch.
  • Fluttering: Similar because both suggest delicate, irregular motion. Differs because fluttering is airy and intermittent (wings, lashes, a fluttering heart), while tremulous is vibratory unsteadiness tied to the tremble family.
  • Quaking: Similar because both describe shaking, often from fear. Differs because quaking is stronger and often larger-scale (quaking with rage; the earth quaking), while tremulous denotes slight, fine-grained trembling.
