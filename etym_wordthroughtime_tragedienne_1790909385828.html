- Mid-19th c. - Borrowed directly into Victorian English from French (tragédienne) to describe the celebrated female stars of the Comédie-Française and European tragic theatre (such as Mademoiselle Rachel and Sarah Siddons).
- Late 19th & Early 20th c. - Reached its cultural zenith during the legendary international rivalry between Sarah Bernhardt (the grandiose, romantic golden voice) and Eleonora Duse (the pioneering realist and master of psychological subtext).
- Mid-20th c. - Embraced in opera criticism to celebrate dramatic soprano Maria Callas, whose revolutionary dramatic acting resurrected classical bel canto tragedy into electrifying theatrical realism.
- Late 20th c. to Present - Used in theatrical scholarship for classical performers, alongside a common colloquial usage for someone who indulges in excessive, histrionic daily drama.
