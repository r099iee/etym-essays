• banditry: Similar because both name organized robbery by outlaw groups; differs because banditry covers any outlaw-gang activity of any scale, while brigandage specifically implies armed, quasi-military bands operating in rural or mountainous terrain.
  • marauding: Similar because both involve roving to plunder; differs because marauding is chiefly an adjective or gerund describing the act of raiding for prey or goods, while brigandage is a noun for the settled practice, system, or way of life.
  • piracy: Similar because both are organized robbery with violence committed by outlaws; differs because piracy is robbery at sea (or, by extension, of intellectual property), while brigandage is its land-based counterpart.
  • highway robbery: Similar because both are robbery of travellers on the roads; differs because highway robbery names the single crime of stopping and robbing travellers, while brigandage names the entire bandit system with its structure, hierarchy, and livelihood.
  • pillage: Similar because both involve plundering and seizing goods; differs because pillage emphasizes the taking of booty from a place or population, while brigandage emphasizes the organized criminal way of life of the robbers themselves.
  • rapine: Similar because both denote the violent seizure of property; differs because rapine is a formal, rhetorical word for plundering violence without implying a bandit organization, while brigandage names the specific armed-band system.
  • raiding: Similar because both are armed incursions to seize goods; differs because raiding is a one-off attack or a tactic used by many groups (armies, tribes, guerrillas), while brigandage is a sustained criminal occupation.
  • robbery: Similar because both involve taking property by force or threat; differs because robbery is the generic legal term for the individual crime, while brigandage is a systemic, organized, and historically specific phenomenon.
  • predation: Similar because both describe preying on others for gain; differs because predation is a broad biological and economic metaphor, while brigandage is concrete, historical, and tied to armed outlaws.
  • insurgency: Similar because both involve armed groups operating against state authority; differs because insurgency implies a political goal and a claim to legitimacy, while brigandage is criminal self-enrichment — though governments often deliberately blurred the line.
