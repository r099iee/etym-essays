• Game: Similar because both refer to the meat of wild animals taken through hunting; differs because game is a broad umbrella term that includes birds (pheasant, grouse), small mammals (rabbit, hare), and large beasts, whereas venison refers specifically to deer meat in modern English.
  • Beef: Similar because both are rich red meats derived from hooved ruminants; differs because beef comes exclusively from domesticated cattle and features higher fat marbling and milder flavor, whereas venison comes from wild or farmed cervids and is distinctly leaner, darker, and more gamey.
  • Mutton: Similar because both are dark, flavorful meats from grazing animals with French-derived culinary names; differs because mutton is the mature meat of domesticated sheep with a characteristic fatty, woolly flavor profile, while venison is lean, iron-rich deer meat.
  • Pork: Similar because both are meat names introduced into English via Norman French; differs because pork is white/pink meat from domestic swine, while venison is dense red meat from game deer.
  • Fallow: Similar because it relates to deer (specifically fallow deer); differs because fallow is an adjective/noun describing the living animal or species, whereas venison is the dressed culinary meat.
