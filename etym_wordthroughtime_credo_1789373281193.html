- Classical & Early Christian Era (4th–6th c.) - Liturgical Confession of Faith: The Latin incipit ('I believe') of the ecumenical creeds, spoken at baptism and liturgy to confirm orthodox Christian doctrine.
- High Medieval to Renaissance Era (12th–16th c.) - Musical & Sacramental Pillar: Integrated into the Ordinary of the Mass; transformed into monumental polyphonic choral compositions by composers such as Josquin and Palestrina.
- Enlightenment & 18th c. - Secularization of Belief: Transitioned from strict church dogma to denote any philosophical system or deistic summary of principles held by an individual thinker.
- 19th c. (Romantic & Victorian) - Aesthetic and Personal Manifesto: Adopted by artists, poets, and critics (including Arnold and Pater) to formulate their 'artistic credo' or personal moral philosophy independent of organized religion.
- 20th–21st c. - Corporate, Civic, and Ethical Code: Widely applied to institutional ethical codes, corporate mission statements, and personal existential declarations.
