- 1769 — Jeanne Bécu is presented at Versailles and becomes “Madame du Barry”; the name is court gossip before it is history.
- 1774 — after Louis XV’s death she is exiled to Louveciennes, and “du Barry” becomes shorthand for favouritism and the misuse of royal intimacy.
- 1793 — her execution turns the name into a Revolutionary emblem of aristocratic vice.
- 1837 — Carlyle’s “Dame Dubarry” fixes a clipped, archaic English form, half honorific and half sneer.
- Late 19th–20th c. — biographies, novels and films make her a stock heroine of the courtesan’s rise and fall, and the name acquires glamour again.
- 21st c. — chiefly historical and heritage usage; “du Barry” survives as a byword for the last royal favourite of the ancien régime.
