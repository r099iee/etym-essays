• Strong: Similar because both denote power and firmness; differs because strong is the general word for physical or abstract power, while sturdy specifically implies a solid, well-knit build with compact resistance.
  • Robust: Similar because both mean strong and well-built; differs because robust stresses vigorous health and powers of recovery, while sturdy stresses solid, compact resistance.
  • Tough: Similar because both endure hardship; differs because tough emphasises resistance to damage, while sturdy emphasises a sound, well-knit build.
  • Hardy: Similar because both withstand harsh conditions; differs because hardy is used of creatures and plants able to survive cold and hardship, while sturdy describes physical build and firmness.
  • Stout: Similar because both mean solidly strong; differs because stout leans toward thick-set bulk, sometimes fatness, whereas a creature can be perfectly sturdy without being large.
  • Burly: Similar because both mean strong; differs because burly is big, heavy, and strong, typically of persons, while sturdy implies no largeness at all.
  • Stalwart: Similar because both mean dependable strength; differs because stalwart adds loyalty, courage, and moral steadfastness to bodily sturdiness.
  • Solid: Similar because both mean firm and reliable; differs because solid suggests the absence of gaps or weakness and can be wholly abstract, while sturdy foregrounds physical construction and build.
  • Durable: Similar because both mean long-lasting; differs because durable stresses capacity to withstand wear over time, while sturdy stresses present solidity of build.
  • Rugged: Similar because both mean resistant to rough treatment; differs because rugged suggests rough, uneven, weather-beaten strength, while sturdy is clean and compact.
  • Well-built: Similar because both praise construction; differs because well-built is a plain, general term for good construction, while sturdy adds the nuance of compactness and resistance.
