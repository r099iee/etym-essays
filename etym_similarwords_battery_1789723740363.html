• Caged hen: Similar because it denotes a confined layer; differs because caged is generic, while battery specifies the intensive tiered wire-cage system.
  • Factory-farmed bird: Similar because it evokes industrial poultry production; differs because factory-farmed covers broilers and barns too, while battery is specifically egg-laying cages.
  • Broiler: Similar because it is another intensive poultry product; differs because broilers are meat chickens grown for slaughter, while battery hens are egg layers kept for a season.
  • Accumulator: Similar because in British older use it means rechargeable electrical battery; differs because it is technical-electrical, lacking the agricultural or artillery senses.
  • Artillery unit: Similar because it shares the grouped-for-bombardment origin of battery; differs because it denotes guns and gunners, not hens or cells.
  • Cell (electrical): Similar because a battery is technically cells grouped together; differs because cell is the single unit, battery the pack, and neither involves poultry.
  • Assault: Similar because in law assault and battery pair beating with threat; differs because assault is attempted violence, battery the completed touching, both human-legal not avian.
  • Barrage / volley: Similar because they capture the pounding multiplicity behind battery metaphors; differs because they are events of firing or questions, not stored devices or cages.
