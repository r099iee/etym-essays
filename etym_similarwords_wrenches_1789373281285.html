• Twists: Similar because both involve rotational force and angular distortion away from a straight line; differs because twists can be gentle, playful, gradual, or decorative (like twisting thread or a path twisting through woods), whereas wrenches implies sudden, violent, and agonizing force that risks mechanical fracture or bodily trauma.
  • Wrings: Similar because both denote forceful torsional manipulation; differs because wrings specifically implies bilateral twisting in opposite directions accompanied by compressive squeezing to extract liquid (wringing a towel) or crushing agony (wringing one's hands), whereas wrenches denotes a unilateral, tearing pull that forcibly jerks or dislocates.
  • Contorts: Similar because both describe the deformation of an object, body, or text into an unnatural shape; differs because contorts focuses on the resulting grotesque, folded, or serpentine visual appearance (such as an acrobat contorting or a face contorting with rage), whereas wrenches emphasizes the sudden, violent muscular or mechanical action that causes the dislocation.
  • Strains: Similar because both involve exceeding natural physical or structural limits; differs because strains highlights continuous excessive tension, over-stretching, and gradual fatigue under a heavy burden without necessarily twisting, whereas wrenches highlights an abrupt, rotational snapping or jerking motion.
  • Distorts: Similar because both characterize the corruption of an original form, truth, or syntactic balance; differs because distorts is an abstract, general term denoting any warping, blurring, or optical deviation from accuracy (e.g., distorted sound or distorted statistics), whereas wrenches is intensely physical, tactile, and violent, evoking the physical agony of the twisting act.
  • Dislocates: Similar because both describe moving a component out of its proper anatomical or structural socket; differs because dislocates denotes the resulting clinical separation of joints or systemic displacement, whereas wrenches describes the violent physical torque that precipitates that dislocation.
