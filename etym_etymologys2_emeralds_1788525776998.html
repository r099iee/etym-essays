Ancient Near Eastern / Semitic (2nd millennium BCE)
*b-r-q / bāreqet (Hebrew) / barraqu (Akkadian)
"flashing stone, lightning gem, radiant green stone"
(loaned across ancient trade routes into Indo-Iranian languages)
↓
Sanskrit (classical)
marakata (मरकत)
"emerald, green gemstone"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 6th c. BCE)
smáragdos (σμάραγδος)
"emerald, brilliant green gemstone"
↓
Classical Latin (1st c. BCE)
smaragdus
"emerald, green gem"
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
*esmaralda / *esmaraldus
"emerald"
(developing prosthetic e- before initial s-cluster and liquid consonant alternation r/l)
↓
Old French (12th c.)
esmeralde / esmeraude
"emerald gemstone"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
emeraude / emeralde
"emerald"
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
emerald
"precious green gemstone"
↓
Modern English (plural form)
emeralds
"multiple emerald gemstones; supreme green treasures"

Note: The word's migration across antiquity represents an inter-continental trade pathway: from Semitic roots for 'lightning/flashing' into Sanskrit marakata, Greek smáragdos, Latin smaragdus, and eventually Romance esmeralde with prosthetic e-.
