• Conduit: Similar because both are artificial channels for conveying fluids; differs because a conduit is a general pipe or tube for carrying water, gas or cables, while an aqueduct is specifically a water-conveying structure, often monumental.
  • Channel: Similar because both are passages that convey water; differs because a channel may be natural (a river channel) or small, while an aqueduct is a purpose-built artificial waterway.
  • Canal: Similar because both are artificial waterways; differs because a canal is an open navigable waterway for boats and irrigation, while an aqueduct is a water-supply conduit, often a closed or arched structure, not navigable.
  • Pipeline: Similar because both convey a liquid over a distance; differs because a pipeline is a modern pressurized pipe system (for water, oil, gas), while an aqueduct is usually a gravity-flow open or arched channel.
  • Pipe: Similar because both are tubes that carry water; differs because a pipe is a simple small-diameter conduit, while an aqueduct is a large engineered structure.
  • Duct: Similar because both name a tube or channel that conducts a flow (and "aqueduct" literally contains "duct"); differs because a duct is generic (air ducts, tear ducts), while an aqueduct is specifically for water and historically large.
  • Watercourse: Similar because both are channels in which water flows; differs because a watercourse is usually a natural stream or a legal term for a water body, while an aqueduct is artificial.
  • Sluice: Similar because both are water channels with controlled flow; differs because a sluice is a gated channel for regulating or releasing water (as in millraces), not a long-distance supply line.
  • Flume: Similar because both are artificial water channels; differs because a flume is an open trough or chute for conveying water (often for logs, gold-mining, or flow measurement), much smaller and shorter than an aqueduct.
  • Raceway: Similar because both are engineered channels for flowing water; differs because a raceway (mill race) conducts water to drive a wheel or turbine, while an aqueduct supplies water for consumption.
  • Tunnel: Similar because both can carry water underground; differs because a tunnel is a passage dug through ground or rock for any purpose (road, rail, water), not specifically a water conduit.
  • Culvert: Similar because both are conduits passing water; differs because a culvert is a modest buried drain that carries a stream under a road, while an aqueduct is a major supply structure.
  • Siphon: Similar because both move water without pumping; differs because a siphon uses atmospheric pressure over an inverted tube, while an aqueduct relies on gravity down a continuous gradient.
  • Artery (metaphorical): Similar because both are figurative main carriers of a vital flow; differs because an artery is biological (a blood vessel) and only metaphorically a supply route, while an aqueduct is literally an engineered waterway.
  • Waterway: Similar because both are routes for water; differs because a waterway is any navigable route for boats (river, canal, channel), while an aqueduct is a supply conduit, generally not navigable.
