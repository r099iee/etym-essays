“Thumping” first named heavy, pounding, rhythmic blows — the impact and dull noise of something struck repeatedly with force.
“Thumping” gained a colloquial intensifying life in the 19th century: a “thumping lie” meant a huge one, and the word came to mean remarkably large or emphatic.
“Thumping” in 20th-century journalism and speech flourished as an intensifier — “a thumping majority,” “a thumping win” — carrying an amused, boastful energy, more British than American.
“Thumping” today works in both registers at once — literal heavy beating (a thumping heart, thumping bass) and informal emphatic hugeness — its “-ump” syllable itself enacting the muffled impact.
