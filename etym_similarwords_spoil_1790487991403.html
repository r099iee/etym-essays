• booty: Similar because it means plunder taken in war; differs because booty is colloquial and piratical, while spoil is literary and classical.
  • plunder: Similar because it means goods seized by force; differs because plunder can be verb and noun for the act, while spoil stresses the stripped thing.
  • loot: Similar because it means stolen goods; differs because loot is slangy and modern, while spoil is elevated and biblical.
  • pillage: Similar because it means systematic wartime robbery; differs because pillage stresses ravaging, while spoil stresses the share divided.
  • prey: Similar because it is seized by predator or warrior; differs because prey is hunted (animals, victims), while spoil is stripped (arms, goods).
  • prize (war prize): Similar because it is captured ship or award; differs because prize is lawful and honourable, while spoil hints at greed.
  • ruin: Similar because to spoil is to ruin value; differs because ruin is total destruction, while spoil can be partial impairment.
  • damage: Similar because both impair; differs because damage is physical and compensable, while spoil is qualitative and often irreversible.
  • impair / mar: Similar because they lessen excellence; differs because impair is formal-technical, mar literary-slight, while spoil covers food, plans, and children broadly.
  • taint / corrupt: Similar because spoiled food or character is tainted; differs because taint implies contamination, while spoil implies stripping of goodness.
  • pamper / indulge: Similar because to spoil a child is to pamper; differs because pamper is kindly excess, while spoil judges the result as harmful.
  • despoil: Similar because it is the formal doublet meaning to strip; differs because despoil is solemn and legal, while spoil is everyday.
