• Insignificant: Similar because both denote little importance or consequence; differs because insignificant stresses a total lack of impact or weight, while trivial can cover minor everyday things that still hold a fleeting charm.
  • Trifling: Similar because both mean of little value; differs because trifling carries stronger contempt and annoyance, dismissing things as petty or paltry, while trivial may be merely light and unimportant.
  • Petty: Similar because both describe small, minor matters; differs because petty emphasizes small-mindedness or a narrow, spiteful fussing over details, while trivial is more neutral about the thing itself.
  • Frivolous: Similar because both describe something not serious; differs because frivolous stresses a lack of substance and a flighty refusal to take things seriously, implying moral or intellectual fault, while trivial is a cooler, more factual judgment.
  • Negligible: Similar because both mean so small as to be disregarded; differs because negligible is a precise analytical term (as in science and statistics) for amounts too small to matter, while trivial belongs to everyday qualitative speech.
  • Minor: Similar because both mean lesser in importance; differs because minor is a neutral comparative term (minor vs. major), while trivial pushes further toward "not worth attention at all."
  • Commonplace: Similar because both can mean ordinary and unremarkable; differs because commonplace stresses frequency and familiarity, while trivial stresses smallness of consequence.
  • Unimportant: Similar because both mean lacking importance; differs because unimportant is the plain, direct term, while trivial adds a note of dismissal or amusement at how small the thing is.
