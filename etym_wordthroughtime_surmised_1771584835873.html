- [Late Middle English legal-forensic sense] - 'Surmise' enters English as a noun for a formal allegation or charge brought on slight evidence; connotation is legalistic, cautious, and faintly suspicious.
- [16th century: the shift to speculation] - The word moves to mean a supposition or guess formed from scanty evidence; connotation becomes intellectual - the quiet guesswork of the scholar and the detective rather than the accuser.
- [Modern usage] - 'Surmise' is now a formal, literary verb for forming a conclusion by intuition on thin evidence; connotation is refined and old-fashioned, rarely heard in speech, where 'guess' and 'infer' have largely taken over.
