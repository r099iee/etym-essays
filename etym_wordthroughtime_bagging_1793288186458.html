- c. 1200 — the sack: bagge names a soft flexible container of cloth or hide, the farmer's and pedlar's essential tool.
- 15th c. — the verb fills it: to bag is to put corn or goods into bags, and millers and maltsters price the operation.
- 18th c. — cloth goes baggy: breeches, sails, and skins are said to bag where they belly and wrinkle, and bagging becomes a look as well as a job.
- 19th c. — the sportsman's boast: shooters bag head of game, anglers bag trout, and the full game-bag becomes the emblem of a good day.
- 20th c. — slang spreads it: children bag sweets and seats, critics bag rivals out in Australia, and shoppers bag bargains in every high street.
- Today — harvest and high street together: hop-pockets survive as heritage craft while supermarkets weigh every bagging of apples and every baggy jumper with the same soft word.
