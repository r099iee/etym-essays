Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*kápōts / *kap-ut-
"head"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
caput
"head; leader, chief person, origin, capital"
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th–6th c. CE)
capitāneus
"chief, prominent; captain, commander"
↓

chevetaine / chevetain
"captain, commander, military leader"

↓
Anglo-Norman & Middle English (c. 13th–14th c.)
cheventeyn / chieftayn

↓
Early Modern English (c. 16th–17th c.)
chieftain
"head of a Highland clan, tribal leader"
↓
Modern English (18th–21st c.)
chieftains
"leaders or headmen of clans, tribes, or factions"

Note on doublets: Chieftain and captain are historical etymological doublets. Both originate from Late Latin capitāneus. Chieftain represents the early popular Old French phonological evolution (where Latin initial /k/ palatalized to /tʃ/ before /a/, yielding chevetaine), whereas captain was re-borrowed later from Latinized Old French / Provençal retaining the unpalatalized /k/.
