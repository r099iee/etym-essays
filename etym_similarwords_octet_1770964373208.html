• octave: Similar because both signify the number eight in artistic contexts; differs because octave strictly names the opening eight lines of a Petrarchan sonnet or a musical interval of eight diatonic degrees, while octet is the general term for any group of eight, including eight performers or eight data bits.
  • stanza: Similar because both name a grouped unit of verse; differs because stanza refers to any recurring line-grouping of a poem regardless of length (four, six, eight, or ten lines), whereas octet mandates precisely eight lines.
  • quatrain: Similar because both are numbered verse units; differs because a quatrain has exactly four lines—half the length and thematic capacity of an octet.
  • septet: Similar because both name numbered musical or poetic groups; differs because a septet is a group of seven, one fewer than an octet, and in music is a far rarer ensemble form.
  • quintet: Similar because both name numbered chamber-music ensembles; differs because a quintet is for five performers, while an octet is for eight.
  • quartet: Similar because both name numbered ensembles and are used in the same Italian-derived series; differs because a quartet is a group of four, half the size of an octet.
  • byte: Similar because both denote eight bits in computing; differs because byte is the everyday term for an eight-bit unit of memory, while octet is the precise standards-based term used in networking when the exact 8-bit width must be guaranteed.
  • sestet: Similar because both name a numbered section of a sonnet; differs because the sestet is the six-line closing unit of a Petrarchan sonnet, while the octet is its eight-line opening unit.
  • octagon: Similar because both begin with the same "eight" morpheme; differs because an octagon is an eight-sided geometric figure (a shape), not a group of eight people, lines, or bits, so the similarity is purely etymological rather than semantic.
