EFFUSED is a single-root word; its chain is linear: 1. Proto-Indo-European *ǵʰew- 'to pour, to make a libation' (c. 4000–3500 BCE) — source of Vedic Sanskrit juhóti 'he pours (a libation)' and Greek khéō 'I pour'. 2. Latin fundere 'to pour, shed, melt, cast (metal)' (attested from the 3rd c. BCE, Plautus), from the extended PIE root *ǵʰewd-. 3. Latin effundere 'to pour out, shed, spill, lavish' (ex- 'out' + fundere), attested from the 2nd c. BCE. 4. Latin effūsus, past participle of effundere, 'poured out, spread abroad, profuse' (attested classical Latin). 5. English effuse — the adjective 'spread out, diffuse' borrowed directly from Latin effūsus in the early 16th century, and the verb 'to pour out' formed from the same Latin material (attested from the 17th century); EFFUSED is its past tense and past participle (modern English). Note on spelling: 17th–18th-century poetry also used the elided form effus'd; the modern spelling effused is standard.
