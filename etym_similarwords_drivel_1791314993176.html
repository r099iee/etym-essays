• Twaddle: Similar because both refer to silly, empty talk; differs because twaddle is milder, quaint, and slightly bookish, lacking the physical salivary disgust inherent in the origins of drivel.
  • Drool: Similar because drool shares both the literal salivary and metaphorical foolish senses; differs because drool is more commonly used in modern English for literal saliva or intense desire (“drooling over a sports car”), while drivel is predominantly verbal nonsense.
  • Babble: Similar because both denote foolish, incoherent speech; differs because babble emphasizes childish, rapid, or indistinct vocalization (echoic of infant sounds), whereas drivel emphasizes the vacuity and stupidity of the content.
  • Balderdash: Similar because both denote nonsensical assertions; differs because balderdash carries an emphatic, archaic exclamation of outrage (“Pure balderdash!”) often used to reject an outright falsehood.
  • Prattle: Similar because both mean foolish chatter; differs because prattle implies cheerful, harmless, childish chattering rather than contemptible foolishness.
  • Blather: Similar because both denote rambling, foolish talk; differs because blather highlights the sheer volume, length, and noisy continuity of the rambling speech.
  • Gibberish: Similar because both describe meaningless language; differs because gibberish sounds like an unintelligible foreign tongue or scrambled syllables, whereas drivel consists of real words formed into stupid ideas.
  • Claptrap: Similar because both dismiss verbal nonsense; differs because claptrap refers specifically to insincere, manipulative rhetoric designed to trap applause from an audience.
  • Bosh: Similar because both mean absurd nonsense; differs because bosh is an informal nineteenth-century borrowing from Turkish (meaning “empty”), functioning largely as an interjection of disbelief.
  • Piffle: Similar because both describe trivial nonsense; differs because piffle has an airy, harmless, lightweight comic flavor, lacking the biting contempt of drivel.
  • Tommyrot: Similar because both denote utter rubbish; differs because tommyrot is vintage British slang with a robust, folksy tone.
  • Slaver: Similar because both historically refer to saliva dripping from the mouth; differs because slaver focuses on predatory lust, flattery, or animal drooling, rarely used for intellectual nonsense.
