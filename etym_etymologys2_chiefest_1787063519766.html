Proto-Indo-European (c. 4000–3000 BCE)
*kaput-
"head"

↓

Latin (classical)
caput
"head; chief, leader; top; capital"

↓

Vulgar Latin (c. 5th–8th c.)
capum (sound change)
"head"

↓

Old French (9th–12th c.)
chief
"head, leader, principal, top"

↓

Middle English (c. 1300)
chief
"head, ruler, leader" (noun); "principal, main" (adj.)

↓

Middle English (14th c.)
chiefest (chief + -est)
"most chief, foremost, most important"

↓

Modern English (present; literary-archaic)
CHIEFEST

Note: the PIE root is reconstructed as *kaput- (some scholars prefer *kaper-); the shift of Latin caput to French chef (English chief) shows the regular weakening of the intervocalic -p- in the development from Latin to Old French.
