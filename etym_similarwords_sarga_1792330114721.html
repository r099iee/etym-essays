• canto: Similar because both refer to a major structural chapter or division within a long epic or narrative poem; differs because canto is derived from Italian (literally "song") reflecting medieval European sung recitation (as in Dante's Divine Comedy), whereas sarga is derived from the Sanskrit root for creative emission and must follow rigorous Indian metrical closure conventions.
  • chapter: Similar because both denote an organized structural subdivision of a continuous written work; differs because chapter (from Latin capitulum, "little head") is a generic prose or poetic heading, whereas sarga specifically belongs to elevated Sanskrit verse epics (mahākāvya) and carries cosmological undertones.
  • parvan: Similar because both are major division markers in ancient Indian epic literature; differs because parvan (literally "joint, knuckle, lunar knot") is the massive book-level division used in the Mahābhārata (comprising 18 huge parvans), whereas a sarga is a smaller, tightly unified canto within a poem (the Rāmāyaṇa is divided into kāṇḍas, which are further subdivided into sargas).
  • adhyāya: Similar because both function as chapter divisions in Sanskrit literature; differs because an adhyāya (from adhi-i, "to study, read") designates a study lesson or didactic lecture chapter in philosophical, scientific, and legal texts, whereas sarga is strictly an artistic narrative canto or cosmic creation.
  • ullāsa: Similar because both are structural section titles in Sanskrit works; differs because ullāsa (literally "splendor, joyous flash") is typically applied to divisions of rhetorical and dramatic treatises (such as Mammaṭa's Kāvyaprakāśa), whereas sarga is confined to epic narrative verse.
  • rhapsody: Similar because both describe recited episodes of heroic epic poetry; differs because a rhapsody in ancient Greece was a stitched-together performance unit of Homeric verse chanted by a rhapsode, whereas a sarga is an authorial literary division governed by strict shifting meter rules at its conclusion.
  • prakaraṇa: Similar because both represent textual divisions in classical Indian works; differs because a prakaraṇa is a topical section or explanatory monograph focusing on a single philosophical question, or a type of social drama in dramaturgy, whereas a sarga is a verse canto.
