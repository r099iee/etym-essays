- 1500s–1600s sense of making lame or feeble in body - clinical, surgical perception; used of wounds, disease, and bleeding that lame the limbs
- 1700s–1800s extension to nerves, constitution, and vigour - polite medical-moral perception; debilitating habits, climates, luxuries said to soften robust constitutions
- Late 1800s–early 1900s psychological turn - empathetic yet pathologising perception; debilitating grief, neurasthenia, overwork, and mental strain recognised as genuinely weakening
- Mid–late 1900s institutional and economic metaphor - technocratic perception; debilitating inflation, strikes, deficits, and bureaucracy figured as sick bodies
- Today broad causative for any strength-sapping force - neutral clinical to hyperbolic everyday use; ranges from debilitating chemotherapy to a debilitating hangover, always entailing disproportionate effort
