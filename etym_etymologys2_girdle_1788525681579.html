PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500 BCE)
*gʰerdʰ-
"to enclose, encircle, bind, surround"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*gurdilaz
"belt, girdle, cincture" (instrumental noun from *gurdijaną "to gird")
↓
Old English (c. 8th–10th c.)
gyrdel
"belt, sash, girdle, purse, zone"
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
girdel / gurdel
"girdle, belt, waist-sash, chivalric band"
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
girdle
"waist-belt, encircling band, perimeter, floral ring"
↓
Modern English (Present)
girdle
"belt, sash; encircling floral ring; anatomical skeletal arch"

Note: Formed from the Proto-Germanic verbal root *gurdijaną ("to gird") combined with the instrumental suffix *-ilaz (Old English -el). Closely cognate with English girth, gird, yard, garden, and garth, all descending from PIE *gʰerdʰ-.
