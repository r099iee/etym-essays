• Consecrates: Similar because both involve making someone or something sacred; differs because consecrates is the broad theological act of dedicating something to divine service, whereas anoints specifically requires the physical application of holy oil or ointment.
  • Smears: Similar because both involve applying a substance across a surface; differs because smears is crude, messy, and careless, while anoints conveys deliberate care, solemn ritual, or refined grooming.
  • Blesses: Similar because both impart divine favor; differs because blesses can be performed purely through spoken invocations or hand gestures, lacking the physical medium of oil.
  • Ordaigns: Similar because both designate individuals for high office; differs because ordains specifically refers to formal ecclesiastical investiture into the priesthood through laying on of hands, whereas anoints evokes royal and charismatic sanction.
  • Rubs: Similar because both describe the physical motion of applying pressure; differs because rubs is purely mechanical and lacks the specific context of oil, medicinal unguent, or ceremonial prestige.
  • Designates: Similar because both identify a successor; differs because designates is a dry administrative selection, while anoints implies dramatic, top-down favoritism and special consecration.
