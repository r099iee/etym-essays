Proto-Germanic (reconstructed, c. 500 BCE)
*tramp-
‘to tread heavily, stamp’
(imitative stem)
↓
Middle Low German (13th c., attested)
trampen
‘to stamp, tramp’
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
trampen
‘to walk heavily, tread’
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
tramp + -ing
‘heavy walk’ + participle ending
‘walking heavily’
↓
Modern English (current)
tramping
‘walking heavily; hiking’

Note: the stem is shared across Germanic — Old Norse trampa, Middle Dutch trampen, German trampeln — and is treated as imitative of the footfall; proposed deeper PIE connections (sometimes to *dreb-, to run or trip) are speculative and not generally accepted, so the chain properly begins with the Germanic stem.
