- 1300s - [Culinary Dry-Roasting] - Emerged in Middle English as *perchen* / *parchen*, referring to roasting grains, beans, or seeds over dry heat without oil.
- 1500s - [Environmental Scorching & Drought] - Applied to the scorching action of the summer sun, hot winds, and severe drought on agricultural land.
- 1600s–1800s - [Somatic Agony & Extreme Thirst] - Firmly associated with human dehydration, describing dry tongues, feverish throats, and travelers dying of thirst.
- 1900s–Present - [Literary Adverbial Intensifier] - Employed in elevated narrative prose (such as Tolkien's description of Mirkwood) as an evocative intensifier for agonizing thirst.
