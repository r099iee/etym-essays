PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*gerbʰ-
"to scratch, carve, incise, scrape into a surface"
Note: Combined with PIE *dis- / *dwo- ('apart, asunder') > Greek διά (diá, 'through, across, thoroughly').

Ancient Greek (c. 8th–5th c. BCE)
γράφειν (gráphein)
"to scratch, scrape; write, draw, represent by lines"
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 5th–4th c. BCE)
διαγράφειν (diagráphein)
"to mark out by lines, delineate, draw; describe, register"
↓
Classical Greek (c. 4th c. BCE)
διάγραμμα (diágramma)
"geometrical figure, marked-out plan; musical scale, harmony"
Note: Formed from diagráphein + action/result noun suffix -ma. Attested in Plato's Meno and Aristotle's Metaphysics.
