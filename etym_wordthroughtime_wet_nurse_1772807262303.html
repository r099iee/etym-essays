- 17th century - the compound 'wet-nurse' emerges to name a woman hired to suckle and care for another's child (contrasted with a 'dry-nurse')
- 18th-19th century - a morally contested staple of gentry nurseries: necessary luxury for the elite yet criticized as maternal neglect and dangerous for the child
- 20th century - breastfeeding practices shift and the role fades from common employment; the word turns historical and period-flavored
- Contemporary - rare and archaic-tinged; modern discourse prefers 'milk donor' or 'surrogate breastfeeder', while 'wet-nurse' survives in historical and literary settings
