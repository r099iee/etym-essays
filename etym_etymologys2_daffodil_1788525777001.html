Pre-Greek / Mediterranean Substrate (pre-1st millennium BCE)
*asphodel-
"asphodel plant, flowering geophyte"
(non-Indo-European Mediterranean plant name absorbed into Hellenic speech)
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE; Homeric)
asphódelos (ἀσφόδελος)
"asphodel plant, flower of the Elysian Meadows"
↓
Classical Latin (1st c. CE; Pliny)
asphodelus
"asphodel"
↓
Medieval Latin (c. 11th–12th c.)
affodillus / affodilla
"asphodel / wild narcissus plant"
(displaying apheresis of initial vowel a- and diminutive -illus suffix)
↓
Old French (13th c.)
affodille / asphodile
"asphodel flower"
(frequently encountered in phrases like 'fleur d'affodille')
↓
Middle English (c. 1400)
affodill / affadille
"asphodel; yellow wild narcissus"
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
daffodill / daffadilly
"yellow trumpet narcissus flower"
(formed via prosthetic d- from French 'd'affodille' [d' + affodille] or Dutch article 'de affodil')
↓
Modern English
daffodil
"bright yellow trumpet-flowered spring plant (Narcissus pseudonarcissus)"

Note: The initial d- is an intriguing linguistic development: English speakers absorbed the French prepositional contraction d'affodille ("of the asphodel") or Flemish/Dutch definite article de affodil ("the asphodel") directly into the root noun, while transferring the name from the pale Mediterranean Asphodelus to the golden wild British Narcissus.
