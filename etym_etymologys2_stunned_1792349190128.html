Note on historical debate & lexical convergence: Historical philologists (OED, Skeat, Pokorny) emphasize that Modern English stun and its past participle stunned arose from a complex confluence of two closely related Germanic and Romance streams: (1) Anglo-French estoner (from Vulgar Latin *extonāre, literally "to strike with thunder", which also yielded modern English astonish and astound), and (2) native Old English stunian ("to crash, strike against, make a loud noise", from Proto-Germanic *stunōną). Both roots ultimately trace back to the Indo-European onomatopoeic/expressive base *(s)tenh₁- ("to thunder, roar"). The phonetic erosion of the initial French es- was accelerated by the presence of the native English verb.
