jigjog: a jerky, bouncing, uneven motion or pace — a short, irregular, up-and-down way of moving, faster than a walk but less fluid than a steady run; used of horses and riders, of carts and carriages, and of any jolting, bobbing motion. Primary sense — a jerky, bouncing pace. A jigjog is a motion that oscillates between the light, quick spring of a jig and the heavy, mechanical thump of a jog: an up-and-down, jolting wobble rather than a smooth, gliding stride. It is used of horses and their riders (a jigjog foot-pace, a jigjog trot), of carts and carriages rattling over rough roads, and of any person or thing bobbing unevenly along. The word always carries a faint comic edge of indignity and mechanical restlessness: the motion is hurried and energetic but ungraceful — jarring rather than flowing, awkward rather than elegant. The two elements. The word is a reduplicative compound (of the same family as zig-zag, ding-dong, and tick-tock) that joins two near-synonymous verbs:
  • Jig — a lively, springy dance or its quick hopping step; a light, rapid, bobbing motion.
  • Jog — a slow, steady, jolting pace; a nudge or shake; the heavy, bumping movement of a person or cart.
The vowel alternation of the reduplication — the high front vowel /ɪ/ of "jig" against the low back vowel /ɒ/ of "jog" — mimics the actual up-and-down oscillation of the movement being named: the sound of the word bounces the way the motion does. Full polysemy map (all senses):
  • A jerky, bouncing, uneven pace or motion (of a horse, rider, cart, or person) — primary sense.
  • As an adverb: "moving jigjog down the road" — with a jolting, bobbing motion.
  • As an attributive adjective: "a jigjog foot-pace", "a jigjog trot" — describing an irregular, jolting rhythm.
  • Dialectal: the jog-trot itself — a slow, monotonous, bumping canter.
  • By extension, any irregular, bumpy, stop-and-start rhythm — of music, speech, or work — though this use is rare.
Near-synonym differentiation:
  • Jigjog vs trot: a trot is a standardized, rhythmic two-beat diagonal horse gait, steady and maintainable; a jigjog is irregular, springy, and jarring, lacking the trot's disciplined beat.
  • Jigjog vs jolt: a jolt is a single sudden shock or lurch; a jigjog is a continuous series of small bounces.
  • Jigjog vs hobble: hobble implies lameness, pain, or structural damage that restricts movement; jigjog is energetic motion that is merely uncoordinated.
  • Jigjog vs clatter: clatter foregrounds the loud noise of feet or wheels striking a hard surface; jigjog focuses on the bouncing motion itself.
  • Jigjog vs shuffle: shuffle is a dragging, sliding walk; jigjog is a bouncy, up-and-down motion.
  • Jigjog vs bounce: bounce is any springy rebound and is neutral; jigjog specifically names an irregular, jolting, comically ungraceful bobbing.
Register and usage. Jigjog is a colloquial, dialectal, now rather rare word, beloved of descriptive and comic prose. It is emphatically onomatopoeic and expressive — its very sound enacts its meaning — which makes it hard to translate and resistant to formal use. In the excerpt, "a jigjog foot-pace" describes the hurried, bouncy, undignified way the speaker's companions once moved them about: faster than a walk, less fluid than a gallop, a hasty, erratic, jolting rhythm that contrasts sharply with the slow, stately approach the speaker later performs with style. The word is a small triumph of sound symbolism: it lets the reader feel the bumping in the mouth as it is read.
