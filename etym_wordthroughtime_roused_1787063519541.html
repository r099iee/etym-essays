- [14th–15th c.] - [Medieval Falconry & Hunting Origin] Emerged in Anglo-Norman and Middle English sporting manuals as rousen, describing a hunting hawk shaking its feathers, or huntsmen driving game from their lairs.
- [16th–17th c.] - [Human Awakening & Emotional Stirring] Expanded dramatically into Elizabethan literature and drama to describe waking sleeping humans, shaking off melancholy, and inciting passion or martial fury.
- [18th–19th c.] - [Political & Military Mobilization] Standardized in patriotic and revolutionary discourse for rallying entire nations, armies, and working-class movements ("roused to arms", "roused to action").
- [20th–21st c.] - [Everyday Wakefulness & Technical Jargon] Preserved as an evocative literary term for physical waking, emotional inspiration, and specialized industrial agitation in brewing and chemical engineering.
