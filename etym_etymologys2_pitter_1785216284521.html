CHAIN FOR ‘PITTER’ (imitative; no PIE root is reconstructable):
1. Echoic base — pit, an imitative element for a light, quick tap, first evidenced inside the reduplicated adverbial pitapat / pit-a-pat “with a quick trembling beat, of the heart” (16th c.).
2. pit + frequentative suffix -er → pitter, used within the reduplicative pair pitter-patter “a rapid light double-tapping” (the pair attested late 17th c.).
3. The noun pitter “the lighter, quicker element of the pitter-patter beat” (semi-independent use attested from the 18th–19th c.) → PITTER.
Note: pitter never had an independent pre-history; it was born inside the ablaut-reduplication system of English echo-words and only later detached from patter. Its derivation is onomatopoeic, so no attested PIE or even Old English ancestor exists.
