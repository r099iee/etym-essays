- 10th–11th c. - Established as a powerful feudal castellany based at the fortified promontory of Montmorency north of Paris, led by Bouchard I (Bouchard le Barbu); recognized as one of the preeminent regional military dynasties of the Île-de-France.
- 12th–14th c. - Rose to supreme national prominence under the Capetian monarchy, assuming the proud hereditary title Premiers Barons Chrétiens (First Christian Barons) and Premiers Barons de France; held the premier military office of Constable of France.
- 16th c. (Renaissance) - Reached the pinnacle of royal favor and wealth under Anne de Montmorency (1492–1567), Grand Master and Constable of France, who was elevated to Duke of Montmorency and built the magnificent Château d'Écouen and Château de Chantilly.
- 17th–18th c. (Ancien Régime) - Divided into illustrious cadet branches (Montmorency-Bouteville, Montmorency-Laval, Montmorency-Luxembourg); celebrated in European court society as the archetype of supreme aristocratic pedigree.
- 1789–1815 (Revolutionary & Napoleonic Eras) - Faced persecution during the Reign of Terror; scions joined the royalist émigré diaspora, where their name served in foreign capitals (such as St. Petersburg and London) as the gold standard of French noble legitimacy.
- 19th c.–Present - Immortalized in historical and literary works (e.g., Tolstoy's War and Peace, Chateaubriand's memoirs, and French historiography) as the quintessential symbol of ancient French dynastic peerage and feudal antiquity.
