QUILTS — plural of quilt, a warm bed covering made of a padded layer enclosed between two layers of fabric and kept in place by lines of through-stitching. 1. The sense in this passage. In David Hawkes's translation of The Story of the Stone (Hong lou meng), Xi-feng has already sent across some lilac-coloured hangings, brocade quilts, satin coverlets "and the like" for Dai-yu's bedding. Here "brocade quilts" are not neutral bedding: they signal status, textile luxury and the Jia household's capacity to wrap a vulnerable guest in soft, protective comfort. The plural form and the collocation with brocade push the sense away from a humble patchwork coverlet toward courtly, multi-layered bedclothes whose stitching and fabric advertise wealth as much as warmth. The tone is material and atmospheric — comfort as social enclosure — rather than merely practical insulation against cold. In the inventory of soft furnishings that constructs Dai-yu's sheltered, pampered environment, the quilts are part of a system in which warmth is inseparable from rank, gift-exchange and feminine household management. 2. Core meaning. A quilt is a padded bed covering in which a layer of filling (wool, cotton, down or silk) is enclosed between two layers of fabric and secured by lines of stitching that pass through all the layers, so that the filling cannot shift out of place. The through-stitching — not merely the padding — is the defining feature of the object. 3. Full polysemy inventory.
  • Bed covering (primary sense): a padded coverlet for warmth, ranging from humble patchwork to luxurious brocade, silk or velvet.
  • The craft and technique: "quilting" — the stitching itself and the act of making a quilt; "to quilt" means to stitch two fabrics together through a layer of padding so the filling stays put.
  • Quilted garments and materials: a quilted jacket, doublet or waistcoat, or quilted armour — padding applied to clothing, historically also for defensive wear.
  • Sleeping quilt (modern outdoor jargon): an ultralight down-filled sleeping-bag alternative used by campers and backpackers, from the 21st-century gear industry.
  • Figurative and pattern senses: anything made or conceived in patchwork fashion, as in "a patchwork quilt of cultures or policies"; quilted textures in geology, materials science and design.
  • Historical sense: in earlier English and in the Romance source words, the term could also name a mattress or stuffed pad (from Latin culcita), the ancestor covering both cushion and bedcover.
4. Register and collocations. Typical collocations include "patchwork quilt", "down quilt", "silk / brocade quilt", "quilted coverlet", "quilted jacket", and the verb forms "to quilt a garment" and "quilting bee". The register ranges from household and craft English through technical textile terminology to historical and literary description of elite interiors. In the novel's register, the word participates in a scene of domestic display in which bedding doubles as a demonstration of hospitality and rank. 5. The semantic bridge. The word descends from Latin culcita, "mattress, cushion, stuffed sack" — a padded thing in the most concrete sense. As the object evolved from seat and pad to bed-covering, the defining feature became the through-stitching that traps the filling; the noun quilt and the verb to quilt then split apart, and every modern sense — bed covering, craft, garment, gear — still preserves the double logic of padding plus stitching. Brocade quilts in this scene occupy the luxury-bedding niche: padded, stitched, fabric-rich, and freighted with social meaning.
