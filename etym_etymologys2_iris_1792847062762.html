Ancient Greek (8th c. BCE, Homer)
ἶρις (îris)
‘rainbow, halo round sun or moon’
‘also the goddess Iris, messenger’
↓
Classical Greek, botanical use (4th–1st c. BCE)
ἶρις
‘the iris plant, named for its’
‘many rainbow-like colours’
↓
Latin, borrowed (1st c. BCE)
īris
‘rainbow, iris plant, rainbow gem’
↓
Middle English, via Old French and Latin (14th c.)
iris
‘rainbow, the iris flower’
↓
Modern English (current)
iris
‘flower, ring of the eye, lens’
‘diaphragm’

Note: the deeper origin of Greek îris is unknown and debated. Older handbooks guessed at links with words for speaking or rainbows elsewhere, but modern authorities such as Beekes treat it as possibly Pre-Greek substrate vocabulary with no secure deeper root, so the chain above begins honestly with the attested Greek form.
