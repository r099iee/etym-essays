• diverse: Similar because it describes variety; differs in referring to the mixture itself rather than to the deliberate act of selecting from many sources.
  • varied: Similar in range; differs in being neutral and general, with no suggestion of taste or choice.
  • wide-ranging: Similar in breadth; differs in emphasising extent of coverage rather than the combining of different traditions.
  • heterogeneous: Similar in mixed composition; differs in being technical and neutral, describing the product rather than the selector’s method.
  • catholic: Similar in breadth of taste; differs in implying generous universality and sympathy rather than selective borrowing.
  • syncretic: Similar in combining traditions; differs in implying fusion into a new whole, especially in religion or philosophy, where eclectic only implies selection.
  • hybrid: Similar in mixed origin; differs in focusing on the crossing of two or more forms, not on a person’s discriminating taste.
  • magpie: Similar as a metaphor for collecting from everywhere; differs in being informal and disparaging, suggesting hoarding without discrimination.
  • selective: Similar in the root sense of choosing; differs in emphasising discrimination and exclusion rather than breadth of sources.
  • miscellaneous: Similar in mixture; differs in implying randomness and lack of principle, exactly what eclectic claims not to be.
  • broad: Similar in scope; differs in being a plain measure of width, without any implication of combining distinct sources.
