- Late Latin (c. 4th–6th c.): flasco / flasconem designated a bottle or wicker-encased vessel for liquids.
- Old French (c. 12th c.): Developed into flacon / flascon, denoting a large container or pitcher for wine.
- Middle English (c. 14th c.): Borrowed into English as flagon / flagoun, establishing itself as the standard tavern and banquet serving vessel.
- 17th – 18th Century: Codified in Anglican ecclesiastical canons as the sacred vessel for communion wine; used as a commercial measure of liquid volume.
- 19th Century – Modern: Phased out of everyday household utility in favor of modern glass bottles and pitchers; preserved in fantasy literature, historical fiction, and liturgical church plate.
