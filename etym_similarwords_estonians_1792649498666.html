• Finns: Similar because both belong to the Finnic branch of the Uralic language family, share profound cultural, linguistic, and choral traditions, and inhabit adjacent shores of the Gulf of Finland; differs because Finns inhabit the Scandinavian-Shield boreal north, were historically shaped by Swedish crown rule rather than Baltic German feudal serfdom, and speak Finnish.
  • Baltic peoples: Similar because both are indigenous inhabitants of the eastern Baltic littoral and share modern geopolitical solidarity in the Baltic states (Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania); differs because Baltic peoples (Latvians and Lithuanians) speak Indo-European Baltic languages, whereas Estonians are Uralic-speaking Finnic people.
  • Livonians: Similar because Livonians are the closest historical Finnic southern neighbors of Estonians, inhabiting the Gulf of Riga and sharing ancient folklore, fishing, and linguistic roots; differs because Livonian is an almost extinct indigenous language of Latvia, and Livonians never achieved modern sovereign statehood.
  • Maarahvas: Similar because it is the exact historical endonym (autonym) by which ethnic Estonians designated themselves for centuries prior to the 1850s; differs because maarahvas literally emphasizes agrarian peasant connection to the local soil ("earth-folk") rather than modern national sovereignty or geopolitical citizenship.
  • Balts: Similar because common colloquial usage loosely groups Estonians into the regional "Baltic" collective; differs because linguistically and anthropologically Balts refers strictly to Baltic Indo-European speakers (Latvians, Lithuanians, Old Prussians), excluding Finnic Estonians.
  • Karelians: Similar because Karelians are closely related Finnic kin who also preserved archaic runic folk singing (the source of the *Kalevala*); differs because Karelians inhabit the northwestern borderlands of Russia and eastern Finland and were culturally influenced by Russian Orthodoxy rather than Lutheran Baltic German institutions.
  • Votians: Similar because Votians are an indigenous Finnic minority historically dwelling directly northeast of Lake Peipus in Ingria, linguistically closest to North Estonian dialects; differs because Votians were absorbed into the Russian cultural sphere and are today critically endangered.
  • Scandinavians: Similar because Estonians share deep historical, maritime, religious (Lutheran), and societal values with Nordic cultures, often self-identifying as a Nordic country; differs because Scandinavians speak North Germanic Indo-European languages and possess distinct historical statehood lineages.
  • Compatriots / Countrymen: Similar because both denote fellow members of a shared civic or cultural homeland; differs because compatriots is an abstract political descriptor of shared citizenship, whereas Estonians specifically denotes a distinct historical ethno-linguistic group and nationality.
  • Citizens of Estonia: Similar because both describe the legal population of the Estonian state; differs because citizens of Estonia is a purely civic and legal status that includes Russian, Ukrainian, and other ethnic minorities holding Estonian passports, whereas Estonians often carries specific ethnic and linguistic connotations.
