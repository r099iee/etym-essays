- 17th Century (Early Modern English) - Emerged in British road surveys, travel diaries, and turnpike legislation as stone pillars physically erected every thousand paces or mile to reckon highway distance.
- 18th to Early 19th Century (Turnpike Era & Industrial Revolution) - Solidified as standardized stone monuments mandated by British Turnpike Trusts; synonymous with physical travel, coaching halts, postal stages, and the fatigue of long-distance walking.
- Mid-19th Century (Victorian Era) - Acquired strong figurative and literary resonance in Victorian novels and essays, shifting from physical highway stones to significant stages in human lifespan, careers, and national history (prominently used by Oscar Wilde in *The Devoted Friend*).
- 20th Century (Modern Science & Systems Engineering) - Formalized in developmental psychology (pediatric developmental milestones) and project management (Gantt charts, critical path deliverables, and engineering progress checkpoints).
- 21st Century (Contemporary Digital Era) - Universally deployed in tech development (software release milestones), corporate roadmaps, and personal celebration vernacular across global English.
