• Sacking: Similar because both describe putting goods into coarse containers; differs because sacking names the rough jute material and the sack itself, while bagging names the operation of filling and the resulting bulge.
  • Packing: Similar because both prepare goods for transport or sale; differs because packing is the broad general term (boxes, crates, cases), while bagging specifies flexible sacks and their sagging shape.
  • Baling: Similar because both compress harvests into transportable units; differs because baling presses hay, wool, or cotton into hard rectangular bales, while bagging pours loose produce into soft sacks.
  • Pocketing: Similar because both put something into a small container for keeps; differs because pocketing is personal and often sly (pocketing change, pocketing an insult), while bagging is commercial or hearty and out in the open.
  • Sagging: Similar because both describe hanging loose under weight; differs because sagging is pure droop without fullness (sagging roof, sagging spirits), while bagging implies bellied fullness like a loaded sack.
  • Bulging: Similar because both picture swelling outward with contents; differs because bulging stresses tight outward pressure (bulging muscles, bulging files), while bagging stresses loose wrinkled fullness with room to spare.
  • Harvesting: Similar because in the source sentence bagging is one harvest operation among many; differs because harvesting is the whole gathering of the crop, while bagging is only its final packing stage.
  • Capturing: Similar because sporting bagging means securing game or prizes; differs because capturing is formal and often military or technical, while bagging is cheerful slang with the game-bag in view.
  • Hoarding: Similar because both accumulate goods into store; differs because hoarding stresses secretive piling for oneself, while bagging stresses the honest commercial filling of sacks for market.
