- Old English–Middle English (as *blǣtan* / *bleten*): Used purely in pastoral agriculture to describe the natural vocal cry of sheep, goats, and lambs. - Purely descriptive agrarian terminology.
- 1500s–1600s: Extended figuratively in Early Modern English to describe human beings crying out in distress, weeping loudly, or uttering weak, trembling laments. - Conveyed helplessness and pitiful vocalization.
- 1800s–Early 1900s: Solidified in British and Australian colloquial speech as an agent noun for a chronic whiner, foolish chatterer, or socially inept talker (popularized in Wodehousian humor). - Carried lighthearted derision, affectionate contempt, and social comedy.
- Mid-1900s–Present: Retained in British/Commonwealth colloquial registers for annoying complainers or informants, maintaining its expressive pastoral caricature of sheep-like futility.
