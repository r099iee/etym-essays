• Cower: Similar because both signify shrinking in terror; differs because cower describes an overt, physical crouching or stooping posture adopted to avoid a blow or escape notice, whereas quail emphasizes the internal psychological loss of courage and heart.
  • Flinch: Similar because both denote an instinctive reaction to danger; differs because flinch is a momentary, spasmodic, involuntary physical wince or jerk away from immediate physical pain or impact, while quail represents a sustained, lingering paralysis of resolution.
  • Blench: Similar because both describe recoiling or faltering in dread; differs because blench is an archaic, poetic near-synonym heavily tied to turning pale (blanching) and flinching back from a horrifying sight.
  • Quake: Similar because both involve intense bodily reactions to terror; differs because quake denotes continuous, violent physical shaking, trembling, or vibrating (as an earthquake or shaking knees), whereas quail focuses on the collapse of bravery.
  • Shrink: Similar because both denote drawing back from something daunting; differs because shrink is a broad, common verb of contraction and aversion (e.g., shrinking from responsibility), lacking quail's dramatic, elevated literary register.
  • Recoil: Similar because both involve drawing back; differs because recoil is a dynamic, spring-like physical rebound away from something disgusting, horrifying, or explosive (like the kick of a gun).
  • Wince: Similar because both register distress; differs because wince is a brief facial grimace or slight bodily flinch triggered by acute physical pain or embarrassing memories.
  • Tremble: Similar because fear produces both; differs because tremble describes involuntary, rhythmic shivering or quivering of the limbs or voice.
  • Funk (British Slang): Similar because in Bertie Wooster's vocabulary both describe cowardice; differs because funk is colloquial, informal British public-school slang for panicky, disgraceful cowardice or evasion of duty ('in a blue funk').
  • Waver: Similar because both involve weakening resolve; differs because waver signifies indecision, hesitation, or fluctuating back and forth between two choices, rather than outright terror.
