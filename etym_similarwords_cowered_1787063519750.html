• Crouch: Similar because both mean to lower the body close to the ground; differs because "crouch" is a neutral posture (for hiding, springing, or resting), while "cower" specifically adds fear or submission and usually implies trembling.
  • Cringe: Similar because shrinking from something; differs because "cringe" stresses recoiling with the body drawn in, often from disgust or embarrassment as much as fear, and now dominates in figurative use ("cringe-worthy").
  • Shrink: Similar because drawing back or away from a threat; differs because "shrink" can be purely figurative or gradual and does not imply crouching low.
  • Flinch / Wince: Similar because a momentary reaction of fear or pain; differs because both are quick, small, involuntary responses, while "cower" describes a sustained, collapsed posture over time.
  • Quail: Similar because losing courage and shrinking; differs because "quail" is mostly literary and refers to the inner loss of heart (often "to quail at"), not necessarily to any physical crouching.
  • Grovel: Similar because abasing oneself low to the ground; differs because "grovel" adds abjectness and often crawling before a superior — an element of active self-humiliation that "cower" lacks.
  • Huddle: Similar because crouching or pressing together; differs because "huddle" describes people (or animals) drawing together for warmth or protection, without the individual terror and trembling of "cower".
  • Skulk: Similar because keeping low and out of sight; differs because "skulk" stresses sneaking with hostile or shameful intent, whereas "cower" stresses passive fear.
  • Tremble / Shudder: Similar because bodily reactions to fear; differs because these describe the shaking itself rather than the posture of lowering the body.
