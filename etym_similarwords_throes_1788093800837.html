• Pangs: Similar because both name sudden sharp pains, especially of birth; differs because pangs are individual stabs of pain, while throes are the whole convulsive struggle — much larger in scale.
  • Spasms: Similar because both name involuntary muscular contractions; differs because a spasm is a clinical single contraction, while throes is the dramatic, literary word for the whole violent bout.
  • Convulsions: Similar because both name violent involuntary bodily motion; differs because convulsions is the medical term for the motions themselves, while throes emphasises the accompanying agony and struggle.
  • Paroxysms: Similar because both name sudden violent attacks of pain; differs because paroxysm is technical (a paroxysm of coughing), while throe is archaic, poetic and chiefly plural.
  • Agony: Similar because both mean "extreme suffering"; differs because agony is the general word for the suffering itself, while throes are the visible bodily struggles of death or birth.
  • Anguish: Similar because both mean "intense distress"; differs because anguish is mental and emotional (the anguish of loss), while throes are physical convulsions, or processes compared to them.
  • Travail: Similar because both name painful labour, especially childbirth; differs because travail emphasises the toil and effort of labour and now usually means struggle generally, while throes keep the physical pang.
  • Torment: Similar because both mean "severe pain"; differs because torment implies inflicted, sustained torture (divine or human), while throes are the natural spasms of the body or of a process.
  • Torture: Similar because both mean "extreme inflicted pain"; differs because torture is deliberately inflicted by an agent, while throes are suffered without any torturer.
  • Distress: Similar because both name severe strain; differs because distress is broad and often emotional, while throes is dramatic and physical — the word of the deathbed and the battlefield.
  • Suffering: Similar because both name the experience of pain; differs because suffering is the long-term, general condition, while throes are acute, violent spasms at a crisis.
  • Struggle: Similar because both name a violent effort; differs because struggle is neutral about pain (a struggle can be joyful), while throes are agonising — and throes always implies that the process is gripping the victim, not the other way round.
  • Fits: Similar because both name seizure-like bodily states; differs because fits is the everyday word for seizures (a fit of coughing, an epileptic fit), while throes is literary and reserved for death, birth and grand upheavals.
  • Seizures: Similar because both name uncontrolled bodily attacks; differs because seizure is strictly clinical, while throes is poetic and metaphorical.
  • Death rattle: Similar because both belong to the vocabulary of dying; differs because death rattle is the specific audible breathing sound of the last moments, while throes are the convulsions themselves, and extend to birth and to metaphor.
