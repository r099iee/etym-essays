dubiously — In the manner of one who is doubtful, uncertain, or suspicious; in a way that expresses or implies doubt; in the manner of someone literally "of two minds." The word is the adverb of dubious, and it does exact dramatic work in the passage: Maspic "sniff[s] the air dubiously" on the Martian waste, and the adverb colors the whole gesture with doubt, suspicion, and a certain intellectual caution. The nuance of the adverb, as opposed to the adjective, is that it makes uncertainty a manner — a way of holding the body, of approaching the world. The sniffing is tentative because the character is not sure what his senses are telling him in an alien place; the word hesitates between two readings, "with doubt" and "with suspicion," and in that hesitation lives the comedy and the tension of the line. It is also, subtly, a word about the mind divided against itself: etymologically, to be dubious is to be "of two minds" (Latin dubius, built on duo, "two"), and the adverb paints a man literally pulled between trusting and distrusting the air he breathes.

The word's full range of senses may be set out as follows:

  • Manner-sense (the passage's use): in a doubtful, uncertain manner — done with hesitation, suspicion, or intellectual caution; the manner of a person of two minds.
  • Verdict-sense: in a manner suggesting questionable quality, honesty, or legitimacy — "dubiously acquired," "a dubiously obtained permit"; here the doubt is transferred from the agent to the object, which is judged of doubtful worth.
  • Intentional/affect-sense: with an expression or tone that registers distrust or disbelief — "he looked at me dubiously"; the word shades toward skeptical or suspicious in face-to-face uses.

Its synonyms carve the same territory at different angles:

  • Doubtfully is the plainest — uncertainty without suspicion.
  • Skeptically implies a habit or method of questioning, an intellectual posture rather than a single moment.
  • Suspiciously narrows to distrust of a specific object and can sound accusatory.
  • Warily names caution in the face of danger, fear of a concrete threat.
  • Questioningly implies an open request for information.
  • Dubiously is the broadest of the group: it fuses hesitation, doubt, and mild distrust, and it carries a learned, Latin sheen that doubtfully lacks — as befits a word built on the Latin for "two."

In collocation, dubiously attaches to verbs of gesture and judgment — looked dubiously, sniffed dubiously, shrugged dubiously, eyed dubiously, regarded dubiously — and to participles of acquisition and provenance — dubiously obtained, dubiously acquired. Register: formal-literary, with a Latinate flavor; in narrative prose it almost always modifies a bodily gesture, performing doubt rather than stating it, which is exactly the work it does on Maspic's sniff.
