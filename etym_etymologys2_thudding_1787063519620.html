Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*(s)tewd- / *tewd-
"to strike, beat, thrust, push"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*þuddijaną
"to push, thrust, strike violently"
↓
Old English (c. 9th c.)
þyddan / þēodan
"to thrust, press, strike, stab"
↓
Northern Middle English / Early Scots (c. 1375)
thud
"a violent blast of wind; a heavy blow"
↓
Middle Scots / Early Modern English (c. 1513)
thudden / thudde
"to make a dull sound of impact; to fall with a blow"
↓
Modern English (c. 1720)
thudding
"producing a heavy dull sound; beating with low-frequency impact"
Note: Historical evolution strongly reinforced by phonaesthetic sound symbolism mimicking low acoustic resonance.
