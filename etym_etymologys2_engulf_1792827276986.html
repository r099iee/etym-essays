Ancient Greek (8th c. BCE, Homer)
κόλπος (kólpos)
‘bosom, lap; bay, gulf’
↓
Late Latin (c. 4th–6th c.)
colfus, colpus
‘bay, gulf, bosom of a sail’
↓
Italian (13th c., Dante)
golfo
‘gulf, bay, deep water’
↓
Old French (14th c.)
golfe
‘gulf, bay; whirlpool’
↓
Middle English (16th c., 1550s)
gulf
‘deep chasm; devouring whirlpool’
↓
Modern English (1550s, en- + gulf)
engulf
‘to swallow up as in a gulf’

Note: the prefix en- continues Latin in- meaning ‘in, into’, from PIE *en meaning ‘in’; Greek kólpos itself has no certain deeper etymology and is treated by Beekes as possibly Pre-Greek substrate vocabulary.
