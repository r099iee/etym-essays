• Canines: Similar because both refer to elongated, pointed mammalian teeth frequently deployed for seizing prey or physical defense; differs because canines is a precise anatomical dental classification (the teeth situated between incisors and premolars) that typically remain enclosed within the mouth, whereas tusks project prominently and continuously beyond the lips and can originate from incisors as well as canines.
  • Fangs: Similar because both designate sharp, protruding teeth adapted for offensive wounding or predation; differs because fangs typically denote hollow or grooved teeth used to deliver biochemical venom (as in vipers and spiders) or the sharp grasping teeth of carnivores, whereas tusks are massive, continuously growing dentin structures primarily utilized for sparring, digging, and physical deterrence rather than venom injection.
  • Horns: Similar because both are prominent, pointed cranial appendages utilized for intraspecific sparring, sexual display, and territorial defense; differs because horns develop directly from the frontal cranial bones and consist of an interior bony core enveloped by a permanent keratin sheath (in bovids) or compressed keratin fibers (in rhinoceroses), whereas tusks are specialized, mineralized teeth composed of dentin and ivory.
  • Antlers: Similar because both are formidable projecting weapons borne on the animal's head used to wage dominance battles during the mating season; differs because antlers are true bone structures grown, shed, and regenerated annually by male cervids (deer), whereas tusks are permanent, non-branching dental organs that grow perpetually without shedding.
  • Ivory: Similar because ivory is the exact mineralized substance (dense dentin) of which mammalian tusks are predominantly composed; differs because ivory designates the chemical material, commercial commodity, or artistic carving medium, whereas tusks refers to the living, biological organ and anatomical implement itself.
  • Pincers: Similar because both function in the animal kingdom as formidable external protruding implements used for grasping, combat, and mechanical defense; differs because pincers are jointed invertebrate chelae or modified limbs (as found in crustaceans and scorpions), possessing no dental or skeletal relationship to mammalian tusks.
