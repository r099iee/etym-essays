- 14th to 17th Century: Established as a distinguished Russian noble surname (*Novosil'tsev* / *Novosil'tsov*), derived from the toponym *Novosil* and the Old East Slavic term *novosel* (new settler, new homesteader).
- 1801 to 1805: Nikolai Novosiltsov rose to international prominence as a member of Alexander I's "Secret Committee" (*Neglasny Komitet*) and as special envoy to London, where the "Novosiltsov Dispatch / Instructions" laid the diplomatic foundations for the Third Coalition against Napoleon.
- 1813 to 1830: Novosiltsov served as the powerful Imperial Commissioner in the Kingdom of Poland, simultaneously drafting the *Charter of the State of the Russian Empire* (1820)—the first federal constitutional project in Russian history—while drawing fierce opposition from Polish patriots.
- 1860s (Tolstoy's *War and Peace*): Immortalized worldwide in the opening page of Leo Tolstoy's *War and Peace* (Book 1, Chapter 1) as a cultural and diplomatic shorthand for the tense geopolitical maneuvers of the St. Petersburg court in July 1805.
- 20th to 21st Century: Evaluated in global historiography as a complex, contradictory imperial figure: an enlightened early liberal constitutional thinker and diplomatic visionary on one hand, and a strict imperial administrator of the Nicholas I era on the other.
