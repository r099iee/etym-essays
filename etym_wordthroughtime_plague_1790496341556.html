- Latin plaga - a blow, stroke, wound, then misfortune; physical and moral at once
- Late Latin / Vulgate plagae - the ten plagues of Egypt as divine blows; scriptural and terrible
- 14th c. Black Death as the Plague - bubonic pandemic fixes plague as epidemic horror; mass graves and quarantine
- Shakespeare's curse - a plague on both your houses; rhetorical, everyday, hyperbolic
- 1665 Great Plague of London - Bills of Mortality, searchers, red crosses; administrative and literary (Pepys, Defoe)
- Regency school plague-and-pet (Ruskin's aunt) - beloved little tormentor; mischievous and affectionate
- Modern harass-verb and metaphor - plagued by emails, a plague of locusts, to avoid like the plague; productive, often jocular
