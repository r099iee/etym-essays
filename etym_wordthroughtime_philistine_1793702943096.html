- Ancient People - Adversaries of Israelites, biblical connotation of foreignness, opposition, and eventual defeat.
- German University Slang (late 17th c.) - Pejorative for townspeople, signifying uncultured, materialistic, anti-intellectual outsiders to academic life.
- English Literary Critique (19th c.) - Popularized by Matthew Arnold to describe the English middle class, embodying materialism, narrow-mindedness, and indifference to high culture and intellectual pursuits.
- Modern Usage - A person smugly conventional, whose interests are utilitarian and commercial, lacking aesthetic sensibility and often hostile to art, literature, and intellectual depth.
