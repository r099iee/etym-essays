Chain for root word 'bogs' (non-PIE, Celtic origin): - Proto-Celtic *boggo- 'soft, tender, yielding' (reconstructed; the base of the Celtic soft-words; exact form before the 1st millennium BCE uncertain, though *boggo- is the standard reconstruction). - Old Irish bocc (also boc) 'soft, tender, pliant, damp' (attested in the early medieval period; the ancestor of modern Irish bog 'soft, tender'). - Irish bog 'soft, damp, tender' + place/abstract suffix -ach → Irish bogach 'soft ground, marsh, bog, quaking place' (attested; the exact source of the English loan). - English bog 'wet, spongy, waterlogged ground' (borrowed from Irish/Scots Gaelic in the early 16th century; among the earliest English uses is Gavin Douglas's Eneados, c. 1513). - Modern English bogs, the plural of bog (the standard plural, attested from the 16th century onward). - Final node, bold: bogs. Debate note: the word has no certain Proto-Indo-European ancestry. Some etymologists have speculated about a link to PIE *bʰewgʰ- 'to bend' or *bʰew- 'to swell, inflate,' imagining 'swollen, soft ground,' but the Celtic form *boggo- is not securely connected to any PIE root, and the deeper origin is best stated as unknown. The word is essentially confined to Celtic and to the languages that borrowed from it.
