• Threw: Similar because both mean to propel something through the air by a movement of the arm; differs because threw is the neutral, general term while flung adds force, speed, or emotional energy.
  • Hurled: Similar because both imply great force and violence; differs because hurled stresses the power and distance of the throw more explicitly than flung, which emphasises suddenness and abandon.
  • Cast: Similar because both describe throwing; differs because cast is more formal, literary, and often implies aim or deliberation (e.g. casting a net or a glance), whereas flung is careless and energetic.
  • Tossed: Similar because both describe throwing something lightly; differs because tossed suggests a gentle, casual, or playful motion, far weaker in force than flung.
  • Sling: Similar because both involve vigorous throwing, often of a long object or by a swinging motion; differs because sling carries ballistic or weaponry overtones, while flung is more general.
  • Pitched: Similar because both mean to throw; differs because pitched emphasises aim and distance (as in pitching a ball or a tent) and lacks the emotional violence of flung.
