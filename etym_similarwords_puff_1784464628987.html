• Pant: Similar because both mean to breathe hard and fast from exertion; differs because pant stresses rapid, shallow, open-mouthed breathing and is usually of running animals and people, while puff stresses the audible, blasting quality of each breath.
  • Gasp: Similar because both describe a sharp intake or expulsion of breath; differs because gasp is a sudden, involuntary breath often of surprise or pain, while puff is a regular, effortful or deliberate expulsion of air.
  • Huff: Similar because both describe a burst of breath and a state of offended breathing; differs because huff implies indignation or sulkiness ('in a huff') and a strong single expulsion, while puff is about exertion and blowing, without the offended mood.
  • Blow: Similar because both verbs mean to produce a current of air from the mouth; differs because blow is the general verb for any air-current, while puff specifies a short, small, repeated emission or cloud.
  • Gust: Similar because both denote a sudden emission of air; differs because a gust is a strong, brief rush of wind, while a puff is a small, soft, often deliberate emission of air, smoke, or breath.
  • Whiff: Similar because both denote a faint emission of air or scent; differs because a whiff stresses smelling a faint odour, while a puff stresses a visible, cloud-like blowing or swelling of air or smoke.
  • Breath: Similar because both relate to the air taken in and expelled; differs because breath is the neutral, clinical term for the air itself, while puff is the vigorous, audible expulsion of it.
  • Blurb: Similar because both belong to promotional writing; differs because a blurb is a short publicity summary on a book cover, while puff (and puffery) is the broader practice of inflated, uncritical praise of anything.
  • Plug: Similar because both can mean an act of promotional boosting ('to plug a product'); differs because plug stresses repeated, often informal endorsement, while puff stresses hollow, inflated praise that swells the subject without substance.
  • Hype: Similar because both mean exaggerated promotion; differs because hype is modern marketing slang for intensive publicity campaigns, while puffery is the older, literary term for inflated praise.
