- Late 16th Century (Tudor / Elizabethan Botanical Era) - First documented in print in English herbals (John Gerard's *Herball*, 1597) as *Sweet William*, naturalized as an esteemed cottage-garden floral pink.
- 17th Century (Jacobean & Restoration Floriculture) - Cultivated universally across English rural gardens; simultaneously celebrated in British broadside ballads where "Sweet William" serves as the universal archetype of the steadfast, devoted lover.
- Mid-18th Century (Hanoverian vs. Jacobite Era, 1746) - Co-opted into post-Culloden political folklore celebrating Prince William, Duke of Cumberland (in contrast to the Scottish Jacobite labeling of ragwort as *Stinking Willy*), despite the name predating the Duke by over 150 years.
- 19th Century (Victorian Floriography) - Formally codified in the Victorian "Language of Flowers" as the universal botanical emblem of gallantry, dexterity, and true devotion; prominently featured in rural literature by Oscar Wilde.
- 21st Century (Contemporary Heritage Gardening) - Re-entered global cultural consciousness in 2011 when Catherine Middleton included Sweet William in her royal bridal bouquet for Prince William, cementing its modern heritage prestige.
