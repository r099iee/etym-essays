• Creed: Similar because both share the exact same Latin root (*credo*) and denote an authoritative statement of fundamental beliefs; differs because creed is the fully naturalized English doublet most frequently applied to established religious confessions or broad political dogmas, whereas credo retains a classical Latin gravitas and is favored for personal, artistic, or organizational philosophies.
  • Tenet: Similar because both refer to a principle, doctrine, or belief held to be true; differs because a tenet is an individual, specific doctrine within a larger philosophical or religious system, whereas a credo represents the comprehensive, unified manifesto or overarching statement of faith.
  • Manifesto: Similar because both represent formal public declarations of intentions, motives, or views; differs because a manifesto is typically an urgent, combative, and politically or avant-gardely oriented call to public action (e.g., *The Communist Manifesto*, *The Futurist Manifesto*), whereas a credo emphasizes introspective conviction and enduring foundational values.
  • Doctrine: Similar because both encompass a body of principles or teachings that guide belief and practice; differs because doctrine carries strong institutional, legalistic, or dogmatic authority imposed from above by a church or state, whereas a credo can be entirely personal, spontaneous, and self-chosen.
  • Motto: Similar because both serve as guiding watchwords summarizing an individual's or institution's guiding aim; differs because a motto is a brief, catchy phrase or slogan (often heraldic), whereas a credo is a broader, substantive declaration of deep-seated philosophical or spiritual convictions.
