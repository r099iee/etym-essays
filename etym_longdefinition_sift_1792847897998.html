In the source sentence sift carries its great figurative sense: to examine words closely, shaking them as in a sieve, so that lies fall through and whatever truth remains is kept. Gollum's speech is a heap of half-truths, evasions, and wheedling, and the listener must sift his words exactly as a miller sifts meal: patiently, suspiciously, letting nothing pass unweighed.

Core literal meaning in detail: to sift is to pass a granular substance, flour, sand, ash, or grain, through a mesh or sieve so that fine particles fall through and coarse lumps, husks, and rubbish stay behind. The motion is rhythmic shaking; the tool is the sieve, one of humanity's oldest machines; the purpose is purification by separation. Bakers sift flour to aerate it and remove weevils, builders sift sand for mortar, prospectors sift gravel for gold, and every kitchen still keeps the small trembling instrument.

All senses, primary and secondary:

  • To pass through a sieve (primary literal sense, from Old English): shaking flour, soil, or ashes through mesh to sort fine from coarse. The bridge to all else is the visible act itself, separation by patient agitation, which the mind instantly recognises as a picture of thinking.
  • To examine closely in order to extract truth (primary figurative sense, the source meaning): to sift evidence, testimony, or data, keeping the sound and discarding the worthless. The bridge runs straight from the kitchen: statements are poured in a heap, shaken by questioning, and only what is solid remains in the mesh of judgement.
  • To sift through a mass of material (phrasal extension): to work slowly through papers, records, wreckage, or search results. The bridge shifts the sieve from tool to journey: the searcher moves through the heap as flour moves through mesh, and thoroughness is the point.
  • To sift out the unwanted (separative): to remove suspects from a list, errors from a manuscript, or applicants from a pile. The bridge inverts the emphasis: here the mesh keeps the good and the shaking discards the bad, but the sieving image serves either direction.
  • To fall or drift down finely, like sifted flour (intransitive, poetic): as when snow sifts through bare branches or light sifts through leaves. The bridge is the manner of descent: gentle, scattered, particle by particle, the world itself performing the miller's gesture.
  • Technical — SIFT in computing (acronymic homograph, 1999): the Scale-Invariant Feature Transform, an algorithm that sifts images for distinctive keypoints. The bridge is deliberate wordplay by its inventor: the program does to pixels what the sieve does to grain, keeping only the features that matter.

Register and connotation: sift is honest and methodical, the verb of patient inquiry, the standby of judges, scholars, auditors, and detectives. Unlike scrutinise, which can feel adversarial, or winnow, which belongs to harvest fields, sift suggests domestic patience applied to grave matters, the kitchen wisdom carried into the courtroom. Gandalf's use is characteristically shrewd: with a liar, charm and threats both fail, and only slow sifting serves.
