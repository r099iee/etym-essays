- Medieval food-fish: In Middle English, 'carp' named a hardy freshwater fish farmed in monastic fishponds and eaten as everyday table food. Register: plain, utilitarian.
- Ornamental estate fish: In the early modern era, carp were stocked in the ornamental ponds of manor houses as a sign of genteel wealth and leisure. Connotation: cultivated rural gentility.
- Modern common fish: Today the carp is seen as an abundant, mud-loving bottom-feeder of warm, sluggish waters; the word carries a faintly dismissive, plain-Jane flavour when set against more prized sport fish. Register: mildly disparaging.
