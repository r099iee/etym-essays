Proto-Semitic (reconstructed c. 3rd millennium BCE)
*šabat-
"to cease, desist, terminate labor"

Middle English (c. 13th–15th c.)

Note on historical debate: Philologists and Assyriologists have long debated whether the Hebrew root š-b-t is related to Akkadian šapattu or šabattu, which designated the Babylonian festival of the full moon on the fifteenth day of the lunar month (regarded as an 'unfavorable' or propitiatory day when kings abstained from certain activities). While 19th-century pan-Babylonian scholars argued that Israel borrowed the institution directly from Mesopotamia, modern Semitic philologists emphasize that the biblical šabbāṯ represents a distinct sociological and theological breakthrough: it severed the day of rest from astrological lunar phases, institutionalized an invariant seven-day cadence for all social classes including slaves and draught beasts, and grounded its observance in historical covenant and divine liberation rather than astrological dread.
