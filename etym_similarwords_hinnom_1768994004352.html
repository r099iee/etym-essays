• Gehenna: Similar because it names the same place of fiery punishment; differs because Gehenna is the Greek form used in the New Testament and the theological place of eschatological fire, while Hinnom is the historical valley itself.
  • Tophet: Similar because it is tied to the same biblical site; differs because Tophet names the specific cult-site within the valley where the fires to Molech burned, not the whole valley.
  • Sheol: Similar because it names the biblical underworld; differs because Sheol is the shadowy common grave of all the dead — gloomy but not fiery, a place of silence rather than punishment.
  • Hades: Similar because it denotes the realm of the dead; differs because Hades is the neutral Greek realm of the shades, with no connotation of torment or fire.
  • Tartarus: Similar because it names a place of punishment for the wicked; differs because Tartarus is the Greek pit of torment beneath Hades, mythological rather than biblical, and never a real place.
  • Hell: Similar because it denotes the place of eternal punishment; differs because hell is the general English and doctrinal term — generic and abstract — whereas Hinnom is a specific real valley whose name became a byword.
  • Abyss: Similar because it names a place of bottomless depth associated with the underworld; differs because the abyss is a pit of void or chaos, not a place of fire and judgement.
  • Inferno: Similar because it names a place of great fire and suffering; differs because inferno is literary and Dantean — an image of burning, not a scriptural geography with a documented history.
  • Perdition: Similar because it names eternal damnation or ruin; differs because perdition is an abstract state of utter loss and ruin, not a place or a valley with a name.
  • Netherworld: Similar because it names the world of the dead below; differs because netherworld is a broad, vague term covering many afterlives, with none of Hinnom's precise biblical and historical identity.
  • Pandemonium: Similar because it names the capital of hell in Milton's Paradise Lost; differs because Pandemonium is literary invention (Greek pan "all" + daimon "demon"), a place of demons and uproar, not a real valley of judgment.
  • Avernus: Similar because it names an ancient entrance to the underworld; differs because Avernus is the Roman lake-portal to Hades, a sulfurous but pre-Christian geography without Hinnom's moral judgment.
