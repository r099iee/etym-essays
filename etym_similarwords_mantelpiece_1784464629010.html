• Mantel: Similar because it names the fireplace shelf or hood and shares the same stem; differs because ‘mantel’ alone can name just the shelf or the hood in trade speech, while mantelpiece is the everyday compound for shelf-and-frame as a unit.
  • Chimneypiece: Similar because it is the surround over the fireplace; differs because it is the grander, more formal 18th-century architectural term for the whole structure in marble or stone, while mantelpiece is the homely everyday word.
  • Hearth: Similar because it belongs to the fireplace; differs because the hearth is the floor level of the fire, not the shelf above and around it.
  • Overmantel: Similar because it sits above the fireplace; differs because it names a panel or mirror above the shelf, not the shelf itself.
  • Fireplace: Similar because it names the whole hearth installation; differs because the fireplace is the complete structure where fire burns, while the mantelpiece is only its decorative shelf and surround.
  • Mantle: Similar because it is the older spelling and semantic twin of mantel, both from the Latin ‘cloak’ word; differs because mantle still primarily means a cloak or covering garment (and the Earth’s layer), while mantelpiece names the concrete shelf.
  • Grate: Similar because it belongs to the fireplace and is part of the hearth installation; differs because the grate is the metal basket holding the fire, not the shelf above it.
  • Ledge: Similar because both are flat projecting surfaces on which things can stand; differs because a ledge is any narrow projecting shelf in any context, while a mantelpiece is specifically the fireplace shelf.
  • Shelf: Similar because the mantelpiece is fundamentally a shelf; differs because shelf is the generic word for any flat board of storage, while mantelpiece names the particular ornamental fireplace unit with its surround.
