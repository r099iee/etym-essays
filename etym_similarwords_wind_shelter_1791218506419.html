• Windbreak: Similar because it blocks wind to protect people, crops or buildings; differs because it usually denotes a row of trees or permanent fence, more substantial than a branch screen.
  • Shelterbelt: Similar because it shelters land from wind; differs because it specifically means an extended planting of trees, an agricultural term, not a small camp screen.
  • Windscreen: Similar because it screens against wind on terraces and vehicles; differs because it often implies glass or transparent panels and British usage for car windscreens.
  • Lean-to: Similar because it is a minimal sloped shelter of branches or planks; differs because it has a roof for rain, while a wind-shelter may be only a vertical screen.
  • Bivouac: Similar because it is a temporary open shelter for travellers; differs because it denotes the encampment itself, often without structure, rather than the screen.
  • Bothy: Similar because it shelters hikers in exposed country; differs because it is a small lockable hut with walls and roof, far more enclosed.
  • Hut: Similar because it is a simple small dwelling; differs because a hut is roofed and hut-like permanence exceeds a mere wind screen.
  • Shack: Similar because it is a crudely built shelter; differs because it implies roofed, if ramshackle, habitation rather than an open screen.
  • Refuge: Similar because it offers protection from harsh conditions; differs because it is abstract and general, including mountain huts and legal asylum, not specifically anti-wind.
  • Screen: Similar because a wind-shelter screens airflow and view; differs because screen is generic for any partition, including indoor and decorative senses.
  • Hedge: Similar because hedges shelter gardens from wind; differs because hedge denotes living shrubs with boundary and wildlife functions beyond wind.
  • Fence: Similar because fences can break wind around yards and stock; differs because fence primarily marks boundary and containment, not microclimate.
  • Canopy: Similar because it gives overhead cover outdoors; differs because it protects from sun and rain from above, while wind-shelter protects laterally from draughts.
  • Cover: Similar because it denotes protection generally; differs because it is vague and includes foliage, lid and insurance senses far beyond wind.
  • Lee: Similar because being in the lee means being sheltered from wind; differs because lee is the sheltered side or position itself, not the structure providing it.
