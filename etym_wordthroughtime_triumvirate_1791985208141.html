- 1580s — the word appears in English, first as the name of the Roman office of triumvirs.
- 1650s — the sense extends to any group of three rulers or leaders, often with a suggestion of irregular power.
- 18th c. — political theory and satire use it for oligarchic cliques and for the Roman alliances.
- 19th c. — applied to non-political trios, from cabinets to schools of poets.
- 20th c. — common in journalism and corporate language for a three-person leadership team.
- 21st c. — still current, often half-ironic, and increasingly used of any influential trio.
