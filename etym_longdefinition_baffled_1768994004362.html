baffled: past participle of "to baffle," carrying two interlocked senses. The primary modern sense is total bewilderment or perplexity — the mind reduced to helpless confusion by something it cannot organize or understand ("the detectives were baffled"). The older, still-live sense is "thwarted, checked, frustrated, brought to nothing." In the passage — "a weird moaning as of baffled demons" — the word chiefly exploits the older sense: the storm-demons are thwarted, their moaning aimless and weird because the roaring zoorth has scattered their voices and rendered them impotent; they are not merely puzzled but defeated, turned back from their object, their purpose broken.

Polysemy and secondary senses:
  • Primary modern sense — bewildered / perplexed: utterly confused, unable to explain or solve: "baffled by the puzzle," "police are baffled."
  • Older sense — thwarted / frustrated: checked, defeated, prevented from achieving an aim: a baffled plan, a baffled enemy, baffled hopes. This is the sense in "baffled demons."
  • Mechanical / technical sense: "to baffle" (a current) — to check, deflect, or deaden a flow; hence the noun baffle, a plate or device (a baffle plate, a baffle board) that turns a fluid, sound, or light aside and reduces its force (from 17th-c. nautical "to baffle the wind," of sails so trimmed that the wind's force is wasted).
  • Historic ritual sense (c. 1540s) — to subject to public disgrace or infamy: the "baffling" of a perjured knight, whose spurs were hacked from his heels and his arms hung up ignominiously; a residue of this "disgrace" still tints the modern word, so that being baffled carries a faint undertone of being publicly undone, emptied, and mocked.
  • Register — "baffled" is the stock word of the detective story, the police report, and the scientific mystery; informal-to-neutral prose of narrative helplessness.
  • Context and texture — in the passage, "baffled demons" are agents of the storm itself: their moaning is "weird" and without direction because the howling zoorth has broken their voices. They are thwarted, their menace turned back upon themselves, and the participle makes a natural force into a defeated intelligence — the sand that stings the explorers' faces is the same element that has scattered the demons' voices, so that the storm is at once the baffler and the baffled.
  • Collocations — "baffled by," "a baffled attempt," "baffled expectations," "baffled rage," "baffled purpose," "utterly baffled"; the adjective "baffling" and the noun "bafflement" extend the same senses.
  • Register note — neutral to formal; the word is at home in journalism ("police remain baffled") and in literary narrative of helplessness, and it never sounds technical, which is precisely why the detective story has made it its own: the baffled detective is the archetype of the modern mind confronting the inexplicable.
