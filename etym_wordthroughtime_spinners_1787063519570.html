- Old English (c. 9th c.): spinnere referred to domestic artisans who drew out and spun fibers into wool or linen yarn.
- Middle English (c. 14th c.): Extended dialectally and poetically as a name for the spider itself ('long-legg'd spinner').
- 17th – 18th Century: Applied by naturalists to the silk-extruding organs (spinnerets) of spiders and silkworms; expanded during the Industrial Revolution to mechanized spinning machines.
- 19th Century: Adopted in sport angling for revolving metallic lures and in cricket for spin bowlers.
- Modern Era: Widely used across biological anatomy, textile engineering, cricket analytics, kinetic gadgets (fidget spinners), and literary descriptions of monstrous arachnids.
