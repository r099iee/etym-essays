• Danger: Similar because both name exposure to possible harm; differs because danger is the general, everyday term covering any degree of threat, while peril implies grave, immediate, life-threatening jeopardy and carries a distinctly literary weight.
  • Jeopardy: Similar because both mean exposure to serious harm or loss; differs because jeopardy emphasizes being in a vulnerable position where loss or conviction is imminent and at stake (double jeopardy, to put at risk), while peril stresses the active, present character of the threat itself.
  • Hazard: Similar because both can mean a source or cause of danger; differs because hazard refers to a potential obstacle or risky condition (a golf hazard, a chemical hazard) or a chance risk, whereas peril is the active state of extreme danger and the dangerous thing itself.
  • Risk: Similar because both involve the possibility of loss or harm; differs because risk is a quantifiable, calculated probability of loss, the word of insurance tables and decision theory, while peril is the visceral presence of mortal threat, not a statistic.
  • Threat: Similar because both denote something that endangers; differs because a threat is the announcement or sign of harm (a threat of war, a credible threat), whereas peril is the harm itself already present.
  • Menace: Similar because both convey a sense of impending harm; differs because menace emphasizes the menacing quality or person - a menacing appearance or a menacing figure - rather than the state of being endangered that peril denotes.
  • Endangerment: Similar because both describe the condition of being exposed to harm; differs because endangerment is a neutral, legalistic noun (child endangerment, reckless endangerment) with none of peril's literary gravity.
