• recondite: similar because both denote knowledge or language beyond ordinary understanding; differs because "recondite" stresses the hiddenness of the subject-matter (little-known), "abstruse" stresses the difficulty of the presentation.
  • obscure: similar because both mean hard to perceive or understand; differs because "obscure" ranges from dim/dark to unknown-to-the-public and is far more common, while "abstruse" is specifically intellectual opacity.
  • esoteric: similar because both suggest understanding reserved for the few; differs because "esoteric" implies an inner circle of initiates, "abstruse" merely difficulty.
  • arcane: similar because both convey mystery and hidden knowledge; differs because "arcane" suggests secrecy (Latin arcanum, "secret") and often the occult, "abstruse" scholarly complexity.
  • opaque: similar because both mean impenetrable to understanding; differs because "opaque" is usually about statement or style not letting light through, "abstruse" about the thinker's depth or obscurity.
  • inscrutable: similar because both mean unable to be understood; differs because "inscrutable" applies to faces, motives, and persons, "abstruse" to texts, arguments, and ideas.
  • incomprehensible: similar because both mean beyond grasp; differs because "incomprehensible" is absolute (cannot be understood at all), "abstruse" merely very hard.
  • knotty: similar because both describe intricately difficult problems; differs because "knotty" is colloquial and implies tangles to untie, "abstruse" formal and implies depth of concealment.
  • recherche: similar because both mark the rare, refined, and difficult; differs because "recherché" (French, "sought out") implies preciosity and affectation, "abstruse" honest difficulty.
  • deep / profound: similar because both suggest meaning below the surface; differs because "deep/profound" are admiring of depth, "abstruse" often implies excess or imposition.
