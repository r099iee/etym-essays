• Sputtering: Similar because both describe a rapid series of small explosive sounds; differs because sputtering stops at the noise and says nothing about weakness or outcome, while fizzling implies a flame or venture that is failing or dying out.
  • Sizzling: Similar because both name the hissing sound of something hot; differs because sizzling is the loud, appetizing hiss of fat in a hot pan — energetic, attractive, almost celebratory, the exact opposite of fizzling in spirit.
  • Hissing: Similar because both are high-pitched streams of escaping sound; differs because hissing is continuous, steady, and often deliberate, with no implication of weakness or extinction, whereas fizzling sputters weakly toward failure.
  • Crackling: Similar because both are sounds of burning; differs because crackling is the dry, sharp, vigorous snapping of burning wood, crisp and cheerful, while fizzling is damp, feeble, and on the verge of going out.
  • Whimpering: Similar because both describe ending weakly and feebly; differs because whimpering is a human or animal sound of distress, while fizzling is mechanical, gaseous, and comic.
  • Waning: Similar because both name growing weaker; differs because waning is gradual and neutral (the moon wanes), while fizzling is noisy, damp, and deflationary.
  • Sparkling: Similar because both involve small bursts of light and sound; differs because sparkling is bright, lively, and celebratory (sparkling wine, sparkling wit), the confident opposite of fizzling's damp failure.
  • Guttering: Similar because both describe a weak, dying flame; differs because guttering (of a candle) stresses the wavering, molten spilling of the flame, while fizzling stresses the sputtering sound and the impending failure.
