- Late 19th century: the ingredients are already slang — clip “strike smartly,” one “a blow,” and ear-hole “ear” — but the full phrase belongs to schoolboy and servant talk rather than literature.
- 1920s–30s: P. G. Wodehouse makes the construction a signature of Bertie Wooster’s narration; it becomes a marker of upper-class British comedy and period charm.
- Mid-20th century: the phrase survives as dated slang, increasingly associated with old-fashioned discipline and with the fictional world of Jeeves and Wooster.
- Late 20th century: it becomes a period quotation, used for pastiche and for signalling vintage Britishness; actual use declines sharply.
- 21st century: archaic and comic; understood mainly through Wodehouse reprints, adaptations, and language commentary, and avoided in serious writing because of its casual violence.
