Scraps is the plural concrete noun denoting small, detached, irregular pieces or leftover portions of a larger whole—most commonly food remnants from a meal, discarded cuttings of fabric, leather, or paper, or discarded fragments of recyclable industrial material. In addition, the term carries robust colloquial, investigative, and pugilistic meanings in modern English.

The term is utilized across several distinct practical, technical, and figurative spheres:

  • Culinary and Domestic Sense (Food Leftovers and Refuse):

    Small, uneaten portions of food remaining after a meal has been concluded (such as meat trimmings, bread crusts, or vegetable peelings), typically gathered from plates and either discarded, repurposed into broths and hashes, or fed to household pets and livestock (e.g., "table scraps"). In historical famines, the desperate foraging for scraps on forest floors or behind kitchens was a grim marker of survival.

  • Industrial and Metallurgical Sense (Recyclable Metal Waste):

    Discarded metal parts, turnings, old machinery, automotive hulls, and structural beams collected in scrapyards for melting down and re-refining into fresh steel or alloy products. In metallurgical economics, *scrap* constitutes a fundamental raw commodity in global supply chains, reducing the energy needed to smelt virgin iron ore.

  • Craft, Textile, and Paper Sense (Remnant Materials):

    Trimmings and leftover swatches of fabric, leather, yarn, or paper left over after a garment, quilt, or bookbinding project has been cut. These remnants are commonly collected by artisans for patchwork quilts, collage art, or scrapbooks.

  • Investigative and Metaphorical Sense (Fragmentary Clues and Evidence):

    Small, isolated, incomplete pieces of information, evidence, memory, or historical text. Historians, forensic detectives, and archaeologists routinely work with "scraps of evidence" or "scraps of parchment" to reconstruct forgotten events and solve complex investigations.

  • Colloquial and Pugilistic Sense (Informal Fights and Tussles):

    An informal, short, relatively minor physical brawl, schoolyard fistfight, or heated verbal altercation (derived from the 19th-century slang verb *to scrap*, meaning to fight aggressively or tussle competitively).

  • Discarding and Abandonment Sense (The Verbal Base 'To Scrap'):

    In project management and engineering, the verb *to scrap* means to discard, cancel, or abandon an entire plan, machine, or policy as obsolete or unworkable, consigning it figuratively to the scrapheap.

Semantic Evolution and Polysemy:
The evolution of scraps is tied to the ancient physical action of scraping. Originating from Old Norse skrap ("scrapings, trifles"), the word literally signified what was scraped off a trench, table, or cutting block with a knife. As medieval English households scraped trenchers clean, these "scrapings" naturally became the synonymous noun for domestic leftovers. During the Industrial Revolution, the term expanded from small organic scraps to mountain-sized piles of discarded metal trimmings in steel mills. In the late 19th century, boxing slang adopted scrap to denote a rough, scrappy brawl, completing a semantic arc from culinary debris to industrial metallurgy and street combat.
