- [16th-century conqueror] - The name Pizarro entered English with the history of the conquest of Peru: Francisco Pizarro, the conqueror of the Incas who captured Atahualpa and founded Lima - a name of sudden, ruthless fame.
- [Romantic-diagnostic use] - In 18th and 19th century histories (as in Macaulay) the surname became an epitome of conquest as plunder - paired with Cortes, standing for the sword that added realms and extracted gold without building anything durable.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'Pizarro' names the historical figure and, more broadly, the archetype of the ruthless conquistador, its moral colouring dark and diagnostic, a byword for imperial rapacity.
