- Latin aequus plus vox, equal plus voice; logical and neutral
- Late Latin aequivocatio, calling by the same name; scholastic and technical
- Medieval equivocatio, fallacy of two senses; schoolmen and disputatious
- 1380s English equivocation borrowed via French; learned and logical
- Shakespeare's Porter, equivocator in Macbeth (1606); Gunpowder, treasonous, and comic
- Jesuit controversy, Garnet's Treatise of Equivocation (1598); theological and deadly
- 18th-century polite equivocation, evasive answer; parliamentary and social
- Victorian nursery sin, Ruskin's grandfather unforgiveable; domestic and moral
- Modern logic textbooks, fallacy of equivocation; pedagogical and dry
- Modern politics, non-denial denial and constructive ambiguity; spin and peace
