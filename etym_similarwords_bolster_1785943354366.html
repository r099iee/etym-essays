• Reinforce: Similar because both mean to make stronger; differs because reinforce implies adding secondary layers, troops, or material for protection, while bolster implies placing a prop beneath to prevent sagging.
  • Support: Similar because both mean to hold something up; differs because support is the general, all-purpose word for bearing weight or backing, while bolster adds the specific image of a cushion placed underneath.
  • Sustain: Similar because both mean to keep going; differs because sustain means holding up continuously over time (also to nourish), while bolster means giving a targeted boost of strength.
  • Strengthen: Similar because both mean to increase strength; differs because strengthen is a broad, neutral verb, while bolster suggests external support added to a thing that might otherwise fail.
  • Prop up: Similar because both mean to hold something from falling; differs because prop up is informal and suggests the object would collapse instantly without the prop, while bolster is positive reinforcement.
  • Shore up: Similar because both mean to support a failing thing; differs because shore up is a sea-faring and building image (a shore = a prop against a ship or wall) and often implies emergency rescue of something already failing.
  • Underpin: Similar because both describe support placed beneath; differs because underpin is technical (foundations of a wall) and impersonal, while bolster is used of persons, morale, and arguments.
  • Buttress: Similar because both mean architectural or structural support; differs because a buttress is a massive exterior wall or tower built against outward thrust, while a bolster is an under-cushioning support at a point of load.
  • Fortify: Similar because both mean to strengthen; differs because fortify implies building defensive walls, adding nutrients, or making something robust against attack, while bolster supports internal confidence or arguments.
  • Brace: Similar because both mean to support against pressure; differs because brace is a rigid framework holding parts together or steadying the body, while bolster is soft padding or figurative backing.
  • Buoy: Similar because both mean to keep something up; differs because buoy is specifically to keep afloat (physical or figurative morale), while bolster is to hold something up from beneath on a stable base.
  • Uphold: Similar because both mean to support; differs because uphold is elevated and moral (uphold the law, uphold a decision), while bolster is a practical strengthening.
  • Boost: Similar because both mean to raise or increase; differs because boost implies lifting upward or increasing a quantity or mood, while bolster implies supporting from beneath.
  • Cushion: Similar because both are padded supports and softeners; differs because cushion is itself a noun-object and a verb meaning to soften impact, while bolster means to hold something up.
