Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *reu- / *rū- "to roar, bellow, murmur, whisper" (reconstructed; a root of vocal sounds). [DEBATED: glosses and forms vary by handbook, and the semantic bridge from 'noise/speech' to 'secret counsel' is culturally inferred, not directly attested.] → Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *rūnō "secret, mystery, confidential counsel; secret speech or whisper" (reconstructed, late 2nd–1st millennium BCE; attested across Germanic by Gothic rūna, Old Norse rún, Old English rūn). → Stage 3 — Old English rūn "secret, counsel, mystery; a runic character" (attested from the 8th century CE, in glosses, compounds such as rūncræftig 'skilled in mysteries', and in runic inscriptions). → Stage 3b — Old Norse rún "secret, mystery; a runic letter" (Viking Age; the central literary witness, appearing constantly in Eddic poetry and sagas as both alphabet and spell). → Stage 4 — Middle English roun / rune "secret, whisper, counsel; later a runic letter" (12th–15th centuries CE; the secret-sense survives in forms of the verb roun 'to whisper'). → Stage 5 — Modern English RUNE "a letter of the Germanic alphabets; a cryptic or magical mark" (revived and specialized from the 16th century CE onward through antiquarian scholarship and the runic revival). NOTE on the debate: the PIE stage is reconstructed on the evidence of the Germanic and Celtic forms (Old Irish rún 'secret, mystery' is a probable loan from or cognate of the same base), but exact glosses and the precise shape of the root differ among handbooks. The Germanic stage *rūnō is secure, as is the semantic double track from 'secret/counsel' to 'letter': the letters were carved into hard surfaces and were felt to be exclusive, hidden knowledge. The word also passed into Finnish as runo 'poem, song', a notable loan showing how far the Germanic word spread.
