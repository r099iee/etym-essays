- Carpenter's rod — medieval sense of straight bar of wood or iron; perceived as plain workaday object, prison bar, door bar.
- Social barrier — 14th–17th century extension to courtroom bar, tavern bar and exclusion (barred); connotation of authority dividing insiders from outsiders.
- Musical timekeeper — 17th century adoption for the vertical staff line and then the enclosed measure; seen as learned ensemble discipline, keeping singers together.
- Cheerful fragment — 19th–20th century popular idiom to hum or sing a bar; perception of casual tunefulness, pub pianolas, music-hall choruses.
- Modern technical-popular split — contemporary musicians count bars (twelve-bar blues, sixteen bars) while drinkers prop up the bar; Wodehouse happily puns across both, humming at the bar behind bars of song.
