Single root word: bindle (American slang noun; dialectal alteration of bundle under the influence of the verb bind). The derivation chain therefore traces the underlying bundle word, of which bindle is the American offspring. Derivation chain: 1. Proto-Indo-European root *bhendh-, "to bind, tie, join, fasten together" — reconstructed, c. 4500–2500 BCE. Attested derivatives: Vedic Sanskrit badhnāti "he ties" and bandhu "kinsman, relative" (one bound by blood), Ancient Greek pentherós "father-in-law" (one bound by marriage), Lithuanian beñdras "companion." 2. Proto-Germanic strong verb *bindaną, "to bind, tie with cord" — reconstructed, c. 500 BCE–c. 200 CE; descends into Old English bindan (modern English bind), Old High German bintan (modern German binden), Old Norse binda. 3. Proto-Germanic nominal derivative *bundilaz, "a little bound-together thing, a bunch, a bundle" — a diminutive formation on the zero-grade noun *bundą "bundle, bond" — reconstructed; attested descendants: Old English byndele (a bound bundle), Middle Dutch bondel, Old High German buntil / bundle, German Bündel (diminutive of Bund "bundle, bond"), Dutch bundel. 4. Middle English bundel, "a bundle, package, parcel; a collection of things tied or wrapped together" — attested c. 1300. 5. English bundle (noun), "a collection of objects bound or wrapped together for carrying or transport" — from the 14th c. onward; also a verb ("to bundle up"), with a specialised North American sense of "to sleep together wrapped" in the courting custom of bundling (17th–18th c.). 6. American English bindle, "a tramp's or hobo's bundle of possessions wrapped in cloth, carried over the shoulder" — first attested in US hobo slang c. 1890s; a dialectal reshaping of bundle under the influence of the verb bind. Related formations: bindlestiff (c. 1900s) "a hobo who carries a bindle," and the drug-slang bindle (20th c.) "a folded paper packet of powdered drugs." Final node: bindle. DEBATED / NUANCED ETYMOLOGY: The standard account (OED, Merriam-Webster) treats bindle as an alteration of bundle influenced by bind. A minority view holds that the word may have been reinforced or shaped by German Bündel "bundle" in the polyglot hobo camps of the American West; the two explanations are not mutually exclusive and both point to the same Germanic family. The underlying chain from PIE *bhendh- through Proto-Germanic *bindaną / *bundilaz to bundle is secure and standard.
