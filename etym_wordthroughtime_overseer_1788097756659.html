- c. 1500–1600 - a plain agent noun: “one who looks over or superintends labour” - wholly neutral and workaday, the office of foremen, wardens, and clerks
- 1597 – 19th c. - the “overseer of the poor”, a parish officer who collected the poor-rate and set paupers to work - civic and bureaucratic, mildly respected, the word fixed in statute
- 17th – 19th c. Atlantic world - the plantation “overseer”, hired manager of enslaved workers, whip in hand - the word absorbs the brutal associations of the slave economy and becomes a symbol of cruelty
- 19th c. Britain - the factory, mine, and dock “overseer”, foreman “over the men” - industrial and disciplinary, the everyday word for the man in charge at work
- 20th – 21st c. - “overseer” lingers in formal, historical, and legal registers - neutral “supervisor” now preferred in business, while “overseer” increasingly evokes plantation history or parish records
