• Interlaced: Similar because both describe strands or lines that cross over and under one another; differs because interlaced emphasizes the physical over-under weaving process (as in braids or basketry), whereas reticulated denotes the overall net-like geometric structure and open apertures.
  • Netlike: Similar because both describe a form resembling a fisherman's net; differs because netlike is a simple, Anglo-Saxon descriptive term, whereas reticulated is a technical, formal Latinate descriptor utilized in biological taxonomy, histology, and architectural history.
  • Lattice-like: Similar because both refer to an intersecting grid; differs because lattice-like typically implies rigid, straight slats or diagonal wooden/metallic strips crossing at regular angles, while reticulated can describe organic, branching, or curved cellular webs.
  • Trabeculated: Similar because both describe internal anatomical meshes; differs because trabeculated refers specifically to thick, fleshy or bony beams and muscular ridges (such as inside the heart ventricles or spongy bone), whereas reticulated refers to fine, delicate fiber networks.
  • Cancellate: Similar because both describe porous, grid-like structures; differs because cancellate is primarily used in osteology and zoological shell morphology for porous, spongy lattices, whereas reticulated emphasizes the net-patterned surface or open-cell struts.
  • Plexiform: Similar because both denote an intricate weave; differs because plexiform describes an interwoven network of blood vessels, nerves, or paths (a plexus) that intertwine, whereas reticulated emphasizes uniform mesh windows and geometric regularity.
  • Meshed: Similar because both designate an arrangement of open loops or screen-like apertures; differs because meshed is a broad practical and mechanical descriptor, whereas reticulated carries specific historical, botanical, and taxonomical precision.
