- 16th c. - The verb restring (usually re-string) is coined from string with the prefix re-; its past participle restrung first appears as a plain, practical record of re-fitting cords, bowstrings, or instrument strings.
- 17th-18th c. - The participle is used almost entirely of physical re-cording — harp and lute strings, and later violin-family instruments — in musicians’ and tradesmen’s registers; perception is neutral and technical.
- 19th c. - Restrung extends to pearl necklaces and prayer beads in jewellers’ catalogues and to wider domestic repair, while Romantic poetry re-discovers the biblical harp image, lending the form a poetic charge of revival after sorrow.
- 20th c. - The word enters everyday speech for tennis and badminton racquets and for guitars; it also becomes a quiet symbol of resilience in prose and verse (“a restrung harp”, “a restrung life”).
- 21st c. - Restrung remains the standard past form for instruments and racquets, while its figurative use — restoration of voice, community, or spirit — keeps the ancient Psalm 137 resonance alive.
