Echoic/imitative base — the noise of jaw-biting and mastication (sound-symbolic origin; no secure PIE etymon; the OED connects the family with jam "to press tightly", v.) → Middle English chamen / champen "to bite upon something, gnash the teeth" (late 14th century; spelling variant chamb recorded) → Early Modern English champ "to chew noisily, crunch" (c. 1520) → English champ "of horses, to bite repeatedly and impatiently" (c. 1570) → English idiom champ on / at the bit "to be restless and eager" (figurative, 1640) → English champ (noun) "the act of biting repeatedly" (c. 1600) → Modern English champing "chewing or biting down noisily and eagerly" (present participle) Note on the debate: The origin is uncertain. Most dictionaries treat the word as echoic — cham-cham-cham pictures the noise of jaws working — and the OED cautiously links it with jam (v.), "to press tightly," on the same sound-symbolic model. A Scandinavian or Low German influence has been suggested (Swedish kampa "to chew, squeeze"), but the evidence is suggestive, not conclusive. The word is NOT related to Latin campus, the source of French champ "field" — that is a separate homonym of entirely different origin, and the two must not be confused.
