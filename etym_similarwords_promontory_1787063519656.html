• headland: Similar because both name high land jutting into the water; differs because "headland" is the more general everyday term stressing the high exposed land, while "promontory" is more formal and stresses rock.
  • cape: Similar because both are points of land projecting into water; differs because a cape need not be high or rocky and is usually lower and broader, while a promontory is specifically an elevated projection.
  • peninsula: Similar because both are land reaching into water; differs because a peninsula is a large body of land nearly surrounded by water and joined by a narrow isthmus, whereas a promontory is smaller and attached by a broad base.
  • point: Similar because both name a tapering projection of land into water; differs because "point" stresses the narrow tip and is usually low-lying (e.g. Point Reyes), lacking the elevation of a promontory.
  • spur: Similar because both describe a projection, especially of a mountain or ridge; differs because a spur is a ridge descending from a higher mountain and need not reach water, whereas a promontory always extends into a body of water.
  • cliff: Similar because both can be high rocky faces; differs because a cliff is a steep vertical face of rock and does not necessarily project into water, while a promontory is defined by jutting outward into it.
  • bluff: Similar because both may be high land; differs because a bluff is a steep, usually rounded bank, while a promontory is a sharp rocky projection into water.
  • foreland: Similar because both are land projecting toward the sea; differs because "foreland" is a more archaic and literary term for a cape or headland and does not carry the stress on height.
  • ness / naze: Similar because both are Old-English-derived names for a projecting point of land (e.g. Sheerness, Caithness); differs because these survive mainly in place-names and dialect, whereas "promontory" is the standard learned term.
  • ridge: Similar because both describe an elongated elevated feature; differs because a ridge is a long narrow elevation of the land surface and is not defined by projecting into water.
