• myrrh: Similar because both are famous biblical oleo-gum-resins tapped from desert trees in southern Arabia and Northeast Africa and burned as sacred aromatics; differs because myrrh is harvested from Commiphora species, possesses a dark reddish-brown hue and a sharp, earthy, bitter-spicy fragrance associated with mourning and embalming, whereas frankincense is pale golden, sweet, balsamic, and associated with divine worship and joyous sanctification.
  • olibanum: Similar because it is the exact technical, commercial, and pharmacological synonym for frankincense; differs because olibanum (derived from Medieval Latin via Arabic al-lubān) is primarily utilised in botanical, medical, and trade registers, whereas frankincense is the standard literary, cultural, and everyday English term.
  • incense: Similar because frankincense is the quintessential historical variety of incense; differs because incense is a broad generic category encompassing any natural or synthetic fragrant material burned for its aroma (including woods, herbs, and resins like sandalwood, agarwood, and benzoin), whereas frankincense specifies the single resin of the Boswellia tree.
  • copal: Similar because it is an aromatic tree resin burned for spiritual purification and ritual ceremonies; differs because copal originates from Mesoamerican and tropical trees (such as Protium and Hymenaea) and was central to Mayan and Aztec rituals rather than Near Eastern and Mediterranean traditions.
  • benzoin: Similar because it is a balsamic resin tapped from Styrax trees used extensively in incense and perfumery; differs because benzoin possesses a distinctly sweet, heavy, vanilla-like aroma and crystalline structure native to Southeast Asia (Sumatra, Siam).
  • bdellium: Similar because it is a gum resin obtained from related Commiphora species native to arid regions; differs because bdellium produces a milder, less refined scent, historically regarded as an economical substitute or adulterant for frankincense and myrrh.
  • galbanum: Similar because it is an ancient aromatic gum resin mentioned alongside frankincense in biblical incense recipes (such as the sacred Ketoret); differs because galbanum is tapped from Persian umbelliferous plants (Ferula) and yields an intensely green, bitter, pungent, earthy fragrance rather than a sweet balsamic one.
  • elemi: Similar because it is a fragrant oleoresin with citrus and pine notes used in perfumery and varnishes; differs because elemi is harvested from the Canarium luzonicum tree native to the Philippines and has a softer, honey-like consistency.
  • storax: Similar because it is a sweet, resinous plant exudate used in sacred incenses; differs because storax (or styrax) is derived from Liquidambar or Styrax species and exhibits a heavier, floral-spicy, medicinal undertone.
  • ambergris: Similar because it is a legendary historical perfumery fixative and luxury aromatic of great antiquity; differs entirely in origin and profile, being an animal secretion produced in the digestive tract of sperm whales with a marine, fecal-sweet aroma, rather than a botanical resin burned upon coals.
