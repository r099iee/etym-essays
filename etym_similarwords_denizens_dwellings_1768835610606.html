• Inhabitants: Similar because both name people who occupy a place; differs because inhabitant is a plain, neutral census word for anyone present and living there, whereas denizen adds a legal or formal flavour of belonging and accepted standing.
  • Residents: Similar because both name people living in a place; differs because resident is modern, administrative, and time-bound (a hotel resident), while denizen is older, more formal, and implies permanent natural belonging.
  • Occupants: Similar because both name people who occupy a space; differs because occupant is temporary and spatial (occupants of a car, a room), carrying no sense of belonging or citizenship.
  • Citizens: Similar because the denizen historically held partial civic rights like a citizen; differs because citizen implies full political membership of a state, whereas denizen denotes a resident with a limited or localised standing.
  • Natives: Similar because both can mean people of a place by birth or long residence; differs because native stresses birth or original belonging, while denizen works through law and residence rather than birth.
  • Dwellers: Similar because both name those who live in a place (dwellers of the forest); differs because dweller is simple and almost poetic, while denizen carries a formal, quasi-legal dignity.
  • Populace / population: Similar because both name the body of people living in an area; differs because populace is collective and massed, whereas denizen picks out individual residents with status.
  • Tenants: Similar because both can name people who occupy premises by permission; differs because tenant is a specific legal relation involving rent and lease, with no sense of permanent native belonging.
  • Aborigines / autochthones: Similar because both name original inhabitants; differs because these stress first occupancy of a land in anthropology and myth, while denizen is a legal, present-tense resident.
  • Homes: Similar because both are where people live; differs because home is emotional and familial, whereas dwelling is formal, structural, and administrative.
  • Houses: Similar because both are places of residence; differs because house names a specific building type, while dwelling covers any inhabited place, including flats, huts, and caves.
  • Residences: Similar because both are formal names for a place of abode; differs because residence is the official, often grander term for where someone formally resides, while dwelling is the plainer general term.
  • Abodes: Similar because both are formal words for a home; differs because abode is literary and slightly old-fashioned, while dwelling is the administrative and everyday formal term.
  • Habitats: Similar because both can name the place where beings live; differs because habitat is a biological and environmental term for the natural home of a species, not a human building.
  • Lodgings: Similar because both are places where someone lives; differs because lodgings are rented rooms, temporary and partial, whereas a dwelling is a full, permanent residence.
  • Domiciles: Similar because both are formal legal words for a place of residence; differs because domicile is a precise legal concept of permanent home for law and tax, colder than dwelling.
