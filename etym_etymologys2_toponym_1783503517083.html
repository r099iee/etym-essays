TOPONYM is a scholarly compound of two Greek elements; each element has its own separate chain. CHAIN 1 — the element topos "place": Greek tópos (τόπος, "place, region, location"; attested from the 5th c. BCE in Herodotus and tragedy; NO secure Proto-Indo-European etymology — Beekes judges it most likely a Pre-Greek substrate word, a relic of the non-Indo-European language(s) of the Bronze-Age Aegean; the older claim connecting it to an Indo-European root meaning "to appear" is now generally abandoned) → Greek topikós (τοπικός, "of a place, local"; 4th c. BCE, Aristotle's Topica) → Latin topica ("commonplaces of argument," the rhetorical stock of arguments; via the rhetorical tradition) → English topic ("subject of discourse"; 15th c.) [sister branch] AND, in parallel, the combining form topo- in modern scientific coinages (topography 15th c., topology 17th c., toponymy 1876, toponym 1899) → TOPONYM ◄ TARGET CHAIN 2 — the element -onym "name": PIE *no-men- / *h₁nómn̥ ("name"; one of the most securely reconstructed roots in the family; attested in Latin nōmen, Old English nama, Sanskrit nā́man, Old Church Slavonic imę, Hittite lāman, Old Irish ainm, Armenian anun) → Greek ónoma (ὄνομα, "name," Attic) / ónyma (dialectal variant, the -onym- stem used in Greek compounds) → Greek combining element -ōnymos / -ōnymon (in ancient compounds: epṓnymos "named after," anṓnymos "nameless," pseudṓnymos "falsely named," homṓnymos "bearing the same name") → modern European scientific/technical compounds on the Greek model (19th c.: synonym, antonym, pseudonym, toponym) → English toponym (1899, a back-formation from toponymy, 1876) → TOPONYM ◄ TARGET
