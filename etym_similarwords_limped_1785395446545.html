• Hobbled: Similar because both describe painful, constrained walking; differs because hobbling is clumsier and heavier, often from tied feet or stiffness, whereas a limp is controlled though painful.
  • Staggered: Similar because both describe an injured or unsteady way of moving; differs because staggering implies losing balance and lurching as if about to fall, while limping is deliberate movement that persists through pain.
  • Shuffled: Similar because both describe a laboured gait; differs because shuffling means sliding the feet without lifting them — slow but not painful, carrying none of the injury that a limp signals.
  • Hopped: Similar because both are uneven, single-sided motions; differs because hopping is rhythmic, energetic and uses one leg at a time, whereas a limp alternates both legs with one bearing the weight.
  • Lumbered: Similar because both describe walking with difficulty; differs because lumbering is the heavy, clumsy gait of a large body, with no suggestion of injury or pain.
  • Tottered: Similar because both describe unsteady walking; differs because tottering threatens a fall and suggests weakness or age, while limping is a controlled, persistent advance on a damaged leg.
  • Faltered: Similar because both can describe movement that loses its smoothness; differs because faltering is a loss of momentum, courage or certainty in anything at all — not a specific physical gait.
  • Dragged: Similar because both suggest slow, painful progress; differs because dragging implies pulling a limb or oneself with great effort and no fixed rhythm, while a limp keeps a beat with one sound leg.
  • Crawled: Similar because both describe slow, effortful movement; differs because crawling is on hands and knees, a far more extreme reduction, whereas limping is still upright walking.
  • Waddled: Similar because both describe an uneven, distinctive walk; differs because waddling is a swaying, rocking gait of a heavy or duck-like body, with no implication of pain or injury.
