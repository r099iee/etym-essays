• Malay: Similar because both refer to the indigenous Austronesian people, culture, and language of the Malay Peninsula and eastern Sumatra; differs because 'Malay' is primarily an ethnic, cultural, and linguistic designation (e.g., 'the Malay people', 'the Malay language'), whereas 'Malayan' historically served as a geographic, civic, and territorial adjective encompassing all inhabitants of the historical Federation of Malaya regardless of ethnic background.
  • Malaysian: Similar because both pertain to the sovereign geography of the Malay Peninsula; differs because 'Malaysian' is the modern post-1963 civic and national demonym applying to the entire multi-ethnic nation of Malaysia (including the Bornean states of Sabah and Sarawak), whereas 'Malayan' strictly pertains to the pre-1963 historical entity or the geographical peninsula alone.
  • Austronesian: Similar because Malayan languages and peoples belong directly to this overarching ethno-linguistic classification; differs because 'Austronesian' is a massive macro-family spanning from Madagascar to Easter Island, including thousands of languages, representing a far higher taxonomic and phylogenetic category.
  • Sundanese: Similar because both denote indigenous peoples and languages of Maritime Southeast Asia within the Sunda geographic region; differs because 'Sundanese' refers specifically to the distinct ethnic group and language of western Java, possessing its own unique linguistic structure, culture, and history distinct from the Malayic group.
  • Indonesian: Similar because Standard Indonesian is a standardized register of the literary Malay language, sharing deep linguistic and historical roots with Malayan speech; differs because 'Indonesian' is the national and political designation of the Republic of Indonesia, encompassing hundreds of distinct indigenous ethnic groups across the Indonesian archipelago.
  • Peninsular: Similar because 'Malayan' frequently functions as a geographic synonym for the Malay Peninsula; differs because 'peninsular' is a generic geographical adjective applicable to any geographic peninsula (e.g., Iberian, Korean, Arabian, or Florida peninsulas) without inherent cultural or linguistic specificity.
  • Maritime Southeast Asian: Similar because Malaya lies at the geographic heart of Maritime Southeast Asia (the Malay Archipelago or Nusantara); differs because 'Maritime Southeast Asian' is an expansive geographic and anthropological umbrella term including the Philippines, Indonesia, Brunei, East Timor, and Singapore.
  • Indochinese: Similar because the Malay Peninsula is contiguous with mainland Southeast Asia; differs because 'Indochinese' pertains historically and geographically to the continental region north of the Isthmus of Kra (Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia, Thailand, and Myanmar), characterized by deep historical Sinitic and Indic cultural syntheses rather than insular Austronesian maritime traditions.
