• Blockade: Similar because both cut a place off from supplies and communication; differs because a blockade may be purely naval or economic and need not involve attacks or the intention to take the place, whereas a siege normally combines blockade with active offensive operations.
  • Encirclement: Similar because surrounding a position is the core geometry of both; differs because encirclement is a component or a single manoeuvre and applies to armies in the field, while a siege is a full prolonged operation against a place.
  • Investment: Similar because the formal military term for surrounding a fortress to cut it off, the opening phase of a siege; differs because it is technical and largely archaic (17th–19th century usage), while siege is the everyday word.
  • Beleaguerment / beleaguering: Similar because surrounding with hostile intent; differs because it stresses harassment and pressure rather than formal procedure, and the adjective beleaguered has drifted toward “overwhelmed by difficulties or requests”.
  • Leaguer: Similar because it names a siege-camp or a besieging force (from French ligue/Dutch leger “camp”); differs because archaic and dialectal, surviving only in historical writing.
  • Besiegement: Similar because the noun of the verb besiege with the same meaning; differs because rare, clunky and mostly figurative, never the standard military word.
  • Assault / Attack: Similar because offensive military actions against a place; differs because they are single, short-duration actions — an assault may end a siege but is not one; siege denotes the whole protracted operation.
  • Cordon: Similar because a ring of troops or police sealing an area; differs because it is a security measure (police, customs), not a military capture operation, and is usually brief.
  • Ordeal / Trial / Pressure: Similar to the figurative sense (“a prolonged siege of trouble”); differs because they are fully general and carry no military image.
  • Containment: Similar in keeping something confined; differs because it is a defensive strategy aimed at preventing expansion, not an operation to take a place.
