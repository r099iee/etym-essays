Note: The word is a learned compound built from Latin circum (‘around’, an adverbial accusative related to circus) and stāre (‘to stand’, from the ubiquitous PIE root *stā-). In classical Latin rhetoric, Cicero and Quintilian established the concept of circumstantiae—the seven essential circumstances defining any act: quis (who), quid (what), ubi (where), quibus auxiliis (by what means), cur (why), quomodo (how), and quando (when). The adjectival derivative circumstantiālis was coined in medieval scholasticism to distinguish these accidental properties from the metaphysical substance of an entity.
