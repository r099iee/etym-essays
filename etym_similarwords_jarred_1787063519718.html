• Jolted: Similar because both describe a sudden abrupt shock to the body; differs because a jolt is a single sharp movement (as of a vehicle or electric shock), while jarring stresses the harsh, grating quality of the impact — jolt is motion, jar is discord.
  • Jostled: Similar because both can describe rough physical contact; differs because jostling is the shoving of a crowd — repeated, bustling, personal — whereas jarred is a single hard impact with a hard surface.
  • Shaken: Similar because both describe being violently agitated; differs because shaken is far broader (it covers emotional upheaval, tremors, and simple shaking) and softer, while jarred implies a sharp, rough, unpleasant strike.
  • Rattled: Similar because both can mean shaken or unnerved; differs because rattling suggests a rapid clattering noise and light, nervous agitation, while jarring is heavier, more painful, more discordant.
  • Grated: Similar because both describe a harsh, scraping, unpleasant sound; differs because grating is specifically a grinding friction sound, while jarring covers jolts, clashes, and emotional shocks as well as noise.
  • Clashed: Similar because both describe elements in harsh conflict; differs because clashing names the collision of two parties or colours more directly, while jarring emphasises the unpleasant sensory or emotional effect of that collision.
  • Vibrated: Similar because both involve a shaking movement; differs because vibration is neutral, mechanical, and even pleasant (a phone vibrates), while jarring is always violent and unpleasant.
  • Rocked: Similar because both describe a violent back-and-forth disturbance; differs because rocking can be gentle and rhythmic, whereas jarring is always a single sharp, discordant shock.
  • Startled: Similar because both describe a sudden disruption of composure; differs because startling is a surprise to the senses or nerves, brief and neutral, while jarring carries roughness, harshness, and often physical pain.
  • Unsettled: Similar because both describe disturbing someone’s calm; differs because unsettling is a slow, creeping discomfort, while jarring is a sudden, violent, grating blow to body or mood.
