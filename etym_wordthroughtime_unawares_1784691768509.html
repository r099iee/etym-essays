- 16th-century coinage - unawares is built from the negative of aware with the old adverbial genitive -s; it means without awareness, and the idiom 'to take or catch (someone) unawares' fixes itself in the language of ambush, mishap and vigilance
- 17th-18th century - the word becomes standard in prose of surprise and mischance: men are caught unawares by enemies, storms and sudden events; the adverb carries a sharp sense of suddenness
- 19th-20th century - as the adjective unaware and its plain adverb spread, unawares retains the specific force of sudden, unprepared surprise: one may be unaware of a fact for years, but one is caught unawares in a single moment
- Contemporary sense - the fixed phrase 'caught unawares' is the word's true home, a slightly literary or rhetorical term for surprise that catches one off guard
