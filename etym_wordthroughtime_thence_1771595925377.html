- [Old English: 'from that place'] - 'Thence' enters English as the adverb meaning 'from that place', the outward-looking member of the here/there family; connotation is spatial and directional.
- [Middle to early modern English: from that source, from that time] - The word broadens from place to origin and sequence: 'thence' comes to mean 'from that source or cause' and 'from that time onward'; connotation becomes logical and temporal as well as spatial.
- [Contemporary sense] - 'Thence' survives mainly in formal and legalistic prose, pressed into purely logical service - 'from that statement' - the pivot of an argument, an adverb of the courtroom and the lawbook; connotation is judicial, scrupulous, and deliberately distancing, marking the point of origin of a logical motion.
