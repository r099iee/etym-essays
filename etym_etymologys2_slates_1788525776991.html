PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (reconstructed)

*skleyd- / *skey-d- / *skey-

"to split, cleave, cut off, separate"

(ancient root for splitting and tearing; source of slit, schism, shed, and science)

↓

Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)

*slītanan / *slaitijaną

"to tear, slit, split into pieces"

↓

Old Low Franconian / Frankish (c. 5th–8th c. CE)

*slaitan / *slita

"to split, shatter; splinter, shard"

↓

Old French (12th c.)

esclat (noun) / esclater (verb)

"splinter, shard, piece broken off; to burst, shatter"

(whence modern French éclat 'splendor, bursting forth, fragment')

↓

Old French (dialectal / Picard / Norman) (12th–13th c.)

esclate / esclatte (feminine noun)

"thin split tile of stone, slate roofing shingle, splinter"

↓

Anglo-Norman / Middle English (14th c.; c. 1320–1380)

sclat / slat / sclate (plural sclates / slates)

"thin flat roofing stone, slate tile"

↓

Early Modern to Modern English (16th c. – present)

SLATES

"split metamorphic roofing tiles; writing tablets; candidate rosters; severely criticizes"
