T.A. 1–1050] — Dwelt in the upper vales of Anduin between the Misty Mountains and Greenwood the Great, living in hillside burrows and trading amicably with mountain Dwarves — [perception: regarded by neighboring Northmen and Dwarves as shy, quiet, subterranean earth-dwellers]
- [Middle Third Age / c. T.A. 1050–1601] — First among the three Hobbit breeds to undertake the perilous crossing of the Misty Mountains into Eriador as the shadow of Sauron lengthened over Greenwood (Dol Guldur) — [perception: revered in Hobbit annals as the brave pioneer kindred who opened the westward roads toward the Shire]
- [Late Third Age / Shire Era c. T.A. 1601–3019] — Settled the Shire and intermingled with arriving Stoors and Fallohides, forming the vast numerical and cultural bedrock of Shire Hobbitry (Bag End, Hobbiton, Michel Delving) — [perception: celebrated as the archetypal Shire-folk, embodying the quintessential Hobbit virtues of peace, hearty appetite, and earthiness]
- [Publication Era / 1954–present] — Introduced in Tolkien's Prologue to The Lord of the Rings and expanded in contemporary adaptations (such as the Rings of Power series), becoming a household fantasy name — [perception: celebrated by literary critics and millions of readers as the primal, heartwarming archetype of the pastoral halfling]
