- 19th-century hotel slang — American houses needed a brisk word for the boy who answered bells; bell-boy and jocular bell-hop (one who hops to the bell) competed in lobbies.
- c.1900–1930s — bellhop standardised in the United States as the uniformed luggage-carrier; youthful, male, tipped, and low-paid; the job's miseries entered memoir and labour reportage.
- Mid-20th century — pop-cultural icon: films, cartoons, and advertisements fixed the pillbox cap and brass buttons as shorthand for hotel life; tone turned affectionate-nostalgic in fiction while remaining weary in autobiography.
- Late 20th century — self-service travel, smaller staffs, and gender-neutral language pressed the word; houses renamed the role bell attendant though guests still said bellhop.
- Present — period-flavoured but understood everywhere: kept for charm in boutique hotels, preserved in memoirs like Vance's to signal hard, humble beginnings.
