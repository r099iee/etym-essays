- [18th century: Italian loan] - 'Patina' enters English as the green or brown film that forms on old bronze and copper; connotation is antiquarian and neutral - the chemical ageing of metal.
- [19th century: the connoisseur's revaluation] - Collectors and connoisseurs revalue the film from mere corrosion to 'honourable antiquity'; patina connotes age, authenticity, and accumulated character - the admired skin of a treasured object, the opposite of shiny newness.
- [20th-21st century: the metaphorical bloom] - The word extends metaphorically to any surface quality or character acquired through time and use - the patina of experience, of history, of an old friendship; connotation is now warmly admiring, of depth and survival rather than mere age.
