- [Middle English moral sense] - Frail first means morally weak - easily tempted, liable to sin - the 'frail flesh' of religious writing, a reproachful or pitying word.
- [16th-18th century physical shift] - The sense moves to bodily weakness and delicacy: a frail body, frail health; 'frail vessel' still tags a woman's supposed fragility.
- [19th-century aesthetic paradox] - Romantic and Victorian writers make frailty beautiful - a slender, delicate, almost translucent grace, prized as refinement.
- [Contemporary register] - Now chiefly medical and sympathetic (frail elderly, frail health) and literary for delicate grace; the moral sense survives only archaically.
