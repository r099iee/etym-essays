Note on competing etymological hypotheses: Historical Romance lexicographers have long debated the exact etymology of Old French accoustrer. The prevailing scholarly consensus (endorsed by the Oxford English Dictionary, Meyer-Lübke, and Wartburg's Französisches Etymologisches Wörterbuch) derives the verb from Vulgar Latin *costūra ("sewing, seam", source of Modern French couture), signifying the physical act of pinning, basting, and stitching together clothing to fit a wearer. A rival historical hypothesis proposed that accoustrer originated from Old French coustre ("a sacristan or church warden", derived from Medieval Latin custor / Classical Latin custos, "guard, keeper"), suggesting that the word originally referred to the duties of the sacristan in preparing the sacred altar vessels and vestments for church liturgy. However, early Old French literary attestations overwhelmingly emphasize the physical tailoring, basting, and dressing of garments, lending decisive philological weight to the *costūra derivation.
