• Enrage: Similar because both mean to fill someone with intense anger; differs because 'enrage' conveys an immediate, explosive, violent boiling of temper, whereas 'infuriate' often emphasizes the aggravating process that pushes someone past the breaking point of patience.
  • Incense: Similar because both denote provoking extreme indignation; differs because 'incense' (from Latin incendere, to set on fire) emphasizes righteous, dignified, or moral outrage.
  • Exasperate: Similar because both involve strong emotional frustration; differs because 'exasperate' emphasizes exhausting someone's patience through repetitive annoyances, rather than triggering aggressive wrath.
  • Provoke: Similar because both describe triggering an emotional reaction; differs because 'provoke' is a broader, neutral verb meaning to elicit any reaction (such as laughter, curiosity, or retaliation), whereas 'infuriate' is strictly confined to violent rage.
  • Inflame: Similar because both denote escalating passionate feelings; differs because 'inflame' suggests fanning embers of desire, rebellion, or unrest into larger flames across a group.
  • Irk: Similar because both involve causing displeasure; differs because 'irk' denotes mild, tedious irritation or weariness, entirely lacking the explosive heat of 'infuriate'.
  • Madden: Similar because both suggest driving someone to the point of frenzy; differs because 'madden' places greater emphasis on losing one's mental sanity or cognitive clarity.
  • Gall: Similar because both denote deep vexation; differs because 'gall' emphasizes the bitter, humiliating sting of wounded pride or unfair treatment.
