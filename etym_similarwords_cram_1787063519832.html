• Hardtack: Similar because both are hard, dry, long-lasting travelling biscuits eaten by soldiers and sailors; differs because hardtack is the historical naval and military term (biscuit of flour and water), while cram is Tolkien's dwarven equivalent and a more general word for packed provisions.
  • Stuff: Similar because both mean to force things into a tight space; differs because stuff is broader and softer (stuffing a cushion, stuffing a bird), while cram implies more pressure, tightness, and volume.
  • Pack: Similar because both mean to fill a space efficiently; differs because pack is orderly and methodical, whereas cram is hurried, careless, and overfull.
  • Jammed: Similar because both describe being wedged tightly; differs because jammed implies being stuck or blocked, while crammed implies filling with pressure.
  • Ram: Similar because both involve forcing with pressure; differs because ram is violent and uses impact (ramming a gate), while cram is about compression into volume.
  • Bone up / swot: Similar to the study sense because both mean last-minute intensive studying; differs in that swot (British) and bone up are specific idioms while cram is the universal standard term.
