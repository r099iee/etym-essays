• Legendary: Similar because both mean "famous through stories"; differs because legendary is the modern everyday word (legendary guitarist, legendary party), while fabled is literary and slightly archaic.
  • Mythical: Similar because both mean "belonging to myth and story"; differs because mythical is about myth proper (gods and heroes) and often implies "untrue", while fabled stresses fame through storytelling, with truth optional.
  • Mythological: Similar because both describe things from the world of myth; differs because mythological is the technical adjective for the body of myth (mythological figures), while fabled is a general word for story-fame.
  • Storied: Similar because both mean "celebrated in story"; differs because storied emphasizes history and tradition (a storied career, storied halls), while fabled leans toward wonder and remoteness.
  • Celebrated: Similar because both mean "famous, much praised"; differs because celebrated is plain fame without narrative content (a celebrated surgeon), while fabled says the fame comes from tales.
  • Renowned: Similar because both mean "widely known and admired"; differs because renowned is formal but secular and factual, while fabled keeps a flavour of the fabulous and half-unreal.
  • Famous: Similar because both mean "known to many"; differs because famous is the neutral everyday word, while fabled is more intense, older-sounding, and story-flavoured.
  • Fabulous: Similar because both come from the same Latin root and both can mean "mythical, legendary"; differs because fabulous has drifted to "wonderful, amazing" (and colloquially "great!"), while fabled stayed in the realm of legend.
  • Apocryphal: Similar because both describe stories of doubtful truth; differs because apocryphal is skeptical and scholarly (an apocryphal tale = probably false), while fabled is admiring and neutral about truth.
  • Fictitious: Similar because both can mean "invented, made up" (the oldest sense of fabled); differs because fictitious is factual and dismissive (a fictitious name), while fabled has lost most of that sense in favour of "legendary".
  • Folkloric: Similar because both belong to traditional storytelling; differs because folkloric is the technical, academic word for folk tradition, while fabled is the poetic word.
  • Epic: Similar because both describe grand, story-worthy scale (an epic journey); differs because epic is an adjective of scale and genre, while fabled is about being celebrated in tale.
  • Proverbial: Similar because both mean "so well known as to be quoted"; differs because proverbial belongs to sayings (the proverbial pot calling the kettle black), while fabled belongs to narratives.
