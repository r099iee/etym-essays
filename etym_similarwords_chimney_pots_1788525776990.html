• cowls: Similar because both are protective devices installed at the top of chimney flues to manage smoke exhaust; differs because a cowl is specifically a hood, bonnet, revolving metal vane, or louvered cap fitted over or inside a pot to prevent rain ingress and eliminate wind-induced downdrafts, whereas a chimney-pot is the main upright ceramic pipe itself.
  • flues: Similar because both are integral conduits for venting smoke and combustion gases; differs because a flue is the entire internal vertical duct or passageway running through the masonry structure from the fireplace to the roof, whereas the chimney-pot is merely the terminal pipe capping the top of the flue.
  • chimney-stacks: Similar as external masonry structures visible on roofs; differs because a chimney-stack is the substantial brick or stone architectural pillar containing one or more flues, upon which the individual chimney-pots are mounted.
  • smokestacks: Similar as tall vertical conduits for venting smoke; differs because a smokestack refers to large-scale, heavy industrial steel or masonry chimneys found on factories, power plants, locomotives, and steamships rather than domestic ceramic rooftop fittings.
  • vents: Similar as openings designed to discharge gases or air; differs because vent is a broad, generic mechanical term lacking the specific high-temperature fireclay construction and rooftop architectural heritage of chimney-pots.
  • caps: Similar as terminal coverings fitted over chimney openings; differs because a cap is typically a flat stone or concrete slab designed to seal off an unused chimney or deflect rain, whereas a chimney-pot is an open, functional draft-enhancing extension.
  • pipes: Similar as hollow tubular conduits; differs because pipe is an ultra-generic term encompassing plumbing, drainage, and gas lines, lacking the distinct ceramic material and fireplace draft function of chimney-pots.
