- Early 15th c. learned borrowing apertura opening - anatomical, philosophical, and architectural hole in scholastic texts - formal-Latinate, rare, scholarly
- 16th-17th c. natural philosophy aperture - aperture of the eye, of a wound, of heaven in medical and theological writing - technical-descriptive, still bookish
- 17th-18th c. optical aperture - Newton, Huygens, telescope makers use aperture for telescope diameter and aperture stop - precision-instrument sense, scientific revolution
- 19th c. photographic aperture dominates - Daguerre, Talbot, f-numbers, aperture priority; aperture becomes everyday among photographers - mechanical-calibrated, creative control
- 20th c. antenna and physics aperture - effective aperture, numerical aperture, synthetic aperture radar; aperture abstracted from hole to capture area - mathematical, engineering
- Contemporary sci-fi portal plus figurative opportunity - a dark aperture in midair, aperture of opportunity - literary-cinematic marvel and business jargon, mysterious or strategic
