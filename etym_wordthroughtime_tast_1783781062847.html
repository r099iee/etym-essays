Middle English Sensory Arrival - Tast enters Middle English around 1300 from Old French taster ("to touch, try"), where tasting was understood as a refined kind of touching; it names both the physical sense of flavor and the act of sampling.
Early Modern Figurative Growth - By the 16th-17th centuries taste has developed its great figurative reach — "to taste of knowledge," and the aesthetic and moral sense of discernment ("good taste") — while Milton's spelling tast keeps an old-fashioned, scriptural gravity.
18th Century Aesthetic Theory - Taste becomes a central term of Enlightenment aesthetics, the faculty of refined judgment, so the word spans everything from the apple's flavor to cultural discernment.
Contemporary Sense - Today taste covers the gustatory sense, the act of sampling, and aesthetic judgment, while the archaic spelling tast survives only in early modern texts like Paradise Lost.
