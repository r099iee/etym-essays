- Old English – wær means “aware, wary”, and wærlīce “warily” is recorded — vigilance as plain prudence, a positive virtue of watchfulness.
- Middle English – “be war” (be on your guard) is a didactic commonplace; wariness is still about awareness, not yet suspicion.
- c. 1550–1560 – wary and warily are re-formed from war(e) + -y / -ly; the meaning crystallises as cautious watchfulness against danger.
- 18th–19th c. – in the novel, warily shifts toward interpersonal caution and distrust: characters eye, answer and approach each other warily.
- 20th–21st c. – “tread warily” becomes a fixed political and diplomatic phrase; journalism uses warily for guarded reactions (markets, analysts, negotiators).
- Today – warily means acting with alertness to harm or deception — the ancient “awareness” sharpened into modern suspicion.
