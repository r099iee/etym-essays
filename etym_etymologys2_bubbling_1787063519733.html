Middle Low German / Middle Dutch (13th c.)
bobbel / bubbele
“bubble; small sphere of liquid film”
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
bobel
“bubble, small globule of air in liquid”
↓
Modern English (14th c. onward)
bubble
“to rise in bubbles; to make a bubbling sound”
↓
Present-day English (21st c.)
BUBBLING
“present participle: producing bubbles or their sound; effervescent, lively”
NOTE: The medieval Germanic forms are themselves imitative (echoic) of the sound of rising gas; the exact route of borrowing into English (Middle Dutch vs. Middle Low German, or an internal English formation on the echoic pattern) is not fully settled, and no PIE ancestor can be reconstructed for an onomatopoeic word of this kind.
