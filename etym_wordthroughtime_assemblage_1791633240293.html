- Late 15th–16th Century: Gathering of Persons - Borrowed from Middle French into Early Modern English to denote a gathering, crowd, or meeting of people, particularly a formal social, military, or political convocation.
- 17th–18th Century: Mechanical & Architectural Joining - Extended into carpentry, stonemasonry, and mechanical engineering to designate the physical fitting together of prefabricated parts (e.g., timber framing, joinery, gear assemblies).
- Mid-20th Century: Artistic Revolution & Found-Object Sculpture - Coined in art criticism by Jean Dubuffet in 1953 and canonized internationally by the 1961 Museum of Modern Art exhibition *The Art of Assemblage*, designating three-dimensional art composed of disparate found objects.
- Late 20th–21st Century: Continental Philosophy & Critical Theory - Adopted into post-structuralist theory (translating Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari's *agencement*), defining dynamic, open-ended socio-technical networks that continually reconfigure power, agency, and meaning.
