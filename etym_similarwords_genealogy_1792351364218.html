• pedigree: Similar because both trace ancestry and linear descent through successive generations; differs because pedigree originally denoted a physical genealogical diagram resembling a crane's foot (Old French pié de grue) and is frequently applied to domesticated animals (thoroughbred horses, show dogs) to certify purebred bloodlines, whereas genealogy is a broader historical discipline and human cultural narrative.
  • lineage: Similar because both identify continuous descent from a specific common ancestor; differs because lineage emphasizes the single unbroken biological or dynastic thread linking an individual back to an progenitor, whereas genealogy encompasses the entire branching network, kinship systems, collateral lines, and scholarly study thereof.
  • ancestry: Similar because both refer to an individual's forebears and ancestral roots; differs because ancestry is a collective noun denoting the persons from whom one is descended (one's forebears collectively), whereas genealogy refers to the structured, organized account, chart, or scientific investigation of those relationships.
  • descent: Similar because both concern hereditary transmission from ancestral origins; differs because descent is a legal and biological vector emphasizing downward transmission of blood, legal rights, titles, or properties from ancestor to heir (e.g., "line of descent"), whereas genealogy is the comprehensive systematic science of charting such descents.
  • bloodline: Similar because both evoke hereditary biological connections across generations; differs because bloodline carries strong visceral, aristocratic, or genetic connotations of inherited physical temperament and racial/familial purity, whereas genealogy is an analytical, documentary, and historical discipline.
  • heritage: Similar because both involve that which is inherited from preceding generations; differs because heritage encompasses intangible cultural values, traditions, monuments, architectural relics, and communal history, whereas genealogy strictly charts genealogical descent and biological or legal kinship connections.
  • extraction: Similar because both indicate ancestral origin or genealogical derivation; differs because extraction is typically used in formal or literary phrasing to denote an individual's socio-ethnic or regional origins (e.g., "a scholar of French extraction"), whereas genealogy refers to a detailed ancestral tree or treatise.
  • parentage: Similar because both relate to biological and genealogical origin; differs because parentage focuses narrowly and immediately on one's immediate mother and father, whereas genealogy spans deep historical time across multiple generations and remote ancestral founders.
  • stirps: Similar because both denote lines of genealogical descent; differs because stirps is a technical Roman and civil law term denoting the main stem or family root from which distinct branches spring for the purposes of intestate estate division (per stirpes), whereas genealogy is a general cultural and historical term.
  • stemma: Similar because both represent genealogical relationship trees; differs because stemma is heavily specialized in classical philology and textual criticism to designate a tree diagram showing the descent and variant relationships of surviving manuscript copies from an original archetype (stemma codicum), whereas genealogy primarily concerns biological and family lineages.
  • family tree: Similar because both describe the branching generational chart of a family; differs because family tree is a popular, informal graphic metaphor, whereas genealogy serves as both the technical academic discipline and the overarching philosophical framework of historical derivation.
  • phylogenesis: Similar because both trace genealogical descent over extended temporal spans; differs because phylogenesis operates in macro-evolutionary biology to map the evolutionary development and diversification of an entire species or higher taxonomic group over geological epochs, whereas genealogy primarily investigates familial, individual, or micro-lineage descent.
