• Valenciennes: Similar because both are world-renowned historical Flemish/French bobbin laces celebrated for their delicacy; differs because Valenciennes lace is worked with the same thread for both the pattern and the mesh (creating a completely flat texture), whereas Mechlin outlines its floral motifs with a thicker, glossy gimp or cordonnet thread.
  • Alençon: Similar because both are luxury European laces used for aristocratic trimmings and cravats; differs because Alençon is a needle lace (point d'Alençon) worked with a needle on parchment, known as the 'Queen of Lace', with a heavier cordonnet padded with horsehair, whereas Mechlin is an ultra-light bobbin lace.
  • Chantilly: Similar because both are famous Continental bobbin laces; differs because Chantilly is predominantly famous for its delicate black silk lace with shadow effects, popular in 19th-century shawls and fans, whereas historical Mechlin is typically white or ivory flax/linen lace.
  • Brussels lace: Similar because both originated in neighboring Belgian textile centers of Flanders; differs because Brussels lace is famous for its intricate appliqué and point de gaze techniques often worked in separate pieces joined together, whereas Mechlin is historically worked in a single continuous piece on the pillow.
  • Point de Venise: Similar because both represent high-status historic European laces worn by nobility; differs because Point de Venise is an opulent, heavy Italian needlepoint lace featuring raised sculptural scrolls and picots, whereas Mechlin is ethereal, light, and airy.
  • Guipure: Similar because both are decorative laces used in haute couture and bridal wear; differs because Guipure is a heavy, open lace where motifs are linked by bars or plaits without any background net or mesh, unlike the intricate hexagonal mesh of Mechlin.
  • Tatting: Similar because both produce delicate openwork lace; differs because tatting is a handcraft constructed of knotted rings and chains made with a small shuttle, whereas Mechlin is an industrial/guild masterpiece made with dozens of bobbins on a lace pillow.
  • Filigree: Similar because both describe delicate, web-like ornamental work; differs because filigree refers to intricate metalwork formed from twisted gold or silver wires, whereas Mechlin is a textile lace made of fine linen thread.
