- incantations (in English from the 14th century, Latin 'incantatio', 'a singing upon') - formulas chanted or sung to work magic; the spell as sound, the voice as instrument, rhythm and resonance believed to bind reality; later, any mesmerizing, ritually intoned recitation.
- cantraps (Scots 'cantraip', in literary English from the 18th century) - the small, crooked, mischievous charm of lowland fairy magic: a knot to bind a neighbor's churn, a whispered verse to sour milk; the sly, earthbound register of sorcery, the magic of thresholds and crossroads.
- runes (from Old Norse 'rūn' / Old English 'rūn', 'secret, mystery, whispered counsel') - the letters of the ancient Germanic alphabet, each felt to be a mystery; by extension any secret symbolic writing that holds the force it names — magic for the eye and the hand rather than the voice.
- thaumaturgies (in learned English from the 16th century, Greek 'thaumatourgia', 'wonder-working') - the trained, technical performance of the marvelous; miracle as applied method, the most elevated, almost clinical register in which a human will wrenches and molds space.
