Proto-Indo-European *strey- “to stretch, stretch out”, extended in the dental form *streydʰ- to the taking of long, stretching steps (the ultimate reconstruction is debated; some lexicographers leave the root unassigned) → Proto-Germanic *strīdaną “to stride, step wide” → Old English strīdan “to stride” (attested; the verb of long, deliberate steps) → Middle English striden “to stride, to mount, to ride” → English stride (v.) “to walk with long steps” → frequentative formation STRIDE + -le (the iterative suffix seen in wrestle from wrest, sparkle from spark, nestle from nest) → English straddle (c. 1560; earlier stradle, strodle) “to stand astride, with legs wide” (the vowel shortening stride → straddle is characteristic of such frequentative forms) → straddle + -ing → straddling (present participle/gerund, the exact form of this card). Debate note: the PIE etymon *strey- is widely cited but not universally accepted; the Germanic family itself (*strīdaną and its offspring) is solid, and English stride is its direct descendant.
