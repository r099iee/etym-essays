- Mid-20th Century (1950s): Arnheim's Art and Visual Perception - Introduced by Gestalt psychologist Rudolf Arnheim as an experimental demonstration: moving a circular black disk across a white square to map perceptual vector fields and induction.
- 1960s to 1970s: Foundation of design pedagogy and Bauhaus-inspired visual literacy - Adopted in art academies and graphic design textbooks (e.g., Donis A. Dondis, Kepes) to train designers in spatial balance, framing, and visual hierarchy.
- 1980s to 1990s: Human-computer interaction and GUI cursor theory - Influenced emerging theories of screen layout, visual weight, and focal points in interface design, comparing mouse pointers and floating icons to roving focal stimuli.
- 21st Century: Cognitive aesthetics, neuroaesthetics, and computational vision - Cited in empirical aesthetics and computational modeling of visual saliency maps, representing the classical Gestalt proof that perception generates emergent structural forces beyond retinal input.
