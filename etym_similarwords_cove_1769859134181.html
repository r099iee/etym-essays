• Bay: Similar because both name coastal bodies of water bounded by land; differs because a bay is large and open to the sea on several sides, while a cove is small, enclosed, and deeply sheltered.
  • Inlet: Similar because both name arms of the sea cutting into the land; differs because inlet is any narrow opening from the sea and carries no strong idea of enclosure, whereas a cove is specifically sheltered and pocket-like.
  • Harbour / Harbor: Similar because both name sheltered coastal waters; differs because a harbour is a purpose-made or natural refuge for shipping with docks and works, while a cove is a natural feature, usually too small for harbourside activity.
  • Haven: Similar because both name a place of shelter on the water; differs because haven is a literary word for any place of refuge (also figurative, "safe haven"), while cove stays strictly geographic and literal.
  • Anchorage: Similar because both describe a place where a boat can lie sheltered; differs because anchorage names the functional ability to anchor rather than the landform itself.
  • Bight: Similar because both name a curve or bend in a coastline; differs because a bight is a broad, open, gentle indentation of the shore with no enclosure, while a cove is small, tight, and sheltered.
  • Creek: Similar because both can name a small arm of the sea or inlet; differs because creek in American English more often means a small stream or river, and its tidal-inlet sense lacks the strong idea of sheltered enclosure that a cove has.
  • Fjord / Firth: Similar because both name long narrow arms of the sea; differs because these are vast, deep, glacier-carved inlets, while a cove is small and pocket-sized.
  • Lagoon: Similar because both name sheltered coastal water; differs because a lagoon is a shallow body separated from the sea by a sandbank or reef, often nearly enclosed, whereas a cove opens directly into the sea behind sheltering headlands.
  • Recess: Similar because both name a sheltered hollow or niche set back into a surface; differs because recess is general (also rooms, walls, time off), while cove is usually coastal.
  • Hollow: Similar because both name a sunken, sheltered place; differs because hollow is generic for any depression in a surface, while cove retains the specific senses of a sheltered coastal or mountain pocket.
  • Nook: Similar because both name a small sheltered corner; differs because nook is about interior or domestic corners (a nook in a wall), while cove is primarily about outdoor, coastal geography.
  • Chap / Fellow / Bloke (slang sense): Similar because they are informal words for a man, as the dated slang "cove" is; differs because chap, bloke, and fellow are current and neutral-British, while slang "cove" is archaic and belongs to thieves' cant and old sea stories.
