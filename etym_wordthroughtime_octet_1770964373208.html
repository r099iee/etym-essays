- 19th c. 'A group of eight musicians' — borrowed into English from Italian ottetto (from otto 'eight') as the established term for an eight-member musical ensemble, a formal and technical musical register.
- Later 19th c. 'An eight-line poem or stanza' — carried over from prosody, where octave described the opening eight lines of a sonnet; octet became the general name for any self-contained eight-line verse unit, used with artistic dignity.
- Contemporary 'Any set of eight people or things; a byte of eight bits' — generalized far beyond music and verse to count anything in eights, and adopted in computing for an eight-bit byte, so the word now spans artistic and technical fields alike.
