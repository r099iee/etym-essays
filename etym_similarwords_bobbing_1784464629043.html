• Bouncing: Similar because both describe repeated up-and-down motion; differs because bouncing is springier, higher, and more energetic, while bobbing is low, quick, and light.
  • Nodding: Similar because both describe a quick dipping movement; differs because nodding is specifically the head's motion of assent or greeting, while bobbing can involve the whole body or any floating object.
  • Weaving: Similar because both describe continuous, unsteady movement; differs because weaving is lateral and sinuous, while bobbing is short vertical repetition.
  • Fidgeting: Similar because both describe restless small motion; differs because fidgeting is broader, nervous restlessness of hands and body, while bobbing is the specific quick up-and-down dip.
  • Dipping: Similar because both describe a downward plunge or lowering movement; differs because dipping stresses the downward motion alone, while bobbing stresses the whole quick up-and-down cycle.
  • Bounding: Similar because both involve movement against a surface; differs because bounding is leaping with big strides, while bobbing stays low in short dips.
  • Jiggling: Similar because both describe small, quick, slightly comic movement; differs because jiggling is a shakier, looser motion, while bobbing is a defined up-and-down dip.
  • Bobbing and weaving: Similar because the idiom is built directly on this participle; differs because the paired phrase couples the vertical dip with lateral dodging in a boxing context.
