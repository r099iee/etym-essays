Earliest stage to present WHOOPED (imitative; no PIE ancestry): - Old French huper / houper "to shout, cry out; a hunter's halloo" (echoic; the English loan's immediate source). - Middle English whopen / houpen "to shout with a loud, excited voice" (mid-14th c.); noun houp "a loud call, hooting cry" (14th c.). - English whoop (verb) "to utter a loud, excited cry"; the wh- spelling fixed from c. 1450, an unetymological flourish, while the pronunciation stayed closer to "hoop". - Derived formations in English: whooping cough (1739), war-whoop (1761), whooping crane (1791), whoop it up (1881), whoopee (1845), whoopee cushion (1931). - Modern English WHOOPED (past tense, -ed) "uttered loud, wild, high-pitched cries of excitement, triumph, or derision." Note: no Proto-Indo-European ancestry exists — the word is imitative, and the Old French source itself is echoic. Parallel echoic formations elsewhere (Middle Dutch hupen "to shout", German hurra "hurrah") confirm the sound-symbolic nature of the family.
