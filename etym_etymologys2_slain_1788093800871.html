Note: the word belongs to the Germanic family of striking (Gothic slahan, Old Norse slá, German schlagen "to strike, hit"); the PIE root is standardly reconstructed as *slak-, but its wider extra-Germanic cognates are debated — Welsh llach "blow, stroke" is often adduced, while the old comparison with Greek laskō "to burst with noise" is now generally rejected. The class VI ablaut (slēan – slōg – slægen) is regular and produces the modern slew/slain without irregularity.
