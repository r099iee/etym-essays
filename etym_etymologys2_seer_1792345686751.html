PIE (reconstructed)
*sekʷ-
"to see, notice, perceive"
(root of Germanic *sehwaną; cf. Hittite šak(k)- "to know")
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*sehwaną
"to see, look, perceive visually"
↓
Old English (c. 8th c.)
sēon
"to see, behold, observe, discern"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
sēn
"to see, perceive visually or mentally"
↓
Middle English (c. 1380; Wycliffe Bible)
sēere
"one who sees; a prophet, diviner"
(formed from sēn + agent suffix -ere; glossing Latin videns / Hebrew ro'eh)
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.; King James Bible)
seer
"prophet, divine visionary"
↓
Modern English (18th c.–present)
seer
"spiritual prophet; intuitive or secular forecaster"
