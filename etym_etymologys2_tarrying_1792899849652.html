Middle English (early 14th c.)
tarien, taryen
‘to delay, detain, hinder’
↓
Middle English (late 14th c.)
tarien
‘to linger, stay, remain’
↓
Early Modern English (16th c.)
tarry
‘to loiter, wait, abide’
↓
Modern English (current)
tarrying
‘lingering, sojourning’

Note: deeper origin is obscure and honestly so marked. The verb is sometimes compared with Old French tarder (from Latin tardare, to delay, from tardus, slow), but the sounds do not align cleanly and the OED leaves the etymology unsettled. It must not be confused with tardy, which genuinely descends from that Latin line through Old French tardif.
