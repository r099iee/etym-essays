• pulled: Similar because both name drawing something toward or along; differs because "pulled" is the general verb for exerting traction, while "towed" implies drawing something along behind, usually by a rope, chain, or vehicle.
  • dragged: Similar because both name hauling something along; differs because "dragged" suggests effort, friction, or unwilling motion (often along the ground), while "towed" is the smooth, controlled trailing of a vessel or vehicle behind a power source.
  • hauled: Similar because both name pulling a load with effort; differs because "hauled" stresses heavy labour and cargo (hauling timber, hauling a net), while "towed" stresses the trailing of a craft or vehicle behind another.
  • tugged: Similar because both are used of boats (a tugboat tows a ship) and of pulling; differs because "tugged" means a sharp, repeated, forceful pull, while "towed" is a steady continuous pull along a path.
  • drawn: Similar because both name pulling along (the raft "was drawn out of the current"); differs because "draw" is a general, often literary verb for pulling, while "towed" specifically describes pulling behind by a line or vehicle.
  • lugged: Similar because both name carrying or pulling something heavy; differs because "lugged" is informal and suggests clumsy effort with a bulky object, whereas "towed" is technical and orderly.
  • trailed: Similar because both name something drawn along behind; differs because trailing implies following loosely in the wake without necessarily being attached or powered, while towing is a controlled pull by rope or coupling.
  • warped: Similar because both are nautical terms for moving a vessel by ropes; differs because warping moves a ship by hauling on a line made fast to a fixed point, while towing is done by another moving craft.
