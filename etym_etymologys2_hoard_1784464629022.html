Chain for root word hoard: PIE *ḱeudh- "to hide, conceal, cover" (reconstructed; reflected in the daughter languages) → Proto-Germanic *huzdą "hidden treasure, hoard" (reconstructed; the *-zd- cluster develops from *ḱeudh- by Germanic sound changes) → Old English hord "treasure; a secret store; hoard" (attested from the 8th c. CE, including Beowulf, where the dragon guards the hord) → Middle English hord / hoord / hoard (12th–15th c. CE) → Modern English hoard (noun and verb), hoard (this card) Debated note: the Greek verb keuthein "to hide" is often cited alongside Germanic *huzdą under the same PIE *ḱeudh-, but the correspondence is debated in handbooks and should be treated with caution. The English line itself is securely Germanic and native. Do not confuse with horde "a swarming crowd," a separate loan ultimately from Turkic via Eastern European languages — it is not derived from *huzdą.
