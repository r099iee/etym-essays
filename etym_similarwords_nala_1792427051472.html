• Viśvakarman: Similar because both represent divine architects and master builders in Hindu epic mythology; differs because Viśvakarman is the primordial divine artificer of the celestial realms and creator of all heavenly palaces and weapons, whereas Nala is his earthly/vānara son whose engineering genius is focused specifically on the oceanic causeway to Laṅkā.
  • Maya: Similar because both are celebrated master architects and builders in Sanskrit literature; differs because Maya (Mayāsura) is an architect of the Asuras and Daityas associated with magical illusions, subterranean palaces, and assembly halls (such as the Maya Sabhā in the Mahābhārata), while Nala constructs through physical structural engineering and divine floating boons.
  • Daedalus: Similar because both represent archetypal mythological master builders and engineers whose inventions overcome impossible physical obstacles; differs because Daedalus belongs to Greco-Roman myth, constructing labyrinthine prisons and avian wings that invite hubris, whereas Nala acts in pious service to divine righteousness (Dharma) and epic kingship.
  • Setukāra: Similar because both designate a builder or engineer of bridges; differs because Setukāra is a generic occupational noun in Sanskrit for any bridge-constructor, whereas Nala is the specific proper name of the mythic architect of the primordial oceanic bridge.
  • Bāhuka: Similar because both names refer to the heroic protagonist of the Nalopākhyāna in the Mahābhārata; differs because Bāhuka is the pseudonym and physically deformed guise assumed by King Nala during his exile when serving King Ṛtuparṇa as a master charioteer.
  • Architect: Similar because both denote an expert designer and director of complex physical construction projects; differs because architect is an abstract Greco-Latin occupational title, whereas Nala is a concrete epic protagonist embodying divine lineage, martial valor, and engineering mastery.
  • Rāma Setu: Similar because both terms are intrinsically linked to the physical causeway across the Palk Strait; differs because Rāma Setu names the entire sacred bridge after the divine prince who commissioned it, whereas Nala names the individual artisan whose hands laid the foundations.
  • Charioteer (Sūta): Similar because King Nala in his disguise as Bāhuka was the greatest driver of horses in the epic world; differs because a sūta is a courtly bardic and equestrian profession, whereas Nala was an imperial monarch and consecrated sovereign of Niṣadha.
