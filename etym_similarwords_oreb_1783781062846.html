• Horeb: Similar because Horeb is the same name for the same holy mountain, sharing every reference; differs because Horeb is the standard English spelling (KJV, scholarship), while Oreb is Milton's poetic variant that drops the initial H in the Vulgate-derived tradition.
  • Sinai: Similar because Sinai is the twin name of the same mountain of God, paired with Horeb/Oreb in scripture and in Milton's line; differs because Sinai is preferred by the Yahwist/Priestly sources and stresses the lawgiving theophany (Exodus 19–20), while Horeb is preferred by the Elohist source and stresses the burning bush, the rock, and the still small voice.
  • Nebo: Similar because Mount Nebo is the other great mountain of Moses, where he viewed the Promised Land and died (Deuteronomy 34); differs because Nebo is the mountain of the view and death, while Horeb/Oreb is the mountain of the call, the water, and the Law.
  • Carmel: Similar because Mount Carmel is another biblical mountain of theophany and of God's answer by fire (Elijah's contest, 1 Kings 18); differs because Carmel is the northern headland of the contest with Baal, while Horeb is the southern desert mount of the still small voice that follows that contest in the Elijah story.
  • Tabor: Similar because Mount Tabor is another holy mountain of Israel associated with a divine manifestation; differs because Tabor is in Galilee and carries the transfiguration of Christ, while Horeb carries the Old Testament theophanies of the wilderness.
  • Gilboa: Similar because Mount Gilboa is another named biblical mountain with a famous narrative (Saul's death); differs because Gilboa is a site of defeat and mourning, while Horeb is the site of revelation and covenant.
  • Seir: Similar because Mount Seir is another wilderness mountain of the desert regions named in the wanderings; differs because Seir belongs to Edom and the Esau tradition, while Horeb belongs to the Moses and theophany tradition.
