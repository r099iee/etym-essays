• Wade: Similar in moving through water on foot; differs because wade is the general word for walking in water at any depth, with no implication of a designated crossing or of reaching the other side, while fording implies crossing a river at a shallow, passable place.
  • Cross: Similar in going over a body of water; differs because cross is entirely generic and could be by bridge, boat, or road, whereas fording specifically means wading at a natural shallow.
  • Traverse: Similar in travelling across country; differs because traverse is formal and applies to any terrain, not specifically to wading a river at a ford.
  • Ferry: Similar in crossing water; differs because ferry means carrying people or goods across by boat, while fording is walking through the shallows on foot.
  • Negotiate: Similar in getting across a difficult piece of ground; differs because negotiate is general and technical — to deal with any obstacle — while forded names the specific act of wading at a shallow.
  • Pass over: Similar in going across; differs because pass over is literary and general, with no image of wading, stones, or a designated shallow.
  • Span: Similar in reaching across; differs because span implies bridging (a bridge spans a river), while fording is wading through the water itself.
  • Plod: Similar in walking with effort; differs because plod describes the heavy, slow step, without the specific geography of a shallow river crossing.
