Proto-Indo-European (c. 3rd millennium BCE)
*smei- / *smeid-
"to smear, wipe, rub"
↓
Proto-Germanic (1st millennium BCE-CE)
*smītaną
"to throw, fling, smear; to strike"
↓
Old English (8th c. CE)
smītan
"to smear, daub, defile; to strike, kill"
↓
Middle English (12th-14th c.)
smiten
"to strike, beat, slay, punish"
↓
Middle English (13th-14th c., past tense)
smote
"struck, beat, slew"
↓
smote

Nuance: the root originally meant 'to smear', and the sense 'to strike' grew through the intermediate idea of throwing, flinging, or dashing; the old 'smear' sense survives in cognates such as Old Norse smíða 'to forge' and English smith, which belongs to the same family through the idea of working material by blows.
