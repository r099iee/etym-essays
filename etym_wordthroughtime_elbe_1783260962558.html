- Classical antiquity - the Elbe (Latin Albis) was the eastern frontier of the Roman world, a river beyond which Roman arms and Roman religion never prevailed, so it entered history as the boundary of civilization.
- Macaulay's usage - in the 19th century Macaulay made the Elbe the emblem of the unconquered pagan Germanic homeland, the source of the superstitions that Ida and Cerdic carried to Britain, the antithesis of the Romanized Rhine.
- Contemporary sense - the Elbe is a major German and Czech river (Czech Labe), a name of historical, geographic, and political registers, remembered in Elbe Day and in the geography of Hamburg and Dresden.
