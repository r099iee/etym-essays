• Pulled: Similar because pulling is the foundational physical action involved in yanking; differs because pulled is a neutral, general term that can describe a slow, gentle, smooth, or continuous motion, whereas yanked specifically demands a sharp, sudden, violent, and jarring jerk.
  • Jerked: Similar because both involve sudden, sharp, spasmodic motions; differs because jerked can describe involuntary muscle spasms or quick movements in any direction (e.g., jerking one's head in surprise), whereas yanked specifically implies applying an external grasping tensile pull upon an object.
  • Snatched: Similar because both involve quick, forceful physical actions; differs because snatched emphasizes the swift, opportunistic seizing or grabbing of an object (often furtively or with quick fingers), whereas yanked emphasizes the heavy, forceful pulling motion applied after seizing.
  • Wrenched: Similar because both denote violent pulling force; differs because wrenched specifically involves a twisting, torquing, or rotational strain that often causes damage, sprain, or mechanical distortion (e.g., "wrenched his ankle", "wrenched the door off its hinges").
  • Tugged: Similar because both denote pulling at something; differs because tugged suggests repeated, small, or moderate pulling efforts (e.g., "a child tugged at her mother's skirt"), whereas yanked conveys a single, powerful, abrupt heave.
  • Withdrawn: Similar because something yanked from broadcast is withdrawn; differs because withdrawn is a formal, polite, and bureaucratic term devoid of the abrupt, decisive, and jarring violence implied by yanked.
