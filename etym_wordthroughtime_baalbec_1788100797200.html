- Hellenistic age: the Greek-speaking world renames the sanctuary Heliopolis, "City of the Sun" — the city enters classical literature under a Greek identity.
- Medieval centuries: the Semitic name Ba'al-bakk ("Baal of the plain") persists in Syriac and Arabic; the colossal Roman temples stand half-ruined, half-fortified, and the site keeps its sacred prestige.
- 1600s–1700s: early modern Europe rediscovers Balbec — travellers and engravers (Robert Wood, 1757) popularise the ruins; English spellings wobble between Balbec, Baalbec and Balbek.
- 1800s: Baalbec settles as the form in Victorian travel books and magazines; Wilde's Happy Prince (1888) sends the swallows' companions to nest "in the Temple of Baalbec" — the very emblem of exotic eastern ruins.
- 1900s–today: Baalbek standardises in scholarship and cartography; the city becomes a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1984) and a festival stage — while "Baalbec" survives as the older, more literary spelling of Wilde's page.
