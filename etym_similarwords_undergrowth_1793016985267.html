• Underbrush: Similar because both name low tangled growth beneath trees; differs because underbrush is the American everyday word, while undergrowth is the standard British and ecological term.
  • Understorey: Similar because both describe vegetation layers below the canopy; differs because understorey is the technical ecologist term for all lower tiers, while undergrowth stresses the tangled woody mass itself.
  • Thicket: Similar because both are dense tangles hard to walk through; differs because a thicket is a discrete clump or brake, while undergrowth is the continuous low layer of a whole wood.
  • Bracken: Similar because bracken often forms the field layer of woods; differs because bracken is specifically the tall fern itself, while undergrowth includes ferns, brambles, shrubs, and saplings together.
  • Brambles: Similar because brambles are the thorniest part of undergrowth; differs because brambles are blackberry canes alone, while undergrowth is the whole mixed community.
  • Coppice: Similar because coppice shoots form managed undergrowth; differs because coppice is wood deliberately cut for regrowth (hazel, chestnut), while undergrowth may be wild and unmanaged.
  • Scrub: Similar because both are low woody vegetation; differs because scrub grows in the open on heaths and hillsides, while undergrowth grows specifically beneath taller trees.
  • Tangle: Similar because both impede walkers with interlaced stems; differs because tangle is the general snarled state of anything, while undergrowth is living woodland vegetation.
  • Ground cover: Similar because both carpet the forest floor; differs because ground cover is low herbs and creepers planted or native, while undergrowth rises knee to head high and blocks passage.
