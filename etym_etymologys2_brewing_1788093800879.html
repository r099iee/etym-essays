PIE (c. 4500–3500 BCE)
*bʰreuh₁-
“to boil, bubble, effervesce”
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – c. 100 CE)
*breuwaną
“to brew, ferment,”
“to make by boiling”
(class II strong verb)
↓
Old English (c. 9th c.)
brēowan
“to brew (ale, beer), ferment”
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
brewen
“to brew; to mix, concoct,”
“to contrive (fig.)”
↓
Early Modern English (16th–17th c.)
brew
“to make beer; fig. of storms
and troubles: to gather”
↓
Modern English (present)
brewing
“developing, gathering (of trouble,
storm, news); making beer or tea”

Note: Cognates include German brauen, Dutch brouwen, Old Norse brugga. The same root, with metathesis of the initial cluster (*bʰr- > fr-), gave Latin fervēre “to boil”, source of English fervent and ferment.
