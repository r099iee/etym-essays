- Old English — the miller's verb: siftan shakes flour and meal through hair or cloth sieves, a daily kitchen and bakery labour.
- Middle English — moral sieving: preachers urge hearers to sift truth from flattery and doctrine from heresy, the kitchen act become spiritual hygiene.
- Early modern — law and learning: courts sift testimony and scholars sift authorities; Shakespeare's age trusts nothing unsifted.
- 18th–19th c. — industry refines it: millers, miners, and builders mechanise sifting with graded meshes, while Darwin sifts barnacles and Lyell sifts strata.
- 20th c. — information overload: journalists, auditors, and scientists sift data, evidence, and archives; thoroughness becomes a professional creed.
- Today — from kitchens to code: bakers still sift flour by hand while algorithms named SIFT sift pixels for robots, and Gandalf's advice holds everywhere: with liars, only patient shaking serves.
