• Mandarinfish: Similar because it is the most famous species of dragonet, often used synonymously in popular discourse; differs because 'Mandarinfish' refers to a specific genus/species (Synchiropus splendidus), while 'dragonet' refers to the entire family Callionymidae.
  • Goby: Similar because gobies are also small, benthic marine fish with large heads and often cryptic coloration, sharing similar habitats and ecological niches; differs because gobies belong to the family Gobiidae, distinct from Callionymidae, and typically lack the elaborate finnage and preopercular spine characteristic of many dragonets.
  • Blennie: Similar because blennies are another group of small, bottom-dwelling marine fish, often found in rocky crevices and tide pools, sharing a similar small size and benthic lifestyle; differs because blennies (family Blenniidae) have distinct morphological features, such as comb-like teeth and often fleshy cirri on their heads, and their body shape is generally more elongated and less depressed than dragonets.
  • Wyrmling: Similar because it denotes a young or small dragon, aligning with the less common, diminutive sense of 'dragonet'; differs because 'wyrmling' is exclusively used for mythical dragons, whereas 'dragonet' primarily refers to a specific type of fish.
  • Hatchling: Similar because it refers to a newly hatched creature, implying smallness and youth, which could apply to a 'baby dragon' interpretation of 'dragonet'; differs because 'hatchling' is a general term for any creature that has recently hatched from an egg, lacking the specific 'dragon-like' connotation.
