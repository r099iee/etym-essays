PIE *skaap- / *skep- "to cut, split, hew" (reconstructed; also the source of Latin scapus "shaft, stem, pole," Greek skeptron "staff, sceptre," and English shave) → Proto-Germanic *skaftaz "shaft, pole, spear-stem" (reconstructed; the thing cut or cleaved from a tree) → Old English sceaft "shaft of a spear, spear; pole, handle" (attested 8th-9th c.) → Middle English schafte "shaft of a spear, handle, pole" (12th-15th c.) → Modern English shaft "long slender body, stem, column, beam" → plural shafts (the rootWord as given here; the plural of shaft). NOTE: The reconstruction PIE *skaap- "to cut, split" → Germanic *skaftaz is the standard account and is widely accepted; the semantic link is the pole cut from a tree. No stage is seriously debated.
