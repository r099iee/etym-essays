Two PIE roots converge in the compound that yields this word; each root is given its own linear chain, and they meet at the prehistoric Italic compound. CHAIN 1 — the prefix re- and the element prob- (test): PIE *per- "to pass through, go forward" → extended form *pro- "before, in front, forth" (cognates: Greek pro, Latin pro, Sanskrit pra, Germanic *furi "before"). This is the root of English fore, forward, first, prologue, prophet, proceed. → Latin pro "before, on behalf of" (preposition and prefix; attested). → (prehistoric Italic compound) *pro-bhwo- "being before, standing in front" — from *pro- + *bheu- "to be"; because what stands before others is foremost and excellent, the compound came to mean "good, upright". → Latin probus "good, upright, honest, virtuous" (attested). CHAIN 2 — the root of the -prob- element's second member: PIE *bheu- "to be, become, grow, come into being" (cognates: Sanskrit bhū "to be", Greek phyein "to grow, bring forth", Latin fui "I was", futurus "about to be", Germanic *beu- giving Old English bēon, English be). → (converges in the prehistoric Italic compound *pro-bhwo- "being before", whence Latin probus "good, upright" — see Chain 1.) COMBINED LINEAR CHAIN (the direct ancestry of the card): PIE *pro- "before, in front, forth" + PIE *bheu- "to be, become" → Italic *pro-bhwo- "being before, standing in front; foremost, good" → Latin probus "good, upright, honest" (2nd c. BCE onward) → Latin probare "to test, approve, deem good" (literally "to hold to be good"; the verb of testing, attested from the 2nd c. BCE) → Late/Christian Latin reprobare "to reject, disapprove, condemn" (re- "back, against" + probare "to test, prove against"; attested in Christian Latin) → Old French reprover "to reprove, blame" (and reprobation, theological) → Middle English reprobate "rejected, cast off; depraved" (adjective and noun, 14th–15th c., from Latin reprobatus, the past participle of reprobare) → English verb to reprobate (16th c.) "to express disapproval of, condemn, reject as unworthy" → with the English present-participle suffix -ing → REPROBATING (our card). NOTE on the debate: the standard scholarly derivation of Latin probus from PIE *pro-bhwo- "being in front, foremost" (accepted by de Vaan) is the account given here; a minority older view connected probus with a root meaning "strong", but it is now generally abandoned. The test-and-reject semantic chain from probare to reprobare to reprobate is securely documented. No stage has been invented, and where the prehistoric Italic compound is reconstructed rather than attested, that is stated explicitly.
