- Ancient Israel & Biblical Period (c. 1000–400 BCE) - Bestowed as a revered Hebrew theophoric name expressing solemn covenantal faith ('My God is He'), immortalized by the young speaker Elihu son of Barachel in the Book of Job (chapters 32–37).
- Hellenistic and Patristic Era (c. 3rd c. BCE–5th c. CE) - Transliterated into Greek (*Elioús*) in the Septuagint and Latin (*Eliu* / *Elihu*) in Jerome's Vulgate, interpreted by Christian theologians as an enigmatic prophetic figure standing between human philosophy and divine theophany.
- Protestant Reformation and English Puritanism (16th–17th c.) - Revived enthusiastically as a baptismal given name among English Puritans who rejected Catholic saint names in favor of austere, textually faithful Old Testament appellatives.
- Colonial American Onomastics & Collegiate Patronage (18th c.) - Brought to colonial Boston and Connecticut, crowned in 1718 when East India Company nabob Elihu Yale endowed the Collegiate School in New Haven, permanently linking the name to Yale College and the nickname 'Elis.'
- 19th to 21st Century - Preserved across American public life through distinguished legal, diplomatic, and literary figures (such as statesman Elihu Root and peace activist Elihu Burritt), as well as Yale's venerable senior society, Elihu (founded 1903).
