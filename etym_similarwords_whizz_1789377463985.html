• Hiss: Similar because both are onomatopoeic words mimicking sharp, sibilant aerodynamic sounds; differs because hiss is a voiceless alveolar fricative ([s]) describing the escape of steam, gas under pressure, or the warning sound of a serpent, whereas whizz is a voiced, buzzing sound ([z]) specifically produced by an object flying rapidly through the air.
  • Zoom: Similar because both describe rapid physical movement and the acoustic pitch of high velocity; differs because zoom denotes a deep, continuous, resonant low-frequency hum or drone (such as an accelerating aircraft engine or sports car) and often implies steep upward climbing, whereas whizz emphasizes a sharp, whistling rush.
  • Whistle: Similar because both denote high-pitched sounds produced by air rushing past a narrow aperture or moving body; differs because whistle is a clear, musical, tone-based acoustic note produced intentionally by human lips or wind across a flute/pipe, whereas whizz is a rasping, rushing, mechanical friction sound.
  • Zip: Similar because both are snappy onomatopoeias signifying energetic, high-speed motion; differs because zip emphasizes a sudden, sharp, instantaneous darting motion (often with a light cracking or sliding sound, like a zipper), whereas whizz suggests a sustained aerodynamic rush over a longer flight path.
  • Buzz: Similar because both feature a voiced alveolar sibilant vibration ([z]); differs because buzz describes the low-frequency, stationary, continuous vibration of bee wings, electrical currents, or insect swarms, whereas whizz inherently requires ballistic or airborne forward locomotion.
  • Flash: Similar because both describe events that pass with breathtaking, instantaneous speed; differs because flash is fundamentally visual, signifying a momentary burst of bright light or a sight that vanishes in an instant, whereas whizz is primarily auditory and kinetic, anchoring in sound and velocity.
  • Prodigy: Similar because both can colloquially designate an individual of extraordinary intellectual or technical ability; differs because prodigy is an elevated, formal psychological and sociological term typically applied to child geniuses who display master-level adult performance, whereas whiz is an informal, affectionate slang term for anyone with quick, effortless aptitude.
