Note on morphology and mythological etymology: In Sanskrit grammatical tradition (Pāṇini 4.1.82–83), the formation of Maithilī represents a standard secondary taddhita patronymic/relational derivation. The base toponym Mithilā undergoes initial vowel strengthening (vṛddhi), converting the short vowel i into the diphthong ai, followed by the addition of the feminine nominal suffix -ī (ṅīp), producing maithilī ("the woman belonging to Mithilā"). In traditional Purāṇic etymology (as recorded in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa and Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa), the city Mithilā was named after King Mithi, who was miraculously generated by the holy seers by churning (*manthana*) the corpse of his father King Nimi to prevent the royal lineage from perishing without an heir; because he was produced by churning, he was named Mithi, his capital was named Mithilā, and his descendants were known as the Maithilas.
