queried:
- 16th c. - 'to query' enters English meaning to ask or seek information, borrowed as a verb from the language of inquiry; in the same period a 'query' is the question mark placed in a margin to flag a doubtful passage
- 17th-18th c. - the sense deepens from asking to testing: to query a statement is to doubt and probe its truth, and the word becomes the tool of examination and verification rather than mere curiosity
- 19th c. - the word formalises into commerce and publishing: a 'query' is a business question and a 'query letter' a professional inquiry, the vocabulary of the desk rather than the drawing room
- Contemporary - computing gives the word its sharpest modern form: a query is a formal search executed against a database, and to query is to interrogate a system for verification - the instrumental act of testing exactly what one doubts
