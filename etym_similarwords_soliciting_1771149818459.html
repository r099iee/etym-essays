• requesting: Similar because both mean asking for something; differs because requesting is neutral, polite, and non-persistent, while soliciting implies deliberate, often repeated or public urging.
  • entreating: Similar because both are earnest appeals for something; differs because entreating is emotional and almost desperate, appealing to mercy, while soliciting is more calculated, transactional, or formal.
  • beseeching: Similar because both describe urgent asking; differs because beseeching is intensely pleading and humble, while soliciting may be businesslike or even aggressive.
  • petitioning: Similar because both mean formally asking an authority for something; differs because petitioning usually implies a written or institutional request to a body, whereas soliciting is often person-to-person.
  • importuning: Similar because both imply pressing persistence; differs because importuning is more annoying, nagging, and relentless, bordering on harassment, while soliciting keeps a veneer of legitimacy.
  • appealing (to): Similar because both involve seeking a favourable response; differs because appealing often suggests an emotional or rights-based plea, while soliciting tends to be practical and goal-directed.
  • canvassing: Similar because both involve the systematic seeking of support or business; differs because canvassing implies going door-to-door or house-to-house to gather opinions or orders, while soliciting may occur by any means.
  • lobbying: Similar because both are efforts to win support; differs because lobbying is specifically political or institutional persuasion over time, while soliciting can be a single immediate request.
  • begging: Similar because both ask for something desired; differs because begging implies neediness and appeals to pity, while soliciting implies an active professional or social transaction.
  • accosting: Similar because both involve approaching someone aggressively; differs because accosting is purely the sudden physical approach (often in the soliciting-of-prostitution context) and lacks the general "requesting" meaning.
