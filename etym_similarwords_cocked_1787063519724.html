• tilted: Similar because both mean angled to one side; differs because tilted is neutral physical angle, cocked adds the idea of deliberate, alert positioning.
  • turned: Similar because both mean moved to a new position; differs because turned is general, cocked implies a sharp, deliberate, ready posture.
  • raised: Similar because both mean lifted upward; differs because raised is bland and lacks the ready-alert or jaunty nuance of cocked.
  • pricked: Similar because both describe ears raised to listen; differs because pricked applies specifically to ears (like an animal's), cocked more broadly.
  • angled: Similar because both mean set at an angle; differs because angled is technical and neutral, cocked is vivid and suggests intent.
  • canted: Similar because both mean obliquely inclined; differs because canted is nautical or technical jargon for slanting position, without the alertness.
  • primed: Similar because both (of a weapon) mean made ready; differs because primed means loaded and prepared, cocked specifically means the hammer is drawn back.
