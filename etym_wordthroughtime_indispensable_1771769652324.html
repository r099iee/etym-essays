- [16th-17th century cannot be dispensed] - [From 'dispense' (to distribute or do without); first means that which cannot be set aside or waived]
- [17th century duty and obligation] - [Takes hold in moral and religious prose: an indispensable duty is one that cannot be excused, carrying ethical weight]
- [18th-19th century practical necessity] - [Widens to things and roles: indispensable to a purpose, absolutely required for a task]
- [20th century to present modern overuse] - [Now common for people, tools, and skills, sometimes loosely ('our indispensable intern'); retains the core force of 'cannot be done without' but is often hyperbolic in everyday speech]
