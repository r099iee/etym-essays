• Edinburgh: Similar because it is the other great Lothian castle-capital; differs because Edinburgh is the political capital still inhabited, while Stirling is the strategic gateway now museal.
  • Tantallon: Similar because Ruskin pairs them as empty Scott castles; differs because Tantallon is a baronial sea-ruin, while Stirling is a royal palace-fortress.
  • Snowdoun: Similar because it is Scott's romantic alias for Stirling Castle; differs because Snowdoun is poetic invention, while Stirling is the charter name.
  • Bannockburn: Similar because it is the battlefield two miles from Stirling; differs because Bannockburn names the 1314 victory, while Stirling names the town and castle contested.
  • Stirling Bridge: Similar because it is the Forth crossing below the rock; differs because the Bridge names Wallace's 1297 battle, while Stirling names the whole position.
  • Doune Castle: Similar because it is the nearby Stewart fortress often confused on film with northern castles; differs because Doune is a courtyard castle on the Teith, while Stirling is a palace on a crag.
  • Scone: Similar because it is the other inauguration-place of Scottish kings; differs because Scone names the stone and abbey of crowning, while Stirling names the cradle and school of kings.
  • Knight of Snowdoun (FitzJames): Similar because he is the knight missing from Stirling in the source; differs because the Knight is the man Scott invents, while Stirling is the stones he should haunt.
  • Abbey Craig / Wallace Monument: Similar because it overlooks Stirling and commemorates its hero; differs because the Craig is the hill and monument, while Stirling is the town and castle below.
  • Bridge of Allan: Similar because it is the spa suburb and university town adjoining Stirling; differs because Bridge of Allan is Victorian leisure, while Stirling is medieval power.
