• Nettles: Similar because they are the thistle's constant companion in waste places, the pair of the source sentence; differs because nettles wound chemically with stinging hairs, while thistles wound mechanically with spines.
  • Briars: Similar because both are spiny armed plants of neglected ground; differs because briars are woody scrambling rose or bramble stems, while thistles are herbaceous upright weeds that die back yearly.
  • Brambles: Similar because both form impenetrable armed thickets; differs because brambles bear blackberries and root at the tip, while thistles bear nectar tufts and drift on down.
  • Teasels: Similar because their spiny combed heads are constantly mistaken for giant thistles; differs because teasels are stately biennials once used to raise cloth nap, while thistles are true pasture weeds.
  • Burdock: Similar because it is another tall burr-bearing weed of waste ground; differs because burdock clings with hooked burrs rather than pricking with spines, and its roots are eaten.
  • Gorse: Similar because it is the spiny evergreen scourge of open ground; differs because gorse is a woody coconut-scented shrub, while thistles are soft-stemmed herbs beneath their armour.
  • Thorns: Similar because both name sharp plant defences; differs because thorns are the weaponed stems of shrubs and trees, while thistles are whole herbaceous plants built entirely of prickle.
  • Ragwort: Similar because it shares the thistle's ground, the tall weed of neglected pasture; differs because ragwort's danger is slow liver poison to livestock, while the thistle's is honest immediate pain.
  • Docks: Similar because docks and thistles colonise the same disturbed soils; differs because docks are broad soft leaves famous for soothing nettle stings, while thistles soothe nothing.
