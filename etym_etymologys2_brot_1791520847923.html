Note: the deeper origin of Proto-Germanic *braudą is disputed. One hypothesis connects it with *breuwaną "to brew" and a PIE root meaning "to boil, seethe"; another takes the original sense as "piece, fragment" and explains the food sense by the breaking of bread. No PIE node is asserted here because the evidence does not decide between them. English bread, Dutch brood and Old Norse brauð are cognates; Gothic used the unrelated hlaifs.
