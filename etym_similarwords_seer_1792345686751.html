• prophet: Similar because both refer to an individual who proclaims hidden or future realities revealed by divine power; differs because prophet emphasizes the public proclamation and moral mandate of a messenger speaking on behalf of God, whereas seer focuses primarily on the receptive perceptual act of direct spiritual sight and vision.
  • oracle: Similar because both are consulted by seekers wishing to know the will of the gods or the outcome of future events; differs because an oracle traditionally refers to the sacred physical sanctuary or the deity's enigmatic utterance itself, often spoken in riddling, ambiguous verse through an ecstatic priestess, whereas a seer is the person possessing continuous inner visionary faculties.
  • soothsayer: Similar because both claim to predict upcoming events; differs because soothsayer carries a more secular, commercial, or folkloric connotation of fortune-telling (literally a "truth-sayer"), frequently associated with street divination or dramatic superstition, lacking the elevated spiritual and theological gravitas of a seer.
  • diviner: Similar because both uncover hidden knowledge; differs because a diviner relies upon external technical rituals, mechanical instruments, and symbolic arts (such as casting lots, reading entrails, or astrology), whereas a seer perceives realities directly through internal, intuitive psychic illumination.
  • clairvoyant: Similar because both claim paranormal perception beyond physical sight; differs because clairvoyant is a modern 19th-century parapsychological term emphasizing extrasensory perceptual faculties (such as reading sealed letters or viewing distant locations), whereas seer carries ancient sacred, scriptural, and existential resonance.
  • augur: Similar because both interpret the future for leaders and communities; differs because an augur was specifically a Roman civic and religious official tasked with observing institutional ritual omens (such as the flight patterns of sacred birds), whereas a seer receives unprompted mystical visions.
  • haruspex: Similar because both were consulted in antiquity regarding impending destiny; differs because a haruspex was an Etruscan and Roman specialist who practiced extispicy (examining the entrails and livers of sacrificed animals), representing a tactile sacrificial art rather than intuitive spiritual vision.
  • sibyl: Similar because both deliver inspired prophecies; differs because a sibyl was specifically an ancient Greco-Roman prophetic woman who uttered ecstatic divine frenzies under the direct possession of Apollo, often preserved in fixed books of oracles, whereas seer is gender-neutral and encompasses broad historical and philosophical paradigms.
  • visionary: Similar because both discern ideal or future states that ordinary people cannot grasp; differs because visionary is commonly applied to utopian thinkers, imaginative artists, or strategic leaders with radical forward-looking ideas, regardless of whether any literal supernatural or extrasensory perception is claimed.
  • mystic: Similar because both experience direct, immediate communion with spiritual realities; differs because a mystic seeks personal experiential union (*unio mystica*) with the divine essence, whereas a seer focuses specifically on the perceptual disclosure of visions, prophecies, and cosmic truths.
  • sage: Similar because both are venerated for extraordinary wisdom; differs because a sage derives profound understanding from lifelong experience, moral philosophy, and mature contemplation, whereas a seer is endowed with spontaneous, revelatory sight.
  • prognosticator: Similar because both foretell future trends and outcomes; differs because prognosticator is a formal, intellectual, or medical-style term implying calculated prediction based on current diagnostic symptoms and observed data patterns.
  • scryer: Similar because both perceive hidden or distant scenes; differs because a scryer specifically employs a reflective physical medium—such as a crystal ball, mirror, or bowl of dark water—to induce visual hallucinations or psychic visions.
