- Old English — bodian means to announce or proclaim, a messenger’s public word, neutral and official.
- Middle English — forboden and foreboden grow beside it, to announce beforehand, often of dreams and tokens foretelling fate.
- 16th–17th c. — forebode settles as to presage evil, at home in Shakespeare, ballads, and sermons, the prophet’s gloomy verb.
- 18th–19th c. — the inward turn deepens, my heart forebodes becomes the stock phrase of novels and letters, dread felt rather than declared.
- Tolkien’s moment — forbodes keeps the archaic for- spelling for Elvish gravity, sorrowful foresight rather than panic.
- Today — the noun foreboding owns everyday speech and psychology, while the verb survives in literature as a deliberate archaism for fateful dread.
