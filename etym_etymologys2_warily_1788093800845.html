PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*wer-
“to watch, guard, perceive”
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*waraz
“aware, cautious”
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
wær
“aware, wary, cautious”
↓
Middle English (12th–15th c.)
war(e)
“aware, on one's guard”
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1550s)
wary
“cautious, watchful against danger”
↓
English (1560s)
WARILY
“in a cautious, watchful manner”

Note: Old English already had the adverb wærlīce “warily”, but the modern word is a fresh 16th-century formation (wary + -ly), not an unbroken survival of the Old English adverb. The root *wer- also underlies Latin verērī “to fear, revere” and English aware, beware, ward, and guard (via French from Frankish).
