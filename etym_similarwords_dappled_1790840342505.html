• Mottled: Similar because both describe surfaces marked with patches of different colors; differs because mottled often implies irregular, blotchy, marbled, or uneven patches (sometimes associated with bruised skin or stone), whereas dappled carries a distinctly aesthetic, gentle, rounded, and natural beauty (like horse coats or sunlit leaves).
  • Spotted: Similar because both involve dot-like markings; differs because spotted is a generic, geometric descriptor of distinct dots or stains, whereas dappled implies softer, blended, overlapping, or shade-varying round patches.
  • Speckled: Similar because both refer to multi-colored markings; differs because speckled consists of tiny, fine, peppered flecks (like a robin's egg or trout), whereas dappled consists of larger, broader patches of tone and color.
  • Flecke / Flecked: Similar because both denote scattered surface marks; differs because flecked refers to small, distinct streaks or specks of color sprinkled across a background, lacking the circular, light-and-shadow softness of dappled.
  • Pied: Similar because both describe two- or multi-toned coloration in nature; differs because pied typically denotes sharply contrasting, bold blocks of black and white (like a magpie) rather than subtle, blended gradations.
  • Variegated: Similar because both describe varied coloration; differs because variegated is a technical botanical and horticultural term referring to multi-colored leaves or flowers with streaks, bands, and distinct borders.
