• unusual: Similar because both mean "out of the ordinary"; differs because "unusual" is a neutral statistical term about frequency, while "unwonted" implies a personal, habitual baseline that is being broken.
  • unaccustomed: Similar because both carry "not accustomed"; differs because "unaccustomed" is now almost always predicative and about the subject's inexperience ("unaccustomed to the cold"), whereas "unwonted" is usually attributive and describes the surprising thing itself.
  • uncharacteristic: Similar because both mean "not typical of a person"; differs because "uncharacteristic" is a cool, analytical observation ("uncharacteristic rudeness"), while "unwonted" adds an archaic, literary charge of sudden psychological displacement.
  • exceptional: Similar because both flag departure from the usual; differs because "exceptional" ranges from merely unusual to outstandingly good, while "unwonted" never implies praise and often carries unease.
  • abnormal: Similar because both denote deviation from a norm; differs because "abnormal" is clinical and judgmental, implying defect or pathology, whereas "unwonted" is descriptive and non-pathological.
  • atypical: Similar because both mean "not typical"; differs because "atypical" is formal-technical register (medicine, statistics), while "unwonted" is literary and historically weighted.
  • singular: Similar because both mean "remarkably out of the ordinary"; differs because "singular" stresses uniqueness and oddity in one object, while "unwonted" stresses deviation from an established personal or local habit.
  • novel: Similar because both mean "not previously seen"; differs because "novel" is neutral and forward-looking (new to the world), while "unwonted" is backward-looking (departing from what was customary).
  • strange: Similar because both mean "not familiar or usual"; differs because "strange" is broad, conversational and can mean merely unfamiliar to the speaker, while "unwonted" specifically measures deviation from a habitual pattern.
  • rare: Similar because both may mean "seldom occurring"; differs because "rare" is purely about frequency of occurrence, with no sense of personal habit or psychological rupture.
  • outlandish: Similar because both convey foreignness to the familiar; differs because "outlandish" adds bizarre, ridiculous, or exotic excess, which "unwonted" entirely lacks.
