- 1830s — Coined as part of the scientific instrument name stroboscope by Austrian inventor Simon Stampfer, derived from Greek strobos ("whirling, spinning") to describe rotating slotted discs creating optical motion illusions.
- 1930s–1940s — Harold "Doc" Edgerton at MIT pioneers the electronic xenon flashtube, creating high-speed "stroboscopic photography" to capture microsecond scientific phenomena like bullets and splashing drops.
- 1960s — Colloquially clipped in English from stroboscope to strobe; adopted into aviation engineering for aircraft anti-collision wingtip beacons.
- 1970s — Explodes into global youth and entertainment culture as psychedelic discotheque and rock concert lighting, transforming live dance floors into hypnotic freeze-frame tableaus.
- 1980s–present — Standardized in professional digital photography (studio strobe heads, flash sync), computing hardware (memory strobe pulses), and industrial machine vision inspection systems.
