• Girth: Similar because both refer to a strap passing under an equine's belly; differs because 'girth' is the standard, universal term in English riding and saddle-making, whereas 'belly-band' is frequently used in draft harnesses, driving gear, and blanket attachments.
  • Cinch: Similar because both secure equine gear around the barrel; differs because 'cinch' specifically refers to Western riding saddles (made of cord, mohair, or leather with a ring cinch buckle), whereas 'belly-band' belongs to English driving, draft harness, or blanket terminology.
  • Surcingle: Similar because both are straps encircling a horse's body; differs because a 'surcingle' passes completely around the entire circumference of the horse (over the back or saddle) to keep a blanket or pack in place, whereas a 'belly-band' connects underneath to harness shafts or saddle billets.
  • Saddle-girth: Similar because both pass under the stomach; differs because 'saddle-girth' is exclusively tied to riding saddles, while 'belly-band' encompasses carriage harnesses, working draft gear, and infant abdominal binders.
  • Waistband: Similar because both are bands around a midsection; differs because 'waistband' belongs exclusively to human clothing (trousers, skirts), while 'belly-band' applies to animal harnesses, infant medical binders, or publishing book wrappers.
  • Binder: Similar because both bind or wrap around a circumference; differs because 'binder' is a broad mechanical, office, or agricultural term without the specific equestrian anatomical focus.
