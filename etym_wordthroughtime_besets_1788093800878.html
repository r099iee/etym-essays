- Surrounding physically (placing things about, garrisoning, decorating) — concrete Old English inheritance, a craftsman's and soldier's word.
- Assailing in battle (ME besetten) — the language of chronicles and sieges: invest the castle, hem in the foe, press the garrison — martial and grim.
- KJV 1611 “Thou hast beset me behind and before” — devotionally transfigured: the ring becomes divine presence, hence solemn and holy in tone.
- “The sin which doth so easily beset us” (Hebrews 12:1) — sin as encircling weakness; the phrase “besetting sin” carries the word into moral essay writing.
- Figurative 18th–19th c. drift — “beset with difficulties/dangers” — now chiefly abstract; the physical sense fades and the passive voice takes over.
- Polar idiom “beset in ice” — the ring becomes frozen ocean; helpless immobility, not attack, is now the image.
- Modern news register — “beset by scandal/crises” — a weighty, formal, slightly archaic verb kept alive by journalism, liturgy and fantasy prose.
