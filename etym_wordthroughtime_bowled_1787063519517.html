- [13th–14th c.] - [Middle English Gaming Origin] Adopted from Anglo-Norman boule into Middle English as bowle, specifically denoting the rolling of spherical balls in lawn bowls and skittle games across greens.
- [15th–16th c.] - [Physical Collision & Metaphorical Toppling] Developed the sense of knocking someone down or overturning them like bowling pins through sudden momentum or running collision (bowled over).
- [17th–18th c.] - [Sporting Specialization & Smooth Motion] Formalized in early cricket terminology for delivering the ball toward the wicket, while also adopting the colloquial phrase bowling along for swift, smooth carriage travel.
- [19th–21st c.] - [Psychological Astonishment & Modern Idioms] Popularized throughout Victorian and modern literature as the standard idiomatic expression for being overwhelmed by surprise, awe, or gratitude (bowled over).
