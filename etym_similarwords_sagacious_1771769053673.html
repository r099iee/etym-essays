• Shrewd: Similar because both name keen practical judgment and the ability to see through deception; differs because shrewd is commercial and worldly — canny about bargains, people, and self-interest, with a hint of low cunning — whereas sagacious is more elevated, disinterested, and formal, a dignity that shrewdness lacks.
  • Astute: Similar because both denote quick, accurate discernment; differs because astute is more tactical — swift to spot advantage or deception in a situation — while sagacious implies deeper, more reflective insight and a far-seeing cast rather than speed of maneuver.
  • Acute: Similar because both describe sharpness of perception; differs because acute can be purely sensory or momentary (acute hearing, acute pain) and lacks the far-seeing, reflective breadth that sagacious carries; acuteness is a point, sagacity is a survey.
  • Perspicacious: Similar because both are formal, bookish words for penetrating mental vision; differs because perspicacious emphasizes clarity of insight — seeing through to the heart of a matter — while sagacious emphasizes sound judgment and practical good sense; perspicacious is the more intellectual, sagacious the more prudential.
  • Wise: Similar because both imply good judgment and discernment; differs because wise names experienced, moral, settled understanding — a sage may be slow where a sagacious man is quick — wisdom is contemplative and principled, whereas sagacity is pointed, applied, and practical.
  • Judicious: Similar because both describe sound, well-considered judgment; differs because judicious stresses fairness, balance, and the weighing of evidence in decisions, while sagacious stresses keenness of perception and instinctive penetration rather than judicial deliberation.
  • Insightful: Similar because both describe deep understanding; differs because insightful emphasizes the depth and novelty of understanding gained, a modern, everyday word, while sagacious emphasizes the sharpness and reliability of judgment, an elevated, slightly archaic one.
  • Discerning: Similar because both describe the ability to perceive distinctions; differs because discerning focuses on discrimination between fine differences, taste, and judgment of quality, whereas sagacious focuses on overall practical good sense and penetration in judgment.
  • Perceptive: Similar because both denote keen observation; differs because perceptive is the ordinary modern word for noticing what others miss, while sagacious adds the dimension of long-range judgment and carries a Latinate dignity that perceptive lacks.
  • Canning (canny): Similar because both denote practical cleverness in sizing up people and situations; differs because canny is Scots-derived, cautious, and thrifty, tinged with wariness about one's own interests, whereas sagacious is grander, more disinterested, and free of the commercial caution canny implies.
  • Intelligent: Similar because both involve mental power; differs because intelligent names raw cognitive ability with no commitment to wisdom or application, while sagacious specifically names superior judgment applied to the practical world — intelligence directed and seasoned.
  • Omniscient-like farsighted: Similar because both imply seeing beyond the immediate moment; differs because farsighted emphasizes planning for the future and anticipation of consequences, a narrower, more economic sense, while sagacious covers present discernment and past insight as well.
