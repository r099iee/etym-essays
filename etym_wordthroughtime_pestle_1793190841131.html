- Roman world — the pounder's name: Latin pistillum names the hand-pounder beside every mortarium, pounding grain and medicine.
- Middle Ages — kitchen and leechcraft: pestell works in English kitchens and monastic pharmacies, crushing spices worth more than silver.
- Early modern — science adopts it: chemists and apothecaries standardize pestle shapes in brass, glass, and porcelain for the laboratory bench.
- 19th c. — folklore goes airborne: illustrated Russian tales show the iron pestle as Baba Yaga's oar, and the tool becomes famous far beyond any kitchen.
- 20th c. — machines echo it: stamp mills and pharmacy grinders mechanize the pounding while cooks rediscover stone and wood.
- Today — craft revival: chefs, herbalists, and cocktail makers prize the pestle again, the slow hand way against the roar of the blade-grinder.
