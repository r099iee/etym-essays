• Blanket: Similar because both are bed coverings used for warmth; differs because a blanket is a single woven or knitted sheet without the quilt's sandwich of filling held in place by through-stitching, and it carries no ornamental-craft connotation.
  • Coverlet: Similar because both top a bed; differs because a coverlet is primarily a decorative top spread and may lack thick padding entirely, whereas a quilt is defined by its padded, stitched layers.
  • Counterpane: Similar because both are ornamental bed coverings; differs because counterpane names the quilted or woven top cover and is more archaic and literary, often without the thick padded filling.
  • Duvet: Similar because both provide warmth through fill; differs because a duvet is a filled bag (usually inside a removable cover) without the dense patterned through-stitching that historically defines a quilt.
  • Comforter: Similar because both are puffy padded bed coverings; differs because comforter stresses loose, lofty fill (often only lightly stitched) and is chiefly American, lacking the craft and status associations of the quilt.
  • Eiderdown: Similar because both are warm padded coverings; differs because eiderdown specifically names a filling of duck or goose down and refers to a duvet-like or quilted cover, narrower in reference than quilt.
  • Mattress / pallet: Similar because the Latin ancestor culcita meant both cushion and mattress; differs because a mattress is what one lies on, not what is drawn over the body, and it lacks the stitched-pattern meaning.
  • Quilted garment / wadded jacket: Similar because both use padded stitched layers of the same craft; differs because these are clothing rather than bed coverings, applying the quilting technique to wearables instead of bedding.
