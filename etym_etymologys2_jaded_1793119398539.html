Middle English (late 14th c.)
jade
‘worn-out horse, nag’
↓
Early Modern English (verb) (c. 1600)
to jade
‘to tire out, ride hard’
↓
Early Modern English (participle) (1630s)
jaded
‘worn out, exhausted’
↓
Modern English (from 1770s, current)
jaded
‘dulled by overindulgence’

Note: the deeper origin of jade the horse-word is disputed; older dictionaries suggest Old Norse jalda meaning mare, but the evidence is thin and most modern authorities leave the noun of unknown origin rather than guessing.
