Origin debated; no secure PIE root for the whole formation is established, so the chain is given from the earliest plausible attested form, with the uncertain steps marked. Latin cavea "a hollow, an enclosure, a cage" (attested 2nd–1st c. BCE) → Old French / regional Norman-Picard cage (13th–14th c., "cage"; with the expressive verbal suffix) → dialectal Old French / Picard cageoler "to chatter like a jay in a cage" (regional, attested 16th c.) → French cajoler "to coax, to wheedle, to flatter; to babble, to prattle like a bird" (16th c.; Rabelais) → English cajole "to coax by flattery" (17th c.; first recorded 1646) → English cajoling (present participle / verbal adjective, late 17th–18th c.) → Modern English CAJOLING (persuading by sustained coaxing or flattery).

Debated / nuanced note: the OED and the French dictionaries mark the origin of French cajoler as obscure. The chain above follows the widely taught "cage + bird-chatter" analysis (cageoler), but competing accounts propose (a) a blend with French enjôler "to entice, to wheedle" (itself a cage-word from Latin cavea via Old French), and (b) independent Norman or Picard formations meaning "to chatter like a jay." Latin cavea → French cage is secure, but the exact formation of cajoler is not; no letter labels are used, and the reconstructed intermediate stages are explicitly flagged as proposed rather than attested.
