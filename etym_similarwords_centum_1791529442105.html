• hundred: Similar because it is the English meaning of centum; differs because hundred is the ordinary numeral and does not name the linguistic branch.
  • satem: Similar because it is the complementary label, also taken from the numeral "hundred"; differs because it names the eastern branch with sibilant reflexes, the opposite of centum.
  • cent: Similar as a descendant of Latin centum meaning one hundredth of a currency unit; differs because it is a monetary unit, not a language label.
  • century: Similar as a derivative of centum; differs because it means a period of a hundred years or a Roman military unit, not the numeral itself.
  • centurion: Similar as a Roman derivative of centum; differs because it names an officer commanding a century.
  • percent: Similar as a Latin-derived expression meaning "per hundred"; differs because it is a proportion, not a numeral label.
  • centennial: Similar as a derivative meaning a hundredth anniversary; differs in being an adjective or noun of commemoration.
  • hecaton: Similar as the Greek cognate of "hundred", found in hecatomb; differs because it is Greek and survives only in learned compounds.
  • Italic: Similar as the branch of Indo-European to which Latin belongs; differs because it names a genealogical subgroup, not the centum sound type.
  • Tocharian: Similar as a centum language; differs because it is an extinct branch of Central Asia and is centum despite its eastern location.
  • Indo-European: Similar as the family being classified; differs because it is the whole family, not one side of the isogloss.
