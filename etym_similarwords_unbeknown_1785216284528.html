• unknown: Similar because both mean not known; differs because unknown is the neutral, general word for anything not known by anyone, with no sense of a particular person being kept in the dark, and it stands freely before a noun (an unknown land).
  • ignorant: Similar because both relate to lack of knowledge; differs because ignorant describes the subject's own inner state of lacking knowledge (he was ignorant of the facts), whereas unbeknown describes the fact that someone else is left unaware — the ignorance is observed from outside.
  • secret: Similar because both imply something hidden from others; differs because secret implies deliberate concealment by someone who knows, while unbeknown carries no implication of intent — the goblins simply never learned of the creatures, whether the creatures intended concealment or not.
  • covert: Similar because both mean hidden or not revealed; differs because covert names deliberate, planned concealment (covert operations) in a formal register, where unbeknown is accidental or neutral and emphatically archaic.
  • unaware: Similar because both involve lack of knowledge; differs because unaware is an adjective describing the person (he was unaware), while unbeknown is a predicative construction with the party in a to-phrase (unbeknown to him).
  • ignored: Similar because both can involve something not noticed; differs because ignored implies a choice or act of disregarding, where unbeknown implies no one even thought of it.
