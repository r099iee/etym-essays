• sensuality: Similar because both involve the stimulation and enjoyment of bodily pleasures; differs because sensuality encompasses all rich sensory experiences (including taste, touch, smell, sound, and visual luxury) without necessarily being focused on sexual desire or psychological erotic tension, whereas eroticism centers specifically on the aesthetic, psychological, and amorous dynamics of sexual desire.
  • carnality: Similar because both relate to physical passion; differs because carnality carries a raw, heavy, somatic connotation emphasizing base fleshly appetites often framed in moral or unrefined terms, whereas eroticism emphasizes intellectualized, aestheticized, and symbolic expressions of love and passion.
  • lasciviousness: Similar because both involve strong sexual arousal; differs because lasciviousness has an explicitly pejorative moral register denoting lewd, unrestrained, or indecent sexual forwardness, whereas eroticism denotes an elevated artistic, philosophical, or psychological quality.
  • prurience: Similar because both touch upon sexual curiosity; differs because prurience describes an unwholesome, morbid, or itching obsession with the sexually illicit or scandalous, whereas eroticism is a legitimate aesthetic and emotional domain.
  • voluptuousness: Similar because both evoke rich amorous pleasure; differs because voluptuousness emphasizes lush physical indulgence, fullness of form, and luxurious sensory immersion, whereas eroticism emphasizes psychological tension, romantic desire, and symbolic meaning.
  • libidinousness: Similar because both stem from sexual drive; differs because libidinousness refers to excessive, unbridled lust or high sex drive at a biological level, whereas eroticism refers to the cultural, artistic, and mental styling of desire.
  • concupiscence: Similar because both denote intense sexual desire; differs because concupiscence is a formal, often theological term denoting fallen human lust or vehement yearning for worldly pleasures, whereas eroticism is an aesthetic and philosophical appreciation of amorous life.
