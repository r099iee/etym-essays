PEDESTALS is a compound word with two root components — the "foot" element (Italian piede) and the "standing place" element (Italian stallo). Complete linear chains for EACH root word in isolation, then the combined line. ROOT WORD ONE — the "foot" element (Italian piede) LINEAR CHAIN 1 (foot): Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *ped- “foot, step” (c. 4000 BCE), the root of walking and standing, attested through its many descendants: Greek poús, podós, Latin pēs, pedis, Sanskrit pád. Stage 2 — Latin pēs, pedis (m.) “foot” (Classical Latin, 1st c. BCE), the ordinary Latin word for the foot of a body, animal, or thing; it supplied the Romance “foot” words and English loanwords such as pedal, pedestrian, biped, quadruped, impediment, and expedition. Stage 3 — Italian piede “foot” (from Vulgar Latin *pedem, the accusative of pēs), the everyday Italian word that forms the first element of piedistallo. ROOT WORD TWO — the "standing place" element (Italian stallo) LINEAR CHAIN 2 (stand): Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *stel- “to place, put, stand; a standing place” (c. 4000 BCE), a root of placement and stability; its wider family includes Latin stella “star” (“the one set/placed”), Greek stéllō “to set in order, send,” and Lithuanian stóti “to stand.” Stage 2 — Proto-Germanic *stallaz “standing place, position, place” (a suffixed formation on the root), the ancestor of English stall, install, forestall, and of the Romance loan stallo. Stage 3 — Frankish *stal “position, standing, stable, place” (the Germanic word of the Franks, carried into Gallo-Romance during the settlement of Gaul). Stage 4 — Old French estal “place, station, stall, position” (recorded by the 10th–11th c.; later French étal “market stall, display table”). Stage 5 — Italian stallo “seat, stall, position, support” (a Germanic loan settled in Italian, used in the compound piedistallo). COMBINED COMPOUND (the word itself) Stage 6 — Italian piedistallo “a pedestal, literally ‘foot of a stall’” (pie(de) “foot” + di “of” + stallo “stall, support”; attested in Italian Renaissance art and architecture vocabulary, 16th c.). Stage 7 — French piédestal “pedestal” (borrowed from Italian; the French form dates from the 16th c.). Stage 8 — English pedestal “the base supporting a statue, column, or vase” (first recorded c. 1560s, during the English vogue for Italian architecture); plural PEDESTALS, the exact form of this card. NOTES - The literal anatomy of the compound is “foot of a stall”: the block at the feet (piede) of the statue, acting as its stall (stallo) or standing place. - The -estal element is Germanic, not Latin: stallo in Italian is a loan from a Germanic word (ultimately from PIE *stel-), not a descendant of Latin statio or stare. - The sense of reverent idealisation (“to put on a pedestal”) develops in English from the 17th century, transferred from the physical base to social and psychological elevation. - The intermediate Old French stage of the compound is debated in the details (some authorities treat the French form as directly re-formed on Italian), but the Italian origin of the word is secure.
