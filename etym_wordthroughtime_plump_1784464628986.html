Late Medieval Sound-Symbolic Adverb - Plump enters late medieval English as a sound-symbolic adverb from the Low Countries' expressive family (Middle Dutch plompen), meaning "with a sudden heavy fall" — weight plus a blunt thud.
Early Modern Verb Formation - From the adverb English builds the verb: to plump is to fall or set something down heavily and abruptly, as if it were a falling mass — unceremonious, without elegant serving.
Figurative "Plump For" - In the 19th century the decisive drop extends figuratively: to "plump for" a candidate is to come down heavily on one side, the physical thud becoming a decision.
Contemporary Sense - Today the verb plump (set down heavily) is old-fashioned but alive, and the adjective plump ("pleasingly fat") is a separate branch of the same expressive family, distinct from the drop-verb.
