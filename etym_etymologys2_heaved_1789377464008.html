Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*kh₂p-yé-ti / *kap-
"to grasp, seize, take hold with the hands"
Note: Fundamental PIE root underlying Latin capere ('to take, capture') and Proto-Germanic *habjaną.
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–1st c. CE)
*habjaną (Class VI Strong Verb: past *hōf, participle *hafanaz)
"to lift, raise up, take hold of, bear weight"
Note: Underwent Grimm's Law (*k → *h, *p → *b/f); directly ancestral to German heben and Dutch heffen.
↓
Old English (c. 700–1100 CE)
hebban (past: hōf, past participle: hafen)
"to lift, elevate, raise high, exalt"
↓
Middle English (c. 1150–1450)
heven (strong past: hōf / hef; emerging weak past: heved / hevede)
"to lift, raise, throw; to swell, rise up (of seas); to utter a sigh"
Note: Strong verb began transitioning into the weak dental preterite (-ed) conjugation; figurative respiratory senses emerge.
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1500–1700)
heaved (standard preterite & participle; nautical 'hove' preserved)
"lifted with effort, hauled, threw; exhaled deeply from the chest"
↓
Modern English (present)
