• Fern: Similar because it names the same class of non-flowering, spore-bearing plants; differs because fern is the broad family word covering everything from delicate maidenhairs to tropical tree ferns, whereas bracken names one big, coarse, invasive species.
  • Brake: Similar as the close etymological cousin (same root) meaning a fern or a thicket of fern or brushwood; differs because brake names the clump or thicket rather than the species and is now archaic or dialectal.
  • Heather: Similar as the other great moorland plant covering hillsides; differs because heather is low, woody, purple-flowered, and evergreen, while bracken is tall, herbaceous, tawny, and dies back each autumn.
  • Brush: Similar in denoting wild vegetation covering open ground; differs because brush is low and woody, whereas bracken is herbaceous and can grow head-high.
  • Scrub: Similar as rough, low wild vegetation; differs because scrub implies stunted woody growth and dry, poor ground, while bracken is a single tall fern species.
  • Underbrush / undergrowth: Similar as the tangled growth beneath a woodland; differs because these imply a mixed woody thicket, whereas bracken is a single dominant fern and can stand alone in the open.
  • Reed / rush: Similar as wild plants of open land; differs because reeds and rushes favour wet ground and standing water, while bracken prefers dry, well-drained slopes.
  • Covert / wildwood: Similar in suggesting cover that hides; differs as these denote the woodland or thicket itself rather than a particular fern species.
