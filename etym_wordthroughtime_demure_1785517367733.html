- [14th century settled phase] - Borrowed from Anglo-Norman, demure meant 'settled, mature, grave, staid', applied to sober, composed persons of steady years — a word of age and steadiness rather than youth and shyness.
- [15th-16th century modesty shift] - 'Sober and composed' shaded into 'quiet, reserved, and modest in demeanour', and the word came to be used chiefly of women's bearing.
- [17th century ambiguity] - Demure acquired a double edge: it could mean genuine modesty or affected, performing coyness — the knife-edge between the two that novelists have exploited ever since.
- [Contemporary ironic revival] - In the 2020s the word returned to cultural prominence through the viral idiom 'very demure, very mindful', recasting its composed propriety as a playful, self-aware performance.
