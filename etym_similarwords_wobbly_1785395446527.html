• Shaky: Similar because both denote general unsteadiness; differs because shaky suggests rapid fine trembling or vibration (cold hands, nervous fingers) rather than slow side-to-side swaying.
  • Unsteady: Similar because both describe loss of stability; differs because unsteady is the neutral, formal term for any instability, without wobbly's comic, homely warmth.
  • Rickety: Similar because both describe unsteadiness; differs because rickety names structural weakness and decay in objects and is harsher in tone, where wobbly is warmer and more about motion than decay.
  • Tottery: Similar because both describe an unsteady gait; differs because tottery is rarer, more old-fashioned, and narrower (applied to wavering walkers), while wobbly covers tables, wheels, and voices too.
  • Teetering: Similar because both describe precarious imbalance; differs because teetering implies a deliberate, agency-laden balancing on a brink, rather than the exhausted, passive sway of wobbly.
  • Unstable: Similar because both mean not fixed or firm; differs because unstable is technical and clinical, suggesting a real risk of collapse, while wobbly is domestic and comic, suggesting a wobble rather than a fall.
  • Precarious: Similar because both suggest something in danger of giving way; differs because precarious is grave and dramatic, while wobbly is light, homely, and faintly amusing.
  • Jiggly: Similar because both describe loose, shaking motion; differs because jiggly is quicker, smaller, and more playful, while wobbly is a slower, larger side-to-side rock.
