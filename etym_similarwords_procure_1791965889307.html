• obtain: Similar because it is the plain formal synonym for getting possession of something; differs because obtain is neutral about method and effort, while procure implies deliberate seeking, arrangement or intermediaries.
  • acquire: Similar because both denote coming to possess something; differs because acquire is typically gradual or transactional — acquiring a taste, a company, a skill — and carries no suggestion of contrivance.
  • secure: Similar because both mean succeeding in getting something that is not automatically available; differs because secure emphasizes success against competition or risk, while procure emphasizes the process of arranging and supplying.
  • get: Similar because it is the ordinary verb for obtaining; differs because get is informal and unlimited in scope, with none of the formality, care or bureaucratic colour of procure.
  • gain: Similar because both can mean coming to have something desirable; differs because gain stresses an increase or advantage — gaining time, weight, experience — rather than the act of acquisition itself.
  • attain: Similar because it implies effortful achievement; differs because attain takes goals, standards and statuses, not physical goods or services, and it says nothing about supply.
  • purchase: Similar because buying is one common way of procuring; differs because purchase names the commercial transaction specifically and cannot cover gifts, favours or the sexual sense.
  • requisition: Similar because it is an official way of procuring supplies; differs because requisition is a formal demand or seizure by authority, not the general act of obtaining.
  • pander: Similar because it covers the sexual sense of supplying a person for another’s gratification; differs because pander names the moral offence of gratifying base desires, while procure is the neutral-sounding legal verb for recruiting someone into prostitution.
