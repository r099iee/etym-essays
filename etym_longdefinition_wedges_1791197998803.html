Wedges is the plural of wedge: V-shaped or triangular blocks, thick at one end and tapering to a thin edge at the other, driven, pressed, or placed into a gap to split, lift, tighten, secure, or shape something.

In the source sense, stone wedges are sharpened flakes of flint or other hard rock hammered into wood grain to split logs, into bone to open marrow, or under boulders to lift them. Modern steel felling and splitting wedges do the same work with sledgehammers. Door wedges jam under doors, shim wedges level furniture and window frames, and wooden feather-and-wedge sets split quarried stone with astonishing control.

  • Tool / simple-machine sense: One of the six simple machines: a portable inclined plane. Splitting wedge, felling wedge, shim, chock, quoin, spike wedge. Mechanical advantage comes from converting forward drive into sideways splitting force.
  • Shape-based food / object senses: A wedge of cheese, cake, lemon, or watermelon - a triangular cut. A wedge of land or territory - a tapering parcel. The bridge is visual: same V profile.
  • Footwear / fashion sense: Wedge heels or wedges - shoes with a solid tapering sole that is thick at heel and thin at toe, as opposed to stilettos. Wedge haircut - hair cut to a V silhouette.
  • Sport senses: In golf, wedges are high-lofted clubs (pitching wedge, sand wedge, lob wedge, gap wedge) for short, high shots that wedge under the ball. In American football and rugby, the flying wedge is a V formation to drive through defence (now largely banned as dangerous).
  • Figurative / political senses: To drive a wedge between people means to divide them. The thin end / thin edge of the wedge means a small initial intrusion that will widen inevitably. A wedge issue is a divisive political topic used to split an opponent's coalition. Here the splitting force becomes social.

Across domains the operative idea is insertion that separates or secures: the wedge goes into a narrow opening and, by being thicker than the opening, forces change. This makes it conceptually kin to chisels (which cut) and levers (which pry), but distinct: chisels pare, levers pivot, wedges insinuate.

Emotionally wedges feel clever, minimal, and slightly aggressive. A wedge is the small thing that moves the big thing - David's tool. In plural wedges suggests a carpenter's pouch of shims, a golfer's set of lofts, or a political strategist's divisive tactics, ranging from handy to sinister.

Finally, safe use of wedges illustrates their quiet physics: drive along the grain, never across it; oil steel wedges to prevent mushrooming; wear eye protection because a glancing sledge blow can spall hardened steel; and for felling, set two plastic wedges alternately to lift without binding the saw. Arborists teach that a two-dollar wedge correctly placed steers tons of falling timber more surely than brute pushing. That disproportion - grams of taper directing tonnes of trunk - is why the wedge remains first among simple machines in the woods, even in an age of hydraulics.
