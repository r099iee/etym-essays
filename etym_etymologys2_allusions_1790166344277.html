PIE (reconstructed, prehistory)
*leid- / *loid-
"to play,"
"to let go frequently"

Latin (6th c. BCE – Classical)
ludere
"to play, sport,"
"to jest, mimic"
↓
Latin (Classical, 1st c. BCE)
ludus
"game, play,"
"school, pastime"
↓
Latin (Classical)
alludere
"to play with, jest,"
"to refer to mockingly"
↓
Late Latin (3rd–5th c. CE)
allusio, allusionem
"a playing with,"
"a reference to"
↓
Middle French (16th c.)
allusion
"reference, hint"
↓
English (1540s–1610s)
allusions
"indirect references,"
"hints to poets, texts"

Note: PIE *leid- is de Vaan's reconstruction; some handbooks cite *loid- variant. The Latin development alludere → allusio → French allusion → English is secure and attested in OED, Etymonline and Merriam-Webster.
