• most obscure: Similar because it is the identical superlative formed analytically; differs because it is the plain, modern form, lacking the archaic dignity of obscurest.
  • darkest: Similar because both intensify darkness and hiddenness; differs because darkness is purely physical, while obscurity also covers meaning and fame.
  • dimmest: Similar because both describe low visibility; differs because dimness concerns faint light, not concealment or difficulty of understanding.
  • murkiest: Similar because both evoke gloom and indistinctness; differs because murkiness implies dirty, foggy darkness, with no intellectual or social senses.
  • vaguest: Similar because both mean “least clearly defined”; differs because vagueness is a quality of statements and impressions, while obscurity extends to persons and places.
  • most abstruse: Similar because both mean “hardest to understand”; differs because abstruseness is purely intellectual and often technical, lacking the darkness and anonymity senses.
  • most recondite: Similar because both describe the little known and hard to grasp; differs because recondite implies esoteric learning, while obscure is broader.
  • most hidden: Similar because both describe concealment from sight or knowledge; differs because hidden is a plain verb-derived adjective with no register of dignity.
  • most unremarkable: Similar because both can describe the unnoticed; differs because unremarkable is merely uninteresting, while obscurity implies being covered over or forgotten.
  • uncelebrated: Similar because both can describe lack of fame; differs because uncelebrated is narrowly about praise, with no darkness or difficulty senses.
