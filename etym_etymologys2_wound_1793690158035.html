*wendh-

Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE - 200 CE)
*windaną
"to wind, turn"
↓
Old English (c. 450-1150 CE)
windan
"to turn, twist, coil, plait, weave; to go in a circuitous path"
[Strong verb, class III; past tense wand, past participle wunden]
↓
Middle English (c. 1150-1500 CE)
winden
"to turn, twist, coil; to go in a circuitous path"
[Past tense wounde, wound; past participle wounden]
↓
Modern English (c. 1500-present)
wind
"to turn, twist, coil"
[Past tense and past participle: wound]
↓
wound
"past tense/participle of 'wind' (to twist, turn)"

Proto-Indo-European (c. 4500-2500 BCE)
*wenh₁-
"to strike, hit, wound"
[Also *wen- "to strike, hit"]
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE - 200 CE)
*wundō
"wound, injury"
[From *wun- "to strike, hit"; also verb *wundōną]
↓
Old English (c. 450-1150 CE)
wund
"a wound, sore, injury"
[Also as adjective "wounded"; verb wundian "to wound"]
↓
Middle English (c. 1150-1500 CE)
wounde
"an injury, cut, lesion"
[Verb "wounden" also developed from this noun]
↓
Modern English (c. 1500-present)
wound
"an injury; to inflict an injury"
↓
wound
"an injury; past tense/participle of 'to wound' (to injure)"
