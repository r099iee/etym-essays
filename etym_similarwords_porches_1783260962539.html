• Portico: Similar because both are covered entrances supported by columns; differs because "portico" is the learned classical word for a monumental, columned walkway or entrance, while "porch" is the everyday word, often for a humbler structure.
  • Veranda: Similar because both are covered, open-sided structures attached to a house; differs because a veranda is usually a long open gallery running along the side of a house (often wrapping around), while a porch is a roofed entrance at the door.
  • Stoop: Similar because both are outdoor entrances with cover; differs because "stoop" (from Dutch stoep) is a small raised platform or steps before a door, typically without columns, common in urban American row houses.
  • Lanai: Similar because both are sheltered outdoor spaces attached to a house; differs because "lanai" is the Hawaiian term for a roofed veranda or patio open on all sides, more a living space than an entrance.
  • Piazza: Similar because both name a covered entryway; differs because "piazza" in New England usage means a wide porch or veranda, while in Italian it means a public square — the word carries both senses.
  • Gallery: Similar because both are covered, often columned spaces attached to a building; differs because a gallery is a long open walkway or balcony (the southern "gallery" being a veranda-like porch), not specifically an entrance.
  • Loggia: Similar because both are covered colonnaded spaces; differs because a loggia is an open-sided gallery on an upper or ground floor, part of Italianate architecture, not necessarily at the entrance.
  • Colonnade: Similar because both involve a row of columns supporting a roof; differs because a colonnade is a long covered walkway, usually free-standing and public, not attached at a doorway.
  • Narthex: Similar because both are covered entrance spaces of a church; differs because a narthex is an interior vestibule or lobby at the west end of an early Christian church, while a porch is exterior and roofed.
  • Vestibule: Similar because both are entrance spaces; differs because a vestibule is an interior lobby or hallway inside the building, whereas a porch is an exterior covered structure.
  • Entrance / Doorway: Similar because both mark the way into a building; differs because these name the opening itself, without the covered shelter that defines a porch.
  • Threshold: Similar because both mark the boundary between outside and inside; differs because a threshold is the literal sill at the door, a line rather than a structure.
  • Patio: Similar because both are outdoor spaces attached to a house; differs because a patio is an open, uncovered paved area (Spanish), while a porch has a roof.
  • Deck: Similar because both are outdoor platforms attached to a house; differs because a deck is an open wooden platform, usually unroofed and for leisure, not an entrance.
  • Arcade: Similar because both are covered walks with a series of arches; differs because an arcade is a long public walkway of arches, usually connecting shops or buildings, not a single house entrance.
  • Walkway: Similar because both are transitional outdoor structures; differs because a walkway is a path for passage, with no roof, whereas a porch shelters a threshold.
