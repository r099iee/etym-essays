• Amusement: Similar because both denote pleasant, non-work activity; differs because amusement stresses entertainment and light pleasure and is now often trivial, while recreation keeps the idea of restoring or renewing strength.
  • Diversion: Similar because both turn the mind away from labour; differs because diversion emphasises deflecting attention from work, whereas recreation emphasises actively building one's strength up again.
  • Pastime: Similar because both name a pleasant way of occupying time; differs because a pastime is simply a way of passing time, with no claim to refreshment or renewal.
  • Leisure: Similar because both belong to free time; differs because leisure names free time itself — a condition, not an activity — while recreation is the activity pursued within that time.
  • Relaxation: Similar because both reduce strain; differs because relaxation means easing of tension, often through inactivity, while recreation implies active renewal through a chosen pursuit.
  • Refreshment: Similar because both restore vigour; differs because refreshment is physical and immediate (food, drink, rest), while recreation is structured pleasant activity with a restorative effect.
  • Play: Similar because both are spontaneous, enjoyable activity; differs because play is free, unstructured, and ends in itself, while recreation may be organised, scheduled, and restorative in purpose.
  • Sport: Similar because both are leisure activities; differs because sport implies competitive physical exercise governed by rules, while recreation covers any restorative pastime, active or sedentary.
  • Hobby: Similar because both are chosen leisure pursuits; differs because a hobby is a steady, specialised interest cultivated over time, whereas recreation is any refreshing activity, occasional or regular.
  • Entertainment: Similar because both please and occupy; differs because entertainment is largely passive enjoyment provided to an audience, while recreation implies the subject's own participation and renewal.
  • Repose: Similar because both are taken after labour; differs because repose is rest and quiet stillness, the opposite of the activity that recreation implies.
  • Rest: Similar because both restore the powers after exertion; differs because rest is ceasing to act, while recreation is a different kind of acting that rebuilds the powers.
