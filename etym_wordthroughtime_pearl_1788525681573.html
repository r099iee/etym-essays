- [Latin / Late Latin (c. 1st–6th c. CE)] - perna / pernula (literally "little ham-shaped mussel") shifted colloquially to *perla for the precious gem - [connotation: Mediterranean maritime trade and classical Roman luxury]
- [Old French & Middle English (c. 1200–1450)] - perle entered English as the supreme symbol of purity, virginity, and celestial beauty (celebrated in the 14th-century Middle English poem Pearl) - [connotation: spiritual sanctity, heavenly grace, and peerless nobility]
- [Early Modern English (c. 1500–1700)] - pearl expanded into idiomatic metaphors (pearl of great price, cast pearls before swine) and typography (pearl type) - [connotation: priceless wisdom, rare beauty, and miniaturized craftsmanship]
- [18th–19th c.] - pearl became a staple colour and texture term in Romantic and Victorian Aesthetic literature (Wilde's "pink and pearl"), as well as culinary naming (pearl barley) - [connotation: delicate pastel iridescence, aristocratic elegance, and natural beauty]
- [20th–21st c.] - development of cultured pearls by Kokichi Mikimoto alongside modern industrial applications (pearl paints, pearl finishes) - [connotation: timeless classic elegance, cultured luxury, and pearlescent visual sheen]
