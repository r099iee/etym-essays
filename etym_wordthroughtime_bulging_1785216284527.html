- [13th century: the noun] - Middle English bulge/bouge (via Old French boulge/bouge from Latin bulga) means "wallet, purse; hump" - a leather pouch that swells when full; the word is bound up with rounded containers and protuberance.
- [16th century: the verb is born] - The noun's sense "hump, rounded protuberance" generates the verb to bulge - at first "to bend, to curve outward," then "to swell out, to protrude" - as a full wallet gives its shape to any rounded distension.
- [17th-19th century: the adjective hardens] - The present participle bulging fixes as an adjective for things that stand out roundly - bulging pockets, bulging muscles, bulging eyes; the core sense is outward pressure of a flexible container's contents against its walls, vivid, physical, and slightly informal.
- [Contemporary sense] - Bulging is the standard word for a swelling that should not be there - an unsightly distension of a normally flat surface - carrying a deliberately grotesque, repellent edge when applied to the body (bulging blind eyes) while keeping its homely idiom of overfull containers (a bulging suitcase).
