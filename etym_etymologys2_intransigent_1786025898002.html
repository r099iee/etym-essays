Proto-Indo-European (c. 3500 BCE)
*h₂eǵ- (*ag-)
"to drive, draw out, move, conduct"
↓
Latin (c. 200 BCE)
agere
"to drive, do, act, manage"
↓
Latin (c. 100 BCE)
transigere
"to drive through, settle a dispute, come to an agreement"
(Prefix trans- "across, through" + agere)
↓
Late / Medieval Latin (c. 1200 CE)
transigens (stem transigent-)
"coming to terms, compromising"
↓
Spanish (1873 CE)
intransigente / los intransigentes
"uncompromising; radical Spanish federal republicans"
(in- "not" + transigente "compromising")
↓
French (c. 1876 CE)
intransigeant
"uncompromising in politics or art"
↓
Modern English (1879 CE)
INTRANSIGENT
"refusing to compromise or agree"
