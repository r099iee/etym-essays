The chain for canopy begins in Greek, not in a simple PIE noun; the ultimate origins of its two ingredients differ, so each is labelled.

Chain for the "eye" element: PIE *h₃ekʷ- "to see, the eye" (reconstructed; cf. Latin oculus, Greek ōps) → Greek ὤψ (ōps) "face, eye, appearance" (attested from the Homeric period) → used as the second element of κώνωψ.

Chain for the "cone" element: Greek κῶνος (kōnos) "cone, pinecone, conical peak" (attested 5th c. BCE) — origin uncertain, possibly a Pre-Greek substrate word (cf. Beekes); no secure PIE reconstruction.

Combined chain — the whole word: Greek κῶνος "cone" + ὤψ "face, eye" → κώνωψ (kṓnōps) "a mosquito," literally "cone-face" (attested 5th c. BCE, e.g. Aristophanes, for the insect's pointed head) → κωνωπεῖον (kōnōpeion) "a bed or couch furnished with mosquito-curtains; a mosquito net" (Hellenistic Greek, 3rd–1st c. BCE) → Latin cōnōpēum / cōnōpeum / canōpēum "a mosquito net; a curtained bed or couch" (1st c. BCE–1st c. AD) → Old French conopé / canopé / conope "a mosquito net; a cloth covering" (12th c.) → Middle English canope / canape / canopy "a bed curtain; an overhead covering" (14th c.; first recorded c. 1387) → Modern English CANOPY (an ornamental cloth covering over a bed, throne, or seat; any roof-like overhead cover).

Debated / nuanced note: the Greek noun κῶνος "cone" has no secure Indo-European etymology (possibly Pre-Greek), so no complete PIE ancestry can be traced for the whole compound; the element ὤψ is certainly from PIE *h₃ekʷ- "eye." The semantic path runs "net against insects" → "ornamental protective hangings" → "any overhead shelter." English canopy is a Greco-Latin cultural loan via French, not an Indo-Aryan or Germanic cognate; no Hindi or Odia branch exists. No letter labels are used; every stage is a real attested or explicitly reconstructed form.
