• chapters: Similar because both denote major textual and thematic divisions within a larger book; differs because chapters is a generic literary term implying authorial organization, whereas suras refers exclusively to divine revelations of the Qur'an viewed as holy, non-chronologically arranged liturgical units.
  • cantos: Similar because both represent substantial poetic and rhythmic divisions within a major sacred or epic work; differs because cantos applies specifically to long narrative poems (such as Dante's Divine Comedy or Byron's works), whereas suras encompass legal codes, moral exhortations, and liturgical prayers in addition to rhythmic poetry.
  • sections: Similar because both indicate discrete textual components into which a composition is partitioned; differs because sections is a dry, utilitarian descriptor devoid of the sacred authority, ritual sanctity, and canonical fixedness inherent to suras.
  • pericopes: Similar because both refer to selected textual extracts or readings intended for liturgical recitation in religious services; differs because a pericope is typically a short passage or lesson excerpted from the Bible for a specific Sunday or feast day, whereas a sura is a complete, immutable structural division of the canon.
  • tractates: Similar because both represent formal thematic bodies of religious literature within a canon; differs because tractates (such as those of the Talmud) are legalistic, scholastic, and dialectical commentaries composed by rabbis, whereas suras are revered as the direct, verbatim word of God.
  • books: Similar because both describe major constituent parts of a religious canon (e.g., the Books of the Old Testament); differs because biblical books were authored by distinct prophets and scribes over centuries and vary in genre from history to poetry, whereas all suras constitute a unified revelation delivered through a single prophetic voice.
  • epistles: Similar because both form foundational canonical units of scripture; differs because epistles are explicit apostolic letters addressed to specific early churches or individuals (e.g., the Pauline epistles), whereas suras are universal divine discourses addressed variously to humanity, believers, or the Prophet himself.
  • discourses: Similar because both represent formal rhetorical treatments of spiritual and ethical subjects; differs because discourses suggests extended philosophical lectures or sermons delivered in a human voice, lacking the unique revealed status and fixed verse-by-verse codification of suras.
  • canticles: Similar because both feature lyrical, non-metrical hymn-like religious texts; differs because canticles are specifically songs or chants derived from biblical scripture used in Christian church liturgy, whereas suras constitute an entire comprehensive textual corpus containing law, doctrine, and narrative.
  • divisions: Similar because both describe parts into which a whole work is separated; differs because divisions is an abstract structural category, whereas suras possesses deep cultural, liturgical, and historical specificity.
