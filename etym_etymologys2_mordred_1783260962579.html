Derivation chain for MORDRED (single root word; a Brittonic/Celtic personal name with a debated possible Latin ancestor): 1. Proto-Indo-European *med- "to measure, give due measure" (reconstructed root; reflexes include Latin meditari "to ponder", modus "measure", mederi "to heal", Greek medesthai "to devise"), c. 4th-3rd millennium BCE. 2. Latin moderor, moderari "to restrain, govern, moderate" (denominative verb built on the *med- family with an -r- extension; attested 1st c. BCE, e.g., Cicero). 3. Latin moderatus "moderate, temperate, controlled" (past participle of moderor, used also as a cognomen; attested 1st c. BCE-CE). 4. Brittonic adoption: Latin Moderatus — early Welsh Medraut / Medrawt "Mordred" (personal name; attested in the Annales Cambriae, which record under the year 537 "the strife of Camlann, in which Arthur and Medraut fell"; the annals as compiled are 10th-century but preserve earlier material). The phonetic steps are debated: the -Moder- base shows syncope and diphthongization in Brittonic, and no wholly regular derivation has been demonstrated. An alternative, rival hypothesis derives Medraut natively from Brittonic *medr-, "ability, skill" (Welsh medru "to aim, be able"), on which view the name means "the able/skilful one" (see note below), c. 5th-6th c. CE. 5. Medieval Latin Modredus (Latinized form of the Welsh name; Geoffrey of Monmouth, Historia Regum Britanniae, c. 1136). 6. Anglo-Norman / Middle English Mordred, Mordrede (via French-language romances — Wace's Roman de Brut c. 1155, Layamon's Brut c. 1200 — and Malory's Le Morte Darthur, 15th c.), 12th-15th c. CE. 7. Modern English MORDRED (standard literary form fixed by Malory and the Arthurian canon), 15th c.-present. Note on the debate: the origin of the Welsh name Medraut is genuinely uncertain. The Latin-Moderatus hypothesis (favoured in older scholarship and popular reference works) has no fully regular phonetic path, and the leading Celticist Rachel Bromwich, editing the Trioedd Ynys Prydein, judged that no wholly satisfactory Celtic etymology of Medraut had been established; the native Brittonic *medr- hypothesis is likewise plausible but unproven. The chain from the attested Old Welsh form Medraut onward (Latinization Modredus, Middle English Mordred) is secure; the deepest stages are a stated uncertainty, not an attested record.
