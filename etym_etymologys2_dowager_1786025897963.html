DOWAGER from Old/Middle French douagiere "a widow with a dower", from douage "dower", from douer "to endow", from Latin dotare "to endow", from dos, dotis "dowry", from PIE *deh3- "to give".

PIE *deh3-
"to give" - the root of Latin dare and dos, Greek didomi, Sanskrit da-

▼

Latin dos, dotis
"dowry, marriage portion, a gift" - noun formed from *deh3- with -s (2nd c. BCE)

▼

Latin dotare
"to endow, to provide with a dowry" - denominative verb from dos (Classical Latin)

▼

Old French douer
"to endow, to provide a dower" - from Latin dotare (Old French re-shaping)

▼

Old French douage
"dower" - the widow's allotted portion of her late husband's estate (douer + suffix -age)

▼

Old French douagiere / Middle French douagiere
"a widow holding a dower; a widow of rank" (13th-15th c.)

▼

English dowager
"a widow with a title or estate derived from her late husband" (1520s, from French douagiere)

▼

DOWAGER
"a widow with a title or property derived from her late husband" (the modern English noun on this card)

Notes: (1) The same root *deh3- gives the sibling words dower, dowry, and endow, all borrowed into English at different times. (2) The exact Middle French spelling fluctuated (douagiere, douagere, douagier); English fixed on dowager. The masculine form douagier ("a widower endowed with a dower") existed in French but never entered general English use.
