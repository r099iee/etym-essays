- Late Middle / Early Modern English (monk + -ery) - Originally a fairly neutral collective noun for the state, dwelling, or way of life of monks, describing monastic communities as institutions.
- Reformation era (16th-17th century) - Acquires a sharply pejorative and satirical edge, used in Protestant polemic to reduce monastic life to a hollow, mechanical, money-driven "business" and to mock its spiritual pretensions.
- 18th-19th century (dissolution era) - Remains an anti-clerical buzzword in historical and political writing, evoking contempt for secluded, superstitious institutional life.
- Contemporary sense (archaic, historical, or jocular) - Largely obsolete in everyday speech, revived only for period color or as a wry, dismissive term for monkish seclusion, with no neutral or reverent register left.
