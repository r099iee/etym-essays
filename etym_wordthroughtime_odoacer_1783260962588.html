- 5th-century Italy - Odoacer (c. 433-493), a Scirian soldier allied with the Heruli, rose to command the imperial mercenaries and in 476 deposed the child-emperor Romulus Augustulus, sending the Western regalia to Constantinople.
- The end of the Western Empire - by ruling Italy for seventeen years as the first non-Roman king of Italy rather than taking the purple, he became the conventional figure marking the fall of the Western Roman Empire, a man of hard, datable historical fact.
- Theodoric's coup - his reign ended when the Ostrogothic king Theodoric invaded Italy in 489 and assassinated him in 493, so his name sits on the opposite side of the drama from Theodoric's.
- Contemporary sense - Odoacer names the turning point between antiquity and the Middle Ages, the barbarian who closed the book on the Western emperors, and in Macaulay's catalogue he stands for documented historical agency against mere legend.
