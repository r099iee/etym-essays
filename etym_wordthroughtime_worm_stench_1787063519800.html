- Worm (first element) - In Old English wyrm meant dragon, serpent and earthworm alike — creatures of dread and legend.
- Stench (second element) - In Old English stenc was neutral, meaning simply "an odour, good or bad".
- Worm narrows - From the Renaissance on, worm loses its dragon sense to serpent and snake, and comes to mean invertebrate, parasite, or a contemptible person.
- Stench pejorates - In Middle English stench narrows from "any smell" to "a foul smell", and keeps only the negative sense in modern usage.
- The two are joined - Tolkien compounds them in The Hobbit (1937) to give a single, blunt, archaic word for the foul odour of a dragon's lair.
- Modern perception - Worm-stench is felt today as a vividly literary, fantasy-tinged word, summoning dragons, hoards and caves, while its two parts live separate ordinary lives.
