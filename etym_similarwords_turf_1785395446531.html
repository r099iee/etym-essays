• sod: Similar because it is grass and roots bound into earth, the near-synonym of turf; differs because it more often names a cut block or slab of turf than the standing surface itself, and "sod" carries a coarser, dialectal or American register.
  • soil: Similar because it is the ground underfoot; differs because it is loose mineral and organic earth with no root-bound structure — turf is the living skin, soil the bare substance beneath it.
  • earth: Similar because it names the ground as substance; differs because it is the undifferentiated planet-and-soil word, lacking turf's precise sense of grass roots matted into a surface.
  • grassland: Similar because it names land covered with grass; differs because it is a neutral ecological category of any grass-dominated area, while turf specifically denotes the root-knitted surface layer itself.
  • pasture: Similar because it is grassy ground; differs because it names vegetation by its use for grazing rather than by its physical root-mat structure, and it always implies livestock.
  • lawn: Similar because it forms a smooth grassy expanse; differs because it is the cultivated, mown, artificially maintained surface of gardens and parks, not the wild, grazed turf of open country.
  • greensward: Similar because it is the poetic word for a smooth grassy expanse; differs because it is a literary, elevated term describing appearance, whereas turf is the plain, functional word for the root-bound ground itself.
  • heath: Similar because it is an open expanse of low ground vegetation; differs because it is dominated by low shrubs and heathers on poor acidic soil, not by the close grass of turf.
  • peat: Similar because peat is the dried, bog-derived form of the same root-bound material, dug and burned as turf; differs because peat names the compacted fuel or the bog deposit, not the living grassy surface.
  • sward: Similar because it is an alternative literary term for the grassy surface; differs because it is archaic and poetic, whereas turf carries the full modern range from lawns to horse-racing to territory.
