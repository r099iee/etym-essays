• Storm: Similar because both describe turbulent atmospheric motion; differs because a storm is large, sustained, and threatening, while a flurry is a brief, harmless miniature that exhausts itself at once.
  • Squall: Similar because both denote a sudden, short-lived gust of wind, often with precipitation; differs because a squall is more violent and typically brings rain or snow, whereas a flurry is lighter and may be only a scattering of particles.
  • Hurry: Similar because both imply speed and quickness; differs because hurry stresses purposeful haste toward a goal, while flurry implies disordered, chaotic agitation with no particular purpose.
  • Bustle: Similar because both suggest energetic, rapid activity; differs because bustle is busy and purposeful (people doing things), while a flurry is disordered, uncontrollable, and directionless.
  • Commotion: Similar because both name a disturbance or excitement; differs because commotion is a general noisy uproar that can last, while a flurry is short, light, and quickly over.
  • Frenzy: Similar because both involve wild, excited motion or emotion; differs because frenzy is violent, intense, and often prolonged, while a flurry is brief, light, and transient.
  • Spurt: Similar because both denote a sudden, short burst; differs because a spurt is a directed jet along one line (a spurt of blood, a spurt of effort), while a flurry disperses in many directions at once.
  • Stir: Similar because both mean a brief disturbance of calm; differs because stir is milder and often deliberate or figurative (to cause a stir), while a flurry is quicker, more swirling, and more physical.
  • Scramble: Similar because both imply rapid, disorderly movement; differs because scramble suggests urgent, competitive effort by people or animals, while a flurry is an impersonal swirl of matter or motion.
  • Shower: Similar because both can describe a brief fall of fine precipitation; differs because a shower is steadier and wetter, while a flurry is wind-driven, whirling, and dispersed.
