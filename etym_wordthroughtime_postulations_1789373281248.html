- 15th Century (c. 1420s) - Borrowed into Late Middle English from Old French and Latin; exclusively used in ecclesiastical canon law and legal supplications to denote a formal petition for an ecclesiastical office or exemption from canonical impediment.
- 16th to 17th Century - Broadens during the Renaissance and Scientific Revolution into formal logic, Euclidean geometry, and natural philosophy; characterizes foundational axioms, primary claims, and mathematical postulates required for systematic demonstration.
- 18th Century (The Enlightenment) - Elevated into moral philosophy and epistemology by Immanuel Kant, whose "postulates of pure practical reason" established postulations as necessary, rational foundations for human ethics and freedom.
- 19th Century - Flourishes across physical sciences, non-Euclidean geometries, and political theory; designates fundamental theoretical models, axiomatic physics, and party platforms.
- 20th to 21st Century - Deepens in cultural, literary, and aesthetic criticism; characterizes the overarching doctrines and manifesto credos of artistic movements (such as realist vs. symbolist postulations in Proustian scholarship) while retaining its exact canonical and mathematical roles.
