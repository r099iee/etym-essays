Note on the homonym: the modern word form "fray" is also a verb meaning "to wear thin by rubbing" (frayed rope, frayed nerves). That is a separate word from a different etymon, shown below for completeness (its deepest link is debated, so no PIE stage is asserted as certain):
Latin (classical)
fricāre
"to rub"
(PIE ancestry uncertain; often linked, without certainty, to *bhrei- "to rub, cut")
↓

"to rub, scrape, bruise"
↓
Middle English (c. 1400)
fraien
"to wear away by rubbing"
↓
English
fray
"to become ragged or worn at the edges"
