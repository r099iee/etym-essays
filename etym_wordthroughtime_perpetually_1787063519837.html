- Latin perpetuus "continuous, unbroken, lasting throughout" — from per- "through" + the root of petere "to seek, go toward."
- Late Latin — the adverb perpetualiter and the noun perpetuitas are formed.
- Middle English (14th century) — perpetual enters English as an adjective meaning "lasting forever."
- 15th century — perpetually is formed with the adverbial -ly suffix; used in religious, legal, and philosophical contexts.
- 16th–19th century — spreads to scientific and general use (perpetual motion, perpetual snow).
- 20th–21st century — the adverb is common in everyday emphatic use: "perpetually late," "perpetually in trouble," while retaining its formal meaning of everlasting continuity.
