• Footstep: Similar because both name the tread of a walking foot; differs because footstep stresses the step as movement or its mark, while footfall stresses the sound of striking.
  • Tread: Similar because both cover the manner and noise of walking; differs because tread is the gait itself (a heavy tread), while footfall is the audible instant of each step.
  • Tramp: Similar because both name heavy audible walking; differs because tramp is loud, weary, and purposeful (the tramp of boots), while footfall can be feather-light.
  • Clatter: Similar because both are percussive sounds of movement; differs because clatter is harsh and chaotic (pans, hooves on cobbles), while footfall is a single soft human stroke.
  • Padding: Similar because both describe soft quiet steps; differs because padding evokes bare feet or paws on soft ground, while footfall covers any step heard by a listener.
  • Echo: Similar because a footfall in a hall returns as one; differs because echo is the reflected sound, while footfall is the original stroke that caused it.
  • Thud: Similar because a heavy footfall lands with one; differs because thud is the dull impact-noise in general, while footfall names its walking source.
  • Foot traffic: Similar because both count passers-by in the retail sense; differs because foot traffic is the American commercial term, while footfall in this sense is British and journalistic.
  • Step: Similar because both mark one movement of one foot; differs because step is plain and multipurpose (dance steps, steps taken), while footfall is atmospheric and tied to sound and silence.
