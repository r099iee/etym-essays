• Flowing: Similar because both describe liquid movement; differs because flowing is smooth, steady and continuous, while gushing is sudden, violent and copious — flow at pressure.
  • Pouring: Similar because both mean "emitting in quantity"; differs because pouring can be deliberate and controlled (pour tea), while gushing is uncontrolled and forceful.
  • Streaming: Similar because both mean "flowing freely"; differs because streaming is continuous and steady (streaming tears), while gushing is a burst under pressure.
  • Spouting: Similar because both mean "ejecting forcibly"; differs because spouting comes from a point source in a jet (a whale spouts), while gushing is a broader outrush.
  • Spurting: Similar because both mean "ejecting in bursts"; differs because a spurt is a short, pulsing jet, while a gush is a larger, sustained flow.
  • Jetting: Similar because both mean "issuing in a stream"; differs because jetting suggests a narrow high-pressure stream, while gushing suggests flood-like abundance.
  • Spewing: Similar because both mean "ejecting forcefully"; differs because spew is usually of nastiness (spewing insults, vomit) and pejorative, while gush can be neutral or affectionate.
  • Torrential: Similar because both mean "flood-like"; differs because torrential is an adjective of quantity (torrential rain), while gushing is a participle of the act of pouring.
  • Effusive: Similar because both describe overflowing emotion or praise; differs because effusive is formal and neutral-to-positive, while gushing leans colloquial and mildly disapproving.
  • Exuberant: Similar because both describe overflowing feeling; differs because exuberant is joyful and energetic (an exuberant welcome), while gushing implies an excess of sentiment that may embarrass.
  • Fulsome: Similar because both mean "over-abundant in praise"; differs because fulsome is strongly negative (fulsome flattery — insincere), while gushing can be sincere though excessive.
  • Lavish: Similar because both mean "abundant, unstinting"; differs because lavish is about quantity of gifts or praise given, while gushing is about the outpouring of feeling itself.
  • Profuse: Similar because both mean "pouring forth plentifully"; differs because profuse is formal (profuse apologies), while gushing is vivid and physical.
  • Demonstrative: Similar because both describe openly shown feelings; differs because demonstrative is a neutral, clinical word for visible affection, while gushing implies excess of sentiment.
  • Sentimental: Similar because both describe exaggerated emotion; differs because sentimental stresses the emotion's tenderness or falseness, while gushing stresses its sheer volume and flow.
  • Maudlin: Similar because both mean "overly emotional"; differs because maudlin implies tearful, self-pitying excess and is harsher, while gushing can be happy and affectionate.
  • Saccharine: Similar because both mean "excessively sweet"; differs because saccharine describes the sweetness of tone (a saccharine film), while gushing describes the act of pouring out praise.
  • Cloying: Similar because both mean "excessive to the point of disgust"; differs because cloying is the effect on the listener (sickly-sweet), while gushing is the speaker's behaviour.
  • Rhapsodic: Similar because both mean "ecstatically enthusiastic"; differs because rhapsodic is elevated and admiring (a rhapsodic review), while gushing is everyday and slightly comic.
