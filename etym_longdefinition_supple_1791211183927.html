supple describes what bends easily without breaking, combining physical flexibility with graceful adaptability. In its most concrete sense it means pliant, flexible, and easily bent, as of supple branches, leather, or limbs that yield to pressure and return to form.

In the Durant passage, supple branches are young, pliable shoots, likely willow or hazel, that can be woven into baskets. The emphasis is on workability: unlike dry brittle wood, supple wood submits to the weaver's hands, allowing tight, beautiful forms. This craft sense underlies all others.

Core physical senses today include:

  • Pliant and flexible material: supple leather, supple fabric, supple skin that is soft, smooth, elastic, and pleasant to touch. Cosmetics promise supple skin, meaning moisturized and resilient rather than dry or stiff.
  • Lithe and agile body: a supple dancer, gymnast, or athlete whose joints move through full range with ease and grace. Implies youth, health, training, and fluid control rather than mere looseness.
  • Bending naturally: supple boughs, supple stems, supple reeds that sway in wind without snapping. Botanical and poetic use for living flexibility.
  • Softened by work: technical leather and equestrian sense: leather made supple by oiling and working; a horse suppled by exercises that loosen neck, back, and haunches.

Extended and figurative senses:

  • Adaptable mind and character: a supple intellect, supple imagination, supple prose that adjusts quickly to new facts, shifts tone gracefully, and avoids rigidity. Generally positive, suggesting versatility and responsiveness.
  • Diplomatic and political agility: supple diplomacy, supple negotiations, supple economy that bends with circumstance rather than breaking. Business jargon praises supple organizations.
  • Compliant, sometimes servile: with a negative tinge, a supple flatterer, supple conscience, supple spine can mean overly yielding, obsequious, lacking firm principle. The same yielding admired in leather is suspect in morals. Context and adjectives disambiguate.
  • Voice and sound: a supple voice, supple phrasing in music, meaning fluid, expressive, capable of subtle modulation without strain.

Grammatically supple is chiefly an adjective with comparative suppler, supplest and adverb supplely, noun suppleness, verb to supple meaning to make flexible. It collocates with limbs, bodies, leather, skin, branches, minds, prose, diplomacy. Register is standard literary and everyday, slightly elegant but not archaic. It differs from merely flexible by adding grace and smoothness; from elastic by not implying snap-back; from floppy by implying controlled strength.

Stylistically supple is praised precisely because it avoids both rigidity and floppiness: rigid breaks, floppy collapses, while supple sustains form through movement. Trainers, editors, and artisans alike value this middle virtue of strong softness that endures handling and time.
