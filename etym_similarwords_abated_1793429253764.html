• Subsided: Similar because both denote a decrease in intensity or violence; differs because subsided emphasizes settling downward or sinking to a lower level (like floodwaters or swelling), whereas abated emphasizes the slackening of active fury, force, or violent blows.
  • Slackened: Similar because both describe a loss of momentum or speed; differs because slackened evokes the loosening of a taut rope or slowing of pace, while abated denotes a comprehensive reduction in violent power.
  • Diminished: Similar because both involve reduction in magnitude; differs because diminished is a broad, quantitative term applicable to size, numbers, reputation, or wealth, whereas abated specifically highlights the decline of turbulent pressure, violence, or pain.
  • Wanelled: Similar because both describe a gradual decline; differs because waned suggests a natural, cyclical fading (like the moon or daylight) rather than the spent fury of a turbulent storm.
  • Mitigated: Similar because both result in a less severe condition; differs because mitigated requires an active human or external agent intentionally softening or moderating the severity of a hardship, whereas abated often occurs autonomously as a natural process.
  • Relented: Similar because both signify an easing of severity; differs because relented implies a conscious, emotional choice by a person or fate to abandon harshness and show mercy, whereas abated is an impersonal physical or legal reduction.
  • Eased: Similar because both convey relief from pressure or distress; differs because eased is everyday, gentle vocabulary focusing on comfort, while abated is formal and literary, emphasizing the exhaustion of violent force.
  • Quelled: Similar because both describe the cessation of turmoil; differs because quelled is actively aggressive, meaning forcibly crushed or suppressed by external power, whereas abated describes the turmoil dying away.
