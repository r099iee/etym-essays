- 1440s - *Late Middle English*: Borrowed via Anglo-French and Old French *penal* from Late Latin *poenalis*, initially used to describe painful spiritual penance, divine retribution, or severe physical suffering.
- 1500s–1600s - *Early Modern English Law*: Formalized in English common law and royal statutes to describe criminal punishments, punitive forfeitures, and judicial sentences.
- 1700s–1800s - *Imperial Expansion & Enlightenment Reform*: Associated with the rise of 'penal colonies' (Botany Bay, Devil's Island) and Enlightenment criminal justice reforms led by Cesare Beccaria and Jeremy Bentham.
- Late 19th–20th c. - *Modern Jurisprudence*: Standardized globally in national legal frameworks as 'penal code' (e.g., Indian Penal Code, French Code Pénal) and the academic field of penology.
- Contemporary Era - *Criminology & Social Criticism*: Extensively examined in sociological critiques of mass incarceration (such as Michel Foucault's *Discipline and Punish* on the 'penal-carceral continuum').
