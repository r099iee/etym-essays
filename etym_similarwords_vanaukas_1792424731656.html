• vānara: Similar because both describe the simian allies of Rama dwelling in the wild; differs because vānara is an ethnonymic noun ("forest-being / forest-person") denoting the civilized, speaking monkey race of Kiṣkindhā, whereas vanaukas is an ecological descriptive compound ("forest-dweller") shared equally with hermits, birds, and forest predators.
  • kapi: Similar because both refer to monkeys in Sanskrit narrative; differs because kapi is a primary zoological noun rooted in physical restless traits and ancient Vedic roots, whereas vanaukas identifies the creature purely by its ecological habitat and wilderness residence.
  • vānaprastha: Similar because both refer to forest-dwellers; differs because vānaprastha is strictly a sociological and religious term for a human householder who has entered the third āśrama (forest hermit stage) according to the Manusmṛti, whereas vanaukas is a versatile poetic noun applied to monkeys, wild animals, birds, and hermits alike.
  • araṇyacāra: Similar because both mean "roamer of the forest" (araṇya + cāra); differs because araṇyacāra emphasizes continuous, restless wandering through the wilderness, while vanaukas emphasizes permanent dwelling or having one's ancestral home and shelter (okas) in the woods.
  • śākhāmṛga: Similar because both describe monkeys in wild habitats; differs because śākhāmṛga pinpoints arboreal locomotion ("branch-beast"), whereas vanaukas encompasses the entire forest ecosystem as a home.
  • muni: Similar because both can refer to forest-dwelling ascetics; differs because muni denotes a silent sage or seer characterized by spiritual ecstasy, vow of silence, and profound meditation, whereas vanaukas refers descriptively to the physical fact of residing in the wild forest.
  • mṛga: Similar because both designate wild creatures inhabiting the forest; differs because mṛga primarily denotes quadrupeds, game animals, or deer (from the root mṛg "to hunt/track"), whereas vanaukas is a broader spatial classification that explicitly includes primates, birds, and ascetics.
  • kānana-vāsī: Similar because both literally translate to "forest-dweller"; differs because kānana-vāsī is a late, prosaic compound using the common verb root vas ("to reside"), whereas vanaukas is an elevated classical poetic compound utilizing the archaic Vedic nominal stem okas ("shelter, sanctuary, ancestral home").
