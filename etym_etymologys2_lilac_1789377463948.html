Sanskrit (c. 1500–500 BCE)
nīla (नील)
"dark blue, sapphire, indigo-colored"
Note: Primary Indo-Aryan color term for deep blue; also an epithet of Shiva (Nīlakaṇṭha, 'blue-throated').
↓
Middle Persian / Classical Persian (c. 6th–12th c. CE)
nīl → nīlak (نیلک)
"indigo dye → bluish, light blue; the lilac shrub"
Note: Diminutive/adjectival suffix -ak attached to nīl ('indigo').
↓
Arabic (c. 12th–14th c.)
līlak (ليلك)
"the lilac tree, fragrant blue-flowered shrub"
Note: Liquid dissimilation or dialectal shift from initial /n/ to /l/ (nīlak > līlak).
↓
Ottoman Turkish (c. 15th–16th c.)
leylak
"lilac tree, fragrant lilac flower"
↓
Spanish / Italian (c. 16th c.)
lila / lillac
"the lilac shrub"
↓
French (c. 1570–1600)
lilas
"lilac bush and its fragrant flower"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1625–1670)
lilac / lelack
"the lilac shrub; later pale violet color"
↓
Modern English (present)
LILAC
