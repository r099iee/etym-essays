- Late 14th c. – Of the Christian Church or ordained clergy - Entered Middle English via Anglo-Norman; strictly demarcated the sacred spiritual realm from feudal, secular baronies and lay jurisdiction.
- 15th to 16th c. – Church jurisdiction & canon law authority - Became the focal battleground between papal canon law courts and royal common law, climaxing in the English Reformation and the Act of Supremacy.
- 17th c. – High Church episcopal polity vs Puritan reform - Connoted state episcopal governance and established hierarchy, championed by Richard Hooker against Puritan anti-clerical agitation.
- 18th to 19th c. – Academic discipline & antiquarian architecture - Epitomized by university professorships ("Ecclesiastical History") and the Gothic ecclesiological revival, perceived as stately, learned, and traditional.
- 20th c. to Present – Formal institutional demarcation & bureaucratic metaphor - Contrasts church governance with secular civic administration; used figuratively for rigid, cloistered institutional politics in non-religious bodies.
