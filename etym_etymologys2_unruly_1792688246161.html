Note on Germanic-Romance morphological fusion: The word unruly is a classic exemplar of English middle-period morphological integration. While the root rule was imported from Anglo-Norman French (reule, from Latin rēgula), it was completely naturalized into the Germanic grammatical system. Rather than adopting an Anglo-Norman negative adjective (such as désordonné or irrégulier), Middle English speakers freely wrapped the Romance noun with native Anglo-Saxon affixes: the Proto-Germanic negative prefix *un- (from PIE *n̥-) and the adjectival suffix -ly (from Proto-Germanic *-līkaz / Old English -līc, from PIE *leig- "body, like, appearance"). This productive hybrid synthesis allowed English to retain the dignity of the Latin root while imbuing the word with visceral Germanic immediacy.
