- [Early 14th century: compressed lips] - Moteren names speech made with nearly closed lips — low, mumbling, barely audible, already private and self-directed rather than social.
- [16th-17th century: suspicion & discontent] - Muttering gains a dark social register: the mutterer is the grumbler and the potential plotter, and the noun mutter (1630) is the sound of hidden complaint.
- [18th-19th century: inwardness & comedy] - The word settles as the speech of solitude — old men and malcontents grumbling to themselves — half-suppressed, never meant to be clearly heard.
- [Contemporary sense] - Mutters is the low, closed-mouthed speech of private irritation or thought, and in Gollum's riddle the wind itself mutters: the same muffled, speech-like noise that has belonged to the word for seven centuries.
