- [early 17th c.: medical temperament] - Borrowed from Greek idiosynkrasia ("own blend"), the word first names a person's physical constitution and special susceptibility to disease or stimuli, a clinical term.
- [17th-18th c.: personal character] - It widens from the body to the mind: a person's characteristic temperament, mode of thought and behaviour, their private blend of qualities.
- [19th c.: the individual quirk] - It becomes a neutral descriptive term for a habit or trait peculiar to a single person, keeping a mildly medical, diagnostic flavour.
- [Modern: everyday individuality] - Idiosyncratic now labels any personal quirk or distinctive stylistic stamp, comparative and descriptive rather than judgemental, and capable of a wry, amused tone when the quirk is comically overdone.
