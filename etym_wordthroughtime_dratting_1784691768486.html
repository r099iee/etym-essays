- 17th century - "dratting" descends not from sound changes but from social history: "God rot (him or it)!" was a genuine curse wishing decay and ruin, but English developed an elaborate machinery of minced oaths to keep the force of swearing while avoiding blasphemy.
- 19th century - the imprecation was whittled down through "'d rot" to "drat", an interjection first recorded about 1815, and it behaved exactly like its parent: an exclamation ("Drat it!") and a verb, producing the adjective "dratted" ("that dratted cat") and the participial "dratting".
- Modern English - the word is homely, old-fashioned, and gently comic: sharp frustration with an almost apologetic mildness, the strongest language a period English kitchen or a children's book allows itself, and precisely what Tolkien gives his blundering dwarves.
