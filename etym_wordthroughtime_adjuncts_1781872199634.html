- Joined-on matter in early English - Since the 16th century adjuncts (from Latin adjunctus, 'joined to') have named things added to something else as secondary or accidental rather than essential.
- Grammatical sense - By the 18th century the word gains a precise grammatical meaning: a non-essential clause or phrase that modifies a sentence element, mirroring the notion of something attached but not core.
- Modern academic and structural use - Today adjuncts range from the academic rank of adjunct professor (attached but not core faculty) to any secondary accompaniment in aesthetics and ceremony that must be distinguished from the essential virtue.
