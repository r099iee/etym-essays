- 1850s to 1860s: Diplomatic Title for the Shogun - Borrowed into English by Commodore Matthew Perry and Townsend Harris to designate the Tokugawa Shogun of Japan; perceived by Western diplomats as an exotic, high-status oriental royal title signifying de facto sovereign power.
- 1860s to 1870s: Presidential Affectionate Moniker - Adopted colloquially in Washington political circles by Abraham Lincoln's personal secretaries as an intimate, half-ironic nickname for the President, connoting resolute executive power, quiet strength, and unshakeable will.
- 1880s to 1920s: Gilded Age Industrial Titan - Transferred metaphorically to the colossal American monopolists and capitalists of the Gilded Age (railroad, oil, and steel barons); viewed with a mixture of public awe, envy, and moral condemnation regarding monopolistic excess.
- 1930s to 1980s: Standard Capitalist Archetype - Cemented in journalistic, literary, and cinematic culture (such as F. Scott Fitzgerald's 'The Last Tycoon') as the glamorous, all-powerful commander of studio systems, media empires, or corporate conglomerates.
- 1990s to Present: Tech Billionaire and Gaming Jargon - Expanded to describe modern Silicon Valley founders and real estate moguls, while simultaneously becoming the definitive global term for business-simulation video games (e.g., 'RollerCoaster Tycoon'); perceived neutrally or admiringly as an emblem of entrepreneurial mastery.
