- 14th c. - noun pile "heap, stack" and verb pilen "to heap up" borrowed from Old French; concrete senses (wood, stone, grain).
- 15th-17th c. - extends to money and abstract accumulation; "piled up" becomes common for growing masses.
- 16th-18th c. - a large building is a "pile"; "piled" describes imposing masonry, closing the circle back to the Latin pillar.
- 19th c. - Volta's pile (the battery stack) adds a technical "pile"; the word enters electrical science.
- 20th c. - crowd and figurative senses: "pile into a car", "pile on pressure", football's "piling on".
- 21st c. - everyday participle for abundance and overload; "piled high with" is the standard idiom.
