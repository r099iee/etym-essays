• Protest: Similar because both voice objection; differs because protest can be public, political, or collective, while remonstrate is interpersonal and reasoned.
  • Expostulate: Similar because both make earnest objection; differs because expostulate is more rhetorical and pleading, while remonstrate emphasizes setting out the grounds for complaint.
  • Object: Similar because both register disagreement; differs because object is neutral and brief, without the moral re-showing of fault that remonstrate carries.
  • Admonish: Similar because both correct someone's behavior; differs because admonish leans toward warning and counsel from a position of authority, while remonstrate often comes from one without coercive power.
  • Reprove: Similar because both express disapproval of conduct; differs because reprove is more openly hierarchical and punitive, the voice of authority, whereas remonstrate relies on argument and feeling.
  • Rebuke: Similar because both sternly criticize; differs because rebuke is sharp and can be a public rebuff, while remonstrate is reasoned and persuasive in intent.
  • Reason with: Similar because both involve setting out reasons to change someone's mind; differs because reason with is neutral and intellectual, while remonstrate adds reproachful moral urgency.
  • Complain: Similar because both voice dissatisfaction; differs because complain is everyday and self-directed, while remonstrate is formal, principled, and addressed to the wrongdoer.
  • Challenge: Similar because both contest a person's position; differs because challenge is more aggressive or competitive, while remonstrate is earnest and reform-seeking.
  • Remonstrance: Similar because it is the noun of the same protest act; differs because remonstrance names the formal statement or petition of grievances, often institutional or parliamentary, while remonstrate is the verbal action.
