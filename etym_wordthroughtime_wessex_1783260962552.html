- Late-5th-century foundation - Wessex, land of the West Saxons, was the kingdom founded in southern England by Cerdic's line, its very name evoking the seax knife of the Saxon warrior cult.
- 9th-10th-century ascendancy - under Alfred and his successors Wessex became the kingdom that withstood the Danes and unified England, and so the name carried the prestige of the English monarchy's birthplace.
- Later centuries and the 19th-century revival - after the Norman Conquest the name lapsed as a political term but was revived by antiquarians and by Thomas Hardy, who used Wessex as the literary name for the Dorset countryside.
- Contemporary sense - Wessex names both the historical kingdom and Hardy's fictional region, and is familiar through the royal title Earl of Wessex, remaining an emblem of Saxon England and of Alfred.
