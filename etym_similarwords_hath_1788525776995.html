• has: Similar because has is the direct modern equivalent representing the exact same grammatical person, tense, and meaning (third-person singular present of have); differs because has is the universally standard contemporary form descended from northern Middle English, whereas hath is the archaic, formal, and liturgical southern form.
  • haveth: Similar as an uncontracted historical Middle English variant; differs because haveth retains the full two-syllable stem and inflection before phonological contraction reduced it to the single syllable hath.
  • doth: Similar as an archaic third-person singular present auxiliary verb ending in -th; differs because doth is the third-person form of do (used for action, emphasis, or periphrastic negation) rather than have (possession and perfect aspect).
  • saith: Similar as an archaic third-person singular present indicative form with the -th suffix; differs because saith is the historical form of says (from say), denoting speech rather than possession.
  • possesses: Similar in the lexical sense of holding ownership or containing an attribute; differs because possesses is a Latinate lexical verb that cannot function as a grammatical auxiliary to construct perfect verb tenses.
  • holds: Similar for physical or abstract retention of property or office; differs because holds emphasizes physical grip, spatial containment, or legal title, lacking auxiliary grammatical function.
  • bears: Similar in expressing the carrying of marks, attributes, or burdens (e.g., bearing wounds); differs because bears emphasizes weight, endurance, or outward display rather than simple possession.
  • owns: Similar for legal proprietorship; differs because owns is strictly limited to property ownership and personal acknowledgment, whereas hath spans auxiliary aspect, experience, and abstract condition.
  • harbors: Similar for maintaining feelings, secrets, or shelter within; differs because harbors carries a specific connotation of concealing, sheltering, or nurturing negative sentiments (e.g., harboring resentment).
