- "to make foul, pollute with filth" - physical soiling, the oldest sense (Old English befȳlan)
- "to defile morally, to corrupt with sin" - transferred to moral and religious pollution; heavy in homiletic and devotional writing (Old English through Middle English)
- "to desecrate, to violate what is sacred" - intensifies in religious and poetic usage, applied to holy places and oaths (Middle English onward)
- "to pollute water, air, and land" - the ecological application grows with industrialisation and urban sewage (18th-19th c.)
- modern - formal and literary; moral charge retained; the concrete animal sense (birds fouling a roost) still alive; everyday speech prefers "dirty", "soil", "pollute"
