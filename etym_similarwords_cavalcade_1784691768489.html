• Procession: Similar because both are formal lines of people moving together; differs because a procession can be on foot and is usually religious or ceremonial, while a cavalcade specifically involves riders on horseback.
  • Parade: Similar because both are formal public displays; differs because a parade is held for display or celebration (often with an audience and a route), while a cavalcade is a dignified company of riders that may simply be travelling.
  • Convoy: Similar because both are groups travelling together; differs because a convoy travels for mutual protection (ships, lorries, military vehicles), while a cavalcade moves for ceremony and spectacle.
  • Retinue: Similar because both are companies accompanying someone; differs because a retinue or train is the following of a great person, emphasising service and dependence, while a cavalcade emphasises the mounted spectacle itself.
  • Cortège: Similar because both are formal processions; differs because cortège is most commonly used of funeral processions, while a cavalcade is festive or ceremonial in a wider sense.
  • Motorcade: Similar because both are processions of vehicles; differs because a motorcade specifically involves motor vehicles and was coined in the 20th century as a back-formation from cavalcade.
  • Column: Similar because both are lines of marchers or riders; differs because "column" is a neutral military or marching term without pageantry, while a cavalcade carries ceremonial grandeur.
  • Pageant: Similar because both are public spectacles; differs because a pageant is the display itself (with floats, costumes, tableaux), while a cavalcade is a procession of riders that may be part of a pageant but is not identical with it.
