Derivation chain for FLIT (the target root word is bold at the final stage): 1. PIE *pleu- "to flow, float, swim; watery, buoyant motion" (reconstructed; c. 4500–2500 BCE). 2. Proto-Germanic *fleutaną "to float, flow" (reconstructed; ancestor of Old Norse fljóta and Old English flēotan "to float" — source of English fleet, and related to flood and flow). 3. Old Norse fljóta "to float, flow, swim" (attested in Old Norse prose and poetry, 12th–13th c.). 4. Old Norse flytja "to cause to float; to carry, convey, remove" (causative formation from fljóta, i.e. "to make something float or move"; attested in Old Norse texts). 5. Middle English flitten / flytten "to move, migrate, change residence, depart" (borrowed into northern and Scots English from Old Norse flytja; first recorded c. 1200–1300). 6. Modern English flit "to move lightly and swiftly; (informal) to depart secretly — do a (moonlight) flit" (light-motion and absconding senses recorded from the 16th to the 19th centuries onward). Debated / nuanced stages: The rapid light-motion sense of Modern English flit may have been reinforced or partly blended with continental West Germanic verbs such as Middle Dutch vletten "to glide, float" and Middle High German vlitten "to move, put", and possibly with native English dialect forms; the precise mixing is debated. The standard OED derivation, however, runs through Old Norse flytja (the causative of fljóta), and no handbook treats any intermediate stage as unattested beyond the usual reconstructed PIE and Proto-Germanic lemmas.
