• Contused: Similar because it is the exact medical term for a bruise; differs because it is clinical, rare, and formal — bruised is the ordinary word for the same thing.
  • Injured / Hurt: Similar because both name physical harm; differ because they cover every kind of damage (cuts, breaks, sprains) while bruised specifies impact damage without an open wound.
  • Battered: Similar because it describes repeated blows and their marks; differs because it implies many blows over time and greater severity, often with visible damage beyond bruising.
  • Black-and-blue: Similar because it literally describes the colouring of a bruise; differs because it is colloquial, descriptive idiom rather than a definitional word, and it is usually a predicate ('his arm was black-and-blue').
  • Sore: Similar because bruised flesh is sore; differs because sore describes the pain and tenderness alone, without the discolouration or the cause.
  • Wounded: Similar because both describe injury; differs because wounded suggests a break in the skin or a deep emotional blow, a graver register than a bruise.
  • Damaged / Dented (of fruit): Similar because bruised fruit is damaged fruit; differ because they are general words covering all harm, while bruised names the specific soft, discoloured mark.
  • Offended / Hurt (emotionally): Similar because both can name emotional injury; differ because offended is about slights and insult alone, while a 'bruised' feeling or ego implies injury that aches and needs time to heal.
