• Pablum: Similar because both name soft infant food and both came to mean bland, intellectually undemanding material; differs because pablum is a 20th-century trademarked cereal name generalized to metaphor, while pap is the older, more purely native word with a longer literary history.
  • Porridge: Similar because both are soft cereal foods eaten from a bowl; differs because porridge (oatmeal, etc.) is a substantial adult staple, while pap is specifically the semi-liquid food of infants and invalids — and lacks pap's contemptuous figurative sense.
  • Gruel: Similar because both are thin, semi-liquid foods fed to the weak; differs because gruel (thin oatmeal soup) was the punishing fare of the poor and of Oliver Twist's workhouse, carrying overtones of bare subsistence, while pap was kindly, motherly fare.
  • Mush: Similar because both are soft, shapeless masses of food; differs because mush is a cornmeal porridge and by extension any pulpy mass, while pap is weaning food and by extension bland intellectual fare.
  • Mash: Similar because both name soft, crushed, pulpy food; differs because mash is crushed solid matter (mashed potato) fed to infants or animals, while pap is boiled-to-softness liquid nourishment.
  • Drivel: Similar because both mean worthless, empty talk or writing; differs because drivel emphasizes foolish, slobbering nonsense, while pap emphasizes soft, sentimental blandness.
  • Tripe: Similar because both dismissively name worthless material — "what a load of tripe"; differs because tripe (stomach meat, then garbage) is coarse and disgusting, while pap is soft and merely insipid.
  • Hogwash: Similar because both are contemptuous terms for nonsense; differs because hogwash (swill for pigs) is blatant rubbish, while pap is harmless-looking but mind-numbing material.
  • Slop: Similar because both name soft, semi-liquid food of low quality; differs because slop is wasteful, messy, or swill-like, while pap is deliberately gentle and nourishing.
  • Swill: Similar because both name cheap liquid or semi-liquid fare; differs because swill is hog-food or alcoholic garbage, coarse and disgusting, whereas pap is innocent, milky softness.
  • Nonsense: Similar because both describe content without intellectual value; differs because nonsense is broadly meaningless or absurd, while pap is specifically soft, sentimental, undemanding material that is not nonsense but banality.
  • Piffle: Similar because both dismissively label trivial, empty material; differs because piffle is foolish chatter with a comic ring, while pap is inert blandness with a maternal image.
  • Spoon-feeding: Similar because both evoke feeding ideas to a passive public; differs because spoon-feeding is the verb of the process, while pap is the substance spoon-fed.
