• Imprint: Similar because both identify a publisher on a book; differs because an imprint is the publisher’s official name and address — a verbal, legal statement — whereas a colophon is a pictorial device.
  • Logo: Similar because both are identifying graphic marks; differs because logo is the general modern word for any company mark and carries no bibliographic history or bookish flavour.
  • Device: Similar because both can name a printer’s emblem; differs because device is the older printing term for such emblems (Aldus’s dolphin-and-anchor), while colophon has modernised into the publisher’s registered trademark emblem.
  • Trademark: Similar because both concern protected identifying symbols; differs because trademark is the legal term for any protected name, sign, or symbol, while colophon names the publishing-specific emblem.
  • Emblem: Similar because both are symbolic devices standing for a person or thing; differs because emblem is generic — any symbolic picture — while colophon is specifically the book-publisher’s identifying mark.
  • Masthead: Similar because both identify a publication; differs because masthead names the title block of a newspaper or magazine printed repeatedly in the paper, whereas a colophon belongs to books.
  • Badge: Similar because both are marks of identity; differs because a badge is worn or displayed as a sign of rank or membership, while a colophon is printed to mark a publisher’s product.
  • Ex libris: Similar because both are identifying book marks; differs because an ex libris (bookplate) marks a private owner’s copy, while a colophon identifies the publisher of the edition.
  • Insignia: Similar because both are distinguishing marks of identity; differs because insignia connotes official or military rank and is not tied to the book trade.
