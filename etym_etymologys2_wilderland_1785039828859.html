TREE 1 — the WILDER element. PIE root *ghwer- "wild beast, untamed creature" (attested in Greek θήρ thēr and Latin ferus; the direct Germanic ancestor of 'wild' is debated) OR PIE *welt- "woodland, wild" (proposed by Watkins as source of Germanic *wilthijaz, cf. Welsh gwyllt 'wild' — the connection is debated) → Proto-Germanic *wilthijaz "wild, untamed" (c. 5th c. BCE–1st c. CE) → Old English wilde "wild, untamed, uncultivated" (c. 8th–11th c.) → clipped/archaic form wildern, wilder "wild, desolate" (dialectal variant of wilderness; attested in older English) → Tolkien's Wilderland (1937, The Hobbit; his English rendering of the Elvish name Rhovanion). Note on debate: the ultimate source of Proto-Germanic *wilthijaz is not certain — Watkins links it to PIE *welt- 'woodland', while the old connection to *ghwer- 'wild beast' (Greek thēr, Latin ferus) is phonologically problematic and now generally rejected; the English adjective 'wild' and its clipped form 'wilder' are themselves well attested, but the PIE stage is reconstructed on debated evidence.

TREE 2 — the LAND element. PIE root *lendh- "open land, heath, steppe" (attested across Celtic, Germanic, Balto-Slavic) → Proto-Germanic *landą "land, ground, territory" (c. 5th c. BCE–1st c. CE) → Old English land "ground, soil, country" (c. 8th–11th c.) → Modern English land "earth, territory, country" → Wilderland (compound of wilder + land, coined 1937).
