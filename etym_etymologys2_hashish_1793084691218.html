Arabic (verb root) (ancient, pre-Islamic)
ḥašša
‘to mow, cut dry grass’
↓
Classical Arabic (medieval, 10th–13th c.)
ḥašīš
‘dry herb, hay; hemp herb’
↓
French (early 19th c., c. 1800–1845)
hachisch
‘cannabis resin from Egypt’
↓
Modern English (mid-19th c., from 1840s)
hashish
‘pressed cannabis resin drug’

Note: the derivation of assassin from hashish via French assassin and Italian assassino was proposed by Silvestre de Sacy in 1809 and long repeated, but modern Ismaili scholarship, notably Farhad Daftary, holds there is no evidence the Nizaris used hashish; hashishiyya was likely a general term of abuse meaning low rabble.
