PIE (Proto-Indo-European) (c. 3500–2500 BCE)
*temh₁- / *temp-
"to cut, divide; or to stretch, span"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000–500 BCE)
*tempos-
"section of duration, season, proper occasion"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE)
tempus (gen. temporis)
"time, season, opportunity, weather, condition"
Note: Neuter third-declension noun; also yielded tempus ('temple of the head', where skin is stretched or timed with the pulse).
↓
Old French (c. 9th–13th c.)
tens / tans
"time, duration, season, weather"
Note: The Latin unaccented final -us weakened, leaving stem tens; the Latin etymological -p- was reintroduced by Renaissance humanists in Middle French.
↓
Middle French / Modern French (c. 14th c.–present)
temps
"time, duration, weather, tense, beat"
↓
