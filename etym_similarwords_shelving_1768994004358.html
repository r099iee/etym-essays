• sloping: Similar in describing a gradual incline; differs because sloping is a uniform, featureless incline, while shelving suggests flat stages, a slope built ledge by ledge like a staircase.
  • terraced: Similar in suggesting flat steps on a slope; differs because terraced implies artificial, regular steps cut by labor or agriculture, while shelving is the natural step-wise incline of rock left by receding water.
  • inclined: Similar in denoting an angled surface; differs because inclined is geometric and technical, the language of planes, while shelving combines the naturalness of rock with the suggestion of flat ledges.
  • stepped: Similar in rising or falling in stages; differs because stepped is generic, while shelving alone names the homely image of the household shelf applied to the land - the shore furnished with shelves as a wall might be.
  • graded: Similar in describing a gradual change in elevation; differs because graded implies deliberate engineering (a graded road, graded land), while shelving is the natural step-wise fall of rock.
  • slanting: Similar in describing an oblique surface; differs because slanting is a straight, featureless angle, without the ledge-like stages that shelving implies.
  • declining: Similar in describing a downward slope; differs because declining is general and also means 'refusing', while shelving is specific to stepped landforms.
  • descending: Similar in moving downward; differs because descending is a general verb of motion, while shelving describes the form of the ground itself.
  • shoaling: Similar in describing a seabed that becomes shallower gradually; differs because shoaling is a nautical term for water growing shallow, while shelving names the stepped form of the shore or bottom.
  • gently graded: Similar in describing a gradual, easy slope; differs because gently graded focuses on ease of gradient, while shelving adds the image of flat ledges.
  • benched: Similar in suggesting a series of flat steps in a slope; differs because benched is used of constructed terraces (benched railway cuttings), while shelving is the natural rock form.
  • ledged: Similar in describing a slope with flat projecting steps; differs because ledged is rarer and less specific, while shelving carries the full domestic-to-geological history of the word shelf.
