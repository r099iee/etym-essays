• Smile: Similar because both are pleased or polite movements of the mouth; differs because a smile need not show the teeth and carries no implication of malice, mockery, or strain.
  • Smirk: Similar because both are tooth-bearing or mouth-twisting expressions with an edge; differs because a smirk is more narrowly smug and unpleasant, while a grin can be neutral, friendly, or genuinely happy.
  • Sneer: Similar because both can express contempt through the mouth; differs because a sneer curls the lip and adds open scorn, whereas a grin may simply bare the teeth without overt derision.
  • Beam: Similar because both name a broad, bright facial expression; differs because a beam is radiant and kindly — the opposite emotional colour of a knowing or predatory grin.
  • Laugh: Similar because both are expressions of amusement; differs because a laugh is vocal and involves breathing and sound, while a grin is silent and purely visual.
  • Grimace: Similar because both involve distortion of the face; differs because a grimace is usually a sign of pain, disgust, or effort, with no implication of pleasure, whereas a grin at least pretends to smile.
  • Leer: Similar because both can be unpleasant tooth-and-eye expressions; differs because a leer is specifically lascivious or sly, while a grin ranges across joy, mischief, and endurance.
  • Grim (as in 'grin and bear it'): Similar because of the shared word-family echo of hardship; differs because 'grim' is an adjective describing mood, not a facial expression, and pairs with grin only in the endurance idiom.
