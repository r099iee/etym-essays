• Gasp: Similar because it describes laboured breathing; differs because a gasp is a single sharp convulsive inhalation after surprise, pain, or a blow — a one-off event — while panting is a rapid, continuous rhythm of short breaths.
  • Huff: Similar because it is audible, effortful breathing; differs because huffing is louder, more theatrical, almost indignant — the breath of grumpiness or showing off — whereas panting is genuinely effortful and unglamorous.
  • Puff: Similar because it is short breathing under exertion; differs because puffing is lighter and more comic (the wolf "huffing and puffing"), while panting is the faster, open-mouthed rhythm of real effort or agitation.
  • Wheeze: Similar because it describes audible, laboured breathing; differs because a wheeze adds an abnormal whistling quality from narrowed airways, implying illness or age, which panting entirely lacks.
  • Heave: Similar because it describes the chest's effortful rise and fall; differs because heaving is slower and more profound — deep, sighing breaths often of grief or exhaustion — while panting is quick and shallow.
  • Hyperventilate: Similar because it is rapid, shallow breathing; differs because hyperventilating is a clinical term for over-breathing that upsets blood chemistry, often from anxiety, whereas panting is a normal response to exertion or emotion.
  • Blow: Similar because it is forceful expulsion of breath; differs because blowing can be slow, deliberate, and aimed (blowing out a candle), while panting is involuntary, rapid, and undirected.
  • Sigh: Similar because it is a sound of the breath under feeling; differs because a sigh is a single long, audible exhalation of relief, sadness, or weariness, whereas panting is a fast, shallow, repeated rhythm.
  • Breathless: Similar because it describes the state that follows or accompanies panting; differs because breathless is the adjective for the resulting condition (out of breath), not the continuous action that panting names.
  • Yearn: Similar because the figurative "pant for" means to long for something; differs because yearn is a general word for deep longing, while panting for something pictures eager, breathless desire — wanting something so much you gasp toward it.
  • Crave: Similar because both can express intense desire; differs because craving suggests appetite or addiction, whereas panting for pictures breathless eagerness and is more literary in its object.
  • Long: Similar because both describe wanting something deeply; differs because longing is quiet and often patient, while panting for is eager, impatient, and breathless — the desire of a runner near the finish.
