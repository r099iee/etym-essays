- [Vedic Samhitā Period (c. 1500–1000 BCE)] — the constituent roots *marut* (the roaring storm deities who ride lightning-chariots with Indra) and *ātman* (the life-breath, innermost vital self) were worshipped in Ṛgvedic sacrifice as primal forces of atmospheric fury and cosmic life-breath — [perception: venerated as fierce, golden-helmed atmospheric storm gods and the metaphysical foundation of life]
- [Epic Itihāsa Age (c. 500–200 BCE; Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa & Mahābhārata)] — crystallized into the celebrated patronymic compound *Mārutātmaja* ("son of Māruta"), deployed by Sage Vālmīki to extol the vanara hero Hanumān and by Vyāsa for the mighty warrior Bhīmasena — [perception: revered as a majestic heroic kenning conveying superhuman strength, invulnerability, and boundless speed inherited from the wind god]
- [Classical Sanskrit Kāvya & Natya (c. 4th–10th c. CE)] — celebrated by classical dramatists (such as Kālidāsa and Bhavabhūti) as an elevated poetic title for Hanumān, symbolizing unyielding devotional loyalty (*bhakti*), philosophical eloquence (*vyākaraṇa-jñāna*), and selfless heroic sacrifice — [perception: admired as a refined poetic ornament exemplifying dharmic nobility, martial virtue, and linguistic elegance]
- [Medieval Bhakti Renaissance (c. 14th–17th c.)] — woven into regional devotional stotras, temple liturgies, and Saint Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas* alongside vernacular equivalents (*Mārutinandana*, *Pavanasuta*), elevating Hanumān into a supreme household protective deity — [perception: worshipped fervently across every strata of Indian society as the remover of suffering, guardian against evil spirits, and bestower of courage]
- [Modern Devotional & Indological Era (19th–21st c.)] — continuously chanted in daily prayers, classical music concerts, and global Hindu liturgical recitations (such as the *Sundarakāṇḍa* parayana), while analyzed in academic comparative epic scholarship as an archetypal Indo-European divine patronymic — [perception: universally recognized as an ancient sacred title embodying spiritual devotion, physical vitality, moral steadfastness, and selfless service]
