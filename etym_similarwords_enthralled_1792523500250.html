• captivated: Similar because both denote having one's attention, affection, or interest held completely; differs because captivated (from Latin captivus, prisoner) leans heavily toward intellectual fascination, wit, and delightful charm, whereas enthralled carries a deeper, trance-like emotional surrender and stronger historical echoes of absolute, helpless subjugation.
  • mesmerized: Similar because both depict unbroken, spellbound absorption where the subject cannot look away; differs because mesmerized derives from Franz Anton Mesmer's 18th-century animal magnetism and implies a glassy-eyed, semi-conscious, hypnotic paralysis of the senses, whereas enthralled conveys active, awe-filled wonder and emotional appreciation.
  • spellbound: Similar because both indicate an experience akin to being placed under an irresistible charm; differs because spellbound explicitly relies on the folklore metaphor of an incantation or magical spell freezing a person in physical and verbal immobility, whereas enthralled highlights voluntary emotional and cognitive surrender.
  • fascinated: Similar because both convey intense interest and attraction; differs because fascinated (from Latin fascinum, an evil eye or amulet) can be purely cerebral, clinical, or detached (e.g., a scientist fascinated by a viral mutation), lacking the breathtaking emotional intoxication inherent in enthralled.
  • beguiled: Similar because both involve being charmed and held by elegance, speech, or melody; differs because beguiled frequently retains an undercurrent of cunning, deception, distraction, or subtle trickery (from guile), whereas enthralled is an earnest, genuine, and transparent surrender to beauty.
  • enchanted: Similar because both describe being filled with magical delight and wonder; differs because enchanted (from Latin incantare, to chant a magical formula) evokes fairy-tale delight, whimsical romance, and radiant joy, whereas enthralled has a darker, more intensely gripping, and profound hold on the psyche.
  • ravished: Similar because both signify overwhelming emotional transport; differs because ravished (from Latin rapere, to seize violently) conveys ecstatic, violent sensory transport bordering on physical or emotional seizure, whereas enthralled maintains a prolonged, contemplative state of rapt devotion.
  • enslaved: Similar because etymologically enthralled literally meant enslaved; differs because enslaved today retains exclusively the harsh, non-consensual, and oppressive reality of physical servitude, legal bondage, or chemical addiction, whereas enthralled has shifted almost completely into positive aesthetic enjoyment.
  • riveted: Similar because both describe attention that is utterly fixed and incapable of wandering; differs because riveted uses an industrial and metallurgical metaphor (fastened like a metal bolt or rivet), emphasizing rigid, immoveable focus often prompted by shock, suspense, or horror, rather than artistic bliss.
  • entranced: Similar because both denote slipping into an absorbed mental state; differs because entranced emphasizes crossing a psychological threshold into a dreamlike trance, semi-somnolence, or mystical altered state of consciousness, whereas enthralled retains acute emotional sensitivity to the inspiring art.
  • charmed: Similar because both involve finding an experience deeply pleasing and attractive; differs because charmed is far lighter, more casual, polite, and socially agreeable, completely lacking the intense, breathless gravity of enthralled.
  • hypnotized: Similar because both indicate that one's conscious will has been captured by an external force; differs because hypnotized carries a clinical, psychological, or stage-performance connotation involving susceptibility to suggestion, whereas enthralled describes voluntary aesthetic transcendence.
