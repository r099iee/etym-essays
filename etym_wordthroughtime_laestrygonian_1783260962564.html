- Homeric origin - Laestrygonian entered European letters from Homer's Odyssey, naming the mythical race of giant, man-eating cannibals whose city brought near-total destruction to Odysseus's fleet.
- Classical afterlife - in Latin and Renaissance literature the epithet carried an archaic, epic gravity, a measure of absolute superstitious dread of the wild margins beyond the civilized world.
- Modern usage - Laestrygonian survives as a deliberately literary, almost recherché adjective, found in Homeric scholarship and ornate historical prose, essentially never in casual speech, and Gibbon's use of it shows Britain as unthinkable and man-eating as Homer's Telepylus.
