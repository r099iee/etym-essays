• Czecho-Slovakia: The hyphenated spelling imposed in 1938 and revived in 1990; it denotes the same territory but encodes a looser, more federal arrangement, so the hyphen is a political statement.
  • Czechoslovak: The adjective and demonym; same referent, differing only in grammatical function and in its continued use by diaspora bodies.
  • Czech Republic: One of the two successor states; it shares the Czech lands and most of the industry, but excludes Slovakia and is the product of the 1993 split.
  • Czechia: The short geographic name for the Czech Republic, promoted since 2016; similar territory, but it avoids the awkward official name and has no Slovak component.
  • Slovakia: The other successor state; similar language and history, but it was the junior partner and its separate statehood dates from 1993 (and 1939–1945).
  • Bohemia: The western historical land of the Czechs; similar as a metonym for the Czech part, but it is older, regional and excludes Moravia and Slovakia.
  • Czech lands: The collective term for Bohemia, Moravia and Silesia; similar as a territorial label, but it is ethnic-geographic rather than a state name.
  • Austria-Hungary: The empire from which Czechoslovakia was carved; similar in territory, but it was a dynastic monarchy rather than a national federation.
  • Velvet Divorce: The name of the 1993 dissolution; similar in reference, but it names the event rather than the state.
  • Yugoslavia: A parallel South Slav compound state; similar as a twentieth-century Slavic creation, but it dissolved through war rather than negotiation.
