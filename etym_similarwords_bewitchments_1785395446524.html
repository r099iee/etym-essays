• Enchantment: Similar because both denote magical charming or a spellbinding effect; differs because enchantment is songlike, pleasing, and morally neutral (the enchantment of an evening), while a bewitchment always names an act and its agent, a doer and a sufferer.
  • Spells: Similar because both are acts of magic performed by a practitioner; differs because spells are the narrower formulaic utterances, a subset of the wider art, while bewitchments name the act and its effect on the victim.
  • Charms: Similar because both exert magical power over a person; differs because charms suggest light, talismanic, or verbal magic, often benign, while bewitchments carry the fuller weight of binding and transforming.
  • Sorcery: Similar because both belong to the domain of harmful or potent magic; differs because sorcery names the practitioner's whole system of arts and ethos, while bewitchment names the single act and its result.
  • Witchcraft: Similar because both involve the witch's art; differs because witchcraft is the entire cultural craft and its reputation, whereas bewitchment is the specific act of charming a victim.
  • Glamour: Similar because both are magic that alters perception; differs because glamour is specifically a spell of the eyes changing how things appear, while bewitchment binds or changes the victim's state more broadly.
  • Conjuration: Similar because both are acts of magical power; differs because conjuration emphasizes summoning or invoking spirits and demons, while bewitchment works directly on the victim.
  • Fascination: Similar because both describe an overwhelming hold over attention; differs because fascination is usually natural or psychological, whereas bewitchment claims supernatural agency.
  • Captivation: Similar because both describe being held in thrall; differs because captivation is a secular figure for charm, while bewitchment carries the literal magical mechanics.
  • Beguilement: Similar because both involve deceiving or charming someone; differs because beguilement stresses deception and wiles, not necessarily supernatural magic.
  • Mesmerism: Similar because both describe an invisible influence of one person upon another; differs because mesmerism is a pseudo-scientific hypnotic force, not folk magic.
  • Witching: Similar because both derive from the witch and her craft; differs because witching is a rarer, more archaic synonym, while bewitchment is the standard modern noun for the act and state.
