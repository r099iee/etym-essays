• Beasts: Similar because both refer to non-human animals and uncivilized, savage individuals; differs because 'beasts' carries biblical, physical, and wild animal connotations (often majestic or four-legged), whereas 'brutes' emphasizes the specific absence of rationality, empathy, and refined intellect.
  • Monsters: Similar because both describe frightening, cruel, and destructive entities; differs because 'monsters' implies unnatural, grotesque, or horrifying deviation from nature or supreme wickedness, whereas 'brutes' implies crude, dull, animalistic savagery.
  • Savages: Similar because both describe individuals lacking civilized refinement; differs because 'savages' has strong anthropological and historical baggage regarding uncivilized tribes, while 'brutes' focuses on unthinking physical violence and bestial cruelty.
  • Barbarians: Similar because both describe coarse, uncultured, and aggressive people; differs because 'barbarians' emphasizes cultural foreignness, destruction of civilized arts, and organized hordes, whereas 'brutes' focuses on individual physical cruelty and senseless animal instinct.
  • Fiends: Similar because both describe wicked, hurtful beings; differs because 'fiends' implies demonic cunning, malicious delight in suffering, and supernatural evil, whereas 'brutes' act out of heavy, unreasoning, dull animal instinct.
  • Thugs: Similar because both denote violent, lawless men; differs because 'thugs' specifically implies criminal gang affiliation, extortion, and hired street violence, while 'brutes' is a broader moral and bestial condemnation.
  • Animals: Similar because both categorize the non-human biological kingdom; differs because 'animals' is the neutral, scientific, and objective biological hypernym, while 'brutes' is loaded with philosophical and emotional connotations of irrationality and savagery.
  • Ruffians: Similar because both refer to violent, rough men; differs because 'ruffians' emphasizes rowdy, disorderly street delinquency, whereas 'brutes' highlights overpowering, callous, and unfeeling physical cruelty.
