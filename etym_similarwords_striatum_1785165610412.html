• Basal ganglia: Similar in being the subcortical motor-and-reward system of which the striatum is the chief input station; differs because basal ganglia is the umbrella group of nuclei (striatum, globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra), while the striatum is only its principal receiving core.
  • Caudate nucleus: Similar in being one of the two anatomical halves of the dorsal striatum; differs because it names a single structure within the striatum, not the whole "striped body."
  • Putamen: Similar in being the other half of the dorsal striatum, joined to the caudate in motor and habit function; differs because putamen alone refers to just one nucleus and lacks the striatal "striped" image.
  • Nucleus accumbens: Similar in being a key dopamine-driven reward structure; differs because it is the most famous member of the ventral striatum, whereas the striatum proper spans both dorsal and ventral halves.
  • Globus pallidus: Similar in being a subcortical nucleus of the basal ganglia processing motor and reward signals; differs because it is the output-side structure downstream of the striatum, not the input station, and it is not striped in the same way.
  • Substantia nigra: Similar in being a midbrain nucleus intimately connected to the striatum; differs because it supplies the dopamine to the striatum rather than receiving the cortex's plans, and it is named for its dark pigment, not its stripes.
  • Cerebral cortex: Similar in being a forebrain structure involved in planning action; differs because the cortex is the sheet of grey matter at the surface where plans are computed, while the striatum is the deep input station that turns those plans into learned, automatic action.
  • Striated muscle: Similar in sharing the Latin stria ("stripe") and the striped look; differs because striated muscle is skeletal muscle tissue whose banding is visible under the microscope, a completely different structure from the brain's striatum.
