• Prince: Similar because both name a royal son and heir; differs because prince is the general European title, while Tsarevitch is specifically Russian and carries Orthodox and autocratic associations.
  • Cesarevich: Similar because both name a Russian imperial heir; differs because cesarevich was the formal style of the designated successor after 1797, while Tsarevitch in strict use means any tsar's son.
  • Tsar: Similar because both belong to the Russian imperial house and share the same root; differs because the tsar is the ruler himself, while the Tsarevitch is his son, power in waiting rather than in hand.
  • Tsarevna: Similar because both are children of the tsar in the same tales and courts; differs because tsarevna is the daughter, often the prize or the wise helper, while the Tsarevitch is the son and usual quester.
  • Heir apparent: Similar because both designate the next in line to a throne; differs because heir apparent is cold constitutional language, while Tsarevitch is warm with storybook and tragedy.
  • Dauphin: Similar because both name the pampered eldest son of an absolute monarchy; differs because the dauphin belongs to Bourbon France, while the Tsarevitch belongs to Orthodox Russia and its folklore.
  • Crown prince: Similar because both mean the prince destined for the crown; differs because crown prince is the standard modern title worldwide, while Tsarevitch died as a living title in 1918 and now sounds historical.
  • Princeling: Similar because both can mock a small-scale or sheltered young royal; differs because princeling is openly diminutive and often contemptuous, while Tsarevitch keeps dignity even in irony.
