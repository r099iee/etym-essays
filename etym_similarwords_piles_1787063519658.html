• stakes: Similar because both are pointed posts driven into the ground; differs because stakes are usually smaller and used for marking, fencing, or tethering, while piles are massive load-bearing supports.
  • posts: Similar because both are long upright supports; differs because a post is generally set in or on the ground to bear a vertical load and is not necessarily driven deep, whereas piles are driven down through weak ground or water.
  • pillars: Similar because both are vertical supporting members; differs because a pillar is usually of masonry, stone, or formed concrete standing on a foundation, while a pile is a driven stake, typically timber or driven concrete.
  • columns: Similar because both are vertical structural supports; differs because columns are architectural load-bearers of shaped stone or concrete, whereas piles are buried or submerged foundation members.
  • pylons: Similar because both are tall supports; differs because pylons carry cables or mark entry points and are not buried as foundations.
  • piers: Similar because both can support structures over water; differs because a pier is the masonry or concrete structure (or walkway) resting on the bed, while piles are the individual driven stakes a pier may itself stand on.
  • foundations: Similar because both provide structural support; differs because a foundation is the whole engineered base of a building, of which piles are only one (deep-foundation) method.
  • stilts: Similar because both raise a building above the ground or water; differs because stilts are lighter, adjustable legs, whereas piles are heavy supports driven deep into the bed.
  • props: Similar because both are supports holding something up; differs because props are temporary bracing or struts, whereas piles are permanent driven foundations.
