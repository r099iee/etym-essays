• Linger: Similar because both mean staying in a place longer than necessary; differs because linger often implies a reluctant, dreamy, or affectionate slowness (lingering over a farewell), while tarry is a plainer, more deliberate delay, often for practical waiting.
  • Delay: Similar because both mean putting off action; differs because delay is the neutral, everyday verb for postponement of any kind, while tarry is literary and specifically means lingering or waiting in a physical place rather than merely postponing a task.
  • Dawdle: Similar because both involve slowness; differs because dawdle carries contemptuous overtones of laziness, idling, or wasting time, whereas tarry can be a purposeful choice to wait or to stay.
  • Loiter: Similar because both mean staying in a place; differs because loiter has negative, legalistic overtones of idling suspiciously (loitering with intent), while tarry is innocent and often dignified, even biblical.
  • Wait: Similar because both involve remaining in expectation; differs because wait is the plain, universal term for staying until something happens, while tarry adds a literary, often archaic colouring and can mean simply staying without expecting anything.
  • Stay: Similar because both mean remaining in a place; differs because stay is the neutral, all-purpose verb for remaining, while tarry foregrounds the delay or the deliberate prolonging of the stay.
  • Abide: Similar because both mean to remain or to wait and share a solemn, literary register; differs because abide also means "to tolerate" or "to endure" and carries a more biblical, almost legal weight, while tarry is specifically about delaying or lingering.
  • Sojourn: Similar because both involve spending time in a place; differs because sojourn is a noun and verb for a temporary stay in a foreign place, while tarry focuses on the act of delay rather than the length or nature of the visit.
  • Procrastinate: Similar because both involve postponement; differs because procrastinate implies putting off a specific duty out of unwillingness, while tarry suggests innocent delay, lingering, or waiting rather than shirking.
  • Dally: Similar because both mean spending time without urgency; differs because dally implies frivolous, flirtatious, or trifling behaviour, whereas tarry is neutral and often grave or patient.
  • Lag: Similar because both involve falling behind in time; differs because lag is used of progress, speed, or performance (lagging behind schedule), while tarry is used of a person deliberately staying.
