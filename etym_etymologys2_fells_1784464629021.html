Chain for root word fells (landscape sense; plural of fell): PIE *pels- / *pel- "rock, cliff, stone" (reconstructed; debated — see note below) → Proto-Germanic *felis- / *falisa- "rock, cliff" (reconstructed; handbook shapes vary) → Old Norse fjall, later fell "mountain, fell, high rocky ground" (attested in Eddaic verse and sagas, 10th–13th c. CE) → Middle English fel / fell "hill, mountain; high barren ground" (northern dialects, from the 14th c. CE; the loan was carried by Norse settlement of the 9th–11th c.) → Modern English fell (northern landscape term), plural fells (this card) Debated note: the deeper PIE source of the Germanic rock-word is uncertain. Many handbooks connect Proto-Germanic *felis- to a PIE root *pel- / *pels- "rock, cliff, stone," but the cognate set outside Germanic is thin and the reconstruction is not universally accepted; some scholars prefer to label the Germanic term as of uncertain or substrate origin. The English line itself is secure from Old Norse fjall onward. Separate trees: the adjective fell "fierce, deadly," the verb fell "to cut down," and the past tense of fall are unrelated homonyms and must not be merged with this landscape word.
