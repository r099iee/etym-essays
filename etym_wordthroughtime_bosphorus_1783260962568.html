- Ancient Greek geography - the Bosphorus, ox-ford, commemorates the myth of Io, the maiden turned cow who swam the strait, and was known to classical geographers as the narrow water between Europe and Asia.
- Byzantine and Ottoman fame - as the waterway of Constantinople the Bosphorus became the emblem of the imperial city and of the polished race that dwelt by its shores, and control of it a perennial theme of strategy.
- Modern geopolitics - today the Bosphorus remains a living strait of Turkey, governed by the Montreux Convention of 1936, bridged since 1973, and famous in travel and world affairs as the division between Europe and Asia.
