- Deep past — cloth before looms: steppe herders beat wetted wool into mats for tents, rugs, and cloaks, felt before weaving.
- Old English — the craft named: felt already means pressed wool-cloth, workaday wear for common folk.
- Middle Ages — hatters rise: fulled hoods are blocked into hats, and felt begins its long association with headwear.
- 16th–19th c. — beaver and topper: beaver-felt makes fortunes and tall hats, the gentleman's crown from beaten fur.
- 20th c. — industry and schoolroom: billiard tables, pianos, and craft-tables all use felt, humble and ubiquitous.
- Today — yurts to markers: nomads still roof with it while children draw with felt-tips, one ancient mat serving tent and pen.
