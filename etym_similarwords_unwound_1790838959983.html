• Uncoiled: Similar because both refer to releasing something from a spiral or wound shape; differs because 'uncoiled' emphasizes the physical release of spring-like spirals or loops (like a snake or wire spring), while 'unwound' applies broadly to cords, tapes, spools, whips, and psychological tension.
  • Unspooled: Similar because both describe unwrapping material from a cylindrical core; differs because 'unspooled' specifically requires a reel, bobbin, or spool (like film or fishing line), whereas 'unwound' can describe anything coiled around itself or another object.
  • Unraveled: Similar because both describe freeing wound fibers; differs because 'unraveled' often implies structural breakdown, fraying, or disintegration (such as a knitted sweater falling apart or a mystery being deciphered), whereas 'unwound' is an intentional, orderly uncoiling.
  • Unfurled: Similar because both involve opening something rolled or wrapped; differs because 'unfurled' specifically describes opening rolled flat fabrics (such as sails, flags, or banners) to the wind or view.
  • Relaxed: Similar in the psychological sense of releasing tension; differs because 'relaxed' is a generalized emotional or muscular state, while 'unwound' uses the mechanical metaphor of a loosened clockwork spring.
  • Decompressed: Similar in releasing pressure; differs because 'decompressed' is technical (atmospheric/hyperbaric) or corporate jargon, whereas 'unwound' is warm, vivid, and idiomatic.
