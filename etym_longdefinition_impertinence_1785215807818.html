IMPERTINENCE is a noun meaning "insolence, disrespect, or rudeness; the quality of not being pertinent or proper." In the sentence before us — "But it savours almost of impertinence even to attempt to lift the veil on a mystery like this" — the word denotes a presumptuous, rude intrusion: the impropriety of attempting to lift the veil on a private mystery. The tone is grave and protective: the writer implies that certain intimate matters (here, the personal life of John Stuart Mill and Harriet Taylor) lie outside the legitimate scope of public inquiry, so that even trying to uncover them is an act of disrespectful overstepping. The word carries the flavor of moral censure rather than mere discourtesy — the prying is not simply rude but improper, an intrusion into what does not belong to the curious observer.

  • Sense 1 — insolence, disrespect, rudeness (the modern sense): conduct or speech that is presumptuously bold, meddling, or disrespectful toward another person or another's business; the quality of overstepping the bounds of propriety. This is the dominant modern sense: the student's impertinence toward the teacher, the impertinence of a personal question.
  • Sense 2 — intrusion and prying (the sense of the present passage): the improper curiosity or conduct of stepping into matters that are not one's own — meddling, prying, presuming where one has no rightful place. The present sentence treats the attempt to uncover a private mystery as "savouring almost of impertinence": the intrusion into a guarded personal life is the very type of the overstep.
  • Sense 3 — logical irrelevance (the historical root sense): "that which does not pertain to the matter at hand; irrelevance, absurd unsuitability." This was the earliest English sense, inherited from Latin, and it survives today only in formal or technical uses: an impertinent consideration in logic is one that is beside the point; an impertinent detail in a document is one that does not belong to it. The modern "rudeness" sense is a later shift from the idea of not belonging to the matter to the idea of not belonging to one's own business.
  • Sense 4 — a piece of impertinence (countable): "an instance of impertinent conduct or speech," as in what an impertinence!, a piece of impertinence. The noun is used both uncountably (the quality) and countably (the act).
  • Historical bridge: the word is formed with the negative prefix in- (assimilated to im-) + pertinence, from Latin pertinere "to stretch out to, to belong to, to concern." Literally, impertinence is "not pertaining." The semantic history moves from the logical sense (a remark that does not pertain to the subject — irrelevance) to the social sense (conduct that does not pertain to one's own business — meddling, intrusion) to the moral sense (conduct that oversteps propriety — rudeness, insolence). The bridge is the idea of boundary: what does not pertain to the matter or to one's own sphere is, by extension, improper to touch or say.
  • Register and connotation: formal and literary, though somewhat dated in casual speech, where cheek, nerve, sauce, or rudeness often take its place. It is common in reproachful and censorious discourse and in formal descriptions of presumptuous conduct; as the present passage shows, it is also the precise word for the impropriety of prying into guarded private matters.
  • Differentiating terms: impudence (shameless boldness and audacity in speech), insolence (contemptuous, haughty disrespect toward authority), effrontery (brazen cheek with disregard for good manners), rudeness (the general term for discourteous behavior, without the idea of trespass) — impertinence uniquely retains the root sense of "not pertaining": conduct that steps outside the boundaries of one's own business.

Derivatives and form. The adjective impertinent ("insolent, presumptuous; irrelevant"), the adverb impertinently, and the rare noun impertinentness extend the word; the near-homophone impertinent (adjective) and the noun impertinence are pronounced with stress on the second syllable in RP, /ɪmpˈɜːtɪnəns/. The opposite of impertinence is pertinence or relevance on the logical axis, and deference or respectfulness on the social axis.
