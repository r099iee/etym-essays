• Whinnying: Nearest synonym; both name horse vocalizations. Differs because in popular usage whinnying often suggests a softer, higher, shorter, or more affectionate call, while neighing implies a fuller, louder, more resonant cry carrying across distance.
  • Nickering / whickering: Both are horse-made noises. Differs because these are soft, low, closed-mouth equine sounds of greeting or anticipation — far quieter and more intimate than the open, projecting neigh.
  • Braying: Both name equid vocalizations. Differs because braying is species-specific to donkeys, mules, and asses, with a harsher, more raucous timbre; using braying for a horse is a category error.
  • Snorting: Both can occur in equine agitation. Differs because snorting is an expiratory nasal burst without the pitched, voiced contour of a full neigh — one is a call type, the other a breath noise.
  • Squealing: Both are loud equine sounds. Differs because squealing is a sharper, higher, often agonistic or frightened sound, not the resonant call used for contact or recognition.
  • Whinny (noun/verb): Often treated as a synonym of neigh. Differs in aspect and emphasis: neighing as a participle stresses ongoing audible action in a scene, while "a whinny" names a single call.
