PIE (c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*keh₂p-
"to seize, grasp, take"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 1st c. CE)
*hafjaną
"to lift, raise"
↓
Old English (attested 8th–11th c.)
hebban
"to lift up, raise; exalt"
↓
Middle English (c. 1200)
heven
"to lift, raise; throw; utter"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–)
HEAVE
"to lift/throw with effort; to swell"
"informal: to retch"

NOTE: The PIE root *keh₂p- “to seize” (LIV) is the standard reconstruction, with Latin capere and Greek kṓpē "handle" as cognates; the semantic bridge from "seize" to "lift" is grasping a load in order to raise it. The pre-English continental stage *hafjaną is a class VI strong verb; Old English hebban was strong (past hōf, participle hafen), but by Middle English the conjugation was re-levelled to weak (heven, hevede), and only the nautical fossil hove ("hove to") preserves the strong past. Old English heofon "heaven" is probably formed on the same root, "the raised vault," though some dictionaries prefer a different Germanic word for it; the connection is generally accepted but not absolutely certain.
