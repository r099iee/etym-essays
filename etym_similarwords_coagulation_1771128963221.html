• Clotting: Similar because both describe a liquid, especially blood, solidifying; differs because clotting is specific to blood and has a rough, urgent, emergency tone, while coagulation also covers milk, latex, and chemical suspensions.
  • Curdling: Similar because both turn a liquid into solid lumps; differs because curdling is dairy-specific and natural (sour milk), while coagulation is a formal process term of much broader scope.
  • Congealing: Similar because both make a liquid semi-solid; differs because congealing implies solidification by cooling (congealed gravy, set fat), while coagulation involves chemical or biological clumping of particles into a mass.
  • Thickening: Similar because both increase a liquid's density; differs because thickening is a simple, often reversible rise in viscosity, while coagulation is an irreversible phase change into a mass.
  • Solidification: Similar because both are a liquid-to-solid change; differs because solidification is the general term including freezing and setting, while coagulation is a chemically specific clumping of particles.
  • Setting: Similar because both describe a liquid becoming firm; differs because setting usually implies hardening on its own (jelly, concrete), while coagulation stresses particles joining together.
  • Gelation: Similar because both form a semi-solid network; differs because gelation forms a gel throughout the liquid by cross-linking, while coagulation forms clumps or a curd.
  • Flocculation: Similar because both involve dispersed particles clumping; differs because flocculation is the gentle, often reversible aggregation into loose 'flocs', while coagulation makes a dense, irreversible mass.
  • Clabbering: Similar because both describe milk thickening; differs because clabbering is the dialectal word for unpasteurised milk souring and thickening naturally, without the formal scientific register of coagulation.
  • Precipitation: Similar because both separate a solid from a liquid; differs because precipitation forms a crystalline or suspended solid falling out of solution, while coagulation clumps already-present particles into a mass.
