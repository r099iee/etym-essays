- Used from the late 1500s onward for the sound of a stringed instrument being strummed — neutral, technical, tied to music.
- In the 17th–18th centuries extended to any low continuous humming or vibrating sound (drums, wind, throats, later machines) — a descriptive aural word.
- In the 19th century the separate thread-sense surfaces: thrums as loose weaving threads and nautical canvas padding, with "thrumming" meaning to trim or cover with fringes — a craft strand entirely distinct in meaning.
- In the 20th century becomes a staple of descriptive and fantasy prose for deep drone — engines, bass notes, a dragon's breath — atmospheric, slightly archaic, and weighted.
- Today describes any deep low felt vibration — engines, aircraft, loudspeakers, insect wings — both the literal sound and, by extension, a pulsing atmosphere.
