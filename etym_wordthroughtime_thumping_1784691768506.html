- 16th-century coinage - thump is an echoic verb: its voiceless th, short low vowel and closing -mp reproduce the dull, low-frequency impact of a heavy thing falling on a solid surface; it names the heavy, clumsy blow of fist, cudgel or fall
- 18th-19th century extended senses - thump is applied to the heart beating hard with fear or exertion, and to any repeated heavy striking; the word pictures great weight moving without grace
- 19th-20th century British intensive sense - thumping grows a second life as a participial adjective meaning 'very large, emphatic, striking', chiefly in British colloquial use: a thumping majority, a thumping great lie
- Contemporary sense - the physical blow and the emphatic colloquial adjective coexist; thumping still sounds like what it names, a dull resounding percussion of blows
