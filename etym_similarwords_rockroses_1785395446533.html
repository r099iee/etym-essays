• rose (Rosa): Similar because the rockrose's five-petalled, papery flower is the source of the second element of the name and genuinely resembles a wild rose; differs because the rockrose is not a true rose — Rosa belongs to the Rosaceae while Cistus and Helianthemum are in the Cistaceae — and the shrub is evergreen, resinous, and rock-dwelling where the rose is a thorny, often climbing plant of richer soil.
  • sunrose (Helianthemum): Similar because sunrose is the alternative name for the very same plants that horticulture also calls rockrose, sharing genus, flower, and habitat; differs because "sunrose" fixes on the flowers' love of open light while "rockrose" fixes on their rocky home, and the two names are interchangeable in practice.
  • heather / ling (Calluna, Erica): Similar because it is a low, hardy, evergreen shrub of poor, open ground with masses of small flowers; differs because heather evokes damp, acidic northern moorland and purple heath, while rockroses suggest sun-baked southern scrub and calcareous garigue with papery, short-lived blossoms.
  • gorse / furze (Ulex): Similar because it is a tough, sun-loving shrub of poor, rocky, coastal and heathy ground with abundant yellow flowers; differs because gorse is a spiny, leafless, pea-family shrub with coconut-scented yellow flowers and prickly pods, not a rose-flowered Cistaceae.
  • rock cress (Arabis): Similar because it favours the same rocky habitats, hence the shared "rock" element of the name; differs because it is a small, low crucifer (cabbage family) with four-petalled cross-shaped flowers, not a woody, resinous shrub with showy rose-like blossoms.
  • mountain avens (Dryas octopetala): Similar because it is an alpine and northern plant of dry, stony ground with white, rose-like, five-petalled flowers; differs because it is a true rose relative (Rosaceae) — a creeping herbaceous shrub of high mountains — whereas the rockrose is a Mediterranean Cistaceae.
  • cistus / Cistus: Similar because it is the very plant, the botanical genus name of the true rockrose; differs because "cistus" is the technical Latin-Greek name used in botany and horticulture, while "rockrose" is the English common name.
  • mallow (Malva): Similar because it bears single, five-petalled, shallowly cup-shaped flowers on low shrubs or herbs; differs because the mallow is a soft, downy, moisture-loving plant of waysides and meadows, not a resinous evergreen of rocks, and belongs to a different family.
  • thyme (Thymus): Similar because it shares the same sun-baked, rocky Mediterranean habitat and is named beside the rockrose in the passage; differs because it is a tiny-leaved aromatic herb of the mint family, not a flowering shrub, and is valued for scent and flavour rather than blossom.
