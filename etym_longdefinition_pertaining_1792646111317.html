pertaining (present participle and participial adjective) — Having reference, relation, or connection to; being relevant, appropriate, or applicable to a particular subject, person, domain, or sphere of discourse; or belonging to as a legal right, proper accompaniment, attribute, or possession. Derived through Anglo-Norman and Middle English from the Latin verb pertinēre (literally "to reach through, extend to, hold onto"), pertaining functions as an indispensable relational connector in legal drafting, scholarly classification, administrative taxonomy, and grammatical lexicography.

Primary sense — semantic reference, relevance, and thematic relation. In academic writing, forensic disputation, and everyday communication, pertaining (almost universally followed by the preposition to) establishes that a piece of information, evidence, argument, or inquiry bears directly upon the matter under consideration. In this capacity, it qualifies documents, records, data, and discussions that fall strictly within the scope of a designated investigation ("all files pertaining to the merger"). Unlike loose association, pertaining to indicates an intrinsic, substantive relationship governed by logical, operational, or topical relevance.

Sense 2 — grammatical and lexicographical definition formula. In philology, historical linguistics, and dictionary lexicography—the precise context of Donald M. Ayers' citation in the card’s source—pertaining to functions as the universal canonical gloss formula for defining relational and adjectival suffixes. In classical Latin and Greek derivational morphology, suffixes such as -ālis, -āris, -īnus, -icus, and -ārius are formally designated as "relational suffixes" because their primary morphological function is to convert a nominal root into an adjective meaning "of, relating to, or pertaining to" the base noun. Thus, when Ayers explains that the Latin adjective precārius is etymologically "pertaining to prayer" (from prex, stem prec-, "prayer"), he utilizes pertaining to as the standard lexicographical tool to demonstrate how a word originally describing that which is obtained through earnest begging or prayer drifted semantically into "dependent on the will of another," and ultimately into "insecure, risky, precarious."

Sense 3 — legal property rights, appurtenances, and feudal tenure. In real property law, conveyancing, and historical jurisprudence, pertaining describes incorporeal rights, easements, water privileges, mineral access, and structural appendages that legally attach or belong to a principal estate or office (commonly phrased in traditional conveyances as "the lands, tenements, hereditaments, and all rights pertaining thereto"). In this legal sense, the property or right does not exist in isolation, but derives its title and validity from its perpetual attachment to the dominant tenement or sovereign office.

Sense 4 — institutional competence, jurisdiction, and official duties. In administrative and constitutional law, pertaining delineates the boundaries of official authority, executive power, and statutory jurisdiction. Charters and statutes enumerate the specific functions, privileges, and responsibilities "pertaining to the office of the Chancellor" or "matters pertaining to national defense." In this sphere, it serves to prevent jurisdictional overreach by defining the legitimate operational boundaries of state organs.

Sense 5 — philosophical, moral, and natural appropriateness. In classical ethics and literature, pertaining describes qualities, behaviors, dress, and moral decorum that naturally belong to or befit a particular station in life, age, or virtue. Philosophers speak of the "dignity pertaining to human nature" or the "duties pertaining to citizenship," highlighting a moral harmony between an entity’s ontological nature and its outward obligations.

  • In lexicographical definitions — pertaining to [X]: the standard morphological translation formula for relational adjectives derived from nouns (e.g., *lunar* = "pertaining to the moon").
  • In legal conveyancing and property law — rights pertaining thereto, privileges pertaining to the estate: designates appurtenances, easements, and servitudes legally annexed to land.
  • In archival and evidentiary administration — documents pertaining to the inquiry, evidence pertaining to the case: establishes rigorous standards of discovery relevance in civil and criminal litigation.
  • In institutional governance — prerogatives pertaining to the crown, duties pertaining to the presidency: defines the constitutional perimeter of executive authority.
  • Syntactic collocations — overwhelmingly governs prepositional phrases headed by to (pertaining to), occasionally taking adverbial modifiers like strictly, directly, exclusively, or specifically.

Register, tone, and semantic precision. Pertaining occupies a formal, measured register. While colloquial English frequently substitutes simpler prepositions such as about or phrasal verbs like having to do with, pertaining to conveys formal precision, institutional legitimacy, and logical coherence.
