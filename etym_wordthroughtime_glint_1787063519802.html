- Middle English verb - "Glinten" (c. 1400) means "to glance aside, to flash" — likely borrowed from Norse, one of many "g-l-" shine-verbs.
- Verb years - For three centuries glint stays mostly a verb of quick, glancing light in poetry and prose.
- A noun is born - Around 1826 the noun "glint" appears: the momentary flash itself, separated from the action of flashing.
- The Romantic and adventure age - 19th-century prose makes glint a stock word for eyes, swords, waves and above all gold — quick, sharp, exciting.
- Modern figurative use - "A glint of hope", "a glint in his eye": the word extends from light to the smallest trace of feeling or possibility.
- Today - Glint remains vivid and compact: the tiny flash of metal, water or intent, as when Bilbo's light catches the gold in Smaug's hall.
