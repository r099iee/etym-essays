RENDEZVOUS is a frozen French imperative phrase, "rendez-vous" = "present yourselves". Two root words make up the compound; each chain is given separately.

===== CHAIN 1: RENDEZ (from Latin reddere "to give back") =====

PIE *deh3-
"to give" - the root of Latin dare, Greek didomi, Sanskrit da-

▼

Latin dare
"to give" (1st c. BCE)

▼

Latin reddere
"to give back, to return, to restore, to render" - re- ("back", from PIE *wert- via metathetic *wret-) + dare (Classical Latin)

▼

Old French rendre
"to give back, to yield, to surrender, to render, to present" (9th-10th c., from Latin reddere)

▼

Middle French se rendre
"to betake oneself, to go, to present oneself" (reflexive use of rendre)

▼

French rendez-vous
"present yourselves" - imperative plural of se rendre, frozen as a noun meaning "a place of assembly, an appointment" (16th c.)

▼

English RENDEZVOUS
"a meeting at an agreed time and place" - borrowed 1590s, first in the military sense of an assembly point

===== CHAIN 2: VOUS (from Latin vos "you") =====

PIE *wos / *yos
"you" (plural pronoun stem; also source of Latin vos, Gothic jus, Greek hymeis)

▼

Latin vos
"you" (plural; from Imperial times also a respectful singular in formal address)

▼

Old French vos / vous
"you" (plural and polite singular pronoun; 9th c. onward)

▼

Middle French vous
"you" - the pronoun element inside the frozen imperative rendez-vous ("present yourselves")

▼

English RENDEZVOUS
"a meeting at an agreed time and place" - the complete borrowing, ending in the pronoun -vous

Notes: (1) The prefix re- in reddere is generally held to derive from PIE *wert- "to turn", with metathesis producing *wret- - hence "turned back", i.e. "back, again"; this remains the standard but debated account. (2) In French the imperative was originally a genuine plural command ("you (all) present yourselves"); it froze into a noun in the 16th century before English borrowed it.
