Note on historical semantic evolution: The semantic trajectory of plea represents one of the most remarkable transformations in legal lexicography. It originates from the PIE root *pleh₂k- ("flat, smooth"), which evolved in Latin into the idea of smoothing over disagreement—hence placēre ("to please, seem good"). The impersonal passive formula placet ("it pleases, it is resolved") yielded the past participle placitum ("that which has been agreed upon, a decree"). In post-Roman Gaul and the Frankish Empire, the sovereign assemblies where royal decrees were promulgated and disputes settled were designated placita. By the time the word passed through Old French (plait) into Anglo-Norman (plé), it had transformed into the lawsuit itself. In the English common law courts, the term became specialized into the formal answer or defense submitted by the defendant to counter the charge, which eventually broadened into general English as any desperate, heartfelt petition for mercy or assistance.
