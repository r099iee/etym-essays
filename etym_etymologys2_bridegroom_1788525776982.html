Note on Nuance & Folk Etymology: The transformation of Old English guma into modern groom is one of the most famous examples of folk etymology in the English language. When the archaic word gome ('man') fell out of general vernacular use in the late 15th century, English speakers erroneously substituted the familiar word groom ('serving-lad, attendant'), permanently altering the compound's spelling and phonology while preserving its original meaning.
