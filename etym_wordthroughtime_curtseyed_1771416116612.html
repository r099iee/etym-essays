- [16th-17th century courtesy gesture] - Curtsey begins as 'courtesy', the general courtly bow of respect, performed by both sexes, with the woman's version already a dip of the knee.
- [18th-19th century ceremonial peak] - The curtsey becomes a fixed feminine ritual of the ballroom and drawing-room, codified by dancing-masters and etiquette books; graceful, formal and socially required.
- [20th century retreat] - As egalitarian manners spread, the curtsey dwindles to formal royal presentations, theatre and ballet, acquiring an air of old-fashioned ceremony.
- [Contemporary register] - Now largely archaic or deliberately formal and playful; a gesture of the past, or a respectful nod to monarchy, no longer part of everyday politeness.
