• Exiled: Similar because both mean forced (or compelled) absence from one's country; differs because exile can be self-chosen (a voluntary exile) and often implies a continuing connection and the hope of return, while banishment is a formal, punitive casting-out by another's authority.
  • Expelled: Similar because both mean forced out; differs because expulsion is procedural and institutional (expelled from school, from parliament, from a party) and usually of narrower, less tragic scope.
  • Deported: Similar because both mean sent away from a country; differs because deportation is the modern administrative removal of foreign nationals by a state — legal-bureaucratic, without banishment's formal penalty and pathos.
  • Ostracized: Similar because both mean socially cast out; differs because ostracism is informal social exclusion by a community's silent judgment (from the Athenian voting with ostraka, potsherds), whereas banishment is declared and physical.
  • Outlawed: Similar because both mean placed outside the protection of law; differs because outlawry denies legal standing entirely (the outlaw could be slain with impunity), while banishment is a sentence of departure that presupposes the law it applies.
  • Excommunicated: Similar because both are formal castings-out by authority; differs because excommunication is religious — exclusion from the church and sacraments — and spiritual rather than physical.
  • Cast out: Similar because both mean thrown out of a group into rejection or homelessness; differs because 'cast out' is the biblical, communal and often moral frame (casting out demons, cast out of the tribe), less institutional than banishment.
  • Driven out: Similar because both mean forced away; differs because 'driven out' emphasises the pressure and pursuit (mobs, armies, storms) rather than the decree of an authority.
  • Displaced: Similar because both mean removed from a place; differs because displacement is impersonal, mass and modern-sounding (refugees, wartime displacement) — statistical rather than individual and judicial.
  • Dispelled: Similar in the figurative sense because both mean made to leave the mind; differs because dispelling is gentle and gradual (dispelled by daylight, by evidence), whereas banishing is decisive, deliberate and willed.
  • Dismissed: Similar because both mean sent away; differs because dismissal is informal, everyday and non-punitive (a servant dismissed, an idea dismissed), with no exile and no tragedy.
