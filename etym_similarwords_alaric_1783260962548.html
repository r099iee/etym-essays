• Theodoric: Similar because he is the other famous Gothic king in the same passage and shares the same *-reiks 'ruler' name-element; differs because Theodoric was an Ostrogoth who ruled Italy from Ravenna for three decades, whereas Alaric was a Visigoth who sacked Rome and died a wanderer in 410.
  • Attila: Similar because he is the archetypal barbarian conqueror of the later Roman empire; differs because Attila was king of the Huns, an Asiatic nomadic people, and never took Rome, while Alaric was a Germanic (Gothic) chieftain who did.
  • Genseric (Geiseric): Similar because he sacked Rome too — from the sea, in 455; differs because Genseric was king of the Vandals and his sack was far more destructive, while Alaric's Visigothic sack of 410 was relatively restrained and spared the churches.
  • Odoacer: Similar because he was a Germanic warlord who seized Italy and deposed the last western emperor in 476; differs because Odoacer ruled Italy as patrician rather than king and was himself overthrown by Theodoric.
  • Clovis: Similar because he is a fellow Germanic dynasty-founder and 'zealous Christian' king in the same passage; differs because Clovis was a Frank who converted to Catholic Christianity and founded the Merovingian dynasty in Gaul, and he later defeated Alaric II at Vouillé.
  • Alaric II: Similar because he bears the same royal name in the next generation; differs because Alaric II was the Visigothic king in Gaul and Spain who issued the Breviarium Alaricianum (Roman law code, 506) and was slain by Clovis in 507.
  • Radagaisus: Similar because he was a Gothic/Scirian king who invaded Italy before Alaric; differs because Radagaisus was a pagan who was crushed by Stilicho in 406, whereas Alaric was a Christian who reached and sacked Rome.
  • Brennus: Similar because he is the earlier archetype of a barbarian taking Rome (c. 390 BC); differs because Brennus was a Gaulish chief of the ancient world, eight centuries before Alaric, and his sack was plunder without the religious restraint of 410.
  • Alboin: Similar because he is another 'zealous Christian' dynasty-founder named in the same sentence; differs because Alboin was king of the Lombards who conquered northern Italy in 568, more than half a century after Alaric.
