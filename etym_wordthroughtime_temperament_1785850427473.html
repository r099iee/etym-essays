- Late 14th–15th c.: proper proportion, mixture, moderation — material and moral "right balance" readings
- 16th–17th c.: humoral medicine — temperament as the blend of bodily humors determining health, complexion, and character type (sanguine, choleric, etc.)
- 17th–18th c.: moral-psychological essays — temperament as inherent constitution of mind and passions; still linked to physiology
- 19th c.: romantic "artistic temperament"; national character talk; gradual detachment from strict humorology
- 20th c.: psychological and popular personality language — stable emotional disposition; research on infant temperament; adjective temperamental picks up pejorative volatility
- Contemporary: everyday "nature/disposition"; technical residues in music (equal temperament) and historical medicine; perception is of something relatively given, not chosen like character habits
