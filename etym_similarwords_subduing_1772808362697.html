• Conquering: Similar because both mean gaining control over a people or territory by force; differs because "conquer" stresses the act of winning and occupying, while "subdue" stresses the continuing state of being brought under and kept down, including the quieting of resistance.
  • Subjugating: Similar because both mean bringing under domination, literally "under the yoke"; differs because "subjugate" is harsher and more explicitly about permanent political servitude, while "subdue" is broader and can also mean simply quieting or taming.
  • Vanquishing: Similar because both mean defeating decisively; differs because "vanquish" is about winning a single contest or battle, while "subdue" implies the longer process of bringing something under continuing control.
  • Overpowering: Similar because both mean prevailing over by superior strength; differs because "overpower" stresses sheer physical force in the moment, while "subdue" adds the result of obedience or stillness afterwards.
  • Suppressing: Similar because both mean putting down or holding back something; differs because "suppress" implies stifling or stopping (a revolt, an idea, a feeling), while "subdue" implies winning mastery and taming rather than merely silencing.
  • Quelling: Similar because both mean putting an end to resistance or disturbance; differs because "quell" is narrower (rebellions, fears, riots) and suggests crushing the disturbance itself, while "subdue" covers the whole bringing-under process including the aftermath.
  • Mastering: Similar because both mean gaining control or command over something; differs because "master" stresses skill and complete command and is often used of skills or emotions, while "subdue" stresses force and subordination.
  • Taming: Similar because both mean bringing a wild or unruly thing under control; differs because "tame" is used especially of animals and implies domestication and gentling, while "subdue" may be violent and is not restricted to animals.
  • Pacifying: Similar because both mean reducing resistance and restoring calm; differs because "pacify" suggests making peace (often by diplomacy), while "subdue" frankly admits the use of force and conquest.
  • Crushing: Similar because both mean defeating completely and harshly; differs because "crush" is more violent and final, destroying the resistance, while "subdue" implies bringing under control that may be relatively gentle in its results.
  • Reducing: Similar because both mean bringing to a lower state or condition; differs because "reduce" is broader and weaker (reducing someone to obedience, reducing noise), while "subdue" retains the image of active conquest or mastery.
  • Quieting: Similar because both mean making something less loud, active, or intense; differs because "quiet" is purely about diminishing noise or activity, while "subdue" can also mean full conquest, and "subdued" colours or light are merely toned down.
  • Chastening: Similar because both mean disciplining into obedience; differs because "chasten" implies punishment meant to humble, while "subdue" implies control by force or mastery rather than correction.
  • Dominating: Similar because both mean exercising control or superiority over; differs because "dominate" stresses being in command and pre-eminent, while "subdue" stresses the act of forcing the other into subjection.
