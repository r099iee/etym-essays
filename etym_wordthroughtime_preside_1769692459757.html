- 17th c. 'To sit in front of, to protect, to govern' — entered English with the Latin idea of occupying a seat of guardianship or watch over others.

- 18th–19th c. 'To occupy the head of a table or assembly' — becomes the standard term for the host or officer who sits in the position of honor and directs proceedings.

- 20th–21st c. 'To act as chairperson or authority' — institutionalized in courts, meetings, and boards; registers as formal and procedural, suggesting control and supervision rather than protection.
