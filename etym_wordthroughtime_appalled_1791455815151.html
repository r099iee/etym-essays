- 16th c. — "paled, made pale" — the participle describes a physical change of colour; fear is present only as the cause of the pallor.
- c. 1600 — "daunted, dismayed" — the participle becomes an adjective of inner state, and the modern psychological sense is born.
- 17th–18th c. — "shocked morally" — the word moves from street and battlefield into sermon, courtroom and printed polemic.
- 19th c. — "appalling" = "shocking, dreadful" — reformist prose applies it to slums, slavery and factory conditions, turning the adjective into an instrument of social indictment.
- 20th c. — "appalling" = "very bad" — colloquial British usage weakens the word; "appalling weather" is a complaint rather than a moral crisis.
- 21st c. — "I'm appalled" — a ready-made public stance in news comment and social media, signalling shared values as much as private feeling.
