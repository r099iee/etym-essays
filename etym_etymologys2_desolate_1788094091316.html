dēsōlāre
"to leave alone, abandon, lay waste"

↓
Latin (1st c. BCE–1st c. CE)
dēsōlātus
"abandoned, deserted, laid waste"
(past participle)
↓
Middle English (mid-14th c.)
desolat
"lonely, forsaken; laid waste"
↓
Modern English (16th–17th c.)
desolate

Note: The ultimate origin of Latin sōlus is debated: the traditional derivation from the PIE reflexive *se- ('self' → 'by oneself, alone') is plausible but not proven, and no certain cognate outside Italic is agreed. English took the adjective directly from Latin as a learned loan of the 14th century, though Old French desolé reinforced the later verb.
