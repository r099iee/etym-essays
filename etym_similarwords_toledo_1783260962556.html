• Toletum: Similar because it is the same city under its Latin name, and Toledo is its direct descendant; differs because Toletum is the ancient Roman form attested in the sources, while Toledo is the modern Spanish and English form shaped by Arabic and Romance change.
  • Ṭulayṭulah: Similar because it is the same city under its Andalusian Arabic name, in use during the Islamic period (8th–11th centuries); differs because the Arabic form belongs specifically to the Muslim phase of the city's history and has its own phonological shape.
  • Castile: Similar because Toledo was the early capital of Castile and the heart of the kingdom; differs because Castile is the region and kingdom (named for its castles, castillos), not the single city.
  • Tagus / Tajo: Similar because the city is set in the great loop of this river, which served as its natural moat; differs because the name is that of the river, not the city, and the two are distinct toponyms.
  • Toledo steel / blade: Similar because the city's fame made its name a metonym for fine swords, and the phrases literally begin with the place-name; differs because they name the product (the steel or the sword), not the place.
  • Visigothic: Similar because Toledo was the capital of the Visigothic kingdom, so the two names always appear together in history; differs because Visigothic names the people and period, while Toledo is the specific city.
  • School of Translators: Similar because this celebrated institution was located at Toledo and is inseparable from the city's medieval fame; differs because the term names the circle of scholars and the translation project, not the city.
  • El Greco: Similar because the painter is inseparable from Toledo, which he made his home and subject; differs because the name (literally "the Greek", from the artist's Cretan origin) designates the man, not the place.
  • Madrid: Similar because both are historic capitals of Spain, and Madrid displaced Toledo as the court city in 1561; differs because Madrid is the modern capital and a vastly larger metropolis, while Toledo is the older, now provincial seat.
  • Segovia: Similar because it is another great historic city of Old Castile with Roman aqueduct and medieval monuments; differs because Segovia never held Toledo's roles as Visigothic capital, translation centre, or primatial see.
