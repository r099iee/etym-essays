- 4th–8th c. (Gothic *haiþnō* / Old English *hǣðen*) - Rural paganism & Christianization: Coined by early Germanic translators to render Greek *ethnikos* / Latin *paganus*, designating rural dwellers of the heathlands who clung to the Old Gods.
- 9th–15th c. (Old & Middle English) - Doctrinal othering & Holy War: Applied indiscriminately by medieval European Christendom to Norse Viking raiders, Saracens, Mongols, and non-Christian adversaries.
- 16th–19th c. (Colonial & Victorian Era) - Imperial civilizing mission: Deployed by European colonizers and missionaries to classify indigenous peoples across the globe as unchristian souls requiring salvation and subjugation.
- 20th c. - Domestic & humorous insult: Diluted into a mild, colorful colloquialism for individuals (especially children) lacking refined manners, domestic order, or church attendance.
- Late 20th–21st c. - Modern Neopagan reclamation: Reclaimed with pride as an authentic self-appellation by modern practitioners of Heathenry and Germanic polytheistic reconstructionism.
