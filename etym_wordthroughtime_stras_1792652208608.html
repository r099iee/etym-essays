- [Vedic & Pre-Classical Era (c. 1500–600 BCE)] — verbal root *śās-* functioned as an active verb of divine and royal commanding and ritual instruction — [perception: revered as sacred divine injunctive authority and cosmic ordering]
- [Classical Codification & Sūtra Age (c. 500 BCE–500 CE)] — developed into a distinct literary genre of authoritative treatises, manual codes, and textbooks across law, statecraft, drama, and philosophy — [perception: celebrated as the supreme intellectual authority and essential curriculum for kings, judges, and scholars]
- [Medieval Scholastic Era (c. 6th–17th c.)] — interpreted through layered scholastic commentaries (*bhāṣya*), serving as the theoretical framework for feudal administration and temple culture — [perception: revered as sacred, timeless canon regulating society, ethics, and metaphysics]
- [Colonial British Encounter (c. late 18th–19th c.)] — transliterated as *shaster* / *shastra* by Orientalists (Jones, Halhed); codified into rigid "Anglo-Hindu Law" by British colonial courts — [perception: viewed by Europeans as the mysterious Hindu equivalent of biblical legal statutes]
- [Modern Academic & Vernacular Period (20th–21st c.)] — transformed across South Asia into the universal scientific suffix (*-śāstra*) for academic disciplines (physics, economics, sociology) — [perception: admired both as a sacred repository of classical Indian civilization and as the foundational linguistic vehicle for modern scientific knowledge]
