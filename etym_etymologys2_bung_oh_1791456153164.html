Scots (early 19th c.)
bung
"to throw, fling, sling"

↓
British slang (1921)
bung-ho!
"a drinking toast"

↓
British slang (1923–1934)
bung-ho / bung-oh
"a farewell, cheerio"

↓
bung-oh
"so long; cheers"

Note: the derivation from the verb bung “to throw” is the proposal recorded by Green’s Dictionary of Slang; other dictionaries label the compound’s origin simply as uncertain. The “stopper” noun bung, from Middle Dutch bonghe, is a different word and is not the documented source. The particle ho is an old exclamatory call, as in heave-ho and tally-ho.
