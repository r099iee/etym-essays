- Late 14th to 15th Century - Legal & Formal Register: Specifically used in English common law to describe contracts (indentures) executed on parchment and severed along an undulating or zigzagging line resembling teeth so that matching the physical cuts authenticated the agreement.
- 16th to 17th Century - Physical & Natural Expansion: Extended into geographical and biological descriptions, characterizing jagged coastlines with sheltered coves, notched botanical leaf margins, and heraldic borders.
- 18th to 19th Century - Typographical & Cartographical Standardization: Formalized as a precise technical term in printing and publishing for setting text inward from a margin, while continuing as standard geographical parlance for navigable, harbor-rich coastlines.
- 20th to 21st Century - Computational & Digital Hierarchy: Widely adopted in software engineering and document processing for indented lines of nested code and outline structures, retaining its foundational sense of deliberate inward displacement.
