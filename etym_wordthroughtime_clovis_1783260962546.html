- Late-medieval historical tradition - Clovis appears in Frankish and French historiography as the founder of France: the first barbarian king of the West to embrace Catholic, Nicene Christianity, baptized at Reims on Christmas Day about 496.
- 16th-19th-century development - as historians compared the Germanic dynasties, Clovis came to stand for the pivotal conversion of the West, the type of the convert-king who founds both a dynasty (the Merovingians) and a nation (France) in the baptismal font.
- Contemporary sense - the name is recognized chiefly in historical writing and is understood as the older form of Louis and Ludwig; the Clovis who united the Franks is now remembered less for his massacres than for the crown, the baptism, and the birth of France.
