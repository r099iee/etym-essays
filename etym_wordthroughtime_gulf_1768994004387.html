- Entered English in the 14th century via French from Greek kolpos, "bosom, fold, hollow" — at first a deep cavity or engulfing hollow, even the pit that swallows the dead.
- By the 15th century it was mapped onto the sea as a large bay nearly closed by land, a basin of water with shores that holds its depth like a hollow in the earth.
- In the 18th and 19th centuries it gained its great figurative power: the unbridgeable distance between people, classes, or views — "the gulf between rich and poor."
- Today it serves both duties at once: a geography vast enough to defeat the torchbeam, and the widest word we have for separation.
