• Wharf: Similar because both are structures for loading and unloading ships; differs because wharf is a broad, often large and industrial term for any such structure built along or at an angle to the shore, while quay is specifically a solid, continuous embankment parallel to the water's edge.
  • Pier: Similar because both extend into the water for mooring; differs because a pier projects outward on pillars or piles with water flowing beneath, while a quay is a solid retaining wall running along the shore.
  • Dock: Similar because both are places where ships berth; differs because dock technically names the enclosed area of water between wharves or piers (though popularly the structures themselves), while quay names the land platform itself.
  • Jetty: Similar because both are harbour structures at the water's edge; differs because a jetty is a projecting structure that breaks waves or protects a harbour mouth, while a quay is built along the shore for cargo and passenger handling.
  • Landing stage: Similar because both are places where passengers and goods come ashore; differs because a landing stage is often a light or floating structure for small boats, while a quay is a solid, permanent embankment.
  • Landing place: Similar because both are spots where boats put in; differs because a landing place is a generic, often informal term for any point of disembarkation, while quay names a specific engineered structure.
  • Embankment: Similar because both are raised banks of earth or stone along water; differs because an embankment is built mainly to hold back or confine water (a river bank or causeway), while a quay is built specifically as a platform for ships and cargo.
  • Waterfront: Similar because both denote the land immediately beside water; differs because waterfront is the whole district or stretch of land along a body of water, while a quay is the specific paved landing structure within it.
  • Harbour: Similar because both belong to the world of shipping; differs because a harbour is the sheltered body of water for ships, while a quay is the built landing platform on its edge.
  • Mole / breakwater: Similar because both are solid stone constructions at the water's edge; differs because a mole or breakwater is built to shelter a harbour from the sea and often has no cargo-handling role, while a quay is built for loading and unloading.
  • Slipway: Similar because both are waterfront places where boats are serviced; differs because a slipway is an inclined ramp for launching or hauling boats, while a quay is a horizontal platform alongside the water.
  • Staith: Similar because both are landing places for bulk cargo; differs because a staith is a specific coal-loading structure on northern English rivers such as the Tyne, while quay is the general word.
