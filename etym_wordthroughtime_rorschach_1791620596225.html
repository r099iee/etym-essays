- 1921 - Coined as a medical eponym upon the publication of Hermann Rorschach's Swiss psychiatric monograph Psychodiagnostik; perceived as a radical, experimental diagnostic tool for analyzing perception and schizophrenia.
- 1930s–1950s - Imported into the United States (via David Levy, Samuel Beck, and Bruno Klopfer); rapidly elevated into the premier projective test of mid-century psychoanalysis, acquiring an aura of clinical authority and subconscious mystery.
- 1960s–1970s - Broadened beyond clinical psychology into public intellectual essays, literary criticism, and political commentary; transformed into a popular metaphor for any ambiguous stimulus onto which people project their personal ideologies.
- 1980s - Solidified in popular culture as an icon of psychological mystery and moral ambiguity, prominently embodied by the inkblot-masked antihero in the graphic novel Watchmen.
- 1990s to Present - Subjected to intense empirical debate (the 'Rorschach Wars') regarding psychometric validity and scoring standardization (Exner Comprehensive System), while simultaneously thriving as an indispensable cultural idiom for subjective interpretation in media and politics.
