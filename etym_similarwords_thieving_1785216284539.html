• Stealing: Similar because both name the act of taking what is not yours; differs because "stealing" is the neutral umbrella term, while "thieving" is blunter, more habitual, and more openly contemptuous.
  • Pilfering: Similar because both denote petty, sneaky theft; differs because pilfering is specifically small, repeated pilferage, whereas thieving is the broader, blunter word for the vice in general.
  • Filching: Similar because both describe sly, furtive taking; differs because filching is lighter and more playful — to snatch something small and slip away — without the moral weight of thieving.
  • Purloining: Similar because both mean wrongful taking; differs because purloining is an elevated, literary, or euphemistic word, whereas thieving is plain and everyday.
  • Embezzling: Similar because both are forms of dishonest taking; differs because embezzlement is the misuse of money entrusted to one's care (a white-collar crime), not the stealthy theft of a thief.
  • Robbing: Similar because both mean taking illegally; differs because robbery is open, confrontational seizure by force or threat, while thieving is typically stealthy and non-violent.
  • Burgling: Similar because both are property crimes of theft; differs because burglary involves unlawful entry into buildings, a specific location element that thieving does not have.
  • Poaching: Similar because both are furtive, illegal taking; differs because poaching is domain-specific — illegal hunting or taking of game and fish — not general theft.
  • Shoplifting: Similar because both are forms of petty theft; differs because shoplifting is location-specific (stealing from shops) and nearly clinical, without thieving's habitual, sneaking flavour.
  • Nicking / Pinching / Swiping: Similar because all are informal slang for stealing; differs because these are light, jocular colloquialisms, whereas thieving is the plain, serious, morally loaded word.
  • Plundering: Similar because both are wrongful taking; differs because plundering is large-scale, violent seizure (in war or disorder), the opposite of the sly, small-scale act that thieving names.
  • Appropriating / Misappropriating: Similar because both mean taking something not one's own; differs because these are formal, bureaucratic words for taking over or diverting resources, without the sneaking, habitual connotation of thieving.
