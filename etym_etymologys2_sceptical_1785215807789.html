Greek (c. 5th c. BCE)
σκέπτομαι (sképtomai)
"to consider, examine, look about"
↓
Greek (c. 3rd c. BCE)
σκεπτικός (skeptikós)
"inquisitive; of the Skeptics"
↓
Latin (2nd–3rd c. CE)
scepticus
"of the Skeptics; a skeptic"
↓
English (16th c.)
sceptic (also skeptic)
"a doubter; a follower of the Skeptics"
↓
English (17th c.)
sceptical
"inclined to doubt; disbelieving"

Debate note: British sceptic keeps the Latinised spelling <sc> (Latin scepticus), while American skeptic restores the Greek <k>, as in σκέψις (sképsis) "inquiry, doubt". The -pt- is identical in both spellings, reflecting the Greek π of σκέπτομαι.
