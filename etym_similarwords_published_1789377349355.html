• Promulgated: Similar because both denote making a law, decree, or announcement known to the general public; differs because promulgated specifically implies formal state, legal, or religious decree that gives a law binding, operative authority, whereas published applies universally to literature, journalism, software, and everyday media.
  • Issued: Similar because both refer to releasing something into circulation or making it available; differs because issued often focuses on the formal administrative release of documents, licenses, currency, or uniforms, whereas published emphasizes the act of making content readable, accessible, and openly communicated.
  • Disseminated: Similar because both involve spreading information or ideas to a wide audience; differs because disseminated suggests scattering information broadly like seed (often through informal, oral, or decentralized channels), whereas published implies a deliberate, formal, and often centralized act of editorial release.
  • Proclaimed: Similar because both signify public declaration; differs because proclaimed emphasizes oral, public announcement with fanfare and sovereign authority (as by a royal herald), whereas published usually relies on written, printed, or recorded media.
  • Circulated: Similar because both describe passing information among many people; differs because circulated highlights the movement and transmission of a document from person to person within a group, whereas published marks the definitive initial act of releasing it to the public domain.
  • Printed: Similar because both are closely linked to the physical production of text on paper; differs because printed denotes the mechanical process of applying ink to paper (which may remain entirely private, confidential, or unpublished), whereas published denotes the act of releasing that material to the public.
