• Regretted: Similar because both express sorrow over a past act. Differs because “regret” is purely personal disappointment — possibly selfish — while “repent” implies moral acknowledgment and, religiously, amendment of life.
  • Rued: Similar because both mean “bitterly regretted”. Differs because “rue” is archaic, poetic and emotional, while “repented” is the standard formal word.
  • Deplored: Similar because both express strong disapproval of something done. Differs because “deplore” is usually directed at an external state of affairs one condemns, not at one’s own fault.
  • Lamented: Similar because both involve sorrow over a loss or act. Differs because “lament” emphasizes open weeping and mourning, while “repent” emphasizes inward remorse and, in the religious sense, the will to change.
  • Felt remorse / was remorseful: Similar because remorse is exactly the emotion repentance names. Differs because “remorse” describes the feeling alone, while “repent” (older and fuller) includes the turning-from — the change of mind and the turn to amendment.
  • Had second thoughts: Similar because both describe wishing an action undone. Differs because this idiom is casual, lightweight and often intellectual (reconsideration), while “repented” carries moral or spiritual weight.
  • Recanted: Similar because both involve giving up a previously held position. Differs because “recant” means formally withdrawing a statement or belief, especially a public or heretical one; “repent” is about sorrow for deeds.
  • Atone(d): Similar because in religious idiom both belong to the cycle of sin and reconciliation. Differs because “atone” names the act of making amends (at-one-ment), where “repent” names the internal sorrow that should precede it.
  • Relented: Similar because both can mark a softening after opposition. Differs because “relent” concerns giving way to mercy or persuasion, not remorse for one’s own deeds.
  • Changed his mind: Similar because the deepest etymology of “repent” (Latin re- + paenitere; Greek metanoia) is literally a change of mind. Differs because the modern plain phrase is neutral and ordinary, stripped of the sorrow and moral seriousness that “repent” retains.
