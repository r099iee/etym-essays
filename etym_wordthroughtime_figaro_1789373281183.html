- Late 18th Century (1775–1789) - Coined by Beaumarchais for his rebellious valet; perceived as an inflammatory, subversive symbol of Third Estate defiance against feudal privilege.
- 1786 & 1816 - Immortalized by Mozart and Rossini; perceived universally as a joyous, sparkling comic hero of European high culture and vocal virtuosity.
- 1826–Late 19th Century - Adopted as the title of Paris's premiere satirical weekly and later daily newspaper; perceived as an elegant, razor-witted forum for cultural gossip, political debate, and Belle Époque literature.
- 20th Century - Evolves into France's dignified centre-right broadsheet newspaper of record; also enters colloquial French and English as a playful synonym for a barber.
- Contemporary Era - Recognized globally both as the flagship conservative French media brand (*Groupe Figaro*) and as one of Western opera's greatest titular characters.
