• Muted: Similar because both describe sound that has been softened and reduced in intensity; differs because muted can be an intentional artistic restraint or a mechanical dampener (such as a trumpet mute), whereas muffled specifically suggests sound passing through an absorbent, obstructive medium like cloth, snow, or a wall.
  • Dampened: Similar because both involve reducing acoustic vibrations or energy; differs because dampen is a physics/acoustic term for absorbing resonance and vibration, whereas muffle emphasizes wrapping or enclosing with soft physical materials.
  • Deadened: Similar because both imply taking the sharpness and vibrancy out of sound or pain; differs because deadened suggests complete loss of vitality, numbness, or total sonic absorption (soundproofing), whereas a muffled sound is still audible as a dull, indistinct thud or murmur.
  • Smothered: Similar because both involve wrapping or covering to suppress output; differs because smothered implies choking, suffocating, or totally extinguishing a sound, flame, or life, whereas muffled simply alters acoustic clarity.
  • Swaddled: Similar because both involve wrapping someone tightly in cloths or garments; differs because swaddled refers specifically to wrapping infants or sick persons securely in bands of cloth for warmth and immobility, without the primary acoustic connotations of muffle.
  • Shrouded: Similar because both involve concealing something under a covering; differs because shrouded carries solemn, ghostly, or funerary associations (winding sheets for corpses, veils of fog), whereas muffled focuses on physical warmth, disguise, or acoustic deadening.
  • Stifled: Similar because both involve holding back or suppressing expression; differs because stifle refers to internally checking or choking back an emotional outburst (such as stifling a laugh, yawn, or sob), whereas muffled describes the physical sound barrier.
  • Cloaked: Similar because both describe concealing someone or something under an outer layer; differs because cloaked emphasizes visual concealment and strategic secrecy, while muffled emphasizes wrapping around the face/body or acoustic suppression.
