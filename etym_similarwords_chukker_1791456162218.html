• chukka: similar because it is the same polo period and the usual British spelling, especially in chukka boot; differs because it is simply a spelling variant of chukker.
  • period: similar because it is a generic timed division of a game; differs because it is the neutral superordinate term, with no equestrian or Indian flavour.
  • quarter: similar because it is a timed segment in American football, basketball and other sports; differs because of its fixed length and its different sporting conventions.
  • half: similar because it is one of two main divisions of a match; differs because it is much longer than a chukker and divides the game into two rather than six.
  • inning: similar because it is a division of a cricket or baseball game; differs because it is defined by outs or batting turns rather than by a fixed clock.
  • set: similar because it is a division of a tennis or volleyball match; differs because it is defined by games or points and has no fixed duration.
  • heat: similar because it is a single race or round in athletics, swimming or horse racing; differs because it is a qualifying contest rather than a segment of one match.
  • round: similar because it is a stage in boxing, a tournament or a negotiation; differs because of its wider figurative range and its origin in circular motion rather than in polo.
  • turn: similar because it is a period of action or a player’s opportunity; differs because it is far more general and lacks any fixed sporting length.
  • stint: similar because it is a fixed period of work or duty; differs because of its labour context and its implication of effort rather than play.
  • spell: similar because it is a short period of activity, especially in British usage; differs because it is informal, indefinite and not tied to a clock.
  • session: similar because it is a period of an activity or meeting; differs because of its administrative, educational or therapeutic contexts.
  • bout: similar because it is a short period of intense activity, especially boxing; differs because of its suggestion of struggle and its lack of a fixed time limit.
  • chakkar: similar because it is the Hindi source word for a circle, turn or round; differs because it is the etymon rather than an English match term, and because of its much wider everyday meanings.
  • chakra: similar because it is a Sanskrit cognate meaning “wheel, circle”, familiar in yoga; differs because of its religious and anatomical sense, and it does not name a period of play.
