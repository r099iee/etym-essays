• Monarch: Similar because both denote a supreme ruler; differs because monarch stresses singleness of rule (Greek mónos "alone" + árchein "to rule"), while sovereign stresses supremacy and the absence of any higher power, and is also used of states and coins.
  • King: Similar because both denote a hereditary ruler of a realm; differs because king is a specific rank of male monarch, while sovereign is the generic word for supreme authority and can be applied to queens and, in theory, to any supreme power.
  • Queen: Similar because both denote a female monarch; differs because queen is a specific royal rank, while sovereign names the office of supreme authority itself, regardless of the monarch's sex.
  • Ruler: Similar because both denote one who governs; differs because ruler is the plain, generic term for anyone in authority, from a chief to a president, without the majesty and legal absoluteness of sovereign.
  • Emperor: Similar because both denote a supreme ruler; differs because emperor rules an empire and claims a higher imperial dignity, while sovereign is the constitutional term for final authority in a state.
  • Potentate: Similar because both denote a powerful ruler; differs because potentate is a dignified and slightly exotic word, often applied to foreign or Eastern rulers, without the precise legal content of sovereign.
  • Autocrat: Similar because both denote a ruler holding absolute power; differs because autocrat stresses power exercised without check or counsel, while sovereign stresses supreme lawful authority which may be constitutional.
  • Despot: Similar because both denote a supreme ruler; differs because despot carries a pejorative sense of arbitrary tyranny, while sovereign is a neutral, legal, and dignified term.
  • Dynast: Similar because both denote a hereditary ruler; differs because dynast emphasizes membership in a ruling family across generations, while sovereign emphasizes the office of supreme power itself.
  • Overlord: Similar because both denote one who holds authority over others; differs because overlord implies a feudal relation of dominance over subordinate lords, while sovereign is the supreme independent authority.
  • Suzerain: Similar because both denote a higher power over subordinate states; differs because suzerain is a technical term for a dominant state with nominal control, while sovereign implies complete and absolute supremacy.
