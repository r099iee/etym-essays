• Sins: Similar because both denote moral wrongdoing and transgressions against ethical or spiritual standards; differs because sins primarily emphasize religious transgressions that violate divine commandments and incur spiritual guilt before God, whereas vices describe ingrained, habitual character flaws and psychological dispositions that can be evaluated in secular, moral, or social contexts.
  • Flaws: Similar because both refer to imperfections that mar an individual's character or performance; differs because flaws is a broader, gentler term encompassing accidental weaknesses, errors in judgment, physical defects, or cognitive limitations, whereas vices specifically denotes morally culpable, corrupting habits and destructive indulgences.
  • Foibles: Similar because both characterize shortcomings in human behavior; differs because foibles refers to minor, harmless eccentricities, quirks, or amusing personal weaknesses (such as vanity about one's hair or punctuality obsession) that provoke affectionate smiles rather than moral condemnation, whereas vices involve genuine ethical harm, depravity, or physical self-destruction.
  • Depravities: Similar because both describe severe departures from moral goodness; differs because depravities represents an extreme, monstrous degree of wickedness, perversion, and utter moral rot that shocks the conscience, whereas vices can range from everyday indulgences (smoking, gambling) to standard ethical failings.
  • Iniquities: Similar because both convey gross injustice and moral corruption; differs because iniquities carries a heavy biblical, prophetic, and legal connotation of systemic unfairness, heinous crimes, and oppressive wickedness, whereas vices focuses more on personal habits, lifestyle choices, and internal character defects.
  • Weaknesses: Similar because both denote areas where an individual lacks moral resistance or fortitude; differs because weaknesses highlights the vulnerability, fragility, or lack of willpower that allows temptation to overcome a person, whereas vices emphasizes the actively practiced, ingrained habitual behavior resulting from that surrender.
  • Transgressions: Similar because both designate violations of established moral boundaries; differs because transgressions refers to discrete, individual acts of overstepping a boundary, rule, or law (viewed as isolated events), whereas vices denotes the continuous, permanent, and habituated internal state of character that generates those acts.
