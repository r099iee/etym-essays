Greek pauo (παύω) "to make cease, to bring to a halt, to stop" — the PIE ancestry is uncertain (Beekes classes the verb as of uncertain origin, possibly expressive); the chain therefore begins at the attested Greek verb [attested from Homer, c. 8th century BCE] > Greek pausis (παῦσις) "a stopping, a cessation, a pause" — verbal noun in -sis [attested from the 5th century BCE] > Latin pausa "a pause, a halt, a stop" — a Greek loanword [Latin, 1st century BCE onward] > Latin pausare "to halt, to rest, to cease" — denominative verb of late and Christian Latin [Late Latin, c. 4th century CE onward] > Late Latin repausare "to cause to rest thoroughly, to give rest to" — intensive re- + pausare [Late Latin / early medieval Latin] > Old French reposer (verb) "to rest"; repos (noun) "rest, repose" [Old French, c. 10th-12th century] > Middle English reposen (verb) and repos (noun) "rest, stillness" [Middle English, c. 1400] > Modern English repose "a state of rest, ease, or peaceful inactivity" [from c. 1400 onward] HOMONYM NOTE (a different word): a second English repose "to place, to lay (as in 'to repose trust in someone')" descends from Latin reponere "to place back" (re- + ponere, past participle repositus), not from pausare; the two verbs converged on the same spelling in French and merged in English orthography, but they remain etymologically distinct and must not be merged.
