• Headstrong: Similar because both mean self-willed and hard to control; differs because headstrong stresses rash, impulsive self-will and heedless action, whereas wilful adds the note of deliberation — the wilful person knows what they are doing and does it anyway.
  • Obstinate: Similar because both mean stubbornly fixed; differs because obstinate describes an immovable refusal to change one's mind even against reason, while wilful emphasizes the positive act of choosing to follow one's own desire.
  • Stubborn: Similar because both mean resistant to persuasion; differs because stubborn is blunter and more animal — mule-like — while wilful insists on the conscious, deliberate choice of the will.
  • Deliberate: Similar because both mean intentional; differs because deliberate is neutral and factual, while wilful carries moral blame — the deliberate act performed in defiance of duty.
  • Intentional: Similar because both mean done on purpose; differs because intentional is the general, neutral word, while wilful adds the sense of deliberate obstinacy or culpable knowledge.
  • Wayward: Similar because both mean turning from the proper path; differs because wayward implies perverse, unpredictable turning aside, while wilful implies steady, deliberate self-direction.
  • Self-willed: Similar because both are literally "full of one's own will"; differs because self-willed is a transparent synonym of wilful's core sense, while wilful also carries the legal sense of deliberate, knowing intent.
  • Determined: Similar because both describe a person fixed on a course; differs because determined is neutral or admiring, while wilful carries disapproval and suggests defiance of others' wishes.
