Root word NOSING, one linear derivation chain:
1. PIE *nas- "nose" (prehistoric reconstruction; the near-universal human word for the nose, attested in many branches).
2. Proto-Germanic *nasō "nose" (reconstructed).
3. Old English nosu "nose" (attested from the 8th c.).
4. Middle English nose (noun) "nose" (attested 13th c.).
5. English nose (verb), 16th c., "to smell, scent" — a denominal verb formed from the noun (attested c. 1570s).
6. English to nose about / nose (into), 17th c., "to pry, search about inquisitively" (attested 17th c.).
7. English NOSING (present participle) "prying, sniffing, or searching intrusively" — as in "slinking and nosing about."

Note on the debate: the PIE root *nas- is well established (cognates include Latin nāsus, Greek rhī́s with an odd initial, Sanskrit nāsā, Lithuanian nósis, Old Church Slavonic nosъ); the Sanskrit form passed through the Prakrits into Hindi nāk (नाक) and Odia nāka, descendants of the same root. The verb is securely denominal, derived from the English noun nose rather than inherited from Germanic. Derivatives: nosy, "inquisitive," recorded from the 1880s; to nose out, "to discover by scent," from the 19th century.
