- Mid-17th Century (1630s–1660s): Celebrated Amsterdam master portraitist, historical painter, and printmaker - Reconstructed from the Germanic baptismal name Rembrandt; renowned across the Dutch Republic and European princely courts for dramatic biblical narratives and lifelike civic guard group portraits (e.g. The Night Watch).
- Late 17th to 18th Century: Controversial Dutch realist criticized by French and academic classicists - Regarded ambivalently by Neoclassical critics like Roger de Piles, who admired his color and light but condemned his refusal to follow idealized Greco-Roman anatomical proportions, viewing his rugged realism as vulgar.
- 19th Century (Romantic and Impressionist Era): Re-canonization as the supreme painter of the human soul and light - Celebrated by Delacroix, Van Gogh, and European Romantics as an independent, solitary genius whose expressive brushwork and deep empathy elevated the human condition above academic academicism.
- 20th to 21st Century: Global cultural icon, artistic archetype, and technical lighting standard - Universally synonymous with pinnacle artistic mastery, psychological depth, and the eponymous 45-degree triangle lighting technique ubiquitous in cinematography and digital photography.
