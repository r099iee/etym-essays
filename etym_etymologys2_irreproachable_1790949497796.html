Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*ne / *n̥- (negation) + *pro- (forward, near)
"not" + "forward, before"
↓
Latin (c. 1st c. BCE)
in- + re- + prope
"not" + "back" + "near, close"
↓
Vulgar Latin (c. 6th–8th c.)
*repropiare
"to bring near, hold against, cast back"
↓
Old French (c. 1150–1300)
reprochier / reprochable
"to blame, censure / deserving blame"
↓
Middle French (c. 1500)
irréprochable
"faultless, blameless, above censure"
↓
Early Modern English (c. 1590s–present)
irreproachable
"beyond criticism, blameless, faultless"
