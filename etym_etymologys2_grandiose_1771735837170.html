Latin grandis "big, great, full-grown, abundant" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE, Plautus) ├── pre-Latin origin UNCERTAIN: no secure PIE etymon is reconstructed; the OED marks the origin obscure; an old speculation links it to a base meaning "grown" (kin to "grow"), but the evidence is thin │ └── Italian grandioso "full of grandeur, magnificent" (grande + -oso "full of") │ └── French grandiose "magnificent" (borrowed from Italian, 16th c.) │ └── English grandiose (borrowed c. 1840, in the admiring sense; later acquiring the pejorative sense of inflated, overweening grandeur, including the psychiatric phrase "grandiose delusions") NOTE (debate): because no PIE etymon for grandis can be established with confidence, the honest chain begins at Latin itself and makes no forced Indo-European claim; the derivation of English grandiose from Italian via French is not in dispute.
