• plain: Similar because both name flat, open, low country; differs because a plain is defined by the absence of relief — a flat expanse with nothing rising from it — whereas a lowland is defined by relative elevation and may include marshes, river flats, and rolling ground: a whole region, not a single feature.
  • valley: Similar because both are low land lying between heights; differs because a valley is a linear depression between hills, usually with a river running along it, whereas a lowland is a broader, open stretch of sunken country without that confinement.
  • basin: Similar because both denote low-lying land; differs because a basin is a depression drained by a river system, a geological rather than visual category, whereas lowland is a landscape category seen as level, habitable ground.
  • marshland: Similar because both are low, flat, wet ground; differs because a marsh is waterlogged and undrainable, often with reeds and standing water, whereas a lowland need be only low in relation to its surroundings and is typically habitable, farmed country.
  • flat / flats: Similar because both name level land; differs because flats are strictly level expanses, often tidal or alluvial, while lowland is a comparative term that includes gently rolling ground, not only flatness.
  • bottoms: Similar because both name low, low-lying land; differs because bottoms is American dialect for the low-lying land along a river (river bottoms), a narrower, local usage, whereas lowland is the general, systematic geographic term.
  • champaign: Similar because both name open, level country; differs because champaign is an archaic, poetic word for open plain, whereas lowland is the standard modern geographic term with no archaic colour.
  • delta: Similar because both can be low, flat land near water; differs because a delta is specifically the fan of alluvium at a river mouth, formed by deposited silt, whereas a lowland is a broad region defined only by low elevation.
  • meadow / mead: Similar because both can name low, grassy ground; differs because a meadow is a field of grass, typically mown for hay, a small agricultural feature, whereas a lowland is a whole region of low country.
  • low country / Low Countries: Similar because both name low-lying national or regional ground; differs because the Low Countries is a fixed historical proper name for the Netherlands and Belgium, while lowland is the general geographic term for any low-lying region.
