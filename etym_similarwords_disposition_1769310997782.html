• Temperament: Similar because both name a person's innate mental and emotional constitution; differs because temperament stresses the emotional and reactive side (the "mix" of moods), while disposition emphasises the habitual, settled cast of character.
  • Temper: Similar because both can name the prevailing emotional state; differs because temper is narrower and more volatile — it usually points to anger-management ("a violent temper") or a passing frame of mind, whereas disposition is the durable character.
  • Character: Similar because both describe the moral and mental make-up of a person; differs because character is largely shaped by values, principles, and conduct, while disposition is more natural, constitutional, and temperamental.
  • Nature: Similar because both can name the inborn quality of a person or thing; differs because nature is the broadest term (everything intrinsic, including instincts and physical constitution), while disposition picks out the ordered arrangement of temperament.
  • Make-up: Similar because both name the composite of a person's qualities; differs because make-up is informal and analytic (the "parts" one is made of), while disposition is formal and describes the overall settled character.
  • Constitution: Similar because both can name the body-and-mind make-up of a person; differs because constitution is primarily physical (health, physique), while disposition is primarily mental and emotional.
  • Personality: Similar because both describe the distinctive set of a person's characteristics; differs because personality is the modern psychological term for the whole self, while disposition emphasises the underlying temperament.
  • Mood: Similar because both can name a state of mind; differs because mood is temporary and fluctuating, while disposition is a settled, long-term condition.
  • Inclination: Similar because both can name a leaning toward something; differs because inclination is a specific, directed preference or impulse, while disposition is the general underlying temperament that produces such leanings.
  • Tendency: Similar because both can name a habitual leaning; differs because tendency is neutral and used of things and events as well as persons, while disposition is more characterological.
  • Propensity: Similar because both name a natural inclination; differs because propensity is narrower and often negative or statistical ("a propensity for violence"), while disposition is broader and morally neutral.
  • Predilection: Similar because both name a leaning; differs because predilection is a conscious liking or partiality for something, while disposition is an innate orientation.
  • Penchant: Similar because both name a strong inclination; differs because penchant is for a specific taste or activity, while disposition is the general cast of character.
  • Proclivity: Similar because both name a natural inclination; differs because proclivity is a formal, often statistical or moral leaning, while disposition describes everyday character.
  • Bent: Similar because both name a natural leaning; differs because bent is colloquial and specific (a bent for music), while disposition is general and formal.
  • Humour (as in "in good humour"): Similar because both can name a prevailing state of mind; differs because humour, in this older sense, is a passing frame of temper, while disposition is the standing arrangement of character.
  • Arrangement: Similar because both name the ordering of things in space; differs because arrangement is a neutral, general word for layout, while disposition in the spatial sense implies deliberate, strategic, or systematic placing.
  • Deployment: Similar because both name the positioning of forces; differs because deployment is specifically the strategic spreading of military resources, while disposition is the resulting arrangement, whether military, spatial, or legal.
  • Layout: Similar because both can name the arrangement of physical things; differs because layout is concrete and modern (buildings, pages), while disposition is formal and can be abstract.
  • Disposal: Similar because both derive from the same Latin stem and can name the settling of property; differs because disposal stresses the act of getting rid of something, while disposition stresses the arrangement or settlement of an estate or matter.
  • Settlement: Similar because both can name the legal finalising of an estate or claim; differs because settlement is a broad legal and financial term, while disposition names the specific act of transferring or arranging property.
