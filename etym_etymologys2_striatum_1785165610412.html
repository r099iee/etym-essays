Chain for striatum:

  • Proto-Indo-European *streig- / *strig- "to stroke, rub, graze; to draw a line" (reconstructed, per Pokorny; the deeper assignment of stria is uncertain).
  • Latin stringere "to draw tight, touch, graze" (attested 1st century BCE; the traditional cognate of stria; also in strictus → English strict).
  • Latin stria "a furrow, groove, flute of a column" (attested 1st century BCE; pre-Italic etymology uncertain).
  • Latin striatus "furrowed, grooved, striped" (adjectival derivative, classical–post-classical).
  • Neo-Latin corpus striatum "the striped body" (anatomical name, first used in the 17th century).
  • Modern scientific English striatum — the full phrase contracted to the bare neuter noun (19th–20th centuries).

Note: Latin stria has no certain pre-Italic etymology; its assignment to the *strig- root follows Pokorny's grouping and the traditional association with stringere, "to graze, draw lines." The 17th-century coinage corpus striatum is well documented in anatomical Latin.
