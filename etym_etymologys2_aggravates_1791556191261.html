Proto-Indo-European (c. 4500 BCE)
*gʷerh₂-
"heavy, weighty"
↓
Proto-Italic (c. 1000 BCE)
*grawis
"heavy, burdensome"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
gravis
"heavy, weighty, severe, serious"
↓
Classical Latin (1st c. BCE)
aggravare
"to make heavier, add weight to, oppress"
↓
Late Latin (4th c. CE)
aggravatus
"weighed down, burdened, made worse"
↓
Anglo-French / Middle English (late 15th c.)
aggravaten
"to increase the gravity or burden of"
↓
Modern English (16th c.–present)
aggravates
"worsens, intensifies; irritates, annoys"

Note: Built from Latin prefix ad- ('to, toward', assimilated to ag- before g) + gravare ('to weigh down', from gravis 'heavy'). Cognate with Greek barys ('heavy', as in barometer) and Sanskrit guru ('weighty, venerable teacher').
