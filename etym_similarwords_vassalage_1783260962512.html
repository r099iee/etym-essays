• Servitude: Similar because both denote subordination to another's authority; differs because servitude is the general condition of service without feudal ceremony, while vassalage implies a legal, oath-bound relation.
  • Bondage: Similar because both are states of subjection; differs because bondage goes further into unfreedom — the bondsman is not bound by the reciprocal oaths a vassal honours.
  • Serfdom: Similar because both are feudal conditions of dependence; differs because the serf is tied to the land itself, without the honourable mutual contract of vassalage.
  • Subjection: Similar because both mean subjugation under a superior power; differs because subjection is a plain, political word for being under authority, lacking the medieval, oath-bound colour of vassalage.
  • Subordination: Similar because both denote a lower position in a hierarchy; differs because subordination is a neutral fact of rank that may be honourable, while vassalage specifically names the feudal bond of tenure and homage.
  • Dependence: Similar because both denote reliance on another; differs because dependence is general and may be innocent or economic, while vassalage carries the institutional and historical weight of feudalism.
  • Subjugation: Similar because both denote being brought under another's control; differs because subjugation is achieved by force and implies conquest, while vassalage rests on sworn, reciprocal obligation.
  • Enslavement: Similar because both denote loss of autonomy; differs because enslavement means the complete loss of freedom, while a vassal remains a free man bound by oath and service.
  • Thralldom: Similar because both denote a state of being bound to a master; differs because thralldom is the condition of a thrall or bond-slave, without the honourable feudal contract of vassalage.
  • Clientage: Similar because both denote a dependent relation to a more powerful patron; differs because clientage is the Roman and modern patron-client relation, lacking the military service and homage of vassalage.
  • Fealty: Similar because both belong to the feudal bond between lord and tenant; differs because fealty is the sworn oath of loyalty itself, while vassalage is the whole state or tenure that the oath secures.
  • Suzerainty: Similar because both describe hierarchical relations of power; differs because suzerainty is the dominant lord's authority over a subordinate state, the upper side of the relation, while vassalage is the subordinate side.
