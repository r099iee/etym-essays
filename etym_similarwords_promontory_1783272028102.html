• Headland: Similar because both name a point of high land projecting into the sea; differs because headland is the plain, everyday nautical term with no implication of great elevation, whereas promontory emphasizes height, steepness, and a dramatic, cliff-like jut.
  • Cape: Similar because both are projections of land into water; differs because a cape is usually a broad, navigable point of land that turns the coastline (e.g. Cape of Good Hope), while a promontory stresses elevation and steep drop-offs rather than the coast's turning.
  • Peninsula: Similar because both are land nearly surrounded by water; differs because a peninsula is a large landmass connected to the mainland by a narrow isthmus, whereas a promontory is a high, relatively small point jutting from a larger landmass.
  • Cliff: Similar because both involve steep rock faces by the sea; differs because a cliff is the vertical face itself, which need not project into water, while a promontory is the projecting high point of which a cliff may be one side.
  • Bluff: Similar because both are steep, elevated landforms near water; differs because a bluff is a broad, often river-side bank or headland with a rounded face, while promontory implies a sharp, seaward-pointing projection.
  • Point: Similar because both are land that juts into water; differs because point is the ordinary word for a small projection of any height, while promontory implies a high, dramatic one.
  • Foreland: Similar because both are coastal projections of land; differs because foreland is chiefly British, formal, and often flatter, while promontory stresses height and steepness.
  • Ness / head: Similar because both are old dialect or regional words for a jutting coastal point; differs because ness and head are archaic or localized (Scots, Irish) terms, whereas promontory is the standard learned geographical word.
  • Spur: Similar because both are projections from a larger mass; differs because spur is used of mountains or ribs of rock on land, without the coastal-water association that defines promontory.
  • Projection / prominence: Similar because both name anything that juts out; differs because projection and prominence are abstract and generic, applicable to any protuberance, while promontory is specifically the high coastal or anatomical point.
