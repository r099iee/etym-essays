Proto-Indo-European (c. 3000 BCE)
*lāt- / *leh₁t-
"pole, beam, lath, stake"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE)
*laþþō
"thin board, slat, lath"
↓
Old High German / Frankish (c. 7th c.)
*latta / latta
"lath, thin wooden strip"
↓
Old French (c. 12th c.)
late (lath) → lateiz
"interlaced work of laths,"
"screen of crossed wooden strips"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
latis / latys
"screen of crossed wooden laths"
↓
Modern English (c. 16th c.)
lattice
"framework of crossed slats;"
"regular periodic grid or array"

Note: English lattice entered from Old French lateiz, a collective noun derived from late ('lath, wooden slat'), which was borrowed from Frankish *latta (cognate with Old English lætt, Modern English lath, and German Latte).
