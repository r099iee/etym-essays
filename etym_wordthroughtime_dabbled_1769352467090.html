- [Literal sense (16th c.)] - Entered English meaning "to wet by gentle, repeated splashing"; ducks dabbling in ponds give the word a light, playful, unhurried register. - [Figurative extension (17th c.)] - Gained the sense of engaging in an activity casually, without seriousness or commitment (to dabble in politics); the word keeps its light, amateur, non-committal colouring. - [Contemporary sense] - Both senses coexist: literal, gentle play with water and figurative, superficial involvement in a subject — always implying lightness, lack of depth, and a casual, informal tone.
