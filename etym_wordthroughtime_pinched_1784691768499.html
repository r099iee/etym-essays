- Middle English physical sense - to pinch is to squeeze and nip something hard between thumb and finger, or between two surfaces; a gesture of admonition, playfulness or mere force
- 16th-17th century extended senses - pinch spreads to want and cold: to be pinched with frost, a face pinched thin by hunger or worry, and the penny-wise pinch-penny miser; the word takes on a drawn, sharpened, tightened cast
- 17th-18th century slang sense - in the demotic speech of the streets pinch comes to mean to steal, to lift something furtively and quickly as a pickpocket does, and by the 19th century a 'pinch' is also a police arrest
- Contemporary sense - the physical squeeze and the slang sense of theft and arrest both persist; pinched still pictures a quick, deft manual snatch, the work of nimble fingers rather than of planning
