• Trinidad: Similar because it is the larger sister island forming the unitary state of Trinidad and Tobago; differs because Trinidad is a large, industrialized, continental landmass with oil and gas industries, whereas Tobago is smaller, volcanic/coral, tranquil, and primarily tourism- and conservation-oriented.
  • West Indian: Similar because Tobago is an integral part of the West Indies; differs because 'West Indian' is a broad regional term covering all archipelagos of the Caribbean basin.
  • Antillean: Similar because Tobago belongs to the Lesser Antilles island chain; differs because 'Antillean' is a wide geographical classifier for the entire Caribbean archipelago from Cuba down to Grenada.
  • Windward Islands: Similar because Tobago is geographically situated at the southern terminus of the Windward group; differs because 'Windward Islands' is a regional grouping encompassing Dominica, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent, and Grenada.
  • Archipelago: Similar because Tobago is part of an island chain; differs because an 'archipelago' is a general geographic term for any cluster or group of islands worldwide.
  • Islet: Similar because both denote oceanic landmasses; differs because an 'islet' is a very small, typically uninhabited rocky outcrop, whereas Tobago is a major inhabited island of 300 square kilometers.
  • Cayo: Similar because both are Caribbean insular landforms; differs because a 'cayo' (cay or key) is a low-elevation sandbank or coral reef island (like the Florida Keys), whereas Tobago features a dramatic volcanic ridge and ancient rainforest.
