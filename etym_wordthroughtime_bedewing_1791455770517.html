- Old English – bedēawian: “to moisten with dew,” a plain agricultural and poetic verb.
- Middle English – bedewen: used of dawn, flowers, and tears; gentle, natural moisture.
- 16th–17th c. – literary flourish: poets bedew brows, cheeks, and altars; the verb becomes a marker of elevated style.
- 18th–19th c. – poetic and biblical: “bedewed with tears” is a stock image, and the word grows archaic in prose.
- 20th–21st c. – rare and literary: survives mainly in poetry, historical fiction, and humorous overwriting (as in “persp. bedewing my brow”).
