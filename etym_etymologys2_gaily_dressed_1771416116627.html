GAILY
↓ Frankish (reconstructed)
*gāhi
"quick, impetuous"
↓ Old French (12th c.)
gai
"joyful, bright"
↓ Middle English (13th c.)
gay
"joyful, bright, lively"
↓ English (14th c., gay + -ly)
gaily
"in a bright, cheerful manner"

DRESSED
↓ PIE (reconstructed)
*h₃reǵ-
"to rule, straighten"
↓ Latin (classical)
regere
"to rule, guide, straighten"
↓ Latin (classical)
dirigere
"to direct, arrange,"
"set in line"
(dis- "apart" + regere)
↓ Old French (12th c.)
dresser
"to arrange, set up,"
"straighten"
↓ Middle English (14th c.)
dressen
"to arrange, put in order,"
"clothe"
↓ English (past participle)
dressed
"clothed, attired"

↓ English (compound: gaily + dressed)
gaily-dressed
"dressed in a bright, colorful,"
"cheerful manner"

Debate: The origin of Old French gai is uncertain; it is possibly from Frankish *gāhi "quick," and is also compared with Old High German gāhi.
