• before: Similar because both mean "earlier in time" or "ahead in place," and afore is in origin the older dialectal double of before; differs because before is the modern standard word used in all registers, while afore is archaic or dialectal, carrying a rustic and old-fashioned colour.
  • ere: Similar because both mean "before" and both are now nonstandard in everyday speech; differs because ere is a much older, different word (Old English ǣr "earlier, before") confined to poetry and archaism, while afore is a compound of a- + fore that still lives in dialects and fixed legal and nautical phrases.
  • prior to: Similar because both express "earlier than something"; differs because prior to is formal and Latinate, belonging to official and bureaucratic prose, while afore is homely, spoken, and old-fashioned.
  • previously: Similar because both mean "before now, at an earlier time"; differs because previously is neutral, formal-adverbial English of the modern standard, while afore is dialectal and archaic and always adds a rustic or legal flavour.
  • formerly: Similar because both can mean "at an earlier time"; differs because formerly emphasizes a state that existed in the past and no longer does, while afore (like before) is a general temporal preposition and adverb.
  • earlier: Similar because both indicate a point in time before the present or before another event; differs because earlier is the neutral modern comparative adverb, while afore is the archaic prepositional/adverbial fossil.
  • aforetime / erstwhile: Similar because both are old-fashioned words meaning "in former times"; differs because aforetime is built on afore itself and is purely archaic, while erstwhile is a different (Germanic erst-based) word meaning "formerly, once."
  • aforementioned / aforesaid: Similar because all begin with or contain afore and all refer to something stated previously; differs because aforementioned and aforesaid are frozen legal-adjectival forms of afore, whereas afore itself remains an independent preposition and adverb.
  • in front of: Similar because both can mean "ahead of, before in position"; differs because in front of is the modern, fully productive spatial phrase, while afore's spatial use survives chiefly in the nautical fossil afore the mast.
  • erewhile: Similar because both are archaic words for "before, previously"; differs because erewhile is purely poetic and rare, while afore, though archaic in standard English, is still a living dialect word in parts of Britain and America.
