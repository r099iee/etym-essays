Hebrew Conduit Name - The name Shiloah in the Hebrew Bible denotes the aqueduct and its pool near Jerusalem, literally "the sending [of waters]," an ancient hydraulic waterwork beside the city.
Septuagint and Vulgate Forms - The Septuagint renders the name Silōám and the Vulgate Siloë/Siloa, so the word enters Christian scripture and liturgy with a sacred, geographical register, tied to the healing pool of John 9.
English Bible & Milton - English Bibles standardize Siloam, while the older Latin-derived Siloa is used by Milton in Paradise Lost as the emblem of Judeo-Christian inspiration, the gently flowing brook by the Temple.
Contemporary Sense - Today Siloam is the biblical and historical name of the Jerusalem pool (and the modern Silwan quarter), while Siloa survives mainly as a poetic form from Milton's verse.
