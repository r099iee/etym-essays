• Mitigate: Similar in signifying the reduction of harshness, severity, or painful effects; differs because mitigate (from Latin mitis, "soft/mild") implies actually making something gentler, milder, or less severe in reality, whereas palliate often carries the nuance of superficial concealment, excusing, or temporarily easing symptoms without curing the root pathology.
  • Alleviate: Similar in denoting the lightening of physical or emotional suffering; differs because alleviate emphasizes lightening a heavy burden (from Latin levis, "light"), carrying an intrinsically positive, therapeutic connotation without the undertone of evasion, disguise, or cosmetic spin inherent in moral or political uses of palliate.
  • Extenuate: Similar in the context of diminishing the perceived gravity of an offense or wrongdoing; differs because extenuate (from Latin tenuis, "thin") specifically refers to offering mitigating circumstances or excuses that make a crime seem smaller or less blameworthy in legal/ethical judgment, whereas palliate can apply equally to physical pathologies, social evils, and moral faults.
  • Assuage: Similar in seeking to calm or lessen intensity; differs because assuage focuses on soothing emotional turbulence, grief, hunger, or fear into a state of tranquility and sweetness (from Latin suavis, "sweet"), lacking medical or cosmetic cloaking connotations.
  • Gloss over: Similar in concealing defects or faults under a deceptively smooth exterior; differs because gloss over is an informal, idiomatic expression emphasizing deceptive superficiality and quick dismissal, whereas palliate is formal, academic, and encompasses genuine clinical symptom relief.
  • Cloak: Similar because both trace back directly to covering with a mantle; differs because cloak is primarily literal or purely deceptive (hiding completely from view), while palliate involves moderating the impact, effect, or perception of something already partially acknowledged.
  • Moderate: Similar in bringing an extreme condition toward a temperate middle ground; differs because moderate focuses on restraining excess or maintaining equilibrium rather than disguising a defect or relieving painful suffering.
  • Mollify: Similar in reducing agitation; differs because mollify specifically targets pacifying an angry or hostile person by softening their temper rather than mitigating an abstract fault or physiological symptom.
