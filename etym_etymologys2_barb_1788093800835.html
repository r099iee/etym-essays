PIE (reconstructed)
*bʰardʰ-éh₂
"beard, bristle"
↓
Latin (attested from 3rd c. BCE)
barba
"beard; any beard-like
point or tuft"
↓
Old French (9th–13th c.)
barbe
"beard; point or barb
of an arrow"
↓
Middle English (c. 1300)
barbe
"beard (of man or beast);
barb of a spear"
↓
English (1580s)
barb
"backward-pointing spike of
an arrow or fish-hook"
↓
English (modern)
BARB
"hooked spike; feather branch;
a stinging remark"

Note: The PIE reconstruction is secure in meaning ("beard") but debated in form: most handbooks give *bʰardʰ-, some *bʰars-, and the -s-/-dʰ- variation among the cognates (Latin barba, Old English beard, Old High German bart, Welsh barf, Lithuanian barzdà, Russian borodá) is the reason. The weapon sense developed from the beard metaphor and is already visible in Old French; the English arrow sense is dated to the 1580s.
