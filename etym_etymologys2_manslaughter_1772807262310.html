MAN (first element)
PIE (c. 4000 BCE; conjectured)
*mon-
"man, human being"
(the PIE root of man is uncertain)
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*mann-
"person, human being"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
mann
"person, human being"
↓
English (12th c.)
man
"a person; an adult male"

SLAUGHTER (second element)
↓
PIE (c. 4000 BCE; uncertain)
*slak-
"to strike"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*slahaną
"to strike, hit"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE – 200 CE)
*slahtą
"a killing, slaughter"
↓
Old Norse (9th–14th c.)
slátr
"butcher's meat, slaughtered meat"
↓
English (14th c.)
slaughter
"the killing of people or animals"

COMPOUND
↓
English (14th c.; man + slaughter)
manslaughter
"the unlawful killing of a person without malice aforethought"

Debate: The PIE ancestry of man is disputed; PGmc *mann- is secure, but *mon- is only conjectural. Slaughter also merged with the native Old English cognate slēaht "slaughter" alongside the Old Norse loan slátr.
