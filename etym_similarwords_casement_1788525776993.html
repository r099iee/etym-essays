• window: Similar because a casement is a specific type of window aperture that admits light and air; differs because window is a generic umbrella term covering all glazed or unglazed wall openings, whereas casement specifically denotes a side-hinged sash that swings open like a door.
  • sash: Similar as a movable glazed framework within a window; differs because in standard modern usage a sash (or sash window) refers to a frame that slides vertically or horizontally on tracks or counterweighted cords, rather than swinging on hinges.
  • fanlight: Similar as a glazed decorative window unit; differs because a fanlight is a small semicircular or rectangular transom window positioned above a doorway, designed to illuminate a hallway rather than swing open for full ventilation.
  • transom: Similar as a hinged window unit; differs because a transom window is set above a door or larger window and typically pivots horizontally on a top or bottom hinge (hopper style), whereas a casement hinges vertically at the side.
  • dormer: Similar as a window structure in historic architecture; differs because a dormer is a vertical roof projection containing a window that projects from a sloping roof, which may itself contain a casement or a sash.
  • oriel: Similar as an ornate window style in medieval and Tudor buildings; differs because an oriel is a projecting bay window cantilevered out from an upper floor supported by corbels, serving as an architectural structure rather than the hinged mechanism itself.
  • embrasure: Similar as an opening associated with historic window reveals; differs because an embrasure is the flared, beveled opening or recess in a thick masonry wall (originally for archers or cannons), not the glazed wooden frame.
  • lattice: Similar because historic casements frequently featured diamond-mesh leaded glass or wooden framework; differs because lattice refers strictly to the criss-crossed diagonal woodwork or lead caming pattern, not the hinged opening sash.
  • shutter: Similar as a hinged barrier attached to a window opening; differs because a shutter is typically a solid or louvered wooden/metal panel designed for security, light blocking, or storm protection, lacking glass glazing.
