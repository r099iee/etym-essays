Root 1 — TINGED:

PIE (uncertain)
*teng-
"to soak, dye"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
tingere
"to dye, soak, tinge"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
tinctus
"(past participle) dyed, tinged"
↓
English (15th c.)
tinge
"to dye, color slightly"
↓
English (16th c.)
tinged
"slightly colored or influenced"

Debate: the PIE root *teng- "to soak, dye" is uncertain; tingere may instead be of non-PIE origin. English tinge comes from Latin tingere, while the participle tinctus underlies tincture.

↓
Latin (prefix)
in-
"not"
↓
English (16th c.)
imperceptible
"not perceptible; impossible to"

Debate: imperceptible was formed in English (or via French imperceptible) as in- "not" + perceptible, ultimately from Latin percipere "to take wholly, perceive" (per- "through" + capere "to take"). The chain is secure.

Root 4 — DRAFT:

PIE (uncertain)
*dʰrāǵ- / *dʰregʰ-
"to draw, drag"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE–500 CE)
*draganą
"to draw, carry"
↓
Old English (8th–11th c.)
dragan
"to draw, drag"
↓
Middle English (13th c.)
draught
"something drawn; a current of air,"
"a drawing, a plan"
↓
English (16th c.)
draft
"a small current of air"
"a drawing, a plan"

Debate: draft is simply the -f- spelling of draught, the older standard form. The noun draught derives from Old English *dreaht / *dræht, the participle-based noun of dragan "to draw", which also underlies drag and draw.
