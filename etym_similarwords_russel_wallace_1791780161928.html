• Charles Darwin: Similar because both were preeminent 19th-century British naturalists who independently conceived and co-published the theory of evolution by natural selection; differs because Darwin came from great personal wealth, formulated his theory over two decades in England, published On the Origin of Species, and held a strictly materialistic view of human mental evolution, whereas Wallace was a self-funded commercial collector who deduced natural selection in the tropical wilderness of Ternate, founded evolutionary biogeography, and maintained heterodox beliefs in spiritualism and social reform.
  • Thomas Henry Huxley: Similar because both were stalwart Victorian defenders and popularizers of evolutionary theory; differs because Huxley was primarily an institutional comparative anatomist, paleontologist, and combative London public polemicist ("Darwin's Bulldog"), whereas Wallace was primarily an itinerant field collector, tropical naturalist, and geographical theorist.
  • Joseph Dalton Hooker: Similar because both were pioneering Victorian naturalists and key figures in the presentation of evolutionary theory at the Linnean Society; differs because Hooker was a botanical taxonomist and institutional administrator who served as Director of the Royal Botanic Gardens at Kew, whereas Wallace worked as an independent field explorer and zoologist.
  • Henry Walter Bates: Similar because both were working-class English self-taught naturalists who co-founded tropical field biology during their joint 1848 expedition to the Amazon Basin; differs because Bates focused on entomology and formulated the theory of defensive mimicry in butterflies (Batesian mimicry), whereas Wallace extended his theoretical insights across global biogeography, island speciation, and social philosophy.
  • Ernst Mayr: Similar because both were foundational evolutionary biologists and biogeographers who revolutionized our understanding of geographic speciation and island biology; differs because Mayr was a 20th-century architect of the Modern Evolutionary Synthesis integrating genetics and population statistics, whereas Wallace established classical 19th-century organismal biogeography without knowledge of Mendelian genetics.
  • Jean-Baptiste Lamarck: Similar because both proposed comprehensive mechanisms for the transmutation of species across geological time; differs because Lamarck proposed the inheritance of acquired characteristics and an innate vitalist drive toward complexity, whereas Wallace identified natural selection acting upon preexisting variations as the driving mechanism of adaptation.
  • Peter Kropotkin: Similar because both were 19th-century naturalist-explorers who studied wildlife in remote regions (Wallace in the tropics, Kropotkin in Siberia) and drew profound egalitarian social conclusions from their observations; differs because Kropotkin was a Russian revolutionary prince and philosopher who formulated the theory of anarcho-communism and mutual aid, whereas Wallace was a British biologist and socialist land reformer.
