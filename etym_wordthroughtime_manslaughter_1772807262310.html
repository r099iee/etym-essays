- 13th-14th century - literally 'the slaughter of a man'; early English law begins distinguishing the deliberate, premeditated murder from other homicides
- 15th-17th century - the legal category stabilizes: unlawful killing of a human without malice aforethought, punishable more leniently than murder
- 18th-19th century - subdivided into voluntary and involuntary manslaughter as doctrine develops
- Contemporary - a formal statutory offense that marks both gravity and a doctrinal gap below murder, a gap that wealth and influence can still exploit in practice
