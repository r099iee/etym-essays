• Mooring posts: Similar because both take ships’ lines ashore; differs because mooring post is the plain descriptive phrase, while bollard is the technical harbour word with its mushroom-headed shape.
  • Bitts: Similar because deck bitts belay the same lines as quay bollards; differs because bitts are paired shipboard timbers or steel, while bollards stand ashore or on deck as single stout posts.
  • Cleats: Similar because both secure ropes by friction turns; differs because a cleat is the smaller horned fitting for yachts and jetties, while a bollard is the heavy post for ships and quays.
  • Piles: Similar because both are driven timbers at the water’s edge; differs because piles are the foundation rows carrying stages and piers, while bollards are the finished mooring furniture mounted upon them.
  • Pillars: Similar because both are uprights that catch light at night; differs because a pillar bears weight upward to roofs and lamps, while a bollard takes strain sideways from ropes and wheels.
  • Posts: Similar because every bollard is a post; differs because post is the bare general word, while bollard specifies squat maritime or traffic strength.
  • Bumpers: Similar because both stop vehicles by mass; differs because bumpers ride on the vehicle, while bollards stand in the ground and refuse to move.
  • Barriers: Similar because both divide safe from dangerous ground; differs because a barrier spans and closes, while bollards punctuate — they guard without blocking feet.
  • Capstans: Similar because both handle heavy mooring lines on quays; differs because a capstan turns to haul, while a bollard stands fast to hold.
