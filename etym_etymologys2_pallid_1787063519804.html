PIE
(c. 4500–2500 BCE)
*pel-
"pale, grey, dusky"
↓
Proto-Italic
(c. 1000–400 BCE)
*palēō
"to be pale"
↓
Latin
(classical period)
pallēre
"to be pale, to turn pale,
to look wan"
↓
Latin
(classical period)
pallidus
"pale, wan, colourless,
greenish"
↓
English
(1580s, borrowed)
pallid
"pale, lacking colour"
↓
English
(modern)
pallid
"very pale; wan; lacking
vigour or life"

Note: the same root *pel- appears in Greek polios "grey, hoary", Old English fealu "fallow, pale" and Sanskrit palita- "grey". The Latin line (*pel- → *palēō → pallēre → pallidus → English pallid) is secure and uncontroversial.
