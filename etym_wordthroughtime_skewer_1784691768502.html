- Ancient & pre-industrial sense - the skewer is the smaller cousin of the spit: a short pin, cut from a green twig or forged of metal, that holds meat together, closes slits and threads small pieces for grilling; among the most ancient of cooking implements
- Medieval & early modern kitchen sense - skewers are everyday kit of the cook, pinning stuffing in place, lacing joints to the spit and building kebab-like dishes; wooden and metal skewers sit beside the hearth
- 19th-20th century figurative extension - the word grows a sharp figurative life: to skewer is to impale an opponent or argument with a stabbing attack, and a 'skewering' criticism becomes standard in journalism and commentary
- Contemporary sense - the literal grilling pin is as common as ever (kebabs, satay), while the vivid modern sense of 'skewer' as to demolish an argument with a single pointed thrust is now perhaps the more energetic use
