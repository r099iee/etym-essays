• Steward: Similar because both are senior household officers managing staff and supplies. Differs because the steward traditionally managed the whole estate, finances, and accounts and could outrank the butler; the butler’s domain is the house proper, the wine, and the dining room.
  • Valet: Similar because both are trusted male servants in a great house. Differs because the valet attends the master’s person, clothes, and dressing; the butler runs the household’s male service, not the master’s body.
  • Footman: Similar because both serve in a grand household under the same service hierarchy. Differs because the footman is a junior servant who waits at table, runs errands, and attends carriages; the butler commands the footmen and serves wine.
  • Manservant: Similar because both are male domestic servants. Differs because manservant is the generic word for any male servant; butler is the specific senior rank.
  • Major-domo: Similar because both are chief stewards of a household (Italian maggiordomo). Differs because major-domo suggests a large Mediterranean or royal establishment and is more formal and rare; butler is the English household word.
  • Maître d’hôtel: Similar because both manage dining and service. Differs because the maître d’ runs a public restaurant’s dining room; the butler runs a private household’s.
  • Housekeeper: Similar because both are senior domestic officers. Differs because the housekeeper is traditionally female and controls the female servants, linen, and cleaning; the butler controls the male staff, wine, and silver.
  • Cup-bearer: Similar because both served and guarded the wine, and the butler was historically a cup-bearer. Differs because cup-bearer is the ancient and biblical office (often ceremonial, occasionally treacherous); butler is the later English institutional title.
  • Seneschal: Similar because both are household officers of rank. Differs because the seneschal was a medieval steward of a whole estate or castle, with judicial powers; the butler stayed within the house and the cellar.
  • Chamberlain: Similar because both are senior officers of a great household. Differs because the chamberlain managed a royal or noble’s chambers, finances, and ceremonies; the butler is confined to domestic service and wine.
  • Retainer: Similar because both are long-serving household servants. Differs because retainer is a legal-historical term for any servant kept in a household’s employ, of any rank; butler is the specific chief manservant.
  • Servant: Similar because both are household employees. Differs because servant is the colourless umbrella word for all domestic labour; butler denotes rank, trust, and a wine-based origin.
  • Factotum: Similar because both do a household’s varied work. Differs because factotum (“do everything”) implies a jack-of-all-trades without rank, while the butler is a highly specialised head of service.
  • Sommelier: Similar because both are wine professionals. Differs because the sommelier is a restaurant wine expert who advises and serves guests; the butler historically guarded and decanted the household’s cellar as part of a wider office.
