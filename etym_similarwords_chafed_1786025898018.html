• Fretted: Similar because both describe growing internally distressed, agitated, or worn down; differs because fretted emphasizes persistent anxious worry and mental erosion, whereas chafed emphasizes sharp irritation, resentment, and restlessness against external restraint.
  • Bristled: Similar because both denote a visible reaction of annoyance or offense; differs because bristled implies an immediate, defensive, spiky reaction like raised hackles, while chafed represents simmering, continuous friction and restless impatience.
  • Galled: Similar because both derive from physical skin abrasion and extend to emotional irritation; differs because galled conveys deeper bitterness, humiliation, and lasting vexation, whereas chafed emphasizes active restlessness and frustration under immediate confinement.
  • Irritated: Similar because both signify experiencing annoyance or inflammation; differs because irritated is a broad, generic term for physical or mental discomfort, whereas chafed specifically evokes the mechanical or metaphorical friction of being constrained.
  • Abraded: Similar because both involve the wearing away or damage of a surface through contact; differs because abraded is strictly physical and clinical, lacking the psychological sense of rebellious impatience inherent in chafed.
  • Fumed: Similar because both represent escalating emotional agitation; differs because fumed emphasizes silent or venting rage and indignant anger, while chafed centers on restless frustration against boundaries or delays.
