- Bright, burning fire (earliest English sense) - a torch, flame, or fiercely burning fire; literal, vivid, and often associated with danger or destruction.
- Metaphorical extension (16th–19th c.) - any brilliant burst of light, color, or activity (“a blaze of glory”); perception shifts from physical menace to dazzling display, fame, and intensity.
- Idiomatic senses (19th–20th c.) - “blaze a trail” (to mark a path), “in a blaze of publicity”; connotes pioneering energy, brilliance, and achievement.
- Contemporary sense - mostly figurative: fierce brightness of light, heat, or public attention; the literal burning sense survives but has acquired energetic, even heroic, positive overtones.
