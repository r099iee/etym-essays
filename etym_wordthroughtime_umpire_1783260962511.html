- [Middle English 'not-equal third'] - 'Umpire' in its current form descends from Middle English numpere, 'the odd, not-equal third', and in law meant the third party called in when two arbitrators cannot agree - the impartial decider.
- [Sports sense] - From the 18th century, as organised games spread (cricket above all), 'umpire' narrowed to the official who enforces the rules of a match - the arbiter of play.
- [Diplomatic metaphor] - In 18th and 19th century political prose it also lived as a grand metaphor: a nation holding 'the place of umpire among European powers', the neutral third who balances quarrels.
- [Contemporary sense] - Today 'umpire' is chiefly a sports official, while the older judicial and diplomatic senses survive as formal or literary uses of the same neutral, rule-keeping figure.
