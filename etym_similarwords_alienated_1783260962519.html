• Estranged: Similar because both denote a severing of connection or affection; differs because estranged stresses the cooling of personal affection in the private sphere (an estranged husband), while alienated can be public, political, and institutional, and in law can even mean to transfer property.
  • Disaffected: Similar because both mean lost to loyalty; differs because disaffected describes a passive condition of discontent or disloyalty, while alienated describes an active act or process that brings the change about.
  • Withdrawn: Similar because both name a state of being set apart from others; differs because withdrawn suggests voluntary retreat, while alienated implies something done to a person or group by someone else.
  • Isolated: Similar because both mean cut off from others; differs because isolation is physical or social separation, often neutral or chosen, while alienation carries the strong connotation of being made other, foreign, or hostile.
  • Marginalized: Similar because both describe being pushed to the edges of a group; differs because marginalize stresses exclusion from the mainstream (often of social groups), while alienate stresses the emotional and relational severing of loyalty or affection.
  • Transfer / convey: Similar because both are legal verbs for moving property to another; differs because transfer and convey are the neutral legal terms for any change of ownership, while alienate is the specific word for making something another's, from which all the emotional senses then develop.
