• sheaths: Similar because both refer to protective external cases or covers engineered to enclose and carry bladed or pointed weaponry; differs because sheaths are narrow, form-fitting cases designed primarily for single edged weapons such as swords, daggers, or knives, whereas quivers are cylindrical or boxed receptacles designed to accommodate multiple loose projectiles (arrows or bolts).
  • cases: Similar because both designate containers or receptacles manufactured to hold, protect, and transport specific physical objects; differs because cases is an extremely broad, generic category encompassing containers for luggage, instruments, glasses, or documents, whereas quivers specifically denotes martial carriers for archery projectiles.
  • holsters: Similar because both are specialized personal cases attached to a soldier's body or saddle for immediate drawing of a ranged weapon; differs because holsters are engineered specifically to carry handguns, pistols, or revolvers, whereas quivers carry arrows or bolts for bows and crossbows.
  • scabbards: Similar because both are traditional military containers worn on the person to hold armaments; differs because scabbards are rigid sheaths exclusively built to protect long bladed edged weapons like broadswords or rapiers, whereas quivers hold bunches of feathered kinetic projectiles.
  • arsenals: Similar because both function metaphorically to describe an individual's or institution's complete stockpile of weapons, tools, or rhetorical resources; differs because arsenals denote massive physical buildings, military installations, or total macroscopic weapon supplies, whereas quivers signify personal, portable containers carried by an individual warrior.
  • repositories: Similar because both describe places, vessels, or figurative receptacles where valuable objects, knowledge, or tools are stored for future deployment; differs because repositories is a formal, abstract, or architectural term denoting stationary storage facilities (such as archives or databases), lacking all martial, archery, and portable associations.
