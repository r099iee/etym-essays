[Root 1: hand (Manual Limb / Grasping Member)]
Proto-Indo-European (reconstructed)
*kond- / *kem- (?)
"to seize, grasp, take hold of"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*handuz
"hand; the grasping member"
(related to *hinþaną "to seize, catch")
↓
Old English (c. 8th c.)
hand / hond
"hand, human grasping limb; manual labor"

[Root 2: loom (Tool / Weaving Machine)]
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*(ga)lōmō / *lōm-
"tool, implement, utensil of work"
↓
Old English (c. 9th c.)
andlōma / gelōma
"tool, utensil, household implement"
↓
Middle English (c. 13th c.)
lome
"machine, tool, specialized weaving frame"
(specialized from general tool to weaving machine)

[Compound Synthesis]
Early Modern to Industrial English (late 18th c.)
hand-loom / handloom
"manually operated loom, distinct from power loom"
↓
Modern English (present)
hand-loom
"non-mechanized, manual weaving machine"
