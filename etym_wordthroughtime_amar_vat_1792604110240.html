- [Vedic & Epic Era (c. 1200–500 BCE)] - formulated in late Vedic hymns and classical Sanskrit epics (Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa) as the luminous celestial residence of Indra and the thirty-three devas, constructed by Viśvakarman - [perception: revered as the ultimate cosmic archetype of heavenly grandeur, architectural perfection, and karmic reward]
- [Classical Historical & Buddhist Era (c. 2nd c. BCE–4th c. CE)] - applied to the magnificent Sātavāhana riverine port and capital Dhānyakaṭaka on the Krishna River, becoming a world-renowned pilgrimage center famous for the Great Stupa and the Amarāvatī school of art - [perception: admired throughout India, Sri Lanka, and Southeast Asia as a thriving epicenter of Buddhist learning, artistic mastery, and monastic life]
- [Classical Sanskrit Kāvya & Medieval Purāṇic Era (c. 5th–16th c.)] - codified in Sanskrit literary treatises as the supreme poetic standard (upamāna) for comparing royal earthly capitals; concurrently depicted in Purāṇic lore as a paradise repeatedly besieged by Asuras - [perception: recognized as the quintessential metaphor for boundless earthly or divine opulence, accompanied by philosophical reminders of the impermanence of worldly power]
- [Modern & Contemporary Era (19th–21st c.)] - rediscovered archaeologically via the British excavation of the Amarāvatī marbles, and subsequently revived as the formal name for the planned capital city of modern Andhra Pradesh - [perception: celebrated as a proud civilizational emblem uniting ancient Buddhist aesthetic heritage with contemporary democratic governance]
