- [Middle English mockery] - Scorn means open mockery and derision - to laugh at someone in contempt; 'to laugh to scorn' is to jeer someone into worthlessness.
- [16th-17th century moral heat] - The word gains moral force: scorn is the contempt of the worthy for the base, hot with disgust and refusal - proud and theatrical in the poetry of the period.
- [18th-19th century refinement] - Writers distinguish it from contempt: contempt is cold and considered, while scorn adds derision, mockery and heat; the scornful person publicly disdains.
- [Contemporary register] - Still strong and emotional: 'to pour scorn on' an idea; used of moral, social and intellectual disdain, more heated than mere contempt.
