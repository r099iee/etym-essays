• Toll roads: Similar because both charge travellers for passage to fund upkeep; differs because toll road is the plain generic term for any such highway, while turnpike-road names the historic trust system with its turning spiked gates.
  • Tollways: Similar because both are tolled through-routes, especially the American motorways; differs because tollway is the modern administrative word, while turnpike-road is historical and literary, smelling of Cobbett and the stage-coach.
  • Highways: Similar because both are main public roads between towns; differs because highway says nothing about payment — the king's highway is free — while turnpike-road exists precisely to charge.
  • Thoroughfares: Similar because both carry through-traffic between places; differs because a thoroughfare stresses openness and passage, while a turnpike-road stresses the gate that interrupts passage until the toll is paid.
  • Causeways: Similar because both are raised, engineered roads carrying travellers over low or wet ground; differs because a causeway is defined by its built-up structure, while a turnpike-road is defined by its toll-gate and trust.
  • Byways: Similar because both thread the countryside Cobbett loved; differs because a byway is the unfrequented lane, free and grassy, while a turnpike-road is the busy tolled artery the byway escapes.
  • Lanes: Similar because Cobbett sets both against the great roads as the true way to see the country; differs because a lane is narrow, rural, and free, while a turnpike-road is broad, metalled, and paywalled.
  • Arteries: Similar because both are figured as the great channels feeding a nation's traffic; differs because artery is pure metaphor — any vital route — while turnpike-road is a concrete historical institution of gates, trusts, and tariffs.
  • Freeways: Similar because both are fast inter-town highways of the motor age; differs because a freeway is free at the point of use by definition, the exact opposite of a turnpike-road, which exists to collect.
