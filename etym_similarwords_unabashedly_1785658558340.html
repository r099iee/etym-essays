• BOLDLY: Similar because both describe acting without hesitation in a way that draws attention; differs because boldly implies confronting danger or risk with courage — feeling fear and going on — while unabashedly has no necessary element of risk, only the absence of shame or embarrassment.
  • BRAZENLY: Similar because both mean acting without shame and in full public view; differs because brazenly carries a distinctly negative charge of insolently flouting decency or rules, while unabashedly is morally neutral to approving and can celebrate candor rather than censure cheek.
  • SHAMELESSLY: Similar because both mean doing something without any sense of shame; differs because shamelessly is almost always an accusation — it says the person has abandoned decency — whereas unabashedly often describes something defensible or even admirable, authenticity worn without apology.
  • OPENLY: Similar because both mean acting without concealment; differs because openly says nothing about composure (one can openly weep or openly gloat), while unabashedly specifies the absence of embarrassment in the face of exposure — the composure itself is the point.
  • AUDACIOUSLY: Similar because both describe bold, confident action; differs because audaciously stresses daring that courts risk or breaks convention, whereas unabashedly stresses freedom from shame rather than appetite for danger.
  • BLATANTLY: Similar because both describe something done in an obvious, unapologetic way; differs because blatantly is used of faults and offenses that are flagrantly, noisily visible (a blatantly false claim), while unabashedly describes a manner of acting rather than an objectionable quality of the act.
  • FRANKLY: Similar because both imply honest, unreserved self-exposure; differs because frankly emphasizes truthfulness of speech and sincerity of tone, while unabashedly emphasizes composure — one can be frank and nervous, but not unabashed and nervous.
  • IMPUDENTLY: Similar because both describe bold self-assertion without respect for what others think; differs because impudently is always pejorative — rude cheek toward authority — while unabashedly can be neutral or admiring.
  • NAKEDLY: Similar because both mean without covering or concealment; differs because nakedly is usually figurative about raw truth or emotion laid bare (nakedly vulnerable), while unabashedly is about the refusal to feel shame rather than about the rawness of what is shown.
  • BLUNTLY: Similar because both describe speech that does not soften itself; differs because bluntly is about direct, abrupt wording without tact, while unabashedly is about fearlessness in the face of judgment and can describe behavior, dress, or emotion as easily as speech.
