• Confusion: Similar because both name a state of mental disorder and mistaking; differs because confusion is the broad everyday word for any jumbling of ideas or facts, while bewilderment implies a more overwhelming, baffled loss of orientation.
  • Perplexity: Similar because both denote inability to make sense of something; differs because perplexity is intellectual and analytical (a puzzle or paradox that resists solution), while bewilderment is more visceral, emotional and disorienting.
  • Bafflement: Similar because both express complete inability to understand; differs because bafflement focuses on the confounding object (a mystery that "baffles" whomever meets it), while bewilderment emphasises the sufferer's internal state of being lost.
  • Puzzlement: Similar because both involve failure to understand; differs because puzzlement is lighter, curious and contained, while bewilderment is deeper, more agitated and more unsettling.
  • Bemusement: Similar because both involve not understanding; differs because bemusement often includes mild amusement or detachment, while bewilderment is serious and disturbing.
  • Mystification: Similar because both involve being made unable to comprehend; differs because mystification suggests a deliberate air of mystery or secrecy arranged by something or someone, while bewilderment is usually spontaneous.
  • Disorientation: Similar because both involve loss of bearings; differs because disorientation is the spatial and clinical term (dizziness, being lost in a place), while bewilderment is chiefly mental — though Tolkien's "bewilderment of the treasure" uses it almost as a spell of disorientation.
  • Daze: Similar because both describe a stunned, unsteady mental state; differs because a daze is a brief, numbed stupor (from a blow or shock), while bewilderment can be sustained and agonised.
  • Stupefaction: Similar because both describe being rendered unable to think; differs because stupefaction implies numbness and dull astonishment (often from wonder or drugs), whereas bewilderment keeps the mind racing in circles.
  • Consternation: Similar because both involve a disturbed, thrown-off balance; differs because consternation adds alarm, dismay and dread (the emotion of a sudden threat), while bewilderment is confusion without necessarily being afraid.
  • Befuddlement: Similar because both describe a muddled, unclear state of mind; differs because befuddlement is colloquial and suggests a mildly clouded, drowsy or intoxicated confusion, while bewilderment is more literary and intense.
  • Wonderment: Similar because both arise when the mind meets something beyond it; differs because wonderment is delighted awe and curiosity, while bewilderment is distressed perplexity.
