• Pliers: Similar because both are two-armed gripping tools; differs because pliers are smaller, precisely pivoted with a joint near the jaws and often cutting edges, built for fine gripping and wire-work, while tongs are long-armed tools for reach and heat-distance.
  • Pincers: Similar because both grip by a pair of pivoted limbs; differs because pincers are hinged like pliers but stronger and used for pulling nails or gripping tightly, while tongs are built for lifting hot objects with leverage.
  • Forceps: Similar because both are gripping instruments; differs because forceps is the medical and surgical twin of pincers — precision surgical grasping — while tongs are the domestic and forge tool for hot or heavy things.
  • Tweezers: Similar because both grasp small objects by a pinching action; differ because tweezers are tiny hand-held nippers for delicate work, while tongs are large, long-armed, and used for reach and heat.
  • Nippers: Similar because both are gripping tools; differ because nippers are cutting pincers (clipping nails, wire), while tongs grasp and lift without cutting.
  • Clamp: Similar because both hold objects; differs because a clamp locks in place and holds work fixed to a bench, while tongs are a free hand-held grip from a distance.
  • Vice: Similar because both grip objects firmly; differs because a vice is mounted and immovable, holding work for cutting, while tongs are portable and grasp things too hot to touch.
  • Scissors / Shears: Similar because both are tools made of two pivoted blades and are plurale tantum like tongs; differ because scissors and shears cut, while tongs grip and lift without cutting.
