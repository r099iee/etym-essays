- [Antiquity–c. 1000] - Ancient Greek *smáragdos* and Latin *smaragdus* refer broadly to any magnificent green gemstone, revered for eye-soothing properties and royal ornamentation - [connotation: sacred divine power, healing vision, and emperor's luxury]
- [c. 1200–1400] - Old French *esmeraude* enters Middle English as *emeraude*, establishing the stone in chivalric romances, royal inventories, and medieval Christian lapidaries - [connotation: courtly splendor, chastity, and heavenly Jerusalem symbolism]
- [1500–1700] - Spanish conquest of South America floods European markets with flawless Colombian emeralds from Muzo, cementing the modern spelling *emerald* and establishing modern gemological prestige - [connotation: pinnacle of exotic wealth, global trade, and aristocratic power]
- [1800–Present] - Industrialization, mineralogy, and Romantic literature establish *emeralds* as both a precise mineralogical standard and an evocative metaphor for vibrant natural beauty and aesthetic perfection - [connotation: classic high-end luxury, vibrant green perfection, and timeless elegance]
