• Killed: Similar because both mean caused death. Differs because killed is neutral and everyday; slew is literary, heroic, and scriptural.
  • Murdered: Similar because both mean unlawful killing. Differs because murdered carries legal and moral condemnation of a specific crime; slew is formless epic violence.
  • Slaughtered: Similar because both mean killed wholesale. Differs because slaughtered suggests mass killing or butchery of animals; slew covers single heroic acts too.
  • Butchered: Similar because both mean killed brutally. Differs because butchered is crude, gory, and contemptuous; slew is dignified.
  • Massacred: Similar because both mean killed in large numbers. Differs because massacred implies killing the defenceless; slew is martial and symmetrical.
  • Assassinated: Similar because both mean deliberate killing. Differs because assassinated is covert, targeted, and modern-political; slew is open combat.
  • Executed: Similar because both mean put to death. Differs because executed is a judicial sentence; slew is extrajudicial combat.
  • Dispatched: Similar because both mean killed efficiently. Differs because dispatched is euphemistic and matter-of-fact; slew is epic.
  • Eliminated: Similar because both mean removed by killing. Differs because eliminated is clinical and bureaucratic; slew is blood-and-blade.
  • Put to death: Similar because both mean killed. Differs because put to death is formal-juridical; slew is warlike.
  • Smote: Similar because it is the archaic past of smite, “struck down”. Differs because smote emphasises the blow itself and is biblical, and need not be fatal.
  • Cut down / Struck down / Felled: Similar because all mean killed in battle. Differs because each names a specific physical metaphor; slew is general.
  • Topped / Bumped off / Done in / Knocked off: Similar because all mean killed (slang). Differs because these are irreverent gangster registers; slew is solemn.
  • Exterminated / Annihilated: Similar because both mean destroyed utterly. Differs because these are impersonal and total; slew is singular and heroic.
