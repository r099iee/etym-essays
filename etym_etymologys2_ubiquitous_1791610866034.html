PIE (c. 4000 BCE)
*kʷo- / *kʷu- + *-bʰi
"interrogative/relative stem" + "locative ending"
↓
Latin (c. 3rd c. BCE)
ubi
"where, in which place"
↓
Latin (1st c. BCE)
ubīque (ubi + -que)
"everywhere, anywhere, in every quarter"
↓
Neo-Latin (theological coining) (c. 1560s–1570s)
ubiquitas
"omnipresence, being everywhere"
↓
English (noun) (c. 1600)
ubiquity
"omnipresence, omnipresent existence"
↓
English (adjective) (c. 1800–modern)
ubiquitous
"present, appearing, or found everywhere"

Note: Latin ubīque was formed from the interrogative/locative adverb ubi ('where') combined with the generalizing enclitic particle -que ('and, ever, any', from PIE *-kʷe). In the late 16th century, Neo-Latin theologians coined ubiquitas to describe the omnipresence of Christ's body. The English adjective ubiquitous was formed around 1800 by attaching the standard Romance adjective suffix -ous to the noun ubiquity.
