• Viscous: Similar because both describe thick, slow-moving semi-fluids that resist flow; differs because viscous is a neutral rheological term focusing on internal friction and thickness, whereas glutinous specifically emphasizes glue-like stickiness, surface adhesion, and culinary or figurative cloyingness.
  • Sticky: Similar because both denote substances that adhere tenaciously upon contact; differs because sticky is the common, everyday Anglo-Saxon descriptor, whereas glutinous is formal, scientific, or literary, carrying connotations of organic slime, starch, or maudlin emotion.
  • Mucilaginous: Similar because both refer to moist, gelatinous, and adhesive organic substances; differs because mucilaginous belongs strictly to botany, pharmacology, and zoology (denoting plant mucilage or bodily secretions) and is almost never transferred metaphorically to human sentiment.
  • Gelatinous: Similar because both describe semi-solid, jelly-like textures derived from dissolved proteins or starches; differs because gelatinous emphasizes structural wobble, firmness, and elasticity (like set jelly), whereas glutinous emphasizes sticky adherence and viscous tackiness.
  • Cloying: Similar because both describe an overwhelming, sickening excess of sweetness; differs because cloying is primarily a sensory and psychological judgment about taste, smell, or sentiment that disgusts through overabundance, whereas glutinous describes physical or stylistic stickiness that clings.
  • Treacly: Similar because both metaphorically describe slow, heavy, syrupy sweetness in tone or prose; differs because treacly evokes dark, thick molasses and heavy golden syrup, carrying a slightly warmer, heavier British nursery connotation than the unctuous stickiness of glutinous.
  • Mawkish: Similar because both criticize insincere, sickly, or exaggerated sentimentality; differs because mawkish focuses on moral and emotional feebleness (often with a sour, nauseating undertone, historically from "maggot"), lacking the physical adhesive imagery inherent in glutinous.
  • Unctuous: Similar because both describe greasy, oily, and unpleasantly smooth physical or behavioral textures; differs because unctuous derives from oil and ointment, emphasizing slippery, insincere flattery and hypocritical piety, whereas glutinous derives from glue and emphasizes clinging stickiness.
  • Adhesive: Similar because both denote the capability of holding surfaces together by sticking; differs because adhesive is a functional, technical, or industrial term for bonding materials, devoid of organic, culinary, or literary emotional connotations.
  • Gummy: Similar because both describe tacky, rubbery, or glue-like textures; differs because gummy suggests a firmer, chewable, or dried-out elastic state rather than the viscous, flowing stickiness denoted by glutinous.
  • Saccharine: Similar because both are used figuratively to condemn exaggerated, artificial sweetness in art or personality; differs because saccharine targets sickeningly sweet flavor and sugary cheerfulness, whereas glutinous emphasizes heavy, clinging sentiment that traps the listener.
