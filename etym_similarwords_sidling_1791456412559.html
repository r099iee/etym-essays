• Edging: moving gradually sideways or along a boundary; very close, but edging stresses slow progress along an edge rather than the furtive manner.
  • Creeping: moving slowly and quietly, often on hands and knees; similar stealth, but more strongly associated with stealthy or scary approach.
  • Stealing (as in “stealing along”): moving secretly; similar furtiveness, but more literary and more strongly criminal or guilty in tone.
  • Sneaking: moving quietly to avoid notice; close, but sneaking implies deliberate concealment and often wrongdoing, whereas sidling can be mere shyness.
  • Slinking: moving quietly and furtively, often with a sense of shame; darker and more self-conscious than sidling.
  • Sliding: moving smoothly and continuously; similar smoothness, but without the sideways or furtive implication.
  • Slipping: moving quietly and quickly, often unnoticed; similar, but emphasises speed and ease rather than lateral orientation.
  • Shuffling: walking with dragging feet; similar awkwardness, but implies heaviness or age rather than stealth.
  • Oblique: slanting or indirect; the adjectival counterpart, but it describes angle or manner rather than physical movement.
  • Furtive: done secretly to avoid notice; captures the manner of sidling but not its sideways motion.
  • Insinuating: subtly ingratiating oneself; the social meaning of sidle up to, but more explicitly manipulative.
  • Ingratiating: trying to please in order to gain favour; the motive behind a sidling approach, without the physical image.
