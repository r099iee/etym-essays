PIE (c. 4500-2500 BCE)
*pu- / *pū-
"to rot, stink, be foul"
↓
Proto-Germanic (c. 500 BCE - 100 CE)
*fūlaz
"foul, dirty, rotten, putrid"
↓
Old English (c. 700 CE)
fūl (adjective)
"foul, rotten, unclean, sinful"
↓
Old English (c. 900 CE)
fȳlan (verb)
"to make foul, to defile"
(umlauted denominal of fūl)
↓
Old English (c. 950-1050 CE)
befȳlan
"to defile, pollute, befoul"
(be- + fȳlan)
↓
Middle English (13th-15th c.)
befylen / befoulen
"to make foul, to defile"
↓
Modern English
BEFOULED

Note (nuance): Middle English also borrowed defoulen from Old French defouler "to trample down, oppress, violate"; the two families converged in meaning and spelling for centuries, and the native befoul absorbed the borrowed word's force before defoul died out in early modern English.
