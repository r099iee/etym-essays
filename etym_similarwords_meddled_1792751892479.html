• interfered: Similar because both denote inserting oneself into situations or affairs where one has neither invitation nor rightful jurisdiction; differs because interfered is broader and can refer to passive, systemic, or physical obstruction (such as electrical static or conflicting legal jurisdictions), whereas meddled carries a distinctly petty, intrusive, and officious personal culpability.
  • tampered: Similar because both involve unauthorized manipulation or disturbance of a status quo; differs because tampered focuses specifically on physical, mechanical, or documentation alteration often done covertly with fraudulent, corrupt, or destructive intent (e.g., tampering with evidence or a brake line), while meddled emphasizes intrusive busybodying and unsolicited involvement.
  • intruded: Similar because both involve crossing boundaries into spaces or matters where one is unwelcome; differs because intruded implies an unlawful or uninvited physical entry into a space, gathering, or private conversation, whereas meddled implies active manipulation or meddlesome interference within the affairs themselves.
  • interposed: Similar because both describe stepping between parties or into the middle of ongoing events; differs because interposed can be formal, neutral, or benevolent (such as a mediator interposing to prevent bloodshed or a diplomat offering good offices), whereas meddled is almost invariably perceived as uninvited, unwelcome, and irritatingly presumption-laden.
  • kibitzed: Similar because both describe offering unsolicited commentary or unsolicited actions in someone else's ongoing activity; differs because kibitzed derives from Yiddish card-playing culture and primarily denotes giving annoying, unasked-for advice or running commentary from the sidelines without possessing actual executive responsibility or hands-on control.
  • encroached: Similar because both describe violating boundaries and overstepping rightful limits; differs because encroached implies a gradual, creeping, and predatory advance upon another's territory, legal rights, or personal sovereignty over time, whereas meddled describes active, direct, and bustling interference in specific affairs.
  • snooped: Similar because both denote inappropriate curiosity about other people's private business; differs because snooped is confined to stealthy, covert investigation and prying into concealed documents or secret spaces, whereas meddled goes beyond looking to actively manipulate, alter, or influence the situation.
  • mingled (archaic): Similar because mingled represents the exact historical ancestor and primary Middle English meaning of meddled (from Old French mesler, to mix); differs because in modern English mingled has retained the neutral, convivial, or physical sense of blending elements or socializing amicably, whereas meddled has undergone complete pejoration to denote exclusively annoying interference.
