• Maroon: Similar because both refer to persons stranded in isolation on remote shores; differs because a 'maroon' specifically refers to someone intentionally abandoned or put ashore by a captain or crew as a deliberate punishment, whereas a 'castaway' is typically stranded through accidental shipwreck or catastrophe.
  • Outcast: Similar because both denote individuals banished or discarded; differs because 'outcast' is strictly sociological and moral, referring to someone rejected from a community or family, without any maritime or survivalist connotation.
  • Pariah: Similar because both represent socially discarded persons; differs because 'pariah' implies intense cultural taboo, ritual untouchability, or systemic social ostracism, originating in the Indian caste system, whereas 'castaway' retains a nautical or survivalist metaphor.
  • Derelict: Similar because both denote abandoned things or persons left to ruin; differs because 'derelict' is primarily used for abandoned ships, drifting wrecks, or dilapidated physical property, or colloquially for destitute vagrants, lacking the romantic heroic survival resonance of 'castaway'.
  • Foundling: Similar because both describe abandoned persons; differs because a 'foundling' is specifically an abandoned infant or young child of unknown parentage found and taken in by strangers.
  • Exile: Similar because both live separated from their homeland; differs because an 'exile' is formally expelled or compelled to leave by political authority, often retaining an active political identity abroad rather than being stranded in wilderness.
  • Shipwreck survivor: Similar because both survive maritime disasters; differs because 'shipwreck survivor' is a purely descriptive factual label, whereas 'castaway' carries deep literary, archetypal, and existential connotations of prolonged isolation and primitive subsistence.
  • Renegade: Similar because both are separated from their original company; differs because a 'renegade' is an active deserter, traitor, or rebel who wilfully abandons a faith, cause, or military command, whereas a 'castaway' is victim to fate or circumstance.
