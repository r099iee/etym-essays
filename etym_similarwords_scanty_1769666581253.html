• Meager: Similar because both mean small in quantity or insufficient; differs because meager emphasizes thinness and poverty (a meager income), while scanty stresses being cut short of the required amount.
  • Sparse: Similar because both mean thin or thinly distributed; differs because sparse refers to scattering over space (sparse population, sparse hair), while scanty refers to overall quantity falling short.
  • Skimpy: Similar because both describe small, barely sufficient amounts, especially of clothing; differs because skimpy is informal and implies something meanly or teasingly small, while scanty is more neutral and formal.
  • Thin: Similar because both describe a small amount or density; differs because thin is a much broader word (thin ice, thin air), while scanty specifically marks insufficiency against a need.
  • Insufficient: Similar because both mean not enough; differs because insufficient is purely quantitative and formal, whereas scanty also has descriptive force about size, dress, and distribution.
  • Deficient: Similar because both mean falling short of a standard; differs because deficient implies a measurable lack or fault, while scanty is more about smallness and thinness of supply.
  • Bare: Similar because both describe the absolute minimum; differs because bare stresses having nothing extra (bare essentials, bare floor), while scanty stresses quantity that falls short.
  • Limited: Similar because both describe restricted amounts; differs because limited is neutral and structural, while scanty carries a tone of insufficiency or skimpy covering.
  • Slender: Similar because both describe a small, thin amount (slender means); differs because slender is often graceful or elegant, while scanty suggests barely adequate or inadequate.
  • Slight: Similar because both mean small in amount; differs because slight stresses smallness of degree (a slight chance), while scanty stresses insufficient quantity of material.
  • Scant: Similar because both share the same root and meaning "barely enough, insufficient"; differs because scant is the older, shorter base (also usable as an adverb meaning "hardly"), while scanty is the full descriptive adjective formed with -y.
  • Paltry: Similar because both describe contemptibly small amounts; differs because paltry adds contempt and worthlessness, while scanty is more neutral about value.
