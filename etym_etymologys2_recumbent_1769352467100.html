Stage 1 — Proto-Indo-European *kewb- (also *keub-, Pokorny *keu-bh-) "to bend, bow, sink down" (reconstructed root, 4th-3rd millennium BCE). Stage 2 — Latin cubare "to lie down, recline, sleep, rest in bed" (Classical Latin, 2nd-1st centuries BCE), together with the parallel nasalized present stem cumbere "to sink down, lie back," used in prefixed compounds. Stage 3 — Latin recumbere "to lie back down, recline, fall back" (Classical Latin, 1st century BCE; re- "back, again" + cumbere). Stage 4 — Latin recumbens, present participle, stem recumbent- "lying back, reclining" (Classical / Imperial Latin); the adjectival form. Stage 5 — English recumbent (adjective) "lying down, reclining," borrowed directly from Latin recumbent- (first recorded c. 1770). —> recumbent. Debate / nuance note: The root *kewb- is broadly accepted, but its zero-grade shape and some claimed cognates (e.g. Sanskrit kubja "humpbacked") are debated. Latin cubare and cumbere also underlie English incumbent, succumb, and (via Greek kubos) cube, all converging on the same ancient "bend, lie" root.
