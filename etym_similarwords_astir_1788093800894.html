• Afoot: Similar because both are a- state-words for motion or progress (an enterprise is afoot, a camp is astir). Differs because afoot belongs to events and schemes underway, while astir belongs to people and places stirring with movement.
  • On the move: Similar because both mean in motion. Differs because the phrase is neutral and transactional; astir implies roused energy, alarm, or bustle.
  • Up and about: Similar because both mean out of bed and active. Differs because the phrase is modern colloquial; astir is literary and often carries collective urgency.
  • In motion: Similar because both denote movement. Differs because the phrase is a plain neutral technical description; astir adds liveliness and expectation.
  • Aflutter: Similar because both name an excited, agitated state. Differs because aflutter suggests nervous, light, emotional fluttering (butterflies); astir suggests purposeful communal movement.
  • Abuzz: Similar because both name a state of excited activity. Differs because abuzz is auditory — full of talk and noise; astir is about movement and implies no sound.
  • Active: Similar because both mean engaged in activity. Differs because active is a plain adjective, usable attributively, with no archaic colour and no rousing nuance.
  • Busy: Similar because both mean occupied with doings. Differs because busy is everyday and implies work; astir implies collective movement and expectancy.
  • Roused: Similar because both mean awakened into activity. Differs because roused is usually caused by an external agent (roused by the trumpet), whereas astir is a self-standing state.
  • Stirring: Similar because it shares the root. Differs because stirring is an active participle describing the thing that moves others; astir describes the state of the movers themselves.
  • Agitated: Similar because both mean disturbed or excited. Differs because agitated is negative and emotional, suggesting turbulence; astir is neutral-to-positive communal readiness.
  • Awake: Similar because both mean out of rest. Differs because awake is purely sensory, with no sense of motion, bustle, or alarm.
