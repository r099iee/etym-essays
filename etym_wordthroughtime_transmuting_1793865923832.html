- Late 14th century - [Alchemical Hermeticism] - Coined in late Middle English as an alchemical verb describing the Hermetic art of converting base metals (lead, tin, copper) into precious gold via the philosopher's stone; carried an aura of esoteric mystery, sacred natural philosophy, and secret craft. - 16th to 17th century - [Metaphorical and Theological Elevation] - Broadened in early modern literature and theology into an elevated figurative verb denoting spiritual refinement, divine grace purifying sinful human nature, or passionate love reshaping human character. - Early 19th century - [Pre-Darwinian Evolutionary Biology] - Adopted in natural history as a central technical term for the 'transmutation of species' (Lamarckian evolution), where living forms were hypothesized to convert gradually across generations, igniting fierce philosophical and religious controversy. - Early 20th century - [Nuclear Physics and Radioactivity] - Reclaimed by modern atomic physics when Ernest Rutherford and Frederick Soddy discovered radioactive decay and demonstrated that radioactive elements naturally decay into other elements, vindicating the ancient alchemical concept at the subatomic level. - Modern / Contemporary - [Active Present Participle / Gerund] - Functioning today both as a participial adjective and verbal noun describing active, ongoing psychological, artistic, financial, or material conversion of raw inputs, grief, or base challenges into triumphant outcomes.
