• pattern poetry: Similar because it arranges lines into pictures; differs because it is the modern English umbrella term, while technopaegnia denotes the specific ancient Greek corpus.
  • shape poetry / figure poem: Similar because form mirrors subject; differs because it is popular and pedagogical, lacking the learned Hellenistic connotation.
  • concrete poetry: Similar because it treats language as visual material; differs because it is a 1950s avant-garde movement with ideological manifestos, not Hellenistic court play.
  • carmina figurata: Similar because they are Latin figured poems imitating Greek models; differs because they are Roman and late-antique, often Christian and panegyrical.
  • calligram: Similar because Apollinaire’s term means beautiful writing as image; differs because it is modern French and individual-artist centred.
  • technopaegnion (singular): Similar because it is the single game-piece; differs because it is singular, while technopaegnia is the plural corpus.
  • riddle / enigma / griphos: Similar because technopaegnia tease with obscurity; differs because riddles hide answers verbally, while technopaegnia hide them visually.
  • epigram: Similar because many figured pieces are dedicatory and brief; differs because epigram stresses inscription and point, not necessarily shape.
  • lipogram / acrostic / anagram: Similar because they are constrained ludic forms; differs because their constraint is lexical-letteral rather than visual-contour.
