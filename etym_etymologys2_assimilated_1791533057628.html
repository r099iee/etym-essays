assimiler

↓
English (15th c.)
assimilate
"to make or become like"
↓
English (16th c.–present)
assimilated
"absorbed and made alike"

Note: the classical Latin verb is usually spelled assimulāre (from ad- + simulāre), with assimilāre as a later variant that prevailed in medieval and modern usage. The PIE root *sem- “one, together” also yields Latin semel “once,” simul “at the same time” and English same; the derivation of similis from it is standard but the exact suffixation is reconstructed, not attested.
