Single root word (EMBROIDERY). Linear chain from the earliest reconstructible stage; the Germanic craft-verb has no securely established PIE root, so the chain begins at Proto-Germanic. → Proto-Germanic *bruzdjan / *bruzdajan 'to embroider, to ornament with thread' (reconstructed; origin beyond Germanic not securely established — no agreed PIE root is usually cited for this verb) → Frankish *brozdon 'to embroider' (early medieval, 5th–8th c.; the West Germanic form transmitted into Gallo-Romance) → Old French broisder / broder 'to embroider' (12th–13th c., from the Frankish verb) → Anglo-French enbrouder / enbroder 'to embroider' (13th–14th c.; en- 'in, into' + broder) → Middle English embrouden (verb) 'to embroider' and embrouderie (noun) 'the art of embroidering' (late 14th c., from Anglo-French) → EMBROIDERY 'the art of decorating cloth with needle and thread; embroidered work' (Modern English; spelling with -oi- influenced from c. 1600 by Middle English broiden, past participle of braid; also the verb embroider and agent noun embroiderer) Note: the Frankish/Germanic origin of the craft-verb is the standard account (OED, Etymonline); because the root is attested essentially only in western Germanic and Romance loan-shapes, responsible trees do not force a PIE source. Do not merge the separate etymon border 'edge, side' (from a word for a plank or a ship's side), which is unrelated despite the resemblance.
