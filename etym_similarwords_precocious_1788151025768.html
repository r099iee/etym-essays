• Gifted: similar because both describe unusual ability in the young; differs because gifted names innate high intelligence whatever its timing, while precocious specifically says "early" — and can be said of a merely advanced, not brilliant, child.
  • Advanced: similar because both mean ahead of the norm; differs because advanced is a neutral, quantitative word (an advanced student) with no judgment of earliness or risk.
  • Premature: similar because both mean "before the expected time"; differs because premature is dyslogistic and suggests unpreparedness or incomplete development, whereas precocious suggests a surprising completeness achieved early.
  • Prodigious: similar because both describe remarkable ability; differs because prodigious measures magnitude (huge, marvellous) rather than timing, and is not restricted to children.
  • Mature: similar because both describe developed minds; differs because mature describes completed development, while precocious describes early development still in progress.
  • Early: similar because both mean "before the usual time"; differs because early is plain, temporal, and unambitious, lacking precocious's implication of unusual ripeness.
  • Budding: similar because both suggest young development beginning to show; differs because budding simply means "promising, starting out", with no implication of being ahead of schedule.
  • Ahead of one's time: similar because both describe a mind unusual for its age; differs because this phrase stresses originality against history itself, while precocious stresses youth against the calendar.
  • Unfledged / callow (antonyms): similar because they belong to the same vocabulary of youth; differs because they mean "not yet developed" — the exact opposite of precocious, useful for defining it by contrast.
