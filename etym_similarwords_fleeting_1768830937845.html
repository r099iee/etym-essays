• Transient: Similar because both mean lasting for only a short time; differs because transient emphasizes the passing from existence (things that come and go), while fleeting emphasizes motion past the observer and is more literary and momentary.
  • Transitory: Similar because both denote short duration; differs because transitory stresses the inherent tendency to pass away (the natural end of a thing), while fleeting stresses how quickly the thing slips by and is gone.
  • Ephemeral: Similar because both mean lasting for a very short time; differs because ephemeral literally means "lasting a single day" and suggests something delicate and short-lived by nature, while fleeting suggests a passing moment that slips away.
  • Momentary: Similar because both denote extreme brevity; differs because momentary is the shortest, an instant's duration, while fleeting can cover slightly longer spans still marked by swift passing.
  • Brief: Similar because both mean short in duration; differs because brief is neutral and factual about length, while fleeting carries a poetic sense of motion and loss.
  • Short-lived: Similar because both mean not lasting long; differs because short-lived is applied to what endures briefly but measurably (lives, careers, trends), while fleeting is applied to what passes almost immediately.
  • Passing: Similar because both mean transient, coming and going; differs because passing is often a mild, almost formal synonym (a passing glance, a passing fashion), while fleeting is more intense and imagistic.
  • Evanescent: Similar because both mean vanishing quickly; differs because evanescent describes what dissolves or fades away gradually like vapor, while fleeting describes what darts past and disappears at once.
  • Fugitive: Similar because both denote what flees and cannot be held; differs because fugitive (of moments, colors, impressions) is rare and highly literary, suggesting flight from capture, while fleeting is the common word for brevity.
  • Temporary: Similar because both mean not permanent; differs because temporary marks a limited but known duration and is neutral (a temporary job), while fleeting marks swift disappearance and is poetic.
  • Impermanent: Similar because both mean not lasting; differs because impermanent is abstract and philosophical (the impermanence of all things), while fleeting is concrete and sensory.
  • Fading: Similar because both concern what is lost to time; differs because fading suggests a gradual loss of color or strength, while fleeting suggests quick passage.
  • Vanishing: Similar because both mean disappearing; differs because vanishing is the act of disappearing entirely, while fleeting is the quality of being on the point of disappearing.
  • Cursory: Similar because both can describe something done quickly; differs because cursory describes a hasty, superficial manner of doing something (a cursory glance over a document), while fleeting describes the brief duration of the thing itself.
  • Quick: Similar because both imply speed; differs because quick emphasizes rapid movement or action, while fleeting emphasizes short duration and passing.
  • Swift: Similar because both imply rapid motion; differs because swift describes speed of movement (a swift runner), while fleeting describes the brevity and evanescence of the thing.
  • Flitting: Similar because both imply quick, restless passing; differs because flitting suggests light, irregular movement (butterflies flitting), while fleeting suggests being carried along and gone.
  • Meteoric: Similar because both describe a short, brilliant career or moment; differs because meteoric is used only of dramatic rises that burn out (a meteoric rise), while fleeting applies to anything brief.
