• Battered: Similar because both mean knocked about by repeated blows; differs because battered stresses the resulting damage and wear (battered walls, battered faces) while buffeted stresses the repeated striking itself.
  • Pounded: Similar because both describe heavy, repeated impact; differs because pounded suggests harder, heavier, more rhythmical blows (waves pounding the shore) and is less used of persons or fortune.
  • Struck / Hit: Similar because all mean receiving a blow; differ because struck and hit can name a single impact, while buffeted always implies a series of blows.
  • Knocked about / Knocked around: Similar because both describe being pushed and struck from side to side; differ because they are colloquial and less dignified, while buffeted is literary and emphatic.
  • Hammered: Similar because it describes repeated heavy striking; differs because it is often used in sport and finance ('hammered by the market') and suggests deliberate attack rather than random battering.
  • Thrashed: Similar because it means beaten repeatedly; differs because thrashed often means defeated soundly or flogged, a more active, intentional violence than being buffeted by the elements.
  • Tossed: Similar because both describe a ship or person thrown about; differs because tossed emphasises the motion of being flung up and down, while buffeted emphasises the impacts received.
  • Tormented / Harassed (figurative): Similar because both describe sustained external pressure on a person; differ because they imply hostile agents with intentions, while buffeted leaves the source impersonal (fate, weather, events).
