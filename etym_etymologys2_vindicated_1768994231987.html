VINDICATED — the English past participle of to vindicate, ultimately from Latin vindicāre "to lay claim to, assert, avenge." The verb vindicāre is a compound of vim (accusative of vis "force") + dicāre "to proclaim," from the phrase vim dicāre "to proclaim one's force/claim." Two complete chains, one for each element. CHAIN 1 (the "force" element, vim — accusative of vis): 1. Proto-Indo-European *weyh₁- "to be strong, to pursue with vigour, to overcome" (reconstructed; c. 4500–3000 BCE). 2. Latin vis (gen. vīris) "strength, force, power" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE); accusative vim "force" — the form fixed in the phrase vim dicāre. 3. Latin vindicāre "to lay claim to, assert, avenge, liberate" — from vim + dicāre (attested from the 1st c. BCE) — the verb whose participle gives English vindicate. 4. Latin vindicātus (perfect passive participle) "claimed, asserted, avenged" (attested). 5. English vindicate (borrowed from Latin vindicātus in the 16th c., c. 1530s–1550s) "to clear, justify, prove right" — and its participle English vindicated, the rootWord on this card: VINDICATED. CHAIN 2 (the "proclaim" element, dicāre): 1. Proto-Indo-European *deyḱ- "to point out, show, declare" (reconstructed; c. 4500–3000 BCE) — also the source of Sanskrit diśati "he shows," Greek deíknumi "to point out," Latin digitus "finger (the pointer)," and the Germanic token / teach family. 2. Latin dicāre "to proclaim, declare, consecrate" (attested from the 3rd c. BCE), formed alongside dicere "to say" (< *deyḱ-). 3. Latin vim dicāre "to proclaim one's force/claim" — the fixed phrase from which vindicāre was fused (attested). 4. Latin vindicāre "to lay claim, assert, avenge" (attested; 1st c. BCE) — merge point with Chain 1. 5. English vindicate (16th c.) → English VINDICATED (this card). Sister-branch notes: *weyh₁- also gave Latin vincere "to conquer" (English victory, vanquish, convince, invincible) and the Germanic win-family (English to win, won; German gewinnen). *deyḱ- gave Sanskrit diś "direction" (Hindi diśā "direction"), Greek deiktikós "pointing out" (English deictic), and Latin index, digit. Debate note: the Greek reflexes of *weyh₁- are disputed and are deliberately omitted here; Sanskrit vīryà- "heroic deed" descends from a different root (vīrá- "man") and is not a straight cognate of the vis family.
