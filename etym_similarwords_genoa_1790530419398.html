• Venice (Venezia): Similar because both were legendary sovereign Italian maritime republics (Repubbliche Marinare) that dominated Mediterranean trade and naval warfare for centuries; differs because Venice looked eastward toward Byzantium and the Levant with an aristocratic oligarchy, while Genoa looked westward toward Spain and France, excelling especially in merchant banking and Atlantic exploration.
  • Pisa: Similar because Pisa was a premier Tuscan maritime republic and Genoa's fiercest arch-rival on the western Italian coast; differs because Pisa's naval power was decisively destroyed by Genoa at the Battle of Meloria in 1284, after which Pisa declined into an inland Tuscan territory.
  • Amalfi: Similar because Amalfi was one of the four principal historical Italian maritime republics; differs because Amalfi flourished much earlier (9th-11th c.) and was subjugated by the Normans, whereas Genoa maintained its sovereign global empire well into the modern era.
  • Florence (Firenze): Similar because both were powerhouse northern Italian Renaissance city-states governed by wealthy banking and merchant dynasties; differs because Florence was an inland center of humanist art, literature, and manufacturing, while Genoa was predominantly a naval, port, and international maritime power.
  • Naples (Napoli): Similar because both are major historic Italian ports on the Tyrrhenian Sea; differs because Naples was the capital of a southern feudal kingdom (the Kingdom of Naples/Two Sicilies), whereas Genoa was an independent northern merchant republic and commercial oligarchy.
  • Marseille: Similar because both are ancient, bustling Mediterranean port cities founded on natural rocky gulfs that handle international maritime trade; differs because Marseille is French (in Provence) and served the French kingdom, whereas Genoa was an independent Italian city-state and naval empire.
  • Livorno (Leghorn): Similar because Livorno is a major Ligurian/Tuscan commercial port city developed to handle Mediterranean shipping; differs because Livorno was engineered in the 16th century by the Medici as a free port for Tuscany, whereas Genoa has an ancient history dating to pre-Roman Ligurians.
