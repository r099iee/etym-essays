Note: the two source elements are shown together in the first stage; they are parallel components, not stages in a descent from one another. The first element is Greek kata "down", whose deeper Indo-European prehistory is not securely reconstructable. The second element, Latin fala "wooden tower", is of uncertain origin and is often thought to be an Etruscan loanword; it is not a normal inherited Latin form. The Vulgar Latin compound is reconstructed rather than directly attested. Old French eschafaut lost its initial e- in Anglo-Norman, giving the English shape, and the final syllable was later anglicised in spelling. Italian catafalco and English catafalque are doublets from the same Latin base.
