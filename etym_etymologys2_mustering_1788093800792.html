Note: the Old French noun mostre already carried the military sense ‘review of troops’; Modern French montrer restored the -n- (from monstrāre), while English keeps the n-less Old French form. Muster and monster are doublets (both from monstrum). The -ing form has been in regular use since Middle English (15th-c. muster rolls and accounts show musteryng).
