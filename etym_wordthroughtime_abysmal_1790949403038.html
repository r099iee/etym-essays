- Classical Greek to Late Latin - Greek ábyssos ('bottomless') becomes Late Latin abyssimus (extreme superlative: the deepest pit).
- Medieval Old French - Evolves into Old French abisme / abysme, describing the bottomless pit of hell or primeval ocean.
- Middle English to Early Modern English - Enters English as abysm; the derived adjective abysmal (c. 1650) initially describes bottomless physical or metaphysical depths.
- 19th Century - Victorian essayists expand abysmal metaphorically to describe boundless, bottomless human ignorance, poverty, or despair.
- 20th to 21st Century - Colloquial semantic bleaching establishes abysmal as standard hyperbole for utterly terrible performance, awful weather, or comic foolishness.
