• Trotting: Similar because both are steady, medium equine gaits of the same bouncing family; differs because a trot is the brisk two-beat diagonal gait, while jogging is the slower, softer, more rocking easy pace.
  • Running: Similar because both are human foot-gaits faster than walking; differs because running is faster, more strenuous, and more urgent, while the jog is regular, light, and sustainable.
  • Ambling: Similar because both are easy, unhurried ways of moving; differs because ambling is smooth and relaxed with no urgency and no bounce, while jogging keeps a steady rocking rhythm and mild exertion.
  • Plodding: Similar because both are steady, unspectacular, rhythmic motion; differs because plodding is slow, heavy, and weary, while jogging is light, regular, and even cheerful.
  • Loping: Similar because both are relaxed, bounding gaits of horse or runner; differs because a lope is a smooth, long-strided, bounding canter — faster and more fluid — while jogging is the short, bouncing, easy pace.
  • Cantering: Similar because both are equine gaits of medium speed; differs because the canter is a smooth three-beat gait faster than the trot, while jogging is the slow, jolting, easy trot beneath it.
  • Jouncing: Similar because both describe bouncing, jolting movement; differs because jounce implies a rough, heavier, uncontrolled bouncing, while jogging is the gentle, steady, repeated shake.
  • Nudging / Shaking: Similar because both are the root physical senses of the verb jog (to nudge, to give a small shake); differs because these name the single small push, while jogging names the sustained rhythmic series of them.
  • Bounding: Similar because both describe lively, springy motion; differs because bounding is energetic leaping with high amplitude, while jogging is low, even, and unambitious in height.
  • Shuffling: Similar because both are easy, unspectacular pedestrian gaits; differs because shuffling drags the feet with little lift and little purpose, while jogging is a deliberate, springy, rhythmic pace.
