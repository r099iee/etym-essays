• Reginald: Similar because both names are direct etymological cognates descending from the Proto-Germanic dithematic name *Raginwaldaz, meaning 'ruler's counselor'; differs because Reginald is the standard anglicized Latinate form common in England and English-speaking countries, whereas Reynaldo is the Spanish and Portuguese form, carrying distinct Romance phonetics and cultural associations with Venezuelan and French history.
  • Renaud: Similar because both derive from the same Germanic root *Raginwaldaz and achieved prominent status in French cultural and literary history; differs because Renaud is the native northern French development (famous from the medieval chivalric hero Renaud de Montauban), while Reynaldo preserves the Iberian full-syllable structure.
  • Ronald: Similar because both names share the identical Proto-Germanic origin (*Raginwaldaz); differs because Ronald developed through Old Norse Rögnvaldr into Gaelic and Scottish English, carrying Celtic and Highland cultural overtones, whereas Reynaldo is an Iberian and Latin American given name.
  • Gabriel Fauré: Similar because both were preeminent French composers of the late 19th and early 20th centuries who mastered the art song (mélodie) and influenced the musical world of Marcel Proust; differs because Fauré was an older, established master and director of the Conservatoire who explored advanced modal harmony, whereas Reynaldo (Hahn) was Proust's personal lover, salon prodigy, and champion of classical Mozartian lyricism.
  • Camille Saint-Saëns: Similar because both were prodigiously gifted French composers, pianists, and teachers who moved in the highest circles of Belle Époque Parisian musical society; differs because Saint-Saëns was Hahn's teacher and an orchestral giant famous for symphonies and concertos, whereas Reynaldo was celebrated above all for vocal intimacy, art songs, and operettas.
