English (echoic formation) (no PIE ancestor, imitative)
whizz / whiz
‘shaped by the sound it names’
↓
Early Modern English (1540s, first recorded)
whizzen, whizz
‘to make a hissing rushing sound’
↓
Early Modern English (17th c.)
to whizz
‘to rush with a hissing noise’
↓
Modern English (18th–19th c., variant spellings)
to whiz, to whizz
‘to hurry, spin, pass swiftly’
↓
Modern English (past tense, current)
whizzed
‘rushed with a hissing sound’

Note: whizz belongs with the echoic family (whirr, whish, wheeze, buzz, hiss) and has no inherited PIE root; dictionaries list it as imitative, first recorded in the 1540s, with single-z whiz now dominant in America and double-z whizz in Britain.
