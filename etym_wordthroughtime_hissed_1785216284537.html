- [Late 14th century: the serpent's voice] - Hissen is the snake's warning — a pure sibilant of menace, so natural an imitation that its meaning is fixed at birth.
- [17th century: steam and the machine age] - The hiss of escaping steam and gas joins the snake's, giving the word a mechanical life that would grow with industry.
- [Early Modern period: public derision] - The theatre makes hiss a verb of contempt: actors and orators are hissed off the stage, and a hiss becomes a public act of shame.
- [Contemporary sense] - Hissed still runs the whole register from serpent to steam to hostile crowd — and to recording static — its meaning essentially untouched after seven centuries: pure unvoiced friction turned into a weapon.
