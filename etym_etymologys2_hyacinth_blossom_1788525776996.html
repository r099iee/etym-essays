=== CHAIN 1: HYACINTH ===
Pre-Greek (Pelasgian / Mediterranean substrate) (reconstructed; non-IE substrate)
*uakin- / *wakinthos
"blue/purple spring flower; deep blue mineral"
(substrate plant name identified by the characteristic Pre-Greek -nth- suffix)
↓
Ancient Greek (c. 8th c. BCE; Homeric Greek)
ὑάκινθος (hyákinthos)
"hyacinth plant/flower; mythological youth; precious blue gem"
↓
Classical Latin (c. 1st c. BCE; Virgil, Ovid)
hyacinthus
"the hyacinth flower; precious sapphire/jacinth stone"
↓
Old French (c. 12th–13th c.)
hyacinthe / jacinte
"jacinth stone, hyacinth flower"
↓
Middle English (c. 14th c.; Wycliffe, Chaucer)
hyacinthe / iacynte
"precious blue/orange gem; hyacinth flower"
↓
Modern English (c. 16th c.)
hyacinth
"bulbous flowering plant (Hyacinthus orientalis)"

=== CHAIN 2: BLOSSOM ===
PIE (Proto-Indo-European root)
*bʰleh₃- / *bʰlō-
"to bloom, flower, swell with foliage or vitality"
↓
Proto-Germanic (reconstructed)
*blōsmaz / *blōstmaz
"flower, blossom, bloom"
↓
Old English (pre-10th c.; West Saxon)
blōstma / blōsma
"blossom, flower of a plant or fruit tree"
↓
Middle English (c. 12th–14th c.)
blosme / blossum / blossom
"flower, bloom, prime of life"
↓
Modern English (c. 15th–16th c.)
blossom
"the flower of a plant; state of flowering"

=== COMPOUND FORMATION ===
English Literary Formation (19th c.; Oscar Wilde, 1888)
HYACINTH-BLOSSOM
"the fragrant flower of the hyacinth, evoking dark, glossy curls"

Note: The first element (hyacinth) belongs to the ancient non-Indo-European Mediterranean substrate language layer preserved in Greek (attested by the characteristic Pre-Greek suffix -nth-, as in korinthos and labyrinthos). The second element (blossom) is a pure native Germanic formation descended from PIE *bʰleh₃- (whence also bloom, blade, and Latin flos / floral).
