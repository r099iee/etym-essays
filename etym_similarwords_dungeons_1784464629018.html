• Cavern: Similar because both are deep underground spaces and both appear in the same song line; differs because a cavern is a large natural rock chamber, while a dungeon is a built or fortress vault-space of confinement or storage.
  • Keep: Similar because the keep is the dungeon's original meaning — the castle's great central tower; differs because a keep is the strong tower above ground, a place of residence and defense, not an underground prison.
  • Donjon: Similar because donjon is simply the French-form spelling of the same medieval word; differs because in English “donjon” is reserved for the architectural tower/keep sense, while “dungeon” means the underground prison or game space.
  • Cell: Similar because both are places of confinement; differs because a cell is a single small prison room, while a dungeon is the whole vaulted underground strong-room or prison complex.
  • Vault: Similar because both are dark, secure underground chambers; differs because a vault stresses arched construction and storage or burial, not imprisonment, whereas a dungeon's core is captivity or dark depth.
  • Oubliette: Similar because it is a medieval underground prison; differs because an oubliette is a specific bottle-shaped pit entered only from a trapdoor above (from French “to forget”), while a dungeon is a general chamber or complex.
  • Prison: Similar because both are places of confinement; differs because a prison is the general, modern, institutional building for holding convicts, while a dungeon is a dark subterranean stronghold-chamber with a castle or fantasy register.
  • Gaol / Jail: Similar because both are places of detention; differs because gaol/jail is the modern civic detention institution, while dungeon carries medieval-fortress and fantasy connotations.
  • Stronghold: Similar because both belong to fortress architecture; differs because a stronghold is the whole defensible place or fortification, while a dungeon is a specific dark chamber within it.
  • Catacomb: Similar because both are dark underground spaces of many chambers; differs because catacombs are underground networks of tombs (often Roman or early Christian), not prisons or strong-rooms.
  • Labyrinth / Maze: Similar because both appear in fantasy as underground adventure spaces; differs because a labyrinth is a confusing network of passages designed to bewilder, while a dungeon is a set of chambers, often of confinement, storage, or adventure.
